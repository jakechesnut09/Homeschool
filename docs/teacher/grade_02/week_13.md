# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 13

## ELA 2: Peter Rabbit: story structure and irregular past tense

**Prepare:** Get The Tale of Peter Rabbit and The Tale of Benjamin Bunny (Beatrix Potter, public domain) and preview both. Make a story map (characters, setting, beginning, problem, events, ending) and oi/oy word cards (coin, boy, soil, toy, oil, joy, point, enjoy).

**Watch for:**
- Adding -ed to irregular verbs (runned, hided, eated). Make a T-chart of 'today / yesterday' verbs and say them in sentences.
- Spelling oy in the middle of a word (boyl). Teach: oi in the middle, oy at the end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Meet Peter
*Student:* Read The Tale of Peter Rabbit and fill a story map; discuss what the beginning tells us (Mother's warning). Phonics: oi/oy words (coin, boy, soil, toy).

*How to teach:* Read The Tale of Peter Rabbit and fill the story map. Discuss Mother's warning at the beginning. Read oi/oy words.

- **Q:** What does Mrs. Rabbit warn her children at the beginning?
  **A:** Don't go into Mr. McGregor's garden — their father had an accident there and was put in a pie by Mrs. McGregor.
- **Q:** What does Peter do instead?
  **A:** He goes straight into Mr. McGregor's garden and eats lettuces, French beans, and radishes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Peter responds
*Student:* Reread and describe how Peter responds to Mr. McGregor chasing him, using the illustrations as evidence. Write 3 sentences about his feelings.

*How to teach:* Reread. Describe how Peter responds when Mr. McGregor chases him, using illustrations as evidence. Write 3 sentences about his feelings.

- **Q:** How does Peter try to escape?
  **A:** He runs, loses his shoes, gets caught in a gooseberry net by his jacket buttons, wriggles out of his jacket, hides in a watering can, and finally squeezes under the gate.
- **Q:** How does Peter feel during the chase? Give evidence.
  **A:** Frightened — he cries big tears in the net, and he is trembling and lost in the garden.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Past tense irregular verbs
*Student:* Grammar: learn ran, went, ate, hid, sat, told, found from the story and complete 10 sentences. Phonics: oi/oy spelling sort.

*How to teach:* Teach irregular past tense from the story (ran, went, ate, hid, sat, told, found) and complete 10 sentences. Sort oi/oy words.

- **Q:** Past tense of hide and eat?
  **A:** hid; ate.
- **Q:** Fix: 'Peter runned under the gate.'
  **A:** Peter ran under the gate.

### Day 4 (Thursday): How it ends
*Student:* Discuss how the ending (chamomile tea vs. blackberries) concludes the story and teaches a lesson. Rewrite the ending in 4 sentences as if Peter had listened.

*How to teach:* Discuss the ending: Peter is sick and gets camomile tea while his sisters get bread, milk, and blackberries. Ask what lesson it teaches. The child rewrites the ending as if Peter had listened.

- **Q:** How does the ending show Peter's consequence?
  **A:** He loses his jacket and shoes, feels sick, and is put to bed with camomile tea while Flopsy, Mopsy, and Cotton-tail have bread, milk, and blackberries.
- **Q:** What lesson does the story teach?
  **A:** Listen to your parents' warnings; disobeying can bring trouble.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 13 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 10 oi/oy words, irregular past-tense quiz, and a story-map check on 'The Tale of Benjamin Bunny' read aloud.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling check and past-tense quiz. Read The Tale of Benjamin Bunny aloud and the child fills a story map.

- **Q:** Why do Benjamin and Peter go into the garden?
  **A:** To get Peter's clothes back from the scarecrow.
- **Q:** Who rescues them from the cat?
  **A:** Old Mr. Benjamin Bunny, Benjamin's father.

**Assessment:** Story map for a new Potter tale, irregular past-tense quiz, spelling check.

**Answer key:** Story map for The Tale of Benjamin Bunny: Characters — Benjamin Bunny, Peter Rabbit, the cat, old Mr. Benjamin Bunny; Setting — Mr. McGregor's garden; Problem — Peter wants his clothes back from the scarecrow and the boys get trapped under a basket by a cat; Ending — Benjamin's father rescues them and takes them home. Irregular past tense (10): run/ran, go/went, eat/ate, hide/hid, sit/sat, tell/told, find/found, see/saw, come/came, get/got. Spelling: coin, boil, soil, point, join, boy, toy, joy, enjoy, oyster (8+ proficient).

## Math 2: Subtracting within 100 using place value

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks and open number line paper. Write 12 quiz problems for Friday (about half need decomposing a ten).

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column (52 − 17 = 45). Build 52 and show you can't take 7 ones from 2 ones — trade a ten first: 35.
- Counting up but forgetting to add the jumps together at the end. Circle each jump and add them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Subtract tens and ones
*Student:* Subtract 68 − 25 with blocks and quick pictures; solve 10 problems without decomposing.

*How to teach:* Build 68 and take away 2 tens and 5 ones to leave 43. The child draws quick pictures and crosses out what is taken away for 10 problems without regrouping.

- **Q:** 68 − 25 = ?
  **A:** 43.
- **Q:** 96 − 52 = ?
  **A:** 44.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Break a ten
*Student:* Subtract 52 − 17 by trading a ten for 10 ones; solve 8 problems with drawings.

*How to teach:* Build 52. Try to take 7 ones from 2 — not enough. Trade 1 ten for 10 ones (now 4 tens 12 ones). Take 7 ones (5 left) and 1 ten (3 tens left): 35. The child solves 8 problems with drawings.

- **Q:** 52 − 17 = ?
  **A:** 35.
- **Q:** Why do we trade a ten in 61 − 24?
  **A:** There is only 1 one, not enough to take away 4, so trade 1 ten for 10 ones (5 tens 11 ones); 61 − 24 = 37.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count up
*Student:* Solve 83 − 48 by counting up on a number line (48 → 50 → 80 → 83 = 35) and compare to the take-away method.

*How to teach:* Solve 83 − 48 by counting up on a number line: 48 → 50 (+2), 50 → 80 (+30), 80 → 83 (+3): 2 + 30 + 3 = 35. Compare with take-away using blocks — same answer.

- **Q:** Solve 83 − 48 by counting up.
  **A:** 35 (2 + 30 + 3).
- **Q:** Solve 70 − 46 by counting up.
  **A:** 24 — 46 → 50 (+4), 50 → 70 (+20).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Check with addition
*Student:* Solve 10 subtraction problems and check each with addition; explain why addition checks subtraction.

*How to teach:* The child solves 10 subtraction problems, then checks each by adding the answer to the number taken away. Ask why it works: the two parts make the whole.

- **Q:** Check 74 − 29 = 45 with addition.
  **A:** 45 + 29 = 74, so it is correct.
- **Q:** Why does addition check subtraction?
  **A:** The answer plus the part taken away must put the whole back together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz of 12 subtraction problems within 100 and one written explanation of a strategy.

*How to teach:* Give the 12-problem quiz and ask for one written strategy explanation using the words tens, ones, and trade.

- **Q:** 80 − 47 = ?
  **A:** 33.
- **Q:** 100 − 64 = ?
  **A:** 36.

**Assessment:** Subtraction quiz with explanation.

**Answer key:** Subtraction quiz (suggested 12): 1) 68 − 25 = 43 2) 52 − 17 = 35 3) 83 − 48 = 35 4) 90 − 36 = 54 5) 74 − 29 = 45 6) 57 − 34 = 23 7) 61 − 24 = 37 8) 45 − 18 = 27 9) 80 − 47 = 33 10) 96 − 52 = 44 11) 73 − 38 = 35 12) 100 − 64 = 36. Explanation rubric: 3 = strategy with correct place-value steps; 2 = correct but vague; 1 = answer only. Proficient: 10/12.

## Science 2: Fair test: Do plants need light?

**Prepare:** Get 4 similar bean seedlings in cups (from last week's bag planted in soil or sprouted earlier), a dark closet, a ruler, a watering cup, and a data table. Label cups LIGHT 1, LIGHT 2, DARK 1, DARK 2.

**Watch for:**
- Watering the plants different amounts. Only light should change.
- Expecting dark plants to die instantly. Plants in the dark often grow tall, thin, and pale/yellow at first as they stretch for light.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the light test
*Student:* Plan a fair test: 2 plants in sunlight, 2 in a dark closet, same water and soil. Write predictions.

*How to teach:* Plan a fair test: 2 plants in light, 2 in a dark closet, same water and soil. Write predictions.

- **Q:** What is the one thing we change?
  **A:** Light.
- **Q:** What do we keep the same?
  **A:** Water, soil, cup size, and type of plant.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Set up
*Student:* Set up the four plants, label each cup (light/dark), and measure and record each plant's starting height in centimeters.

*How to teach:* Set up four plants, label cups, and measure starting heights in cm.

- **Q:** Why measure the starting height?
  **A:** To compare growth later.
- **Q:** Why use 2 plants in each group?
  **A:** In case one plant is different — more plants make results more reliable.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Observe
*Student:* Measure each plant's height and note its leaf color and stem shape; record the numbers in the data table.

*How to teach:* Measure height and note leaf color and stem shape; record in the data table.

- **Q:** What do you measure from?
  **A:** The soil line to the top of the plant.
- **Q:** Why record color?
  **A:** Color shows plant health.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Midweek check
*Student:* Draw both groups and compare color (green vs. yellow) and stem shape.

*How to teach:* Draw both groups and compare color and stem shape.

- **Q:** What color are the dark plants' leaves?
  **A:** Pale or yellow.
- **Q:** Why do they lose color?
  **A:** Plants need light to make food and stay green.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain why only light was changed and what we keep the same in a fair test.

*How to teach:* Explain why only light was changed and what we keep the same.

- **Q:** Why change only one thing?
  **A:** So we know the light caused any difference.
- **Q:** What is a fair test?
  **A:** A test where only one thing changes.

**Assessment:** Investigation plan and data table.

**Answer key:** Plan: question (Do plants need light?), prediction, variable changed (light), kept the same (water, soil, cup, plant type), measurement (height in cm). Data table with dated heights and leaf color. Expected: light plants stay green and sturdy; dark plants turn pale/yellow with thin, stretched or droopy stems.

## People Who Make a Difference: Who makes laws?

**Prepare:** Photos of the U.S. Capitol and California State Capitol; ballot slips; a simple bill flow chart; look up your mayor's and governor's names ahead of time.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the president makes all laws: Congress makes federal laws; the president signs or vetoes them.
- Thinking a bill is money: a bill is a written idea for a new law.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lawmakers
*Student:* Learn that city councils, the California State Legislature in Sacramento, and Congress in Washington, D.C., make laws; look at photos of each building.

*How to teach:* Teach: city councils, the California State Legislature in Sacramento, and Congress in Washington, D.C., make laws.

- **Q:** Where does Congress meet?
  **A:** In the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C.
- **Q:** Where is California's capital?
  **A:** Sacramento.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): How a bill becomes a law
*Student:* Follow a simple flow chart of how an idea becomes a law (idea → bill → vote → signed).

*How to teach:* Follow a flow chart: idea → bill → vote → signed.

- **Q:** What is a bill?
  **A:** A written idea for a new law.
- **Q:** Who signs a national bill to make it law?
  **A:** The president.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family vote
*Student:* Propose a 'family law' (e.g., a new pet rule), debate it, and vote on it.

*How to teach:* Propose, debate and vote on a 'family law.'

- **Q:** What does it mean to vote?
  **A:** To make a choice that is counted with everyone else's to decide.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Local leaders
*Student:* Find the names of your mayor and governor and write one question you would ask them.

*How to teach:* Find your mayor's and governor's names; write a question.

- **Q:** Who is the leader of California?
  **A:** The governor.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Draw a flow chart of how a law is made.

*How to teach:* Draw a lawmaking flow chart.

- **Q:** What are the two parts of Congress?
  **A:** The Senate and the House of Representatives.

**Assessment:** Lawmaking flow chart.

**Answer key:** Flow chart: idea → bill written → lawmakers discuss and vote (in Congress both the House and Senate) → leader signs it (president for national laws, governor for state laws) → it becomes a law. Proficient = steps in correct order with the right lawmakers named.

## Art & Music 2: High and low: pitch

**Prepare:** Xylophone, piano app, or 5 glasses with water and a spoon; scarves; 'Frère Jacques' recording.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing high with loud: pitch is high/low; dynamics is loud/soft.
- Thinking more water makes a higher pitch in glasses: when tapped, more water makes a LOWER pitch.

### Day 1 (Monday): High or low
*Student:* Listen to pairs of notes on a piano app or xylophone and show high (hands up) or low (hands on knees); hear high and low animal sounds.

*How to teach:* Play note pairs; hands up for high, on knees for low; compare animal sounds.

- **Q:** Is a mouse squeak high or low?
  **A:** High.
- **Q:** What is pitch?
  **A:** How high or low a sound is.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water glass scale
*Student:* Fill 5 glasses with different water levels, tap them, and order them from lowest to highest pitch.

*How to teach:* Fill 5 glasses with different water levels; tap and order from lowest to highest pitch.

- **Q:** Which glass sounds lowest when tapped?
  **A:** The one with the most water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Melody maps
*Student:* Draw lines showing the melody of 'Twinkle, Twinkle' going up, down, and staying the same, then follow the map while singing.

*How to teach:* Draw the melody map of 'Twinkle, Twinkle': leap up on the second 'twinkle,' up on 'little,' down on 'star,' then steps down for 'how I wonder what you are.'

- **Q:** Does 'how I wonder what you are' go up or down?
  **A:** Down by steps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scarf melody
*Student:* Move a scarf up and down to match the melody of 'Frère Jacques' and sing it in tune with a recording.

*How to teach:* Move a scarf up and down with 'Frère Jacques'; sing it.

- **Q:** What is melody contour?
  **A:** The shape a melody makes going up, down or staying the same.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Identify high/low in 8 note pairs and draw the contour of a short melody played by a parent.

*How to teach:* Identify high/low in 8 pairs; draw the contour of a parent's melody.

- **Q:** If the notes go C-D-E, which way is the contour?
  **A:** Up.

**Assessment:** Pitch identification and melody-contour drawing.

**Answer key:** Pitch check: correctly identifies which note is higher in 8 pairs (goal 7/8). Contour drawing goes up, down, or stays the same matching the parent's melody. Proficient = 7-8 correct + accurate contour.

## PE 2: My heart is a muscle

**Prepare:** Timer, chart paper; quiet place to feel pulse.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the heart is not a muscle: it is a muscle that pumps blood.
- Counting heartbeats for 15 seconds and calling it the rate: multiply by 4 for beats per minute.

### Day 1 (Monday): Find your heart
*Student:* Place a hand on your chest, feel your heartbeat resting, and learn that the heart is a muscle pumping blood.

*How to teach:* Feel the heartbeat; explain the heart is a muscle that pumps blood.

- **Q:** What does your heart pump?
  **A:** Blood, all around your body.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Before and after
*Student:* Count heartbeats for 15 seconds at rest, then after 1 minute of jumping jacks; record both.

*How to teach:* Count beats 15 seconds at rest and after 1 minute of jumping jacks.

- **Q:** If you count 20 beats in 15 seconds, how many per minute?
  **A:** 80 beats per minute (20 × 4).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Breathing check
*Student:* Notice and describe breathing at rest, after walking and after running in place.

*How to teach:* Notice breathing at rest, after walking, and after running in place.

- **Q:** When was breathing fastest?
  **A:** After running in place.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heart-healthy moves
*Student:* Sort activities (sleeping, walking, soccer, dancing, video games) into 'slow heart' and 'fast heart' groups.

*How to teach:* Sort activities into 'slow heart' (sleeping, video games) and 'fast heart' (soccer, dancing). Walking is in between/slightly faster.

- **Q:** Is soccer slow-heart or fast-heart?
  **A:** Fast heart.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain with your chart how exercise changes your heart rate and breathing.

*How to teach:* Explain the chart.

- **Q:** Why does your heart beat faster when you exercise?
  **A:** Muscles need more oxygen, so the heart pumps more blood faster.

**Assessment:** Heart-rate chart and oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Chart shows resting beats (15 s) and after-exercise beats (higher). Explanation: exercise makes the heart beat faster and breathing quicker because muscles need more oxygen and blood. Example: 22 beats in 15 seconds × 4 = 88 beats per minute. Proficient = chart + explanation with the reason.

## Health 2: Breakfast and healthy snacks

**Prepare:** Snack foods or pictures; paper for a decision-making worksheet; ingredients for apple 'nachos' (apple slices, peanut/seed butter, raisins) or veggie wraps.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all snacks are unhealthy: snacks can be healthy fuel.
- Thinking 'whoa' foods are 'bad': they're for once in a while, not every day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Breakfast power
*Student:* Learn how breakfast fuels the brain; plan 3 balanced breakfasts.

*How to teach:* Explain breakfast fuels the brain; plan 3 balanced breakfasts.

- **Q:** Why is breakfast important?
  **A:** It gives your brain and body energy to start the day.
- **Q:** Give a balanced breakfast.
  **A:** Oatmeal with fruit and milk; eggs, whole-wheat toast and orange slices.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Snack sort
*Student:* Sort snacks into 'go' (every day), 'slow' (sometimes) and 'whoa' (once in a while).

*How to teach:* Sort snacks into go (every day: fruit, veggies, plain yogurt), slow (sometimes: granola bar, pretzels), whoa (once in a while: candy, chips, soda).

- **Q:** Is an apple a go, slow or whoa food?
  **A:** Go.
- **Q:** Is soda go, slow or whoa?
  **A:** Whoa.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decision steps
*Student:* Use a 3-step decision process (choices, think about results, choose) to pick an after-school snack.

*How to teach:* Use 3 steps (list choices, think about results, choose) to pick a snack.

- **Q:** What are the 3 decision steps?
  **A:** List choices, think about what will happen, choose the best one.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Snack lab
*Student:* Make a healthy snack like apple 'nachos' or veggie wraps and wash produce first.

*How to teach:* Wash produce; make apple nachos or veggie wraps.

- **Q:** Why wash produce?
  **A:** To remove dirt and germs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain your snack choice using the decision steps.

*How to teach:* Explain your snack choice with the steps.

- **Q:** What is added sugar?
  **A:** Sugar put into foods when they're made, not naturally in them.

**Assessment:** Decision-making worksheet and explanation.

**Answer key:** Decision worksheet: Step 1 choices listed (e.g., chips, apple with cheese, cookie); Step 2 results thought through (energy, sugar crash, fullness); Step 3 choice with reason ('apple and cheese because it gives energy and has fruit and dairy'). Proficient = all steps with a health-based reason.
