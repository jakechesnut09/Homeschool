# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 20

## ELA 2: Poetry recording and anthology

**Prepare:** Gather a phone or tablet for audio recording, a folder for the anthology, and drawing supplies. Make compound/two-syllable word bingo cards. Choose a new short poem with rhyme, alliteration, and repetition for the unit test.

**Watch for:**
- Reading poems too fast. Mark slashes for pauses and practice breathing at line ends.
- Copying poems without the poet's name. Every poem in the anthology needs its title and author.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gather poems
*Student:* Choose 5 favorite poems from the unit (yours and published) for a personal anthology and copy them neatly.

*How to teach:* Choose 5 favorite poems from the unit (your own and published) and copy them neatly with title and author.

- **Q:** What two things must be with each poem?
  **A:** The title and the poet's name.
- **Q:** Who wrote 'My Shadow'?
  **A:** Robert Louis Stevenson.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice reading each poem at the right pace with expression; mark pauses with slashes. Phonics review game: two-syllable and compound-word bingo.

*How to teach:* Practice reading each poem at the right pace with expression; mark pauses with slashes. Play compound/two-syllable bingo.

- **Q:** What does a slash mark in a poem show you?
  **A:** Where to pause.
- **Q:** Split 'rainbow.'
  **A:** rain-bow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Record
*Student:* Make an audio recording of 3 poems and listen back to self-assess pace and volume. Re-record one to improve it.

*How to teach:* Record 3 poems, listen back to self-assess pace and volume, and re-record one to improve it.

- **Q:** What should you listen for in your recording?
  **A:** Pace (not too fast), volume (loud enough), and expression.
- **Q:** If you rushed, what should you do?
  **A:** Slow down and pause at the slashes and line ends.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Illustrate
*Student:* Add illustrations and a table of contents to the anthology. Write one sentence for each poem explaining why you chose it.

*How to teach:* Add illustrations and a table of contents. Write one sentence for each poem explaining why you chose it.

- **Q:** What goes in a table of contents?
  **A:** Poem titles and their page numbers, in order.
- **Q:** Write a 'why I chose it' sentence.
  **A:** Example: I chose 'The Swing' because it reminds me of the park.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Poetry café: perform 2 poems for family. Unit test: identify rhyme, alliteration, and repetition in a new poem; spelling test of 15 unit words.

*How to teach:* Hold a poetry café: the child performs 2 poems. Give the unit test on a new poem and the 15-word spelling test.

- **Q:** What is alliteration?
  **A:** Repeating the same beginning sound in nearby words.
- **Q:** What is repetition in a poem?
  **A:** Using the same word or line more than once.

**Assessment:** Poetry recording and anthology (rubric), unit test on poetic elements.

**Answer key:** Anthology and recording rubric (3 = proficient): 5 poems copied neatly with titles and authors, table of contents, illustrations, a 'why I chose it' sentence for each; recording read at a good pace, clear volume, with expression. Unit test on the new poem: correctly identify one rhyming pair, one example of alliteration, and one repeated line or word. Spelling (suggested 15): sunshine, rainbow, notebook, playground, raincoat, paper, repeat, explain, jumped, played, wanted, running, hopping, sitting, swimming.

## Math 2: Subtracting within 1,000

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks and place-value mat. Write 10 quiz problems for Friday, including two with zeros.

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column (462 − 128 = 346). Check the ones first: 2 − 8 can't be done, so trade a ten: 334.
- Across zeros (400 − 165): trying to borrow from 0 tens. Trade a hundred for 10 tens first, then a ten for 10 ones — or count up instead.

### Day 1 (Monday): Subtract by place
*Student:* Subtract 586 − 243 with blocks; solve 8 problems without decomposing.

*How to teach:* Build 586 and remove 2 hundreds, 4 tens, 3 ones: 343. The child solves 8 problems without decomposing, crossing out in quick pictures.

- **Q:** 586 − 243 = ?
  **A:** 343.
- **Q:** 750 − 320 = ?
  **A:** 430.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decompose a ten
*Student:* Subtract 462 − 128, breaking a ten into 10 ones; record with quick pictures.

*How to teach:* Build 462: you can't take 8 ones from 2, so break a ten into 10 ones (5 tens 12 ones). 12 − 8 = 4 ones, 5 − 2 = 3 tens, 4 − 1 = 3 hundreds: 334.

- **Q:** 462 − 128 = ?
  **A:** 334.
- **Q:** 824 − 519 = ?
  **A:** 305.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decompose a hundred
*Student:* Subtract 531 − 274, breaking a hundred and a ten; explain each trade.

*How to teach:* 531 − 274: break a ten (2 tens 11 ones; 11 − 4 = 7), then break a hundred (4 hundreds 12 tens; 12 − 7 = 5 tens), then 4 − 2 = 2 hundreds: 257. The child explains each trade.

- **Q:** 531 − 274 = ?
  **A:** 257.
- **Q:** 615 − 238 = ?
  **A:** 377.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Zeros in the middle
*Student:* Subtract across zeros (400 − 165, 302 − 147) using blocks and counting up; compare strategies.

*How to teach:* 400 − 165 with blocks: trade 1 hundred for 10 tens, then 1 ten for 10 ones (3 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones): 235. Compare counting up: 165 → 200 (+35) → 400 (+200) = 235.

- **Q:** 400 − 165 = ?
  **A:** 235.
- **Q:** 302 − 147 = ?
  **A:** 155 (count up: 147 → 150 is 3, → 300 is 150, → 302 is 2; 3 + 150 + 2 = 155).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 10 subtraction problems within 1,000 and check 3 with addition.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-problem quiz; the child checks 3 answers by adding.

- **Q:** 900 − 456 = ?
  **A:** 444.
- **Q:** Check 703 − 298 = 405.
  **A:** 405 + 298 = 703, so it is correct.

**Assessment:** Subtraction within 1,000 quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested 10): 1) 586 − 243 = 343 2) 462 − 128 = 334 3) 531 − 274 = 257 4) 400 − 165 = 235 5) 302 − 147 = 155 6) 750 − 320 = 430 7) 615 − 238 = 377 8) 900 − 456 = 444 9) 824 − 519 = 305 10) 703 − 298 = 405. Addition checks, e.g., 257 + 274 = 531. 9/10 = proficient.

## Science 2: What is a habitat? Surveying living things nearby

**Prepare:** Gather a hula hoop or 1-meter string loop, magnifier, clipboard and tally sheet, and optionally the free Seek app. Choose two very different survey spots (a lawn or garden bed, and a sidewalk crack or gravel). Remind the child to look, not touch, unknown insects and plants; wash hands afterward.

**Watch for:**
- Counting every ant as a new kind. Tally KINDS of living things (ants = 1 kind) for diversity.
- Thinking a habitat is just 'where an animal lives.' A habitat must provide food, water, shelter, and space.

### Day 1 (Monday): Habitat needs
*Student:* Learn that a habitat provides food, water, shelter, and space; walk around the block and list 4 small habitats (tree, lawn, garden bed, puddle) with one animal that lives in each.

*How to teach:* Teach that a habitat provides food, water, shelter, and space. Walk around the block and list 4 small habitats with one animal each.

- **Q:** What 4 things does a habitat provide?
  **A:** Food, water, shelter, and space.
- **Q:** Name an animal that lives in a tree habitat.
  **A:** Examples: squirrel, bird, beetle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hoop survey 1
*Student:* Lay a hoop on a lawn or garden bed, search it for 15 minutes with a magnifier, and tally every different kind of plant and animal (ants, clover, beetles, grass) in a survey sheet.

*How to teach:* Lay the hoop on a lawn or garden bed; search for 15 minutes and tally different kinds of plants and animals.

- **Q:** If you see 10 ants, how many kinds is that?
  **A:** 1 kind.
- **Q:** Why use a hoop?
  **A:** To survey the same-size area fairly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hoop survey 2
*Student:* Repeat the same 15-minute hoop survey in a very different spot (sidewalk crack, gravel, or under a tree) and tally kinds of living things the same way.

*How to teach:* Repeat the 15-minute hoop survey in a very different spot and tally the same way.

- **Q:** Why survey for the same amount of time?
  **A:** So the comparison is fair.
- **Q:** Predict which spot has more kinds.
  **A:** The spot with more plants, water, and shade.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graph the data
*Student:* Make a bar graph comparing the number of different kinds of living things found in each spot, and circle any organism found in both places.

*How to teach:* Make a bar graph comparing the number of kinds in each spot; circle organisms found in both.

- **Q:** Which spot had more kinds of living things?
  **A:** Answer from data (usually the lawn/garden).
- **Q:** What does 'diversity' mean?
  **A:** How many different kinds of living things are in a place.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a claim with evidence: 'The ___ spot had more kinds of living things because it had more ___ (water, shade, plants),' and share it aloud.

*How to teach:* The child writes a claim with evidence and shares it aloud.

- **Q:** Finish the claim.
  **A:** The garden spot had more kinds of living things because it had more plants and water.
- **Q:** Why might the sidewalk crack have fewer kinds?
  **A:** It has little soil, water, food, and shelter.

**Assessment:** Hoop-survey data, bar graph, and claim-evidence sentence.

**Answer key:** Survey data: kinds of living things tallied for each spot. Bar graph with title, two labeled bars, and scale. Claim-evidence (3 = proficient): 'The garden spot had more kinds of living things because it had more plants, water, and shade,' supported by the counts (e.g., 9 kinds vs. 3 kinds).

## People Who Make a Difference: From farm to table: today

**Prepare:** Milk carton, cereal box, flowchart paper; plan a grocery visit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the store makes food: the store sells food grown and processed by others.
- Leaving out transportation: trucks and trains carry food at many steps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Milk's journey
*Student:* Trace milk from cow to dairy to truck to store to your fridge and draw a flowchart.

*How to teach:* Trace milk from cow to fridge; draw the flowchart.

- **Q:** What happens at the dairy processing plant?
  **A:** Milk is heated (pasteurized), packaged and cooled.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cereal journey
*Student:* Trace a box of cereal from wheat field to factory to store.

*How to teach:* Trace cereal from wheat field to factory to store.

- **Q:** What grain is in many cereals?
  **A:** Wheat, oats, corn or rice.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grocery visit
*Student:* Visit a grocery store and find 5 foods grown in California.

*How to teach:* Visit a grocery store; find 5 foods grown in California.

- **Q:** Name a food grown in California.
  **A:** Strawberries, almonds, lettuce, oranges, grapes, tomatoes, avocados.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Jobs
*Student:* Match food jobs to their roles (farmer, processor, distributor, grocer).

*How to teach:* Match food jobs: farmer, processor, distributor, grocer.

- **Q:** What does a distributor do?
  **A:** Moves food from farms and factories to stores.
- **Q:** What does a processor do?
  **A:** Turns raw food into products (cleans, cooks, packages).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain the farm-to-table steps for one food.

*How to teach:* Explain farm-to-table steps for one food.

- **Q:** Who grows the food?
  **A:** Farmers.

**Assessment:** Farm-to-table flowchart.

**Answer key:** Flowchart for milk: cow on dairy farm → milked → truck → processing plant (pasteurized, bottled) → distributor truck → grocery store → home fridge. Cereal: wheat field (farmer) → mill/factory (processor) → truck (distributor) → store (grocer) → home. Proficient = 5+ steps in order with workers named.

## Art & Music 2: Sculpture project and unit reflection

**Prepare:** Clay or recyclables, sketchbook, paint.

**Watch for:**
- Planning from only one view: a sculpture is seen from all sides.
- Critique means criticize: critique is kind, specific talk about art.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Sketch an animal or creature sculpture from 2 views and list the materials needed.

*How to teach:* Sketch the creature from 2 views; list materials.

- **Q:** Why sketch 2 views?
  **A:** A sculpture is 3-D and seen from different sides.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the main form from clay or recyclables.

*How to teach:* Build the main form.

- **Q:** What is the main form?
  **A:** The biggest basic shape, like the body.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Details
*Student:* Add texture and details (eyes, fur, scales) and paint.

*How to teach:* Add texture and details; paint.

- **Q:** How did you add texture?
  **A:** Pressed tools, added materials.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique
*Student:* Hold a family 'art critique': say one thing you like and one question about each other's sculptures.

*How to teach:* Family critique: one like and one question each.

- **Q:** Give an example of a kind critique.
  **A:** 'I like the bumpy texture on the back. How did you make the eyes stay on?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Explain texture, form, and repurposing in your sculpture.

*How to teach:* Explain texture, form and repurposing in your sculpture.

- **Q:** What is form?
  **A:** A 3-D shape with height, width and depth.

**Assessment:** Original sculpture and critique participation.

**Answer key:** Sculpture rubric: 4 = sketch from 2 views, sturdy form, textured details, painted, child explains texture, form and repurposing; 3 = missing one; 2 = missing two; 1 = incomplete. Critique: gives one like and one question.

## PE 2: Throwing and catching games

**Prepare:** Beanbags, laundry baskets at 3 distances, soft balls, couch cushions, graph paper; week-17 wall scores.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing wildly in fast games: speed with control.
- Thinking practice doesn't matter: graph shows improvement.

### Day 1 (Monday): Basket toss
*Student:* Throw beanbags into laundry baskets at 3 distances for points.

*How to teach:* Throw beanbags into baskets at 3 distances for 1, 2, 3 points.

- **Q:** If you score in the 2-point and 3-point baskets, total?
  **A:** 5 points.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Clean your room
*Student:* Play a family game throwing soft balls over a 'net' (couch cushions) before the timer ends.

*How to teach:* Throw soft balls over a cushion 'net' before the timer ends.

- **Q:** What makes it fair?
  **A:** Same rules and time for both sides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hot potato
*Student:* Play hot potato with quick catch-and-throw passes.

*How to teach:* Hot potato with quick catch-and-throw.

- **Q:** How do you pass fast but safely?
  **A:** Quick, soft underhand tosses.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice graph
*Student:* Retake the wall-target test from week 17 and graph both scores.

*How to teach:* Retake the wall-target test; graph both scores.

- **Q:** If week 17 was 4 and today 7, how much better?
  **A:** 3 more hits.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain with your graph how practice changed your throwing accuracy.

*How to teach:* Explain the graph.

- **Q:** How did practice change your accuracy?
  **A:** Explains change using the two bars.

**Assessment:** Before/after accuracy graph.

**Answer key:** Graph: two bars (week 17 hits out of 10 and this week's hits out of 10), labeled. Explanation states the change (e.g., 'from 4 to 7, 3 more hits, because I practiced stepping and following through'). Proficient = accurate graph + explanation.

## Health 2: Heart, lungs and blood

**Prepare:** Tennis ball; plastic bottle, balloons, straw, rubber band, scissors (adult cuts) for a lung model; timer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking lungs are hollow balloons: they are spongy organs full of tiny air sacs.
- Thinking the heart is shaped like a valentine: it's a fist-sized muscle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heart pump
*Student:* Squeeze a tennis ball to feel how hard the heart works; learn it pumps blood.

*How to teach:* Squeeze a tennis ball hard; the heart squeezes like this all day to pump blood.

- **Q:** About how big is your heart?
  **A:** About the size of your fist.
- **Q:** What does the heart pump?
  **A:** Blood.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lung model
*Student:* Build a balloon lung model in a plastic bottle.

*How to teach:* Build a balloon lung model in a bottle; pull the bottom balloon to inflate the inside one.

- **Q:** What does the bottom balloon model?
  **A:** The diaphragm (muscle under the lungs).
- **Q:** What happens when you pull it down?
  **A:** Air rushes in and the 'lung' balloon fills.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Breathing count
*Student:* Count breaths per minute at rest and after jumping.

*How to teach:* Count breaths per minute at rest and after jumping.

- **Q:** Why do you breathe faster after jumping?
  **A:** Your muscles need more oxygen.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Healthy heart
*Student:* List ways to keep heart and lungs healthy (exercise, no smoking).

*How to teach:* List ways to keep heart and lungs healthy.

- **Q:** Name two ways.
  **A:** Exercise, eat healthy, never smoke or vape, avoid secondhand smoke.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how heart and lungs work together to move oxygen.

*How to teach:* Explain how heart and lungs work together.

- **Q:** What is oxygen?
  **A:** A gas in the air your body needs to live.

**Assessment:** Oral explanation using models.

**Answer key:** Oral explanation: lungs breathe in air with oxygen; oxygen passes into the blood; the heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body; blood brings carbon dioxide back to the lungs to breathe out. Proficient = mentions lungs taking in oxygen, heart pumping blood, oxygen delivered to the body.
