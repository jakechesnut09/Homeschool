# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 9

## ELA 2: Nonfiction text features

**Prepare:** Get National Geographic Readers: Frogs! (Elizabeth Carney) or another NatGeo Kids reader, plus 10 mixed library books, sticky flags, and a magnifying glass. Print a short ReadWorks animal passage with headings for Friday and write 6 text-feature questions.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fiction is anything with pictures. Fiction is made up; nonfiction gives true facts — check for headings, photos, captions, and a glossary.
- Reading the whole book to find one fact. Teach jumping to the table of contents or index first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fiction or nonfiction
*Student:* Sort 10 library books into fiction and nonfiction and explain the clues. Take a text-feature tour of Frogs! and flag a heading, caption, bold word, and glossary.

*How to teach:* Sort 10 books into fiction and nonfiction, saying the clue for each. Tour Frogs! and flag a heading, caption, bold word, and the glossary.

- **Q:** Is Frogs! fiction or nonfiction? How do you know?
  **A:** Nonfiction — it gives true facts with real photos, headings, captions, and a glossary.
- **Q:** What does a caption do?
  **A:** It tells about a picture or photo.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Feature scavenger hunt
*Student:* Use the table of contents and index to answer 5 questions from Frogs! (e.g., 'What page tells what frogs eat?'). Phonics: ar words (car, farm, star).

*How to teach:* Use the table of contents and index to answer 5 questions from Frogs!. Read ar words.

- **Q:** Where do you look to find which page tells what frogs eat?
  **A:** The index (look up 'food' or 'eat') or the table of contents.
- **Q:** What is the difference between the table of contents and the index?
  **A:** The table of contents is at the front and lists chapters in order; the index is at the back and lists topics in ABC order with page numbers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Glossary words
*Student:* Look up 5 bold words in the glossary and draw a picture for each. Phonics: or words (corn, storm, horse) in a word sort.

*How to teach:* Look up 5 bold words in the glossary and draw each. Sort or words.

- **Q:** Why are some words in bold print?
  **A:** They are important words; their meanings are in the glossary.
- **Q:** Frogs are amphibians. What does that mean?
  **A:** Animals that live part of their lives in water and part on land (frogs start as tadpoles in water).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nouns and verbs
*Student:* Grammar: sort words from Frogs! into nouns and verbs (frog, leap, pond, swim). Write 4 fact sentences, each with a noun and a verb.

*How to teach:* Sort words from Frogs! into nouns and verbs (frog, leap, pond, swim). Write 4 fact sentences, each with a noun and verb.

- **Q:** Sort: frog, leap, pond, swim.
  **A:** Nouns: frog, pond. Verbs: leap, swim.
- **Q:** Write a fact sentence about frogs with a noun and a verb.
  **A:** Example: Frogs catch insects with their sticky tongues.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 9 review
*Student:* Use text features to answer 6 questions from a new ReadWorks animal passage. Spelling check of 10 ar/or words.

*How to teach:* Have the child use text features to answer 6 questions on a new animal passage, then give the ar/or spelling check.

- **Q:** Which feature helps you find a section quickly?
  **A:** A heading (or the table of contents).
- **Q:** Spell 'storm' and 'farm.'
  **A:** s-t-o-r-m; f-a-r-m.

**Assessment:** Text-feature scavenger hunt (6 questions on a new text) and spelling check.

**Answer key:** Text-feature hunt on the new passage: answers depend on the passage — check that the child uses the correct feature (heading to find a section, caption to explain a photo, bold word + glossary for meanings, table of contents for page numbers). 6/6 or 5/6 = proficient. Spelling (suggested 10): car, farm, star, park, hard, corn, storm, horse, fork, short.

## Math 2: Measuring in inches and feet

**Prepare:** Gather a 12-inch ruler, yardstick, measuring tape, and a box of same-size paper clips. Choose 10 household objects to measure (pencil, spoon, shoe, book). For Friday, draw 6 lines or objects of exact whole-inch lengths.

**Watch for:**
- Starting at the 1 instead of 0 (or at the very end of the ruler). Put a finger on the 0 mark and line the object up there, or count the spaces, not the marks.
- Leaving gaps or overlapping units. Show that gaps make the count too small and overlaps make it too big.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why units
*Student:* Measure a book with paper clips leaving gaps and overlaps, then correctly; discuss why units must be the same size with no gaps.

*How to teach:* Measure a book with paper clips left with gaps, then overlapping, then end-to-end. Compare the three counts and ask which is right and why. Conclude: same-size units, no gaps, no overlaps.

- **Q:** Why do we line up paper clips with no gaps?
  **A:** Gaps leave parts unmeasured, so the count would be too small.
- **Q:** If clips overlap, is the count too big or too small?
  **A:** Too big — some length gets counted twice.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Inches
*Student:* Measure 10 household objects (pencil, spoon, shoe) to the nearest inch with a ruler starting at 0; record in a table.

*How to teach:* Show the 0 mark on the ruler. Line up a pencil at 0 and read the nearest inch. The child measures 10 objects and records them in a two-column table (object | inches).

- **Q:** A crayon ends just past the 3-inch mark. To the nearest inch, how long is it?
  **A:** About 3 inches.
- **Q:** Where do you line up the end of the object?
  **A:** At the 0 mark (the start) of the ruler.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feet and yards
*Student:* Measure a table, a door, and a room with a yardstick or tape; choose the best tool for each and explain.

*How to teach:* Show that 12 inches = 1 foot (a ruler) and 3 feet = 1 yard (a yardstick). Measure a table, a door, and a room; the child picks the tool and says why (tape for long or curved things).

- **Q:** Which tool is best for measuring a room?
  **A:** A measuring tape or yardstick — it is long.
- **Q:** How many inches are in 1 foot?
  **A:** 12 inches.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Estimate first
*Student:* Estimate then measure 8 objects in inches or feet; find how close each estimate was.

*How to teach:* The child estimates, then measures 8 objects, recording both. Find the difference between estimate and measurement and celebrate estimates within 2 inches.

- **Q:** You estimated 6 inches and measured 9 inches. How far off were you?
  **A:** 3 inches (9 − 6 = 3).
- **Q:** Would you measure a shoe in inches or feet?
  **A:** Inches — it is shorter than a foot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Measure 6 pictured objects to the nearest inch and choose the right unit/tool for 6 situations.

*How to teach:* Give the measuring check and the unit/tool quiz. Watch the child's ruler placement on each item and correct the starting point on the spot.

- **Q:** What unit and tool would you use for the height of a door?
  **A:** Feet; a measuring tape or yardstick.
- **Q:** What unit and tool for the length of a spoon?
  **A:** Inches; a ruler.

**Assessment:** Measurement accuracy check and tool/unit choice quiz.

**Answer key:** Measuring check: prepare 6 pictured lines of known length (e.g., 3 in, 5 in, 2 in, 6 in, 4 in, 7 in); accept the exact whole inch. Unit/tool choice (suggested): 1) length of a pencil → inches, ruler 2) length of a room → feet, measuring tape or yardstick 3) height of a door → feet, tape or yardstick 4) length of a crayon → inches, ruler 5) length of a car → feet, measuring tape 6) width of a book → inches, ruler. Proficient: 5/6 measurements correct and 5/6 unit choices.

## Science 2: Reversible changes with heating and cooling

**Prepare:** Gather ice cube trays, chocolate chips, zip bags, warm water, old crayon pieces (paper removed), a muffin tin with liners, and an oven. Safety: an adult handles the oven and hot crayon wax.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking melted chocolate is a new substance. It is still chocolate — just in liquid form.
- Thinking 'reversible' means it looks exactly the same. It means it can change back to its earlier state (a solid again), even if the shape is different.

### Day 1 (Monday): Freeze and melt
*Student:* Freeze water in ice trays and then melt the cubes; record that water becomes ice and back again.

*How to teach:* Freeze water in ice trays, then melt the cubes. Record water → ice → water.

- **Q:** What happens to water in the freezer?
  **A:** It freezes into solid ice.
- **Q:** Can ice turn back into water?
  **A:** Yes — when it warms, it melts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chocolate change
*Student:* Melt chocolate chips in a bag in warm water, then cool them in the fridge; observe the shape change and reversal.

*How to teach:* Melt chocolate chips in a bag in warm water, then cool them in the fridge. Observe shape change and reversal.

- **Q:** What happened to the chocolate when heated?
  **A:** It melted into a liquid.
- **Q:** What happened in the fridge?
  **A:** It became solid again, in a new shape.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crayon remake
*Student:* With an adult, melt broken crayon pieces in a muffin tin in the oven at low heat and cool them into new crayons.

*How to teach:* With an adult, melt crayon pieces in a muffin tin at low heat, then cool into new crayons.

- **Q:** Are the new crayons still crayon wax?
  **A:** Yes — same material, new shape.
- **Q:** Why must an adult help?
  **A:** The oven and melted wax are hot and can burn.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Before and after
*Student:* Make a before/heated/cooled chart for the three investigations.

*How to teach:* Make a before/heated/cooled chart for the three investigations.

- **Q:** What do all three have in common?
  **A:** Heating melted them and cooling made them solid again.
- **Q:** What does reversible mean?
  **A:** A change that can be undone.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain which changes were reversible and give evidence from the chart.

*How to teach:* The child explains which changes were reversible with evidence.

- **Q:** Was melting chocolate reversible? Evidence?
  **A:** Yes — it became solid again in the fridge.
- **Q:** Is freezing water reversible?
  **A:** Yes — ice melts back into water.

**Assessment:** Before/after chart and reversible-change explanation.

**Answer key:** Before/heated/cooled chart: water — liquid → solid ice when cooled → liquid when warmed; chocolate — solid chips → liquid when heated → solid when cooled (new shape); crayons — solid pieces → liquid wax when heated → solid new crayon when cooled. Explanation: all three are reversible because the material went back to a solid when cooled; evidence from the chart.

## People Who Make a Difference: North America: rivers, lakes, and mountains

**Prepare:** Blank North America map, atlas, clay; blue and brown pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing the Rockies and Appalachians: the Rockies are in the west (tall, jagged); the Appalachians are in the east (older, lower, rounded).
- Thinking the Mississippi flows north: it flows south to the Gulf of Mexico.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mountains
*Student:* Locate and label the Rocky Mountains, Appalachian Mountains, and Sierra Nevada on the map; build a clay model of the Rockies.

*How to teach:* Label the Rocky Mountains, Appalachian Mountains and Sierra Nevada; model the Rockies in clay.

- **Q:** Which mountains are in eastern California?
  **A:** The Sierra Nevada.
- **Q:** Which mountain range is in the eastern U.S.?
  **A:** The Appalachian Mountains.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rivers
*Student:* Trace and label the Mississippi, Missouri, Colorado, and Rio Grande rivers; discuss why rivers matter to people.

*How to teach:* Trace and label the Mississippi, Missouri, Colorado and Rio Grande; discuss why rivers matter.

- **Q:** Where does the Mississippi River end?
  **A:** In the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Q:** Why do rivers matter to people?
  **A:** Drinking water, farming, travel and shipping, fishing, power.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Great Lakes
*Student:* Learn the five Great Lakes with the mnemonic HOMES and label them.

*How to teach:* Learn the Great Lakes with HOMES; label them.

- **Q:** What does HOMES stand for?
  **A:** Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, Superior.
- **Q:** Which two countries share the Great Lakes?
  **A:** The United States and Canada.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Memory practice
*Student:* Label the complete map from memory and check with an atlas.

*How to teach:* Label the complete map from memory; check with an atlas.

- **Q:** Which river forms part of the border between Texas and Mexico?
  **A:** The Rio Grande.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Map quiz: label countries, oceans, Great Lakes, 2 rivers, and 2 mountain ranges from memory.

*How to teach:* Give the full map quiz.

- **Q:** What is a landform?
  **A:** A natural shape of the land, like a mountain, valley or plain.

**Assessment:** Complete North America map from memory.

**Answer key:** Map from memory: Canada, U.S., Mexico; Pacific, Atlantic, Arctic; Great Lakes (Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, Superior); 2 rivers (e.g., Mississippi - flows south to the Gulf; Colorado - southwest, through the Grand Canyon; Missouri; Rio Grande - border of Texas and Mexico); 2 mountain ranges (Rocky - west; Appalachian - east; Sierra Nevada - eastern California). Proficient = 12+ of 15 correct.

## Art & Music 2: The color wheel

**Prepare:** Red, yellow, blue tempera or watercolor; brushes; paper plates; water cups; paper towels.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you can mix primaries from other colors: primaries can't be mixed.
- Using dirty brushes: wash between colors to keep colors clean.

### Day 1 (Monday): Primary colors
*Student:* Learn red, yellow, blue as primary colors and paint three blobs; practice brush washing routine.

*How to teach:* Paint red, yellow and blue blobs; practice brush washing.

- **Q:** What are the primary colors?
  **A:** Red, yellow, blue.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mixing
*Student:* Mix orange, green, and purple on a paper plate palette and record each recipe.

*How to teach:* Mix orange, green, purple on a plate; record recipes.

- **Q:** Yellow + blue = ?
  **A:** Green.
- **Q:** Red + blue = ?
  **A:** Purple.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Color wheel
*Student:* Paint a 6-part color wheel on a paper plate.

*How to teach:* Paint a 6-part color wheel on a plate.

- **Q:** What color goes between yellow and blue?
  **A:** Green.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Color hunt
*Student:* Find objects at home in each color of the wheel and photograph or draw them.

*How to teach:* Find objects in each wheel color; photograph or draw.

- **Q:** Find something orange.
  **A:** Example: an orange, a carrot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Name primary and secondary colors and explain how to mix each.

*How to teach:* Name primary and secondary colors and how to mix each.

- **Q:** What are the secondary colors?
  **A:** Orange, green, purple.

**Assessment:** Painted color wheel and oral check.

**Answer key:** Color wheel order: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple (violet), each secondary between its two primaries. Oral: primaries = red, yellow, blue; red + yellow = orange; yellow + blue = green; blue + red = purple. Proficient = all correct.

## PE 2: Balances on different body parts

**Prepare:** Mat or carpet, paper to draw 6 balance cards.

**Watch for:**
- Holding breath to balance: breathe normally and tighten the core.
- Thinking narrow bases are easier: a wider base of support is more stable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Body part balances
*Student:* Balance on 5, 4, 3, 2, then 1 body part, holding each for 5 seconds.

*How to teach:* Balance on 5, 4, 3, 2, then 1 body part, 5 seconds each.

- **Q:** Which is easiest: 5 parts or 1 part?
  **A:** 5 parts, because the base of support is bigger.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wide vs. narrow base
*Student:* Compare balancing with feet wide and feet together; explain which is easier and why.

*How to teach:* Compare feet wide vs. feet together; explain.

- **Q:** Why is a wide base easier?
  **A:** More area supports you, so you're more stable.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Balance cards
*Student:* Draw 6 balance cards (stork, airplane, bridge, tripod, V-sit, frog) and perform them in a shuffled order.

*How to teach:* Draw 6 balance cards (stork, airplane, bridge, tripod, V-sit, frog); perform shuffled.

- **Q:** What is a stork balance?
  **A:** Standing on one foot with the other foot tucked up.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner balance
*Student:* Do partner balances (back-to-back sit-stand, hand-to-hand lean) safely with a family member.

*How to teach:* Partner balances: back-to-back sit-stand, hand-to-hand lean.

- **Q:** How do partners stay safe?
  **A:** Talk, move slowly, push evenly, and let go gently.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold 4 different balances for 5 seconds each and explain what a 'tight core' does.

*How to teach:* Hold 4 balances 5 seconds; explain tight core.

- **Q:** What does a tight core do?
  **A:** Keeps the middle of your body strong and steady so you can balance.

**Assessment:** Balance hold checklist.

**Answer key:** Balance hold checklist: holds 4 balances still for 5 seconds (e.g., stork, airplane, bridge, V-sit). Explanation: a tight core (stomach and back muscles) keeps your body steady. Proficient = 4/4 + explanation.

## Health 2: Protecting skin, eyes and ears

**Prepare:** Dark construction paper and small objects (leave in sun Day 1), sunscreen bottle, sunglasses, hat; UV beads optional.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you only need sunscreen on hot days: UV rays reach you on cloudy and cool days too.
- Thinking loud noise only hurts for a moment: repeated loud sounds can cause permanent hearing loss.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sun print
*Student:* Leave construction paper with objects on it in the sun to see how UV rays fade it; discuss skin.

*How to teach:* Leave paper with objects in the sun; later see faded areas; discuss skin.

- **Q:** Why did the paper fade except under the objects?
  **A:** UV rays from the sun faded the uncovered paper.
- **Q:** What do UV rays do to skin?
  **A:** They can cause sunburn and skin damage.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sun safety
*Student:* Learn 'Slip, Slop, Slap, Wrap' (shirt, sunscreen, hat, sunglasses) and read an SPF label.

*How to teach:* Teach 'Slip, Slop, Slap, Wrap'; read the SPF on a label.

- **Q:** What do Slip, Slop, Slap, Wrap mean?
  **A:** Slip on a shirt, slop on sunscreen, slap on a hat, wrap on sunglasses.
- **Q:** What does SPF tell you?
  **A:** How well sunscreen protects against sunburn (30 or higher recommended).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Eye safety
*Student:* Discuss safety goggles, never looking at the sun and screen breaks (20-20-20 rule).

*How to teach:* Discuss goggles, never looking at the sun, and the 20-20-20 rule.

- **Q:** What is the 20-20-20 rule?
  **A:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ear safety
*Student:* Compare quiet and loud sounds, and learn why loud music and noise can hurt hearing.

*How to teach:* Compare quiet and loud sounds; discuss protecting hearing.

- **Q:** How can you protect your ears?
  **A:** Turn volume down, wear earplugs/earmuffs, move away from loud noise.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a poster about protecting skin, eyes and ears.

*How to teach:* Make a poster about protecting skin, eyes and ears.

- **Q:** Give one tip for each.
  **A:** Skin: sunscreen; eyes: sunglasses; ears: lower the volume.

**Assessment:** Poster with three protection tips.

**Answer key:** Poster has at least one correct tip for each: skin (sunscreen SPF 30+, reapply every 2 hours, shirt, hat, shade), eyes (sunglasses, never look at the sun, goggles for projects, 20-20-20 screen breaks), ears (turn volume down, earplugs or earmuffs for loud places, step away from loud noise). Proficient = 3 accurate tips with pictures.
