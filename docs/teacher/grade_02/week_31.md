# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 31

## ELA 2: How-to writing: steps in a procedure

**Prepare:** Gather a seed packet, a simple trail-mix recipe and ingredients (e.g., cereal, raisins, pretzels; check allergies), measuring cups, and paper. Make a list of 12 multisyllable science words (ingredient, measure, sprinkle, minute, seedling, gather, together, important, number, finally, carefully, container).

**Watch for:**
- Skipping steps that seem obvious. Have someone follow the directions exactly to find missing steps.
- Steps without numbers or time-order words. Number each step and start with first, next, then, last.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read a procedure
*Student:* Read the steps on a seed packet and a simple recipe; discuss why order matters and what happens if a step is skipped.

*How to teach:* Read steps on a seed packet and a recipe. Discuss why order matters and what happens if a step is skipped.

- **Q:** What could happen if you skip 'water the seeds'?
  **A:** The seeds might not sprout.
- **Q:** Why are steps numbered?
  **A:** To show the order to follow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Follow the steps
*Student:* Make trail mix following written steps, then rewrite the steps in your own words with numbers and time-order words. Phonics: multisyllable words (ingredient, measure).

*How to teach:* Make trail mix following written steps, then rewrite the steps in your own words with numbers and time-order words.

- **Q:** Write step 1 for trail mix.
  **A:** Example: First, pour one cup of cereal into a bowl.
- **Q:** Split 'ingredient' into syllables.
  **A:** in-gre-di-ent.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a how-to
*Student:* Choose something you know how to do (make a sandwich, care for a pet, build a block tower) and list materials and steps.

*How to teach:* Choose something you know how to do and list the materials and steps.

- **Q:** List steps for making a sandwich.
  **A:** Example: 1) Get two slices of bread. 2) Spread peanut butter. 3) Put the slices together. 4) Cut it in half.
- **Q:** What goes in a materials list?
  **A:** Everything you need to do the task.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft and test
*Student:* Draft the how-to with an introduction, numbered steps, and a closing; have a family member follow it exactly and note any missing steps.

*How to teach:* Draft the how-to with introduction, numbered steps, and closing. A family member follows it exactly and notes missing steps.

- **Q:** Why have someone follow your steps exactly?
  **A:** To find steps that are missing or unclear.
- **Q:** Write a closing for a sandwich how-to.
  **A:** Example: Now enjoy your tasty sandwich!

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 31 review
*Student:* Revise the how-to after the test, add diagrams, and publish. Spelling check of 12 multisyllable science words.

*How to teach:* Revise after testing, add diagrams, and publish. Give the 12-word spelling check.

- **Q:** Spell 'measure.'
  **A:** m-e-a-s-u-r-e.
- **Q:** How does a diagram help a how-to?
  **A:** It shows what to do in a picture.

**Assessment:** Published how-to text tested by a reader; spelling check.

**Answer key:** How-to rubric (3 = proficient): introduction tells what you will make or do; materials list; numbered steps in correct order with time-order words; a closing; a reader can follow it without help. Spelling (suggested 12): ingredient, measure, sprinkle, minute, seedling, gather, together, important, number, finally, carefully, container.

## Math 2: Two-dimensional shapes

**Prepare:** Geoboard or dot paper, pattern blocks (or paper cutouts), straws with clay or pipe-cleaner connectors.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a turned square is no longer a square ('it's a diamond'). Turn it back and count equal sides and square corners.
- Thinking only the pattern-block hexagon is a hexagon. Any closed shape with 6 straight sides is a hexagon.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sides and angles
*Student:* Sort pattern blocks and cut-out shapes by number of sides and angles; name each group.

*How to teach:* Sort pattern blocks and cutouts by number of sides and angles. Name the groups: triangle (3), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6).

- **Q:** How many sides does a hexagon have?
  **A:** 6.
- **Q:** A shape has 5 angles. What is it?
  **A:** A pentagon.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Quadrilaterals
*Student:* Explore quadrilaterals (square, rectangle, rhombus, trapezoid) on a geoboard and list what they share.

*How to teach:* Make a square, rectangle, rhombus, and trapezoid on a geoboard. List what they share (4 sides, 4 angles).

- **Q:** What do all quadrilaterals have?
  **A:** 4 sides and 4 angles (corners).
- **Q:** How is a rhombus different from a square?
  **A:** A rhombus has 4 equal sides but does not need square corners.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draw to rules
*Student:* Draw shapes with given attributes ('a shape with 5 sides,' 'a quadrilateral with no square corners').

*How to teach:* Read a clue; the child draws a shape that fits.

- **Q:** Draw a quadrilateral with no square corners.
  **A:** Example: a slanted rhombus or a trapezoid with slanted sides.
- **Q:** Draw a shape with 5 sides.
  **A:** Any closed pentagon.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Straw shapes
*Student:* Build shapes with straws and connectors or clay; count sides and vertices.

*How to teach:* Build shapes with straws and connectors; count sides and vertices (corners).

- **Q:** How many vertices does a triangle have?
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** A shape has 4 sides. How many vertices?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Identify and draw shapes from attribute clues (10 items).

*How to teach:* Give the 10-item attribute quiz.

- **Q:** 4 equal sides and 4 square corners?
  **A:** Square.
- **Q:** 6 sides?
  **A:** Hexagon.

**Assessment:** Shape attribute quiz.

**Answer key:** Shape quiz (suggested 10 clues): 1) 3 sides, 3 angles → triangle 2) 4 sides → quadrilateral 3) 5 sides → pentagon 4) 6 sides → hexagon 5) 4 equal sides, 4 square corners → square 6) 4 square corners, sides not all equal → rectangle 7) 4 equal sides, no square corners → rhombus 8) quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides → trapezoid 9) draw a shape with 5 sides → any closed pentagon 10) 6 vertices → hexagon. Proficient: 9/10.

## Science 2: Improve, communicate, and unit check

**Prepare:** Keep last week's designs and data; gather poster paper and markers. Find a short reading about dune grass protecting California beaches. Prepare 4 erosion photos and 3 situations for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Changing many features at once when improving. Change one feature and retest.
- Presenting without data. Include numbers from tests.

### Day 1 (Monday): Improve
*Student:* Change one feature of the better design (taller wall, added 'plants') and retest; record whether results improved.

*How to teach:* Change one feature of the better design and retest; record results.

- **Q:** What did you change?
  **A:** Example: made the wall taller.
- **Q:** Did it improve?
  **A:** Answer from data.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Poster
*Student:* Make a poster showing the problem, both designs, test data, and the improved design.

*How to teach:* Make a poster showing the problem, designs, data, and improved design.

- **Q:** Why include data?
  **A:** To prove which design worked best.
- **Q:** What is the problem?
  **A:** Water washing sand away from the house.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Present
*Student:* Present the poster to family, explaining why the improved design worked better, and answer 2 questions.

*How to teach:* Present the poster and answer 2 questions.

- **Q:** Why did the improved design work better?
  **A:** Example: the taller wall blocked more water.
- **Q:** What would you try next?
  **A:** Example: add plants.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Real world
*Student:* Read about sand dunes and dune grass protecting California beaches and compare them to your designs.

*How to teach:* Read about dune grass protecting California beaches and compare to your designs.

- **Q:** How does dune grass protect beaches?
  **A:** Its roots hold the sand and slow the wind.
- **Q:** Which of your designs is most like dune grass?
  **A:** Example: adding 'plants' to the hill.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Quiz: identify erosion in 4 photos and choose the best solution for 3 situations with a reason.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz.

- **Q:** Best solution for a hillside washing away?
  **A:** Plant grass or shrubs — roots hold soil.
- **Q:** Best solution for beach sand blowing away?
  **A:** Sand fences or dune grass.

**Assessment:** Design poster presentation and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric (3 = proficient): problem, both designs, test data, improved design with results. Unit quiz: erosion identified in 4 photos (e.g., gully, exposed roots, eroded cliff, washed-out path). Solutions: steep hillside soil washing away → plant grass/shrubs (roots hold soil); beach sand blowing away → sand fences or dune grass; river flooding a town → levee. Each with a reason.

## People Who Make a Difference: Rosa Parks, Martin Luther King Jr., and Ruby Bridges

**Prepare:** Rosa Parks' police record (National Archives); an excerpt of Dr. King's 'I Have a Dream' speech (1963); 'The Story of Ruby Bridges' by Robert Coles; hero timeline. Preview to explain unfair laws calmly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Rosa Parks was simply tired: she was tired of unfair treatment and chose to stand up for equal rights.
- Thinking civil rights leaders used violence: they used peaceful protest like boycotts, marches and speeches.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rosa Parks
*Student:* Read a short biography of Rosa Parks and look at her 1955 arrest record from the National Archives; discuss why breaking an unfair rule took courage.

*How to teach:* Read about Rosa Parks; view her police record; discuss courage.

- **Q:** What did Rosa Parks do in 1955?
  **A:** Refused to give up her bus seat to a white man in Montgomery, Alabama, and was arrested.
- **Q:** What happened next?
  **A:** Black residents boycotted Montgomery's buses for over a year until the buses were desegregated.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dr. King
*Student:* Listen to an excerpt of Dr. King's 'I Have a Dream' speech and write your own dream for a fair world.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'I Have a Dream'; write your own dream.

- **Q:** What was Dr. King's dream?
  **A:** That his children would be judged by the content of their character, not the color of their skin.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ruby Bridges
*Student:* Read The Story of Ruby Bridges and discuss how a 6-year-old helped integrate a New Orleans school in 1960.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Story of Ruby Bridges'; discuss.

- **Q:** How old was Ruby Bridges?
  **A:** 6 years old.
- **Q:** What did Ruby do when crowds shouted at her?
  **A:** She stayed brave, walked into school with federal marshals, and prayed for the people shouting.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Timeline
*Student:* Place 1947 (Jackie Robinson), 1955 (bus boycott), 1960 (Ruby Bridges), and 1963 (March on Washington) on the hero timeline.

*How to teach:* Place 1947, 1955, 1960, 1963 on the timeline.

- **Q:** Which came first: the bus boycott or Ruby Bridges?
  **A:** The bus boycott (1955), before Ruby Bridges (1960).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain how each person used peaceful action to make a difference.

*How to teach:* Explain how each used peaceful action.

- **Q:** What is a boycott?
  **A:** Refusing to buy or use something to protest unfairness.

**Assessment:** Civil rights timeline and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Timeline: 1947 Jackie Robinson joins the Dodgers; 1955 Montgomery Bus Boycott begins after Rosa Parks' arrest; 1960 Ruby Bridges integrates William Frantz Elementary in New Orleans; 1963 March on Washington and 'I Have a Dream.' Explanation: Parks peacefully refused to give up her bus seat, leading to a boycott; King led peaceful marches and gave speeches; Ruby bravely went to school each day despite angry crowds. Proficient = correct order + peaceful action for each.

## Art & Music 2: Moving stories: dance, drama, and music together

**Prepare:** Recording of 'Peter and the Wolf' or 'Carnival of the Animals,' props, homemade percussion.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the arts work separately: dance, drama and music together tell a richer story.
- Rehearsing without fixing anything: fix one problem each run-through.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose the story
*Student:* Choose a scene from 'Peter and the Wolf' or 'Carnival of the Animals' and list its characters and events.

*How to teach:* Choose a scene; list characters and events.

- **Q:** Who are the characters in your scene?
  **A:** Lists them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Create movement
*Student:* Create a movement for each character that matches its music (slinky clarinet cat, heavy elephant).

*How to teach:* Create a movement for each character matching its music (slinky clarinet cat, heavy elephant).

- **Q:** How does the cat's music make you move?
  **A:** Sneaky, smooth, slinky steps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add sound
*Student:* Add homemade percussion sound effects and a narrator line for each part of the scene.

*How to teach:* Add percussion sound effects and a narrator line for each part.

- **Q:** What does a narrator do?
  **A:** Tells the story to the audience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the whole piece twice, fixing one problem each time.

*How to teach:* Rehearse twice, fixing one problem each time.

- **Q:** What did you fix?
  **A:** Specific fix.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Perform the moving story for family and explain how dance, drama, and music worked together.

*How to teach:* Perform; explain how dance, drama and music worked together.

- **Q:** How did the music help the acting?
  **A:** It told the mood and character so movements matched.

**Assessment:** Moving-story performance and explanation.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric: each character has movement matching its music; sound effects and narrator lines; smooth sequence after rehearsal; explanation of how dance (movement), drama (character/narration) and music (instruments, tempo, dynamics) worked together. 4 = all; 3 = one weak area; 2 = several gaps; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 2: Cooperative challenges

**Prepare:** Hula hoops, blanket, ball, paper plates as stepping stones.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the fastest person should do everything: everyone contributes.
- Not talking during challenges: plan and communicate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hoop pass
*Student:* Pass a hula hoop around a hand-holding circle without letting go.

*How to teach:* Pass a hoop around a hand-holding circle without letting go.

- **Q:** How did you get the hoop over your body?
  **A:** Step through and duck under while holding hands.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Blanket ball
*Student:* Use a blanket with a partner to toss and catch a ball together.

*How to teach:* Toss and catch a ball with a blanket held by partners.

- **Q:** Why count together?
  **A:** So you lift at the same time.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lava crossing
*Student:* Cross the yard on 'stepping stones' (paper plates) as a team without touching the 'lava.'

*How to teach:* Cross the 'lava' on plates as a team.

- **Q:** What plan helped?
  **A:** Passing plates forward and sharing them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Team juggle
*Student:* Toss a ball in the same order around the family, adding a second ball.

*How to teach:* Team juggle in the same order; add a second ball.

- **Q:** Why keep the same order?
  **A:** So everyone knows who to throw to.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one way your team cooperated and one thing you would do differently.

*How to teach:* Explain how your team cooperated and one change.

- **Q:** What is cooperation?
  **A:** Working together toward a goal.

**Assessment:** Cooperation reflection.

**Answer key:** Reflection: names one specific way the team cooperated (e.g., 'we counted 1-2-3 before tossing') and one change for next time. Proficient = specific example + realistic improvement.

## Health 2: Poisons and household products

**Prepare:** Household product labels (with a parent), stickers; phone to save the Poison Control number.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bright bottles are juice: never taste anything unknown.
- Trying to make someone throw up: call Poison Control right away.

### Day 1 (Monday): Label hunt
*Student:* Find warning labels and the poison symbol on household products with a parent.

*How to teach:* Find warning labels and the poison symbol with a parent.

- **Q:** What do warning words like 'Caution' and 'Danger' mean?
  **A:** The product can hurt you; don't touch or taste.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Safe storage
*Student:* Help check that cleaners are stored up high or locked.

*How to teach:* Check that cleaners are stored up high or locked.

- **Q:** Why store cleaners up high or locked?
  **A:** So young children can't reach them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poison Control
*Student:* Learn the Poison Control number (1-800-222-1222) and add it to the phone.

*How to teach:* Learn Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222; save it in the phone.

- **Q:** What is the Poison Control number?
  **A:** 1-800-222-1222.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Saying no
*Student:* Practice saying no to tasting unknown substances.

*How to teach:* Practice saying no to tasting unknown substances.

- **Q:** What do you say if someone offers you something unknown to taste?
  **A:** 'No, thanks,' and tell an adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Identify 5 dangerous products and explain what to do if someone swallows one.

*How to teach:* Identify 5 dangerous products; explain what to do if swallowed.

- **Q:** Someone swallows a cleaner. What happens next?
  **A:** Tell an adult right away; call Poison Control (or 911 if they are not breathing or won't wake up).

**Assessment:** Identification check.

**Answer key:** Identification: names 5 dangerous products (e.g., bleach, drain cleaner, dishwasher detergent pods, laundry pods, bug spray, medicine, car fluids like antifreeze). What to do: tell an adult right away; adult calls Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (or 911 if the person is unconscious or can't breathe). Proficient = 5 products + correct steps.
