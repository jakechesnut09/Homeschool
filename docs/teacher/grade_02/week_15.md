# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 15

## ELA 2: Feelings, shades of meaning, adjectives and adverbs

**Prepare:** Get Chrysanthemum (Kevin Henkes) and preview it. Collect paint-chip strips from a hardware store for word ladders, and soft c/g word cards (city, cent, face, rice, gem, giant, page, cage).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up adjectives and adverbs. Ask: 'Does it tell about a thing (adjective) or about how someone does something (adverb)?' Many adverbs end in -ly.
- Using 'very' for every strong feeling. Use shades of meaning: glad → happy → thrilled.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chrysanthemum
*Student:* Read Chrysanthemum and use the pictures to show how her feelings change each day of school. Discuss why words can hurt or help.

*How to teach:* Read Chrysanthemum. Using the pictures, the child shows how her feelings change from home to school each day. Discuss why words can hurt or help.

- **Q:** Why does Chrysanthemum stop loving her name?
  **A:** The kids at school tease her because her name is long (13 letters) and she is named after a flower.
- **Q:** What makes her love her name again?
  **A:** Mrs. Twinkle, the music teacher, says her own name is long and she is named after a flower too, and she might name her baby Chrysanthemum.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shades of meaning
*Student:* Order feeling words on paint-chip strips (glad, happy, thrilled; sad, upset, miserable) and verbs (toss, throw, hurl). Use 3 in sentences about Chrysanthemum.

*How to teach:* Order feeling words on paint chips (glad, happy, thrilled; sad, upset, miserable) and verbs (toss, throw, hurl). Use 3 in sentences about Chrysanthemum.

- **Q:** Order from weakest to strongest: thrilled, glad, happy.
  **A:** glad, happy, thrilled.
- **Q:** Use a strong feeling word in a sentence about Chrysanthemum.
  **A:** Example: Chrysanthemum felt miserable when the kids laughed at her name.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adjectives and adverbs
*Student:* Grammar: adjectives describe nouns (a long name) and adverbs describe verbs (she walked slowly); sort 12 words and write 4 sentences. Phonics: soft c and g (city, gem, face, page).

*How to teach:* Teach adjectives (describe nouns) and adverbs (describe verbs). Sort 12 words and write 4 sentences. Read soft c and g words.

- **Q:** In 'She walked slowly,' is slowly an adjective or adverb?
  **A:** Adverb — it tells how she walked.
- **Q:** In 'a long name,' what kind of word is long?
  **A:** An adjective — it describes the noun name.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft my story
*Student:* Draft the fiction story from the plan, using at least 3 adjectives, 2 adverbs, and dialogue. Write a beginning that introduces the character and setting.

*How to teach:* Draft the fiction story from the plan with 3 adjectives, 2 adverbs, and dialogue. The beginning introduces character and setting.

- **Q:** Write a beginning sentence that introduces character and setting.
  **A:** Example: Bear lived on a shelf in Mia's sunny bedroom.
- **Q:** Add an adverb: 'The dog barked.'
  **A:** Example: The dog barked loudly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 15 review
*Student:* Spelling check of soft c/g words, adjective-or-adverb quiz, and reread the draft aloud to find one place to add a feeling word.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling check and adjective/adverb quiz. The child rereads the draft aloud and adds one feeling word.

- **Q:** Adjective or adverb: quickly, purple?
  **A:** quickly — adverb; purple — adjective.
- **Q:** Spell 'city' and 'page.'
  **A:** c-i-t-y; p-a-g-e.

**Assessment:** Adjective/adverb quiz, spelling check, story draft checked for beginning and descriptive words.

**Answer key:** Adjective/adverb quiz (suggested 12): adjectives — long, tiny, purple, happy, loud, soft; adverbs — slowly, quickly, loudly, gently, happily, quietly. Spelling (suggested): city, cent, face, rice, ice, gem, giant, page, cage, huge. Draft check: beginning introduces character and setting; at least 3 adjectives, 2 adverbs, and some dialogue.

## Math 2: One- and two-step word problems within 100

**Prepare:** Draw bar-model mats and write word problem cards (add-to, take-from, compare, two-step). Solve Friday's 6 problems yourself first.

**Watch for:**
- Grabbing the numbers and adding without reading. Have the child retell the story and identify whole vs. part before choosing an operation.
- In two-step problems, stopping after the first step. Have the child label 'Step 1' and 'Step 2' and reread the question.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bar models
*Student:* Draw bar models for add-to, take-from, and compare problems within 100; solve 6 problems.

*How to teach:* Model drawing a bar: whole on top, parts below (or two bars for compare). Solve 6 problems, asking each time 'What do we know — the whole or the parts?'

- **Q:** A class has 28 boys and 35 girls. How many children?
  **A:** 63 children; 28 + 35 = 63.
- **Q:** Ana has 32 stickers. Ben has 15 more than Ana. How many does Ben have?
  **A:** 47 stickers; 32 + 15 = 47.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Two-step problems
*Student:* Solve two-step problems ('There were 45 apples. 18 were sold, then 26 more were picked. How many now?') with labeled steps.

*How to teach:* Read 'There were 45 apples. 18 were sold, then 26 more were picked.' Step 1: 45 − 18 = 27. Step 2: 27 + 26 = 53. The child labels each step.

- **Q:** There were 45 apples. 18 were sold, then 26 more were picked. How many now?
  **A:** 53 apples (45 − 18 = 27, 27 + 26 = 53).
- **Q:** Jo had 50¢, spent 20¢, then earned 15¢. How much now?
  **A:** 45¢ (50 − 20 = 30, 30 + 15 = 45).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Unknown start
*Student:* Solve problems where the start is unknown using bar models and related equations.

*How to teach:* Problem: 'Some books were on a shelf. 17 more were added. Now there are 50.' Draw a bar with ? at the start; solve 50 − 17 = 33. Check: 33 + 17 = 50.

- **Q:** Some books were on a shelf. 17 more were added. Now there are 50. How many at first?
  **A:** 33 books; 50 − 17 = 33.
- **Q:** Some kids were in a pool. 12 got out. 25 are left. How many at first?
  **A:** 37 kids; 25 + 12 = 37.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reasonable?
*Student:* Estimate answers to 6 problems before solving and decide if the final answers make sense.

*How to teach:* Before solving, think 'about how much?' (e.g., 48 + 31 is about 50 + 30 = 80). Solve and check that the answer is close to the estimate.

- **Q:** Estimate 48 + 31, then solve.
  **A:** About 80; exact 79.
- **Q:** Someone said 62 − 19 = 81. Does that make sense?
  **A:** No — subtracting should give less than 62. The answer is 43.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 6 mixed one- and two-step word problems showing a model and equation for each.

*How to teach:* Give 6 mixed problems; each needs a bar model (or drawing) and an equation with a labeled answer.

- **Q:** 72 cards. Gave 30 to Sam and 12 to Eli. How many left?
  **A:** 30 cards; 72 − 30 − 12 = 30.
- **Q:** 60 balloons, 24 popped. How many left?
  **A:** 36 balloons.

**Assessment:** Word problem set with models and equations.

**Answer key:** Word problem set (suggested 6): 1) 45 apples, 18 sold, 26 more picked: 45 − 18 = 27, 27 + 26 = 53. 2) Ana has 32 stickers, Ben has 15 more: 47. 3) 60 balloons, 24 popped: 36. 4) Some books on a shelf, 17 added, now 50: 33. 5) 28 boys + 35 girls = 63 children. 6) 72 cards, gave 30 to Sam and 12 to Eli: 72 − 30 − 12 = 30. Rubric: 3 = correct model, equation, and labeled answer for 5–6 problems; 2 = 4 correct; 1 = 3 or fewer.

## Science 2: Plant needs conclusions

**Prepare:** Have all plant data, poster paper, and markers. Prepare a short quiz (6 items).

**Watch for:**
- Writing a conclusion without evidence. Always include 'My evidence is…' with data.
- Thinking a plant that wilts can never recover. Many recover if watered soon.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final measurements
*Student:* Take final measurements of all plants and complete the data tables.

*How to teach:* Take final measurements and complete the data tables.

- **Q:** Which plant grew tallest?
  **A:** Answer from data (usually a light-and-water plant).
- **Q:** Why take final measurements?
  **A:** To see the full results.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Conclusion
*Student:* Write a conclusion: 'Plants need ___ and ___ to grow. My evidence is…'

*How to teach:* Write a conclusion: 'Plants need ___ and ___ to grow. My evidence is…'

- **Q:** Complete the conclusion.
  **A:** Plants need light and water to grow. My evidence is the plants with both were tallest and greenest.
- **Q:** What is evidence?
  **A:** Data that supports the claim.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rescue
*Student:* Move the closet and unwatered plants to light and water; predict whether they recover.

*How to teach:* Move closet and unwatered plants to light and water; predict whether they recover.

- **Q:** Predict whether the dark plant recovers.
  **A:** It may turn green again once in light, if not too weak.
- **Q:** Why might some not recover?
  **A:** They went too long without what they needed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poster
*Student:* Make a poster of the investigation (question, prediction, test, results, conclusion).

*How to teach:* Make a poster of the investigation.

- **Q:** What parts go on the poster?
  **A:** Question, prediction, test, results, conclusion.
- **Q:** Why include a graph?
  **A:** To show the data clearly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Present the poster and complete a quiz on plant needs and fair tests.

*How to teach:* Present the poster and give the quiz.

- **Q:** What do plants need to grow?
  **A:** Light, water, air, and nutrients.
- **Q:** What happens to plants without water?
  **A:** They wilt.

**Assessment:** Investigation poster and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric (3 = proficient): question, prediction, test setup, results (graph), and conclusion: 'Plants need light and water to grow. My evidence is the plants with light and water were tallest and green; the ones in the dark turned pale and the ones without water wilted.' Quiz (suggested 6): 1) Plants need light, water, air, and nutrients 2) In a fair test you change one thing 3) A plant in the dark turns pale/yellow 4) A plant without water wilts 5) The stem carries water 6) Why use two plants per group? To make results more reliable.

## People Who Make a Difference: Government in other countries and unit check

**Prepare:** World map; photos of Canada's Parliament and Mexico's Congress; the Preamble to the U.S. Constitution (archives.gov).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every country has a president: Canada's government is led by a prime minister.
- Thinking 'general welfare' means money help only: it means the well-being of all the people.

### Day 1 (Monday): Canada
*Student:* Learn how Canada's Parliament makes laws and compare to Congress.

*How to teach:* Learn Canada's Parliament makes laws; the prime minister leads; compare to Congress.

- **Q:** What is Canada's lawmaking group called?
  **A:** Parliament.
- **Q:** Who leads Canada's government?
  **A:** The prime minister.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mexico
*Student:* Learn how Mexico's Congress and president work and compare to the U.S.

*How to teach:* Learn Mexico's Congress and president; compare to the U.S.

- **Q:** Who leads Mexico?
  **A:** A president.
- **Q:** How long is a Mexican president's term?
  **A:** Six years (one term only).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a chart comparing lawmakers and leaders in 3 countries.

*How to teach:* Make a chart comparing lawmakers and leaders in 3 countries.

- **Q:** How are all three alike?
  **A:** All have groups of elected lawmakers and leaders chosen by voters (democracies).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Primary source
*Student:* Look at the Preamble to the U.S. Constitution (National Archives) and discuss 'promote the general welfare.'

*How to teach:* Read the Preamble; discuss 'promote the general welfare.'

- **Q:** How does the Preamble begin?
  **A:** 'We the People of the United States…'
- **Q:** What does 'promote the general welfare' mean?
  **A:** Help the well-being of all the people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Quiz on making, carrying out, and judging laws.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 quiz.

- **Q:** What is a democracy?
  **A:** A government where the people choose leaders by voting.

**Assessment:** Government comparison chart and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Comparison chart: U.S. - lawmakers: Congress (Senate + House); leader: president. Canada - lawmakers: Parliament (House of Commons + Senate); leader: prime minister. Mexico - lawmakers: Congress (Chamber of Deputies + Senate); leader: president. Unit 3 quiz: lawmakers make laws, president/governor/mayor and police carry them out, judges and courts decide. Proficient = chart correct + 80% quiz.

## Art & Music 2: Folk songs and singing games

**Prepare:** Recordings and lyrics: 'De Colores' (Mexico), 'Funga Alafia' (West Africa), 'Sakura Sakura' (Japan), 'Bluebird, Bluebird' (American singing game).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking folk songs have one famous composer: they are passed down through communities.
- Thinking verse and refrain are the same: the refrain repeats the same words each time.

### Day 1 (Monday): De Colores
*Student:* Learn the first verse of the Mexican folk song 'De Colores' and discuss what it celebrates.

*How to teach:* Learn verse 1 of 'De Colores'; it celebrates the colors of nature in spring (fields, birds, rainbow).

- **Q:** Where is 'De Colores' from?
  **A:** Mexico (a Spanish-language folk song).
- **Q:** What does it celebrate?
  **A:** The many colors of nature in springtime.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Funga Alafia
*Student:* Learn the West African welcome song 'Funga Alafia' with movements and sing it as a greeting.

*How to teach:* Learn 'Funga Alafia' with welcoming movements; sing it as a greeting.

- **Q:** What is 'Funga Alafia'?
  **A:** A West African welcome song; 'alafia' means peace or well-being.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sakura
*Student:* Listen to 'Sakura Sakura' from Japan, describe its mood, and draw cherry blossoms while listening.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Sakura Sakura'; draw cherry blossoms.

- **Q:** What does 'sakura' mean?
  **A:** Cherry blossoms.
- **Q:** Describe its mood.
  **A:** Calm, gentle, peaceful.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Singing game
*Student:* Play the American singing game 'Bluebird, Bluebird' with family, keeping the beat while moving.

*How to teach:* Play 'Bluebird, Bluebird': players make windows with raised arms while the bluebird weaves through.

- **Q:** What does the bluebird do?
  **A:** Weaves in and out of the 'windows' (raised arms), then picks a new bluebird.

### Day 5 (Friday): Rehearse and check
*Student:* Choose a favorite folk song, practice the hard parts, and sing it for family, telling where it comes from and why you like it.

*How to teach:* Choose a favorite; practice hard parts; sing it for family with an introduction.

- **Q:** What is a refrain?
  **A:** A part that repeats with the same words.

**Assessment:** Folk song performance with spoken introduction.

**Answer key:** Performance: sings a folk song with mostly correct words and pitch, steady beat; spoken introduction tells where it comes from and why they like it (e.g., 'Sakura is from Japan and is about cherry blossoms; I like its calm melody'). Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Muscle strength and endurance

**Prepare:** Mat, playground bars, fitness log.

**Watch for:**
- Sagging hips in push-ups: keep a straight line.
- Pulling on the neck in curl-ups: hands slide along the mat; neck relaxed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Push-up practice
*Student:* Practice knee push-ups and wall push-ups with a straight back; count good-form reps.

*How to teach:* Practice knee or wall push-ups with a straight back; count good-form reps.

- **Q:** Which muscles do push-ups use?
  **A:** Chest, arms and shoulders.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Curl-ups
*Student:* Do curl-ups sliding fingertips along the mat; count reps in 1 minute.

*How to teach:* Curl-ups sliding fingertips along the mat; count in 1 minute.

- **Q:** Which muscles do curl-ups use?
  **A:** Stomach (abdominal) muscles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Playground strength
*Student:* At a park, try hanging from bars, climbing a ladder and doing monkey-bar swings.

*How to teach:* At a park: hang, climb a ladder, try monkey-bar swings.

- **Q:** Which body parts work when you hang?
  **A:** Hands, arms, shoulders and back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Animal strength circuit
*Student:* Do 30 seconds each of bear crawl, crab walk, plank and frog jumps.

*How to teach:* 30 seconds each: bear crawl, crab walk, plank, frog jumps.

- **Q:** What does a plank strengthen?
  **A:** Your core (stomach and back).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record push-ups, curl-ups and hang time in your fitness log and name the muscles each works.

*How to teach:* Record scores and name muscles.

- **Q:** What is muscle strength?
  **A:** How much force your muscles can make.

**Assessment:** Baseline strength scores in fitness log.

**Answer key:** Baseline scores recorded: push-ups (good form), curl-ups in 1 minute, hang seconds. Muscles: push-ups = chest, arms (triceps), shoulders; curl-ups = abdominals (stomach); hang = hands/forearms, arms, shoulders, back. Proficient = all recorded + correct muscles.

## Health 2: Farm to table

**Prepare:** Local produce; 'From Seed to Plant' by Gail Gibbons (library); a cup, soil and lettuce or herb seeds.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking food comes from the store: it comes from farms and travels to stores.
- Thinking all foods grow all year: many are seasonal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Where food comes from
*Student:* Trace a food (e.g., strawberries, milk) from a California farm to the table; draw the steps.

*How to teach:* Trace a food from a California farm to the table; draw the steps.

- **Q:** What happens after strawberries are picked?
  **A:** They are packed, cooled, and trucked to stores.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Farmers market
*Student:* Visit a farmers market or grocery produce section and list 5 local foods.

*How to teach:* Visit a farmers market or produce section; list 5 local foods.

- **Q:** What is a producer?
  **A:** Someone who grows or makes food or goods, like a farmer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Seasonal foods
*Student:* Learn which fruits and vegetables grow in each season in California.

*How to teach:* Learn California seasons: spring strawberries, asparagus; summer peaches, tomatoes, corn; fall apples, grapes, pumpkins; winter oranges and citrus, broccoli.

- **Q:** Name a fruit that grows in California in winter.
  **A:** Oranges (citrus).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grow a snack
*Student:* Plant a quick herb or sprout lettuce seeds in a cup.

*How to teach:* Plant herbs or lettuce seeds in a cup.

- **Q:** What does a seed need to grow?
  **A:** Water, light, air and soil (nutrients).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain the farm-to-table path for one food.

*How to teach:* Explain the farm-to-table path for one food.

- **Q:** Who are the helpers along the way?
  **A:** Farmers, workers, truck drivers, store workers.

**Assessment:** Farm-to-table drawing.

**Answer key:** Farm-to-table drawing, e.g., strawberries: planted and grown on a California farm, picked by farmworkers, packed and cooled, trucked to a store or market, bought, washed, eaten. Proficient = 5+ correct steps in order.
