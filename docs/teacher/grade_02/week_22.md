# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 22

## ELA 2: Cinderella stories from Africa and North America

**Prepare:** Get Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters (John Steptoe), The Rough-Face Girl (Rafe Martin), and Adelita (Tomie dePaola); read each first. Make a comparison chart (title, place, main character, helper, test, ending) for 5 versions. Suffix cards: -ful, -less, -ly, -er.

**Watch for:**
- Opinions with no reasons ('I like it because I like it'). Reasons must come from the book: 'because Nyasha is kind to everyone she meets.'
- Talking over others in discussion. Practice: wait, listen, then add on ('I agree, and…').

### Day 1 (Monday): Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters
*Student:* Read Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters (Zimbabwe) and add it to a class comparison chart: main character, helper, test, ending. Phonics: suffixes -ful and -less (helpful, careless).

*How to teach:* Read Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters and add it to the chart. Teach -ful (full of) and -less (without).

- **Q:** How are Nyasha and Manyara different?
  **A:** Nyasha is kind and generous; Manyara is selfish and rude.
- **Q:** How does the king test the sisters?
  **A:** He appears in disguise (a hungry boy, an old woman, and the snake Nyoka) to see who is kind; Nyasha is kind, so she becomes queen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Rough-Face Girl
*Student:* Read The Rough-Face Girl (Algonquin) and add it to the chart; discuss what 'seeing' the Invisible Being means. Phonics: suffix -ly (kindly, quickly).

*How to teach:* Read The Rough-Face Girl and add it to the chart. Discuss what 'seeing' the Invisible Being means. Teach -ly.

- **Q:** Why is the girl called Rough-Face?
  **A:** Her face and hands were scarred by sparks from the fire her sisters made her tend.
- **Q:** What does 'seeing' the Invisible Being mean?
  **A:** Seeing his greatness in nature with a true heart — she sees his bow is the rainbow and his sled runner is the Milky Way.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adelita
*Student:* Read Adelita (Mexico) and add it to the chart. Phonics: suffix -er (teacher, farmer, painter) and its meaning 'one who.'

*How to teach:* Read Adelita and add it to the chart. Teach -er meaning 'one who.'

- **Q:** Who helps Adelita?
  **A:** Esperanza, the kind woman who cared for her since she was a baby.
- **Q:** What does 'farmer' mean?
  **A:** One who farms.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Which is best
*Student:* Discuss which Cinderella story is your favorite, giving reasons from the text, while practicing taking turns and building on each other's ideas. Write the opinion sentence and 2 reasons.

*How to teach:* Discuss your favorite Cinderella story, giving reasons from the text and taking turns. Write the opinion sentence and 2 reasons.

- **Q:** Write an opinion sentence with a reason.
  **A:** Example: My favorite is Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters because Nyasha's kindness wins in the end.
- **Q:** How do you build on someone's idea?
  **A:** Say 'I agree, and…' or 'I see it differently because…'

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 22 review
*Student:* Complete the comparison chart for 5 versions and write which two are most alike. Spelling check of 12 suffix words.

*How to teach:* Complete the chart for 5 versions and write which two are most alike. Give the suffix spelling check.

- **Q:** Which two versions both use a lost slipper?
  **A:** Cinderella and Yeh-Shen.
- **Q:** Spell 'helpful' and 'quickly.'
  **A:** h-e-l-p-f-u-l; q-u-i-c-k-l-y.

**Assessment:** Completed comparison chart, opinion sentence with reasons, suffix spelling check.

**Answer key:** Comparison chart (model): Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters — Zimbabwe; Nyasha; helper/test: the king tests the sisters in disguise (hungry boy, old woman, snake Nyoka); kind Nyasha becomes queen. The Rough-Face Girl — Algonquin; the Rough-Face Girl; test: tell what the Invisible Being's bow and sled runner are made of; she sees him in nature (the rainbow and the Milky Way) and marries him. Adelita — Mexico; Adelita; helper: Esperanza, her loving old nanny; event: a fiesta; she marries Javier. Plus Cinderella (France) and Yeh-Shen (China) from last week. Opinion: proficient states a favorite and 2 reasons from the text. Spelling (suggested 12): helpful, careful, hopeful, careless, helpless, kindly, quickly, slowly, teacher, farmer, painter, singer.

## Math 2: Problem solving within 1,000

**Prepare:** Word problem cards (within 1,000), base-ten blocks, and two dice. Prepare the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Rounding incorrectly for estimates (rounding 438 to 500). Find the two nearby hundreds on a number line: 438 is closer to 400.
- In Race to 1,000, overshooting. Plan ahead: 'How much more do I need?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Story problems
*Student:* Solve 5 story problems within 1,000 (e.g., 'A library had 425 books and got 138 more…') with drawings.

*How to teach:* Read each story; the child draws quick pictures or a bar model and writes an equation. Discuss whether to add or subtract.

- **Q:** A library had 425 books and got 138 more. How many now?
  **A:** 563 books.
- **Q:** A school had 600 pencils and used 245. How many are left?
  **A:** 355 pencils.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Estimate
*Student:* Estimate sums and differences using nearest hundred before solving 6 problems.

*How to teach:* Show rounding to the nearest hundred on a number line (438 is closer to 400 than 500). Estimate first, then solve and compare.

- **Q:** Estimate 438 + 271 to the nearest hundred, then solve.
  **A:** 400 + 300 = 700; exact 709.
- **Q:** Estimate 812 − 389, then solve.
  **A:** 800 − 400 = 400; exact 423.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Explain it
*Student:* Write an explanation for how you solved 563 − 287, using place-value words.

*How to teach:* The child writes a step-by-step explanation of 563 − 287 using the words hundreds, tens, ones, and trade.

- **Q:** 563 − 287 = ?
  **A:** 276.
- **Q:** In 563 − 287, why do we break a ten first?
  **A:** 3 ones is not enough to take away 7, so trade 1 ten for 10 ones (13 ones).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game: Race to 1,000
*Student:* Roll dice to build 2-digit and 3-digit numbers and add until reaching 1,000 exactly; record equations.

*How to teach:* Race to 1,000: roll dice to build numbers and add them to a running total until reaching exactly 1,000. Record each equation.

- **Q:** Your total is 870. How much more to reach 1,000?
  **A:** 130.
- **Q:** Your total is 455 and you add 236. New total?
  **A:** 691.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Add and subtract within 1,000 (14 items), mental 10/100 items, and one written explanation.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test, including mental items and one written explanation.

- **Q:** 382 + 418 = ?
  **A:** 800.
- **Q:** 654 − 199 = ?
  **A:** 455.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test (suggested 14): 1) 425 + 138 = 563 2) 563 − 287 = 276 3) 609 + 245 = 854 4) 800 − 372 = 428 5) 136 + 247 = 383 6) 715 − 408 = 307 7) 382 + 418 = 800 8) 654 − 199 = 455 9) 250 + 375 = 625 10) 900 − 125 = 775 11) 147 + 263 = 410 12) 432 − 156 = 276 13) 508 + 92 = 600 14) 1,000 − 600 = 400. Mental: 10 more than 795 = 805; 100 less than 306 = 206. Written explanation rubric for 563 − 287: 3 = uses hundreds/tens/ones words and shows trades correctly (answer 276); 2 = correct answer with partial explanation; 1 = incorrect. Proficient: 12/14 plus a level-3 explanation.

## Science 2: Comparing water habitats: kelp forest, pond, tide pool

**Prepare:** Find the Monterey Bay Aquarium kelp forest live cam or a kelp video, a clear jar, magnifier, and pond-life photos. Safety: an adult goes along to collect pond water; don't drink it, and wash hands after.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kelp is a plant with roots. Kelp is a large algae with a holdfast that grips rocks (not roots).
- Thinking tide pools are always underwater. They fill and empty with the tides.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kelp forest
*Student:* Watch the Monterey Bay Aquarium kelp forest live cam or a kelp video for 10 minutes and list every kind of fish, plant, and animal you see.

*How to teach:* Watch the kelp forest cam or video for 10 minutes and list every kind of fish, plant, and animal.

- **Q:** What animal wraps itself in kelp to rest?
  **A:** The sea otter.
- **Q:** What does kelp provide for animals?
  **A:** Food and shelter.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pond sample
*Student:* Scoop pond or puddle water into a clear jar with an adult, let it settle, and look for tiny swimming organisms with a magnifier; draw 3 you find (or use pond-life photos).

*How to teach:* Scoop pond or puddle water into a clear jar with an adult, let it settle, and look for tiny swimmers; draw 3.

- **Q:** What might you see in pond water?
  **A:** Tiny insects, worms, snails, or algae.
- **Q:** Why let the water settle?
  **A:** So the mud sinks and you can see the living things.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tide pool
*Student:* Read about California tide pools and sketch a tide pool with sea stars, anemones, crabs, and snails, labeling where each lives (rock, water, crack).

*How to teach:* Read about California tide pools and sketch one with sea stars, anemones, crabs, and snails labeled.

- **Q:** Where do anemones attach?
  **A:** To rocks.
- **Q:** What happens to tide pools at low tide?
  **A:** The ocean water goes out, leaving pools of water between rocks.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Land vs. water
*Student:* Add the three water habitats to last week's chart and compare totals with the land habitats; note which habitat had the most kinds overall.

*How to teach:* Add the water habitats to the chart and compare totals with land habitats.

- **Q:** Which water habitat had the most kinds?
  **A:** Usually the kelp forest.
- **Q:** Which habitat had the most kinds overall?
  **A:** Answer from the chart.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Tell a family member which habitat surprised you most and give two facts as evidence; answer 2 questions they ask.

*How to teach:* The child tells a family member which habitat surprised them most, with two facts, and answers 2 questions.

- **Q:** Give a fact about kelp.
  **A:** Kelp can grow very tall and fast, making an underwater forest.
- **Q:** Give a fact about tide pools.
  **A:** Animals there must survive when the water goes out at low tide.

**Assessment:** Updated habitat chart and oral explanation with evidence.

**Answer key:** Updated chart: kelp forest (kelp, sea otter, rockfish, sea urchin, sea star, garibaldi), pond (algae, water insects, tadpoles, snails, duckweed), tide pool (sea star, anemone, crab, snail, mussel, barnacle). Oral explanation (3 = proficient): names the most surprising habitat and gives two accurate facts, answers 2 questions. Expected comparison: the kelp forest often shows the most kinds of living things in water.

## People Who Make a Difference: Weather, land, and water resources

**Prepare:** California physical map; Calisphere photos of the California Aqueduct; bean seeds, 2 cups, soil; a short article on a California drought.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking weather only affects farmers: it changes how much food is in stores and its price.
- Thinking California has plenty of rain everywhere: much farm water comes from Sierra snowmelt carried by canals and aqueducts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Weather and crops
*Student:* Read short scenarios (a frost on orange trees, heavy rain at strawberry harvest, a heat wave) and tell how each changes how much food farmers can sell.

*How to teach:* Read frost, rain and heat-wave scenarios; tell how each changes food sales.

- **Q:** What could a frost do to orange trees?
  **A:** Freeze and damage the oranges, so farmers have fewer to sell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water for farms
*Student:* Look at photos of the California Aqueduct and Central Valley irrigation canals and trace on a map how Sierra snowmelt reaches farms.

*How to teach:* Look at the California Aqueduct photos; trace how Sierra snowmelt reaches farms.

- **Q:** Where does much of California's farm water come from?
  **A:** Melting snow in the Sierra Nevada, carried by rivers, aqueducts and canals.
- **Q:** What is an aqueduct?
  **A:** A large channel or pipe that carries water a long way.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water test
*Student:* Plant beans in two cups, giving one plenty of water and one very little, and record growth twice this week to see why farmers need water.

*How to teach:* Plant beans in two cups (plenty vs. little water); record twice.

- **Q:** Which bean will grow better?
  **A:** The one with plenty of water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Drought choices
*Student:* Read about a California drought and discuss why some farmers left fields empty or planted crops that need less water.

*How to teach:* Read about a California drought; discuss farmers' choices.

- **Q:** What is a drought?
  **A:** A long time with much less rain or snow than normal.
- **Q:** Why did some farmers leave fields empty?
  **A:** There wasn't enough water to grow crops there.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences explaining how weather, land, and water affect the food we can buy.

*How to teach:* Write 3 sentences on weather, land, water and food.

- **Q:** What is a resource?
  **A:** Something people use, like water, land or trees.

**Assessment:** Written explanation linking weather, land, and water to food production.

**Answer key:** Three sentences (accurate examples): 1) Bad weather like frost, heavy rain or heat waves can ruin crops, so there is less food to sell and prices go up. 2) Farmers need good land (flat, rich soil) like the Central Valley to grow lots of food. 3) Water from Sierra snowmelt travels through rivers, aqueducts and canals to irrigate farms; in a drought, farmers grow less. Proficient = all three factors linked to food.

## Art & Music 2: Peter and the Wolf

**Prepare:** Recording of Prokofiev's 'Peter and the Wolf' with narrator; character cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the narrator sings: the narrator speaks the story.
- Mixing up oboe (duck) and clarinet (cat): duck = oboe's nasal quacky sound; cat = clarinet's smooth sneaky low notes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Meet the characters
*Student:* Listen to the theme for each character in 'Peter and the Wolf' (bird = flute, duck = oboe, cat = clarinet, wolf = French horns, Peter = strings).

*How to teach:* Listen to each character's theme.

- **Q:** Which instrument is the bird?
  **A:** Flute.
- **Q:** Which is Peter?
  **A:** Strings.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Listen to the story
*Student:* Listen to the full piece and hold up a character card whenever that theme plays.

*How to teach:* Listen to the full piece; hold up the card when each theme plays.

- **Q:** Which instrument is Grandfather?
  **A:** Bassoon.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Why that instrument
*Student:* Explain why Prokofiev chose each instrument for its character (high, quick flute for the bird).

*How to teach:* Explain why Prokofiev chose each instrument.

- **Q:** Why a flute for the bird?
  **A:** It is high, light and quick like a bird.
- **Q:** Why French horns for the wolf?
  **A:** They sound low, dark and scary.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draw a scene
*Student:* Draw your favorite scene and label the instrument playing.

*How to teach:* Draw your favorite scene; label the instrument.

- **Q:** What happens to the duck?
  **A:** The wolf swallows it (it is still quacking inside at the end).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Match 7 themes to characters and instruments.

*How to teach:* Match 7 themes to characters and instruments.

- **Q:** What is a theme?
  **A:** A melody that represents a character.

**Assessment:** Theme-matching check and labeled drawing.

**Answer key:** 7 themes: Bird - flute; Duck - oboe; Cat - clarinet; Grandfather - bassoon; Wolf - three French horns; Peter - strings; Hunters - woodwinds with timpani and bass drum (gunshots). Labeled drawing names the correct instrument. Proficient = 6-7 correct.

## PE 2: Foot dribbling

**Prepare:** Soccer or playground ball, 6 cones.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking the ball far ahead: use light, soft touches.
- Using only one foot: use inside and outside of both feet.

### Day 1 (Monday): Toe taps
*Student:* Do toe taps on top of the ball and inside-foot passes between feet.

*How to teach:* Toe taps on top of the ball; inside-foot passes between feet.

- **Q:** Which part of the foot taps the ball in toe taps?
  **A:** The bottom of the toes (sole front).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Walking dribble
*Student:* Dribble the ball with soft touches while walking, keeping it close.

*How to teach:* Dribble walking with soft touches.

- **Q:** How close should the ball stay?
  **A:** Within a step of your feet.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cone weave
*Student:* Dribble through a zigzag of 6 cones using inside and outside of foot.

*How to teach:* Weave 6 cones using inside and outside of foot.

- **Q:** Which part of the foot pushes the ball away from your body to the outside?
  **A:** The outside of the foot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Red light/green light
*Student:* Dribble on green and stop the ball with the sole of your foot on red.

*How to teach:* Red light/green light: dribble on green, stop the ball with the sole on red.

- **Q:** How do you stop the ball?
  **A:** Put the sole of your foot on top of it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Dribble through the cone course without losing control and explain strong vs. light touches.

*How to teach:* Run the course; explain strong vs. light touches.

- **Q:** When do you use a light touch?
  **A:** When dribbling to keep control.

**Assessment:** Cone-weave dribble observation.

**Answer key:** Cone-weave observation: keeps ball close with soft touches, uses inside and outside of the foot, completes all 6 cones without the ball rolling away. Explanation: light touches keep control when dribbling; strong kicks send the ball far for passing or shooting. Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Senses and protecting them

**Prepare:** Mystery bags (cotton ball, sandpaper, spoon), blindfold, small foods to taste and smell (check allergies), eye chart printout.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the eyes or ears 'see' or 'hear' alone: the brain interprets their messages.
- Putting small objects in ears to clean them: never put anything in ears.

### Day 1 (Monday): Senses stations
*Student:* Do touch, smell and taste mystery stations with a blindfold.

*How to teach:* Blindfolded touch, smell and taste stations.

- **Q:** What are the five senses?
  **A:** Sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brain messages
*Student:* Learn that sense organs send messages to the brain.

*How to teach:* Teach that sense organs send messages through nerves to the brain.

- **Q:** What carries messages to the brain?
  **A:** Nerves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Eyes & ears check
*Student:* Do a simple eye chart and whisper test; learn about vision and hearing checkups.

*How to teach:* Do a simple eye chart and whisper test; explain checkups.

- **Q:** Why get vision and hearing checks?
  **A:** To catch problems early so you can see and hear well.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Protect senses
*Student:* Make a list of ways to protect each sense organ.

*How to teach:* List ways to protect each sense organ.

- **Q:** How do you protect your skin?
  **A:** Sunscreen, protective clothing, gloves.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match each sense to its organ and one protection tip.

*How to teach:* Match each sense to its organ and a protection tip.

- **Q:** Which organ is for smell?
  **A:** Nose.

**Assessment:** Senses matching.

**Answer key:** Matching: eyes - sight - wear sunglasses/goggles; ears - hearing - keep volume low; nose - smell - avoid sniffing chemicals; tongue - taste - don't taste unknown substances / avoid very hot foods; skin - touch - sunscreen, gloves. Proficient = 5/5 senses with tips.
