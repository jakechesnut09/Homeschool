# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 32

## ELA 2: Friendly letters, formal and informal language

**Prepare:** Gather stationery, an envelope, and a stamp; have a relative's mailing address ready. Write a sample friendly letter to label and 5 informal sentences to rewrite. Prepare two short science texts with comparison questions for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting commas in the greeting and closing. 'Dear Grandma,' and 'Love,' always end with a comma.
- Using slang in formal speech. Practice 'Hey, what's up?' → 'Hello, how are you?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Formal vs. informal
*Student:* Compare how you talk to a friend vs. how you would talk to a museum guide; rewrite 5 informal sentences in formal language.

*How to teach:* Compare talking to a friend vs. a museum guide. Rewrite 5 informal sentences formally.

- **Q:** Make formal: 'Hey, can I see that rock?'
  **A:** Excuse me, may I please see that rock?
- **Q:** When do we use formal language?
  **A:** With adults we don't know well, in letters to businesses, and in presentations.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Letter parts
*Student:* Learn the 5 parts of a friendly letter (date, greeting, body, closing, signature) with commas in the greeting and closing; label a sample letter.

*How to teach:* Learn the 5 friendly-letter parts and label a sample letter.

- **Q:** Name the 5 parts of a friendly letter.
  **A:** Date, greeting, body, closing, signature.
- **Q:** Punctuate the greeting: Dear Grandpa
  **A:** Dear Grandpa,

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Write a letter
*Student:* Write a letter to a relative explaining 3 things you learned about volcanoes, mountains, or earthquakes using science words.

*How to teach:* Write a letter to a relative about 3 things learned about volcanoes, mountains, or earthquakes, using science words.

- **Q:** Write a body sentence with a science word.
  **A:** Example: I learned that magma is melted rock under the ground.
- **Q:** Where does the closing go?
  **A:** After the body, before the signature (e.g., 'Love,').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mail it
*Student:* Edit the letter, address an envelope (capitalize city and state names), and mail it. Read a nonfiction passage independently and log it.

*How to teach:* Edit the letter, address the envelope with capitals for city and state, and mail it. Read a nonfiction passage and log it.

- **Q:** Capitalize: sacramento, california
  **A:** Sacramento, California.
- **Q:** Where does the return address go?
  **A:** The top left corner of the envelope.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Unit test: cold read of two short science texts with questions comparing them and identifying the author's reasons; letter-format and comma check.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test: compare two science texts, find the author's reasons, and check letter format.

- **Q:** Fix: 'your friend' as a closing.
  **A:** Your friend,
- **Q:** What does 'reasons support a point' mean?
  **A:** The author gives facts that show the point is true.

**Assessment:** Friendly letter (format rubric) and unit test on comparing texts.

**Answer key:** Letter format rubric (3 = proficient): date, greeting with comma, body with 3 science facts using science words, closing with comma, signature; envelope with capitalized city and state. Unit test: questions comparing the two texts answered with details from both; the author's point and reasons identified; letter-format items correct (comma after greeting and closing).

## Math 2: Three-dimensional shapes and partitioning rectangles

**Prepare:** Dice, sugar cubes, boxes, grid paper, square tiles.

**Watch for:**
- Counting only the faces you can see on a cube (3). Turn it to count all 6.
- Drawing uneven rows when partitioning. Use grid paper to make same-size squares.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cubes
*Student:* Examine dice, sugar cubes, and boxes; count faces, edges, and vertices of a cube.

*How to teach:* Examine a die. Count faces (6), edges (12), and vertices (8), marking each with a sticker or pencil dot as you count.

- **Q:** How many faces does a cube have?
  **A:** 6.
- **Q:** How many edges and vertices?
  **A:** 12 edges, 8 vertices.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shape hunt
*Student:* Hunt for 3-D shapes at home and record which have square faces.

*How to teach:* Hunt for 3-D shapes at home (cubes, boxes, cans, balls) and record which have square faces.

- **Q:** Does a soup can have square faces?
  **A:** No — its flat faces are circles.
- **Q:** Does every box have square faces?
  **A:** No — many boxes have rectangle faces; only a cube-shaped box has all square faces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partition rectangles
*Student:* Cover rectangles with square tiles and draw rows and columns; count the total.

*How to teach:* Cover a rectangle with square tiles, no gaps or overlaps; draw lines for rows and columns and count.

- **Q:** A rectangle is covered by 2 rows of 5 tiles. How many tiles?
  **A:** 10.
- **Q:** Why no gaps or overlaps?
  **A:** So every part is covered exactly once and the count is correct.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draw grids
*Student:* Partition rectangles into 2 rows of 4, 3 rows of 3, etc. on paper and connect to repeated addition.

*How to teach:* Partition rectangles into 2 rows of 4, 3 rows of 3, etc. on paper and connect to repeated addition.

- **Q:** 3 rows of 3 squares: total and addition?
  **A:** 9; 3 + 3 + 3.
- **Q:** 2 rows of 4?
  **A:** 8; 4 + 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: cube attributes and 6 partition-and-count problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on cube attributes and 6 partitioning problems.

- **Q:** 4 rows of 4 squares: total?
  **A:** 16.
- **Q:** How many vertices on a cube?
  **A:** 8.

**Assessment:** Cube and partitioning quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): cube — 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices, every face a square. Partition-and-count: 1) 2 rows of 4 → 8 squares 2) 3 rows of 3 → 9 3) 2 rows of 5 → 10 4) 4 rows of 2 → 8 5) 3 rows of 5 → 15 6) 4 rows of 4 → 16. Proficient: 8/9.

## Science 2: Landforms and bodies of water

**Prepare:** Gather photos of mountains, hills, valleys, plains, islands, and peninsulas; a California map; clay or play dough; a baking pan; water; and toothpick flags.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing islands and peninsulas. An island has water all around; a peninsula has water on three sides and connects to land.
- Thinking lakes and ponds are salty. Most lakes and ponds are fresh water; the ocean is salt water.

### Day 1 (Monday): Landforms
*Student:* Sort photos of mountains, hills, valleys, plains, islands, and peninsulas and write a one-line definition for each.

*How to teach:* Sort photos of landforms and write a one-line definition for each.

- **Q:** What is a valley?
  **A:** Low land between hills or mountains.
- **Q:** What is a peninsula?
  **A:** Land with water on three sides.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bodies of water
*Student:* Learn ocean, lake, river, pond, stream, and bay, and find a California example of each on a map (Pacific Ocean, Lake Tahoe, Sacramento River, San Francisco Bay).

*How to teach:* Learn ocean, lake, river, pond, stream, bay and find California examples on a map.

- **Q:** Name California's ocean.
  **A:** The Pacific Ocean.
- **Q:** Name a California lake and river.
  **A:** Lake Tahoe; the Sacramento River.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build a model
*Student:* Build a clay landscape in a pan with a mountain, valley, island, and peninsula, then add water to form a lake, river, and ocean.

*How to teach:* Build a clay landscape with a mountain, valley, island, and peninsula; add water.

- **Q:** Where does the river flow?
  **A:** Downhill from the mountains to the ocean or lake.
- **Q:** How do you make an island?
  **A:** Put land with water all around it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Label
*Student:* Label each feature with toothpick flags and photograph the model from above and from the side.

*How to teach:* Label each feature with toothpick flags and photograph from above and from the side.

- **Q:** Which view shows how tall the mountain is?
  **A:** The side view.
- **Q:** Which view shows the shape of the lake?
  **A:** The view from above.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain the model to a family member, pointing to each landform and body of water and telling how they differ.

*How to teach:* Explain the model to a family member, pointing to each feature.

- **Q:** How is a lake different from a river?
  **A:** A lake is still water surrounded by land; a river flows.
- **Q:** How is an island different from a peninsula?
  **A:** An island is surrounded by water; a peninsula is attached to land.

**Assessment:** Labeled landform model and oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Model labels: mountain, valley, island, peninsula, lake, river, ocean all correctly placed. Oral explanation (3 = proficient): points to each feature and tells how they differ (e.g., a mountain is much taller than a hill; a river flows, a lake is still; an island is surrounded by water; a peninsula is connected to land on one side).

## People Who Make a Difference: Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta

**Prepare:** 'Harvesting Hope: The Story of Cesar Chavez' by Kathleen Krull; Calisphere photos of the 1966 Delano-to-Sacramento march; California map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Chavez worked alone: he co-founded the farmworkers' union with Dolores Huerta and thousands joined.
- Thinking a union is a company: a union is a group of workers joining together to ask for fair pay and safe conditions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cesar Chavez
*Student:* Read Harvesting Hope and discuss what farmworkers' lives were like and why Chavez organized them.

*How to teach:* Read 'Harvesting Hope'; discuss farmworkers' lives and why Chavez organized.

- **Q:** What was life like for farmworkers?
  **A:** Long, hard hours in the fields for very low pay, poor housing, and unsafe conditions.
- **Q:** Why did Chavez organize workers?
  **A:** So together they could ask for fair pay and better treatment.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The march
*Student:* Trace the 1966 march from Delano to Sacramento on a California map (about 300 miles) and look at Calisphere photos of the marchers.

*How to teach:* Trace the 1966 march from Delano to Sacramento; view photos.

- **Q:** Why march to Sacramento?
  **A:** It's the state capital, where they could ask leaders to listen.
- **Q:** About how far was the march?
  **A:** About 300 miles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dolores Huerta
*Student:* Read about Dolores Huerta and the motto 'Sí, se puede' (Yes, we can); discuss her role as a leader and teacher.

*How to teach:* Read about Dolores Huerta and 'Sí, se puede.'

- **Q:** What does 'Sí, se puede' mean?
  **A:** Yes, we can (yes, it can be done).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Connect to food
*Student:* Connect Chavez and Huerta to the farm-to-table unit: who picks the grapes and lettuce we buy?

*How to teach:* Connect to farm-to-table: who picks grapes and lettuce?

- **Q:** Who picks much of the fruit and vegetables we buy?
  **A:** Farmworkers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a short paragraph on how Chavez and Huerta made a difference for workers.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph on how Chavez and Huerta made a difference.

- **Q:** What is nonviolence?
  **A:** Working for change without hurting anyone.

**Assessment:** Paragraph and map of the march.

**Answer key:** Paragraph: Chavez and Huerta organized farmworkers into a union (National Farm Workers Association, later the United Farm Workers) to win fair pay, safer conditions and respect, using nonviolent actions like strikes, the grape boycott and the 1966 march from Delano to Sacramento; Huerta's motto 'Sí, se puede' gave hope. Map: Delano (southern San Joaquin Valley) to Sacramento, about 300 miles north. Proficient = accurate paragraph + correct route.

## Art & Music 2: Art about my life: drawing home and community

**Prepare:** Sketchbook, pastels, watercolor, collage paper; all artwork from the year.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking art about your life must be a big event: everyday moments count.
- Using the same material every time: try a different medium.

### Day 1 (Monday): Memory list
*Student:* List 5 favorite events from this year at home or in the community (a birthday, a festival, a hike, a holiday meal) and sketch two.

*How to teach:* List 5 favorite events this year; sketch two.

- **Q:** What is a subject?
  **A:** What the artwork is about.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan the artwork
*Student:* Choose one event and plan an artwork using lines, shapes, colors, and textures from earlier units.

*How to teach:* Choose one event; plan using lines, shapes, colors, textures.

- **Q:** Which art element will you use to show the mood?
  **A:** Names color, line, etc.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Create the artwork with mixed media.

*How to teach:* Create with mixed media.

- **Q:** What is mixed media?
  **A:** Art made using more than one material.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Second piece
*Student:* Create a quick second piece about a place in your community (park, library, market) using a different material.

*How to teach:* Create a second piece about a community place using a different material.

- **Q:** Which material did you use?
  **A:** Names material.

### Day 5 (Friday): Exhibit theme
*Student:* Lay out all your artworks from the year and sort them into possible exhibit themes (Nature, Me and My Family, Around the World).

*How to teach:* Sort all year's artworks into exhibit themes.

- **Q:** Which theme fits your Matisse collage?
  **A:** Nature (organic leaf/seaweed shapes) or another logical theme.

**Assessment:** Two community-life artworks and exhibit theme sort.

**Answer key:** Two artworks about home/community events and places using line, shape, color, texture from earlier units; second piece uses a different material. Theme sort: all year's works grouped into themes like Nature, Me and My Family, Around the World. Proficient = both pieces + logical sort.

## PE 2: Rules, fairness and sportsmanship

**Prepare:** Balls, cones, flags (socks or bandanas).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking arguing wins disputes: use a fair fix (rock-paper-scissors, redo).
- Gloating when winning: say 'good game' to everyone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules matter
*Student:* Play tag with no rules, then with rules; discuss why rules make games fair and safe.

*How to teach:* Play tag with no rules, then with rules; discuss fairness and safety.

- **Q:** Why do games need rules?
  **A:** To keep them fair and safe.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Win or lose
*Student:* Play capture the flag; practice saying 'good game' whether you win or lose.

*How to teach:* Capture the flag; practice 'good game.'

- **Q:** What do you say after a game?
  **A:** 'Good game!' whether you win or lose.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solve it
*Student:* Learn problem-solving steps (stop, talk, choose a fair fix like rock-paper-scissors or a redo) and role-play an 'out or not out' dispute.

*How to teach:* Teach stop, talk, choose a fair fix; role-play an 'out or not out' dispute.

- **Q:** Name a fair fix.
  **A:** Rock-paper-scissors or a redo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make a game
*Student:* Invent a game with 3 rules and teach it to the family.

*How to teach:* Invent a game with 3 rules; teach the family.

- **Q:** What are your 3 rules?
  **A:** Clear rules listed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play your invented game and show good sportsmanship and fair problem solving.

*How to teach:* Play your game; show sportsmanship.

- **Q:** What is sportsmanship?
  **A:** Playing fair, being kind and respectful, win or lose.

**Assessment:** Observation of sportsmanship and problem-solving steps.

**Answer key:** Observation: follows rules, takes turns, says 'good game,' uses steps (stop, talk, choose a fair fix) during a disagreement. Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Tobacco, vaping and alcohol

**Prepare:** Two sponges (one clean, one darkened with brown paint or coffee to model smoke damage), paper and markers. PARENTAL PREVIEW: discuss calmly; avoid shaming family members who smoke.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking vaping is safe or 'just water': vapes contain nicotine and chemicals that harm lungs.
- Thinking secondhand smoke doesn't matter: breathing others' smoke harms health too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Harmful effects
*Student:* Learn how tobacco and vaping harm lungs, heart and teeth.

*How to teach:* Teach how tobacco and vaping harm lungs, heart and teeth; nicotine is addictive.

- **Q:** Name a harm of smoking or vaping.
  **A:** Damages lungs, hurts the heart, stains teeth, causes bad breath.
- **Q:** What does addictive mean?
  **A:** Hard to stop once you start.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lung model
*Student:* Model clean and smoker lungs with sponges and discuss secondhand smoke.

*How to teach:* Compare a clean sponge and a dark, gunky sponge; discuss secondhand smoke.

- **Q:** What is secondhand smoke?
  **A:** Smoke breathed in by people near someone who is smoking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Influences
*Student:* Discuss how friends, family and ads influence choices.

*How to teach:* Discuss how friends, family and ads influence choices.

- **Q:** How might ads make vaping look?
  **A:** Cool, fun, flavored; they hide the harm.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Refusal skills
*Student:* Practice saying no, giving a reason and walking away.

*How to teach:* Practice refusal: say no, give a reason, walk away.

- **Q:** What are the refusal steps?
  **A:** Say no, give a reason, walk away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a 'Smoke-Free Me' poster and role-play a refusal.

*How to teach:* Make a 'Smoke-Free Me' poster; role-play refusal.

- **Q:** Why is alcohol unsafe for kids?
  **A:** It harms a growing brain and body and affects thinking and balance.

**Assessment:** Poster and refusal role-play.

**Answer key:** Poster: clear smoke-free message with at least 2 accurate harms (damages lungs, makes it hard to breathe, harms heart, stains teeth, bad breath, nicotine is addictive). Refusal role-play: says 'No,' gives a reason ('it hurts my lungs'), walks away or changes the subject. Proficient = all.
