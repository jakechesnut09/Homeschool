# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 4

## ELA 2: Henry and Mudge; revising and sharing stories

**Prepare:** Get Henry and Mudge: The First Book (Cynthia Rylant) and preview it. Gather colored pencils, letter tiles for blends, a revision checklist (2 details, feeling sentence, capitals, end marks), and set up an 'author's chair.' For Friday, pick a Frog and Toad story the child hasn't read (e.g., 'The Letter' or 'Alone').

**Watch for:**
- Dropping a sound in blends (reading 'stop' as 'sop'). Tap each sound: /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/.
- Thinking revising means recopying neatly. Revising = adding or changing ideas; editing = fixing capitals, spelling, and punctuation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Henry and Mudge
*Student:* Read Henry and Mudge: The First Book, chapters 1–2, and describe how Henry feels before and after he gets Mudge. Phonics: read initial blends (bl, cr, st, fl) in 15 words.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2. Ask how Henry feels before he gets Mudge and after. Read initial-blend words (bl, cr, st, fl).

- **Q:** How does Henry feel before he gets Mudge, and why?
  **A:** Lonely — he has no brothers or sisters and there are no children on his street.
- **Q:** How does Mudge change after Henry gets him?
  **A:** Mudge grows and grows into a huge dog (180 pounds) — and Henry isn't lonely anymore.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Henry gets lost
*Student:* Read chapter 3 and discuss how Henry and Mudge each respond when Mudge gets lost. Phonics: final blends (-nd, -st, -mp) word building with letter tiles.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 3. Discuss how Henry and Mudge each feel and act when Mudge gets lost. Build final-blend words (-nd, -st, -mp) with tiles.

- **Q:** How do Henry and Mudge feel when Mudge is lost?
  **A:** Both feel scared and sad; Mudge misses Henry and Henry misses Mudge.
- **Q:** Build a word ending in -mp.
  **A:** Examples: jump, camp, lamp, stamp.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise with details
*Student:* Writing: reread the narrative draft and add 2 details and a feeling sentence using a colored pencil ('My heart was pounding'). Edit for capitals and end marks with a checklist.

*How to teach:* Reread the draft and add 2 details and a feeling sentence in colored pencil. Then edit with the checklist for capitals and end marks.

- **Q:** Add a feeling sentence to 'We went to the beach.'
  **A:** Example: My heart was pounding when the big wave came.
- **Q:** What is the difference between revising and editing?
  **A:** Revising changes ideas (adding details); editing fixes capitals, spelling, and punctuation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish
*Student:* Writing: copy the revised narrative neatly, add an illustration for each part, and give it a title. Practice reading it aloud at a steady pace with expression.

*How to teach:* The child copies the revised story neatly, illustrates each part, and adds a title. Practice reading it aloud at a steady pace.

- **Q:** What should a good title do?
  **A:** Tell what the story is about and make people want to read it.
- **Q:** How should you read your story aloud?
  **A:** At a steady pace, loud enough, with expression.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Author's chair: read the published narrative aloud to the family and answer listeners' questions. Complete a unit check: blends/digraph spelling (12 words) and a short story-element quiz on a new Frog and Toad story.

*How to teach:* Author's chair: the child reads the narrative to family and answers questions. Then give the 12-word spelling check and the story-element quiz on a new Frog and Toad story.

- **Q:** Name the story elements.
  **A:** Characters, setting, problem, events, and solution.
- **Q:** In 'The Letter,' what is Toad's problem?
  **A:** He is sad because he never gets any mail.

**Assessment:** Published personal narrative scored with a simple rubric (sequence, details, closing, conventions) plus unit spelling and story-element quiz.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (4 = proficient-plus, 3 = proficient): Sequence — events in clear order with temporal words; Details — at least 2 details and a feeling; Closing — a sentence that wraps up the moment; Conventions — capitals and end marks mostly correct. Spelling (suggested 12): black, crab, stop, flag, frog, sled, hand, best, jump, ship, chin, thank. Story-element quiz on the new Frog and Toad story: characters (Frog, Toad), setting, problem, and solution named correctly (e.g., 'The Letter': Toad is sad because he never gets mail; Frog writes him a letter and a snail delivers it four days later).

## Math 2: Unknowns in all positions and fluency check

**Prepare:** Draw bar-model mats (a long rectangle split into two parts), gather counters and a timer, and have the Week 1 fluency graph ready. Prepare a 40-fact quiz within 20 and 6 word problems.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the box always holds the answer at the end; for 8 + □ = 15 the child writes 23. Read it as 'eight plus what makes fifteen?' and use counters to count up from 8.
- In start-unknown problems, adding the numbers (6 + 14 = 20). Draw the bar: the whole is 14, one part is 6, find the other part: 14 − 6 = 8.

### Day 1 (Monday): Missing addends
*Student:* Solve equations with a box for the unknown (8 + □ = 15, □ − 4 = 9) using counters and a number line.

*How to teach:* Write 8 + □ = 15. Put out 8 counters, then add counters one at a time until there are 15 — count how many you added (7). Repeat with □ − 4 = 9 by asking 'What number, take away 4, leaves 9?' (13).

- **Q:** Solve 8 + □ = 15.
  **A:** 7, because 8 + 7 = 15.
- **Q:** Solve □ − 4 = 9.
  **A:** 13, because 13 − 4 = 9 (9 + 4 = 13).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Start unknown
*Student:* Solve 'Some birds were on a tree. 6 more came. Now there are 14. How many were there at first?' using bar models; solve 5 like it.

*How to teach:* Read the bird story and draw a bar: total 14, known part 6, unknown part '?' at the start. Ask 'Do we know the whole? Which part is missing?' Solve 14 − 6 = 8, then check: 8 + 6 = 14. Solve 5 like it.

- **Q:** Some birds were on a tree. 6 more came. Now there are 14. How many were there at first?
  **A:** 8 birds; 14 − 6 = 8 (check 8 + 6 = 14).
- **Q:** Some kids were at the park. 5 went home. Now 7 are there. How many were there at first?
  **A:** 12 kids; 7 + 5 = 12.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Write your own
*Student:* Write 3 word problems (one with the unknown at the start, middle, end) for a family member to solve and check their answers.

*How to teach:* Help the child write three stories with the same numbers, moving the unknown: end (5 + 4 = ?), middle (5 + ? = 9), start (? + 4 = 9). A family member solves them and the child checks with the equation.

- **Q:** Write an equation for: 'I had some cars. I got 4 more. Now I have 9.'
  **A:** □ + 4 = 9; the unknown is 5.
- **Q:** Which part is unknown in 'Max had 10 pens and lost some. Now he has 6'?
  **A:** The change (middle) is unknown; 10 − □ = 6, so □ = 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fluency stations
*Student:* Rotate through fact games: fact-triangle flash, 'Salute!' card game, and an online fact practice session.

*How to teach:* Set three stations for about 10 minutes each: fact-triangle flash, 'Salute!' (two players hold a card to their foreheads without looking; a caller says the sum; each player figures out their own card), and an online fact practice session. Note facts that are still slow.

- **Q:** In Salute!, the sum is 13 and you see your partner's 6. What is your card?
  **A:** 7, because 6 + 7 = 13.
- **Q:** What is 9 + 8?
  **A:** 17.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Fluency quiz (40 facts within 20, compare to Week 1 baseline) and 6 one-step word problems with unknowns in different positions.

*How to teach:* Give the 40-fact quiz with the timer and graph the score beside the baseline. Then read the 6 word problems; the child writes an equation with a box for each before solving.

- **Q:** Mia had 15 beads. She gave some to a friend. Now she has 9. How many did she give away?
  **A:** 6 beads; 15 − □ = 9, □ = 6.
- **Q:** Some apples were in a bowl. Dad added 5. Now there are 12. How many at first?
  **A:** 7 apples; □ + 5 = 12.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test with fluency comparison to baseline.

**Answer key:** Fluency quiz: score out of 40; add the new bar to the Week 1 graph and compare (growth is the goal). Word problems (suggested): 1) 7 + □ = 15 → 8. 2) □ − 4 = 9 → 13. 3) Some birds were on a tree, 6 more came, now 14 → 8 birds. 4) 12 crayons, gave some away, 5 left → 7 given away. 5) Ana has 9 shells, Ben has 4 more than Ana → 13. 6) 18 cookies, 9 eaten → 9 left. Proficient: 5–6 correct with matching equations.

## Science 2: Testing materials: absorbency and waterproofing

**Prepare:** Gather a paper towel, napkin, sponge, cloth, plastic wrap, foil, an eyedropper or spoon, water, cups, and a paper doll. Cut all materials into same-size squares.

**Watch for:**
- Changing more than one thing (using more water on one material). A fair test keeps everything the same except the material.
- Thinking 'absorbent' means 'waterproof.' Absorbent soaks up water; waterproof keeps water out.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan a test
*Student:* Plan a fair test of which material absorbs water best: same amount of water, same size pieces. Write predictions.

*How to teach:* Plan a fair test: same amount of water, same size pieces. The child writes predictions for each material.

- **Q:** What must stay the same in a fair test?
  **A:** The amount of water and the size of each material piece — only the material changes.
- **Q:** Predict which will absorb most: sponge or foil.
  **A:** Sponge — it has holes that soak up water; foil absorbs none.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Absorbency test
*Student:* Drop 10 drops (or spoonfuls) of water on each material and observe what happens; record in a data table.

*How to teach:* Drop 10 drops of water on each material; observe and record in a table.

- **Q:** What happened to water on the plastic wrap?
  **A:** It beaded up and stayed on top — plastic wrap doesn't absorb water.
- **Q:** Which absorbed the most?
  **A:** Usually the paper towel or sponge.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Waterproof test
*Student:* Test which materials keep a paper doll dry under drips (foil, plastic wrap, cloth, paper).

*How to teach:* Test which materials keep a paper doll dry under drips (foil, plastic wrap, cloth, paper).

- **Q:** Which materials kept the doll dry?
  **A:** Foil and plastic wrap (they are waterproof).
- **Q:** Why did the paper doll under paper get wet?
  **A:** Paper absorbs water and lets it soak through.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analyze
*Student:* Make a bar graph of results and decide the best material for a raincoat and for cleaning a spill.

*How to teach:* Make a bar graph of results and decide the best material for a raincoat and for cleaning a spill.

- **Q:** Best material for cleaning a spill?
  **A:** Paper towel or sponge — they absorb water.
- **Q:** Best material for a raincoat?
  **A:** Plastic — it is waterproof.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a claim and evidence sentence: 'The best material for a raincoat is ___ because ___.'

*How to teach:* The child writes a claim and evidence sentence.

- **Q:** Finish: 'The best material for a raincoat is ___ because ___.'
  **A:** Plastic, because water stayed on top and didn't soak through in our test.
- **Q:** What is evidence?
  **A:** Facts or data from your test that support your claim.

**Assessment:** Data table, bar graph, and claim-evidence sentence.

**Answer key:** Data table: expected results — paper towel and sponge absorb the most; cloth and napkin some; plastic wrap and foil absorb none (water beads up). Waterproof test: foil and plastic wrap keep the doll dry; cloth and paper let water through. Bar graph: labeled axes, bars match data. Claim-evidence sentence: 'The best material for a raincoat is plastic wrap (or foil) because no water got through it in our test.'

## People Who Make a Difference: Interviewing family: then and now

**Prepare:** Interview question sheet, phone or recorder, Venn diagram paper; jacks, marbles or chalk for a long-ago game. Arrange a time with a grandparent or older relative.

**Watch for:**
- Asking yes/no questions: open questions (What? How?) get better stories.
- Thinking everything was worse long ago: some things were harder, some were simpler or fun; compare fairly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the interview
*Student:* Write 6 interview questions for a grandparent or older relative (What toys did you play with? How did you get to school? What did you eat? What chores did you do?).

*How to teach:* Write 6 interview questions for an older relative.

- **Q:** Give an example of a good interview question.
  **A:** 'What games did you play when you were my age?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interview
*Student:* Conduct and record the interview by phone, video, or in person; take notes in drawings and words.

*How to teach:* Conduct and record the interview; take notes in pictures and words.

- **Q:** What is oral history?
  **A:** History told out loud by people who lived it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a Venn diagram comparing your daily life with the interviewee's childhood (school, games, technology, food).

*How to teach:* Make a Venn diagram comparing your life and theirs.

- **Q:** What goes in the middle of a Venn diagram?
  **A:** Things that are the same for both.
- **Q:** What does contrast mean?
  **A:** Tell how things are different.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Games from long ago
*Student:* Play one game the interviewee played as a child (jacks, hopscotch, marbles) and write about it.

*How to teach:* Play a long-ago game (jacks, hopscotch, marbles); write about it.

- **Q:** How is this game different from games you play now?
  **A:** Uses simple objects, no screens or batteries.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Add the interview and Venn diagram to the family history book.

*How to teach:* Add the interview and Venn diagram to the family history book.

- **Q:** What did you learn about your relative's childhood?
  **A:** Specific fact from the interview.

**Assessment:** Interview notes and then/now Venn diagram.

**Answer key:** Interview notes answer at least 5 of 6 questions (toys, school travel, food, chores, etc.). Venn diagram: at least 2 items in each circle and 2 in the middle (e.g., Grandma: walked to school, black-and-white TV; Me: tablet, car ride; Both: played tag, went to school). Proficient = complete notes + balanced Venn.

## Art & Music 2: Line and shape project: Mondrian and my neighborhood

**Prepare:** Image of Mondrian's 'Broadway Boogie Woogie' (1942-43) plus one of his earlier black-line grid paintings; black tape or marker; red, yellow, blue paper; masking tape for hanging; index cards for labels.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Mondrian paintings have black lines: 'Broadway Boogie Woogie' uses yellow lines dotted with little red, blue and gray squares, like New York City traffic and jazz music.
- Mixing up vertical and horizontal: vertical goes up and down like a flagpole; horizontal goes side to side like the horizon.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look at Mondrian
*Student:* Compare Mondrian's 'Broadway Boogie Woogie' with Kandinsky's circles; list how each artist used lines and shapes.

*How to teach:* Compare 'Broadway Boogie Woogie' with Kandinsky's circles; list how each used lines and shapes.

- **Q:** How are Mondrian and Kandinsky different?
  **A:** Mondrian used straight vertical/horizontal lines and rectangles; Kandinsky used circles and curves.
- **Q:** What city inspired 'Broadway Boogie Woogie'?
  **A:** New York City (its street grid, lights and jazz).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Neighborhood map art
*Student:* Sketch the streets near your home as vertical and horizontal lines on paper.

*How to teach:* Sketch streets near home as vertical and horizontal lines.

- **Q:** Which lines are vertical?
  **A:** Up-and-down lines.
- **Q:** Which are horizontal?
  **A:** Side-to-side lines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mondrian city
*Student:* Turn the sketch into a Mondrian-style artwork with black lines and red, yellow, and blue rectangles.

*How to teach:* Turn the sketch into Mondrian style: black lines and red, yellow, blue rectangles.

- **Q:** What are the primary colors?
  **A:** Red, yellow and blue.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mini gallery
*Student:* Hang the unit's artworks in a hallway 'gallery' and label each with title and materials.

*How to teach:* Hang the unit's artworks; label each with title and materials.

- **Q:** What goes on a label?
  **A:** Title and materials (and artist name).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Give a gallery tour naming 5 kinds of lines and both kinds of shapes in your work.

*How to teach:* Give a gallery tour naming 5 lines and both kinds of shapes.

- **Q:** Point to a geometric and an organic shape.
  **A:** Geometric in Mondrian/Kandinsky work; organic in Matisse collage.

**Assessment:** Mondrian-style artwork and gallery tour using art vocabulary.

**Answer key:** Mondrian-style artwork: neighborhood streets drawn as vertical and horizontal lines; rectangles filled with red, yellow, blue. Gallery tour names 5 line types (e.g., vertical, horizontal, zigzag, curved, spiral) and both shape kinds (geometric, organic). Labels have title and materials. Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Movement challenge course

**Prepare:** Home obstacle course from week 3, cones, chalk line, timer and last week's times.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking faster is always better: safe and controlled is the goal; speed comes with practice.
- Thinking encouragement is only for winners: cheer for everyone's effort.

### Day 1 (Monday): Add a twist
*Student:* Add direction changes to your course: sideways shuffle between cones and backward walk on a chalk line.

*How to teach:* Add a sideways shuffle between cones and a backward walk on a chalk line.

- **Q:** How do you move safely backward?
  **A:** Slowly, looking over your shoulder.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Follow the leader
*Student:* Play follow the leader with a sibling or parent, taking turns leading with speed and level changes.

*How to teach:* Follow the leader, switching leaders with speed and level changes.

- **Q:** Show a high, slow move.
  **A:** Tiptoe walking slowly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Animal moves
*Student:* Move through the course as animals (bear crawl, crab walk, frog jump, flamingo balance).

*How to teach:* Move through as animals: bear crawl, crab walk, frog jump, flamingo balance.

- **Q:** Which animal move is a balance?
  **A:** Flamingo (standing on one foot).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Encouragement
*Student:* Cheer a family member through your course using 3 positive phrases, then switch roles.

*How to teach:* Cheer a family member with 3 positive phrases, then switch.

- **Q:** Give an encouraging phrase.
  **A:** 'You've got this!' 'Great job!' 'Keep going!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Run the full course and compare with your week-3 time; tell how practice changed your time.

*How to teach:* Run the full course and compare with week 3.

- **Q:** How did practice change your time?
  **A:** Usually faster, because you learned the course and moved more smoothly.

**Assessment:** Course time comparison and observation of encouraging words.

**Answer key:** Time comparison: child states this week's time vs. week 3 and explains (e.g., 'I got 5 seconds faster because I practiced and knew the course'). Encouraging words observed: 3 positive phrases. Proficient = accurate comparison + 3 phrases.

## Health 2: Standing up to bullying

**Prepare:** 'Stand Tall, Molly Lou Melon' by Patty Lovell (library); paper to trace a hand. Preview: talk calmly, and invite your child to share any experiences.

**Watch for:**
- Calling every mean moment 'bullying': bullying is on purpose, repeated, and hurtful (often with a power difference).
- Thinking telling an adult is tattling: telling to keep someone safe is reporting, not tattling.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read aloud
*Student:* Read 'Stand Tall, Molly Lou Melon' and talk about how Molly handled being teased.

*How to teach:* Read 'Stand Tall, Molly Lou Melon'; talk about how Molly handled teasing.

- **Q:** How did Molly Lou handle Ronald's teasing?
  **A:** She remembered her grandma's advice, believed in herself, and proudly showed what she could do.
- **Q:** Who gave Molly good advice?
  **A:** Her grandma.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): What is bullying
*Student:* Learn the difference between rude, mean and bullying (repeated, on purpose, hurtful).

*How to teach:* Explain rude (accidental, once), mean (on purpose, once), bullying (on purpose, repeated, hurtful).

- **Q:** What three things make something bullying?
  **A:** On purpose, repeated, and hurtful.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Upstander moves
*Student:* Practice 3 upstander moves: say 'Stop', walk away with the target, tell a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Practice upstander moves: say 'Stop,' walk away with the target, tell a trusted adult.

- **Q:** What is an upstander?
  **A:** Someone who helps when they see bullying.
- **Q:** What is a bystander?
  **A:** Someone who sees it but does nothing.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trusted adults
*Student:* Draw a hand and write a trusted adult on each finger.

*How to teach:* Trace a hand; write one trusted adult on each finger.

- **Q:** Who is a trusted adult?
  **A:** A grown-up who keeps you safe and listens, like a parent, grandparent, teacher, coach.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Respond correctly to 3 bullying scenarios and explain the difference between telling and tattling.

*How to teach:* Respond to 3 scenarios; explain telling vs. tattling.

- **Q:** What's the difference between telling and tattling?
  **A:** Telling helps keep someone safe; tattling is trying to get someone in trouble.

**Assessment:** Scenario responses.

**Answer key:** Scenario responses (examples): 1) A kid calls a classmate names every day: say 'Stop,' stand with/walk away with the target, tell a trusted adult. 2) Someone is pushed off the swing again and again: help the person leave and tell an adult. 3) You are left out on purpose repeatedly: tell a trusted adult and find kind friends. Telling vs. tattling: telling helps someone who is hurt or unsafe; tattling just tries to get someone in trouble. Proficient = correct upstander actions in all 3 + accurate difference.
