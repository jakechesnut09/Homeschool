# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 14

## ELA 2: The Velveteen Rabbit and planning a fiction story

**Prepare:** Get The Velveteen Rabbit (Margery Williams, public domain) and read it first; note that the Boy's illness (scarlet fever) and the burning of his toys may be sad for some children. Make a character-change chart and oo/au/aw word cards (moon, food, book, look, sauce, haul, draw, paw). Print a story-mountain organizer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'Real' in the story means 'alive' from the start. Reread the Skin Horse: becoming Real happens when a child loves you for a long, long time.
- Reading oo with only one sound. Sort moon/food (long oo) vs. book/look (short oo).

### Day 1 (Monday): Velveteen Rabbit part 1
*Student:* Read the first half of The Velveteen Rabbit aloud and discuss what the Skin Horse means by 'Real.' Phonics: oo words in two sounds (moon, book).

*How to teach:* Read the first half aloud. Discuss what the Skin Horse means by 'Real.' Read oo words both ways.

- **Q:** According to the Skin Horse, how does a toy become Real?
  **A:** When a child loves it for a long, long time — not just to play with, but really loves it.
- **Q:** Does becoming Real hurt, according to the Skin Horse?
  **A:** Sometimes, but when you are Real you don't mind being hurt.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Velveteen Rabbit part 2
*Student:* Finish the story and chart how the Rabbit changes from beginning to end. Phonics: au/aw words (sauce, draw, paw).

*How to teach:* Finish the story and chart how the Rabbit changes (new and shiny → shabby and loved → a real rabbit). Read au/aw words.

- **Q:** How does the Rabbit change by the end?
  **A:** He goes from a new toy, to a worn toy the Boy loves, to a real living rabbit made Real by the Fairy.
- **Q:** Why were the Boy's toys taken away?
  **A:** The Boy had scarlet fever, and the doctor ordered his toys burned so the germs would not spread.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Past tense in writing
*Student:* Write 5 sentences retelling the Rabbit's adventures using irregular past-tense verbs (was, became, went, grew, saw). Read the sentences to check they sound right.

*How to teach:* Write 5 sentences retelling the Rabbit's adventures with irregular past verbs (was, became, went, grew, saw). Read them aloud to check.

- **Q:** Fix: 'The Rabbit becomed Real.'
  **A:** The Rabbit became Real.
- **Q:** Past tense of grow and see?
  **A:** grew; saw.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan my story
*Student:* Plan an original fiction story with a toy or animal character on a story-mountain organizer: beginning, problem, events, solution, ending.

*How to teach:* Plan an original story about a toy or animal on a story mountain: beginning, problem, events, solution, ending.

- **Q:** What goes at the top of the story mountain?
  **A:** The most exciting part — the biggest moment of the problem.
- **Q:** Give a sample problem for a toy story.
  **A:** Example: A stuffed bear gets left behind at the park.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 14 review
*Student:* Answer who/what/why questions about the Velveteen Rabbit in writing, spelling check of 12 oo/au/aw words, and share the story plan.

*How to teach:* The child answers who/what/why questions in writing, takes the spelling check, and shares the story plan.

- **Q:** Who helped the Rabbit become a real rabbit?
  **A:** The nursery magic Fairy.
- **Q:** Spell 'book' and 'draw.'
  **A:** b-o-o-k; d-r-a-w.

**Assessment:** Written comprehension answers, spelling check, completed story plan.

**Answer key:** Written answers (model): Who gave the Boy the rabbit? It was in his Christmas stocking. What does Real mean to the Skin Horse? When a child loves you for a long, long time, you become Real. Why was the Rabbit thrown out? The Boy had scarlet fever, and the doctor said his toys had to be burned. How did the Rabbit become a real rabbit? The nursery magic Fairy came when he cried a real tear and made him Real. Spelling (suggested 12): moon, food, soon, book, look, foot, sauce, haul, draw, paw, saw, lawn. Story plan: proficient has character, setting, problem, 2–3 events, and solution.

## Math 2: Adding several two-digit numbers

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks and a grocery store flyer (or make price tags under $1). Write 8 multi-addend problems for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Losing track of numbers when adding 3–4 addends. Teach crossing off each number as it is used.
- Forgetting regrouped tens when the ones total is more than 20 (e.g., 24 ones = 2 tens 4 ones). Build the ones with blocks to see.

### Day 1 (Monday): Three addends
*Student:* Add 23 + 15 + 17 by finding a friendly pair first (23 + 17 = 40); solve 8 problems.

*How to teach:* Write 23 + 15 + 17. Ask 'Which two make a friendly number?' (23 + 17 = 40), then add 15: 55. The child solves 8 problems, circling the pair added first.

- **Q:** 23 + 15 + 17 = ?
  **A:** 55 — 23 + 17 = 40, 40 + 15 = 55.
- **Q:** 16 + 14 + 22 = ?
  **A:** 52 — 16 + 14 = 30, + 22 = 52.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Four addends
*Student:* Add four two-digit numbers by adding tens, then ones (24 + 31 + 12 + 18); use blocks to check.

*How to teach:* Add 24 + 31 + 12 + 18 by tens (20 + 30 + 10 + 10 = 70) then ones (4 + 1 + 2 + 8 = 15), then 70 + 15 = 85. Check with blocks.

- **Q:** 24 + 31 + 12 + 18 = ?
  **A:** 85.
- **Q:** 11 + 19 + 25 + 30 = ?
  **A:** 85 (11 + 19 = 30, 30 + 30 = 60, 60 + 25 = 85).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Order doesn't matter
*Student:* Show that 35 + 26 + 15 gives the same sum in any order and explain the property in your own words.

*How to teach:* Add 35 + 26 + 15 in three different orders and compare: always 76. The child explains in their own words that the order doesn't change the sum.

- **Q:** Add 35 + 26 + 15 in two orders.
  **A:** 35 + 15 = 50, + 26 = 76; 26 + 35 = 61, + 15 = 76. Same sum.
- **Q:** Does 8 + 7 equal 7 + 8? Why?
  **A:** Yes, both are 15 — you can add in any order.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shopping list
*Student:* Add prices of 3–4 items from a grocery flyer (rounded to whole cents under $1, e.g., 45¢ + 29¢ + 18¢).

*How to teach:* Pick 3–4 items under $1 from the flyer, write prices in cents, and add. Check whether the total is under or over $1 (100¢).

- **Q:** 45¢ + 29¢ + 18¢ = ?
  **A:** 92¢.
- **Q:** Is 35¢ + 40¢ + 30¢ more or less than $1?
  **A:** More — 105¢ is 5¢ more than $1.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 8 multi-addend problems and explain which numbers you grouped first and why.

*How to teach:* Give 8 multi-addend problems; for two, the child writes which numbers were grouped first and why.

- **Q:** 45 + 29 + 18 = ?
  **A:** 92.
- **Q:** Which pair would you add first in 37 + 13 + 40? Why?
  **A:** 37 + 13 = 50, a friendly ten; then 50 + 40 = 90.

**Assessment:** Multi-addend quiz with reasoning.

**Answer key:** Multi-addend quiz (suggested 8): 1) 23 + 15 + 17 = 55 2) 24 + 31 + 12 + 18 = 85 3) 35 + 26 + 15 = 76 4) 45 + 29 + 18 = 92 5) 16 + 14 + 22 = 52 6) 11 + 19 + 25 + 30 = 85 7) 37 + 13 + 40 = 90 8) 28 + 22 + 16 + 14 = 80. Reasoning: proficient if the child names the friendly pair grouped first (e.g., '23 and 17 make 40') or explains adding tens then ones. 7/8 correct = proficient.

## Science 2: Fair test: Do plants need water?

**Prepare:** Get 4 more seedlings (2 watered, 2 not), a measuring cup, ruler, and graph paper. Continue measuring the light-test plants.

**Watch for:**
- Overwatering 'watered' plants. Give the same small measured amount each time.
- Graphing without a title or labels. Every graph needs a title, labels, and a scale.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the water test
*Student:* Set up 2 watered plants and 2 unwatered plants in the same light; predict results.

*How to teach:* Set up 2 watered and 2 unwatered plants in the same light. Predict results.

- **Q:** What is changed in this test?
  **A:** Water.
- **Q:** Predict the unwatered plants.
  **A:** They will wilt and stop growing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure light test
*Student:* Measure the light-test plants and graph height over time.

*How to teach:* Measure the light-test plants and graph height over time.

- **Q:** What does the graph show?
  **A:** How much each plant grew over time.
- **Q:** Which group is greener?
  **A:** The light group.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Observe water test
*Student:* Observe and record wilting, color, and height of the water-test plants.

*How to teach:* Observe the water-test plants: wilting, color, and height.

- **Q:** What does wilting look like?
  **A:** Leaves and stems droop and become soft.
- **Q:** Why do plants wilt?
  **A:** They don't have enough water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graph
*Student:* Make bar graphs comparing plants with and without light and water.

*How to teach:* Make bar graphs comparing plants with and without light and water.

- **Q:** What goes on the bottom of the graph?
  **A:** The plant groups.
- **Q:** What goes up the side?
  **A:** Height in centimeters.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Share the two graphs with a family member and explain which plants look healthiest and what surprised you.

*How to teach:* Share the graphs with a family member and explain which plants look healthiest and what surprised you.

- **Q:** Which plants look healthiest?
  **A:** The ones with both light and water.
- **Q:** What surprised you?
  **A:** Example: the dark plants grew tall but pale.

**Assessment:** Two bar graphs of plant growth.

**Answer key:** Two bar graphs (3 = proficient): titles, labeled axes (plant; height in cm), equal scale, bars that match data. Expected: watered plants taller and upright; unwatered plants wilted and drooping; light plants green, dark plants pale.

## People Who Make a Difference: Carrying out laws and courts

**Prepare:** Photo of a courtroom; simple costumes (robe for judge); the Goldilocks story; role cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking police make laws: police carry out (enforce) laws; lawmakers make them.
- Thinking judges decide the punishment based on feelings: judges follow the law and the facts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Carrying out laws
*Student:* Learn how mayors, governors, the president, and police carry out laws.

*How to teach:* Teach how mayors, governors, the president and police carry out laws.

- **Q:** What does a police officer do?
  **A:** Enforces laws and keeps people safe.
- **Q:** Who leads the country?
  **A:** The president.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Courts
*Student:* Learn how judges and juries decide whether a law was broken; look at a photo of a courtroom.

*How to teach:* Teach how judges and juries decide whether a law was broken; view a courtroom photo.

- **Q:** What does a jury do?
  **A:** A group of citizens listens to the facts and decides guilty or not guilty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mock trial
*Student:* Role-play a mock trial of Goldilocks with judge, jury, and witnesses.

*How to teach:* Role-play Goldilocks' mock trial.

- **Q:** What evidence shows Goldilocks was in the bears' house?
  **A:** Porridge eaten, chair broken, someone sleeping in Baby Bear's bed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Consequences
*Student:* Discuss fair consequences for breaking rules and laws.

*How to teach:* Discuss fair consequences.

- **Q:** What would be a fair consequence for Goldilocks?
  **A:** Example: apologize, fix the chair, make new porridge.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Match roles (lawmaker, judge, police officer, president) to their jobs.

*How to teach:* Match roles to jobs.

- **Q:** Who decides what a law means in a court?
  **A:** A judge.

**Assessment:** Role-matching quiz and mock trial participation.

**Answer key:** Role matching: lawmaker - makes laws; judge - decides if a law was broken and what is fair; police officer - enforces laws and keeps people safe; president - leader of the country who carries out laws (governor - state; mayor - city); jury - group of citizens who decide guilty or not guilty. Mock trial: plays a role and uses evidence. Proficient = all matches + participation.

## Art & Music 2: Sol, mi, and la

**Prepare:** Curwen hand-sign chart, paper with a 3-line staff, pennies; song lyrics for 'Rain, Rain, Go Away,' 'Star Light, Star Bright,' 'Ring Around the Rosie.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking la is between sol and mi: la is just above sol; mi is below sol.
- Placing mi in the space under sol: when sol is on a line, mi is on the next line down (a skip), and la is the space just above sol.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sol-mi
*Student:* Sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' and 'Star Light, Star Bright' with sol-mi hand signs.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' and 'Star Light, Star Bright' with sol-mi signs.

- **Q:** Which is higher, sol or mi?
  **A:** Sol.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add la
*Student:* Add la with 'Ring Around the Rosie' and echo-sing 8 sol-mi-la patterns with hand signs.

*How to teach:* Add la with 'Ring Around the Rosie'; echo 8 sol-mi-la patterns.

- **Q:** Where is la compared to sol?
  **A:** One step higher than sol.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Penny staff
*Student:* Place pennies on a 3-line staff to show sol-mi-la patterns and sing them.

*How to teach:* Place pennies on a 3-line staff for sol-mi-la; sing them.

- **Q:** If sol is on the middle line, where is mi?
  **A:** On the bottom line.
- **Q:** Where is la?
  **A:** In the space just above sol.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improvise
*Student:* Improvise a 4-beat sol-mi-la answer to a sung question ('What did you eat today?').

*How to teach:* Improvise a 4-beat sol-mi-la answer to 'What did you eat today?'.

- **Q:** Sing an answer using sol, mi and la.
  **A:** Any 4-beat answer using those pitches, e.g., 'I ate pi-zza' on sol-mi-la-sol.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Sing 4 patterns from penny-staff notation and echo 4 patterns with hand signs.

*How to teach:* Sing 4 penny-staff patterns; echo 4 with signs.

- **Q:** What is solfège?
  **A:** Singing syllables (do, re, mi, sol, la…) for pitches.

**Assessment:** Solfège singing and reading check.

**Answer key:** Singing/reading check: sings 4 penny-staff patterns accurately (e.g., sol-mi-sol-la-sol-mi) and echoes 4 patterns with correct hand signs (sol = flat hand, palm toward body; mi = flat hand, palm down; la = relaxed, drooping hand). Proficient = 7-8 of 8 correct.

## PE 2: Aerobic endurance

**Prepare:** Water bottle, timer, safe neighborhood route, fitness log.

**Watch for:**
- Starting too fast: a steady pace lasts longer.
- Waiting until thirsty to drink: drink before, during and after.

### Day 1 (Monday): Walk-jog intervals
*Student:* Alternate 1 minute jogging and 1 minute walking for 8 minutes; sip water after.

*How to teach:* Alternate 1 minute jogging, 1 minute walking for 8 minutes; sip water.

- **Q:** What is endurance?
  **A:** Being able to keep moving a long time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pacing game
*Student:* Jog laps at 'turtle' and 'cheetah' pace and decide which pace lasts longer.

*How to teach:* Jog laps at turtle and cheetah pace; decide which lasts longer.

- **Q:** Which pace lasts longer?
  **A:** Turtle (slow, steady) pace.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Neighborhood loop
*Student:* Walk-jog a neighborhood loop with a parent and record minutes moving without stopping.

*How to teach:* Walk-jog a neighborhood loop; record minutes without stopping.

- **Q:** What is pace?
  **A:** How fast you move over time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fuel & water
*Student:* Choose a healthy pre-activity snack and drink water before, during and after a 10-minute jog.

*How to teach:* Choose a healthy snack; drink water before, during and after a 10-minute jog.

- **Q:** Give a healthy pre-activity snack.
  **A:** Banana, apple slices, whole-grain crackers, yogurt.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Jog or walk-jog continuously for 10 minutes and record the time on your fitness log.

*How to teach:* Jog or walk-jog 10 minutes; record.

- **Q:** Why is hydration important?
  **A:** Your body loses water as sweat; water keeps you cool and energized.

**Assessment:** Fitness log of continuous minutes.

**Answer key:** Fitness log: date and minutes of continuous jog or walk-jog (goal 10 minutes). Proficient = 10 minutes recorded; developing = 6-9; beginning = under 6.

## Health 2: Food labels and advertising

**Prepare:** 3 cereal boxes with Nutrition Facts labels; food ads (TV, magazines, boxes); paper and markers.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing sugar without checking serving size: compare the same serving amounts.
- Believing everything an ad says: ads are made to sell.

### Day 1 (Monday): Label hunt
*Student:* Find the serving size and sugar on 3 cereal boxes.

*How to teach:* Find serving size and sugar on 3 cereals.

- **Q:** Where do you find sugar on a box?
  **A:** On the Nutrition Facts label (Total Sugars / Added Sugars).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare cereals
*Student:* Rank the cereals from least to most sugar per serving.

*How to teach:* Rank cereals by sugar per serving.

- **Q:** If one cereal has 4 g sugar and another 12 g, which has less?
  **A:** The 4 g one.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ad detectives
*Student:* Watch or look at food ads and identify tricks (cartoon characters, toys, bright colors).

*How to teach:* Look at food ads; identify tricks.

- **Q:** Name an ad trick.
  **A:** Cartoon characters, free toys, bright colors, catchy songs, famous people.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design an ad
*Student:* Design a cereal box or ad for a healthy food using honest claims.

*How to teach:* Design a healthy food ad with honest claims.

- **Q:** What is an honest claim?
  **A:** A true statement, like 'has 5 grams of fiber.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how ads try to influence kids and how labels help you choose.

*How to teach:* Explain how ads influence kids and how labels help.

- **Q:** What does 'influence' mean?
  **A:** To affect what someone thinks or does.

**Assessment:** Label comparison and ad design.

**Answer key:** Label comparison: serving size and grams of sugar recorded for 3 cereals, ranked least to most correctly. Ad design: honest claims, healthy food. Explanation: ads use tricks (cartoon characters, toys, bright colors, celebrities, catchy songs) to make kids want products; labels give real facts like sugar and serving size. Proficient = correct ranking + 2 ad tricks + label use.
