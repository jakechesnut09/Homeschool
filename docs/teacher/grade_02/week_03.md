# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 3

## ELA 2: Digraphs and drafting a personal narrative

**Prepare:** Get Frog and Toad Together and preview 'A List,' 'Cookies,' and 'The Garden.' Make digraph cards (sh, ch, th, wh, ck) and a collective-noun matching set (flock/birds, herd/cows, team/players, class/students, school/fish, pack/wolves, bunch/grapes, swarm/bees).

**Watch for:**
- Reading digraphs as two sounds (s-h in 'ship'). Teach that two letters can make one new sound and practice with picture cards.
- Story drafts with 'and then… and then…' Replace with temporal words: first, next, after that, finally.

### Day 1 (Monday): The List
*Student:* Read 'A List' from Frog and Toad Together and make your own to-do list for the day. Phonics: read and write sh/ch/th words (ship, chop, thin, wish, much, bath).

*How to teach:* Read 'A List.' Make the child's own to-do list for the day. Teach sh, ch, th: read and write ship, chop, thin, wish, much, bath.

- **Q:** In 'A List,' why doesn't Toad chase his list when the wind blows it away?
  **A:** Running after the list was not one of the things written on his list.
- **Q:** How does Toad end his day?
  **A:** He writes 'Go to sleep' on the ground with a stick, crosses it out, and goes to sleep.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cookies and willpower
*Student:* Read 'Cookies' and answer why Frog and Toad cannot stop eating cookies and how they try to solve it. Phonics: wh and ck words (when, whip, duck, sock) in a word ladder.

*How to teach:* Read 'Cookies' and ask why they can't stop eating and how they try to stop. Explain 'willpower.' Build a wh/ck word ladder (when → whip; duck → sock).

- **Q:** Why can't Frog and Toad stop eating the cookies?
  **A:** The cookies are very good, and they keep wanting just one more.
- **Q:** How do they try to solve the problem, and what finally happens?
  **A:** They put the cookies in a box, tie it with string, and put it on a high shelf, but they could still open it; Frog gives the cookies to the birds. Toad goes home to bake a cake.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Collective nouns
*Student:* Grammar: match collective nouns to groups (a flock of birds, a herd of cows, a team of players, a class of students) and write 4 sentences using them. Read 'The Garden' aloud with a partner.

*How to teach:* Teach collective nouns (a flock of birds, a herd of cows). Match pairs and write 4 sentences. Read 'The Garden' aloud with a partner, taking turns by page.

- **Q:** What do we call a group of cows? Of students?
  **A:** A herd of cows; a class of students.
- **Q:** In 'The Garden,' what does Toad do to make his seeds grow?
  **A:** He shouts at them, reads them stories, sings songs, reads poems, and plays music. They grow after he falls asleep — really, seeds just need time, sun, and rain.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft my story
*Student:* Writing: draft the small moment chosen last week using temporal words (first, next, then, after that, finally) to show the order. Include what you saw, heard, and felt.

*How to teach:* Draft the small moment using first, next, then, after that, finally. Prompt for senses: 'What did you see? Hear? Feel?'

- **Q:** Name 3 temporal words.
  **A:** first, next, then, after that, finally (any 3).
- **Q:** Add a feeling to: 'I jumped in the pool.'
  **A:** Example: I jumped in the pool and the cold water made me shiver.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 3 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 10 digraph words, collective-noun matching quiz, and read the narrative draft aloud to a family member who asks one question about it.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling check and the collective-noun quiz. The child reads the draft aloud to a family member, who asks one question.

- **Q:** Spell 'wish' and 'duck.'
  **A:** w-i-s-h; d-u-c-k.
- **Q:** What is a group of fish called?
  **A:** A school of fish.

**Assessment:** Spelling check, collective-noun quiz (8 items), and narrative draft with at least 3 temporal words.

**Answer key:** Spelling (suggested 10): ship, chop, thin, wish, much, bath, when, duck, sock, whip. Collective nouns (8): flock of birds, herd of cows, team of players, class of students, school of fish, pack of wolves, bunch of grapes, swarm of bees. Narrative draft: proficient = tells one small moment in order with at least 3 temporal words and at least one detail of what the child saw, heard, or felt.

## Math 2: Subtraction strategies within 20

**Prepare:** Make fact triangles (cut paper triangles; sum at the top corner, addends at the bottom corners), draw a 0–20 number line, and gather 20 counters. Write a 30-item subtraction fact quiz within 20.

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting in the wrong order (writing 6 − 14). Use the fact triangle: the top number (the whole) always comes first in subtraction.
- In compare problems, adding the two numbers because the problem says 'more.' Draw two bars lined up and ask 'how much extra does the longer bar have?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Fact families
*Student:* Build fact families with fact triangles (7, 8, 15) and write the 4 related equations for 6 families.

*How to teach:* Show a fact triangle with 15 on top and 7, 8 on the bottom. Cover each corner and ask for the missing number. The child writes the 4 equations (7 + 8 = 15, 8 + 7 = 15, 15 − 7 = 8, 15 − 8 = 7) for 6 families.

- **Q:** Write the fact family for 6, 9, and 15.
  **A:** 6 + 9 = 15, 9 + 6 = 15, 15 − 6 = 9, 15 − 9 = 6.
- **Q:** In a fact family, which number is the whole?
  **A:** The biggest number (the sum), e.g., 15 in 6, 9, 15.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Think addition
*Student:* Solve 14 − 6 by asking '6 + ? = 14'; practice 20 subtraction facts using the related addition fact.

*How to teach:* Model 14 − 6 by asking 'What goes with 6 to make 14?' Count up or recall 6 + 8 = 14, so 14 − 6 = 8. The child practices 20 subtraction cards, saying the related addition fact for each.

- **Q:** Solve 14 − 6 by thinking addition.
  **A:** 8, because 6 + 8 = 14.
- **Q:** Solve 17 − 9.
  **A:** 8, because 9 + 8 = 17.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Back through ten
*Student:* Use a 0–20 number line to subtract through ten (15 − 7: back 5 to 10, back 2 more to 8).

*How to teach:* On the 0–20 number line, start at 15 and jump back 5 to land on 10, then 2 more to land on 8 (5 + 2 = 7 jumps in all). Practice 6 problems, always stopping at 10 first.

- **Q:** Solve 13 − 5 by going back through ten.
  **A:** 8 — back 3 to 10, then back 2 more to 8.
- **Q:** Solve 12 − 4.
  **A:** 8 — back 2 to 10, back 2 more to 8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare problems
*Student:* Solve 'how many more/fewer' problems with bar models (e.g., 'Leo has 13 stickers, Mia has 8. How many more does Leo have?').

*How to teach:* Draw two bars: Leo's (13) longer than Mia's (8). Line them up and shade the extra part. Ask 'How many more?' and write 13 − 8 = 5 or 8 + 5 = 13. Do 5 similar problems.

- **Q:** Leo has 13 stickers. Mia has 8. How many more does Leo have?
  **A:** 5 more; 13 − 8 = 5.
- **Q:** Sam read 11 books. Kai read 4 fewer than Sam. How many did Kai read?
  **A:** 7 books; 11 − 4 = 7.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Subtraction fact quiz (30 facts) and 4 mixed word problems with drawings and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the 30-fact subtraction quiz, then the 4 mixed word problems. The child must show a drawing (circles or bar model) and an equation for each.

- **Q:** There were 16 grapes. Tom ate some. Now there are 9. How many did he eat?
  **A:** 7 grapes; 16 − 9 = 7.
- **Q:** What is 11 − 3?
  **A:** 8.

**Assessment:** Subtraction fact quiz and mixed word problems.

**Answer key:** Subtraction quiz: 30 facts within 20, score out of 30 (proficient 25+). Suggested items: 14−6=8, 15−7=8, 13−9=4, 12−5=7, 16−8=8, 11−4=7, 17−9=8, 18−9=9, 12−8=4, 15−6=9. Word problems (suggested): 1) Leo has 13 stickers, Mia has 8: 13 − 8 = 5 more. 2) 15 birds, 7 fly away: 8 left. 3) Jen has 9 beads and needs 16: 16 − 9 = 7 more. 4) 6 red + 9 blue = 15 balloons. Each needs a drawing and correct equation.

## Science 2: Describing materials by properties

**Prepare:** Collect 15 household objects made of wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric, paper, and rubber (keep glass items handled by an adult), sorting trays, and a cloth bag for the mystery-bag game.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing an object with its material (a 'spoon' is the object; metal is the material).
- Thinking all metals are shiny or all plastics are bendy. Test each property rather than guessing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Material hunt
*Student:* Find 15 household objects and identify what material each is made of (wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric, paper, rubber).

*How to teach:* Find 15 objects and identify the material of each (wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric, paper, rubber).

- **Q:** What is a spoon usually made of?
  **A:** Metal (some are plastic or wood).
- **Q:** What material is a T-shirt made of?
  **A:** Fabric (cloth, often cotton).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Properties
*Student:* Describe each material's properties (hard/soft, rough/smooth, bendy/stiff, shiny/dull) in a table.

*How to teach:* Describe each material's properties in a table: hard/soft, rough/smooth, bendy/stiff, shiny/dull.

- **Q:** Name two properties of a rubber band.
  **A:** Stretchy (bendy) and soft.
- **Q:** Name two properties of a metal spoon.
  **A:** Hard and shiny (also stiff and smooth).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sort and classify
*Student:* Sort the objects two different ways (by material, then by a property) and explain the rule for each sort.

*How to teach:* Sort the objects by material, then by a property (e.g., bendy/stiff). The child explains the rule for each sort.

- **Q:** Can a wooden ruler and a metal ruler go in the same group?
  **A:** Yes, if sorting by a property like 'stiff' — they are different materials but both stiff.
- **Q:** What is a sorting rule?
  **A:** The property or feature you use to decide which group each object goes in.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mystery bag
*Student:* Identify objects in a bag by touch alone and name their texture and hardness.

*How to teach:* Put objects in a bag; the child identifies them by touch and names texture and hardness.

- **Q:** How can you tell fabric from metal by touch?
  **A:** Fabric is soft and bendy; metal is hard, cool, and stiff.
- **Q:** Which sense do you use in the mystery bag?
  **A:** Touch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Classify 10 new objects by material and properties and justify one classification aloud.

*How to teach:* The child classifies 10 new objects by material and properties and justifies one aloud.

- **Q:** Classify a glass jar.
  **A:** Glass; hard, smooth, see-through (transparent).
- **Q:** Why is a cooking pot made of metal?
  **A:** Metal is strong, hard, and can be heated without melting or burning.

**Assessment:** Material/property classification table.

**Answer key:** Classification table (3 = proficient): each object with correct material and at least 2 correct properties, e.g., spoon — metal, hard, smooth, shiny; sock — fabric, soft, bendy; pencil — wood, hard, stiff; eraser — rubber, soft, bendy; cup — plastic, hard or bendy, smooth; book — paper, bendy, dull. One justified sort aloud.

## People Who Make a Difference: Family artifacts and documents

**Prepare:** 3-5 family artifacts (old photos, letters, recipe cards, tools), index cards for labels, a family document (copy of a birth certificate, old letter, report card), family history notebook.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking an artifact must be very old or valuable: an artifact is any object people made or used that tells about their lives.
- Placing grandparents below the child on the family tree: older generations go above (or at the roots).

### Day 1 (Monday): Artifact museum
*Student:* Collect 3–5 family artifacts and set up a home 'museum'; write a label for each telling what it is and who used it.

*How to teach:* Collect 3-5 artifacts; set up a home museum; write labels (what it is, who used it).

- **Q:** What is an artifact?
  **A:** An object made or used by people that tells about the past.
- **Q:** What is an heirloom?
  **A:** A special object passed down in a family.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ask the artifact
*Student:* Use the questions 'What is it? Who used it? When? Why was it important?' to study one artifact and draw it.

*How to teach:* Study one artifact with What? Who? When? Why important? and draw it.

- **Q:** Who used this artifact?
  **A:** Answer based on family information.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Documents
*Student:* Look at a family document (birth certificate copy, old letter, recipe card, or report card) and record 3 facts it tells.

*How to teach:* Look at a family document; record 3 facts it tells.

- **Q:** What facts does a birth certificate tell?
  **A:** Name, birth date, birthplace, parents' names.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family tree
*Student:* Draw a family tree with yourself, parents, and grandparents (and great-grandparents if known) with names and birthplaces.

*How to teach:* Draw a family tree with names and birthplaces.

- **Q:** What is an ancestor?
  **A:** A family member who lived before you, like a great-grandparent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Start a family history book with the family tree and artifact pages.

*How to teach:* Start the family history book with the tree and artifact pages.

- **Q:** Who is your parent's parent?
  **A:** Your grandparent.

**Assessment:** Family tree and artifact pages.

**Answer key:** Family tree: child, parents, grandparents (great-grandparents if known) in correct generations with names and birthplaces. Artifact pages: each artifact drawn with answers to What is it? Who used it? When? Why important? Proficient = complete tree + 3 artifact pages.

## Art & Music 2: Matisse cut-outs: drawing with scissors

**Prepare:** Images of Matisse's 'The Snail' and 'Icarus' (from his book 'Jazz'), colored paper, scissors, glue sticks, background paper; phone for photos.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking negative space is 'nothing': it is the space around and between shapes, and it can be a shape itself.
- Gluing before planning: try several arrangements first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look at Matisse
*Student:* Look at Matisse's 'The Snail' and learn that he 'drew with scissors' when he was ill; describe the shapes and colors.

*How to teach:* Look at 'The Snail'; explain that when Matisse was ill and used a wheelchair, he 'drew with scissors' by cutting painted paper.

- **Q:** How did Matisse make his cut-outs?
  **A:** He cut shapes from paper he had painted with gouache and had them arranged and pinned or glued.
- **Q:** Why did Matisse start making cut-outs?
  **A:** After a serious illness he could not stand to paint for long, so he cut paper instead.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Free cutting
*Student:* Practice safe scissor use and cut 10 organic shapes freehand without drawing first, inspired by leaves and seaweed.

*How to teach:* Practice safe scissor use; cut 10 organic shapes freehand like leaves and seaweed.

- **Q:** Did you draw first?
  **A:** No, we cut freehand, 'drawing with scissors.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compose
*Student:* Arrange the shapes on a background in three different ways before gluing; take a photo of each arrangement and choose the best.

*How to teach:* Arrange shapes three ways; photograph each; choose the best.

- **Q:** What is composition?
  **A:** How the parts of an artwork are arranged.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Positive and negative
*Student:* Use the leftover paper (negative space) to make a second collage and compare the two.

*How to teach:* Use the leftover paper (negative space) to make a second collage.

- **Q:** What is positive space?
  **A:** The shapes themselves (the objects).
- **Q:** What is negative space?
  **A:** The space around and between the shapes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Present both collages and explain why you chose your arrangement.

*How to teach:* Present both collages; explain the arrangement.

- **Q:** Why did you choose this arrangement?
  **A:** Specific reason: balance, contrast, movement.

**Assessment:** Matisse-style collage and explanation of choices.

**Answer key:** Collage: at least 10 freehand organic shapes arranged thoughtfully; second collage uses negative-space leftovers. Explanation names a reason for the chosen arrangement (balance, color contrast, movement). Proficient = both collages + specific reason; developing = one collage or vague reason.

## PE 2: Over, under, around and through

**Prepare:** Pool noodles, two chairs and a broom, cone, hula hoop, blanket; paper for the course map; timer.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing 'around' and 'through': around goes outside an object; through goes inside it.
- Crawling with head up under low objects: keep the head down and go slowly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Obstacle words
*Student:* Practice going over a pool noodle, under a broom across two chairs, around a cone and through a hoop; name each word.

*How to teach:* Set up: over a noodle, under a broom on two chairs, around a cone, through a hoop. Say each word as your child moves.

- **Q:** Did you go over or under the broom?
  **A:** Under.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Levels
*Student:* Travel the course at high (tiptoes), medium and low (crawl) levels; freeze in a shape at each level.

*How to teach:* Travel the course at high (tiptoe), medium and low (crawl); freeze in a shape at each.

- **Q:** What level is crawling?
  **A:** Low level.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build a course
*Student:* Design and draw a 6-station obstacle course with a parent and set it up in the yard or living room.

*How to teach:* Design and draw a 6-station course together; set it up.

- **Q:** What is an obstacle?
  **A:** Something in the way you must go over, under, around or through.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Timed course
*Student:* Run your course three times, timing each try and recording the times on a chart.

*How to teach:* Run the course 3 times, timing and charting each try.

- **Q:** Which try was fastest?
  **A:** Read from the chart.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Teach the course to a family member using the words over, under, around, through and explain which level each part used.

*How to teach:* Your child teaches the course to a family member using position and level words.

- **Q:** What level did you use going under the broom?
  **A:** Low level.

**Assessment:** Course completed safely; correct use of position and level words.

**Answer key:** Course check: completes 6 stations safely and uses the correct word for each (over the noodle, under the broom, around the cone, through the hoop) and names each level (high = tiptoes, medium = standing/walking, low = crawling). Proficient = all words and levels correct.

## Health 2: Being a good friend

**Prepare:** 'The Invisible Boy' by Trudy Ludwig (library), paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'being nice' means saying yes to everything: good friends are kind but can disagree respectfully.
- Blaming in a conflict ('You always…'): use an I-message that tells how you feel.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read aloud
*Student:* Read 'The Invisible Boy' by Trudy Ludwig and discuss how Brian felt left out.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Invisible Boy'; discuss how Brian felt left out.

- **Q:** Why did Brian feel invisible?
  **A:** Other kids didn't notice him or include him (not picked for games, not invited to a party).
- **Q:** What changed for Brian?
  **A:** New student Justin was friends with him and included him, and Brian became 'colorful.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Friend recipe
*Student:* Write a 'Recipe for a Good Friend' listing ingredients like kindness, sharing and listening.

*How to teach:* Write a 'Recipe for a Good Friend.'

- **Q:** Name two ingredients of a good friend.
  **A:** Kindness, sharing, listening, including others, honesty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening skills
*Student:* Practice active listening: eyes on speaker, nod, ask a question; take turns telling about your weekend.

*How to teach:* Practice active listening: eyes on speaker, nod, ask a question.

- **Q:** What are three active listening moves?
  **A:** Look at the speaker, nod, ask a question.

### Day 4 (Thursday): I-messages
*Student:* Learn 'I feel ___ when ___ because ___' and use it in 3 friend-problem role-plays.

*How to teach:* Teach 'I feel ___ when ___ because ___'; do 3 role-plays.

- **Q:** Make an I-message for 'a friend grabbed my toy.'
  **A:** 'I feel upset when you grab my toy because I was still using it.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Name 3 qualities of a good friend and show one I-message.

*How to teach:* Name 3 qualities of a good friend; show an I-message.

- **Q:** What is empathy?
  **A:** Understanding and caring about how someone else feels.

**Assessment:** Friend recipe and I-message role-play.

**Answer key:** Friend recipe lists at least 4 ingredients (kindness, sharing, listening, including others, honesty, taking turns). Role-play: correct I-message, e.g., 'I feel left out when you don't let me play because I want to be part of the game.' Three qualities named. Proficient = 4+ ingredients + complete I-message.
