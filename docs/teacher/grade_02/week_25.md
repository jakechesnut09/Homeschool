# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 25

## ELA 2: Biography basics: Martin Luther King Jr.

**Prepare:** Get A Picture Book of Martin Luther King, Jr. (David A. Adler) and read it first. Parent preview: it explains segregation and that Dr. King was shot and killed in 1968 — be ready to talk about it calmly. Make a timeline strip, reflexive-pronoun sentences, and -le word cards. Print a ReadWorks biography passage.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a biography is made up. A biography is a true story of a real person's life, told in order.
- Using 'hisself' or 'theirselves.' Correct forms: himself, themselves.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is a biography
*Student:* Read A Picture Book of Martin Luther King, Jr. and learn that a biography tells about a real person's life in order. List 5 important events.

*How to teach:* Read the book and learn that a biography tells a real person's life in order. List 5 important events.

- **Q:** Where and when was Martin Luther King Jr. born?
  **A:** In Atlanta, Georgia, in 1929.
- **Q:** What unfair laws did Dr. King work against?
  **A:** Segregation laws that kept Black and white people apart and treated Black people unfairly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Timeline
*Student:* Put Dr. King's life events on a timeline strip and explain how one event led to the next (unfair laws → bus boycott → March on Washington).

*How to teach:* Put events on a timeline strip and explain how one led to the next.

- **Q:** What happened after Rosa Parks was arrested?
  **A:** Dr. King helped lead the Montgomery bus boycott; people stopped riding the buses until the law changed.
- **Q:** What famous speech did Dr. King give at the March on Washington?
  **A:** The 'I Have a Dream' speech (1963).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reflexive pronouns
*Student:* Grammar: learn myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves, themselves; complete 10 sentences. Phonics: two-syllable words ending in -le (table, little, simple).

*How to teach:* Teach reflexive pronouns and complete 10 sentences. Read -le words.

- **Q:** Complete: She made the card by ___.
  **A:** herself.
- **Q:** Complete: We cleaned the room ___.
  **A:** ourselves.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Paragraph focus
*Student:* Reread 2 pages and write what each paragraph is mostly about. Discuss why Dr. King believed in peaceful protest.

*How to teach:* Reread 2 pages; the child writes what each paragraph is mostly about. Discuss why Dr. King believed in peaceful protest.

- **Q:** Why did Dr. King believe in peaceful protest?
  **A:** He believed nonviolence and love could change unfair laws without hurting people.
- **Q:** How do you find what a paragraph is mostly about?
  **A:** Ask what most of the sentences tell about.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 25 review
*Student:* Answer 5 who/what/when/why/how questions on a ReadWorks biography passage. Spelling check of 10 -le words and a reflexive-pronoun quiz.

*How to teach:* The child answers 5 who/what/when/why/how questions on a biography passage, takes the -le spelling check, and the reflexive quiz.

- **Q:** Fix: 'He did it hisself.'
  **A:** He did it himself.
- **Q:** Spell 'little' and 'purple.'
  **A:** l-i-t-t-l-e; p-u-r-p-l-e.

**Assessment:** Timeline with cause/effect connections, comprehension questions, spelling and grammar quizzes.

**Answer key:** Timeline (model order): 1929 born in Atlanta, Georgia → grew up facing unfair segregation laws → became a minister → 1955–56 led the Montgomery bus boycott after Rosa Parks's arrest → 1963 March on Washington, 'I Have a Dream' speech → 1964 Nobel Peace Prize → 1968 killed in Memphis. Cause/effect: unfair laws → boycott → laws changed. Reflexive pronouns (10): I → myself, you → yourself, he → himself, she → herself, we → ourselves, they → themselves. Spelling (suggested 10): table, little, simple, apple, purple, candle, bubble, puzzle, turtle, middle.

## Math 2: Telling time to five minutes

**Prepare:** Paper plate, brass fastener, card stock for hands, markers, and an analog clock. Write 12 clock times to show on Tuesday.

**Watch for:**
- Reading the hour hand as the next hour when it is almost there (2:50 read as 3:50). The hour hand hasn't reached 3 yet, so it is still 2 o'clock-something.
- Reading the number the minute hand points to as the minutes (pointing to 5 → '5 minutes'). Count by 5s around the clock: 5 means 25 minutes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Make a clock
*Student:* Make a paper-plate clock with hands and label minutes by 5s around the edge.

*How to teach:* Make a paper-plate clock: write 1–12, then the minutes 0, 5, 10 … 55 in a ring outside the numbers. Attach a short hour hand and a long minute hand.

- **Q:** When the minute hand points to 6, how many minutes?
  **A:** 30 minutes.
- **Q:** Which hand is shorter?
  **A:** The hour hand.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Five-minute times
*Student:* Read and show 12 times to the nearest 5 minutes (3:25, 7:50); match analog to digital.

*How to teach:* Show 12 times on the paper clock (3:25, 7:50…). The child reads the hour hand first, then counts by 5s for minutes, and matches analog to digital.

- **Q:** The hour hand is between 3 and 4, and the minute hand is on 5. What time is it?
  **A:** 3:25.
- **Q:** Show 7:50: where are the hands?
  **A:** Hour hand almost to 8; minute hand on 10.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): a.m. and p.m.
*Student:* Sort daily activities into a.m. or p.m. and make a schedule of your day with clock drawings.

*How to teach:* Explain: a.m. = midnight to noon, p.m. = noon to midnight. The child sorts activities and makes a daily schedule with clock drawings.

- **Q:** Is breakfast usually a.m. or p.m.?
  **A:** a.m.
- **Q:** Is 7:00 bedtime a.m. or p.m.?
  **A:** p.m.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Quarter and half past
*Student:* Learn 'half past' and 'quarter past/to' language and tell the time on the house clock at 5 checkpoints today.

*How to teach:* Teach half past (:30), quarter past (:15), and quarter to (:45 — 15 minutes before the next hour). Check the house clock 5 times today.

- **Q:** What time is quarter to 11?
  **A:** 10:45.
- **Q:** What is half past 4?
  **A:** 4:30.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Tell and write 10 times, choose a.m. or p.m., and solve 6 money problems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test: 10 times, a.m./p.m. choices, and 6 money problems.

- **Q:** The minute hand is on 9 and the hour hand is between 6 and 7. What time?
  **A:** 6:45 (quarter to 7).
- **Q:** $1 − 55¢ = ?
  **A:** 45¢.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test (suggested): Times — 1) 3:25 2) 7:50 3) 12:05 4) 9:15 (quarter past 9) 5) 4:30 (half past 4) 6) 10:45 (quarter to 11) 7) 6:10 8) 1:55 9) 8:35 10) 11:20. a.m./p.m.: breakfast a.m.; dinner p.m.; bedtime 8:00 at night p.m.; 10:00 in the morning a.m. Money: a) 3 dimes + 2 nickels = 40¢ b) $1 − 55¢ = 45¢ c) $10 + $5 + $1 = $16 d) 2 quarters + 3 pennies = 53¢ e) $20 − $8 = $12 f) 4 dimes + 1 quarter = 65¢. Proficient: 17/20.

## Science 2: Slow Earth changes: weathering and erosion

**Prepare:** Gather sugar cubes and a jar with a lid, two rough rocks, black paper, a magnifier, a small plastic container with a snap lid, a freezer, and Grand Canyon photos.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking weathering and erosion are the same. Weathering breaks rock into pieces; erosion carries the pieces away.
- Thinking slow changes don't happen because we can't see them. They happen over thousands to millions of years.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shake and break
*Student:* Shake 5 sugar cubes in a jar for 2 minutes, then 2 more, and compare the corners each time to model how rocks bumping in rivers are worn down.

*How to teach:* Shake 5 sugar cubes in a jar for 2 minutes, then 2 more; compare corners.

- **Q:** What happened to the sugar cube corners?
  **A:** They became rounder and broke off into crumbs.
- **Q:** How is this like rocks in a river?
  **A:** Rocks bump together in moving water and wear down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rock rubbing
*Student:* Rub two rough rocks together over black paper for 2 minutes and look at the dust with a magnifier; discuss how long it would take to wear a rock away.

*How to teach:* Rub two rough rocks together over black paper for 2 minutes and look at the dust.

- **Q:** How much dust did you make?
  **A:** Only a little.
- **Q:** How long would it take to wear a rock away?
  **A:** A very long time — years and years.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grand Canyon
*Student:* Read about how the Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon over millions of years and look at the rock layers in photos; write 2 facts.

*How to teach:* Read about how the Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon; look at rock layers and write 2 facts.

- **Q:** What carved the Grand Canyon?
  **A:** The Colorado River.
- **Q:** How long did it take?
  **A:** Millions of years.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ice cracks rock
*Student:* Fill a small plastic container to the top with water, cap it, freeze overnight, and observe how the ice bulged or cracked the container.

*How to teach:* Fill a container with water, cap it, freeze overnight, and observe.

- **Q:** What happened to the container?
  **A:** It bulged or cracked.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** Water expands (takes more space) when it freezes into ice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences explaining why weathering and erosion change land so slowly, using evidence from the sugar cube and ice tests.

*How to teach:* The child writes 3 sentences explaining why weathering and erosion are slow, using evidence.

- **Q:** How does ice crack rocks?
  **A:** Water gets into cracks, freezes, expands, and pushes the rock apart.
- **Q:** What is erosion?
  **A:** Moving bits of rock and soil from one place to another by water, wind, or ice.

**Assessment:** Weathering observations and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Observations: sugar cube corners got rounder and crumbs collected; rock rubbing made only a little dust; frozen water expanded and bulged or cracked the container. Written explanation (3 = proficient): 'Weathering and erosion change land slowly because only tiny bits break off at a time. The rocks made only a little dust after 2 minutes, and ice cracked the container a little — it takes millions of years to carve a canyon.'

## People Who Make a Difference: Economics project: my food's journey

**Prepare:** Poster paper, food packaging, Calisphere and library sources; California map.

**Watch for:**
- Using only one source: check facts in 2 sources.
- Leaving out workers: name the person doing each step.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a food
*Student:* Choose a favorite food (strawberries, tortillas, rice, oranges) and find where it is grown in California on a map.

*How to teach:* Choose a food; find where it's grown in California.

- **Q:** Where are many strawberries grown in California?
  **A:** Coastal areas like Watsonville/Salinas and Oxnard/Santa Maria.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Use 2 sources to find out who grows, processes, transports, and sells it and what weather and water it needs.

*How to teach:* Use 2 sources to research growers, processors, transport, sellers, weather and water.

- **Q:** Why use 2 sources?
  **A:** To check that facts are correct.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poster
*Student:* Make a farm-to-table poster with 5 steps, a map, and labels for each worker's job.

*How to teach:* Make a 5-step poster with map and worker labels.

- **Q:** What is the first step for your food?
  **A:** Growing it on a farm.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the poster to family, explaining one way the food is grown differently today than long ago.

*How to teach:* Present; explain one difference between long ago and today.

- **Q:** How is your food grown differently today?
  **A:** Machines, irrigation, refrigerated trucks.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Quiz: goods vs. services, producers vs. consumers, farm-to-table steps, and a scarcity choice question.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 quiz.

- **Q:** Is a farmer a producer or consumer?
  **A:** Both: a producer of food, and a consumer when buying things.

**Assessment:** Farm-to-table poster and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Poster: 5 steps in order (grow, harvest, process, transport, sell), California map marking where it's grown, labels for each worker, weather/water needs, and one long-ago vs. today difference (e.g., 'strawberries were picked and carried by hand; now trucks with refrigeration get them to stores fast'). Unit 5 quiz: goods vs. services, producer vs. consumer, farm-to-table order, scarcity choice. Proficient = complete poster + 80% quiz.

## Art & Music 2: Papel picado (Mexico)

**Prepare:** Tissue paper, scissors, string, glue; photos of papel picado.

**Watch for:**
- Cutting on open edges: cut along the folds to make symmetrical holes.
- Thinking papel picado is only for one holiday: it's used for many celebrations.

### Day 1 (Monday): Papel picado
*Student:* Look at photos of papel picado banners used in Mexican celebrations like Día de los Muertos and weddings and discuss their purpose.

*How to teach:* Look at papel picado; discuss purposes (Día de los Muertos, weddings, fiestas, holidays).

- **Q:** What is papel picado?
  **A:** Mexican folk art of cut tissue-paper banners.
- **Q:** When is it used?
  **A:** Celebrations like Día de los Muertos, weddings, birthdays and holidays.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice folds
*Student:* Practice folding and cutting small shapes from scrap paper to make symmetrical designs.

*How to teach:* Fold and cut shapes from scrap paper to make symmetrical designs.

- **Q:** What is symmetry?
  **A:** Both halves match like a mirror image.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Make banners
*Student:* Fold and cut 4 tissue-paper flags with designs.

*How to teach:* Fold and cut 4 tissue flags.

- **Q:** Why fold before cutting?
  **A:** One cut makes matching shapes on both sides.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hang
*Student:* String the flags into a banner and hang it.

*How to teach:* String the flags into a banner and hang it.

- **Q:** Where would you hang your banner?
  **A:** Answer varies.

### Day 5 (Friday): Compare
*Student:* Compare papel picado to decorations your family uses for celebrations.

*How to teach:* Compare papel picado to family decorations.

- **Q:** How are they alike?
  **A:** Both decorate to celebrate special days.

**Assessment:** Papel picado banner and comparison.

**Answer key:** Banner: 4 folded and cut flags with symmetrical designs strung together. Comparison names how papel picado and family decorations are alike (decorate for celebrations) and different (materials/traditions). Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Balloon and hand striking

**Prepare:** Balloons, beach ball, string tied between chairs as a net.

**Watch for:**
- Hitting with fists: flat hands give control.
- Chasing the balloon wildly: move feet to get under it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Balloon keep-up
*Student:* Keep a balloon up with flat hands for as many hits as possible.

*How to teach:* Keep a balloon up with flat hands; count hits.

- **Q:** What is striking?
  **A:** Hitting an object with a body part or tool.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body parts
*Student:* Strike the balloon with hand, elbow, knee and foot in a pattern.

*How to teach:* Strike with hand, elbow, knee, foot in a pattern.

- **Q:** Which body part was hardest?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner volley
*Student:* Volley a balloon back and forth over a string 'net' with a partner.

*How to teach:* Volley over a string net with a partner.

- **Q:** What is a volley?
  **A:** Hitting the object back before it touches the ground.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Beach ball
*Student:* Move to a beach ball and strike it upward with two hands (set) 10 times.

*How to teach:* Set a beach ball upward with two hands 10 times.

- **Q:** Where do your hands go for a set?
  **A:** Above your forehead, fingers spread.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Keep a balloon up for 15 hits in your space and explain why a flat hand helps.

*How to teach:* Keep a balloon up 15 times; explain flat hand.

- **Q:** Why use a flat hand?
  **A:** It gives a bigger, even surface for control.

**Assessment:** Keep-up count and observation.

**Answer key:** Keep-up count in self space (goal 15). Explanation: a flat hand makes a bigger, even surface so the balloon goes where you aim. Proficient = 15 + explanation.

## Health 2: Playground and water safety

**Prepare:** A life jacket (or picture), scenario cards, sunscreen; access to a playground.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking strong swimmers can swim alone: always swim with an adult watching.
- Jumping in to save someone: reach or throw, don't go.

### Day 1 (Monday): Playground rules
*Student:* Visit a playground and identify safe and unsafe behaviors.

*How to teach:* At a playground, identify safe and unsafe behaviors.

- **Q:** Name a safe slide rule.
  **A:** One at a time, feet first, sitting down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water safety
*Student:* Learn rules: swim with an adult, wear a life jacket, never swim alone.

*How to teach:* Teach: swim with an adult, wear a life jacket, never swim alone.

- **Q:** When should you wear a life jacket?
  **A:** On boats and in open water, and whenever you aren't a strong swimmer.
- **Q:** What does a lifeguard do?
  **A:** Watches swimmers and helps in emergencies.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reach or throw
*Student:* Practice 'reach or throw, don't go' to help someone in water.

*How to teach:* Practice 'reach or throw, don't go' with a towel or a floating object.

- **Q:** What should you do if someone is struggling in water?
  **A:** Shout for an adult; reach with a pole/towel or throw something that floats; don't go in.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sun & heat
*Student:* Review sun safety and signs of overheating.

*How to teach:* Review sun safety and overheating signs (very sweaty, dizzy, headache, sick to stomach, very tired).

- **Q:** What should you do if you feel too hot and dizzy?
  **A:** Stop, go to shade, drink water, tell an adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort scenario cards into safe and unsafe.

*How to teach:* Sort scenarios into safe and unsafe.

- **Q:** Is running near the pool safe?
  **A:** No, you could slip and fall.

**Assessment:** Scenario sort.

**Answer key:** Scenario sort (examples): safe - swimming with a lifeguard/adult watching, wearing a life jacket on a boat, sliding feet first, drinking water and resting in shade on a hot day; unsafe - swimming alone, pushing on the slide, jumping in to save a friend, running by the pool, playing hard in the heat without water. Proficient = all correct.
