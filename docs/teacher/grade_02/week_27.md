# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 27

## ELA 2: Researching a person who made a difference

**Prepare:** Get Harvesting Hope: The Story of Cesar Chavez (Kathleen Krull) and read it first. Gather index cards in 4 colors (early life, challenge, actions, legacy) and a second source on the child's research person.

**Watch for:**
- Using 's for plurals (two dog's). 's shows ownership: the dog's bone.
- Not capitalizing holidays and places. Names of specific days, cities, and states get capitals: Labor Day, Delano, California.

### Day 1 (Monday): Model biography
*Student:* Read Harvesting Hope and notice how the author shows events in order and why Chavez's march mattered to farmworkers. Identify the author's purpose.

*How to teach:* Read Harvesting Hope. Notice the order of events and why Chavez's march mattered. Identify the author's purpose.

- **Q:** Why did Cesar Chavez lead a march?
  **A:** To get fair pay and better treatment for farmworkers.
- **Q:** Where did the march go?
  **A:** From Delano to Sacramento, California's capital — about 340 miles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Second source
*Student:* Take notes from a second source on your research person and add new facts to note cards (early life, challenge, actions, legacy).

*How to teach:* Take notes from a second source and add new facts to note cards (early life, challenge, actions, legacy).

- **Q:** What is a legacy?
  **A:** What a person is remembered for and the difference they left behind.
- **Q:** What was Cesar Chavez's childhood like?
  **A:** His family lost their farm in Arizona and became migrant farmworkers in California.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Possessives
*Student:* Grammar: singular possessives with 's (Rosa's bus, Cesar's march) and capitals for holidays, places, and names (Labor Day, Delano, California). Fix 10 sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach singular possessives ('s) and capitals for holidays, places, and names. Fix 10 sentences.

- **Q:** Fix: 'cesars march went to sacramento.'
  **A:** Cesar's march went to Sacramento.
- **Q:** Write the possessive: the bus that belongs to Rosa.
  **A:** Rosa's bus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Organize notes
*Student:* Sort note cards into 3 groups (early life, big work, why we remember them) and plan a paragraph for each.

*How to teach:* Sort note cards into 3 groups (early life, big work, why we remember them) and plan a paragraph for each.

- **Q:** Which group: 'born in 1927'?
  **A:** Early life.
- **Q:** Which group: 'led a march for farmworkers'?
  **A:** Big work.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 27 review
*Student:* Draft the first paragraph of the biography report. Spelling check of 12 words with apostrophes and capitalized place names.

*How to teach:* Draft the first paragraph of the biography report and take the spelling check.

- **Q:** What should paragraph 1 start with?
  **A:** A topic sentence introducing the person and early life.
- **Q:** Spell 'California.'
  **A:** C-a-l-i-f-o-r-n-i-a.

**Assessment:** Organized note cards, first report paragraph, capitalization/possessive check.

**Answer key:** Note cards sorted into 3 groups with at least 3 facts each. First paragraph rubric: 3 = topic sentence about early life + 3 facts in own words; 2 = facts without topic sentence; 1 = copied or off topic. Capitalization/possessive check (suggested): Rosa's bus, Cesar's march, Jackie's team, Labor Day, Delano, California, Fourth of July, Atlanta, Georgia, the girl's book. Spelling: can't, don't, it's, I'm, Sacramento, Mexico, Arizona (8+ of 12 proficient).

## Math 2: Length word problems

**Prepare:** Rulers, yardstick or tape, string, yarn, scissors. Choose a small rectangle (table or rug) for Tuesday. Prepare 8 length word problems for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Adding only two sides to find the distance around a rectangle. Walk a finger around all 4 sides and add all 4.
- Mixing units in one problem. Underline the units and keep them the same.

### Day 1 (Monday): Length stories
*Student:* Solve 6 length problems ('A ribbon is 45 cm. 18 cm is cut off. How long is it now?') with drawings and equations.

*How to teach:* Read each length story, draw a bar or number line, and write an equation with units.

- **Q:** A ribbon is 45 cm. 18 cm is cut off. How long is it now?
  **A:** 27 cm; 45 − 18 = 27.
- **Q:** Ropes of 36 in and 25 in are tied end to end. Total length?
  **A:** 61 inches.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Garden fence
*Student:* Measure the sides of a small rectangle (table or rug) and add to find the distance around.

*How to teach:* Measure each side of a table or rug and add all 4 sides to find the distance around.

- **Q:** A table is 40 in long and 25 in wide. What is the distance around?
  **A:** 130 inches (40 + 25 + 40 + 25).
- **Q:** How many sides do you add for a rectangle?
  **A:** 4 sides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Jump contest
*Student:* Measure standing long jumps in inches for family members and write compare problems.

*How to teach:* Each family member does a standing long jump; measure from the start line to the back of the nearest heel. Write compare problems.

- **Q:** Lia jumped 52 in and Max jumped 39 in. How much farther did Lia jump?
  **A:** 13 inches.
- **Q:** Where do you measure a jump to?
  **A:** From the start line to the back of the heel closest to the line.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Two-step lengths
*Student:* Solve 4 two-step length problems with number lines.

*How to teach:* Solve 4 two-step length problems with number lines.

- **Q:** A string is 80 cm. Two 25 cm pieces are cut off. How much is left?
  **A:** 30 cm (80 − 25 − 25).
- **Q:** One board is 30 in. Another is 20 in longer. How long are both together?
  **A:** 80 in (the second is 50 in; 30 + 50).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Solve 8 length word problems using number lines and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test: 8 length word problems with number lines and equations.

- **Q:** A path is 50 m; you walked 32 m. How far is left?
  **A:** 18 m.
- **Q:** A snake is 28 cm and grows 9 cm. How long now?
  **A:** 37 cm.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test (suggested 8): 1) Ribbon 45 cm, 18 cm cut off → 27 cm 2) Ropes 36 in + 25 in → 61 in 3) Lia jumped 52 in, Max 39 in → 13 in farther 4) Fence sides 20, 15, 20, 15 ft → 70 ft 5) String 80 cm, two 25 cm pieces cut → 30 cm left 6) Board 64 in, need 100 in → 36 in more 7) Snake 28 cm grows 9 cm → 37 cm 8) Path 50 m, walked 32 m → 18 m left. Each needs a number line or equation. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 2: Earth changes model and unit check

**Prepare:** Gather sand or soil in a long pan, a cup with a pinhole, a tray to catch water, Grand Canyon photos, poster paper, and markers. Prepare an 8-item quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the sand model shows exactly how long canyons take. The model is much faster; the real canyon took millions of years.
- Posters without evidence. Each event needs a source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Notebook review
*Student:* Review notebook entries on fast and slow changes and highlight the strongest piece of evidence for each.

*How to teach:* Review notebook entries on fast and slow changes; highlight the strongest evidence for each.

- **Q:** What is strong evidence for a fast change?
  **A:** Mount St. Helens before/after photos from one day.
- **Q:** What is strong evidence for a slow change?
  **A:** The Grand Canyon took millions of years to carve.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sand canyon model
*Student:* Tilt a pan of damp sand and let water trickle from a pinhole cup for 10 minutes; photograph or sketch the 'canyon' every 2 minutes.

*How to teach:* Tilt a pan of damp sand and let water trickle from a pinhole cup for 10 minutes; sketch every 2 minutes.

- **Q:** What happened to the sand?
  **A:** Water carved a channel and carried sand downhill.
- **Q:** Where did the sand go?
  **A:** It was deposited at the bottom of the pan.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Model vs. real
*Student:* Compare the sand canyon to Grand Canyon photos; list 2 ways the model is like the real canyon and 1 way it is different (time).

*How to teach:* Compare the sand canyon to Grand Canyon photos: 2 alike, 1 different.

- **Q:** How is the model like the Grand Canyon?
  **A:** Moving water carved a channel and carried material away.
- **Q:** How is it different?
  **A:** The model took minutes; the real canyon took millions of years and is carved in rock.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poster
*Student:* Make a 'Fast vs. Slow' poster with drawings, time labels, and one piece of evidence from a source for each event.

*How to teach:* Make a 'Fast vs. Slow' poster with drawings, time labels, and one source for each event.

- **Q:** What goes on each event card?
  **A:** Drawing, how long it takes, and a source.
- **Q:** Is a glacier valley fast or slow?
  **A:** Slow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Quiz: classify 8 events as fast or slow and explain one using evidence; present the poster.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz and have the child present the poster.

- **Q:** Is an earthquake fast or slow?
  **A:** Fast.
- **Q:** Explain one slow change.
  **A:** The Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon over millions of years.

**Assessment:** Poster and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Model comparison: alike — water carved a channel; sand washed downhill. Different — the model took 10 minutes, the real canyon millions of years (also: rock vs. sand). Unit quiz (suggested 8): earthquake — fast; canyon — slow; volcano eruption — fast; sand dune moving — slow; landslide — fast; glacier carving a valley — slow; flood — fast; rock weathering — slow. One explained with evidence.

## People Who Make a Difference: Sitting Bull and George Washington Carver

**Prepare:** Biographies of Sitting Bull and George Washington Carver; Library of Congress photos of Sitting Bull and of Carver at Tuskegee Institute; peanuts and sweet potatoes (check for peanut allergy; skip tasting if needed).

**Watch for:**
- Believing Carver invented peanut butter: peanut butter existed before him; he developed many other uses for peanuts and sweet potatoes and taught crop rotation.
- Thinking Sitting Bull fought for no reason: he defended his people's land and way of life when the U.S. government broke promises and took Lakota lands.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sitting Bull
*Student:* Read a short biography of Sitting Bull, Lakota leader, and discuss how he worked to protect his people's land and way of life.

*How to teach:* Read a Sitting Bull biography; discuss protecting land and way of life.

- **Q:** Who was Sitting Bull?
  **A:** A Hunkpapa Lakota (Sioux) leader and holy man.
- **Q:** What did he work to protect?
  **A:** His people's land, buffalo hunting and way of life.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Photo study
*Student:* Study a Library of Congress photograph of Sitting Bull and answer: What do you see? What do you wonder? What does it tell us about him?

*How to teach:* Study a Sitting Bull photo: see, wonder, learn.

- **Q:** What can a photo tell us?
  **A:** How he looked and dressed, and clues about his time.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): George Washington Carver
*Student:* Read a biography of George Washington Carver and learn how he taught Southern farmers to rotate crops with peanuts to restore the soil.

*How to teach:* Read a Carver biography; learn crop rotation with peanuts.

- **Q:** Why did farmers' soil wear out?
  **A:** Planting cotton year after year used up nutrients.
- **Q:** How did peanuts help?
  **A:** Peanuts put nutrients (nitrogen) back into the soil.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peanut products
*Student:* Look at a list of products Carver developed and taste peanuts and sweet potatoes (check allergies); look at the LOC photo of Carver in his lab.

*How to teach:* Look at Carver's products; taste peanuts and sweet potatoes if safe; view his lab photo.

- **Q:** Name products Carver made from peanuts.
  **A:** Dyes, paints, cosmetics, cooking oils, and many more (hundreds of uses).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a hero-traits chart for Sitting Bull and Carver with evidence from the texts.

*How to teach:* Complete hero-traits charts with evidence.

- **Q:** Which trait did Carver show and how?
  **A:** Perseverance: he was born into slavery but kept learning and became a scientist.

**Assessment:** Hero-traits chart with evidence for two people.

**Answer key:** Hero-traits chart with evidence: Sitting Bull - courage, leadership, loyalty (led the Hunkpapa Lakota, refused to give up their land and way of life, a leader at the Battle of the Little Bighorn in 1876). Carver - perseverance, curiosity, helpfulness (born into slavery, worked hard for an education, taught farmers to rotate crops with peanuts and sweet potatoes to restore the soil, found hundreds of uses for them). Proficient = 2 traits each with accurate evidence.

## Art & Music 2: Patterns in textiles and baskets: kente cloth and Native California baskets

**Prepare:** Construction paper strips, paper loom; images of kente cloth from Ghana; Calisphere photos of Pomo baskets; yarn and rope or pipe cleaners.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kente colors are random: weavers choose colors for meaning.
- Thinking baskets were only decoration: Pomo baskets were used for cooking, storing, carrying, gifts and ceremonies.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kente cloth
*Student:* Look at images of kente cloth from Ghana and learn that weavers choose colors with meanings (gold for wealth, green for growth); describe the patterns.

*How to teach:* Look at kente; learn color meanings (gold = wealth/royalty, green = growth/harvest, red = sacrifice/strength, black = maturity/ancestors, blue = peace).

- **Q:** Where is kente from?
  **A:** Ghana in West Africa.
- **Q:** What does green mean in kente?
  **A:** Growth, harvest, renewal.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paper weaving
*Student:* Cut slits in a paper 'loom' and weave contrasting paper strips over-under to make a kente-inspired pattern with colors chosen for meaning.

*How to teach:* Weave paper strips over-under on a paper loom.

- **Q:** What are warp and weft?
  **A:** Warp: the threads held in place on the loom; weft: the threads woven over and under across them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Native California baskets
*Student:* Look at Calisphere photos of Pomo baskets, some woven so tightly they hold water, and describe their patterns and everyday and ceremonial uses.

*How to teach:* Look at Pomo baskets; some held water; discuss uses.

- **Q:** How could a basket hold water?
  **A:** It was woven so tightly that it was watertight.
- **Q:** Name a use for Pomo baskets.
  **A:** Cooking, storage, gathering, gifts, ceremonies.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coil a basket
*Student:* Make a small coiled 'basket' by wrapping yarn around rope or a pipe cleaner and spiraling it into a dish shape.

*How to teach:* Make a coiled basket with yarn wrapped around rope or pipe cleaner.

- **Q:** What is coiling?
  **A:** Wrapping and spiraling a bundle around and around to build a basket.

### Day 5 (Friday): Compare
*Student:* Make a T-chart comparing kente cloth and Pomo baskets (materials, patterns, uses, who makes them) and share one similarity aloud.

*How to teach:* T-chart comparing kente and Pomo baskets; share a similarity.

- **Q:** One similarity?
  **A:** Both are woven, have patterns, and carry cultural meaning.

**Assessment:** Paper weaving, coiled basket, and comparison chart.

**Answer key:** Paper weaving: over-under alternating rows, colors chosen with meanings stated. Coiled basket: yarn wrapped and spiraled into a dish. T-chart: Kente - cotton/silk thread, strip-woven on looms in Ghana by Asante and Ewe weavers, bold geometric patterns, worn for special occasions; Pomo baskets - plant fibers (sedge, willow, bulrush), feathers, shell beads, by Pomo weavers of Northern California, used for cooking, storage, gifts and ceremonies. Similarity: both are woven with patterns and carry tradition. Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Striking games

**Prepare:** Paddles or short bat, foam ball, cone or tee, hula hoop, cones for 'holes,' string net.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging too hard to aim: smooth swing toward the target.
- Criticizing instead of coaching: give positive, specific tips.

### Day 1 (Monday): Target strike
*Student:* Hit a foam ball off a cone/tee into a hula hoop zone with a paddle or bat.

*How to teach:* Hit a foam ball off a tee into a hoop zone.

- **Q:** What does aim mean?
  **A:** Pointing your hit where you want it to go.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rally count
*Student:* Rally a balloon with a partner over a string net, counting together.

*How to teach:* Rally a balloon over the net with a partner, counting.

- **Q:** What's your best rally?
  **A:** Record count.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paddle golf
*Student:* Hit a ball along the ground with a paddle through cone 'holes.'

*How to teach:* Paddle golf through cone holes.

- **Q:** Should you swing hard or soft for a short hole?
  **A:** Soft.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coach a partner
*Student:* Give a family member 2 positive tips while they practice striking.

*How to teach:* Coach a family member with 2 positive tips.

- **Q:** Give a positive tip.
  **A:** 'Great job watching the ball; try keeping the paddle flat.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play a short rally game, record your best rally and reflect on your striking skill.

*How to teach:* Play a rally game; record best rally and reflect.

- **Q:** What part of striking improved?
  **A:** Specific answer.

**Assessment:** Best rally score and feedback observation.

**Answer key:** Best rally score recorded; feedback observation shows 2 positive, specific tips (e.g., 'Keep the paddle flat,' 'Watch the ball'). Reflection names one improvement. Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Earthquakes and emergencies

**Prepare:** Backpack, flashlight, water bottles, first-aid items, snacks for an emergency kit; paper with your address and phone number.

**Watch for:**
- Running outside during shaking: drop, cover and hold on until shaking stops.
- Calling 911 for small problems or as a joke: only for real emergencies.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drop, cover, hold on
*Student:* Learn and practice the earthquake drill in several rooms.

*How to teach:* Teach and practice drop, cover, hold on in several rooms.

- **Q:** What are the three steps?
  **A:** Drop to hands and knees, cover your head and neck (under a table), hold on.
- **Q:** What if there is no table?
  **A:** Get down next to an inside wall, cover your head and neck with your arms.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Emergency kit
*Student:* Help build a family emergency kit.

*How to teach:* Help build a family emergency kit.

- **Q:** Name 3 items for an emergency kit.
  **A:** Water, food, flashlight, batteries, first-aid kit, medicines, blanket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Calling 911
*Student:* Role-play a 911 call stating name, address and the emergency.

*How to teach:* Role-play a 911 call: name, address, emergency; stay on the line.

- **Q:** When should you call 911?
  **A:** In an emergency: fire, someone badly hurt or not waking up, danger.
- **Q:** What do you tell the operator?
  **A:** Your name, address, and what is happening; stay on the line.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Know your info
*Student:* Memorize your address and a parent's phone number.

*How to teach:* Memorize address and a parent's phone number.

- **Q:** Say your address.
  **A:** Child recites correctly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Do a surprise earthquake drill and recite your address and phone number.

*How to teach:* Surprise drill; recite address and phone number.

- **Q:** When can you stop holding on?
  **A:** When the shaking stops.

**Assessment:** Drill and memorization check.

**Answer key:** Drill: drops to hands and knees, covers head/neck under a sturdy table, holds on until shaking stops, in any room. Memorization: recites full home address (number, street, city) and a parent's phone number correctly. Proficient = correct drill + accurate recall.
