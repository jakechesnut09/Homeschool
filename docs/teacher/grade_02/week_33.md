# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 33

## ELA 2: Winnie-the-Pooh: following characters across chapters

**Prepare:** Get Winnie-the-Pooh (A. A. Milne, public domain in the U.S.) and preview chapters 1–3. Set up a book-club journal (chapter, characters, what happened, favorite part, question).

**Watch for:**
- Describing characters without evidence ('Pooh is silly'). Add 'because in the story he…'
- Missing the humor. Stop and ask 'Why is this funny?' — often the characters misunderstand something.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pooh and the bees
*Student:* Read Chapter 1 of Winnie-the-Pooh (Pooh and the honey bees) and describe Pooh's plan and why it fails. Start a book-club journal page for the chapter.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 1. Describe Pooh's plan to get honey and why it fails. Start a book-club journal page.

- **Q:** What is Pooh's plan to get honey?
  **A:** He rolls in mud to look like a small black rain cloud and floats up to the bees' tree holding a balloon.
- **Q:** Why does the plan fail?
  **A:** The bees are suspicious, so Christopher Robin has to shoot the balloon with his popgun to bring Pooh down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pooh gets stuck
*Student:* Read Chapter 2 (Pooh visits Rabbit and gets stuck) and discuss how Pooh and Rabbit each respond to the problem. Read Pooh's dialogue in his voice.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 2 (Pooh gets stuck at Rabbit's). Discuss how Pooh and Rabbit each respond. Read Pooh's lines in his voice.

- **Q:** Why does Pooh get stuck in Rabbit's door?
  **A:** He ate too much honey and condensed milk and became too fat to fit through.
- **Q:** How does Rabbit respond?
  **A:** He is annoyed but makes the best of it — he uses Pooh's back legs as a towel-horse.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Character traits
*Student:* Make a character-trait web for Pooh, Piglet, Rabbit, Owl, and Eeyore with one example from the text for each.

*How to teach:* Make a character-trait web for Pooh, Piglet, Rabbit, Owl, and Eeyore with text examples.

- **Q:** Give a trait for Eeyore with evidence.
  **A:** Gloomy — he is always sad and expects bad things.
- **Q:** Give a trait for Piglet.
  **A:** Small and nervous (timid) — he worries during the Woozle hunt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hunting the Woozle
*Student:* Read Chapter 3 (Pooh and Piglet hunt a Woozle) and explain the joke. Book club: discuss with a family member, taking turns and asking questions.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 3 (the Woozle hunt) and explain the joke. Discuss with a family member, taking turns and asking questions.

- **Q:** What is the joke in the Woozle chapter?
  **A:** Pooh and Piglet are following their own footprints around the trees, so the 'Woozles' are really them.
- **Q:** Who figures it out?
  **A:** Christopher Robin (and then Pooh realizes).

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 33 review
*Student:* Answer written who/what/why questions on Chapters 1–3 and do a one-minute fluency read of a Pooh passage.

*How to teach:* The child answers written who/what/why questions on chapters 1–3 and does a one-minute fluency read.

- **Q:** Who is Christopher Robin?
  **A:** The boy who is friends with Pooh and helps the animals.
- **Q:** Why does Pooh want to climb the tree in Chapter 1?
  **A:** He hears buzzing and wants honey.

**Assessment:** Book-club journal entries and written comprehension; fluency record.

**Answer key:** Journal entries: one per chapter with characters, main event, and a question. Written comprehension (model): Ch. 1 — Pooh tries to get honey from the bees by floating up with a balloon while covered in mud to look like a little black rain cloud; it fails because the bees are suspicious, and Christopher Robin shoots the balloon to get him down. Ch. 2 — Pooh eats so much honey and condensed milk at Rabbit's house that he gets stuck in the doorway for a week until he is thin enough to be pulled out. Ch. 3 — Pooh and Piglet follow tracks around a spinney, but the tracks are their own. Fluency: record WCPM.

## Math 2: Equal shares: halves, thirds, fourths

**Prepare:** Paper circles and rectangles, scissors, play dough, crackers or tortillas.

**Watch for:**
- Calling any 2 pieces 'halves.' Halves must be equal in size.
- Thinking equal shares must look the same. Show fourths of a square as 4 small squares and as 4 triangles — each is still one-fourth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Halves
*Student:* Fold paper rectangles and circles into halves different ways; decide which folds make equal shares.

*How to teach:* Fold paper rectangles and circles in half different ways (lengthwise, crosswise, diagonally). Decide which folds make equal shares.

- **Q:** Is a rectangle cut into 2 unequal pieces cut into halves?
  **A:** No — halves must be equal.
- **Q:** How many halves make a whole?
  **A:** 2.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Thirds and fourths
*Student:* Cut play-dough 'pizzas' and paper rectangles into thirds and fourths and label each share.

*How to teach:* Cut play-dough pizzas and paper rectangles into thirds and fourths; label each piece.

- **Q:** How many thirds make a whole?
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** If a pizza is cut into 4 equal pieces, what is each piece called?
  **A:** A fourth (a quarter).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Different shapes, same size
*Student:* Cut identical squares into fourths in three different ways and explain why each piece is still a fourth.

*How to teach:* Cut 3 identical squares into fourths different ways (4 small squares, 4 strips, 4 triangles). Explain why each piece is still a fourth.

- **Q:** A square cut into 4 triangles and another into 4 small squares: are all the pieces fourths?
  **A:** Yes — each is 1 of 4 equal parts of the same-size whole.
- **Q:** Do equal shares need to be the same shape?
  **A:** No, only the same size.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Snack shares
*Student:* Share a tortilla or sandwich equally among 2, 3, or 4 people and describe the shares ('a third of the tortilla').

*How to teach:* Share a tortilla or sandwich equally among 2, 3, or 4 people and describe the shares.

- **Q:** If 3 people share a tortilla equally, what does each get?
  **A:** A third of the tortilla.
- **Q:** More people sharing the same tortilla: bigger or smaller pieces?
  **A:** Smaller pieces.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 test
*Student:* Identify shapes, partition rectangles, and show halves, thirds, fourths (12 items).

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 10 test.

- **Q:** Shade half of a rectangle.
  **A:** 1 of 2 equal parts shaded.
- **Q:** How many fourths make a whole?
  **A:** 4.

**Assessment:** Unit 10 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 10 test (suggested 12): 1) 3 sides → triangle 2) 6 sides → hexagon 3) 4 sides → quadrilateral 4) 5 sides → pentagon 5) rectangle in 2 rows of 3 → 6 squares 6) 3 rows of 4 → 12 squares 7) shade half of a circle (1 of 2 equal parts) 8) show thirds of a rectangle (3 equal parts) 9) show fourths of a square (4 equal parts) 10) choose the picture with equal shares (equal-size parts) 11) a pizza cut into 4 equal pieces: each is a fourth 12) 2 halves make a whole. Proficient: 10/12.

## Science 2: Mapping land and water

**Prepare:** Get a California physical map, a blank California outline, colored pencils, and the bird's-eye photo of last week's model.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a map shows everything. Maps use symbols and colors from a key and show land from above.
- Forgetting the compass rose. Maps need N, S, E, W.

### Day 1 (Monday): California map
*Student:* Study a physical map of California and find the Sierra Nevada, Central Valley, Sacramento River, Lake Tahoe, and Pacific coast; mark them on a blank outline.

*How to teach:* Study a physical map of California; mark the Sierra Nevada, Central Valley, Sacramento River, Lake Tahoe, and Pacific coast on a blank outline.

- **Q:** Where are the Sierra Nevada mountains?
  **A:** In eastern California.
- **Q:** What ocean is along California's west coast?
  **A:** The Pacific Ocean.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map key
*Student:* Create a map key with colors and symbols for mountains, valleys, rivers, lakes, and ocean.

*How to teach:* Create a map key with colors and symbols for mountains, valleys, rivers, lakes, and ocean.

- **Q:** What color usually shows water on maps?
  **A:** Blue.
- **Q:** Why does a map need a key?
  **A:** To explain what the colors and symbols mean.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Map the model
*Student:* Use the bird's-eye photo of last week's model to draw a map with the key and a compass rose.

*How to teach:* Use the bird's-eye photo to draw a map of the model with a key and compass rose.

- **Q:** What does a compass rose show?
  **A:** Directions: north, south, east, west.
- **Q:** Why use the photo from above?
  **A:** Maps show land from above.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Neighborhood map
*Student:* Draw a map of your neighborhood or a nearby park showing hills, creeks, ponds, or the coast, with a key.

*How to teach:* Draw a map of your neighborhood or a park with natural features and a key.

- **Q:** What natural features might you include?
  **A:** Hills, creeks, ponds, or the coast.
- **Q:** Where does the key go?
  **A:** In a corner of the map.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Compare the model and the map: what does the map show well and what does the model show well (height)?

*How to teach:* Compare the model and the map: what each shows well.

- **Q:** What does the model show that the map doesn't?
  **A:** Height — how tall mountains are.
- **Q:** What does the map show well?
  **A:** Shapes and where things are.

**Assessment:** Model map and California map with key.

**Answer key:** California map: Sierra Nevada (mountains along the east), Central Valley (middle), Sacramento River (flows south through the northern Central Valley toward San Francisco Bay), Lake Tahoe (on the eastern border with Nevada), Pacific coast (west). Map key with colors/symbols. Model map with key and compass rose. Comparison: map shows shapes and locations well; the model shows height (how tall mountains are).

## People Who Make a Difference: Sally Ride and Ellen Ochoa: reaching for the stars

**Prepare:** NASA photo of Sally Ride on STS-7 (1983); biographies of Sally Ride and Ellen Ochoa; straws, paper and tape for paper rockets.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking astronauts are only pilots: many are scientists and engineers.
- Thinking only boys can become astronauts: Ride and Ochoa showed girls can too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sally Ride
*Student:* Read a biography of Sally Ride, the first American woman in space (1983), who grew up in Los Angeles, and study a NASA photo of her on the shuttle.

*How to teach:* Read about Sally Ride; study the NASA photo.

- **Q:** Who was the first American woman in space?
  **A:** Sally Ride, in 1983.
- **Q:** What was the space shuttle she flew on?
  **A:** Challenger (mission STS-7).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ellen Ochoa
*Student:* Read about Ellen Ochoa from La Mesa, California, the first Hispanic woman in space and later director of Johnson Space Center.

*How to teach:* Read about Ellen Ochoa.

- **Q:** What 'first' did Ellen Ochoa achieve?
  **A:** First Hispanic woman in space (1993).
- **Q:** What job did she have later?
  **A:** Director of NASA's Johnson Space Center.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paper rockets
*Student:* Build and launch straw paper rockets and discuss how Ride and Ochoa used science and engineering.

*How to teach:* Build and launch straw paper rockets.

- **Q:** What is an engineer?
  **A:** Someone who designs and builds things to solve problems.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Inspire others
*Student:* Discuss how Sally Ride started programs to get girls interested in science; write one way you could inspire someone.

*How to teach:* Discuss Sally Ride's science programs for girls; write how you could inspire someone.

- **Q:** How did Sally Ride inspire others?
  **A:** She started programs and wrote books to get kids, especially girls, excited about science.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Add both astronauts to the hero timeline and chart their traits.

*How to teach:* Add both astronauts to the hero timeline and traits chart.

- **Q:** What is NASA?
  **A:** The U.S. space agency.

**Assessment:** Hero-traits chart and timeline entries.

**Answer key:** Traits chart: Sally Ride - courage, curiosity, inspiring others (first American woman in space in 1983 on the shuttle Challenger; physicist from Los Angeles; started Sally Ride Science for kids). Ellen Ochoa - perseverance, intelligence, leadership (from La Mesa, CA; first Hispanic woman in space in 1993; engineer/inventor; flew 4 missions; later director of Johnson Space Center). Timeline entries 1983 and 1993. Proficient = accurate traits and dates.

## Art & Music 2: Compose a song about my community

**Prepare:** Chrome Music Lab Song Maker, recording device, percussion.

**Watch for:**
- Lyrics that don't fit the beat: clap the words first.
- Melodies that jump randomly: use mostly steps with a few leaps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lyrics
*Student:* Write 4 short lines about your community (places, people, sounds).

*How to teach:* Write 4 short lines about your community.

- **Q:** What are lyrics?
  **A:** The words of a song.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rhythm
*Student:* Clap the rhythm of the lyrics and write it in ta and ti-ti notation.

*How to teach:* Clap the rhythm of the lyrics; write in ta and ti-ti.

- **Q:** What rhythm is 'li-brar-y'?
  **A:** Three syllables: e.g., ti-ti ta (two quick then one longer); accept a rhythm that fits how they speak it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Melody
*Student:* Set the words to a sol-mi-la-do melody using Song Maker or a xylophone.

*How to teach:* Set the words to a sol-mi-la-do melody.

- **Q:** Which note is the lowest?
  **A:** Do.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Perform for a family member, get a 'star and wish,' and revise one line or melody spot.

*How to teach:* Perform; get a star and wish; revise.

- **Q:** What did you change?
  **A:** Specific revision.

### Day 5 (Friday): Record
*Student:* Record the revised song and add a percussion ostinato.

*How to teach:* Record the revised song with a percussion ostinato.

- **Q:** What is your ostinato?
  **A:** A short repeated rhythm, e.g., ta ta ti-ti ta.

**Assessment:** Recorded community song with notation.

**Answer key:** Recorded song: 4 lines of lyrics about community; rhythm written in ta/ti-ti; melody uses sol, mi, la, do; revision from feedback; percussion ostinato added. Proficient = all parts present and steady.

## PE 2: Classic playground and backyard games

**Prepare:** Chalk, playground ball, jump rope.

**Watch for:**
- In four square, catching the ball: hit it with open hands after one bounce.
- Thinking only sports count as exercise: active games count.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hopscotch
*Student:* Play hopscotch with correct hopping and jumping and fair turns.

*How to teach:* Play hopscotch with correct hops and jumps.

- **Q:** How do you land in two side-by-side squares?
  **A:** With one foot in each.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Four square
*Student:* Learn and play four square or a two-square version with a partner.

*How to teach:* Learn four square or two square.

- **Q:** How many bounces before you hit it?
  **A:** One bounce in your square.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kickball
*Student:* Play a mini kickball game using kicking, throwing and catching.

*How to teach:* Mini kickball using kicking, throwing and catching.

- **Q:** How do you get a runner out?
  **A:** Catch a kicked ball in the air, tag the runner, or get the ball to the base first (force out).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Activity plan
*Student:* Make a list of 5 games you can play at home or the park and schedule two.

*How to teach:* List 5 games; schedule two.

- **Q:** Name a game that gets your heart pumping.
  **A:** Tag, soccer, jump rope.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play one game from your list and tell why it is fun and good exercise.

*How to teach:* Play one game; tell why it's fun and good exercise.

- **Q:** Why is active play good for you?
  **A:** Strengthens heart and muscles and is fun with others.

**Assessment:** Activity plan and game play.

**Answer key:** Activity plan: list of 5 active games with 2 scheduled (day/time). Game play: plays fairly and explains why it's fun and good exercise (gets heart pumping, uses muscles). Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Community health helpers

**Prepare:** 'Community Helpers' library books; questions for an interview; card paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only doctors help health: nurses, dentists, pharmacists and public health workers do too.
- Fearing checkups: checkups keep you healthy and find problems early.

### Day 1 (Monday): Health helpers
*Student:* Learn about doctors, nurses, dentists, pharmacists and school nurses.

*How to teach:* Learn about doctors, nurses, dentists, pharmacists and school nurses.

- **Q:** What does a pharmacist do?
  **A:** Prepares medicines and explains how to take them safely.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a health helper or family member about their job.

*How to teach:* Interview a health helper or family member about their job.

- **Q:** What question did you ask?
  **A:** Records question and answer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Checkups
*Student:* Learn what happens at a checkup and why vaccines matter.

*How to teach:* Learn what happens at a checkup and why vaccines matter.

- **Q:** What happens at a checkup?
  **A:** Measure height and weight, listen to heart and lungs, check eyes, ears, and give vaccines if needed.
- **Q:** Why do vaccines matter?
  **A:** They protect you and others from serious diseases.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Thank-you card
*Student:* Write a thank-you card to a health helper.

*How to teach:* Write a thank-you card to a health helper.

- **Q:** Why thank health helpers?
  **A:** They work to keep us healthy and safe.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match 6 helpers to their jobs.

*How to teach:* Match 6 helpers to their jobs.

- **Q:** Who cares for your teeth?
  **A:** A dentist.

**Assessment:** Matching and interview notes.

**Answer key:** Matching: doctor - checks health and treats illness; nurse - cares for patients, gives shots, checks vital signs; dentist - cares for teeth; pharmacist - prepares medicine and explains how to take it; school nurse - helps students who are sick or hurt at school; public health worker - protects the health of a whole community (vaccines, clean water, tracking disease). Interview notes record 3 answers. Proficient = 6/6 + notes.
