# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 34

## ELA 2: Winnie-the-Pooh: structure and points of view

**Prepare:** Preview Winnie-the-Pooh chapters 4 and 6. Gather sticky notes, a story-structure chart (beginning/problem, middle, ending/solution), and a list of 30 irregular words (said, laugh, enough, friend, people, because, again, any, many, does, though, through, eye, four, …).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking point of view means the narrator only. Ask how each character felt about the same event.
- Sounding out irregular words letter by letter. Practice them as whole words by sight.

### Day 1 (Monday): Eeyore's tail
*Student:* Read Chapter 4 (Eeyore loses a tail) and discuss how the beginning introduces the problem and the ending solves it.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 4 (Eeyore loses a tail). Discuss how the beginning introduces the problem and the ending solves it.

- **Q:** Where is Eeyore's tail?
  **A:** Owl is using it as a bell-pull on his door.
- **Q:** How is the problem solved?
  **A:** Pooh brings it back and Christopher Robin nails it back on Eeyore.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eeyore's birthday
*Student:* Read Chapter 6 (Eeyore has a birthday) and tell the gift story from Piglet's point of view and then from Eeyore's.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 6 (Eeyore's birthday). Tell the gift story from Piglet's view, then Eeyore's.

- **Q:** What happens to Pooh's gift?
  **A:** He eats the honey on the way, so he gives Eeyore the empty pot — a 'Useful Pot to Keep Things In.'
- **Q:** What happens to Piglet's balloon, and how does Eeyore feel?
  **A:** Piglet falls and pops it; Eeyore is happy because the burst balloon fits in and out of the pot.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Irregular word review
*Student:* Review 30 irregularly spelled words (said, laugh, enough, friend, people, because) with a speed-read game and a spelling self-check.

*How to teach:* Review 30 irregular words with a speed-read game and a spelling self-check.

- **Q:** Spell 'friend' and 'because.'
  **A:** f-r-i-e-n-d; b-e-c-a-u-s-e.
- **Q:** Which part of 'said' is irregular?
  **A:** The ai says short e.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reader's theater
*Student:* Perform a scene from Eeyore's birthday with different voices for Pooh, Piglet, and Eeyore.

*How to teach:* Perform a scene from Eeyore's birthday with voices for Pooh, Piglet, and Eeyore.

- **Q:** How should Eeyore's voice sound?
  **A:** Slow, low, and gloomy.
- **Q:** How should Piglet sound after popping the balloon?
  **A:** Worried and sorry.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 34 review
*Student:* Story-structure chart for one chapter and a written paragraph explaining how two characters felt differently about the same event.

*How to teach:* The child fills a story-structure chart for one chapter and writes a paragraph on how two characters felt differently about the same event.

- **Q:** How did Pooh and Eeyore feel about the empty honey pot?
  **A:** Pooh felt a little embarrassed about eating the honey; Eeyore was delighted with a useful pot.
- **Q:** What does a story ending do?
  **A:** Solves the problem and wraps up the story.

**Assessment:** Story-structure chart and point-of-view paragraph; reader's theater performance.

**Answer key:** Story-structure chart (Chapter 4): Beginning — Eeyore has lost his tail; Middle — Pooh searches and visits Owl; Ending — Pooh sees Owl's bell-pull is Eeyore's tail, and Christopher Robin nails it back on. Point-of-view paragraph (Eeyore's birthday): Piglet feels upset and embarrassed that he popped the balloon; Eeyore is pleased because he can put the burst balloon in and out of his Useful Pot. Rubric 3 = both characters' feelings explained with evidence. Reader's theater: different voices, fluent reading.

## Math 2: Review: number and operations

**Prepare:** Fact cards, base-ten blocks, and the fluency graph from Weeks 1 and 4. Write 12 within-100 and 10 within-1,000 problems.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing facts and making careless errors. Focus on accuracy first, then speed.
- Choosing the wrong operation in two-step problems. Have the child explain what each step finds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fact fluency
*Student:* Take the fluency quiz (40 facts within 20) and compare with the September baseline graph.

*How to teach:* Give the 40-fact quiz and graph the score next to the baseline. Talk about the growth.

- **Q:** 8 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 15.
- **Q:** 16 − 9 = ?
  **A:** 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Within 100
*Student:* Solve 12 mixed problems within 100 and explain one strategy aloud.

*How to teach:* Solve 12 mixed problems within 100; the child explains one strategy aloud.

- **Q:** 54 + 29 = ?
  **A:** 83.
- **Q:** 81 − 37 = ?
  **A:** 44.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Within 1,000
*Student:* Solve 10 mixed problems within 1,000 using quick pictures where needed.

*How to teach:* Solve 10 mixed problems within 1,000, drawing quick pictures where needed.

- **Q:** 345 + 278 = ?
  **A:** 623.
- **Q:** 702 − 456 = ?
  **A:** 246.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Two-step stories
*Student:* Solve 4 two-step word problems and write one of your own.

*How to teach:* Solve 4 two-step stories and write one of your own.

- **Q:** 120 stickers. Gave away 35, bought 50. How many now?
  **A:** 135 (120 − 35 = 85, 85 + 50 = 135).
- **Q:** A farm had 200 eggs, sold 75, then collected 40. How many now?
  **A:** 165 (200 − 75 = 125, 125 + 40 = 165).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a number-skills self-check (place value, fact families, regrouping) and mark two topics to practice in next week's review stations.

*How to teach:* Complete a self-check (place value, fact families, regrouping) and mark two topics to practice next week.

- **Q:** What is the value of the 6 in 562?
  **A:** 60.
- **Q:** Write the fact family for 5, 8, 13.
  **A:** 5 + 8 = 13, 8 + 5 = 13, 13 − 5 = 8, 13 − 8 = 5.

**Assessment:** Fluency quiz and mixed problem review.

**Answer key:** Fluency quiz: score out of 40, graphed beside earlier scores (growth expected; 36+ shows mastery). Mixed problems (suggested): 54 + 29 = 83; 81 − 37 = 44; 345 + 278 = 623; 702 − 456 = 246; two-step: 120 stickers, gave away 35, bought 50 → 135. Proficient: about 85% correct and a clear explanation.

## Science 2: Where water is found: solid and liquid

**Prepare:** Gather a globe or world map, ice cubes, salt, and cups; find a short reading on the Sierra Nevada snowpack. Safety for the taste test: use clean drinking water and a pinch of table salt, tasting a tiny sip.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking most of Earth's water is fresh. Most is salty ocean water; much of the fresh water is frozen.
- Thinking water is only liquid. Water is also solid (ice, snow, glaciers).

### Day 1 (Monday): Water planet
*Student:* Spin a globe and stop it with a finger 20 times, tallying land vs. water; color oceans, ice caps, and large lakes on a world map.

*How to teach:* Spin a globe and stop it with a finger 20 times; tally land vs. water. Color oceans, ice caps, and large lakes.

- **Q:** Did your finger land more on land or water?
  **A:** Water — most of Earth is covered by water (about 70%).
- **Q:** What is the largest ocean?
  **A:** The Pacific Ocean.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Solid and liquid
*Student:* Observe ice melting in a cup, then list where water is solid on Earth (glaciers, snow, ice caps) and where it is liquid (oceans, rivers, lakes).

*How to teach:* Observe ice melting; list where water is solid and where it is liquid on Earth.

- **Q:** Name places where water is solid.
  **A:** Glaciers, ice caps, snow on mountains.
- **Q:** Name places where water is liquid.
  **A:** Oceans, rivers, lakes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sierra snowpack
*Student:* Read about the Sierra Nevada snowpack melting in spring and filling rivers and reservoirs; draw the path of water from snow to faucet.

*How to teach:* Read about the Sierra Nevada snowpack and draw water's path from snow to faucet.

- **Q:** Where does the snowpack's water go in spring?
  **A:** It melts and flows into rivers and reservoirs.
- **Q:** How does it reach your faucet?
  **A:** Through reservoirs, pipes, and water treatment to homes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fresh vs. salt
*Student:* Taste-test (with an adult) a cup of fresh water and a cup of salty water; sort water bodies into fresh and salt on a chart.

*How to teach:* Taste-test fresh and slightly salty water (tiny sip); sort water bodies into fresh and salt.

- **Q:** Is ocean water fresh or salt?
  **A:** Salt.
- **Q:** Is Lake Tahoe fresh or salt?
  **A:** Fresh.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 check
*Student:* Quiz: label landforms and water on a map, and identify where water is solid or liquid on Earth.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz: label landforms and water on a map; identify solid and liquid water.

- **Q:** Is a glacier solid or liquid water?
  **A:** Solid.
- **Q:** Is a river solid or liquid?
  **A:** Liquid.

**Assessment:** Water-sources chart and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Water-sources chart: solid — glaciers, ice caps, snow (Sierra snowpack); liquid — oceans, rivers, lakes, ponds. Salt — oceans, bays; fresh — most lakes, rivers, ponds, snow, glaciers. Unit quiz: landforms and water correctly labeled on a map; solid vs. liquid places identified. Globe tally: usually about 7 of 10 stops land on water (Earth is about 70% water).

## People Who Make a Difference: Heroes in our community

**Prepare:** Interview questions; phone; card supplies; a short biography of Fred Korematsu (e.g., 'Fred Korematsu Speaks Up'). Preview: explain simply that during World War II the government unfairly forced Japanese Americans to leave their homes and live in camps.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heroes must be famous: everyday people who help others are heroes too.
- Thinking Korematsu lost so he failed: decades later his conviction was overturned and he is honored for standing up for fairness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Local heroes
*Student:* Brainstorm people who make a difference in your community (firefighters, librarians, volunteers, coaches, a family member).

*How to teach:* Brainstorm community heroes.

- **Q:** Name a community helper who makes a difference.
  **A:** Firefighter, librarian, volunteer, coach, nurse.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a community helper or volunteer in person or by phone using 5 prepared questions about why they do their work.

*How to teach:* Interview a community helper with 5 questions.

- **Q:** What is a volunteer?
  **A:** Someone who helps others without being paid.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fred Korematsu
*Student:* Read about Fred Korematsu of Oakland, honored on California's Fred Korematsu Day (Jan. 30), who stood up for fairness for Japanese Americans.

*How to teach:* Read about Fred Korematsu.

- **Q:** What did Fred Korematsu stand up against?
  **A:** The unfair order forcing Japanese Americans into camps during World War II.
- **Q:** When is Fred Korematsu Day?
  **A:** January 30.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Be a hero
*Student:* Plan and do one helpful act for your community (pick up litter, donate books, make cards for a senior center).

*How to teach:* Plan and do one helpful act.

- **Q:** What helpful act did you do?
  **A:** Describes act.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a thank-you card to your interviewee naming the trait you admire.

*How to teach:* Write a thank-you card naming an admired trait.

- **Q:** What trait did your helper show?
  **A:** Example: kindness, dedication, generosity.

**Assessment:** Interview notes, service act, and thank-you card.

**Answer key:** Interview notes with 5 questions and answers; one service act completed and described; thank-you card names a trait (e.g., 'Thank you for being generous with your time at the food bank'). Korematsu facts: from Oakland; in 1942 refused the order sending Japanese Americans to camps; his case went to the Supreme Court; conviction overturned in 1983; Presidential Medal of Freedom 1998; Fred Korematsu Day is January 30 in California. Proficient = all.

## Art & Music 2: Preparing art for presentation

**Prepare:** Construction paper or cardstock for mats, tape, clips, index cards for labels.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking curating is picking favorites only: a curator picks works that fit a theme.
- Labels missing information: title, materials, date.

### Day 1 (Monday): Curate
*Student:* Choose 6 artworks for the exhibit under one theme and explain why each belongs.

*How to teach:* Choose 6 artworks for one theme; explain each.

- **Q:** What does curate mean?
  **A:** To choose and organize artworks for an exhibit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mount and mat
*Student:* Compare framing options (mats, mounting on cardstock, hanging with clips) and mount each piece.

*How to teach:* Compare mats, cardstock mounting, clips; mount each.

- **Q:** Why mount art?
  **A:** It looks finished and protects the work.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Labels
*Student:* Make museum-style labels with title, materials, and date.

*How to teach:* Make museum labels: title, materials, date.

- **Q:** What goes on your label?
  **A:** Title, materials, date (and artist).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Artist statement
*Student:* Write a 3-sentence artist statement about yourself and your favorite piece.

*How to teach:* Write a 3-sentence artist statement.

- **Q:** What is an artist statement?
  **A:** A short writing explaining who you are as an artist and your work.

### Day 5 (Friday): Plan the space
*Student:* Plan where each piece will hang in your home gallery and sketch a layout.

*How to teach:* Plan where each piece hangs; sketch a layout.

- **Q:** Why plan the layout?
  **A:** So the exhibit flows and pieces are seen well.

**Assessment:** Mounted artworks with labels and artist statement.

**Answer key:** Six mounted artworks fitting one theme with reasons; labels with title, materials, date; 3-sentence artist statement (who I am as an artist, favorite piece, why/how made). Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Fitness retest

**Prepare:** Fitness log with week 15-16 scores, timer, ruler, graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing scores with others: compare only with your own past scores.
- Thinking goals must be huge: small, specific goals work best.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strength retest
*Student:* Retest push-ups, curl-ups and hang time and record them next to week-15 scores.

*How to teach:* Retest push-ups, curl-ups, hang time; record next to week 15.

- **Q:** Did your push-ups change?
  **A:** Compare numbers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flexibility retest
*Student:* Retest sit-and-reach and record.

*How to teach:* Retest sit-and-reach.

- **Q:** What does sit-and-reach measure?
  **A:** Flexibility.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Endurance retest
*Student:* Walk-jog continuously as long as possible up to 15 minutes; record minutes and heart rate.

*How to teach:* Walk-jog up to 15 minutes; record minutes and heart rate.

- **Q:** If you count 30 beats in 15 seconds, what is your heart rate?
  **A:** 120 beats per minute.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graph progress
*Student:* Make a bar graph comparing fall and spring fitness scores.

*How to teach:* Bar graph comparing fall and spring.

- **Q:** If fall curl-ups were 12 and spring 18, how many more?
  **A:** 6 more.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Set one summer fitness goal based on your graph.

*How to teach:* Set one summer goal.

- **Q:** What makes a good goal?
  **A:** Specific and measurable, with a plan to practice.

**Assessment:** Fitness retest and goal.

**Answer key:** Fitness retest: spring push-ups, curl-ups, hang, sit-and-reach and endurance minutes recorded next to fall. Bar graph with paired fall/spring bars, labeled. Goal is specific and measurable (e.g., 'do 12 push-ups by August'). Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Healthy environment

**Prepare:** Recycling bins, trash bag, gloves, recyclables for a craft.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking recycling is the only 'R': reducing and reusing come first.
- Picking up sharp or unknown litter: wear gloves; adults handle sharp objects.

### Day 1 (Monday): Three Rs
*Student:* Sort household items into reduce, reuse, recycle and trash.

*How to teach:* Sort household items into reduce, reuse, recycle, trash.

- **Q:** What's the difference between reuse and recycle?
  **A:** Reuse: use again as it is; recycle: make into something new.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Clean air & water
*Student:* Discuss how pollution affects health.

*How to teach:* Discuss how pollution affects health.

- **Q:** How can air pollution affect people?
  **A:** It can make breathing harder and cause coughing or asthma.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Neighborhood cleanup
*Student:* Do a park or street cleanup with gloves and an adult.

*How to teach:* Do a cleanup with gloves and an adult.

- **Q:** Why wear gloves?
  **A:** To protect hands from germs and sharp things.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reuse craft
*Student:* Make something useful from recyclables.

*How to teach:* Make something useful from recyclables.

- **Q:** What did you make?
  **A:** Describes project.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how a clean environment helps people stay healthy.

*How to teach:* Explain how a clean environment helps health.

- **Q:** What is pollution?
  **A:** Harmful waste in air, water or land.

**Assessment:** Cleanup reflection.

**Answer key:** Cleanup reflection: describes what was collected and explains that a clean environment keeps air and water clean, prevents injuries and germs, and helps people stay healthy. Three Rs sort correct (reduce = use less, e.g., reusable bottle; reuse = use again, e.g., jar as pencil cup; recycle = paper, cans, plastic bottles). Proficient = all.
