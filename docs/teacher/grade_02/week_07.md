# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 7

## ELA 2: Folktales from many cultures and character voices

**Prepare:** Get The Little Red Hen (any version) and Stone Soup (Marcia Brown or Jon J Muth). Gather paper bags, markers, and glue for puppets, and long-o/long-i word cards (boat, snow, toe, night, pie, fly).

**Watch for:**
- Reading dialogue in the same flat voice. Point to quotation marks and ask 'Who is talking? How would they sound?'
- Writing the lesson as a plot event. The central message is what the story teaches: 'Sharing makes everyone better off.'

### Day 1 (Monday): The Little Red Hen
*Student:* Read The Little Red Hen and discuss why the hen would not share the bread; decide if that was fair. Phonics: oa/ow/oe words (boat, snow, toe).

*How to teach:* Read The Little Red Hen. Ask why the hen would not share the bread and whether it was fair. Read oa/ow/oe words.

- **Q:** Who helped the Little Red Hen plant, cut, and bake the wheat?
  **A:** No one — the dog, cat, and duck each said 'Not I,' so she did it all herself.
- **Q:** Why didn't the hen share the bread?
  **A:** The others refused to help with any of the work, so she ate it herself (with her chicks).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stone Soup
*Student:* Read Stone Soup and compare its lesson about sharing with The Little Red Hen. Phonics: igh/ie/y words (night, pie, fly) in a sentence-building game.

*How to teach:* Read Stone Soup. Compare its lesson about sharing with the hen's story. Play a sentence-building game with igh/ie/y words.

- **Q:** How do the travelers get the villagers to share food?
  **A:** They say they will make soup from stones; then they suggest the soup would be even better with a carrot or cabbage, and each villager adds something until there is a feast.
- **Q:** How are the lessons of The Little Red Hen and Stone Soup different?
  **A:** The Hen teaches that people who don't help don't share the reward; Stone Soup teaches that when everyone shares a little, everyone has plenty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Voices for characters
*Student:* Practice reading Little Red Hen dialogue using a different voice for the hen, dog, cat, and duck. Make paper-bag puppets of each character.

*How to teach:* Read the hen's story dialogue using a different voice for the hen, dog, cat, and duck. Make paper-bag puppets.

- **Q:** How might the hen's voice sound at the end?
  **A:** Firm or proud: 'Then I will eat it myself!'
- **Q:** What do quotation marks tell a reader?
  **A:** Which words a character says out loud.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Retell in writing
*Student:* Write a 5-sentence retelling of Stone Soup with a beginning, middle, and end and the lesson in the last sentence. Underline time-order words.

*How to teach:* The child writes a 5-sentence retelling of Stone Soup with beginning, middle, end, and the lesson last, then underlines time-order words.

- **Q:** What happens at the beginning of Stone Soup?
  **A:** Hungry travelers come to a village where people hide their food.
- **Q:** Write the lesson as a last sentence.
  **A:** Example: When everyone shared a little, there was a feast for all.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 7 review
*Student:* Perform The Little Red Hen as a puppet show using character voices. Spelling check of 12 long-o and long-i words and a written central-message question.

*How to teach:* Perform The Little Red Hen puppet show. Give the 12-word spelling check and ask the child to write the central message.

- **Q:** What is the central message of The Little Red Hen?
  **A:** If you don't help with the work, you shouldn't expect to share the reward.
- **Q:** Spell 'night' and 'boat.'
  **A:** n-i-g-h-t; b-o-a-t.

**Assessment:** Puppet-show performance (voice and fluency), spelling check, and written central message.

**Answer key:** Puppet show rubric: 3 = different voices for hen, dog, cat, and duck, reads fluently with few errors; 2 = some voice changes, some hesitations; 1 = flat or many errors. Spelling (suggested 12): boat, coat, road, snow, grow, toe, night, light, pie, tie, fly, sky. Central message: The Little Red Hen — those who don't help with the work shouldn't expect to share the reward; Stone Soup — when everyone shares a little, there is plenty for all.

## Math 2: Reading and writing three-digit numbers

**Prepare:** Make place-value arrow cards (hundreds 100–900, tens 10–90, ones 1–9 on strips that stack), and write a number-word list (one–nineteen, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty, sixty, seventy, eighty, ninety, hundred).

**Watch for:**
- Writing expanded form as 4 + 2 + 7 for 427. Use arrow cards to show the 4 is really 400 and the 2 is really 20.
- Writing 'six hundred thirty-two' as 600302. Stack arrow cards 600, 30, and 2 so they overlap into 632.
- Spelling 'forty' as 'fourty.' Point out forty has no u, while four and fourteen do.

### Day 1 (Monday): Expanded form
*Student:* Use place-value arrow cards to show 427 = 400 + 20 + 7 and write expanded form for 10 numbers.

*How to teach:* Lay arrow cards 400, 20, and 7 side by side, then stack them so they show 427. The child writes 427 = 400 + 20 + 7 and does 10 more numbers.

- **Q:** Write 538 in expanded form.
  **A:** 500 + 30 + 8.
- **Q:** Write 604 in expanded form.
  **A:** 600 + 4 (or 600 + 0 + 4).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Number words
*Student:* Practice spelling number words (twenty, forty, hundred) and write 8 numbers in word form (six hundred thirty-two).

*How to teach:* Practice spelling tricky number words (twenty, forty, ninety, hundred). Model writing 632 as 'six hundred thirty-two' — a hyphen joins the tens and ones words. The child writes 8 numbers in words.

- **Q:** Write 632 in words.
  **A:** six hundred thirty-two.
- **Q:** Write 450 in words.
  **A:** four hundred fifty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Many ways
*Student:* Show 245 four ways: blocks, quick picture, expanded form, and as 24 tens 5 ones.

*How to teach:* Show 245 four ways: blocks, quick picture, expanded form (200 + 40 + 5), and 24 tens 5 ones (trade each hundred for 10 tens). Ask why 24 tens and 2 hundreds 4 tens are the same amount.

- **Q:** Show 245 as tens and ones.
  **A:** 24 tens and 5 ones (2 hundreds = 20 tens, plus 4 tens = 24 tens).
- **Q:** How many tens are in 360?
  **A:** 36 tens.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Number riddles
*Student:* Solve riddles ('I have 3 hundreds, 0 tens, and 9 ones') and write 3 riddles for a family member.

*How to teach:* Read riddles aloud and let the child build the answer: 'I have 3 hundreds, 0 tens, and 9 ones' (309). The child writes 3 riddles for a family member and checks the answers.

- **Q:** I have 3 hundreds, 0 tens, and 9 ones. What number am I?
  **A:** 309.
- **Q:** I have 7 ones, 5 hundreds, and 2 tens. What number am I?
  **A:** 527.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Convert 12 numbers among standard, word, and expanded forms.

*How to teach:* Give the 12-item conversion quiz. The child reads each item aloud first. Have them build any missed number with arrow cards to fix it.

- **Q:** Write 'nine hundred fifty-six' in standard form.
  **A:** 956.
- **Q:** Write 173 in expanded form.
  **A:** 100 + 70 + 3.

**Assessment:** Form-conversion quiz (12 items).

**Answer key:** Form-conversion quiz (suggested 12 items with answers): 1) 427 → 400 + 20 + 7 2) 815 → eight hundred fifteen 3) 300 + 60 + 2 → 362 4) five hundred nine → 509 5) 740 → 700 + 40 6) nine hundred ninety → 990 7) 200 + 8 → 208 8) 631 → six hundred thirty-one 9) four hundred forty-four → 400 + 40 + 4 10) 100 + 70 + 3 → 173 11) 956 → nine hundred fifty-six 12) seven hundred twenty → 720. Proficient: 10/12 (minor spelling errors OK if the number is right).

## Science 2: Materials unit project: build a better umbrella

**Prepare:** Gather craft sticks, straws, foil, a plastic bag, fabric, tape, a spray bottle, a small toy, and the Unit 2 data. Prepare a short properties quiz (8 items).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing materials by looks. Use test data: which materials were waterproof and strong?
- Changing several things when improving. Change one part, then retest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define the problem
*Student:* Design a mini umbrella to keep a small toy dry; list criteria (waterproof, sturdy, light).

*How to teach:* Define the problem: design a mini umbrella to keep a toy dry. List criteria: waterproof, sturdy, light.

- **Q:** What are criteria?
  **A:** What the design must do to be successful.
- **Q:** Why must the umbrella be waterproof?
  **A:** To keep water from getting through to the toy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choose materials
*Student:* Use data from earlier tests to choose materials and sketch the design with labels.

*How to teach:* Use earlier test data to choose materials and sketch a labeled design.

- **Q:** Which canopy material does your data support?
  **A:** Plastic bag or foil — they were waterproof.
- **Q:** Which material could make a sturdy handle?
  **A:** A craft stick or straw.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build the umbrella and test it under a spray bottle for 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* Build the umbrella and test it under a spray bottle for 30 seconds.

- **Q:** Why spray for exactly 30 seconds?
  **A:** To test fairly every time.
- **Q:** How do you know if it worked?
  **A:** The toy stays dry.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Improve one part and retest; record how the change affected results.

*How to teach:* Improve one part and retest; record how the change affected results.

- **Q:** If water dripped off the edges onto the toy, what could you change?
  **A:** Make the canopy wider.
- **Q:** Why change only one part?
  **A:** So you know which change made the difference.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Present the umbrella and explain material choices; complete a short quiz on classifying materials by properties.

*How to teach:* Present the umbrella and explain material choices; give the properties quiz.

- **Q:** Why is fabric a poor choice for the canopy?
  **A:** Fabric can absorb water and let it soak through.
- **Q:** Name a property of foil.
  **A:** Waterproof (also shiny and bendy).

**Assessment:** Umbrella design presentation and properties quiz.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric (3 = proficient): explains criteria (waterproof, sturdy, light), names materials and cites earlier test data (e.g., 'plastic was waterproof in our drip test'), reports spray-test result and one improvement. Properties quiz (suggested 8): 1) foil — waterproof, shiny, bendy 2) sponge — absorbent, soft 3) craft stick — stiff, hard (wood) 4) rubber band — stretchy 5) glass — hard, see-through 6) fabric — soft, bendy 7) plastic wrap — waterproof, see-through 8) which is best for a raincoat? plastic.

## People Who Make a Difference: Letter-number grids in my neighborhood

**Prepare:** Grid paper; a printed online map of your neighborhood with a letter-number grid drawn on; colored pencils; a second community map for the quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Naming the number before the letter: say the letter (column) first, then the number (row), like C3.
- Confusing relative and absolute location: relative compares to other places ('next to the park'); absolute is exact (an address or grid square).

### Day 1 (Monday): Grid game
*Student:* Play 'grid battleship' on a 5x5 letter-number grid to practice naming squares like C3.

*How to teach:* Play 'grid battleship' on a 5x5 grid naming squares like C3.

- **Q:** In C3, what does C stand for?
  **A:** The column (letter).
- **Q:** What does 3 stand for?
  **A:** The row (number).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Neighborhood map
*Student:* Look at a printed map of your neighborhood with a grid overlay and find home, school, park, and store by grid square.

*How to teach:* Find home, school, park and store by grid square on a neighborhood map.

- **Q:** What is a grid?
  **A:** Lines that cross to make squares so you can find places.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Relative location
*Student:* Describe places using relative location ('The library is next to the park, north of my house').

*How to teach:* Describe places using relative location.

- **Q:** Describe where your house is using relative location.
  **A:** Example: 'My house is across from the park, south of the school.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draw a grid map
*Student:* Draw your own neighborhood map on grid paper with a key and list the grid square of 6 places.

*How to teach:* Draw your own grid map with a key; list grid squares of 6 places.

- **Q:** What is absolute location?
  **A:** The exact spot, like an address or grid square.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Answer 8 grid-location questions on a new community map.

*How to teach:* Answer 8 grid questions on a new map.

- **Q:** What is in square B2?
  **A:** Answer from map.

**Assessment:** Grid neighborhood map and location quiz.

**Answer key:** Grid map: neighborhood drawn on grid paper with a key and 6 places listed with correct grid squares. Quiz: 8 grid-location answers matching your map (check each letter-number pair). Proficient = 7-8 correct + complete map.

## Art & Music 2: Body percussion and rhythm ostinatos

**Prepare:** Pots, buckets and wooden spoons; a 'We Will Rock You'-style stomp-stomp-clap beat clip.

**Watch for:**
- Stopping the ostinato when someone else sings: an ostinato keeps repeating underneath.
- Improvising longer than 4 beats: answers match the question's length.

### Day 1 (Monday): Body percussion
*Student:* Learn four body-percussion sounds (stomp, pat, clap, snap) and echo 10 four-beat patterns led by a parent or video.

*How to teach:* Learn stomp, pat, clap, snap; echo 10 four-beat patterns.

- **Q:** Name four body-percussion sounds.
  **A:** Stomp, pat, clap, snap.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ostinato
*Student:* Perform a repeated rhythm ostinato while a parent sings 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat.'

*How to teach:* Perform a repeated ostinato while a parent sings 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat.'

- **Q:** What is an ostinato?
  **A:** A short pattern repeated over and over.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bucket drumming
*Student:* Use a bucket or pot to play two layered rhythms with a family member.

*How to teach:* Play two layered rhythms on bucket/pot with a family member.

- **Q:** What does 'layer' mean in music?
  **A:** Two or more parts played at the same time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improvise
*Student:* Improvise 4-beat answers to rhythm 'questions' clapped by a parent.

*How to teach:* Clap 4-beat 'questions'; child improvises 4-beat 'answers.'

- **Q:** What is improvising?
  **A:** Making up music on the spot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a layered body-percussion piece (stomp-pat-clap-snap ostinato under a chanted rhyme) for the family and keep a steady beat throughout.

*How to teach:* Perform the stomp-pat-clap-snap ostinato under a chanted rhyme.

- **Q:** What has to stay steady when the parts layer?
  **A:** The beat.

**Assessment:** Body-percussion performance.

**Answer key:** Performance: plays a steady stomp-pat-clap-snap ostinato under a chanted rhyme without stopping or speeding up. Proficient = steady throughout and pattern repeats correctly; developing = 1-2 slips; beginning = can't keep the pattern.

## PE 2: Jumping for distance and height

**Prepare:** Tape measure, sidewalk chalk, sticky notes; a wall.

**Watch for:**
- Not using arms: swinging the arms adds distance and height.
- Landing on toes and falling back: land on two feet with knees bent, leaning forward.

### Day 1 (Monday): Swing and spring
*Student:* Practice 'swing arms back, bend knees, swing forward, jump' for standing long jumps; mark each landing with chalk.

*How to teach:* Teach 'swing arms back, bend knees, swing forward, jump.' Mark each landing with chalk.

- **Q:** What do your arms do in a long jump?
  **A:** Swing back, then forward hard as you jump.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure it
*Student:* Do 5 standing long jumps, measure the best one in inches, and record it.

*How to teach:* Do 5 jumps; measure the best in inches from the start line to the back of the heels.

- **Q:** Where do you measure to?
  **A:** To the back of the heel closest to the start line.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reach high
*Student:* Stand by a wall and jump to tap a sticky note; move the note up when you reach it 3 times.

*How to teach:* Jump by a wall to tap a sticky note; move it up after 3 successes.

- **Q:** How do you jump higher?
  **A:** Bend knees, swing arms up and push off hard.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Jump patterns
*Student:* Jump a chalk hopscotch and a 'jump ladder' (two feet, one foot, side to side).

*How to teach:* Jump hopscotch and a chalk jump ladder (two feet, one foot, side to side).

- **Q:** What is the difference between a hop and a jump?
  **A:** A hop is on one foot; a jump uses two feet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Retest the long jump and wall jump, compare with Day 2 and explain how practice helped.

*How to teach:* Retest; compare with Day 2.

- **Q:** Did practice help? How?
  **A:** Yes, by improving the arm swing and knee bend.

**Assessment:** Recorded jump scores and oral explanation of cues and practice.

**Answer key:** Recorded scores: best standing long jump in inches on Day 2 and Day 5; wall-jump height. Oral cues: swing arms back, bend knees, swing forward and jump, land on two feet softly. Explanation: practice improves timing and power. Proficient = both scores recorded + all cues.

## Health 2: Healthy teeth

**Prepare:** Mirror, toothbrush, floss; 3 hard-boiled eggs, cups of water, soda and vinegar (start Day 2 and leave overnight).

**Watch for:**
- Brushing for only a few seconds: brush 2 minutes, twice a day.
- Thinking only candy causes cavities: sugary drinks and sticky snacks also feed plaque bacteria.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tooth parts
*Student:* Learn tooth parts (enamel, gum) and how plaque causes cavities; count your teeth in a mirror.

*How to teach:* Teach enamel (hard outer layer) and gums; plaque (sticky germ film) makes acid that causes cavities. Count teeth in a mirror.

- **Q:** What is plaque?
  **A:** A sticky film of germs on teeth that can cause cavities.
- **Q:** What is enamel?
  **A:** The hard outer covering of a tooth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg experiment
*Student:* Soak hard-boiled eggs in water, soda and vinegar overnight to model how drinks affect enamel.

*How to teach:* Soak eggs overnight in water, soda and vinegar; predict what will happen.

- **Q:** What does the eggshell stand for?
  **A:** Tooth enamel.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Check the eggs
*Student:* Observe and record the eggs; discuss why limiting sugary drinks helps teeth.

*How to teach:* Observe and record the eggs; discuss limiting sugary drinks.

- **Q:** What happened to the egg in vinegar?
  **A:** It bubbled and the shell got soft or dissolved.
- **Q:** What happened to the egg in soda?
  **A:** It turned brown/stained.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Brush & floss
*Student:* Practice 2-minute brushing in circles and flossing with a parent; start a brushing chart.

*How to teach:* Practice 2-minute brushing in small circles and flossing; start a brushing chart.

- **Q:** How long and how often should you brush?
  **A:** 2 minutes, twice a day.
- **Q:** Why floss?
  **A:** To clean between teeth where a brush can't reach.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain the egg experiment results and demonstrate proper brushing.

*How to teach:* Explain egg results; demonstrate brushing.

- **Q:** What is a cavity?
  **A:** A hole in a tooth caused by decay.

**Assessment:** Experiment record and brushing demonstration.

**Answer key:** Experiment record: water egg - little or no change; soda egg - stained brown, shell may feel softer; vinegar egg - bubbles formed, shell softened or dissolved. Conclusion: acidic and sugary drinks can stain and weaken enamel, like eggshells. Brushing demo: small circles on all surfaces, along gums, 2 minutes, plus flossing between teeth. Proficient = accurate results + correct brushing.
