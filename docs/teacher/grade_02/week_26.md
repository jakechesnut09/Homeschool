# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 26

## ELA 2: Biographies of courage: Rosa Parks and Jackie Robinson

**Prepare:** Get I Am Rosa Parks and I Am Jackie Robinson (Brad Meltzer). Make a contractions chart and a research folder. Plan a library trip to find 2 sources on a chosen person.

**Watch for:**
- Putting the apostrophe where letters were not removed (did'nt). The apostrophe replaces the missing letter: didn't (did not).
- Research questions that can be answered yes/no. Start with when, where, what, how, why.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rosa Parks
*Student:* Read I Am Rosa Parks and discuss why her choice on the bus mattered. Answer how and why questions in complete sentences.

*How to teach:* Read I Am Rosa Parks and discuss why her choice on the bus mattered. Answer how/why questions in complete sentences.

- **Q:** What did Rosa Parks refuse to do in 1955?
  **A:** Give up her bus seat to a white passenger in Montgomery, Alabama.
- **Q:** Why did her choice matter?
  **A:** It sparked the Montgomery bus boycott, which helped end segregation on buses.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jackie Robinson
*Student:* Read I Am Jackie Robinson and compare the courage of Jackie and Rosa. Grammar: contractions with apostrophes (can't, didn't, he's, they're).

*How to teach:* Read I Am Jackie Robinson and compare his courage with Rosa's. Teach contractions.

- **Q:** What barrier did Jackie Robinson break in 1947?
  **A:** He became the first Black player in modern Major League Baseball (with the Brooklyn Dodgers).
- **Q:** How was Jackie's courage like Rosa's?
  **A:** Both faced unfair treatment calmly and bravely without fighting back, and helped change things.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Choose a person
*Student:* Choose a person who made a difference to research (e.g., Cesar Chavez, Sally Ride, Abraham Lincoln, Helen Keller, Marie Curie). Find 2 books or passages at the library.

*How to teach:* Choose a person to research (e.g., Cesar Chavez, Sally Ride, Abraham Lincoln, Helen Keller, Marie Curie) and find 2 sources.

- **Q:** Why use 2 sources?
  **A:** To find more facts and check that facts agree.
- **Q:** What did Sally Ride do?
  **A:** She was the first American woman to fly in space (1983).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Research questions
*Student:* Write 3 research questions (When and where was this person born? What problem did they work on? How did they change things?). Start notes from the first source.

*How to teach:* Write 3 research questions and start notes from the first source.

- **Q:** Write a research question about Helen Keller.
  **A:** Example: How did Helen Keller learn to communicate?
- **Q:** Turn into a note: 'Abraham Lincoln was born in a log cabin in Kentucky in 1809.'
  **A:** born 1809, log cabin, Kentucky.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 26 review
*Student:* Contractions quiz (12 items), read-aloud fluency check on a biography page, and research notes check.

*How to teach:* Give the contractions quiz, a read-aloud fluency check, and a research-notes check.

- **Q:** What two words make 'they're'?
  **A:** they are.
- **Q:** Write the contraction for 'did not.'
  **A:** didn't.

**Assessment:** Contractions quiz, fluency record, research question sheet and notes.

**Answer key:** Contractions quiz (12): can't (cannot), didn't (did not), he's (he is), they're (they are), I'm (I am), we'll (we will), isn't (is not), don't (do not), it's (it is), you're (you are), wasn't (was not), she'll (she will). Fluency: record WCPM on a biography page. Research sheet: 3 open questions written; notes are key words with the source title.

## Math 2: Number line diagrams

**Prepare:** Measuring tape and a roll of adding-machine tape (or taped-together paper strips) and markers. Prepare 4 partly labeled number lines and 6 problems for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Counting tick marks instead of spaces (jumps). Each jump is the distance between marks; start at 0 with no jump counted yet.
- Assuming every number line counts by 1. Look at two labeled marks and find the jump size first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ruler to number line
*Student:* Compare a measuring tape and a number line; make a 0–100 number line on adding-machine tape with tick marks every 5.

*How to teach:* Lay the measuring tape beside a number line: both start at 0 with equal spaces. On adding-machine tape, mark 0–100 with tick marks every 5 and label them.

- **Q:** How is a number line like a ruler?
  **A:** Both start at 0 and have equal spaces between numbers.
- **Q:** If marks go by 5s, what comes after 35?
  **A:** 40.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Show lengths
*Student:* Mark numbers as lengths from 0 (e.g., 35) and show 35 + 20 and 35 − 12 with jumps.

*How to teach:* Show 35 as a length from 0 to 35. Then jump +20 to 55, and from 35 jump −12 to 23.

- **Q:** Show 35 + 20.
  **A:** Start at 35, jump 20 to land on 55.
- **Q:** Show 35 − 12.
  **A:** Start at 35, jump back 10 to 25, back 2 more to 23.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Equal intervals
*Student:* Fill in missing numbers on number lines counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s.

*How to teach:* Fill missing numbers on number lines counting by 2s, 5s, and 10s; find the jump size first.

- **Q:** Fill in: 12, 14, __, 18.
  **A:** 16 (counting by 2s).
- **Q:** Fill in: 40, 50, __, 70.
  **A:** 60.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Solve on lines
*Student:* Solve 8 addition and subtraction problems within 100 on open number lines.

*How to teach:* Solve 8 problems on open number lines, labeling jumps.

- **Q:** 48 + 25 on a number line.
  **A:** 73 (48 + 20 = 68, + 5 = 73).
- **Q:** 90 − 34 on a number line.
  **A:** 56 (90 − 30 = 60, − 4 = 56).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: label 4 number lines and solve 6 problems with number-line jumps.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz: label 4 number lines and solve 6 problems with jumps.

- **Q:** 27 + 46 = ?
  **A:** 73.
- **Q:** 63 − 28 = ?
  **A:** 35.

**Assessment:** Number line quiz.

**Answer key:** Number line quiz (suggested): label missing numbers — 1) 0, 5, __, 15, __ → 10, 20 2) 40, 50, __, 70 → 60 3) 12, 14, __, 18 → 16 4) 75, 80, __, 90 → 85. Problems on number lines: a) 35 + 20 = 55 b) 35 − 12 = 23 c) 48 + 25 = 73 d) 90 − 34 = 56 e) 27 + 46 = 73 f) 63 − 28 = 35. Proficient: 9/10 with jumps shown.

## Science 2: Comparing fast and slow changes on a timeline

**Prepare:** Make 12 picture cards (flood, eruption, canyon, earthquake, sand dune, landslide, glacier valley, rockfall, wave-worn cliff, river delta, mountain wearing down, mudslide). Get a long paper strip and two short sources on Yosemite Valley (glaciers and rockfalls).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a flood is slow. Floods happen in hours to days.
- Placing events on the timeline by size, not time. Ask 'How long did it take?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Sort events
*Student:* Sort 12 picture cards (flood, eruption, canyon, earthquake, sand dune, landslide, glacier valley) into fast and slow and justify 3 choices aloud.

*How to teach:* Sort 12 cards into fast and slow and justify 3 aloud.

- **Q:** Is a canyon fast or slow?
  **A:** Slow — it forms over millions of years.
- **Q:** Is a landslide fast or slow?
  **A:** Fast.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Time scale strip
*Student:* Make a long paper timeline with marks for seconds, hours, days, years, and millions of years, and place each event card where it belongs.

*How to teach:* Make a timeline strip with seconds, hours, days, years, and millions of years; place each card.

- **Q:** Where does an earthquake go?
  **A:** Seconds.
- **Q:** Where does a glacier valley go?
  **A:** Thousands to millions of years.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Yosemite Valley
*Student:* Read two short sources about how glaciers carved Yosemite Valley slowly and how a rockfall can happen in seconds; list evidence from each.

*How to teach:* Read two sources about Yosemite Valley: glaciers carved it slowly; rockfalls happen in seconds.

- **Q:** How did glaciers shape Yosemite Valley?
  **A:** Huge, slow-moving ice scraped and carved the valley over a very long time.
- **Q:** How fast is a rockfall?
  **A:** Seconds.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare in writing
*Student:* Write a paragraph comparing one fast change and one slow change: what happened, how long it took, and how you know.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph comparing one fast and one slow change.

- **Q:** Name a fast and a slow change in Yosemite.
  **A:** Fast: rockfall. Slow: glaciers carving the valley.
- **Q:** How do you know?
  **A:** From the sources (name them).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Read the paragraph aloud and answer a family member's questions; check that each claim names a source.

*How to teach:* Read the paragraph aloud, answer questions, and check each claim names a source.

- **Q:** Why name a source?
  **A:** To show where your evidence came from.
- **Q:** Is a flood fast or slow?
  **A:** Fast (hours to days).

**Assessment:** Time-scale strip and comparison paragraph.

**Answer key:** Sort: fast — flood, eruption, earthquake, landslide, rockfall, mudslide; slow — canyon, sand dune, glacier valley, wave-worn cliff, river delta, mountain wearing down. Timeline: seconds (earthquake, rockfall), minutes–hours (landslide, eruption), days (flood), years (sand dune moving), millions of years (canyon, glacier valley). Paragraph (3 = proficient): compares one fast and one slow change with what happened, how long, and how we know (source named).

## People Who Make a Difference: What makes a hero? Abraham Lincoln

**Prepare:** Picture-book biography of Abraham Lincoln (library); copies of Grace Bedell's 1860 letter and Lincoln's reply (available online from library and archive collections); a penny and five-dollar bill; Lincoln Memorial photo.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heroes are only superheroes or famous people: heroes are people who show traits like courage and kindness to help others.
- Thinking Lincoln grew up rich and went to fancy schools: he grew up poor and mostly taught himself.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is a hero
*Student:* List people you admire and the traits that make them heroes (courage, honesty, kindness, perseverance); make a 'hero traits' chart to use all unit.

*How to teach:* List admired people and their traits; make a hero-traits chart.

- **Q:** What is a character trait?
  **A:** A quality that describes how a person acts, like honest or brave.
- **Q:** Name two hero traits.
  **A:** Courage, honesty, kindness, perseverance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lincoln's life
*Student:* Read a picture-book biography of Abraham Lincoln and put 5 events on a timeline (log cabin, self-taught reader, lawyer, president, ended slavery).

*How to teach:* Read a Lincoln biography; put 5 events on a timeline.

- **Q:** Where was Lincoln born?
  **A:** In a log cabin in Kentucky.
- **Q:** What number president was Lincoln?
  **A:** The 16th president.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grace Bedell's letter
*Student:* Read the 1860 letter from 11-year-old Grace Bedell suggesting Lincoln grow a beard and Lincoln's reply; discuss what it shows about him.

*How to teach:* Read Grace Bedell's letter (she suggested a beard because his face was thin) and Lincoln's reply.

- **Q:** How old was Grace Bedell?
  **A:** 11 years old.
- **Q:** What does it show that Lincoln answered her?
  **A:** He listened to ordinary people, even children.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lincoln's legacy
*Student:* Look at Lincoln on the penny and five-dollar bill and the Lincoln Memorial photo; discuss why we still honor him and which traits he showed.

*How to teach:* Look at Lincoln on the penny, $5 bill and Lincoln Memorial; discuss traits.

- **Q:** Why is Lincoln honored?
  **A:** He kept the nation together during the Civil War and worked to end slavery.
- **Q:** What nickname shows a trait?
  **A:** 'Honest Abe' (honesty).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a letter to a leader, as Grace did, giving one idea and a reason.

*How to teach:* Write a letter to a leader with one idea and a reason.

- **Q:** What parts does a letter need?
  **A:** Date, greeting, body, closing, signature.

**Assessment:** Lincoln timeline and letter to a leader.

**Answer key:** Timeline in order: born in a log cabin in Kentucky (1809) → taught himself to read → became a lawyer in Illinois → elected 16th president (1860) → led the nation in the Civil War and worked to end slavery (Emancipation Proclamation 1863, 13th Amendment). Letter to a leader: greeting, one idea, a reason, closing and signature. Proficient = correct order + complete letter.

## Art & Music 2: Gyotaku fish printing (Japan)

**Prepare:** Rubber fish replica (or a fish from the market), washable tempera and brush, newsprint or rice paper; museum images of gyotaku.

**Watch for:**
- Using thick paint: a thin layer shows scale detail; thick paint blobs.
- Thinking gyotaku started as art: it started as a fisherman's record.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gyotaku history
*Student:* Learn that Japanese fishermen in the 1800s made gyotaku ('fish rubbing') prints to record the size of their catches, and look at museum examples.

*How to teach:* Learn gyotaku history; look at examples.

- **Q:** What does 'gyotaku' mean?
  **A:** Fish rubbing (gyo = fish, taku = rubbing).
- **Q:** Why did fishermen make them?
  **A:** To record the size of fish they caught.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): First prints
*Student:* Brush a thin layer of paint on a rubber fish, lay paper over it, and rub gently from head to tail; make 2 prints and compare which shows more scale texture.

*How to teach:* Brush thin paint on the fish; lay paper; rub gently head to tail; make 2 prints and compare.

- **Q:** Which print shows more scales?
  **A:** Usually the one with a thinner, even layer of paint.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Color experiments
*Student:* Make 3 more prints using different paint colors and paper, noting which combination shows the most detail.

*How to teach:* Make 3 more prints with different colors and paper.

- **Q:** Which combination showed the most detail?
  **A:** Records observations.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish the best print
*Student:* Choose the best print, paint in the eye with a fine brush, and add a name stamp or signature as Japanese printmakers do.

*How to teach:* Choose the best print; paint in the eye; sign it.

- **Q:** Why paint the eye afterward?
  **A:** The eye doesn't print clearly, so artists paint it in.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Explain in 3 sentences how gyotaku started as a record and became art, and how it connects to the texture rubbings from Unit 5.

*How to teach:* Write 3 sentences connecting history, art and Unit 5 rubbings.

- **Q:** How is gyotaku like a crayon rubbing?
  **A:** Both transfer the real texture of an object onto paper.

**Assessment:** Finished gyotaku print and written reflection.

**Answer key:** Print: clear fish shape with visible scale texture, eye painted in, signature. Reflection (3 sentences) e.g.: 'Japanese fishermen in the 1800s printed fish to record their size. People saw the prints were beautiful, so gyotaku became an art. Like my rubbings, it shows texture by transferring it onto paper.' Proficient = good detail + all three ideas.

## PE 2: Paddle striking

**Prepare:** Paper plates, paint sticks, tape; balloons, foam ball, wall.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging the paddle wildly: short, controlled hits.
- Tilting the paddle: keep the face flat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Make a paddle
*Student:* Make a paddle from a paper plate and paint stick; practice ready position.

*How to teach:* Make a paddle; practice ready position.

- **Q:** What is ready position?
  **A:** Feet apart, knees bent, paddle up in front.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Up-ups
*Student:* Strike a balloon upward with the paddle 10 times, flat face up.

*How to teach:* Balloon up-ups, flat face up, 10 times.

- **Q:** Which way does the paddle face?
  **A:** Flat, facing up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Down-ups
*Student:* Bounce a foam ball down with the paddle, then try alternating up and down.

*How to teach:* Bounce a foam ball down with the paddle; try alternating up and down.

- **Q:** What happens if the paddle tilts?
  **A:** The ball goes sideways.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Wall rally
*Student:* Hit a foam ball to a wall after one bounce and track how many in a row.

*How to teach:* Hit a foam ball to a wall after one bounce; count rally.

- **Q:** What is a rally?
  **A:** Hitting back and forth without missing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Do 10 balloon up-ups and 5 ball bounces and name the paddle cues.

*How to teach:* 10 up-ups and 5 bounces; name cues.

- **Q:** Name a paddle cue.
  **A:** Flat face / watch the ball / ready position / gentle hits.

**Assessment:** Paddle skill counts.

**Answer key:** Paddle counts: balloon up-ups (goal 10) and ball bounces down (goal 5). Cues: ready position (knees bent, paddle up), flat face, watch the ball, gentle hits. Proficient = both goals + cues.

## Health 2: Fire safety

**Prepare:** Smoke alarm (test with a parent), paper for an escape map.

**Watch for:**
- Hiding from fire under a bed or in a closet: get out and stay out.
- Going back in for a pet or toy: never go back inside; tell firefighters.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fire facts
*Student:* Learn about smoke alarms and test one with a parent.

*How to teach:* Teach smoke alarms; test one with a parent (test monthly).

- **Q:** What does a smoke alarm do?
  **A:** Beeps loudly to warn you of smoke or fire.
- **Q:** How often should alarms be tested?
  **A:** Once a month.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stop, drop, roll
*Student:* Practice stop, drop and roll and crawling low under smoke.

*How to teach:* Practice stop, drop, roll and crawling low under smoke.

- **Q:** When do you stop, drop and roll?
  **A:** If your clothes catch fire.
- **Q:** Why crawl low?
  **A:** Cleaner air is near the floor; smoke rises.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Escape map
*Student:* Draw a home escape map with two ways out of each room.

*How to teach:* Draw an escape map with two ways out of each room.

- **Q:** Why two ways out?
  **A:** In case one way is blocked by fire or smoke.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fire drill
*Student:* Hold a family fire drill to the outside meeting place.

*How to teach:* Family fire drill to the meeting place.

- **Q:** Where do you go after getting out?
  **A:** To the family meeting place outside.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain your escape plan and fire rules.

*How to teach:* Explain your escape plan and fire rules.

- **Q:** Should you go back for a toy?
  **A:** No, never go back inside.

**Assessment:** Escape map and drill.

**Answer key:** Escape map: each room shows two ways out (door and window), and an outside meeting place is marked. Rules: get low and go under smoke, feel the door with the back of the hand (if hot, use the other way), stop-drop-roll if clothes catch fire, never go back in, call 911 from outside. Proficient = map complete + 4 rules.
