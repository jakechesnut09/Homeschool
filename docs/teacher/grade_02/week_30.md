# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 30

## ELA 2: Comparing two texts on one topic

**Prepare:** Get How Mountains Are Made (Kathleen Weidner Zoehfeld) and a second mountain text, plus a children's dictionary. Prepare 10 science words to alphabetize and two short rock passages for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Alphabetizing only by the first letter. When first letters match, look at the second letter (magma, mountain).
- Comparing texts by listing only one text's facts. Use 'Both…' and 'Only…' sentence frames.

### Day 1 (Monday): Text one
*Student:* Read How Mountains Are Made and list 4 important points about how mountains form. Use the headings to locate facts.

*How to teach:* Read How Mountains Are Made and list 4 important points about how mountains form. Use headings to find facts.

- **Q:** Name one way mountains form.
  **A:** Plates of Earth's crust push together and fold or lift up the land (others: volcanoes, blocks lifted along faults).
- **Q:** What wears mountains down over time?
  **A:** Wind, water, and ice (erosion).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Text two
*Student:* Read a second text about mountains and list its important points. Mark points that are the same in both texts with a star.

*How to teach:* Read a second mountain text, list its points, and star the points that match.

- **Q:** Why star matching points?
  **A:** To show which ideas both texts agree on.
- **Q:** How do volcanoes make mountains?
  **A:** Lava erupts, cools, and piles up into a mountain.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Complete a two-text chart and write 3 compound sentences comparing the texts ('Both texts say…, but only the second…').

*How to teach:* Complete the two-text chart and write 3 compound sentences comparing the texts.

- **Q:** Complete: Both texts say ___, but only the second ___.
  **A:** Example: Both texts say plates push mountains up, but only the second tells about volcanoes.
- **Q:** Which joining word shows a difference?
  **A:** but.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dictionary skills
*Student:* Alphabetize 10 science words to the second letter and use guide words to look them up in a children's dictionary; copy one definition for each.

*How to teach:* Alphabetize 10 science words to the second letter and use guide words to look them up.

- **Q:** Alphabetize: mountain, magma, lava.
  **A:** lava, magma, mountain.
- **Q:** What are guide words?
  **A:** The words at the top of a dictionary page showing the first and last words on that page.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 30 review
*Student:* Compare two short passages on rocks in a chart, alphabetical order quiz, and a dictionary look-up check.

*How to teach:* Compare two rock passages in a chart, take an ABC-order quiz, and do a dictionary look-up.

- **Q:** Would 'pebble' be on a page with guide words 'peach–pencil'?
  **A:** Yes — 'peb' comes after 'pea' and before 'pen.'
- **Q:** Alphabetize: rock, river, rain.
  **A:** rain, river, rock.

**Assessment:** Two-text comparison chart and sentences; dictionary and ABC-order check.

**Answer key:** Two-text chart: points that both texts share are starred; 3 compound sentences comparing (e.g., 'Both texts say plates push up mountains, but only the second explains erosion'). ABC-order quiz (suggested): crater, earth, fault, lava, magma, mountain, plate, rock, slope, volcano. Dictionary check: child uses guide words to find a word and copies its definition correctly.

## Math 2: Arrays and repeated addition problem solving

**Prepare:** Grid paper, square tiles or square crackers. Prepare the 12-item Unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 2 rows of 6 and 6 rows of 2 have different totals. Turn the array to show it's the same 12.
- Guessing odd/even from the row size (3 rows of 4 = 12 is even). Find the total, then check the ones digit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tile arrays
*Student:* Use square tiles or crackers to build arrays and find totals; record 10 equations.

*How to teach:* Build arrays with tiles or crackers and write 10 repeated-addition equations.

- **Q:** 5 rows of 3 tiles: total?
  **A:** 15 (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3).
- **Q:** 2 rows of 4 crackers?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Garden plans
*Student:* Design a garden with 4 rows of 5 plants and other arrays; write equations and decide if totals are odd or even.

*How to teach:* Design a garden with 4 rows of 5 plants (20) and other arrays. Decide whether each total is odd or even.

- **Q:** 4 rows of 5 plants: total, odd or even?
  **A:** 20, even.
- **Q:** 3 rows of 3 plants?
  **A:** 9, odd.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Same total
*Student:* Find all the arrays that make 12 (2 × 6 as 6+6, 3 × 4 as 4+4+4…) using repeated addition.

*How to teach:* Find all rectangular arrays that make 12 using tiles.

- **Q:** Find arrays that make 12.
  **A:** 1 row of 12, 2 rows of 6, 3 rows of 4 (and 4 of 3, 6 of 2, 12 of 1).
- **Q:** Is 12 odd or even?
  **A:** Even.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mixed problems
*Student:* Solve 6 word problems mixing arrays, equal groups, and odd/even.

*How to teach:* Solve 6 mixed problems on arrays, equal groups, and odd/even.

- **Q:** 3 bags with 5 apples each. Total? Odd or even?
  **A:** 15, odd.
- **Q:** Can 14 kids make 2 equal teams?
  **A:** Yes, 7 + 7.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Odd/even and array test (12 items) with drawings and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the 12-item Unit 9 test.

- **Q:** 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = ?
  **A:** 8.
- **Q:** Is 22 odd or even?
  **A:** Even.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test (suggested 12): 1) 15 → odd 2) 22 → even 3) 16 = 8 + 8 4) 3 rows of 4 → 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 5) 5 rows of 3 → 15 6) 2 rows of 5 → 10, even 7) draw 4 + 4 + 4 → 3 rows of 4 8) arrays for 12: 1 by 12, 2 by 6, 3 by 4 (and turned versions) 9) 4 rows of 5 trees → 20 10) 3 bags of 5 apples → 15 11) 3 rows of 5 → 15, odd 12) 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8, even. Proficient: 10/12.

## Science 2: Design challenge: protect the house on the hill

**Prepare:** Gather a tray of sand, a small toy house or block, 10 craft sticks, foil, cloth, pebbles, clay, a measuring cup, and a ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Testing designs with different hills. Rebuild the same hill shape before each test.
- Ignoring constraints. Count materials before building.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define the problem
*Student:* A toy house sits on a sand hill near a 'river'; list the criteria (house stays put after 2 cups of water) and constraints (only 10 craft sticks, pebbles, clay).

*How to teach:* Define the problem: protect a toy house on a sand hill near a 'river'; list criteria and constraints.

- **Q:** What is a constraint?
  **A:** A limit, like only 10 craft sticks.
- **Q:** What is the criterion for success?
  **A:** The house stays put after 2 cups of water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sketch two designs
*Student:* Sketch two different solutions (e.g., a wall vs. a channel that moves water away) and label how each shape protects the house.

*How to teach:* Sketch two different solutions and label how each protects the house.

- **Q:** How does a channel help?
  **A:** It directs water away from the house.
- **Q:** How does a wall help?
  **A:** It blocks water from reaching the house.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build & test design 1
*Student:* Build design 1, pour 2 cups of water, and measure how far the house moved and how much sand washed away.

*How to teach:* Build design 1, pour 2 cups of water, and measure house movement and sand loss.

- **Q:** How far did the house move?
  **A:** Answer in cm.
- **Q:** Where did the water go?
  **A:** Answer from observation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build & test design 2
*Student:* Rebuild the hill, build design 2, and test it the same way; record results in the same table.

*How to teach:* Rebuild the hill, build design 2, and test the same way.

- **Q:** Why rebuild the same hill?
  **A:** To make the test fair.
- **Q:** Which design did better?
  **A:** Answer from data.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Compare the two designs in a T-chart of strengths and weaknesses and decide which to improve.

*How to teach:* Compare designs in a T-chart and decide which to improve.

- **Q:** Name a strength of your best design.
  **A:** Example: the house didn't move at all.
- **Q:** Name a weakness.
  **A:** Example: water went around the wall.

**Assessment:** Design sketches and comparison T-chart with data.

**Answer key:** Design sketches: two different, labeled solutions (e.g., wall vs. channel). T-chart (3 = proficient): strengths and weaknesses of each with data (how far the house moved in cm and how much sand washed away), and a decision about which to improve.

## People Who Make a Difference: Jackie Robinson

**Prepare:** 'Teammates' by Peter Golenbock; Jackie Robinson's 1958 letter to President Eisenhower (DocsTeach/National Archives); baseball and glove; poster paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking segregation was just a rule of baseball: it was unfair separation of people by race in many parts of life.
- Thinking staying calm meant Jackie didn't care: it took great courage and self-control not to fight back.

### Day 1 (Monday): Teammates
*Student:* Read Teammates by Peter Golenbock and discuss how Jackie Robinson and Pee Wee Reese showed courage in 1947.

*How to teach:* Read 'Teammates'; discuss courage of Jackie Robinson and Pee Wee Reese in 1947.

- **Q:** What team did Jackie Robinson join in 1947?
  **A:** The Brooklyn Dodgers.
- **Q:** What did Pee Wee Reese do?
  **A:** In front of a booing crowd, he put his arm around Jackie to show he was his teammate.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Unfair rules
*Student:* Learn what segregation meant and why it was unfair; discuss how Robinson stayed calm and kept playing.

*How to teach:* Teach segregation and why it was unfair; discuss staying calm.

- **Q:** What is segregation?
  **A:** Keeping people apart because of their race.
- **Q:** Why did Jackie promise not to fight back?
  **A:** Branch Rickey asked him to stay calm so people would focus on his talent and the cause.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Primary source letter
*Student:* Read parts of Jackie Robinson's 1958 letter to President Eisenhower (DocsTeach) and discuss what he asked the President to do.

*How to teach:* Read parts of the 1958 letter to Eisenhower.

- **Q:** What did Jackie ask President Eisenhower?
  **A:** Not to tell Black Americans to be patient, but to act to protect their equal rights now.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Play ball
*Student:* Play catch and learn that Jackie Robinson's number 42 is retired by every Major League team; design a 'Jackie Robinson Day' poster.

*How to teach:* Play catch; learn #42 is retired by every MLB team; design a Jackie Robinson Day poster.

- **Q:** What number did Jackie wear?
  **A:** 42.
- **Q:** When is Jackie Robinson Day?
  **A:** April 15 (the date of his first major league game in 1947).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write how Jackie Robinson made a difference for others, using one detail from the letter.

*How to teach:* Write how Jackie made a difference, with a letter detail.

- **Q:** What is the 'color barrier'?
  **A:** The unfair rule that kept Black players out of Major League Baseball.

**Assessment:** Written response using evidence from the primary-source letter.

**Answer key:** Written response: Jackie Robinson broke Major League Baseball's color barrier in 1947 with the Brooklyn Dodgers, showing courage that opened the way for other Black players; in his 1958 letter he told President Eisenhower that Black Americans should not have to keep waiting patiently and asked him to act now to protect equal rights. Proficient = accurate contribution + detail from the letter.

## Art & Music 2: Story drama: The Three Billy Goats Gruff

**Prepare:** A version of 'The Three Billy Goats Gruff,' simple costume pieces, a chair as the bridge.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking acting is just saying lines: bodies, gestures and voices show character.
- Shouting all the troll's lines: change volume and speed to show feeling.

### Day 1 (Monday): Character bodies
*Student:* Read The Three Billy Goats Gruff and practice how each goat and the troll walk, stand, and speak (tiny trip-trap vs. great big stomp).

*How to teach:* Read the story; practice each goat's and the troll's walk, stance and voice.

- **Q:** Why do the goats want to cross the bridge?
  **A:** To eat the green grass on the hillside on the other side.
- **Q:** How is the little goat's walk different from the big goat's?
  **A:** Little goat: light, quick 'trip-trap'; big goat: heavy, loud stomp.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Voices
*Student:* Practice reading the dialogue with a different voice for each character, changing volume and speed.

*How to teach:* Read dialogue with a different voice for each character.

- **Q:** What does the smallest goat tell the troll?
  **A:** Wait for my bigger brother; he is much bigger and tastier.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): What if?
*Student:* Discuss choices characters could make (What if the troll were lonely? What if the goats shared the grass?) and choose one new plot idea.

*How to teach:* Discuss 'what if' choices; choose a new plot idea.

- **Q:** How does the original story end?
  **A:** The biggest goat knocks the troll off the bridge into the river, and the goats eat grass and get fat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Act it out
*Student:* Act out the original story with family using a chair as the bridge, then act out the new version.

*How to teach:* Act out the original, then the new version.

- **Q:** What is a gesture?
  **A:** A movement of the body or hands that shows meaning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Share both versions informally and tell which choice changed the story most.

*How to teach:* Share both; tell which choice changed the story most.

- **Q:** What is plot?
  **A:** The events of a story in order.

**Assessment:** Participation in guided drama and new-version performance.

**Answer key:** Participation rubric: uses different body, gesture and voice for each character; follows the story's plot; contributes a new plot idea and performs the new version. 4 = all with clear choices; 3 = most; 2 = some; 1 = minimal.

## PE 2: Dance creation

**Prepare:** Music, open space, folk dance video (e.g., Chimes of Dunkirk).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking dance isn't exercise: it raises heart rate.
- Moving off the beat: count out loud '1-2-3…8.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Folk dance
*Student:* Learn a simple folk dance like the Chimes of Dunkirk or a children's line dance from a video.

*How to teach:* Learn a folk dance like Chimes of Dunkirk from a video.

- **Q:** What is a folk dance?
  **A:** A traditional dance passed down in a community.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Freeze dance
*Student:* Play freeze dance, holding a balanced shape when the music stops.

*How to teach:* Freeze dance with balanced shapes.

- **Q:** What do you do when the music stops?
  **A:** Freeze in a balanced shape.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create a dance
*Student:* Create a 16-count dance using skips, slides, turns and a jump.

*How to teach:* Create a 16-count dance: skips, slides, turns, a jump.

- **Q:** How many 8-counts in 16 counts?
  **A:** Two.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice your dance to music 3 times and add a beginning and ending pose.

*How to teach:* Practice 3 times; add beginning and ending poses.

- **Q:** Why add a pose?
  **A:** To show clearly when the dance starts and ends.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform your dance for family and explain which moves raised your heart rate.

*How to teach:* Perform; explain which moves raised heart rate.

- **Q:** Which moves raised your heart rate most?
  **A:** Jumps and skips (big, fast moves).

**Assessment:** Dance performance rubric.

**Answer key:** Dance rubric: 16 counts with skips, slides, turns and a jump; beginning and ending pose; on beat; explains which moves raised heart rate (skips, jumps, slides). 4 = all; 3 = minor timing slips; 2 = missing parts; 1 = incomplete.

## Health 2: Medicine safety

**Prepare:** An empty medicine box and label (with a parent). PARENTAL PREVIEW: keep all real medicines locked up during this week.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking medicine is candy: never take medicine unless a trusted adult gives it.
- Thinking more medicine works better: the right dose is important; too much can hurt.

### Day 1 (Monday): Medicine vs drug
*Student:* Learn medicines help when used correctly and that drugs can harm.

*How to teach:* Teach: medicines help when used correctly; drugs used the wrong way can harm.

- **Q:** What is a medicine?
  **A:** Something that helps the body when you are sick or hurt, used the right way.
- **Q:** What is a prescription?
  **A:** Medicine a doctor orders for one person.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Label reading
*Student:* Look at an empty medicine box label with a parent; find the dose and warnings.

*How to teach:* Look at a label with a parent; find dose and warnings.

- **Q:** What is a dose?
  **A:** How much medicine to take and when.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Only from trusted adults
*Student:* Discuss why only trusted adults give medicine.

*How to teach:* Discuss why only trusted adults give medicine.

- **Q:** Who can give you medicine?
  **A:** A parent or trusted adult, doctor or nurse.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Candy or medicine?
*Student:* Compare look-alike candies and pills and discuss why to never guess.

*How to teach:* Compare look-alike candies and pills (pictures).

- **Q:** If you find a pill that looks like candy, what do you do?
  **A:** Don't eat it; give it to or tell an adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 3 rules for medicine safety.

*How to teach:* Explain 3 medicine safety rules.

- **Q:** Can you take your friend's medicine?
  **A:** No, never take someone else's medicine.

**Assessment:** Oral rules.

**Answer key:** Three rules (any 3 accurate): only take medicine from a trusted adult (parent, doctor, nurse); never take someone else's medicine; follow the label's dose; never guess if something is candy or medicine; keep medicine stored away. Proficient = 3 accurate rules with reasons.
