# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 12

## ELA 2: Author's purpose and publishing an animal report

**Prepare:** Have the child's animal notes, cardstock, markers, and Frogs!, Monarch Butterfly, and a Frog and Toad book on hand. Arrange a family audience or video call for Thursday. Prepare a cold-read nonfiction passage with questions for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every book's purpose is to entertain. Ask 'Did the author want to teach me, tell me a story, or convince me?'
- Ending a report abruptly. Add a concluding sentence that sums up the animal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why did the author write it
*Student:* Compare the purpose of Frogs! (teach facts) to a Frog and Toad story (entertain); decide what question Monarch Butterfly answers. Finish report paragraph 2 (home).

*How to teach:* Compare purposes: Frogs! teaches facts; Frog and Toad entertains. Ask what question Monarch Butterfly answers. Finish report paragraph 2 (home).

- **Q:** What is the author's purpose in Frogs!?
  **A:** To teach (inform) readers facts about frogs.
- **Q:** What is the author's purpose in Frog and Toad Are Friends?
  **A:** To entertain readers with stories.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finish the draft
*Student:* Write paragraph 3 (food) and a concluding sentence. Add a labeled diagram and a glossary of 3 words to the report.

*How to teach:* Write paragraph 3 (food) and a concluding sentence. Add a labeled diagram and a 3-word glossary.

- **Q:** Write a concluding sentence for a report on owls.
  **A:** Example: Owls are amazing night hunters.
- **Q:** What goes in a glossary?
  **A:** Important words and what they mean, in ABC order.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Read the report aloud and fix anything that does not make sense; check spelling of r-controlled and vowel-team words. Make a final copy on cardstock.

*How to teach:* The child reads the report aloud, fixes anything that doesn't make sense, checks spelling, and makes a final copy on cardstock.

- **Q:** Why read your writing aloud?
  **A:** To hear mistakes and parts that don't make sense.
- **Q:** Spell 'shirt' and 'rain.'
  **A:** s-h-i-r-t; r-a-i-n.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the animal report to family or a video call, speaking clearly and answering 2 questions from listeners. Listeners ask one question to gather more information.

*How to teach:* Present to family, speaking clearly. Listeners each ask one question; the child answers.

- **Q:** What does a good presenter do?
  **A:** Speaks loud and clear, looks at the audience, and answers questions.
- **Q:** Give an example of a question to gather more information.
  **A:** Example: 'What do baby owls eat?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Cold read of a nonfiction passage with questions on text features, main topic, and author's purpose. Spelling test of 15 r-controlled and y words.

*How to teach:* Give the cold-read test (text features, main topic, author's purpose) and the 15-word spelling test.

- **Q:** What is the author's purpose of a passage that explains how bees make honey?
  **A:** To teach/inform.
- **Q:** Spell 'first' and 'storm.'
  **A:** f-i-r-s-t; s-t-o-r-m.

**Assessment:** Published 'All About' report (rubric: topic sentence, facts, diagram, conclusion) and unit reading test.

**Answer key:** Report rubric (3 = proficient): topic sentence in each paragraph; at least 3 accurate facts per paragraph in the child's words; a labeled diagram; a concluding sentence; a 3-word glossary. Unit reading test: text features correctly used, main topic stated, author's purpose named (to teach/inform for a nonfiction passage). Spelling (suggested 15): bird, her, turn, first, burn, girl, nurse, farm, star, corn, storm, happy, baby, sky, fly.

## Math 2: Adding within 100 using place value

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks, a printed hundreds chart, and paper for open number lines (a blank line with no numbers). Write 12 practice problems for Friday (mix with and without regrouping).

**Watch for:**
- Writing each column's sum side by side without regrouping (47 + 36 = 713). Build both numbers with blocks and trade 10 ones for a ten: 83.
- On the hundreds chart, moving right for tens instead of down. Remind: down one row = +10, right one square = +1.

### Day 1 (Monday): Add tens and ones
*Student:* Add 34 + 25 with blocks: combine tens (5 tens), then ones (9 ones); solve 10 problems without regrouping.

*How to teach:* Build 34 and 25 with blocks. Push the tens together (3 + 2 = 5 tens), then the ones (4 + 5 = 9): 59. The child solves 10 problems without regrouping, building or drawing each.

- **Q:** 34 + 25 = ?
  **A:** 59 (5 tens and 9 ones).
- **Q:** 52 + 41 = ?
  **A:** 93.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hundreds chart jumps
*Student:* Add 46 + 23 on a hundreds chart by jumping down 2 rows (tens) and right 3 (ones); explain why it works.

*How to teach:* On a hundreds chart, put a finger on 46. Jump down 2 rows (+20) to 66, then right 3 (+3) to 69. Ask why going down a row adds 10.

- **Q:** Use the hundreds chart for 46 + 23.
  **A:** 69 — down 2 rows to 66, right 3 to 69.
- **Q:** Why does moving down one row add 10?
  **A:** Each row has 10 numbers, so the number below is 10 more.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Open number line
*Student:* Add 38 + 27 on an open number line (38 + 20 = 58, + 2 = 60, + 5 = 65) and solve 8 more.

*How to teach:* Draw an open number line. Start at 38, jump +20 to 58, +2 to 60, +5 to 65. The child solves 8 more, labeling each jump.

- **Q:** Solve 38 + 27 on an open number line.
  **A:** 65 — 38 + 20 = 58, + 2 = 60, + 5 = 65.
- **Q:** Solve 45 + 36 on a number line.
  **A:** 81 — 45 + 30 = 75, + 5 = 80, + 1 = 81.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Making a new ten
*Student:* Add 47 + 36 with blocks, trading 10 ones for 1 ten; draw quick pictures for 8 problems that need regrouping.

*How to teach:* Build 47 + 36: 7 + 6 = 13 ones — trade 10 ones for a ten. Now 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 tens and 3 ones = 83. The child draws quick pictures for 8 regrouping problems, circling the 10 ones they trade.

- **Q:** 47 + 36 = ? Explain the trade.
  **A:** 83 — 13 ones become 1 ten and 3 ones, so 8 tens 3 ones.
- **Q:** 29 + 18 = ?
  **A:** 47.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 12 two-digit addition problems using a chosen strategy and explain one in writing.

*How to teach:* Give the 12-problem quiz; the child may choose any strategy. Then they write how they solved one problem in 2–3 sentences.

- **Q:** 63 + 28 = ?
  **A:** 91.
- **Q:** Explain 25 + 48.
  **A:** 73 — e.g., 20 + 40 = 60, 5 + 8 = 13, 60 + 13 = 73.

**Assessment:** Addition quiz with written strategy explanation.

**Answer key:** Addition quiz (suggested 12): 1) 34 + 25 = 59 2) 46 + 23 = 69 3) 38 + 27 = 65 4) 47 + 36 = 83 5) 52 + 41 = 93 6) 29 + 18 = 47 7) 63 + 28 = 91 8) 15 + 75 = 90 9) 44 + 44 = 88 10) 56 + 37 = 93 11) 71 + 19 = 90 12) 25 + 48 = 73. Written explanation rubric: 3 = names a strategy and shows each step with correct place-value words (e.g., '38 + 27: 38 + 20 = 58, + 2 = 60, + 5 = 65'); 2 = correct steps, vague words; 1 = answer only. Proficient: 10/12 and a level-3 explanation.

## Science 2: Parts of plants and plant needs

**Prepare:** Gather a weed with roots (ask before pulling), a celery stalk with leaves, a glass, food coloring, bean seeds, paper towels, zip bags, and tape. Start the bean bag early in the week.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking plants eat soil for food. Plants make their own food using sunlight; roots take in water and minerals.
- Thinking stems are just for holding up the plant. Stems also carry water from roots to leaves.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plant parts
*Student:* Dig up a weed and identify roots, stem, leaves, and flowers; draw and label it.

*How to teach:* Dig up a weed and identify roots, stem, leaves, and flowers. Draw and label it.

- **Q:** What do roots do?
  **A:** Hold the plant in the ground and take in water.
- **Q:** What do leaves do?
  **A:** Use sunlight to make food for the plant.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Celery straw
*Student:* Put a celery stalk in colored water and observe how water travels up the stem over a day.

*How to teach:* Put celery in colored water and observe over a day.

- **Q:** Predict what will happen to the celery.
  **A:** The colored water will move up the stem into the leaves.
- **Q:** What does this show?
  **A:** The stem carries water up the plant.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Seed in a bag
*Student:* Plant bean seeds in wet paper towels in a zip bag on a window to watch roots grow.

*How to teach:* Plant bean seeds in wet paper towels in a zip bag on a window.

- **Q:** What part grows first from a bean seed?
  **A:** The root.
- **Q:** Why keep the towel wet?
  **A:** Seeds need water to sprout.

### Day 4 (Thursday): What do plants need?
*Student:* List what you think plants need and write questions to test (Do plants need light? Water?).

*How to teach:* List what plants need and write questions to test.

- **Q:** What do plants need?
  **A:** Water, light, air, space, and nutrients from soil.
- **Q:** Write a testable question.
  **A:** Do plants need light to grow?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Record day-by-day observations of celery and bean bag; explain the stem's job.

*How to teach:* Record observations of celery and bean bag; explain the stem's job.

- **Q:** Explain the stem's job.
  **A:** It carries water from the roots to the leaves and holds the plant up.
- **Q:** What did you see in the celery leaves?
  **A:** The color of the water.

**Assessment:** Labeled plant drawing and observation log.

**Answer key:** Labeled drawing: roots (take in water, hold plant), stem (carries water, holds plant up), leaves (make food using sunlight), flower (makes seeds). Observation log: dated entries showing colored water moving up celery (colored lines and leaf edges) and bean roots appearing first, then a shoot. Explanation: the stem carries water from roots to leaves.

## People Who Make a Difference: Why we have rules and laws

**Prepare:** Home rules chart; photos of stop and speed-limit signs; 10 sort cards; materials for a made-up game.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking rules and laws are the same: rules are for a home, school or game; laws are made by government for everyone in a community, state or country.
- Thinking rules are just to stop fun: rules keep people safe and make things fair.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules at home
*Student:* List rules at home and explain why each one exists; discuss what would happen without them.

*How to teach:* List home rules and why each exists; discuss life without them.

- **Q:** Why do we have a bedtime rule?
  **A:** So you get enough sleep to be healthy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Laws in the community
*Student:* Go on a walk to find signs (stop sign, speed limit, no littering) and write the law each shows.

*How to teach:* Walk to find signs; write the law each shows.

- **Q:** What law does a stop sign show?
  **A:** Drivers must come to a full stop.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rule or law
*Student:* Sort 10 examples into rules (home, school, game) and laws (community, state, country).

*How to teach:* Sort 10 examples into rules and laws.

- **Q:** Is 'wear a seat belt' a rule or a law?
  **A:** A law.
- **Q:** Is 'clean your room' a rule or a law?
  **A:** A rule (home rule).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fair rules
*Student:* Make up a new game and write 3 fair rules; test whether they work.

*How to teach:* Invent a game with 3 fair rules; test them.

- **Q:** What makes a rule fair?
  **A:** It applies the same way to everyone.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain in writing why laws keep people safe and treat people fairly.

*How to teach:* Write why laws keep people safe and fair.

- **Q:** What is a citizen?
  **A:** A member of a community or country with rights and responsibilities.

**Assessment:** Rule/law sort and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Sort: rules - bedtime at 8, wash hands before dinner, raise your hand in class, take turns in a game, no running inside; laws - stop at a stop sign, obey the speed limit, no littering, wear a seat belt, children must go to school. Written explanation: laws keep people safe (traffic laws prevent crashes) and treat people fairly (everyone follows the same laws). Proficient = 9-10 correct + both ideas.

## Art & Music 2: Color project: a painting with a mood

**Prepare:** Paints, watercolor paper, sketchbook; 8 printed or onscreen artwork images showing different moods.

**Watch for:**
- Picking colors without a plan: choose a palette that fits the mood first.
- Thinking only color shows mood: lines and shapes help too (jagged = tense, curvy = calm).

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose a mood (calm, joyful, spooky, excited) and sketch a scene in the sketchbook, noting which colors will show that mood.

*How to teach:* Choose a mood; sketch a scene; note which colors will show it.

- **Q:** Which colors might show 'spooky'?
  **A:** Dark cool colors, shades, black, purple.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paint the background
*Student:* Paint the background with your chosen palette, using tints and shades for depth.

*How to teach:* Paint the background with the palette, using tints and shades.

- **Q:** How do tints and shades add depth?
  **A:** Lighter areas seem closer or lit; darker areas recede or are in shadow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paint the details
*Student:* Add the main subject and details with line and shape from Unit 1.

*How to teach:* Add main subject and details with lines and shapes from Unit 1.

- **Q:** Which lines fit your mood?
  **A:** Matches, e.g., wavy for calm.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Guess the mood
*Student:* Show the painting to family without telling the mood and have them guess; record their guesses and the clues they used.

*How to teach:* Family guesses the mood; record guesses and clues.

- **Q:** What clues did they use?
  **A:** Colors, lines, subjects.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Sort 8 artwork images by mood and explain one choice; tell what you would change in your own painting next time.

*How to teach:* Sort 8 artwork images by mood; explain one; say what you'd change.

- **Q:** Why did you put this image in 'calm'?
  **A:** Cool colors, soft lines, quiet subject.

**Assessment:** Mood painting, family guesses, and image-sorting check.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = palette, lines and shapes all match the chosen mood; family guesses correctly; sorts 8 images with a clear reason; names a change for next time. 3 = mood mostly clear; 2 = mood unclear or few reasons; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 2: Balance-and-roll routine

**Prepare:** Mat, paper and crayons, calm music; phone to video (optional).

**Watch for:**
- Stopping between parts: practice transitions so moves flow.
- Rushing balances: hold each still for 3 counts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan it
*Student:* Draw a routine of start shape, balance, roll, balance, finish shape.

*How to teach:* Draw the routine: start shape, balance, roll, balance, finish shape.

- **Q:** How many parts in your routine?
  **A:** Five.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Smooth transitions
*Student:* Practice moving from one part of the routine to the next without stopping.

*How to teach:* Practice transitions without stopping.

- **Q:** What is a transition?
  **A:** How you move from one part to the next.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add music
*Student:* Perform the routine to a short piece of calm music, holding each balance for 3 counts.

*How to teach:* Perform to calm music, holding balances 3 counts.

- **Q:** How long do you hold a balance?
  **A:** 3 counts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Polish
*Student:* Video or have a parent watch; pick one part to improve and practice it 5 times.

*How to teach:* Watch or video; pick one part to improve and practice it 5 times.

- **Q:** Which part did you improve?
  **A:** Names a specific part.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the 5-part routine for the family and explain which part was hardest.

*How to teach:* Perform for family; explain the hardest part.

- **Q:** What was hardest and why?
  **A:** Specific answer.

**Assessment:** Routine performance rubric (shapes held, safe roll, smooth transitions).

**Answer key:** Routine rubric: 4 = clear start shape, balance held 3 counts, safe forward or log roll, second balance, finish shape, smooth transitions; 3 = all parts with one wobble or pause; 2 = missing a part or unsafe roll; 1 = incomplete. Child names hardest part.

## Health 2: Fruits, vegetables and water

**Prepare:** A variety of fruits and vegetables (one new to taste), cups, measuring teaspoon, sugar; labels from juice, soda, milk.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking juice is as healthy as whole fruit: whole fruit has fiber and usually less sugar per serving.
- Thinking you only need water when thirsty: drink water throughout the day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Eat the rainbow
*Student:* Make a color chart of fruits and vegetables and taste one new produce item.

*How to teach:* Make a color chart of produce; taste a new one.

- **Q:** Why 'eat the rainbow'?
  **A:** Different colored foods give different vitamins and nutrients.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plant parts we eat
*Student:* Sort vegetables by plant part (root, stem, leaf, seed, fruit), connecting to the Science plant unit.

*How to teach:* Sort vegetables by plant part: carrot (root), celery (stem), lettuce/spinach (leaf), peas/corn (seed), tomato/cucumber (fruit), broccoli (flower).

- **Q:** What part of the plant is a carrot?
  **A:** Root.
- **Q:** What part is celery?
  **A:** Stem.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water for life
*Student:* Learn why the body needs water; track water cups drunk today.

*How to teach:* Learn why the body needs water; track cups today.

- **Q:** Why does your body need water?
  **A:** To stay cool, move nutrients, help digestion and keep every part working.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rethink your drink
*Student:* Compare sugar in juice, soda and milk by measuring teaspoons of sugar into cups.

*How to teach:* Measure sugar: 12-oz soda about 10 teaspoons; 8-oz apple juice about 6; 8-oz plain milk about 3 (natural milk sugar). Use labels: about 4 grams = 1 teaspoon.

- **Q:** Which drink had the most sugar?
  **A:** Soda.
- **Q:** If a label says 24 grams of sugar, about how many teaspoons?
  **A:** About 6 teaspoons (24 ÷ 4).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write 3 reasons to eat fruits and vegetables and drink water.

*How to teach:* Write 3 reasons to eat produce and drink water.

- **Q:** What is a nutrient?
  **A:** Something in food your body needs to grow and stay healthy.

**Assessment:** Written reasons and rainbow chart.

**Answer key:** Three reasons (any accurate): fruits and vegetables give vitamins and minerals; they give fiber that helps digestion; they help your body grow and fight sickness; water keeps the body cool, carries nutrients, and helps every part work. Rainbow chart has a fruit/vegetable for each color. Proficient = 3 accurate reasons + complete chart.
