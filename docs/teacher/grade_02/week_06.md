# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 6

## ELA 2: Fables continued: vowel teams ai/ay and ee/ea

**Prepare:** Preview 'The Ant and the Grasshopper,' 'The Crow and the Pitcher,' and 'The Boy Who Cried Wolf.' Gather a tall cup, water, and pebbles; vowel-team cards (ai, ay, ee, ea); and picture cards for irregular plurals.

**Watch for:**
- Adding -s to every plural (foots, childs, mouses). Make a chart of 'special plurals' and practice them orally.
- Spelling long a with ay in the middle of a word (trayn). Teach: ai in the middle, ay at the end.

### Day 1 (Monday): The Ant and the Grasshopper
*Student:* Read 'The Ant and the Grasshopper' and chart what each character does in summer and in winter. Phonics: ai/ay words (rain, train, play, day).

*How to teach:* Read 'The Ant and the Grasshopper.' Chart what each does in summer and winter. Read ai/ay words.

- **Q:** What do the ant and grasshopper do in summer?
  **A:** The ant works storing food; the grasshopper sings and plays.
- **Q:** What happens in winter, and what is the lesson?
  **A:** The grasshopper has no food and is hungry while the ant has plenty. Lesson: work and prepare ahead for what you will need.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Crow and the Pitcher
*Student:* Read 'The Crow and the Pitcher' and test the idea at home by dropping pebbles into a cup of water. Phonics: ee/ea words (tree, green, beach, read).

*How to teach:* Read 'The Crow and the Pitcher,' then test it: drop pebbles into a cup of water and watch the water rise. Read ee/ea words.

- **Q:** How does the crow get a drink?
  **A:** It drops pebbles into the pitcher one by one until the water rises high enough to drink.
- **Q:** What is the lesson?
  **A:** Little by little does the trick — thinking and keeping at it can solve a problem.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Irregular plurals
*Student:* Grammar: learn plurals that change (foot/feet, child/children, tooth/teeth, mouse/mice, fish/fish) and complete 10 fill-in sentences. Read a fable aloud using complete sentences to answer 'What is the lesson?'

*How to teach:* Teach plurals that change: foot/feet, child/children, tooth/teeth, mouse/mice, fish/fish. Complete 10 sentences. Practice answering 'What is the lesson?' in a complete sentence.

- **Q:** What is the plural of mouse? Of tooth?
  **A:** mice; teeth.
- **Q:** Complete: Two ___ (child) played.
  **A:** children.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fable connection
*Student:* Write 3–4 sentences about a time in your life when one of this week's morals came true. Draw a picture to go with it.

*How to teach:* The child writes 3–4 sentences about a time a moral came true in their life and draws a picture.

- **Q:** Which moral fits saving allowance for a toy?
  **A:** The Ant and the Grasshopper — work and save ahead.
- **Q:** What should the last sentence do?
  **A:** Tell how the moral came true or what was learned.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 6 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 12 vowel-team words, an irregular-plurals quiz, and a fluency read of 'The Boy Who Cried Wolf' with an oral moral check.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling check and plural quiz. The child reads 'The Boy Who Cried Wolf' aloud and tells the moral.

- **Q:** Why didn't the villagers come when the wolf really came?
  **A:** The boy had lied about a wolf before, so they didn't believe him.
- **Q:** Spell 'train' and 'beach.'
  **A:** t-r-a-i-n; b-e-a-c-h.

**Assessment:** Spelling check, 10-item irregular plural quiz, and written fable-to-life connection.

**Answer key:** Spelling (suggested 12): rain, train, wait, play, day, stay, tree, green, feet, beach, read, team. Irregular plural quiz (10): foot/feet, child/children, tooth/teeth, mouse/mice, fish/fish, man/men, woman/women, goose/geese, sheep/sheep, person/people. Fable-to-life connection rubric: 3 = names a moral and tells a real event where it came true, 3–4 sentences; 2 = event without clear link to the moral; 1 = off topic. 'The Boy Who Cried Wolf' moral: if you lie, people won't believe you even when you tell the truth.

## Math 2: Counting and skip-counting to 1,000

**Prepare:** Print a hundreds chart and blank chart sections (or draw grids), and collect about 20 nickels and 20 dimes. Write 6 skip-count patterns and 10 '10/100 more or less' problems for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Counting correctly to 100 but stumbling across later hundreds ('198, 199, 1000'). Use blocks: 10 tens trade for the next hundred, so 199 + 1 = 200.
- Changing the wrong digit when adding 10 (456 + 10 = 556). Ask 'Which digit is in the tens place?' and add one rod.

### Day 1 (Monday): Counting across hundreds
*Student:* Count aloud from 87 to 132 and from 385 to 421, filling a blank chart section; notice what happens at 'hundreds.'

*How to teach:* Count aloud together from 87 to 132 while the child fills a blank chart section, then 385 to 421. Stop at 99→100 and 399→400 and ask what changed (the hundreds digit goes up; tens and ones become 0).

- **Q:** What number comes after 199?
  **A:** 200.
- **Q:** Count on from 397: what are the next 4 numbers?
  **A:** 398, 399, 400, 401.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Skip by 5s and 10s
*Student:* Skip-count by 5s using nickels and by 10s using dimes; skip-count by 10s starting at 23 (23, 33, 43…).

*How to teach:* Lay out nickels and count by 5s; lay out dimes and count by 10s. Then start at 23 on the hundreds chart and jump down by 10s: 23, 33, 43 — only the tens digit changes.

- **Q:** Skip-count by 10s from 23 to 73.
  **A:** 23, 33, 43, 53, 63, 73.
- **Q:** How much are 7 nickels?
  **A:** 35¢ (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Skip by 100s
*Student:* Skip-count by 100s from 100 and from 250; use a number path to show 10 more, 10 less, 100 more, 100 less.

*How to teach:* Skip-count by 100s from 100 to 1,000, then from 250 (250, 350, 450…). Draw a number path and show 10 more/less and 100 more/less for a number like 460 (470, 450, 560, 360).

- **Q:** Skip-count by 100s from 250 to 750.
  **A:** 250, 350, 450, 550, 650, 750.
- **Q:** What is 100 less than 460? 10 more?
  **A:** 360; 470.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mental 10s and 100s
*Student:* Mental math: add or subtract 10 or 100 from numbers between 100 and 900 (e.g., 456 + 100, 702 − 10) — 20 problems.

*How to teach:* Do 20 quick oral problems: add or subtract 10 or 100 from numbers 100–900. After each, ask which digit changed. Watch for crossing a hundred (395 + 10 = 405).

- **Q:** 456 + 100 = ?
  **A:** 556 (the hundreds digit goes up 1).
- **Q:** 702 − 10 = ?
  **A:** 692 (there are 0 tens, so 70 tens − 1 ten = 69 tens, and 2 ones stay).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Fill in missing numbers on 6 skip-count patterns and 10 '10 more/100 less' problems.

*How to teach:* The child fills in 6 skip-count patterns and solves 10 more/less problems. Review any errors with blocks or the number path.

- **Q:** Fill in: 85, 90, 95, __, __
  **A:** 100, 105.
- **Q:** What is 10 more than 390?
  **A:** 400.

**Assessment:** Skip-counting patterns and mental 10/100 more or less check.

**Answer key:** Skip-count patterns (suggested answers): 1) 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 2) 23, 33, 43, 53, 63 3) 100, 200, 300, 400, 500 4) 250, 350, 450, 550, 650 5) 85, 90, 95, 100, 105 6) 370, 380, 390, 400, 410. Mental items: 7) 456 + 10 = 466 8) 456 + 100 = 556 9) 702 − 10 = 692 10) 702 − 100 = 602 11) 390 + 10 = 400 12) 815 − 100 = 715 13) 128 + 100 = 228 14) 600 − 10 = 590 15) 247 − 10 = 237 16) 999 − 100 = 899. Proficient: 14/16.

## Science 2: Hot and cold: insulators

**Prepare:** Gather same-size ice cubes, small cups or plates, foil, cloth, newspaper, bubble wrap, rubber bands, and a timer. Start early in the day — melting may take a few hours.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a coat 'makes heat' or that foil keeps ice 'cold' by itself. Insulators slow heat from moving in or out.
- Checking cubes at different times. Check all at the same time every 15 minutes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Melting predictions
*Student:* Predict which wrapper (foil, cloth, newspaper, bubble wrap, none) will keep an ice cube from melting longest.

*How to teach:* The child predicts which wrapper (foil, cloth, newspaper, bubble wrap, none) will keep ice from melting longest.

- **Q:** Which cube do you predict will melt first?
  **A:** The one with no wrapper.
- **Q:** Why include a cube with no wrapper?
  **A:** To compare — it shows what happens without an insulator.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ice cube test
*Student:* Wrap identical ice cubes and check every 15 minutes; record observations and time to melt.

*How to teach:* Wrap identical ice cubes and check every 15 minutes; record observations and melt time.

- **Q:** Why use identical ice cubes?
  **A:** So the test is fair; only the wrapper changes.
- **Q:** What makes ice melt?
  **A:** Heat from the warm air moves into the ice.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design an ice keeper
*Student:* Design and build an 'ice cube keeper' from two materials and sketch it.

*How to teach:* Design and build an ice cube keeper from two materials and sketch it.

- **Q:** Why might two layers work better than one?
  **A:** More layers (and trapped air) slow heat moving in.
- **Q:** Which materials would you combine?
  **A:** Example: bubble wrap inside and cloth outside.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare designs
*Student:* Test your keeper vs. the best single material; record data and compare strengths and weaknesses.

*How to teach:* Test the keeper against the best single material and compare.

- **Q:** If your keeper lasted longer, what does that show?
  **A:** Your design was a better insulator.
- **Q:** Name a weakness to fix.
  **A:** Example: gaps let warm air in.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a notebook conclusion about which materials make good insulators and why.

*How to teach:* Write a conclusion about which materials make good insulators and why.

- **Q:** What is an insulator?
  **A:** A material that slows heat from moving through it.
- **Q:** Why do we wear coats in winter?
  **A:** Coats are insulators that slow our body heat from escaping.

**Assessment:** Ice-keeper design test with data comparison.

**Answer key:** Data comparison: the unwrapped cube melts first; cloth, newspaper, and bubble wrap slow melting (bubble wrap or layered newspaper often last longest, depending on wrapping). Proficient: data recorded every 15 minutes for each cube, the keeper's time compared with the best single material, and a strength/weakness named. Conclusion: good insulators (cloth, newspaper, bubble wrap, trapped air) slow heat from moving into the ice.

## People Who Make a Difference: Map elements

**Prepare:** Real compass; 3 sample maps (zoo, park, city) printed; sticky notes; a photo of a room taken from above (stand on a chair) and paper for maps.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking north always means 'up' in the real world: north is a direction; on most maps it is at the top.
- Confusing the legend with the title: the title tells what the map shows; the legend explains symbols.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is a map
*Student:* Compare a photo of a room taken from above with a map of the room; discuss what a bird's-eye view is.

*How to teach:* Compare an above photo of a room with a map; explain bird's-eye view.

- **Q:** What is a bird's-eye view?
  **A:** Looking down from above, the way a bird would see.
- **Q:** What is a map?
  **A:** A drawing of a place from above.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map elements
*Student:* Find the title, legend (key), compass rose, scale, and date on three maps (zoo, park, city) and label them with sticky notes.

*How to teach:* Find title, legend, compass rose, scale and date on 3 maps; label with sticky notes.

- **Q:** What does a legend (key) do?
  **A:** Explains what the symbols on the map mean.
- **Q:** What does a scale show?
  **A:** How distance on the map compares to real distance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Directions
*Student:* Use a real compass to find north in your home and label N, S, E, W on the walls; play a directions game ('take 3 steps north').

*How to teach:* Use a compass to find north; label N, S, E, W walls; play a directions game.

- **Q:** If you face north, what direction is behind you?
  **A:** South.
- **Q:** Which direction does the sun rise?
  **A:** East.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Map of my room
*Student:* Draw a map of your bedroom with a title, key, and compass rose.

*How to teach:* Draw a bedroom map with title, key and compass rose.

- **Q:** What symbol did you use for your bed?
  **A:** Child's key symbol.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Quiz: identify map elements on a new map and answer 4 direction questions.

*How to teach:* Quiz on map elements and 4 directions.

- **Q:** What are the four cardinal directions?
  **A:** North, south, east, west.

**Assessment:** Room map with all elements and map-elements quiz.

**Answer key:** Room map: title, key with at least 4 symbols, compass rose (N, S, E, W), items drawn from above. Map-elements quiz: identifies title, legend/key, compass rose, scale and date; 4 direction questions answered correctly using the compass rose. Proficient = all elements + 4/4 directions.

## Art & Music 2: Reading rhythm: ta, ti-ti, and rest

**Prepare:** Rhythm flash cards (draw quarter notes, eighth-note pairs, quarter rests on index cards), rhythm sticks or pots.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking ti-ti takes two beats: two eighth notes share ONE beat.
- Skipping the rest: a rest still takes up a beat in silence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ta and ti-ti
*Student:* Learn the quarter note (ta) and eighth-note pair (ti-ti) and clap 8 four-beat flash cards.

*How to teach:* Teach ta (quarter note, 1 sound per beat) and ti-ti (two eighth notes, 2 sounds per beat). Clap 8 cards.

- **Q:** How many beats is ti-ti?
  **A:** One beat (two quick sounds).
- **Q:** Clap: ta ti-ti ta ta.
  **A:** Clap, clap-clap (quick), clap, clap.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rest
*Student:* Learn the quarter rest (silent beat) and read patterns with rests; play 'rhythm echo' with a parent.

*How to teach:* Teach the quarter rest; read patterns with rests; play rhythm echo.

- **Q:** What do you do on a quarter rest?
  **A:** Stay silent for one beat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Word rhythms
*Student:* Match food words to rhythms (pie = ta, apple = ti-ti, watermelon = ti-ti ti-ti) and write 4 food-word rhythms.

*How to teach:* Match food words: pie = ta; apple = ti-ti; watermelon = ti-ti ti-ti. Write 4 food rhythms.

- **Q:** What rhythm is 'pizza'?
  **A:** Ti-ti.
- **Q:** How many beats is 'watermelon'?
  **A:** Two beats (ti-ti ti-ti).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Play rhythms
*Student:* Play written rhythms on rhythm sticks, pots, or a drum while keeping a steady beat.

*How to teach:* Play written rhythms on sticks or pots while keeping the beat.

- **Q:** Play: ti-ti ti-ti ta rest.
  **A:** Four quick hits, one hit, one silent beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Read and clap 6 new four-beat rhythms accurately and write 2 rhythms that a parent claps.

*How to teach:* Read 6 new rhythms; write 2 for a parent to clap.

- **Q:** How many beats must each card have?
  **A:** Four.

**Assessment:** Rhythm reading and writing check.

**Answer key:** Rhythm reading: claps 6 new 4-beat rhythms correctly (ta = one sound on a beat; ti-ti = two even sounds on one beat; rest = silent beat). Writing: 2 rhythms with exactly 4 beats each that a parent can clap. Proficient = 5-6 correct + 2 correct written rhythms.

## PE 2: Leaping

**Prepare:** Rolled towel or jump rope, sidewalk chalk; a drum for rhythm; tape measure.

**Watch for:**
- Leaping off two feet: a leap takes off from one foot and lands on the other.
- Landing stiff: land softly with a bent knee.

### Day 1 (Monday): Run and reach
*Student:* Run 3 steps and leap over a rolled towel, reaching forward with the lead leg.

*How to teach:* Run 3 steps, leap over a rolled towel reaching forward with the lead leg.

- **Q:** What is a leap?
  **A:** Take off from one foot and land on the other foot, stretching long in the air.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): River leap
*Student:* Draw a chalk 'river' that gets wider; leap across from the narrow end to the wide end.

*How to teach:* Draw a chalk river that gets wider; leap from narrow to wide.

- **Q:** Why run before leaping?
  **A:** Running gives more speed and distance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lead leg switch
*Student:* Practice leaping off the right foot and the left foot; note which is stronger.

*How to teach:* Leap off the right foot, then left; compare.

- **Q:** Which foot is stronger for take-off?
  **A:** Answers vary; practice both.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Leap series
*Student:* Run-run-leap pattern down the yard to a drumbeat; land softly with bent knees.

*How to teach:* Run-run-leap pattern to a drumbeat; land with bent knees.

- **Q:** How do you land softly?
  **A:** Bend your knees and land on the ball of your foot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Leap the widest river you can 3 times, record your best, and explain how a leap differs from a jump.

*How to teach:* Leap the widest river 3 times; record the best.

- **Q:** How is a leap different from a jump?
  **A:** Leap: one foot to the other. Jump: take off from two feet.

**Assessment:** Leap cue checklist and recorded distance.

**Answer key:** Leap checklist: running approach; takes off one foot, lands on the other; legs reach forward and back in the air; soft landing. Record best leap distance. Correct explanation: a leap goes from one foot to the other; a jump takes off from two feet (and usually lands on two). Proficient = all cues + correct explanation.

## Health 2: Germs and handwashing

**Prepare:** Lotion, glitter, soap, timer, a plate of water and ground pepper, dish soap.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a quick rinse is enough: scrub with soap for at least 20 seconds.
- Coughing into hands: cough or sneeze into your elbow so germs don't go onto hands.

### Day 1 (Monday): Glitter germs
*Student:* Put lotion and glitter on hands, shake hands with family and see how 'germs' spread.

*How to teach:* Put lotion and glitter on your hand; shake hands with family; see how 'germs' spread.

- **Q:** What are germs?
  **A:** Tiny living things (like bacteria and viruses) that can make you sick.
- **Q:** How did the glitter show germs spreading?
  **A:** It moved from hand to hand when we touched.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Handwashing steps
*Student:* Learn the 5 handwashing steps (wet, lather, scrub 20 seconds, rinse, dry) and sing 'Happy Birthday' twice.

*How to teach:* Teach 5 steps: wet, lather, scrub 20 seconds, rinse, dry; hum 'Happy Birthday' twice.

- **Q:** How long should you scrub?
  **A:** At least 20 seconds (Happy Birthday twice).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pepper experiment
*Student:* Sprinkle pepper on water and touch it with a soapy finger to see soap push 'germs' away.

*How to teach:* Sprinkle pepper on water; touch with a soapy finger; pepper rushes away.

- **Q:** What happened to the pepper?
  **A:** It moved away from the soapy finger.
- **Q:** What does this show?
  **A:** Soap helps push away and remove germs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cover coughs
*Student:* Practice coughing and sneezing into an elbow and learn how vaccines help protect us.

*How to teach:* Practice coughing and sneezing into an elbow; explain vaccines help the body learn to fight certain germs.

- **Q:** Why sneeze into your elbow?
  **A:** So germs don't spread to your hands and then to other people.
- **Q:** How do vaccines help?
  **A:** They teach your body to fight certain germs so you don't get as sick.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate handwashing correctly and list 3 times you should wash hands.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate handwashing; list 3 times to wash.

- **Q:** Name 3 times to wash hands.
  **A:** Before eating, after the bathroom, after sneezing/coughing (also after playing outside or with pets).

**Assessment:** Handwashing demonstration checklist.

**Answer key:** Handwashing checklist: wet hands; lather with soap; scrub at least 20 seconds (backs, between fingers, under nails); rinse; dry with a clean towel. Times to wash (any 3): before eating, after using the bathroom, after coughing/sneezing/blowing nose, after playing outside, after touching pets, before helping cook. Proficient = all 5 steps + 3 times.
