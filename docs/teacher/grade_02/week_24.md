# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 24

## ELA 2: Publishing opinions and comparatives

**Prepare:** Have opinion drafts, poster paper, markers, and the Cinderella books. Prepare a unit test: two short versions of a folktale, 15 prefix/suffix words to read, and point-of-view questions.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 'more taller' or 'happyest.' Teach -er for 2 things, -est for 3 or more, and change y to i (happier, happiest).
- Reasons without book evidence. Ask 'Which page shows that?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparatives
*Student:* Phonics/grammar: add -er and -est (tall, taller, tallest; happy, happier, happiest) and compare Cinderella characters ('Yeh-Shen's fish was kinder than…').

*How to teach:* Teach -er and -est (tall, taller, tallest; happy, happier, happiest). Compare Cinderella characters with comparatives.

- **Q:** Complete: tall, ___, ___.
  **A:** taller, tallest.
- **Q:** Complete: happy, ___, ___.
  **A:** happier, happiest (change y to i).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise the opinion
*Student:* Strengthen reasons with examples from the books and add a closing that restates the opinion. Check that each reason starts with a linking word.

*How to teach:* Strengthen reasons with book examples and add a closing that restates the opinion. Check each reason starts with a linking word.

- **Q:** Strengthen: 'Yeh-Shen is good because it's nice.'
  **A:** Example: Yeh-Shen is the best because her fish friend's magic bones help her, which shows kindness is rewarded.
- **Q:** What does a strong closing do?
  **A:** Restates the opinion in new words.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit for capitals, end marks, and spelling of prefix/suffix words; publish as a poster with a book cover drawing.

*How to teach:* Edit for capitals, end marks, and prefix/suffix spelling. Publish as a poster with a book-cover drawing.

- **Q:** Fix: 'i think yeh-shen is the best'
  **A:** I think Yeh-Shen is the best.
- **Q:** Spell 'careful.'
  **A:** c-a-r-e-f-u-l.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the opinion poster to family and answer one question. Listen to a family member's opinion and ask a question about their reasons.

*How to teach:* Present the poster to family and answer one question; listen to a family member's opinion and ask about their reasons.

- **Q:** Give a question about someone's reasons.
  **A:** Example: 'Why do you think that part was the most exciting?'
- **Q:** What should a listener do?
  **A:** Look at the speaker, listen without interrupting, then ask a question.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Unit test: compare two short versions of a folktale in a chart and sentences; decode 15 words with prefixes and suffixes; identify point of view.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test: compare two folktale versions, decode 15 words, identify point of view.

- **Q:** Who tells The True Story of the 3 Little Pigs!?
  **A:** The wolf (Alexander T. Wolf).
- **Q:** What does the suffix -less mean?
  **A:** Without.

**Assessment:** Published opinion poster (opinion rubric) and unit test.

**Answer key:** Opinion poster rubric (3 = proficient): opinion clearly stated; 3 reasons each with an example from a book; linking words; closing restates opinion; mostly correct capitals, end marks, prefix/suffix spelling. Unit test: comparison chart correctly completed for the two versions with 2 sentences; 13/15 prefix/suffix words decoded; point of view identified (who is telling the story).

## Math 2: Dollars and money word problems

**Prepare:** Play bills ($1, $5, $10, $20) and coins, price tags, and a pretend catalog (cut from ads) with whole-dollar prices.

**Watch for:**
- Writing both symbols ($0.65¢) or mixing them up. Teach: cents → ¢ after the number (65¢); dollars → $ before the number ($5).
- Making change by guessing. Teach counting up from the price to $1 with coins.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dollars
*Student:* Learn $1, $5, $10, $20 bills and count mixed bills; show that 100¢ = $1.

*How to teach:* Show $1, $5, $10, $20 bills; count mixed bills starting with the largest. Show that 100 pennies (or 4 quarters, or 10 dimes) = $1.

- **Q:** Count: one $20, one $5, two $1.
  **A:** $27.
- **Q:** How many cents equal $1?
  **A:** 100¢.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Money problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems like 'Jo has 2 dimes and 3 nickels. How many cents?' and 'Sam has $5 and $10 bills…'.

*How to teach:* Solve 6 problems with coins and bills. The child writes ¢ or $ correctly in each answer.

- **Q:** Jo has 2 dimes and 3 nickels. How many cents?
  **A:** 35¢.
- **Q:** Sam has a $5 bill and a $10 bill. How much money?
  **A:** $15.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Making change
*Student:* Pay $1 for items costing 65¢, 80¢, 42¢ and count up to find the change.

*How to teach:* Pay $1 for a 65¢ item and count up: 65 → 75 (dime) → 100 (quarter) = 35¢ change. Repeat for 80¢ and 42¢.

- **Q:** An item costs 80¢. You pay $1. What is your change?
  **A:** 20¢.
- **Q:** An item costs 42¢. You pay $1. What is your change?
  **A:** 58¢.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Budget challenge
*Student:* Choose items from a pretend catalog under $20 total and show the addition.

*How to teach:* Choose catalog items totaling under $20 and show the addition. Ask how much money would be left.

- **Q:** Items cost $7, $5, and $6. Total? Is it under $20?
  **A:** $18; yes, with $2 left.
- **Q:** Can you buy a $9 item and a $12 item with $20?
  **A:** No — $21 is $1 too much.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 8 money word problems using $ and ¢ correctly.

*How to teach:* Give 8 money word problems; check the symbols.

- **Q:** You have $20 and spend $7. How much is left?
  **A:** $13.
- **Q:** 3 quarters and 1 dime?
  **A:** 85¢.

**Assessment:** Money word-problem quiz.

**Answer key:** Money quiz (suggested 8): 1) 2 dimes + 3 nickels = 35¢ 2) $5 + $10 + $1 = $16 3) Pay $1 for a 65¢ item → 35¢ change 4) 80¢ item from $1 → 20¢ 5) 42¢ item from $1 → 58¢ 6) $20 − $7 = $13 7) 3 quarters + 1 dime = 85¢ 8) Pencils cost 30¢ each; buy 3 → 90¢. Proficient: 7/8 with symbols used correctly.

## Science 2: Fast Earth changes: volcanoes, earthquakes, landslides

**Prepare:** Find before-and-after photos of Mount St. Helens (USGS) and a short passage about the 1980 eruption. Gather clay or play dough, a cup, baking soda, vinegar, a tray, blocks, marbles, damp soil, and photos of a California coastal landslide.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the baking-soda volcano is how real volcanoes work. Real eruptions are melted rock (magma) and gases from deep underground.
- Thinking all Earth changes take a long time. Volcanoes, earthquakes, and landslides change land in minutes or hours.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mount St. Helens
*Student:* Compare before-and-after photos of Mount St. Helens (1980) and read a short passage about the eruption; record how much the mountain changed in one day.

*How to teach:* Compare before-and-after photos of Mount St. Helens and read about the 1980 eruption. Record how much the mountain changed in one day.

- **Q:** How did Mount St. Helens change?
  **A:** The eruption blew off the top and north side, making it much shorter with a huge crater.
- **Q:** How long did the change take?
  **A:** Minutes to hours (one day).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Model volcano
*Student:* Build a clay volcano around a cup, erupt it with baking soda and vinegar, and draw how the 'lava' changed the land around it.

*How to teach:* Build a clay volcano, erupt it with baking soda and vinegar, and draw how the 'lava' changed the land.

- **Q:** How is the model different from a real volcano?
  **A:** Real lava is melted rock that cools into new rock; the model is just foam.
- **Q:** How can lava change land?
  **A:** It covers the land and hardens into new rock.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Earthquake shake table
*Student:* Build a block tower on a tray resting on marbles, shake the tray, and record what happens; read about a California earthquake and how quickly it changed roads.

*How to teach:* Build a block tower on a tray resting on marbles and shake it. Read about a California earthquake.

- **Q:** What happened to the tower when shaken?
  **A:** It wobbled or fell.
- **Q:** How fast can an earthquake change roads?
  **A:** In seconds — it can crack or break roads.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Landslide
*Student:* Pack damp soil on a tilted tray, pour water at the top, and time how fast the soil slides; compare to photos of a California coastal landslide.

*How to teach:* Pack damp soil on a tilted tray, pour water at the top, and time how fast it slides. Compare to landslide photos.

- **Q:** What made the soil slide?
  **A:** Water made it heavy and slippery, and gravity pulled it down the slope.
- **Q:** Is a landslide fast or slow?
  **A:** Fast — seconds to minutes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Fill a 3-row chart (event, how long it took, evidence and source) for the volcano, earthquake, and landslide.

*How to teach:* Fill a 3-row chart (event, how long, evidence and source).

- **Q:** How long does an earthquake last?
  **A:** Usually seconds to a few minutes.
- **Q:** What is your source for the volcano?
  **A:** Mount St. Helens photos and passage (USGS).

**Assessment:** Fast-change evidence chart citing at least two sources.

**Answer key:** Fast-change chart (3 = proficient): Volcano — Mount St. Helens erupted on May 18, 1980; the top and side of the mountain blasted away in minutes to hours (it lost about 1,300 feet of height); source: USGS photos/passage. Earthquake — shaking lasts seconds to minutes and can crack roads and move ground; source: California earthquake reading. Landslide — soil and rock slide down a slope in seconds to minutes; source: model test and landslide photo. At least two sources named.

## People Who Make a Difference: Money, trade, and markets

**Prepare:** Play money; materials for 5 items to sell (bookmarks, drawings); price tags and a sign.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking barter is easy: both people must want what the other has.
- Thinking profit is all the money you collect: profit is what is left after paying your costs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Barter
*Student:* Try to trade toys or snacks with family members without money and record what problems happen (not everyone wants what you have).

*How to teach:* Try bartering; record problems.

- **Q:** What is barter?
  **A:** Trading goods or services without money.
- **Q:** What problem can happen with barter?
  **A:** The other person may not want what you have.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Money makes it easier
*Student:* Read about how people long ago traded with shells, salt, and coins, and explain how money solves the barter problems.

*How to teach:* Read how people used shells, salt and coins; explain how money helps.

- **Q:** How does money solve barter problems?
  **A:** Everyone accepts money, so you can buy what you want from anyone.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Market prep
*Student:* Make 5 items to sell (bookmarks or drawings), decide prices, and make a sign for your market stall.

*How to teach:* Make 5 items, set prices, make a sign.

- **Q:** How do you decide a price?
  **A:** Think about cost to make it and what buyers will pay.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Market day
*Student:* Run a family market: sell your goods as a producer, then buy from a sibling or parent as a consumer; record sales.

*How to teach:* Run a family market; buy and sell; record sales.

- **Q:** If you sell 4 bookmarks for $2 each, how much do you earn?
  **A:** $8.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences: what you produced, what you consumed, and how buyers and sellers needed each other.

*How to teach:* Write 3 sentences: produced, consumed, needed each other.

- **Q:** What is a market?
  **A:** A place where people buy and sell goods.

**Assessment:** Market sales record and reflection.

**Answer key:** Sales record: items sold, prices, total money (e.g., 3 bookmarks × $1 + 2 drawings × $2 = $7). Reflection: what they produced (bookmarks), what they consumed (bought a snack), and how buyers and sellers needed each other (seller needs buyers' money; buyer wants seller's goods). Proficient = accurate math + all three ideas.

## Art & Music 2: Music in daily life and unit reflection

**Prepare:** Recordings: a lullaby, a march, a dance tune, a commercial jingle; paper and paints.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking music is only for listening for fun: music has purposes (sleep, marching, selling, celebrating, worship).
- Giving preferences without reasons: use music words (tempo, dynamics, instruments).

### Day 1 (Monday): Music everywhere
*Student:* List all the music you hear in one day (ads, video games, stores, church, car radio) and its purpose.

*How to teach:* List all music heard in one day and its purpose.

- **Q:** Why do stores play music?
  **A:** To make shopping pleasant or set a mood.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Purpose sort
*Student:* Listen to a lullaby, a march, a dance tune, and a jingle and explain how each piece's tempo and dynamics fit its purpose.

*How to teach:* Listen to a lullaby, march, dance tune and jingle; explain how tempo and dynamics fit.

- **Q:** Why is a lullaby slow and soft?
  **A:** To calm a baby to sleep.
- **Q:** What is a jingle?
  **A:** A short catchy song in an ad to help you remember a product.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family playlist
*Student:* Interview family about their favorite songs and why they like them; make a family playlist.

*How to teach:* Interview family about favorite songs; make a playlist.

- **Q:** Why does someone in your family like their song?
  **A:** Records their reason.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Music and art
*Student:* Paint while listening to two contrasting pieces and compare the paintings.

*How to teach:* Paint while listening to two contrasting pieces; compare.

- **Q:** How did the music change your painting?
  **A:** E.g., fast music made quick jagged strokes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Tell your favorite piece from the unit, using music words to explain why.

*How to teach:* Tell your favorite piece from the unit with music words.

- **Q:** Explain your favorite using a music word.
  **A:** Uses tempo, dynamics, timbre or instruments.

**Assessment:** Purpose sort and explanation of preferences.

**Answer key:** Purpose sort: lullaby - slow, soft, to calm for sleep; march - steady, strong, medium-fast, to move together/parade; dance tune - lively, strong beat, to dance; jingle - short, catchy, to help people remember a product. Proficient = all 4 correct with tempo/dynamics reasons + favorite explained with music words.

## PE 2: Dribble, pass and team games

**Prepare:** Balls (soccer and basketball), cones.

**Watch for:**
- Standing still after passing: move to an open space.
- Passing to a covered teammate: pass to the open one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pass and move
*Student:* Pass to a partner, then move to a new open spot to receive the next pass.

*How to teach:* Pass to a partner, then move to a new open spot.

- **Q:** What is open space?
  **A:** An area with no defenders where you can get the ball.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dribble tag
*Student:* Play dribble tag where everyone dribbles (by hand) and tries to tag others' balls out.

*How to teach:* Dribble tag (by hand): protect your ball and tap others away.

- **Q:** How do you protect the ball?
  **A:** Keep your body between the ball and the tagger.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 2 vs. 1 soccer
*Student:* Play a short 2 vs. 1 game with a family member defending.

*How to teach:* Short 2 vs. 1 soccer game.

- **Q:** How do two players beat one defender?
  **A:** Pass to the open teammate.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Basketball stations
*Student:* Rotate: stationary dribble, walking dribble, bounce pass, chest pass.

*How to teach:* Stations: stationary dribble, walking dribble, bounce pass, chest pass.

- **Q:** Where should a bounce pass bounce?
  **A:** About two-thirds of the way to your partner.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show a hand dribble, foot dribble and pass and tell how you helped your teammate.

*How to teach:* Show hand dribble, foot dribble and pass; tell how you helped your teammate.

- **Q:** What is a teammate?
  **A:** Someone on your side working toward the same goal.

**Assessment:** Skill station checklist; self-reflection on teamwork.

**Answer key:** Station checklist: stationary dribble, walking dribble, bounce pass, chest pass with correct cues; reflection names one way they helped a teammate (passed, moved to open space, encouraged). Proficient = 4 stations + specific reflection.

## Health 2: Pedestrian, bike and car safety

**Prepare:** Bike, helmet, chalk; a safe crosswalk; your car's booster seat.

**Watch for:**
- Looking only one way before crossing: look left, right, then left again.
- Wearing a helmet tipped back: it should sit level, two fingers above eyebrows.

### Day 1 (Monday): Street smart
*Student:* Practice stop, look left-right-left, and listen at a crosswalk with a parent.

*How to teach:* At a crosswalk with a parent: stop, look left-right-left, listen, cross.

- **Q:** Why look left again?
  **A:** Cars in the nearest lane come from the left, and things can change quickly.
- **Q:** What is a pedestrian?
  **A:** A person walking.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Helmet fit
*Student:* Check helmet fit with the 2-V-1 rule.

*How to teach:* Check helmet fit with 2-V-1.

- **Q:** What is the 2-V-1 rule?
  **A:** 2 fingers above eyebrows, straps make a V under ears, 1 finger under the chin strap.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bike skills
*Student:* Practice hand signals and stopping on a chalk course.

*How to teach:* Practice hand signals and stopping on a chalk course.

- **Q:** What is the signal for a left turn?
  **A:** Left arm straight out to the side.
- **Q:** For stop?
  **A:** Left arm bent down at the elbow, palm back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Car safety
*Student:* Learn why kids ride in booster seats in the back seat with seat belts.

*How to teach:* Learn why kids ride in boosters in the back seat with seat belts. California law: children under 8 ride in a car seat or booster in the back seat (unless 4'9" or taller).

- **Q:** Where is the safest place for kids to ride?
  **A:** In the back seat, buckled in a booster or car seat.
- **Q:** Why use a booster?
  **A:** It lifts you so the seat belt fits across your chest and hips, not your neck and belly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate crosswalk safety and helmet fit.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate crosswalk safety and helmet fit.

- **Q:** Where should you cross the street?
  **A:** At a crosswalk or corner, with an adult.

**Assessment:** Safety demonstration checklist.

**Answer key:** Demonstration checklist: crosswalk - stops at curb, looks left-right-left, listens, crosses with an adult, keeps looking. Helmet 2-V-1: two fingers above eyebrows, straps form a V under each ear, one finger fits under the buckled chin strap. Proficient = all steps.
