# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 29

## ELA 2: Volcanoes and earthquakes: reasons support points

**Prepare:** Get Volcanoes (Franklyn M. Branley, Let's-Read-and-Find-Out) and read it first. Gather clay (or play dough) around a small bottle, baking soda, vinegar, dish soap, red food coloring, and a tray. Print an earthquake passage and homophone pairs.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking magma and lava are different materials. Both are melted rock — magma is underground; lava is magma that has come out.
- Mixing up homophones (there/their). Their = belongs to them; there = a place.

### Day 1 (Monday): Volcanoes
*Student:* Read Volcanoes by Franklyn Branley and study the diagram of a volcano; label magma, crater, lava. Phonics: homophones to/two/too and there/their.

*How to teach:* Read Volcanoes and study the diagram; label magma, crater, lava. Teach to/two/too and there/their.

- **Q:** What is magma?
  **A:** Hot melted rock deep underground.
- **Q:** What is lava?
  **A:** Magma that comes out of a volcano onto the surface.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Author's points
*Student:* Find the point 'Volcanoes can build new land' and list the reasons the author gives (Hawaii, Surtsey). Write: The author says ___ because ___.

*How to teach:* Find the point 'Volcanoes can build new land' and list the author's reasons. Write 'The author says ___ because ___.'

- **Q:** What reasons support 'volcanoes can build new land'?
  **A:** The Hawaiian Islands were made by volcanoes, and the island of Surtsey rose from the sea near Iceland.
- **Q:** Complete: The author says volcanoes build land because ___.
  **A:** lava cools and hardens into new rock, piling up into islands.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Model eruption
*Student:* Build a clay volcano and erupt it with baking soda and vinegar; write 3 steps of what happened. Phonics: homophone pairs (see/sea, right/write).

*How to teach:* Build a clay volcano and erupt it with baking soda and vinegar. Write 3 steps. Teach see/sea and right/write.

- **Q:** Is the model eruption a real volcano?
  **A:** No — it is a chemical reaction making foam; a real eruption is melted rock from underground.
- **Q:** Use 'sea' in a sentence.
  **A:** Example: Surtsey rose out of the sea.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Earthquakes
*Student:* Read a ReadWorks or library passage on earthquakes and identify one point and two reasons. Use context to define 'fault' and 'plates.'

*How to teach:* Read an earthquake passage; identify one point and two reasons. Use context to define 'fault' and 'plates.'

- **Q:** What are plates?
  **A:** Giant pieces of Earth's crust that slowly move.
- **Q:** What is a fault?
  **A:** A crack in Earth's crust where plates move past each other and earthquakes can happen.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 29 review
*Student:* Reasons-and-points organizer on a new science passage; homophone quiz (12 items).

*How to teach:* Complete a points-and-reasons organizer on a new passage and take the homophone quiz.

- **Q:** Fix: 'Their are two volcanoes.'
  **A:** There are two volcanoes.
- **Q:** Choose: I like to ___ (write/right) stories.
  **A:** write.

**Assessment:** Point-and-reasons organizer, homophone quiz, written eruption steps.

**Answer key:** Point-and-reasons organizer: proficient states the author's point and 2 reasons from the passage (e.g., 'Volcanoes can build new land' — reasons: the Hawaiian Islands were built by volcanoes; the island of Surtsey rose out of the sea near Iceland in 1963). Homophone quiz (12): to/two/too, there/their, see/sea, right/write, know/no, hear/here — each used correctly in a sentence. Eruption steps: 1) put baking soda in the volcano 2) poured in vinegar 3) bubbly foam flowed out like lava.

## Math 2: Equal groups and arrays

**Prepare:** Counters, an egg carton, a muffin tin, grid paper. Prepare 8 array pictures and 4 equations for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Adding the number of rows and columns (a 3-by-4 array counted as 3 + 4 = 7). Count the objects: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
- Making rows of unequal length. In an array, every row has the same number.

### Day 1 (Monday): Equal groups
*Student:* Make equal groups of counters (4 groups of 3) and write repeated addition (3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12).

*How to teach:* Make 4 groups of 3 counters. Write 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12. Repeat with other equal groups.

- **Q:** 4 groups of 3: repeated addition and total?
  **A:** 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12.
- **Q:** 5 groups of 2?
  **A:** 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Arrays at home
*Student:* Find arrays at home (egg carton, muffin tin, window panes) and write repeated addition for rows and columns.

*How to teach:* Find arrays at home (egg carton: 2 rows of 6; muffin tin: 3 rows of 4). Write repeated addition by rows and by columns.

- **Q:** An egg carton has 2 rows of 6. Repeated addition?
  **A:** 6 + 6 = 12 (or by columns 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 12).
- **Q:** A muffin tin has 3 rows of 4. Total?
  **A:** 12 (4 + 4 + 4).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build arrays
*Student:* Build arrays up to 5 × 5 with counters and draw them on grid paper; write two equations for each (by rows and by columns).

*How to teach:* Build arrays up to 5 by 5 and draw them on grid paper. Write two equations for each (by rows and by columns).

- **Q:** 3 rows with 5 in each row: two equations?
  **A:** 5 + 5 + 5 = 15 and 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15.
- **Q:** How many in 4 rows of 4?
  **A:** 16.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Array stories
*Student:* Solve 6 array word problems ('There are 4 rows of chairs with 5 chairs each…').

*How to teach:* Solve 6 array stories with drawings and repeated addition.

- **Q:** There are 4 rows of chairs with 5 chairs each. How many chairs?
  **A:** 20 chairs; 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 20.
- **Q:** 3 rows of 3 cookies. How many cookies?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write repeated addition for 8 arrays and draw arrays for 4 equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz: repeated addition for 8 arrays and draw arrays for 4 equations.

- **Q:** Draw an array for 4 + 4.
  **A:** 2 rows of 4.
- **Q:** 5 rows of 5?
  **A:** 25.

**Assessment:** Array quiz.

**Answer key:** Array quiz (suggested): 1) 2 rows of 5 → 5 + 5 = 10 2) 3 rows of 4 → 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 3) 4 rows of 3 → 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 4) 5 rows of 2 → 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 10 5) 3 rows of 5 → 15 6) 4 rows of 4 → 16 7) 5 rows of 5 → 25 8) 2 rows of 3 → 6. Draw: a) 3 + 3 + 3 → 3 rows of 3 b) 4 + 4 → 2 rows of 4 c) 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 → 4 rows of 5 d) 2 + 2 + 2 → 3 rows of 2. Proficient: 11/12.

## Science 2: Testing solutions that slow erosion

**Prepare:** Prepare two trays of soil (one planted with grass seed two weeks earlier, or covered with moss), craft sticks, pebbles, a measuring cup, clear cups to catch runoff, and photos of retaining walls, windbreaks, levees, and sand fences.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a solution must stop erosion completely. Solutions slow erosion; compare how much each reduces it.
- Using different amounts of water for each test. Use the same 2 cups each time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Real solutions
*Student:* Look at photos of retaining walls, rows of trees as windbreaks, levees, and sand fences, and write what problem each solves.

*How to teach:* Look at photos of retaining walls, windbreaks, levees, and sand fences; write what each solves.

- **Q:** What does a levee do?
  **A:** Holds back river water to prevent floods.
- **Q:** What does a row of trees as a windbreak do?
  **A:** Slows wind so it blows away less soil.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plants vs. bare soil
*Student:* Pour 2 cups of water down a tray of bare soil and a tray with grass growing; collect and compare the muddy runoff in cups.

*How to teach:* Pour 2 cups of water down bare soil and a grass tray; compare the runoff.

- **Q:** Which runoff was muddier?
  **A:** The bare soil.
- **Q:** Why did grass help?
  **A:** Roots hold the soil in place.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Barriers
*Student:* Build a craft-stick wall and a pebble wall on a sand hill and test each with the same amount of water; measure how much sand moves.

*How to teach:* Build a craft-stick wall and a pebble wall on a sand hill; test each with the same water.

- **Q:** Which wall moved less sand?
  **A:** Answer from data.
- **Q:** Why use the same water amount?
  **A:** To make it a fair test.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Data table
*Student:* Record results of all four tests (bare, grass, sticks, pebbles) in a table and rank them from least to most sand lost.

*How to teach:* Record all four tests in a table and rank least to most sand lost.

- **Q:** Which lost the most?
  **A:** Bare soil.
- **Q:** Which lost the least?
  **A:** Usually grass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write which solution worked best and one weakness of it, using the data as evidence.

*How to teach:* Write which solution worked best and one weakness, using data.

- **Q:** Weakness of planting grass?
  **A:** It takes time to grow.
- **Q:** Weakness of a pebble wall?
  **A:** Water can flow between pebbles.

**Assessment:** Solution test data table and written conclusion.

**Answer key:** Data table ranking (typical): grass loses the least sand/soil (clearest runoff), then pebble wall, then craft-stick wall, and bare soil loses the most. Conclusion (3 = proficient): names the best solution (usually plants) with data as evidence and one weakness (e.g., grass takes weeks to grow).

## People Who Make a Difference: Albert Einstein and Golda Meir

**Prepare:** 'On a Beam of Light' by Jennifer Berne (Einstein); a biography of Golda Meir; world map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Einstein was always a top student: he was curious and asked big questions, and sometimes struggled in strict schools.
- Thinking immigrants stay in one place: Golda Meir moved from Ukraine to the U.S. and later to Israel.

### Day 1 (Monday): Albert Einstein
*Student:* Read a picture-book biography of Albert Einstein (e.g., On a Beam of Light by Jennifer Berne) and list the questions he asked as a child.

*How to teach:* Read 'On a Beam of Light'; list Einstein's childhood questions.

- **Q:** What object made young Einstein curious?
  **A:** A compass (the needle always pointed north).
- **Q:** What did he imagine?
  **A:** Riding on a beam of light.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Curiosity
*Student:* Write 3 'I wonder' questions of your own about the world, as Einstein did, and choose one to investigate.

*How to teach:* Write 3 'I wonder' questions; choose one to investigate.

- **Q:** What is curiosity?
  **A:** Wanting to know and learn about things.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Golda Meir
*Student:* Read about Golda Meir, who grew up in Milwaukee, taught school, and became prime minister of Israel; locate Ukraine, the U.S., and Israel on a map.

*How to teach:* Read about Golda Meir; locate Ukraine, the U.S. and Israel.

- **Q:** What job did Golda Meir have in the U.S.?
  **A:** Teacher.
- **Q:** What did she become?
  **A:** Prime minister of Israel.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Make a Venn diagram comparing Einstein and Meir (both immigrants, different kinds of work).

*How to teach:* Make a Venn diagram comparing Einstein and Meir.

- **Q:** How are Einstein and Meir alike?
  **A:** Both were immigrants who made a difference.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Add both to the hero timeline and explain which trait each showed most.

*How to teach:* Add both to the hero timeline; name traits.

- **Q:** Which trait did Einstein show most?
  **A:** Curiosity and imagination.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram and hero timeline entries.

**Answer key:** Venn diagram: Einstein - scientist, born in Germany, asked questions about light, time and space, won Nobel Prize; Meir - teacher and leader, born in Ukraine, grew up in Milwaukee, became prime minister of Israel; Both - immigrants who moved to new countries, worked hard, made a difference. Traits: Einstein - curiosity/imagination; Meir - leadership/determination. Proficient = accurate Venn + trait each.

## Art & Music 2: Creative movement: levels, shapes, and pathways

**Prepare:** Open floor, scarves, masking tape, instrumental music.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing locomotor and nonlocomotor: locomotor travels; nonlocomotor stays in place.
- Forgetting middle level: high (tiptoes/reaching), middle (standing/bent), low (near floor).

### Day 1 (Monday): Movement words
*Student:* Practice locomotor movements (walk, skip, gallop, slide) and nonlocomotor movements (twist, bend, stretch, swing) and call them out as a family game.

*How to teach:* Practice locomotor (walk, skip, gallop, slide) and nonlocomotor (twist, bend, stretch, swing) moves; call them out.

- **Q:** Is twist locomotor or nonlocomotor?
  **A:** Nonlocomotor (stays in place).
- **Q:** Is gallop locomotor?
  **A:** Yes, it travels.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Levels and shapes
*Student:* Freeze in high, middle, and low shapes (curved, straight, twisted) when the music stops; make 6 different statues.

*How to teach:* Freeze in high, middle, low shapes (curved, straight, twisted); make 6 statues.

- **Q:** Show a low, curved shape.
  **A:** Curled up close to the floor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pathways
*Student:* Lay masking-tape straight, curved, and zigzag pathways and travel each with a different locomotor movement.

*How to teach:* Tape straight, curved and zigzag pathways; travel each with a different movement.

- **Q:** What is a pathway?
  **A:** The path your body makes as it travels across the floor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Images to movement
*Student:* Create movement inspired by pictures of a storm, a seed growing, and a melting snowman, using scarves.

*How to teach:* Create movement for a storm, a growing seed, a melting snowman with scarves.

- **Q:** How might a melting snowman move?
  **A:** Starts high and slowly sinks to a low level.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a 30-second dance with 3 levels, 2 pathways, and a beginning and ending shape.

*How to teach:* Perform a 30-second dance with 3 levels, 2 pathways, beginning and ending shapes.

- **Q:** What levels did you use?
  **A:** High, middle and low.

**Assessment:** Short dance sequence meeting the movement checklist.

**Answer key:** Movement checklist for the 30-second dance: beginning shape; uses 3 levels (high, middle, low); 2 pathways (e.g., straight, curved, zigzag); ending shape. Proficient = all 5 items.

## PE 2: Hoops, ribbons and rhythm

**Prepare:** Hula hoop, ribbon stick, music.

**Watch for:**
- Hooping with circles of the hips: hula hoop moves with a forward-back rock.
- Ribbon patterns with only the arm: use the wrist for smooth shapes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hoop skills
*Student:* Practice waist hooping, arm hooping and rolling a hoop and chasing it.

*How to teach:* Waist hooping, arm hooping, rolling a hoop and chasing it.

- **Q:** How do you keep a hoop up at your waist?
  **A:** Rock your body forward and back.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ribbon shapes
*Student:* Make circles, figure eights, snakes and spirals with a ribbon stick to music.

*How to teach:* Make circles, figure eights, snakes and spirals with a ribbon.

- **Q:** Draw a figure eight in the air.
  **A:** Two loops connected like the number 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pattern build
*Student:* Create an 8-count pattern with the hoop or ribbon and repeat it 4 times.

*How to teach:* Create an 8-count pattern; repeat 4 times.

- **Q:** How many counts in your pattern?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Jump rope chant
*Student:* Learn a jump rope rhyme (e.g., 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear') and jump to it.

*How to teach:* Learn 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear' and jump to it.

- **Q:** What do you do on 'turn around'?
  **A:** Turn around while jumping.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform your hoop or ribbon pattern and tell how it made you feel.

*How to teach:* Perform the pattern; tell how it felt.

- **Q:** How did it make you feel?
  **A:** Answers vary: happy, proud, calm.

**Assessment:** Pattern performance and reflection.

**Answer key:** Pattern performance: 8-count hoop or ribbon pattern repeated 4 times, on the beat. Reflection names a feeling. Proficient = 4 repetitions on beat.

## Health 2: Online safety and weapons

**Prepare:** Computer or tablet; scenario cards. PARENTAL PREVIEW: keep weapon talk simple and calm; check that any firearms in your home are stored locked and unloaded.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a toy-looking gun is safe to touch: never touch; you can't tell if it's real or loaded.
- Thinking online friends are real friends: you can't be sure who someone is online.

### Day 1 (Monday): Personal info
*Student:* Learn which information stays private online (name, address, school, photos).

*How to teach:* Teach private information: full name, address, school, phone number, photos.

- **Q:** Is it okay to tell someone online your address?
  **A:** No, keep it private.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Online rules
*Student:* Make family online rules: ask first, tell an adult about anything uncomfortable.

*How to teach:* Make family online rules: ask first, tell an adult about anything uncomfortable.

- **Q:** What do you do if a message makes you uncomfortable?
  **A:** Stop, don't answer, tell a trusted adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weapon safety
*Student:* Learn: if you see a gun, don't touch it, leave the area, tell an adult.

*How to teach:* Teach: if you see a gun, don't touch, leave the area, tell an adult.

- **Q:** What do you do if you find a gun?
  **A:** Don't touch it, leave the area, tell an adult right away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice
*Student:* Role-play scenarios about online messages and finding a weapon.

*How to teach:* Role-play online messages and finding a weapon.

- **Q:** Someone online asks for your photo. What do you do?
  **A:** Say no, don't send it, tell a parent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Unit quiz on safety rules from weeks 24–29.

*How to teach:* Give the safety unit quiz.

- **Q:** What is personal information?
  **A:** Facts about you that could help someone find you, like your address or school.

**Assessment:** Safety unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Safety quiz (weeks 24-29, sample): 1) Look left-right-left before crossing. 2) Helmet 2-V-1. 3) Kids ride in the back seat in a booster. 4) Never swim alone; reach or throw, don't go. 5) Stop, drop, roll; crawl low; two ways out; meeting place. 6) Drop, cover, hold on. 7) 911: name, address, emergency. 8) No-Go-Tell. 9) Keep name, address, school and photos private online; tell an adult about uncomfortable messages. 10) If you see a gun: don't touch, leave the area, tell an adult. Proficient = 8/10.
