# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 35

## ELA 2: Book review: opinion writing about a favorite book

**Prepare:** Preview Winnie-the-Pooh Chapter 10. Gather favorite books from the year and a computer or tablet for typing. Write 20 review spelling words from the year (said, friend, because, people, rain, beach, boat, night, bird, turn, happy, sky, unhappy, helpful, didn't, little, write, right, there, their).

**Watch for:**
- Book reviews that retell the whole story, including the ending. Summarize the beginning only — no spoilers.
- Reasons that are just opinions repeated. Each reason should point to something in the book.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finish Pooh
*Student:* Read Chapter 10 (the party for Pooh) and discuss how the book ends. Rate Winnie-the-Pooh with stars and give one reason.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 10 (the party for Pooh). Discuss how the book ends. Rate the book with stars and give a reason.

- **Q:** Why does Christopher Robin give a party?
  **A:** To honor Pooh for his clever idea to rescue Piglet during the flood.
- **Q:** What gift does Pooh get?
  **A:** A special pencil case with pencils.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan a review
*Student:* Choose the favorite book of the year and plan a review: summary without spoiling, opinion, 3 reasons, recommendation.

*How to teach:* Choose the favorite book of the year and plan a review: summary without spoilers, opinion, 3 reasons, recommendation.

- **Q:** What is a spoiler?
  **A:** Telling the ending or a surprise.
- **Q:** Give a recommendation sentence.
  **A:** Example: Kids who love funny animals will enjoy this book.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the book review using linking words and at least 4 strong describing words.

*How to teach:* Draft the review with linking words and at least 4 describing words.

- **Q:** Give 2 describing words for Frog and Toad.
  **A:** Examples: funny, kind, cozy.
- **Q:** Name linking words for reasons.
  **A:** because, also, another reason.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Type and publish
*Student:* Type the review on a computer or tablet with help, add a picture of the cover, and print or share it.

*How to teach:* Type the review with help, add a cover picture, and print or share it.

- **Q:** Which key makes a capital letter?
  **A:** The Shift key.
- **Q:** Why add a cover picture?
  **A:** So readers know which book it is.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 35 review
*Student:* Read the review aloud and listen to a family member's review; spelling test of 20 review words from the year.

*How to teach:* Read the review aloud and listen to a family member's; give the 20-word spelling test.

- **Q:** Spell 'people.'
  **A:** p-e-o-p-l-e.
- **Q:** Spell 'their' (belongs to them).
  **A:** t-h-e-i-r.

**Assessment:** Published typed book review (opinion rubric) and cumulative spelling test.

**Answer key:** Book review rubric (3 = proficient): short summary without the ending; clear opinion; 3 reasons with examples; linking words; recommendation ('Readers who like… will enjoy this'). Cumulative spelling test of 20 words: 17+ proficient.

## Math 2: Review: measurement, data, money, time

**Prepare:** Rulers, coins, a clock, graph paper. Prepare 15 mixed review items for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Measuring from 1 instead of 0. Check the starting point every time.
- Counting coins in random order. Sort from greatest to least value first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Measurement review
*Student:* Measure 5 objects in inches and centimeters and solve 2 compare-length problems.

*How to teach:* Measure 5 objects in inches and cm; solve 2 compare-length problems.

- **Q:** A pencil is 15 cm and a crayon is 9 cm. How much longer is the pencil?
  **A:** 6 cm.
- **Q:** For the same pencil, which number is bigger: inches or cm?
  **A:** Centimeters.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Money and time
*Student:* Count coin sets and tell time at 6 moments today; solve 3 money problems.

*How to teach:* Count coin sets and tell time at 6 moments today; solve 3 money problems.

- **Q:** 2 quarters and 1 dime?
  **A:** 60¢.
- **Q:** The minute hand is on 3 and the hour hand just past 2. Time?
  **A:** 2:15.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Graph project
*Student:* Survey family about favorite summer activity and make a bar graph with 3 questions.

*How to teach:* Survey family about favorite summer activity, make a bar graph, and write 3 questions about it.

- **Q:** Swim 6, bike 4. How many more chose swim?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** What must a bar graph have?
  **A:** A title, labels, and a scale starting at 0 with equal spaces.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shapes and shares
*Student:* Draw shapes from clues and partition shapes into equal shares.

*How to teach:* Draw shapes from clues and partition shapes into equal shares.

- **Q:** Draw a shape with 4 sides and 4 square corners.
  **A:** A square or rectangle.
- **Q:** Divide a circle into thirds.
  **A:** 3 equal parts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Mixed practice set of 15 items across measurement, data, geometry.

*How to teach:* Give 15 mixed items across measurement, data, and geometry.

- **Q:** $1 − 35¢?
  **A:** 65¢.
- **Q:** How many faces on a cube?
  **A:** 6.

**Assessment:** Mixed review set.

**Answer key:** Mixed review (suggested 15): 1) measure a 6 cm line → 6 cm 2) measure a 3 in line → 3 in 3) 12 in − 8 in = 4 in 4) 2 quarters + 1 dime = 60¢ 5) $1 − 35¢ = 65¢ 6) clock showing 4:45 → 4:45 / quarter to 5 7) bar graph: 9 − 5 = 4 more 8) line plot: most common value is the tallest stack 9) triangle → 3 sides 10) hexagon → 6 sides 11) cube → 6 faces 12) halves → 2 equal parts 13) 3 rows of 4 → 12 14) fourths of a circle → 4 equal parts 15) 25 cm + 30 cm = 55 cm. Proficient: 13/15.

## Science 2: Engineering challenge: design a bridge

**Prepare:** Gather paper, 10 straws, craft sticks, 50 cm of tape, two stacks of books 20 cm apart, pennies, a paper cup with string, and the Unit 2 strength data.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a flat sheet of paper is strong. Folding paper (accordion) makes it much stronger.
- Adding pennies unevenly or dropping them. Add one at a time gently.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define the problem
*Student:* Problem: build a bridge across a 20-cm 'river' between two book stacks that holds a cup of pennies; list criteria and limits (10 straws, 1 sheet of paper, 50 cm of tape).

*How to teach:* Define the problem: build a bridge across a 20-cm gap that holds a cup of pennies; list criteria and limits.

- **Q:** What are the limits (constraints)?
  **A:** 10 straws, 1 sheet of paper, 50 cm of tape.
- **Q:** What must the bridge do?
  **A:** Span 20 cm and hold a cup of pennies.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sketch and choose
*Student:* Sketch two bridge designs (flat, folded accordion, arch) and choose materials using your strength-test data from Unit 2.

*How to teach:* Sketch two designs (flat, accordion, arch) and choose materials using strength data.

- **Q:** Which shape is stronger: flat paper or accordion-folded paper?
  **A:** Accordion-folded.
- **Q:** Why use earlier data?
  **A:** To choose the strongest materials.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build the chosen bridge and check that it spans the gap without touching the 'water.'

*How to teach:* Build the bridge and check it spans the gap without touching the 'water.'

- **Q:** How long must it be?
  **A:** Longer than 20 cm so it rests on both stacks.
- **Q:** Why not touch the water?
  **A:** That's a rule of the challenge — the bridge must span the river.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test
*Student:* Hang a cup from the bridge and add pennies one at a time, recording the count when it sags or falls.

*How to teach:* Hang a cup and add pennies one at a time; record when it sags or falls.

- **Q:** How many pennies did it hold?
  **A:** Answer from data.
- **Q:** Why add one at a time?
  **A:** To get an exact count.

### Day 5 (Friday): Improve
*Student:* Change one part (fold the paper, add a straw truss) and retest; record the new penny count.

*How to teach:* Change one part (fold the paper, add a straw truss) and retest.

- **Q:** Did the change help?
  **A:** Answer from data.
- **Q:** Why does folding help?
  **A:** Folds make the paper stiffer so it bends less.

**Assessment:** Bridge design sketches and load-test data.

**Answer key:** Design sketches: two labeled designs (flat, accordion, or arch). Load-test data: pennies held before sagging/falling for the first and improved bridge (folded or trussed designs typically hold many more pennies than flat paper). Proficient: data recorded and the improvement explained ('folding the paper made it stiffer, so it held more pennies').

## People Who Make a Difference: Hero project: living museum

**Prepare:** Library biographies; poster board; costume items; note cards for the speech.

**Watch for:**
- Copying sentences from books: put facts in your own words.
- Speaking about the hero in third person in a living museum: speak as the hero ('I am…').

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose and research
*Student:* Choose one hero from the unit (or another approved hero) and gather 6 facts from 2 sources: early life, challenge, action, and legacy.

*How to teach:* Choose a hero; gather 6 facts from 2 sources.

- **Q:** What is a biography?
  **A:** A true story of a person's life written by someone else.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Timeline and traits
*Student:* Make a 5-event timeline and a list of 3 character traits with evidence for your hero.

*How to teach:* Make a 5-event timeline and 3 traits with evidence.

- **Q:** What evidence shows your hero's trait?
  **A:** Specific action from research.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poster
*Student:* Create a biography poster with a portrait, timeline, traits, and one primary source image or quote.

*How to teach:* Create a biography poster with portrait, timeline, traits and a primary source.

- **Q:** What primary source did you include?
  **A:** Photo, letter, quote from the time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Write and practice a 1-minute speech in first person ('I am Sally Ride…').

*How to teach:* Write and practice a 1-minute first-person speech.

- **Q:** How does a living museum speech start?
  **A:** 'I am ___.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Living museum
*Student:* Present the living museum in costume to family and answer audience questions.

*How to teach:* Present the living museum in costume; answer questions.

- **Q:** What is a legacy?
  **A:** What a person leaves behind that helps others after them.

**Assessment:** Biography poster and living-museum presentation (rubric).

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = 6 accurate facts from 2 sources (early life, challenge, action, legacy), 5-event timeline in order, 3 traits with evidence, primary source image or quote, clear 1-minute first-person speech, answers questions; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = several missing parts or inaccuracies; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 2: Rehearsing the showcase

**Prepare:** Recordings, paper for programs and invitations.

**Watch for:**
- Practicing only from the beginning: practice the tricky spot by itself.
- Thinking audiences don't need manners: they listen quietly and clap at the end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Program
*Student:* Choose 3 musical pieces for the showcase (a folk song, your rhythm composition, your community song) and write a program.

*How to teach:* Choose 3 pieces; write a program.

- **Q:** What is a program?
  **A:** A list of what will be performed, in order.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse each piece and mark one tricky spot to fix.

*How to teach:* Rehearse each; mark one tricky spot.

- **Q:** What is your tricky spot?
  **A:** Names a measure or line.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feedback
*Student:* Perform for a family member and apply one piece of feedback.

*How to teach:* Perform for family; apply feedback.

- **Q:** What feedback did you use?
  **A:** Specific change.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Invitations
*Student:* Design invitations with artwork for family and friends.

*How to teach:* Design invitations with artwork.

- **Q:** What does an invitation need?
  **A:** What, when, where, who.

### Day 5 (Friday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Run the whole showcase (gallery tour plus music) from start to finish.

*How to teach:* Run the whole showcase start to finish.

- **Q:** What is audience etiquette?
  **A:** Listening quietly, not talking, clapping at the end.

**Assessment:** Dress rehearsal with checklist.

**Answer key:** Dress-rehearsal checklist: program lists 3 pieces in order (a folk song, rhythm composition, community song); gallery tour given; tricky spots improved; starts and ends performances clearly (bow). Proficient = all checklist items.

## PE 2: Skill showcase

**Prepare:** Balls, cones, jump rope, paddle, end-of-year checklist with smiley scale.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing to finish: show good form.
- Only rating favorite skills high: be honest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Locomotor test
*Student:* Perform skip, leap, gallop, slide and standing long jump; parent uses checklist.

*How to teach:* Perform skip, leap, gallop, slide, standing long jump.

- **Q:** Which of those uses a two-foot take-off?
  **A:** Standing long jump.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Throw & catch
*Student:* Show overhand throw to a target and catches high and low.

*How to teach:* Overhand throw to a target; catch high and low.

- **Q:** How do you catch a low ball?
  **A:** Pinkies together.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dribble & kick
*Student:* Show hand dribble around cones and kick to a target.

*How to teach:* Hand dribble around cones; kick to a target.

- **Q:** Where do you plant your foot?
  **A:** Beside the ball.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Strike & rope
*Student:* Show paddle up-ups and jump rope forward.

*How to teach:* Paddle up-ups and forward jump rope.

- **Q:** What is the paddle face?
  **A:** The flat hitting surface.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-rate each skill with a smiley scale and pick one to keep practicing.

*How to teach:* Self-rate each skill; choose one to practice.

- **Q:** Which skill will you keep practicing and how?
  **A:** Specific skill and plan.

**Assessment:** End-of-year skill checklist and self-rating.

**Answer key:** End-of-year checklist (cues): skip (step-hop, opposite arms), leap (one foot to the other), gallop (lead foot), slide (sideways, no crossing), long jump (arm swing, two-foot landing), overhand throw (4 cues), catch high/low (thumbs/pinkies), hand dribble, kick (plant, inside of foot), paddle up-ups, jump rope. Proficient = most skills show 2+ cues; self-rating honest with one skill chosen to practice.

## Health 2: Health advocacy

**Prepare:** Poster paper, markers, or a device to record a short video.

**Watch for:**
- Making a message too long: one clear message works best.
- Forgetting the audience: think about who will see it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a message
*Student:* Choose a health topic from this year to teach others.

*How to teach:* Choose a health topic from this year.

- **Q:** What does advocate mean?
  **A:** To speak up to help others make healthy choices.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a poster or short video message.

*How to teach:* Plan a poster or short video.

- **Q:** Who is your audience?
  **A:** Names audience.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Create the poster or video.

*How to teach:* Create the poster or video.

- **Q:** What fact supports your message?
  **A:** Accurate fact.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Share your message with family or neighbors.

*How to teach:* Share with family or neighbors.

- **Q:** How did people respond?
  **A:** Child reports.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why your message matters.

*How to teach:* Explain why your message matters.

- **Q:** Why does your message matter?
  **A:** Explains health benefit.

**Assessment:** Advocacy project rubric.

**Answer key:** Advocacy rubric: 4 = one clear health message, accurate facts, eye-catching design, shared with an audience, explains why it matters; 3 = mostly clear with small gaps; 2 = unclear or inaccurate in parts; 1 = incomplete. Example: 'Wash hands 20 seconds to stop germs.'
