# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 19

## ELA 2: Sensory poems and compound sentences

**Prepare:** Mark Stevenson's 'Rain' and 'Windy Nights' in A Child's Garden of Verses. Make a five-senses chart and gather a rainstick or bell. Write 8 sentence pairs to combine and a list of -ed/-ing words (jumped, played, wanted, running, hopping, sitting).

**Watch for:**
- Joining sentences with 'and' but no comma, or using 'and' for everything. Show that 'but' shows a difference and 'so' shows a result.
- Thinking -ed always sounds like /ed/. Sort jumped (/t/), played (/d/), and wanted (/ed/).

### Day 1 (Monday): Five senses poem
*Student:* Go outside and list what you see, hear, smell, feel, and taste; turn the list into a 5-line senses poem. Read Stevenson's 'Rain' and 'Windy Nights' for models.

*How to teach:* Go outside and list what you see, hear, smell, feel, and taste. Turn the list into a 5-line senses poem. Read 'Rain' and 'Windy Nights' as models.

- **Q:** In Stevenson's 'Rain,' where does the rain fall?
  **A:** All around — on field and tree, on the umbrellas here, and on the ships at sea.
- **Q:** Write a 'hear' line for a senses poem.
  **A:** Example: I hear the birds chirping in the oak tree.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compound sentences
*Student:* Grammar: join two short sentences with and, but, or, so and a comma ('The wind blew, and the leaves fell.'); combine 8 pairs. Phonics: doubling before -ing (run/running).

*How to teach:* Teach joining two sentences with a comma and and, but, or, so. Combine 8 pairs. Practice doubling before -ing (run → running).

- **Q:** Combine: 'I was hungry. I ate an apple.'
  **A:** I was hungry, so I ate an apple.
- **Q:** Add -ing to 'hop.'
  **A:** hopping (double the p).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Endings -ed
*Student:* Phonics: read -ed words with three sounds (jumped, played, wanted) and sort them. Use 3 in compound sentences.

*How to teach:* Read -ed words and sort by sound: /t/ jumped, /d/ played, /ed/ wanted. Use 3 in compound sentences.

- **Q:** What sound does -ed make in 'wanted'?
  **A:** /ed/ (an extra syllable).
- **Q:** Sort: jumped, played.
  **A:** jumped — /t/; played — /d/.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word choice
*Student:* Revise yesterday's senses poem by swapping 3 plain words for stronger words (said → whispered, big → enormous). Read it aloud to compare versions.

*How to teach:* Revise the senses poem by swapping 3 plain words for stronger words (said → whispered, big → enormous). Read both versions aloud.

- **Q:** Give a stronger word for 'big.'
  **A:** enormous, huge, or gigantic.
- **Q:** Give a stronger word for 'went.'
  **A:** Examples: raced, wandered, tiptoed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 19 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 12 -ed/-ing words, a compound-sentence quiz, and an oral reading of the revised poem.

*How to teach:* Give the -ed/-ing spelling check and compound-sentence quiz. The child reads the revised poem aloud.

- **Q:** Combine: 'I like dogs. My sister likes cats.'
  **A:** I like dogs, but my sister likes cats.
- **Q:** Spell 'running' and 'played.'
  **A:** r-u-n-n-i-n-g; p-l-a-y-e-d.

**Assessment:** Compound-sentence quiz, spelling check, revised senses poem.

**Answer key:** Compound-sentence quiz (suggested): 1) The wind blew. The leaves fell. → The wind blew, and the leaves fell. 2) I wanted to play. It was raining. → I wanted to play, but it was raining. 3) We can read. We can draw. → We can read, or we can draw. 4) I was tired. I went to bed. → I was tired, so I went to bed. Spelling (suggested 12): jumped, played, wanted, looked, rained, landed, running, hopping, sitting, swimming, getting, stopping. Revised poem: 3 plain words replaced with stronger words.

## Math 2: Adding within 1,000

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks (at least 9 flats, or paper flats) and a place-value mat. Write 10 quiz problems for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to add the traded ten or hundred. Write a small 1 above the next column, or use partial sums so nothing is lost.
- Misaligning digits (adding 247 + 36 as if 36 were 360). Line up ones under ones in a place-value chart.

### Day 1 (Monday): Add hundreds, tens, ones
*Student:* Add 324 + 153 with blocks and quick pictures by place; solve 8 problems without regrouping.

*How to teach:* Build 324 and 153; combine hundreds (4), tens (7), ones (7): 477. The child draws quick pictures for 8 problems without regrouping.

- **Q:** 324 + 153 = ?
  **A:** 477.
- **Q:** 333 + 444 = ?
  **A:** 777.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compose a ten
*Student:* Add 247 + 136, trading 10 ones for a ten; record with quick pictures and partial sums.

*How to teach:* Build 247 + 136: ones 7 + 6 = 13, so trade 10 ones for a ten. Tens 4 + 3 + 1 = 8, hundreds 3: 383. Record with partial sums too: 300 + 70 + 13 = 383.

- **Q:** 247 + 136 = ?
  **A:** 383.
- **Q:** 418 + 275 = ?
  **A:** 693.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compose a hundred
*Student:* Add 365 + 182, trading 10 tens for a hundred; explain the trade in words.

*How to teach:* Build 365 + 182: tens 6 + 8 = 14 tens, so trade 10 tens for 1 hundred. Hundreds 3 + 1 + 1 = 5, tens 4, ones 7: 547. The child explains the trade aloud.

- **Q:** 365 + 182 = ? What did you trade?
  **A:** 547 — 14 tens became 1 hundred and 4 tens.
- **Q:** 640 + 190 = ?
  **A:** 830.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partial sums
*Student:* Use the partial-sums method (300 + 100, 60 + 80, 5 + 2) for 8 problems and match each to block drawings.

*How to teach:* Show partial sums: 365 + 182 → 300 + 100 = 400, 60 + 80 = 140, 5 + 2 = 7; 400 + 140 + 7 = 547. Match each to a block drawing for 8 problems.

- **Q:** Use partial sums for 156 + 268.
  **A:** 100 + 200 = 300, 50 + 60 = 110, 6 + 8 = 14; 300 + 110 + 14 = 424.
- **Q:** Use partial sums for 509 + 237.
  **A:** 700 + 30 + 16 = 746.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 10 three-digit addition problems and explain one regrouping step.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-problem quiz and ask the child to explain one regrouping step in words.

- **Q:** 295 + 105 = ?
  **A:** 400.
- **Q:** 482 + 319 = ?
  **A:** 801.

**Assessment:** Addition within 1,000 quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested 10): 1) 324 + 153 = 477 2) 247 + 136 = 383 3) 365 + 182 = 547 4) 418 + 275 = 693 5) 156 + 268 = 424 6) 509 + 237 = 746 7) 640 + 190 = 830 8) 333 + 444 = 777 9) 295 + 105 = 400 10) 482 + 319 = 801. Explanation: proficient names the trade (e.g., '6 tens + 8 tens = 14 tens, so I traded 10 tens for 1 hundred'). 9/10 = proficient.

## Science 2: Build a pollinator model

**Prepare:** Gather pipe cleaners, cotton balls, tape, craft sticks, powdered drink mix in two colors, and paper flowers with paper cups glued in the center. Prepare an 8-item unit quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Only testing pick-up, not drop-off. A good pollinator tool must also leave powder on the next flower.
- Thinking cotton always works best. Compare data from two designs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Design a 'hand pollinator' tool that moves colored powder from flower to flower; sketch.

*How to teach:* Design a hand pollinator tool that moves powder from flower to flower; sketch.

- **Q:** What must the tool do?
  **A:** Pick up powder and drop it on another flower.
- **Q:** Which material could act like bee hair?
  **A:** Cotton or pipe cleaner fuzz.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the hand pollinator from pipe cleaners, cotton balls, and craft sticks, shaping the tip to reach into a paper flower.

*How to teach:* Build the hand pollinator, shaping the tip to reach into a paper flower.

- **Q:** Why shape the tip?
  **A:** So it reaches the pollen in the flower's center.
- **Q:** What holds the parts together?
  **A:** Tape.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Test how well it picks up and drops powder on paper flowers; record results.

*How to teach:* Test how well it picks up and drops powder; record results.

- **Q:** How do you know pollen moved?
  **A:** Colored powder from flower 1 appears on flower 2.
- **Q:** Why use two colors?
  **A:** To see which flower the powder came from.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare two designs and decide which works better and why.

*How to teach:* Compare two designs and decide which works better.

- **Q:** Which worked better and why?
  **A:** Answer from data, e.g., fuzzy pipe cleaner moved more powder.
- **Q:** What would you improve?
  **A:** Example: add more cotton.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Present the model and take a quiz on seed dispersal and pollination.

*How to teach:* Present the model and give the unit quiz.

- **Q:** Name two ways seeds travel.
  **A:** Wind and animals (also water).
- **Q:** What is pollination?
  **A:** Moving pollen from one flower to another so plants can make seeds.

**Assessment:** Pollinator model presentation and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Presentation: tool design, test results (powder moved from flower 1 to flower 2), and why. Unit quiz (suggested 8): 1) Dandelion seeds travel by wind 2) Burrs travel on animal fur 3) Birds spread seeds by eating fruit 4) Coconut travels by water 5) Pollen moves from flower to flower 6) Bees, butterflies, hummingbirds, bats are pollinators 7) Plants need pollination to make seeds 8) Pollinators get food (nectar) from flowers.

## People Who Make a Difference: Producers and consumers

**Prepare:** 12 picture cards (bread, shoes, apple, toy, book, bike; haircut, doctor visit, teaching, bus ride, mail delivery, car repair); play money; web paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a person is only a producer or only a consumer: everyone is a consumer, and workers are also producers.
- Thinking services aren't 'real' because you can't hold them: services are work people do for others.

### Day 1 (Monday): Goods and services
*Student:* Sort 12 picture cards into goods (bread, shoes) and services (haircut, doctor visit).

*How to teach:* Sort 12 cards into goods and services.

- **Q:** What are goods?
  **A:** Things you can buy and hold.
- **Q:** What are services?
  **A:** Work people do for others.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Producers
*Student:* Interview a family member about their job: do they produce goods or provide services?

*How to teach:* Interview a family member: goods or services at work?

- **Q:** Does a teacher provide goods or a service?
  **A:** A service.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Consumers
*Student:* List things your family bought this week and identify each as a good or service.

*How to teach:* List family purchases this week; label goods or services.

- **Q:** What is a consumer?
  **A:** A person who buys or uses goods and services.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interdependence
*Student:* Draw a web showing how a baker depends on farmers, customers, and truck drivers.

*How to teach:* Draw a web of how a baker depends on others.

- **Q:** Who does the baker depend on for flour?
  **A:** Farmers who grow wheat and mills that grind it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences about how buyers and sellers need each other.

*How to teach:* Write 3 sentences about how buyers and sellers need each other.

- **Q:** What is a producer?
  **A:** A person who makes goods or provides services.

**Assessment:** Goods/services sort and interdependence web.

**Answer key:** Sort: goods - bread, shoes, apple, toy, book, bike; services - haircut, doctor visit, teaching, bus ride, mail delivery, car repair. Interdependence web: baker linked to farmer (wheat), miller (flour), truck driver (delivery), customers (buy bread), store. Proficient = 12/12 + web with 3+ links.

## Art & Music 2: Repurposed sculpture: Louise Nevelson

**Prepare:** Image of a Louise Nevelson wall sculpture (e.g., 'Sky Cathedral'); clean recyclables, bottle caps, small boxes, tubes; a shallow box; strong glue; one color of paint (black, white or gold).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a sculpture must be carved: assemblages are built from found objects.
- Thinking one color is boring: one color makes you notice form, light and shadow.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look at Nevelson
*Student:* Look at a Louise Nevelson wall sculpture and describe how she turned scraps of wood into art painted all one color.

*How to teach:* Look at a Nevelson wall sculpture; describe how she used wood scraps painted one color.

- **Q:** What did Louise Nevelson build her sculptures from?
  **A:** Found wood scraps (furniture parts, crates) arranged in boxes.
- **Q:** What color did she often use?
  **A:** Black (also white and gold).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Brainstorm with family 5 ways to turn recyclables into a sculpture, sketching ideas.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm 5 ways to turn recyclables into sculpture; sketch.

- **Q:** What does 'repurpose' mean?
  **A:** Using something in a new way.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Glue boxes, tubes, and caps into a shallow box to create a relief sculpture.

*How to teach:* Glue boxes, tubes and caps into a shallow box.

- **Q:** What is a relief sculpture?
  **A:** A sculpture that sticks out from a flat background.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Paint
*Student:* Paint the whole sculpture one color (black, white, or gold) like Nevelson and notice how shadows show the forms.

*How to teach:* Paint the whole sculpture one color; notice shadows.

- **Q:** What does monochrome mean?
  **A:** One color.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Discuss with family how painting it one color changed the look and what objects you repurposed.

*How to teach:* Discuss how one color changed the look and what you repurposed.

- **Q:** How did one color change it?
  **A:** Objects blend together; you notice shapes and shadows.

**Assessment:** Repurposed relief sculpture and discussion.

**Answer key:** Relief sculpture: found objects glued into a shallow box, painted one color. Discussion: one color unifies the objects so you see forms and shadows instead of what the objects were; names items repurposed. Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Partner throw and catch

**Prepare:** Ball, cones, tape to mark 15 feet.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing before the partner is ready: check that 'target hands' are up.
- Standing still for side balls: shuffle sideways to get in front of it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Target hands
*Student:* Throw overhand to a partner's 'target hands'; step back one big step after 3 good catches.

*How to teach:* Throw to a partner's target hands; step back after 3 good catches.

- **Q:** What are target hands?
  **A:** Hands up and open showing where to throw.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Moving catch
*Student:* Shuffle sideways to catch a ball tossed to the side.

*How to teach:* Toss to the side; partner shuffles sideways to catch.

- **Q:** How do you get to a ball tossed to your side?
  **A:** Shuffle sideways to get in front of it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count-up challenge
*Student:* With a partner, count consecutive throw-catch pairs and try to beat your record.

*How to teach:* Count consecutive pairs; try to beat the record.

- **Q:** What is accuracy?
  **A:** Hitting where you aim.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Monkey in the middle
*Student:* Play a 3-person throw-and-catch game, practicing quick throws to an open partner.

*How to teach:* Play a 3-person monkey in the middle, throwing quickly to the open partner.

- **Q:** Where should you throw?
  **A:** To the partner who is open (not blocked).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete 10 throws and catches with a partner at 15 feet and set a record goal.

*How to teach:* 10 throws and catches at 15 feet; set a record goal.

- **Q:** What's your record and goal?
  **A:** Record numbers.

**Assessment:** Partner record and observation of throw/catch cues.

**Answer key:** Partner record: consecutive throw-catch pairs at 15 feet (goal 10). Observation: thrower uses side, T, step opposite, follow through; catcher shows target hands, thumbs/pinkies, gives. Proficient = 10 completed + cues used.

## Health 2: Bones and muscles

**Prepare:** Paper skeleton template, brads, scissors; optional cleaned chicken bone.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bones are dead and dry: living bones grow and repair.
- Thinking muscles push: muscles only pull; pairs work together (biceps bends, triceps straightens).

### Day 1 (Monday): Skeleton
*Student:* Learn major bones (skull, ribs, spine) and their jobs; feel them on your body.

*How to teach:* Teach skull, ribs, spine and their jobs; feel them.

- **Q:** What does the skull protect?
  **A:** The brain.
- **Q:** What do ribs protect?
  **A:** The heart and lungs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paper skeleton
*Student:* Build a paper skeleton with brad joints and label 6 bones.

*How to teach:* Build a paper skeleton with brad joints; label 6 bones.

- **Q:** What is a joint?
  **A:** A place where two bones meet so the body can bend.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Muscles pull
*Student:* Feel the biceps and triceps when bending and straightening your arm.

*How to teach:* Feel the biceps when bending the arm and triceps when straightening.

- **Q:** Which muscle bends your elbow?
  **A:** Biceps.
- **Q:** Which straightens it?
  **A:** Triceps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Strong bones
*Student:* Learn how calcium and exercise build strong bones.

*How to teach:* Teach that calcium (milk, yogurt, cheese, leafy greens) and exercise build strong bones.

- **Q:** Name a food with calcium.
  **A:** Milk, yogurt, cheese, broccoli, kale.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label bones and explain how muscles and bones work together.

*How to teach:* Label bones; explain how muscles and bones work together.

- **Q:** How do muscles move bones?
  **A:** They pull on bones at joints.

**Assessment:** Labeled skeleton.

**Answer key:** Labeled skeleton (6 bones, e.g.): skull - protects the brain; ribs - protect heart and lungs; spine/backbone - holds you up and protects the spinal cord; pelvis/hip; femur (thigh bone, longest bone); humerus (upper arm). Explanation: muscles attach to bones and pull them to move at joints. Proficient = 6 correct labels + explanation.
