# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 36

## ELA 2: Year-end showcase and assessment

**Prepare:** Choose an on-level end-of-year fluency passage with 4–5 comprehension questions. Prepare the phonics/spelling inventory (vowel teams, r-controlled, prefixes/suffixes, two-syllable words, irregular words). Gather the year's writing portfolio and a summer reading list template.

**Watch for:**
- Picking portfolio pieces by neatness only. Choose pieces that show growth and different kinds of writing.
- Setting a vague summer goal ('read a lot'). Make it measurable: '10 books' or '20 minutes a day.'

### Day 1 (Monday): End-of-year reading check
*Student:* Read an on-level passage for a fluency check and answer comprehension questions; compare with the fall and mid-year scores.

*How to teach:* The child reads an on-level passage for one minute and answers comprehension questions. Compare with fall and mid-year scores.

- **Q:** How do you find WCPM?
  **A:** Count words read in one minute and subtract errors.
- **Q:** Why compare to fall?
  **A:** To see how much reading has grown.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Phonics inventory
*Student:* Take the end-of-year phonics and spelling inventory (vowel teams, r-controlled, prefixes/suffixes, two-syllable words) and the reading of irregular words.

*How to teach:* Give the phonics and spelling inventory and the irregular-word reading check.

- **Q:** Spell 'unhappy.'
  **A:** u-n-h-a-p-p-y.
- **Q:** Spell 'night.'
  **A:** n-i-g-h-t.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Portfolio selection
*Student:* Choose 5 best pieces (narrative, fable, report, opinion, poem) and write a one-sentence reflection about each.

*How to teach:* Choose 5 best pieces (narrative, fable, report, opinion, poem) and write a one-sentence reflection for each.

- **Q:** Write a reflection sentence for a fable.
  **A:** Example: My fable shows I learned to end a story with a clear lesson.
- **Q:** Why include different kinds of writing?
  **A:** To show all the kinds of writing you learned.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Showcase
*Student:* Present the portfolio to family, reading one favorite piece aloud and explaining one way you grew as a writer.

*How to teach:* Present the portfolio to family, reading one favorite piece aloud and explaining one way you grew as a writer.

- **Q:** Give an example of growth.
  **A:** Example: I now use temporal words and details in my stories.
- **Q:** What makes a good presentation?
  **A:** Clear voice, eye contact, and answering questions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Summer reading plan
*Student:* Make a summer reading list of 10 books (e.g., more Frog and Toad, Mercer Mayer, Magic Tree House) and set a reading goal. Celebrate the year.

*How to teach:* Make a summer reading list of 10 books and set a reading goal. Celebrate the year.

- **Q:** Name a series to add to the list.
  **A:** Examples: Frog and Toad, Henry and Mudge, Magic Tree House.
- **Q:** Write a measurable reading goal.
  **A:** Example: I will read 20 minutes every day this summer.

**Assessment:** End-of-year fluency and phonics inventory, portfolio presentation and reflections.

**Answer key:** Fluency: record WCPM and compare to fall and mid-year (end-of-grade-2 goal about 90–100 WCPM). Comprehension: 4/5 correct. Phonics inventory: record correct/incorrect for vowel teams (rain, play, tree, beach, boat, snow, night), r-controlled (car, corn, her, bird, turn), prefixes/suffixes (unhappy, reread, helpful, quickly), two-syllable words (paper, raincoat), and irregular words (said, friend, because, people). Portfolio: 5 pieces with one-sentence reflections; presentation explains one way the child grew.

## Math 2: Year-end assessment and math fair

**Prepare:** Print or write the year-end test (two parts), poster board, markers, glue, and photos or drawing paper.

**Watch for:**
- Getting stuck and leaving the rest blank. Remind the child to do what they know first, then return.
- Posters with pictures but no math. Each picture needs a label with numbers and an equation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Year-end test part 1
*Student:* Number and operations: place value, add/subtract within 100 and 1,000, and word problems.

*How to teach:* Give part 1 of the year-end test. Read instructions aloud but not answers.

- **Q:** 58 + 37 = ?
  **A:** 95.
- **Q:** 600 − 245 = ?
  **A:** 355.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Year-end test part 2
*Student:* Take part 2 of the year-end test: measure lengths, read a bar graph and line plot, count money, tell time, and identify shapes and equal shares.

*How to teach:* Give part 2: measurement, graphs, money, time, shapes, and equal shares.

- **Q:** 3 quarters + 2 dimes?
  **A:** 95¢.
- **Q:** The minute hand is on 7 and the hour hand is past 8. Time?
  **A:** 8:35.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Math fair project
*Student:* Plan a 'Math in My Home' poster showing arrays, measurements, money, and a graph from your home.

*How to teach:* Plan a 'Math in My Home' poster with an array (egg carton), a measurement (a table), money (a price), and a graph.

- **Q:** What array could you show from home?
  **A:** Example: an egg carton with 2 rows of 6 = 12.
- **Q:** Name one measurement you could add.
  **A:** Example: the kitchen table is 48 inches long.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build the poster
*Student:* Finish the poster with labeled photos or drawings and practice explaining each math idea aloud in 3 minutes.

*How to teach:* Finish the poster with labeled pictures and practice explaining each idea aloud in about 3 minutes.

- **Q:** Explain your array.
  **A:** Example: 'This muffin tin has 3 rows of 4, so 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 cups.'
- **Q:** Why label pictures with numbers?
  **A:** So people can see the math in each picture.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present & celebrate
*Student:* Present the math fair poster to family and review the fluency growth graph from the year.

*How to teach:* The child presents the poster to family; review the fluency growth graph from the year together.

- **Q:** How did your fluency scores change this year?
  **A:** Answer from the graph — scores should rise from the Week 1 baseline.
- **Q:** What math idea are you most proud of?
  **A:** Any specific answer with an example.

**Assessment:** Year-end test and math fair presentation.

**Answer key:** Year-end test part 1 (suggested): 1) 467 = 400 + 60 + 7 2) 528 < 582 3) 58 + 37 = 95 4) 90 − 46 = 44 5) 356 + 278 = 634 6) 600 − 245 = 355 7) 45 stickers, gave away 18, got 20 more → 47. Part 2: 8) measure lines (accept ±1 unit) 9) answer graph questions from the given bar graph and line plot 10) 3 quarters + 2 dimes = 95¢ 11) clock reading 8:35 12) 5 sides → pentagon 13) shade one fourth. Math fair rubric: 4 = arrays, a measurement, money, and a graph each shown with correct numbers and clearly explained in about 3 minutes; 3 = all shown, minor errors; 2 = 2–3 ideas; 1 = 1 idea.

## Science 2: Science fair and year review

**Prepare:** Gather bridge data, the science notebook, a trifold or poster board, markers, and materials to demonstrate one investigation live. Prepare a year-end quiz (10 items).

**Watch for:**
- Displays with pictures but no data. Each investigation needs question, test, data, and conclusion.
- Mixing up reversible and irreversible on review. Ask 'Can it go back?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare bridges
*Student:* Make a T-chart comparing the first and improved bridges' strengths and weaknesses and explain which shape worked best.

*How to teach:* Make a T-chart comparing the first and improved bridges and explain which shape worked best.

- **Q:** Which shape held more pennies?
  **A:** Answer from data (usually folded/accordion).
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** It was stiffer and spread the weight.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choose investigations
*Student:* Review the science notebook and choose 3 favorite investigations (e.g., plant needs, ice keeper, erosion design) to display.

*How to teach:* Review the notebook and choose 3 favorite investigations to display.

- **Q:** Why choose investigations with good data?
  **A:** Data shows what you found out.
- **Q:** What was the question in the plant investigation?
  **A:** Do plants need light and water?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build the display
*Student:* Make a science fair board with the question, test, data, and conclusion for each chosen investigation.

*How to teach:* Make a science fair board with question, test, data, and conclusion for each.

- **Q:** What goes in the conclusion?
  **A:** What you found, with evidence.
- **Q:** Why include a graph?
  **A:** It shows data clearly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the science fair board to family or friends, demonstrating one investigation live.

*How to teach:* Present the board and demonstrate one investigation live.

- **Q:** What makes a strong presentation?
  **A:** Clear voice, showing data, explaining the conclusion.
- **Q:** What will you demonstrate?
  **A:** Example: the absorbency test.

### Day 5 (Friday): Year-end check
*Student:* Take a year-end review quiz covering materials, heating and cooling, plant needs, pollination, habitats, and Earth changes.

*How to teach:* Give the year-end review quiz.

- **Q:** Is melting chocolate reversible?
  **A:** Yes.
- **Q:** What do roots do to slow erosion?
  **A:** Hold soil in place.

**Assessment:** Science fair presentation and year-end quiz.

**Answer key:** Science fair rubric (3 = proficient): 3 investigations each with question, test, data, conclusion; one live demo explained clearly. Year-end quiz (suggested 10): 1) Best raincoat material — plastic (waterproof) 2) Melting ice — reversible 3) Cooking an egg — irreversible 4) Plants need light and water 5) Wind carries dandelion seeds 6) Bees move pollen 7) Desert animals survive with little water (e.g., tortoise in burrow) 8) Earthquake — fast change 9) Grand Canyon — slow change 10) Plant roots slow erosion.

## People Who Make a Difference: Celebrating our stories: year review

**Prepare:** Family history book, blank North America map and the fall map for comparison, hero timeline, vocabulary cards, year-end quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up producer and consumer or law and rule: review with examples.
- Forgetting the Great Lakes: use HOMES.

### Day 1 (Monday): Family history review
*Student:* Reread the family history book and add one new page about this year's events.

*How to teach:* Reread the family book; add a page about this year.

- **Q:** What new event did you add?
  **A:** Event from this year.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map from memory
*Student:* Label a blank map of North America from memory (countries, oceans, Great Lakes, rivers, mountains) and compare with the fall map.

*How to teach:* Label a blank map from memory; compare with the fall map.

- **Q:** What does HOMES help you remember?
  **A:** The Great Lakes: Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, Superior.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Government and economics
*Student:* Play a review game matching vocabulary (law, judge, producer, consumer, trade, treaty) to meanings.

*How to teach:* Play a vocabulary matching game.

- **Q:** What does a judge do?
  **A:** Decides whether a law was broken in court.
- **Q:** What is a treaty?
  **A:** An agreement between nations.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heroes reflection
*Student:* Write which hero inspired you most and how you could make a difference.

*How to teach:* Write which hero inspired you and how you could make a difference.

- **Q:** Which hero inspired you and why?
  **A:** Names a hero with an accurate reason.

### Day 5 (Friday): Year-end check
*Student:* Take a year-end review quiz and share your favorite project from the year.

*How to teach:* Give the year-end quiz; share your favorite project.

- **Q:** Is a haircut a good or a service?
  **A:** A service.

**Assessment:** Map from memory and year-end quiz.

**Answer key:** Map from memory: Canada, U.S., Mexico; Pacific, Atlantic, Arctic; Great Lakes (HOMES); Mississippi and another river; Rocky and Appalachian Mountains. Year-end quiz (sample): primary vs. secondary source; a timeline order; map elements; rule vs. law; who makes/carries out/judges laws; import vs. export; treaty; goods vs. services; scarcity/trade-off; one hero and their contribution. Proficient = 80%.

## Art & Music 2: Art and music showcase

**Prepare:** Home gallery set up, instruments, programs, thank-you note paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking growth means everything is perfect: growth is improvement over time.
- Forgetting to thank guests: thank-you notes show appreciation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Set up
*Student:* Hang the exhibit and set up the performance space.

*How to teach:* Hang the exhibit; set up performance space.

- **Q:** What is an exhibit?
  **A:** A public display of artwork.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Showcase
*Student:* Give a gallery tour and perform the 3 music pieces for guests.

*How to teach:* Give a gallery tour and perform 3 pieces.

- **Q:** What will you tell guests about your favorite piece?
  **A:** Title, materials, idea.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Audience
*Student:* Practice good audience etiquette by watching a family member's performance or talent.

*How to teach:* Watch a family member's performance with good etiquette.

- **Q:** How do you show respect as an audience?
  **A:** Sit quietly, watch, clap at the end.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflect
*Student:* Compare a fall artwork with a spring artwork and describe your growth using art words.

*How to teach:* Compare fall and spring artworks using art words.

- **Q:** How did your art grow?
  **A:** Uses art words: more detail, better color mixing, texture.

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebrate
*Student:* Write a thank-you note to guests and choose one art and one music goal for summer.

*How to teach:* Write a thank-you note; choose summer goals.

- **Q:** What is one art goal and one music goal?
  **A:** Specific goals.

**Assessment:** Showcase performance, gallery tour, and written reflection.

**Answer key:** Showcase: gives a gallery tour using art words and performs 3 pieces. Written reflection compares a fall and spring artwork using art words (e.g., line, color, texture, detail) and states one art and one music summer goal. Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Family field day

**Prepare:** Cones, pillowcases, beanbags, water balloons, ribbon/paper for awards, water and sunscreen.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking field day is only about winning: effort, teamwork, sportsmanship.
- Skipping water and sunscreen: plan breaks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan stations
*Student:* Plan 6 field-day stations (sack race, beanbag toss, relay, long jump, three-legged race, water balloon toss).

*How to teach:* Plan 6 stations and their rules.

- **Q:** How do you do a three-legged race?
  **A:** Partners tie their inside legs together and walk/run in step.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Make awards
*Student:* Make ribbons for effort, teamwork and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Make ribbons for effort, teamwork and sportsmanship.

- **Q:** Who earns a sportsmanship award?
  **A:** Someone who plays fair and is kind win or lose.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Field day
*Student:* Lead family or friends through all stations, with water breaks and sunscreen.

*How to teach:* Lead family through stations with water breaks and sunscreen.

- **Q:** How do you stay safe in the heat?
  **A:** Drink water, rest in shade, wear sunscreen and a hat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflect
*Student:* Write or draw your favorite part of PE this year and how you got stronger.

*How to teach:* Write or draw your favorite part and how you got stronger.

- **Q:** How did you get stronger this year?
  **A:** Uses fitness log evidence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present your PE portfolio (fitness log, graphs, reflections) to family.

*How to teach:* Present your PE portfolio to family.

- **Q:** What does your graph show?
  **A:** Explains fall vs. spring scores.

**Assessment:** Field-day leadership and PE portfolio.

**Answer key:** Field-day leadership: explains each of 6 stations, keeps safety (water, sunscreen), encourages others. PE portfolio: fitness log, graphs, reflection. Proficient = all.

## Health 2: My healthy year

**Prepare:** Health portfolio (trackers, logs, posters), paper for a summer plan.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking healthy habits stop in summer: keep them going.
- Setting too many goals: choose a few specific ones.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look back
*Student:* Review your health portfolio from the year.

*How to teach:* Review the health portfolio.

- **Q:** Which project are you proudest of?
  **A:** Child explains.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Goal review
*Student:* Check progress on hygiene and activity goals.

*How to teach:* Check hygiene and activity goals.

- **Q:** Did you meet your goals?
  **A:** Uses trackers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Summer plan
*Student:* Write a summer health plan with food, activity, sleep and safety goals.

*How to teach:* Write a summer plan with food, activity, sleep and safety goals.

- **Q:** Give a summer safety goal.
  **A:** Wear sunscreen and a helmet; swim with an adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Health fair
*Student:* Hold a family health fair showcasing projects.

*How to teach:* Hold a family health fair.

- **Q:** What will you teach at your table?
  **A:** Names a topic.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Year-end oral review of key health ideas.

*How to teach:* Year-end oral review.

- **Q:** How many minutes of activity do kids need daily?
  **A:** 60.
- **Q:** Name the 5 food groups.
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy.
- **Q:** What do you do in an earthquake?
  **A:** Drop, cover, hold on.

**Assessment:** Portfolio and summer plan.

**Answer key:** Summer plan has specific goals for food (e.g., eat a fruit or vegetable at every meal), activity (60 minutes daily), sleep (9-12 hours, set bedtime), and safety (sunscreen, helmet, swim with an adult). Oral review covers feelings/calming, hygiene, nutrition, body systems and safety. Proficient = 4 specific goals + accurate review.
