# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 28

## ELA 2: Writing and presenting a biography report

**Prepare:** Gather poster board, markers, the child's notes, and simple dress-up items for a 'living museum.' Prepare a new short biography passage with questions for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Speeches read too fast or too quietly. Practice with a timer and 'speak to the back of the room.'
- Timelines out of order. Number events first, then copy them onto the poster.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft paragraphs 2–3
*Student:* Write the 'big work' and 'why we remember them' paragraphs with facts from notes and a concluding sentence.

*How to teach:* Write the 'big work' and 'why we remember them' paragraphs with facts and a concluding sentence.

- **Q:** Write a concluding sentence about Cesar Chavez.
  **A:** Example: Cesar Chavez is remembered for helping farmworkers get fair treatment.
- **Q:** What does the 'why we remember them' paragraph tell?
  **A:** How the person changed things and why they still matter.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise for order and clarity; edit with checklist for capitals, apostrophes, and spelling. Make a final copy.

*How to teach:* Revise for order and clarity; edit for capitals, apostrophes, and spelling. Make a final copy.

- **Q:** Fix: 'sally ride flew in space in 1983'
  **A:** Sally Ride flew in space in 1983.
- **Q:** Why check the order of sentences?
  **A:** So the life story makes sense from beginning to end.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Visual display
*Student:* Make a poster with a portrait, a 5-event timeline, and a quote or fact box to go with the report.

*How to teach:* Make a poster with a portrait, a 5-event timeline, and a quote or fact box.

- **Q:** How should timeline events be arranged?
  **A:** In order from earliest to latest.
- **Q:** What goes in a fact box?
  **A:** An interesting fact or a quote by the person.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Living museum
*Student:* Rehearse a 1-minute 'living museum' speech in first person as your research person ('I am Sally Ride…'), speaking audibly in complete sentences.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a 1-minute first-person speech ('I am Sally Ride…'), speaking audibly in complete sentences.

- **Q:** Start a living museum speech as Abraham Lincoln.
  **A:** Example: I am Abraham Lincoln. I was the 16th President of the United States.
- **Q:** Why speak in first person?
  **A:** Because you are pretending to be the person.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Present the living museum to family. Unit test: read a new short biography, sequence events, and identify the main topic and author's purpose.

*How to teach:* Present the living museum to family. Give the unit test on a new biography.

- **Q:** What is the author's purpose of a biography?
  **A:** To inform readers about a real person's life.
- **Q:** What is the main topic of a biography?
  **A:** The person whose life it tells.

**Assessment:** Biography report and poster (research rubric), presentation, unit test.

**Answer key:** Research rubric (3 = proficient): 3 paragraphs (early life, big work, why we remember them) with accurate facts in own words; concluding sentence; poster with portrait, 5-event timeline in order, and a quote/fact box; speech in first person, audible, complete sentences. Unit test on the new biography: events sequenced correctly; main topic named; author's purpose = to inform about a real person's life.

## Math 2: Odd and even numbers

**Prepare:** Counters (20), a basket of socks or shoes, hundreds chart, crayons, linking cubes.

**Watch for:**
- Deciding odd/even from the tens digit (thinking 32 is odd). Only the ones digit decides.
- Thinking 0 is neither odd nor even. 0 has no leftover, so it is even.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pairing
*Student:* Pair up counters or socks for numbers 1–20; if every item has a partner, it's even. Record results.

*How to teach:* Pair up counters or socks for numbers 1–20. If every item has a partner, the number is even; one left over means odd. Record in a two-column chart.

- **Q:** Is 9 odd or even? How do you know?
  **A:** Odd — 4 pairs and 1 left over.
- **Q:** Is 12 odd or even?
  **A:** Even — 6 pairs, none left over.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Equal addends
*Student:* Write each even number to 20 as a doubles fact (12 = 6 + 6) and build each with cubes.

*How to teach:* Write each even number to 20 as a double (12 = 6 + 6) and build it with two equal cube towers.

- **Q:** Write 14 as two equal addends.
  **A:** 7 + 7.
- **Q:** Write 18 as two equal addends.
  **A:** 9 + 9.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Patterns
*Student:* Color evens on a hundreds chart and describe the ones-digit pattern; predict whether 47 and 86 are odd or even.

*How to teach:* Color the even numbers on a hundreds chart and look at the ones digits (0, 2, 4, 6, 8). Predict 47 and 86.

- **Q:** Is 47 odd or even?
  **A:** Odd — it ends in 7.
- **Q:** Is 86 odd or even?
  **A:** Even — it ends in 6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Odd + even
*Student:* Investigate even + even, odd + odd, and even + odd with 10 examples each; write rules.

*How to teach:* Test even + even, odd + odd, and even + odd with 10 examples each. The child writes the rules.

- **Q:** What is odd + odd?
  **A:** Even (e.g., 3 + 5 = 8).
- **Q:** What is even + odd?
  **A:** Odd (e.g., 4 + 3 = 7). Even + even is even.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: classify 15 numbers or groups as odd/even and write 5 even numbers as equal addends.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz: classify 15 numbers or groups and write 5 even numbers as equal addends.

- **Q:** Is 0 odd or even?
  **A:** Even.
- **Q:** Write 20 as equal addends.
  **A:** 10 + 10.

**Assessment:** Odd/even quiz.

**Answer key:** Odd/even quiz (suggested 15): 7 odd, 12 even, 15 odd, 20 even, 9 odd, 14 even, 47 odd, 86 even, 33 odd, 18 even, 101 odd, 56 even, a group of 11 counters odd, a group of 16 counters even, 0 even. Equal addends: 8 = 4 + 4; 10 = 5 + 5; 14 = 7 + 7; 18 = 9 + 9; 20 = 10 + 10. Proficient: 18/20.

## Science 2: How wind and water change land

**Prepare:** Gather sand and soil, a baking pan or tray, a watering can, drinking straws, newspaper, a ruler, and safety glasses for the wind test. Find photos of California cliff erosion (e.g., Pacifica).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking erosion only removes land. Eroded material is deposited somewhere else.
- Blowing toward someone's face during the wind test. Wear glasses and blow away from people.

### Day 1 (Monday): Water erosion
*Student:* Build a sand hill in a tray, sprinkle it with a watering can for 1 minute, and draw where sand was removed and where it was deposited.

*How to teach:* Build a sand hill, sprinkle it with water for 1 minute, and draw where sand was removed and deposited.

- **Q:** Where did the sand go?
  **A:** Down the hill to the bottom.
- **Q:** What is deposition?
  **A:** When moved sand or soil is dropped in a new place.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wind erosion
*Student:* Make a flat sand pile on newspaper, blow through a straw from 10 cm away for 30 seconds, and measure how far sand traveled.

*How to teach:* Blow through a straw at a sand pile from 10 cm for 30 seconds and measure how far sand traveled.

- **Q:** How far did the sand travel?
  **A:** Answer from measurement.
- **Q:** How is this like real wind?
  **A:** Wind picks up and moves sand and soil, especially in dry places.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Erosion walk
*Student:* Walk the neighborhood or a park to photograph or sketch erosion (gullies, exposed tree roots, washed-out paths) and note whether water or wind caused it.

*How to teach:* Walk the neighborhood or a park and sketch or photograph erosion; note water or wind.

- **Q:** What does exposed tree roots show?
  **A:** Soil was washed or blown away.
- **Q:** What makes a gully?
  **A:** Running water carrying soil away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coastal erosion
*Student:* Read about California cliff erosion (e.g., Pacifica) and look at photos of homes near eroding cliffs; discuss why it is a problem for people.

*How to teach:* Read about California cliff erosion and look at photos; discuss why it's a problem.

- **Q:** What erodes coastal cliffs?
  **A:** Ocean waves (and rain).
- **Q:** Why is it a problem for people?
  **A:** Homes and roads near the edge can fall as cliffs wear away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Draw before-and-after pictures of the water and wind models and label erosion and deposit areas.

*How to teach:* Draw before-and-after pictures of the models and label erosion and deposit areas.

- **Q:** Label the top of the hill after watering.
  **A:** Erosion — sand removed.
- **Q:** Label the bottom of the hill.
  **A:** Deposit — sand dropped.

**Assessment:** Before/after erosion drawings with labels.

**Answer key:** Before/after drawings (3 = proficient): water model shows sand removed from the top of the hill and deposited at the bottom; wind model shows sand moved away from the pile and deposited farther along the paper (distance measured in cm); erosion and deposit areas labeled.

## People Who Make a Difference: Science heroes: Louis Pasteur and Marie Curie

**Prepare:** Milk or juice carton labeled 'pasteurized'; biographies of Louis Pasteur and Marie Curie; photo of Curie in her lab; 2 bread slices and a zip bag (keep the moldy slice sealed and don't sniff it; discard after).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pasteurization adds chemicals: it heats milk or juice to kill harmful germs.
- Thinking women couldn't be scientists then: they faced unfair barriers, and Marie Curie succeeded anyway.

### Day 1 (Monday): Louis Pasteur
*Student:* Read a short biography of Louis Pasteur and find the word 'pasteurized' on a milk or juice carton; explain how heating kills germs.

*How to teach:* Read about Pasteur; find 'pasteurized' on a carton; explain heating kills germs.

- **Q:** What does pasteurized mean?
  **A:** Heated to kill harmful germs.
- **Q:** What country was Pasteur from?
  **A:** France.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Germs experiment
*Student:* With an adult, leave a slice of bread out and keep another sealed; observe mold over the week and connect to Pasteur's discoveries about germs.

*How to teach:* Leave one bread slice out and seal another; observe mold.

- **Q:** What is mold?
  **A:** A fungus that grows on food, especially when it's left out.
- **Q:** How does this connect to Pasteur?
  **A:** He showed tiny living things (germs) make food spoil.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marie Curie
*Student:* Read a biography of Marie Curie and learn how she became the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and the first person to win two.

*How to teach:* Read about Marie Curie's two Nobel Prizes.

- **Q:** What was special about Marie Curie's Nobel Prizes?
  **A:** She was the first woman to win one and the first person to win two (physics 1903, chemistry 1911).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perseverance
*Student:* Look at a photo of Marie Curie in her laboratory and discuss how she kept working when people said women could not be scientists.

*How to teach:* Look at Curie's lab photo; discuss perseverance.

- **Q:** What is perseverance?
  **A:** Keeping on even when things are hard.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 2 sentences each on how Pasteur and Curie made a difference in people's lives today.

*How to teach:* Write how Pasteur and Curie made a difference.

- **Q:** How does Pasteur help you today?
  **A:** Pasteurized milk is safe to drink; vaccines prevent diseases.

**Assessment:** Written comparison of two scientists' contributions.

**Answer key:** Written comparison (2 sentences each): Pasteur - showed that germs cause food to spoil and people to get sick; invented pasteurization so milk and juice are safe today, and made vaccines (like the rabies vaccine). Curie - discovered radium and polonium and studied radioactivity; her work helped lead to X-rays and cancer treatments; first woman to win a Nobel Prize and first person to win two. Proficient = accurate contribution for each with a today connection.

## Art & Music 2: Murals and public art in communities

**Prepare:** Photos of Chicano Park murals (San Diego) and the Great Wall of Los Angeles; butcher paper; tempera paints.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking murals are graffiti: murals are planned public artworks, usually with permission and community input.
- Thinking public art is only decoration: it tells community stories and history.

### Day 1 (Monday): California murals
*Student:* Look at photos of Chicano Park murals and the Great Wall of Los Angeles and discuss what stories they tell and who they are for.

*How to teach:* Look at Chicano Park murals and the Great Wall of Los Angeles; discuss stories and audience.

- **Q:** What does Chicano Park show?
  **A:** Murals painted on freeway bridge pillars in San Diego celebrating Mexican American (Chicano) history and culture.
- **Q:** What is the Great Wall of Los Angeles?
  **A:** A very long mural (about half a mile) led by Judith Baca, painted with young people, showing California history.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Local public art
*Student:* Find a mural, statue, or sculpture in your community (in person or online), photograph or sketch it, and write who might enjoy it and why.

*How to teach:* Find local public art; sketch it; write who might enjoy it.

- **Q:** What is public art?
  **A:** Art in public places that everyone can see.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design
*Student:* Sketch a mural design that tells a story about your family or community, including people, places, and a pattern border.

*How to teach:* Sketch a mural design about family or community with a pattern border.

- **Q:** What story does your mural tell?
  **A:** Child explains.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Paint
*Student:* Transfer the design to butcher paper and paint it, using a color palette chosen for mood.

*How to teach:* Transfer to butcher paper; paint with a mood palette.

- **Q:** What is a muralist?
  **A:** An artist who paints murals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Present the mural to family and explain how public art can make a community stronger; name one cultural art form from this unit and its use.

*How to teach:* Present; explain how public art strengthens a community; name one cultural art form.

- **Q:** How does public art help a community?
  **A:** Shares stories and history, brings people together, makes places beautiful.

**Assessment:** Mural presentation with explanation of public art.

**Answer key:** Mural presentation: design tells a family/community story with people, places and pattern border, mood palette. Explanation: public art makes a community stronger by sharing its stories and history, bringing people together, and making places beautiful. Names one cultural art form (papel picado, gyotaku, kente, Pomo baskets) and its use. Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Jump rope skills

**Prepare:** Jump rope sized so handles reach armpits; long rope; jump-count log.

**Watch for:**
- Turning with the shoulders: turn with wrists near hips.
- Running into the long rope at the wrong time: enter right after the rope hits the ground going away.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rope sizing & turns
*Student:* Size the rope (handles to armpits), then practice turn-stop-jump over the rope.

*How to teach:* Size the rope; practice turn-stop-jump.

- **Q:** How do you size a rope?
  **A:** Stand on the middle; handles reach the armpits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Continuous forward
*Student:* Jump forward continuously, counting jumps in 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* Jump forward continuously; count in 30 seconds.

- **Q:** How many jumps in 30 seconds?
  **A:** Record count.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Backward jumps
*Student:* Practice turning the rope backward and jumping over it.

*How to teach:* Turn the rope backward and jump.

- **Q:** When jumping backward, where does the rope start?
  **A:** In front of you, swinging up over your head to the back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Long rope
*Student:* Jump a long rope turned by two family members; try running in.

*How to teach:* Jump a long rope; try running in.

- **Q:** When do you run in?
  **A:** Right after the rope hits the ground and moves away from you.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record most forward jumps in a row and try 5 backward jumps.

*How to teach:* Record best forward streak; try 5 backward.

- **Q:** What is rhythm in jump rope?
  **A:** Jumping in a steady pattern with the turns.

**Assessment:** Jump-count log.

**Answer key:** Jump-count log: best forward streak (goal 10+), jumps in 30 seconds, 5 backward jumps attempted. Proficient = 10+ forward and some backward success.

## Health 2: Safe and unsafe touch and strangers

**Prepare:** Scenario cards you write in advance. PARENTAL PREVIEW: read this week first. Use correct, calm language; reassure your child that they will never be in trouble for telling you about a touch, secret, or person that makes them uncomfortable.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only strangers can be unsafe: anyone who asks to break body-safety rules is not following rules; tell a trusted adult.
- Thinking you must keep every secret: secrets about touching or that make you feel unsafe must always be told.

### Day 1 (Monday): Safe vs unsafe
*Student:* Discuss safe touches (hugs from family) and unsafe ones, and that your body belongs to you.

*How to teach:* Discuss safe touches (hugs from family, high fives, doctor check with parent present) and unsafe ones; your body belongs to you.

- **Q:** Who is the boss of your body?
  **A:** I am.
- **Q:** What is a safe touch?
  **A:** A touch that is kind and makes you feel safe, like a hug from family when you want it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): No-Go-Tell
*Student:* Practice saying No, getting away and telling a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Practice saying 'No!' firmly, getting away, and telling a trusted adult.

- **Q:** What are the three steps?
  **A:** Say No, Go (get away), Tell a trusted adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Strangers & tricky people
*Student:* Learn about tricky people and never going anywhere without permission.

*How to teach:* Teach 'tricky people': anyone asking a child for help, offering gifts, or asking you to keep secrets. Never go anywhere without a parent's permission.

- **Q:** Should an adult ask a kid for help finding a lost pet?
  **A:** No, adults ask other adults for help; say no and tell.
- **Q:** Can you go with someone without asking your parent first?
  **A:** No, always check first.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Secrets vs surprises
*Student:* Distinguish surprises (fun, temporary) from unsafe secrets.

*How to teach:* Contrast surprises (fun, told soon, like a birthday gift) with unsafe secrets (make you feel bad, 'never tell').

- **Q:** Is a birthday present a secret or a surprise?
  **A:** A surprise; it will be told soon and makes people happy.
- **Q:** What do you do with an unsafe secret?
  **A:** Tell a trusted adult right away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Respond to 4 scenarios with No-Go-Tell.

*How to teach:* Respond to 4 scenarios with No-Go-Tell.

- **Q:** Who will you tell?
  **A:** A trusted adult, like a parent.

**Assessment:** Scenario responses.

**Answer key:** Scenario responses using No-Go-Tell (examples): 1) Someone wants to touch you where a swimsuit covers: say 'No!', get away, tell a trusted adult. 2) A person in a car asks you to help find a puppy: say no, go to your grown-up, tell. 3) Someone says 'Don't tell your mom about our secret game': say no, leave, tell. 4) An adult you don't know offers a ride home: say no, go, tell. Proficient = all 4 use No-Go-Tell and child names a trusted adult.
