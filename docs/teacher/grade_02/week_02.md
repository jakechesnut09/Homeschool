# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 2

## ELA 2: How characters respond to problems

**Prepare:** Preview 'A Lost Button' and 'A Swim' in Frog and Toad Are Friends. Gather sticky notes, drawing paper, a problem/solution chart, and short o/u/e word cards (hop, lot, pond, bug, cup, bed, net, sun, hen, fox).

**Watch for:**
- Retelling every tiny detail or retelling out of order. Use first, next, then, last on fingers — one event per finger.
- Calling verbs or describing words nouns. Ask 'Can you see it or touch it, or is it a person or place?' — those are nouns.

### Day 1 (Monday): A Lost Button
*Student:* Read 'A Lost Button' and list on sticky notes each button Frog and Toad find; discuss how Toad feels as each wrong button is found. Phonics: read short-o words (hop, lot, pond).

*How to teach:* Read 'A Lost Button.' Each time a button is found, the child writes it on a sticky note (black, two holes, small, thin, square). Ask how Toad feels each time. Then read short-o words.

- **Q:** Why did Toad keep saying 'That is not my button'?
  **A:** Each button was wrong: his button was white, with four holes, big, round, and thick.
- **Q:** How did Toad feel as each wrong button was found?
  **A:** More and more upset and angry — he finally shouts and runs home.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Problem and solution
*Student:* Fill in a problem/solution chart for 'A Lost Button' and tell how Toad responds at the end (he sews buttons on the jacket). Phonics: short-u and short-e word sort (bug, bed, cup, net).

*How to teach:* Fill a problem/solution chart together. Ask how Toad makes things right at the end. Then sort short-u and short-e words.

- **Q:** What is the problem in 'A Lost Button'?
  **A:** Toad lost a button from his jacket.
- **Q:** How does Toad fix things at the end?
  **A:** He finds his button on his floor, sews all the buttons onto his jacket, and gives it to Frog as a present.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Nouns are naming words
*Student:* Grammar: circle nouns (people, places, things) in 8 sentences from the story, then list 10 nouns you can see at home. Read 'A Swim' with expression.

*How to teach:* Teach: a noun names a person, place, or thing. Circle the nouns in 8 sentences from the story, then list 10 nouns at home. Read 'A Swim' with expression (Toad's lines sound embarrassed).

- **Q:** Circle the nouns: 'Toad lost a button on the path.'
  **A:** Toad, button, path.
- **Q:** In 'A Swim,' why doesn't Toad want anyone to see him?
  **A:** He thinks he looks funny in his bathing suit.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Small moment ideas
*Student:* Writing: brainstorm 3 'small moments' from your own summer and pick one; sketch it in 3 boxes (beginning, middle, end). Tell the story aloud to a listener using details.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm 3 small moments (one short event, like losing a tooth), pick one, and sketch it in 3 boxes. The child tells the story aloud with details.

- **Q:** Which is a small moment: 'my summer' or 'the time I caught a frog'?
  **A:** The time I caught a frog — one short event.
- **Q:** What goes in the three boxes?
  **A:** The beginning, middle, and end of the moment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 2 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 10 short-o/u/e words, retell 'A Lost Button' in order using first/next/last, and write one sentence about how Toad changed. Add titles to reading log.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling check. The child retells 'A Lost Button' using first, next, then, last, and writes one sentence about how Toad changed (angry → generous).

- **Q:** How did Toad change in 'A Lost Button'?
  **A:** He went from angry and upset to sorry and generous — he gave Frog a jacket with all the buttons.
- **Q:** Spell 'pond' and 'bug.'
  **A:** p-o-n-d; b-u-g.

**Assessment:** Problem/solution chart, spelling check, and oral retell checked for correct sequence.

**Answer key:** Problem/solution chart: Problem — Toad lost a button from his jacket. Attempts — Frog and Toad search and find wrong buttons (black, two holes, small, thin, square; Toad's was white, four holes, big, thick, round). Solution — Toad finds his button on his floor at home, sews all the found buttons on his jacket, and gives it to Frog. Spelling (suggested): hop, lot, pond, bug, cup, sun, bed, net, hen, pet (8+ proficient). Retell: correct order (lost the button → searched and found wrong buttons → Toad got angry and went home → found it on his floor → sewed the buttons on the jacket and gave it to Frog).

## Math 2: Make-a-ten and near doubles

**Prepare:** Draw or print double ten-frames, gather 20 counters in two colors, and a deck of cards (remove face cards; Ace = 1). Make fact cards for 8 + and 9 + facts and near doubles.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the leftover after making a ten (9 + 5 → 10 + 5 = 15). Have the child physically move 1 counter from the 5 to the 9 so they see 4 left: 10 + 4 = 14.
- Adding 1 to the wrong double for near doubles (6 + 7 = 7 + 7 + 1). Say 'use the SMALLER double, then add 1': 6 + 6 + 1 = 13.

### Day 1 (Monday): Make a ten
*Student:* Solve 9 + 5 by moving one counter to make 10 + 4 on a double ten-frame; practice with 8 + and 9 + facts.

*How to teach:* Put 9 counters in the top ten-frame and 5 in the bottom. Ask 'How can we fill the top frame?' Move 1 counter up: now 10 + 4 = 14. Practice 8 + and 9 + facts the same way, saying the new equation aloud each time.

- **Q:** Use make-a-ten to solve 9 + 6.
  **A:** 15 — move 1 from the 6 to the 9: 10 + 5 = 15.
- **Q:** Use make-a-ten to solve 8 + 5.
  **A:** 13 — move 2 from the 5 to the 8: 10 + 3 = 13.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Near doubles
*Student:* Use doubles to solve near doubles (6 + 7 = 6 + 6 + 1) and explain the strategy in words; sort 20 facts by best strategy.

*How to teach:* Show 6 + 7 with counters: 'I see a 6 + 6 double hiding, plus 1 more' — 12 + 1 = 13. The child explains a near double in their own words, then sorts 20 fact cards into piles: doubles, near doubles, make a ten, count on.

- **Q:** Solve 7 + 8 with a near double.
  **A:** 15 — 7 + 7 = 14, plus 1 more = 15.
- **Q:** Which strategy fits 9 + 4 best and what is the answer?
  **A:** Make a ten: 10 + 3 = 13.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 put-together/take-apart problems (e.g., 'Ana has 8 red and 7 blue marbles…') with a drawing and an equation.

*How to teach:* Read each problem twice. The child underlines the question, draws circles or a quick sketch, and writes an equation with the answer labeled. Ask 'Are we putting together or taking apart?'

- **Q:** Ana has 8 red and 7 blue marbles. How many marbles does she have?
  **A:** 15 marbles; 8 + 7 = 15.
- **Q:** There are 13 apples. 5 are green and the rest are red. How many are red?
  **A:** 8 red apples; 13 − 5 = 8 (or 5 + 8 = 13).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fact games
*Student:* Play 'Make Ten Go Fish' and 'Doubles War' with cards; record 10 facts from each game.

*How to teach:* Make Ten Go Fish: ask for the card that makes 10 with one in your hand. Doubles War: each player flips a card and doubles it; the larger double wins. The child records 10 facts from each game on paper.

- **Q:** In Make Ten Go Fish you hold a 3. What card do you ask for?
  **A:** A 7, because 3 + 7 = 10.
- **Q:** In Doubles War you flip an 8. What is your double?
  **A:** 16 (8 + 8).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on 30 addition facts within 20 and 3 word problems; explain one strategy aloud.

*How to teach:* Give the 30-fact quiz, then read the 3 word problems aloud. Finish by asking the child to choose one fact and explain the strategy out loud while you listen for clear steps.

- **Q:** Explain how to solve 8 + 6 with make-a-ten.
  **A:** Move 2 from the 6 to the 8 to make 10; 4 are left; 10 + 4 = 14.
- **Q:** What is 6 + 5?
  **A:** 11 (near double: 5 + 5 + 1).

**Assessment:** Fact quiz (30) and strategy explanation.

**Answer key:** Fact quiz: 30 sums within 20, score out of 30 (proficient 25+). Suggested items include 9+5=14, 8+6=14, 7+8=15, 6+6=12, 9+7=16, 8+8=16, 5+7=12, 9+9=18, 8+4=12, 6+7=13. Word problems (suggested): 1) 8 red + 7 blue marbles = 15; 2) 12 cars, 9 are red, how many not red? 12 − 9 = 3; 3) 9 books + 6 books = 15. Strategy explanation: proficient if the child names a strategy and walks through it correctly (e.g., 'For 9 + 6 I moved 1 to the 9 to make 10, then 10 + 5 = 15').

## Science 2: Engineers solve problems

**Prepare:** Gather a can opener, clothespin, zipper (on a jacket), paper, tape, paper clips, index cards, pennies, and 5 pencils. Write the design steps on a card: ask, imagine, plan, create, improve.

**Watch for:**
- Building before planning. Remind: engineers ask questions and sketch first.
- Thinking a design 'failed.' Testing shows what to improve — that's part of the process.

### Day 1 (Monday): What engineers do
*Student:* Look at everyday tools (can opener, clothespin, zipper) and discuss what problem each solves; record 3 in your notebook.

*How to teach:* Look at a can opener, clothespin, and zipper. Ask what problem each solves. The child records 3 in the notebook.

- **Q:** What problem does a clothespin solve?
  **A:** It holds things like wet clothes or papers together so they don't fall or blow away.
- **Q:** What problem does a zipper solve?
  **A:** It closes a jacket or bag quickly and keeps it shut.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Define the problem
*Student:* Problem: pencils keep rolling off the table. Ask questions and list what a solution must do.

*How to teach:* Present the problem: pencils keep rolling off the table. The child asks questions and lists what a solution must do (hold 5 pencils, not roll, use paper and tape).

- **Q:** Why do pencils roll?
  **A:** They are round (or six-sided) and roll on a smooth surface when pushed.
- **Q:** What must the pencil holder do?
  **A:** Hold the pencils so they don't roll off the table.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sketch
*Student:* Sketch two designs for a pencil holder from paper and tape and label how the shape keeps pencils from rolling.

*How to teach:* Sketch two pencil-holder designs from paper and tape and label how the shape keeps pencils from rolling.

- **Q:** What shape would stop pencils from rolling?
  **A:** A cup or a box with sides, or a tray with raised edges.
- **Q:** Why label a sketch?
  **A:** So others understand what each part is and does.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build & test
*Student:* Build the best design and test it with 5 pencils; record results and one improvement.

*How to teach:* Build the best design and test it with 5 pencils. Record results and one improvement.

- **Q:** If 2 pencils fell out, what could you improve?
  **A:** Make the walls taller or the base wider.
- **Q:** Why test with the same 5 pencils each time?
  **A:** So the test is fair.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Explain the design steps (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve) using your pencil holder as the example.

*How to teach:* The child explains the design steps (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve) using the pencil holder as the example.

- **Q:** What happens in the 'improve' step?
  **A:** You change the design to make it work better and test again.
- **Q:** Which step comes first?
  **A:** Ask — figure out the problem.

**Assessment:** Labeled design sketch and test results.

**Answer key:** Design sketch (3 = proficient): two labeled sketches showing materials and how the shape stops pencils from rolling (e.g., walls, a cup shape, or a flat bottom). Test results: number of pencils held without rolling (out of 5) recorded, plus one specific improvement (e.g., 'make the walls taller').

## People Who Make a Difference: My timeline

**Prepare:** Baby photos, adding-machine tape or long paper strip, markers; 6 morning-routine picture cards; a list of key family dates from a parent.

**Watch for:**
- Putting events in the order remembered instead of the order they happened: check years.
- Uneven spacing on the timeline: each year gets the same space.

### Day 1 (Monday): Order events
*Student:* Put 6 picture cards of a morning routine in order, then talk through the events of your own life using before and after.

*How to teach:* Order 6 morning-routine cards; talk through your life using before and after.

- **Q:** What comes before eating breakfast?
  **A:** Example: waking up and getting dressed.
- **Q:** What is a sequence?
  **A:** The order things happen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gather facts
*Student:* Ask a parent for your birth date and 6 important events (first steps, first tooth, starting school, a move, a sibling's birth) with years.

*How to teach:* Ask a parent for birth date and 6 important events with years.

- **Q:** What year were you born?
  **A:** Child's correct birth year.
- **Q:** What is an event?
  **A:** Something that happens.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build the timeline
*Student:* Make a timeline on adding-machine tape with one mark per year from birth to now and place each event with a drawing or photo.

*How to teach:* Make a timeline with one mark per year; place each event.

- **Q:** If you were born in 2018 and started kindergarten in 2023, how many years apart?
  **A:** 5 years.
- **Q:** Which end of the timeline is the oldest event?
  **A:** The left (the beginning).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Storyboard
*Student:* Make a 4-box storyboard of one year of your life and tell it aloud in order.

*How to teach:* Make a 4-box storyboard of one year and tell it in order.

- **Q:** What happened first in your storyboard?
  **A:** Child's first event.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Present the timeline and answer questions using 'first, next, before, after, last.'

*How to teach:* Present the timeline using first, next, before, after, last.

- **Q:** What happened right after you learned to walk?
  **A:** Answer from their timeline.

**Assessment:** Personal timeline with events in correct order.

**Answer key:** Timeline: equal marks for each year from birth year to now, 6 events placed in the correct year and order with drawings/photos; oral presentation uses first, next, before, after, last correctly. Proficient = all events correctly ordered and placed.

## Art & Music 2: Geometric and organic shapes: Kandinsky

**Prepare:** Image of Kandinsky's 'Squares with Concentric Circles' (1913), cut-paper shapes, cups or a compass for tracing, oil pastels, ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking organic shapes are just 'messy': organic shapes are natural, curvy, free-form (leaves, clouds).
- Thinking abstract art has no plan: Kandinsky chose colors and shapes carefully to create feelings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shape sort
*Student:* Sort cut-paper shapes into geometric (circles, squares, triangles) and organic (leaf, puddle, cloud) and find both kinds in your home.

*How to teach:* Sort cut shapes into geometric and organic; find both at home.

- **Q:** Is a leaf geometric or organic?
  **A:** Organic.
- **Q:** Is a window pane geometric or organic?
  **A:** Geometric (rectangle).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Look at Kandinsky
*Student:* Look at Kandinsky's 'Squares with Concentric Circles' and describe the shapes, colors, and patterns you see.

*How to teach:* Look at Kandinsky's painting; describe shapes, colors and patterns.

- **Q:** What shapes did Kandinsky repeat?
  **A:** Circles inside circles (concentric circles) inside squares.
- **Q:** What is abstract art?
  **A:** Art that uses shapes, colors and lines instead of showing real things.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Concentric circles
*Student:* Draw a 3x4 grid and fill each square with concentric circles in oil pastel, using different colors in each ring.

*How to teach:* Draw a 3x4 grid; fill each square with concentric circles in oil pastel, a different color in each ring.

- **Q:** How many squares in a 3x4 grid?
  **A:** 12.
- **Q:** What are concentric circles?
  **A:** Circles inside each other with the same center.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish and add
*Student:* Finish the Kandinsky-style grid and add a border of geometric shapes.

*How to teach:* Finish the grid; add a border of geometric shapes.

- **Q:** What shapes are in your border?
  **A:** Geometric shapes such as triangles, squares, circles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Tell which square you like best and why, using the words geometric, pattern, and contrast.

*How to teach:* Tell which square you like best using geometric, pattern, contrast.

- **Q:** What is contrast?
  **A:** A big difference, like light next to dark or warm next to cool colors.

**Assessment:** Kandinsky-inspired circles artwork and reflection.

**Answer key:** Artwork: 3x4 grid (12 squares) with concentric circles in each, varied colors in each ring, border of geometric shapes. Reflection uses 'geometric,' 'pattern,' 'contrast' correctly (e.g., 'I like the square with yellow next to purple because the contrast makes it pop'). Proficient = complete grid + all three words used correctly.

## PE 2: Changing speed and direction

**Prepare:** Drum or pot and spoon, cones, sidewalk chalk; index cards for cue cards.

**Watch for:**
- Going backward without looking: glance over the shoulder and go slowly.
- Changing speed but not the skill's form: keep good form at every speed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Speed dial
*Student:* Move to a drumbeat: slow beats = slow walk, medium = jog, fast = quick feet; switch speeds with the beat.

*How to teach:* Beat slowly (walk), medium (jog), fast (quick feet); change speeds with the beat.

- **Q:** What speed do fast beats mean?
  **A:** Move fast (quick feet).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Direction drill
*Student:* Travel forward, backward and sideways between cones, looking over the shoulder when moving backward.

*How to teach:* Travel forward, backward and sideways between cones, looking over the shoulder backward.

- **Q:** Name three directions.
  **A:** Forward, backward, sideways.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Zigzag & curve
*Student:* Draw straight, curved and zigzag pathways in chalk and travel each with three different locomotor skills.

*How to teach:* Draw straight, curved and zigzag chalk paths; travel each with three locomotor skills.

- **Q:** Which pathway has sharp corners?
  **A:** Zigzag.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Traffic jam
*Student:* Play 'cars and traffic lights': green = fast, yellow = slow, red = stop, 'reverse' = backward, 'turn' = change direction.

*How to teach:* Play cars and traffic lights: green fast, yellow slow, red stop, 'reverse' backward, 'turn' change direction.

- **Q:** What does yellow mean?
  **A:** Slow down.
- **Q:** What does 'reverse' mean?
  **A:** Move backward.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Follow a 6-part cue card (e.g., slow-zigzag-backward) and draw your favorite pathway.

*How to teach:* Give a 6-part cue card; your child performs it, then draws a favorite pathway.

- **Q:** What is the difference between speed and direction?
  **A:** Speed is how fast; direction is which way you move.

**Assessment:** Cue-card sequence performed accurately; pathway drawing.

**Answer key:** Cue-card sequence: performs all 6 parts in order (e.g., slow-zigzag-backward-fast-sideways-stop) correctly. Pathway drawing correctly labeled (straight, curved or zigzag). Proficient = 6/6; developing = 4-5; beginning = 3 or fewer.

## Health 2: Calming strategies

**Prepare:** Jar with lid, water, glitter glue or clear glue and glitter (adult seals lid with glue), pillow, book, feelings chart; GoNoodle Flow or kids yoga video.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking calming down means hiding feelings: calming helps you think so you can solve the problem.
- Breathing fast when trying to calm down: slow, deep belly breaths work best.

### Day 1 (Monday): Breathing tools
*Student:* Practice balloon breathing, five-finger breathing and counting to 10.

*How to teach:* Model balloon breathing (slowly fill your belly like a balloon, let it out), five-finger breathing (trace up a finger breathing in, down breathing out), and counting to 10.

- **Q:** How do you do five-finger breathing?
  **A:** Trace up each finger while breathing in and down while breathing out.
- **Q:** Why do slow breaths help?
  **A:** They slow your heart and body so you feel calmer.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Calm-down jar
*Student:* Make a glitter calm-down jar and watch the glitter settle while breathing slowly.

*How to teach:* Make a glitter calm-down jar; shake it and breathe slowly as glitter settles.

- **Q:** How is the jar like your feelings?
  **A:** When upset, thoughts swirl like glitter; when you calm down, they settle.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Calm corner
*Student:* Set up a calm corner at home with a pillow, book and feelings chart.

*How to teach:* Set up a calm corner with a pillow, book and feelings chart; agree on how to use it.

- **Q:** When can you use the calm corner?
  **A:** When you have a big feeling and need to calm down.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mindful movement
*Student:* Do a GoNoodle Flow or kids yoga video and notice how your body feels after.

*How to teach:* Do a GoNoodle Flow or yoga video; notice how your body feels.

- **Q:** How did your body feel after?
  **A:** Answers like relaxed, slower breathing, calmer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Role-play a frustrating moment (losing a game) and use one calming strategy.

*How to teach:* Role-play losing a game and use one calming strategy.

- **Q:** What is stress?
  **A:** A worried or pressured feeling when something is hard.

**Assessment:** Role-play demonstration of a calming strategy.

**Answer key:** Role-play: in a pretend frustrating moment (losing a game), child names the feeling, uses one strategy correctly (balloon breathing, five-finger breathing, counting to 10, calm-down jar, calm corner), then says a calm next step ('Good game, can we play again?'). Proficient = all three parts.
