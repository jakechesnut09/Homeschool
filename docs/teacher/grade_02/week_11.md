# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 11

## ELA 2: Main topic, paragraphs, and diagrams

**Prepare:** Get From Tadpole to Frog (Wendy Pfeffer) and 2 library books about an animal the child chooses. Prepare a 3-box note sheet (body, home, food) and a y-word list (happy, funny, baby, sky, fly, cry, my, puppy). Print a new 3-paragraph passage for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing the main topic with one detail ('frogs have long legs'). Ask 'What is the WHOLE book mostly about?'
- Writing a paragraph without a topic sentence. The first sentence should tell what the whole paragraph is about.

### Day 1 (Monday): Main topic
*Student:* Read From Tadpole to Frog and name the main topic; reread 3 paragraphs and tell what each one is mostly about. Phonics: y as long e and long i (happy, sky).

*How to teach:* Read From Tadpole to Frog and name the main topic. Reread 3 paragraphs; the child tells what each is mostly about. Teach y as long e (happy) and long i (sky).

- **Q:** What is From Tadpole to Frog mostly about?
  **A:** How a frog grows and changes from an egg to a tadpole to a grown frog.
- **Q:** How does a tadpole breathe underwater?
  **A:** With gills, like a fish.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Diagrams help
*Student:* Study the diagram of the frog life cycle and explain what it shows that the words do not. Draw your own labeled diagram of a tadpole.

*How to teach:* Study the life-cycle diagram. Ask what it shows that the words don't (all stages at once, in order). The child draws a labeled tadpole.

- **Q:** What does the diagram show that the words do not?
  **A:** All the stages side by side in order, so you can see how the body changes.
- **Q:** In what order does a tadpole grow legs?
  **A:** Back legs first, then front legs; then the tail shrinks away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Choose an animal
*Student:* Choose an animal for an 'All About' report, read 2 library books on it, and take notes in 3 boxes: body, home, food.

*How to teach:* The child chooses an animal, reads 2 library books, and takes notes in 3 boxes: body, home, food.

- **Q:** What 3 boxes do you use for notes?
  **A:** Body, home, and food.
- **Q:** Why use 2 books instead of 1?
  **A:** To find more facts and check that facts match.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Topic sentences
*Student:* Learn that an informative paragraph starts with a topic sentence; draft paragraph 1 (body) with a topic sentence and 3 facts. Phonics: y word sort.

*How to teach:* Teach topic sentences. Draft paragraph 1 (body) with a topic sentence and 3 facts. Sort y words.

- **Q:** Write a topic sentence for a paragraph about an owl's body.
  **A:** Example: Owls have bodies made for hunting at night.
- **Q:** Sort: happy, sky.
  **A:** happy — y says long e; sky — y says long i.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 11 review
*Student:* Read a new 3-paragraph passage and write the main topic and the focus of each paragraph. Spelling check of y-as-vowel words.

*How to teach:* The child reads a new 3-paragraph passage and writes the main topic and focus of each paragraph, then takes the y-word spelling check.

- **Q:** How do you find a paragraph's focus?
  **A:** Look at the first sentence and ask what most sentences are about.
- **Q:** Spell 'puppy' and 'cry.'
  **A:** p-u-p-p-y; c-r-y.

**Assessment:** Main-topic and paragraph-focus organizer on a cold read; first report paragraph.

**Answer key:** Cold-read organizer: main topic stated correctly for the passage and one focus per paragraph (answers depend on the passage). First report paragraph rubric: 3 = topic sentence about the animal's body + 3 accurate facts in own words; 2 = facts but no topic sentence; 1 = fewer than 2 facts. Spelling (suggested): happy, funny, baby, puppy, sunny, sky, fly, cry, my, try.

## Math 2: Comparing lengths

**Prepare:** Rulers (inch and cm), ribbon or string, scissors, a toy car and a board or large book for a ramp, and masking tape to mark start lines. Prepare Unit 3 test objects and 4 word problems.

**Watch for:**
- Adding the two lengths to find 'how much longer.' Lay the objects side by side and point to only the extra part — subtract or count up.
- Comparing measurements in different units (8 inches vs. 15 cm). You can only compare when both are in the same unit.

### Day 1 (Monday): How much longer
*Student:* Measure two pencils and find how much longer one is by counting on from the shorter length.

*How to teach:* Measure two pencils (e.g., 7 in and 4 in) and lay them side by side at a starting line. Count on from the shorter length to the longer: 5, 6, 7 — 3 inches longer. Write 7 − 4 = 3.

- **Q:** Pencil A is 7 inches and pencil B is 4 inches. How much longer is A?
  **A:** 3 inches; 7 − 4 = 3.
- **Q:** A crayon is 9 cm and a marker is 14 cm. How much longer is the marker?
  **A:** 5 cm; 14 − 9 = 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ribbon compare
*Student:* Cut ribbons of different lengths, measure each in cm, and write 5 'how much longer' sentences.

*How to teach:* Cut 4–5 ribbons of different lengths. The child measures each in cm and writes 5 sentences: 'The red ribbon is __ cm longer than the blue ribbon,' with the subtraction shown.

- **Q:** A red ribbon is 25 cm and a blue ribbon is 16 cm. How much longer is the red one?
  **A:** 9 cm; 25 − 16 = 9.
- **Q:** What operation finds 'how much longer'?
  **A:** Subtraction (or counting up from the shorter to the longer).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Toy car race
*Student:* Roll toy cars down a ramp, measure distances, and find the difference between the farthest and shortest rolls.

*How to teach:* Set up a ramp and roll a toy car 5 times from the same start, marking where it stops. Measure each distance in inches, find the farthest and shortest, and subtract to find the difference.

- **Q:** The farthest roll was 48 inches and the shortest was 30. What is the difference?
  **A:** 18 inches; 48 − 30 = 18.
- **Q:** Why must every roll start at the same line?
  **A:** So the measurements are fair to compare.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family lengths
*Student:* Measure family members' arm spans in inches and write compare sentences ('Dad's arm span is 14 inches longer than mine').

*How to teach:* Measure arm spans (fingertip to fingertip, arms stretched wide) of family members in inches. The child writes compare sentences using subtraction.

- **Q:** Dad's arm span is 66 inches and yours is 48. How much longer is Dad's?
  **A:** 18 inches; 66 − 48 = 18.
- **Q:** Mom's span is 62 inches, yours is 48. How much longer?
  **A:** 14 inches; 62 − 48 = 14.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Measure objects in two unit systems, estimate lengths, and solve 4 'how much longer' problems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test: measure objects in two units, estimate lengths, and solve 4 'how much longer' problems with equations.

- **Q:** For the same object, will the number be bigger in inches or in centimeters?
  **A:** Centimeters, because they are smaller units.
- **Q:** A rope is 35 inches and a string is 20 inches. How much longer is the rope?
  **A:** 15 inches.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test (suggested): measuring — 3 objects measured in inches and cm (accept ±1 unit; the cm number should always be larger). Estimates — reasonable choices (e.g., a door about 7 feet or 2 meters; a pencil about 7 inches or 18 cm). Problems: 1) Pencil A 7 in, pencil B 4 in: 3 in longer. 2) Ribbons 25 cm and 16 cm: 9 cm longer. 3) Car rolled 48 in and 30 in: 18 in farther. 4) Dad's arm span 66 in, child's 48 in: 18 in longer. Proficient: accurate measures and 3/4 problems with equations.

## Science 2: Matter unit project: recycled materials

**Prepare:** Collect clean recyclables (boxes, tubes, caps, egg cartons), glue, tape, scissors, a camera or drawing paper, and juice and ice-pop molds. Prepare an 8-item quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a melted-and-refrozen ice pop is a completely different material. It is still frozen juice (reversible), though the shape may change.
- Gluing so pieces can't come apart for the rebuild. Use tape where you plan to take it apart.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Collect clean recyclables and plan a new object (toy, organizer) to build from them; sketch it.

*How to teach:* Plan a new object (toy, organizer) from recyclables and sketch it.

- **Q:** What object will you build?
  **A:** Example: a pencil organizer from tubes and a box.
- **Q:** Why sketch first?
  **A:** To plan which pieces go where.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the object with glue and tape, counting how many of each piece you used, and label its parts.

*How to teach:* Build the object, count pieces used, and label parts.

- **Q:** How many tubes did you use?
  **A:** Answer from count.
- **Q:** What is each part for?
  **A:** Example: tubes hold pencils; the box is the base.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rebuild
*Student:* Disassemble and rebuild into a second, different object; photograph or draw both.

*How to teach:* Disassemble and rebuild into a different object; draw or photograph both.

- **Q:** Did you use the same pieces?
  **A:** Yes — same pieces, new object.
- **Q:** How is this like the block house?
  **A:** Same pieces were rearranged into a new object.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ice pop test
*Student:* Freeze juice pops and let one melt and refreeze; discuss if it is the same as before.

*How to teach:* Freeze juice pops; let one melt and refreeze. Discuss if it's the same.

- **Q:** Is the refrozen pop still juice?
  **A:** Yes — freezing and melting are reversible.
- **Q:** What might be different?
  **A:** Its shape (it may not fit the mold shape).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Quiz on pieces/new objects and reversible/irreversible changes; present the recycled builds.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz and have the child present the recycled builds.

- **Q:** Is baking a cake reversible?
  **A:** No.
- **Q:** Is melting ice reversible?
  **A:** Yes.

**Assessment:** Recycled-object presentation and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Presentation: shows 2 different objects from the same recyclables with piece counts. Unit quiz (suggested 8): 1) Can the same blocks make a different object? Yes 2) Melting ice — reversible 3) Baking bread — irreversible 4) Freezing water — reversible 5) Burning paper — irreversible 6) Melting crayons and cooling — reversible 7) Cooking an egg — irreversible 8) A refrozen ice pop is still juice — reversible change.

## People Who Make a Difference: Urban, suburban, and rural California

**Prepare:** Calisphere photos of Los Angeles, a suburb, and a Central Valley farm; California map; chart paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking rural means 'poor' or 'empty': rural areas have farms, ranches and small towns with lots of open land.
- Thinking suburbs are cities: suburbs are mostly neighborhoods of houses near a city.

### Day 1 (Monday): Three communities
*Student:* Look at photos of Los Angeles, a suburb, and a Central Valley farm and list what land is used for in each.

*How to teach:* Look at photos of Los Angeles, a suburb and a Central Valley farm; list land uses.

- **Q:** What is the land used for in a city?
  **A:** Buildings, homes, stores, offices, roads.
- **Q:** What is land used for in the Central Valley?
  **A:** Farming (crops, orchards) and ranching.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a three-column chart comparing housing, jobs, transportation, and land use.

*How to teach:* Make a three-column chart: housing, jobs, transportation, land use.

- **Q:** What is suburban?
  **A:** An area of mostly homes just outside a city.
- **Q:** What is rural?
  **A:** Countryside with farms and lots of open land.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Map it
*Student:* Mark 3 urban, 3 suburban, and 3 rural places on a California map.

*How to teach:* Mark 3 urban (e.g., Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego), 3 suburban, 3 rural places on a California map.

- **Q:** Is Los Angeles urban, suburban or rural?
  **A:** Urban.

### Day 4 (Thursday): My community
*Student:* Decide whether your community is urban, suburban, or rural and give 3 reasons.

*How to teach:* Decide your community type; give 3 reasons.

- **Q:** Why is your community that type?
  **A:** Evidence about housing, jobs, transportation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Quiz on map elements, grids, North America labels, and land use types.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 quiz.

- **Q:** What does a compass rose show?
  **A:** Directions: north, south, east, west.

**Assessment:** Land-use chart and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Land-use chart: Urban - apartments/tall buildings, office and store jobs, buses/trains, land mostly buildings and roads; Suburban - houses with yards, many people drive to jobs in the city, cars, land for homes, schools, shopping centers; Rural - farmhouses far apart, farming/ranching jobs, cars and trucks, land for crops and animals. Unit 2 quiz: map elements, grid squares, North America labels, land-use types correct. Proficient = chart complete + 80% quiz.

## Art & Music 2: Tints, shades, and Monet's gardens

**Prepare:** Image of Monet's 'Water Lilies,' white and black paint, one color, watercolor paper, brushes; a spot outdoors.

**Watch for:**
- Adding too much black for shades: add a tiny dot at a time.
- Thinking Impressionists painted every detail: they used short dabs to capture light quickly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Value strip
*Student:* Make a 7-step value strip from one color by adding white (tints) on one side and black (shades) on the other.

*How to teach:* Make a 7-step value strip: middle pure color, white added on one side, black on the other.

- **Q:** What is a tint?
  **A:** A color plus white (lighter).
- **Q:** What is a shade?
  **A:** A color plus black (darker).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Monet's Water Lilies
*Student:* Look at Monet's 'Water Lilies' and notice the short brushstrokes and many tints of blue and green; describe the time of day you think it shows.

*How to teach:* Look at Monet's 'Water Lilies'; notice short brushstrokes and many tints of blue and green.

- **Q:** What did Monet paint again and again?
  **A:** The water lily pond in his garden at Giverny, France.
- **Q:** What art style was Monet part of?
  **A:** Impressionism.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paint outside
*Student:* Sit outside like Monet and paint a garden, tree, or sky using short dabbed brushstrokes.

*How to teach:* Paint outside with short dabbed strokes.

- **Q:** Why did Monet paint outside?
  **A:** To capture real light and color as it changed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Add light and shadow
*Student:* Finish the landscape by adding tints where the sun hits and shades where shadows fall.

*How to teach:* Add tints where the sun hits and shades where shadows fall.

- **Q:** Where do you put shades?
  **A:** In shadows.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Point to 3 tints and 3 shades in your painting and explain how you mixed them.

*How to teach:* Point to 3 tints and 3 shades; explain mixing.

- **Q:** What is value?
  **A:** How light or dark a color is.

**Assessment:** Value strip and observational landscape.

**Answer key:** Value strip: 7 steps, gradual change: tints (white added) lighter on one side, pure color in middle, shades (black added) darker on the other. Landscape: short dabbed strokes; tints where sun hits, shades in shadow. Reflection points to 3 tints and 3 shades and explains mixing. Proficient = smooth value change + correct identification.

## PE 2: Rolling safely

**Prepare:** Soft mat, mattress or grassy gentle slope. Clear space; parent ready to spot.

**Watch for:**
- Putting the head down on top of the head: tuck chin to chest so you roll on the back of the shoulders.
- Opening the tuck: stay rounded like a ball.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rolling review
*Student:* Practice log rolls and egg rolls on a mat, keeping arms in and body tight.

*How to teach:* Review log and egg rolls with arms in and body tight.

- **Q:** What does 'body tight' mean?
  **A:** Muscles squeezed so the body moves as one piece.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rocking chair
*Student:* Rock back and forth in a tuck, chin on chest, to learn the rounded back for a forward roll.

*How to teach:* Rock back and forth in a tuck, chin on chest.

- **Q:** Why rock first?
  **A:** To learn a rounded back for the forward roll.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Forward roll on a slope
*Student:* Roll forward down a gentle grass slope or folded mattress: hands down, chin tucked, push with feet.

*How to teach:* Roll forward down a gentle slope: hands down, chin tucked, push with feet.

- **Q:** Where does your chin go?
  **A:** Tucked to your chest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Forward roll on flat
*Student:* Practice forward rolls on the flat mat with a parent spotting at the hips.

*How to teach:* Forward rolls on flat mat; spot at hips.

- **Q:** What part of your body touches the mat first after your hands?
  **A:** The back of your shoulders (not your head).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform 2 forward rolls with chin tucked and say the 3 safety cues.

*How to teach:* Perform 2 forward rolls and say 3 cues.

- **Q:** Say the forward roll cues.
  **A:** Hands down, chin tucked, round back (push with feet).

**Assessment:** Forward-roll checklist: hands placed, chin tucked, rounded back.

**Answer key:** Forward roll checklist: hands flat on mat shoulder-width; chin tucked to chest; rounded back; push with feet; rolls straight. Safety cues: hands down, chin tucked, round back. Proficient = 2 rolls with all cues; stop practice if your child complains of neck discomfort.

## Health 2: MyPlate food groups

**Prepare:** MyPlate printable (myplate.gov), grocery flyers, scissors, glue; 15 food cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cheese is protein: on MyPlate, cheese is in dairy (beans and eggs are protein).
- Thinking potatoes and corn are grains: potatoes are vegetables; corn kernels count as a vegetable (cornmeal/popcorn are grains).

### Day 1 (Monday): MyPlate
*Student:* Learn the 5 food groups using MyPlate and name 3 foods in each.

*How to teach:* Teach the 5 MyPlate groups; name 3 foods for each.

- **Q:** Name the 5 food groups.
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy.
- **Q:** Which group is yogurt?
  **A:** Dairy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flyer sort
*Student:* Cut foods from grocery flyers and glue them onto a MyPlate poster.

*How to teach:* Cut foods from flyers; glue onto MyPlate.

- **Q:** Where do beans go?
  **A:** Protein (they can also count as vegetables).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plate check
*Student:* Draw your dinner and check which food groups it had and which were missing.

*How to teach:* Draw your dinner; check groups present and missing.

- **Q:** Which group was missing from your dinner?
  **A:** Child identifies.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Food group game
*Student:* Play 'food group freeze': run to the corner for the food group called.

*How to teach:* Play food group freeze.

- **Q:** Which group is rice?
  **A:** Grains.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort 15 food cards into groups and plan one balanced lunch.

*How to teach:* Sort 15 cards; plan a balanced lunch.

- **Q:** Which part of MyPlate is about half the plate?
  **A:** Fruits and vegetables together make about half.

**Assessment:** Food sort and balanced lunch plan.

**Answer key:** Food sort (example 15): Fruits - apple, banana, orange; Vegetables - carrot, broccoli, potato; Grains - bread, rice, oatmeal; Protein - chicken, eggs, beans; Dairy - milk, yogurt, cheese. Balanced lunch includes all 5 groups (e.g., turkey sandwich on whole-wheat bread, carrots, apple, milk). Proficient = 13+/15 + balanced lunch.
