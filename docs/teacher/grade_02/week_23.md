# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 23

## ELA 2: Point of view: whose story is it?

**Prepare:** Get The Three Little Pigs (traditional) and The True Story of the 3 Little Pigs! (Jon Scieszka). Have Cinderella and the opinion organizer handy. Pick a short new fable for Friday (e.g., 'The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking point of view only means 'I' or 'he.' Ask 'Who is telling this story, and what do they think happened?'
- Opinion drafts with reasons but no linking words. Add because, also, another reason, and finally.

### Day 1 (Monday): Traditional pigs
*Student:* Read The Three Little Pigs and retell it from the pigs' point of view. Practice voices for each pig and the wolf.

*How to teach:* Read The Three Little Pigs and retell it from the pigs' point of view, practicing voices for each pig and the wolf.

- **Q:** Why does the third pig's house stand?
  **A:** It is made of bricks, which the wolf can't blow down.
- **Q:** How would the pigs describe the wolf?
  **A:** Big, bad, and mean — he tries to blow their houses down and eat them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The wolf's side
*Student:* Read The True Story of the 3 Little Pigs! and discuss how the wolf tells it differently. Chart what the pigs think vs. what the wolf claims.

*How to teach:* Read The True Story of the 3 Little Pigs!. Chart what the pigs think vs. what the wolf claims.

- **Q:** Why does the wolf say he went to the pigs' houses?
  **A:** To borrow a cup of sugar for his granny's birthday cake.
- **Q:** What does the wolf blame for blowing the houses down?
  **A:** A bad cold that made him sneeze.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stepsister's view
*Student:* Retell a scene from Cinderella in 4–5 sentences from a stepsister's point of view. Read it aloud in her voice.

*How to teach:* Retell a Cinderella scene in 4–5 sentences from a stepsister's point of view and read it in her voice.

- **Q:** Write a first sentence from a stepsister's view.
  **A:** Example: I could not believe the prince only danced with that mysterious girl!
- **Q:** What word should a first-person retelling use?
  **A:** I (me, my).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Opinion draft
*Student:* Draft the opinion piece 'The Best Cinderella Story' with an opening, opinion, 3 reasons using because, also, and another reason, and a concluding sentence.

*How to teach:* Draft 'The Best Cinderella Story' with an opening, opinion, 3 reasons with linking words, and a conclusion.

- **Q:** Name 3 linking words for reasons.
  **A:** because, also, another reason (or finally).
- **Q:** What does the concluding sentence do?
  **A:** Restates the opinion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 23 review
*Student:* Read a short new fable and tell how two characters see the event differently. Peer/parent check of the opinion draft for linking words.

*How to teach:* Read a new fable and tell how two characters see the event differently. Check the opinion draft for linking words.

- **Q:** In 'The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse,' how does each mouse see city life?
  **A:** The town mouse loves its rich food; the country mouse thinks it is too scary and dangerous.
- **Q:** Find the linking words in: 'I like it because it is funny. Also, the pictures are bright.'
  **A:** because, also.

**Assessment:** Point-of-view retelling and opinion draft with at least 3 linking words.

**Answer key:** Point-of-view retelling (stepsister): proficient = 4–5 sentences in first person ('I') that show the stepsister's feelings and stay true to story events. Opinion draft: opening, clear opinion, 3 reasons with at least 3 linking words (because, also, another reason), and a conclusion. New fable check (Town Mouse and Country Mouse): the town mouse thinks the country is boring and plain; the country mouse thinks the city is too dangerous — she prefers a simple, safe life.

## Math 2: Coins and counting money

**Prepare:** Real or play coins (pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters) and a coin purse or cup. Price pretend-store items under $1.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a nickel is worth more than a dime because it is bigger. Value is not size: a dime is 10¢, a nickel 5¢.
- Counting each coin as 1. Sort coins first, then skip-count by each coin's value, starting with the greatest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Coin values
*Student:* Sort real coins and learn values (1¢, 5¢, 10¢, 25¢); skip-count dimes by 10 and nickels by 5.

*How to teach:* Sort coins and name them: penny 1¢, nickel 5¢, dime 10¢, quarter 25¢. Skip-count a row of dimes by 10s and a row of nickels by 5s.

- **Q:** Which is worth more: a nickel or a dime?
  **A:** A dime (10¢), even though it is smaller.
- **Q:** How much are 6 dimes?
  **A:** 60¢.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Count mixed coins
*Student:* Count mixed coins starting with the greatest value (25, 35, 40, 41); count 10 handfuls and record.

*How to teach:* Count mixed coins starting with the greatest value: quarter 25, dime 35, nickel 40, penny 41. Count 10 handfuls and record.

- **Q:** Count: 1 quarter, 1 dime, 1 nickel, 1 penny.
  **A:** 41¢.
- **Q:** Count: 2 quarters, 2 dimes, 1 penny.
  **A:** 71¢.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ways to make
*Student:* Show 50¢ four different ways with coins and record with drawings.

*How to teach:* Show 50¢ four ways (2 quarters; 5 dimes; 1 quarter, 2 dimes, 1 nickel; 10 nickels) and draw each.

- **Q:** Show 50¢ with quarters only.
  **A:** 2 quarters.
- **Q:** Show 50¢ with 1 quarter plus dimes and nickels.
  **A:** Example: 1 quarter + 2 dimes + 1 nickel = 50¢.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coin store
*Student:* Run a pretend store with items under $1; pay with exact change and record each purchase.

*How to teach:* Run a pretend store; the child pays exact change for items under $1 and records each purchase as coins and total cents.

- **Q:** An apple costs 37¢. Which coins pay exactly?
  **A:** Example: 1 quarter, 1 dime, 2 pennies.
- **Q:** A toy costs 85¢. Show it with quarters and a dime.
  **A:** 3 quarters + 1 dime.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Count 8 coin collections and solve 3 word problems with cents.

*How to teach:* Give the coin-counting quiz (8 collections) and 3 word problems.

- **Q:** Jo has 2 dimes and 3 nickels. How many cents?
  **A:** 35¢.
- **Q:** Mia has 60¢ and spends 25¢. How much is left?
  **A:** 35¢.

**Assessment:** Coin-counting quiz and word problems.

**Answer key:** Coin quiz (suggested 8): 1) 3 dimes = 30¢ 2) 2 quarters, 1 dime = 60¢ 3) 1 quarter, 2 nickels, 3 pennies = 38¢ 4) 4 nickels = 20¢ 5) 3 quarters = 75¢ 6) 1 quarter, 1 dime, 1 nickel, 1 penny = 41¢ 7) 5 dimes, 2 pennies = 52¢ 8) 2 dimes, 3 nickels = 35¢. Word problems: a) Jo has 2 dimes and 3 nickels → 35¢. b) A pencil costs 45¢; Sam pays with 2 quarters → 5¢ change. c) Mia has 60¢ and spends 25¢ → 35¢ left. Proficient: 10/11.

## Science 2: Habitat diorama project

**Prepare:** Gather a shoebox, construction paper, clay, twigs, sand, cotton, paint, and index cards. Help the child find 2 sources on the chosen habitat. Prepare an 8-item matching quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Placing animals in the wrong habitat (a polar bear in the desert). Check each animal with a source.
- Labels that only name the animal. Labels should tell what it eats and where it shelters.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose and research
*Student:* Choose one habitat (redwood forest, Mojave Desert, kelp forest, tide pool, or pond) and use 2 sources to list 6 living things and what each needs.

*How to teach:* Choose a habitat and use 2 sources to list 6 living things and what each needs.

- **Q:** Name 2 living things in a pond habitat.
  **A:** Examples: frog, duckweed, dragonfly.
- **Q:** Why use 2 sources?
  **A:** To find more facts and check them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build the background
*Student:* Paint or line a shoebox to show the habitat's land, water, and sky, and add plants made from paper, twigs, or clay.

*How to teach:* Paint or line the shoebox to show land, water, and sky; add plants.

- **Q:** What would the background show for a desert?
  **A:** Sand, rocks, a bright sky, cactus.
- **Q:** What would a kelp forest show?
  **A:** Water, tall kelp, a rocky sea floor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add animals and labels
*Student:* Model at least 4 animals in clay or paper and add index-card labels telling what each eats and where it shelters.

*How to teach:* Model at least 4 animals and add labels telling what each eats and where it shelters.

- **Q:** Write a label for a desert tortoise.
  **A:** Desert tortoise — eats grasses and plants; shelters in a burrow.
- **Q:** Write a label for a sea otter.
  **A:** Sea otter — eats sea urchins, crabs, and clams; rests wrapped in kelp.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the diorama, naming how many kinds of living things it shows, and compare it with one habitat from the chart (more or less diversity and why).

*How to teach:* Present the diorama, naming how many kinds of living things it shows, and compare with another habitat.

- **Q:** Does your habitat have more or less diversity than the desert? Why?
  **A:** Answer with reasons (more water and plants = more kinds).
- **Q:** What should a presenter do?
  **A:** Speak clearly and point to each part.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Quiz: match 8 organisms to habitats and explain which of two pictured habitats has more diversity.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz.

- **Q:** Where does a sea star live?
  **A:** Tide pool (rocky ocean shore).
- **Q:** Where does a roadrunner live?
  **A:** Desert.

**Assessment:** Diorama presentation (rubric) and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Diorama rubric (3 = proficient): habitat clearly shown (land, water, sky); at least 6 accurate living things including 4 animals; labels tell food and shelter; comparison with another habitat explained. Quiz (suggested 8): Joshua tree — desert; banana slug — redwood forest; sea otter — kelp forest; sea star — tide pool; tadpole — pond; roadrunner — desert; owl — forest; anemone — tide pool. Diversity question: the habitat with more water and plants has more kinds.

## People Who Make a Difference: Limited resources and choices

**Prepare:** Crackers, play money, a pretend restaurant menu, grid paper (20 squares) for a farm, a family grocery list and budget.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking scarcity only means 'none': scarcity means not enough for everyone's wants.
- Thinking a choice costs nothing: every choice has a trade-off: what you give up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scarcity
*Student:* Share 5 crackers among 7 people and discuss what to do when there is not enough; define scarcity in your own words.

*How to teach:* Share 5 crackers among 7 people; define scarcity.

- **Q:** What is scarcity?
  **A:** Not having enough of something for everyone who wants it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Menu choices
*Student:* With $10 of play money, order from a pretend menu and write what you chose and what you gave up (the trade-off).

*How to teach:* Order from a menu with $10; write what was chosen and given up.

- **Q:** If you spend $7 on pizza, how much is left from $10?
  **A:** $3.
- **Q:** What is a trade-off?
  **A:** What you give up when you make a choice.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Farmer's choice
*Student:* Plan a 20-square grid-paper farm with limited land and water; decide how many squares of almonds, tomatoes, and lettuce to plant and explain why.

*How to teach:* Plan a 20-square farm; divide among crops; explain.

- **Q:** If you plant 8 squares of almonds and 7 of tomatoes, how many for lettuce?
  **A:** 5 squares.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family grocery list
*Student:* Help plan a real grocery list within a budget, crossing out wants that do not fit.

*How to teach:* Plan a real grocery list within a budget; cross out wants.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a need and a want?
  **A:** A need is necessary to live (food, water); a want is nice to have.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write about one choice you made this week, what you gave up, and why.

*How to teach:* Write about one choice and its trade-off.

- **Q:** Why do people have to make choices?
  **A:** Resources like money and time are limited.

**Assessment:** Trade-off explanations and grid-farm plan.

**Answer key:** Trade-off explanations name the choice, what was given up, and why (e.g., 'I bought a burger for $6 and lemonade for $4, so I gave up the $3 dessert because I was hungrier for the meal'). Grid farm: 20 squares divided among almonds, tomatoes, lettuce with a reason tied to limited land/water (almonds need water year-round). Proficient = clear trade-offs + reasoned plan.

## Art & Music 2: Dynamics and tempo: Carnival of the Animals

**Prepare:** Saint-Saëns 'Carnival of the Animals' recordings: 'The Elephant,' 'Wild Horses' (Hémiones), 'The Swan,' 'Aquarium'; homemade instruments; paper.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing dynamics with tempo: dynamics = loud/soft; tempo = fast/slow.
- Thinking 'piano' means the instrument: in music it also means soft.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dynamics
*Student:* Learn forte (loud) and piano (soft) and sing 'Bingo' changing dynamics on signal.

*How to teach:* Teach forte (loud) and piano (soft); sing 'Bingo' changing dynamics on signal.

- **Q:** What does forte mean?
  **A:** Loud.
- **Q:** What does piano mean in music?
  **A:** Soft.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tempo
*Student:* Learn fast (allegro) and slow (largo) and move like animals to 'The Elephant' and 'Wild Horses' from Carnival of the Animals.

*How to teach:* Teach allegro (fast) and largo (slow); move to 'The Elephant' and 'Wild Horses.'

- **Q:** Is 'The Elephant' allegro or largo-like?
  **A:** Slow (like largo).
- **Q:** What tempo is 'Wild Horses'?
  **A:** Very fast (allegro).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening map
*Student:* Listen to 'The Swan' and 'Aquarium' and describe dynamics and tempo for each.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'The Swan' and 'Aquarium'; describe dynamics and tempo.

- **Q:** Describe 'The Swan.'
  **A:** Slow and soft, a smooth cello melody.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Your animal
*Student:* Choose an animal and play it on a homemade instrument with dynamics and tempo that fit; family guesses the animal.

*How to teach:* Play an animal on a homemade instrument; family guesses.

- **Q:** How would you play a mouse?
  **A:** Soft and fast, high sounds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Identify dynamics and tempo in 4 excerpts and say which piece you prefer and why.

*How to teach:* Identify dynamics and tempo in 4 excerpts; state preference.

- **Q:** Who composed 'Carnival of the Animals'?
  **A:** Camille Saint-Saëns.

**Assessment:** Dynamics and tempo listening check with preference.

**Answer key:** Listening check (4 excerpts): The Elephant - slow, heavy, fairly loud low double bass; Wild Horses - very fast (allegro/presto), loud, racing pianos; The Swan - slow, soft (piano), smooth cello; Aquarium - medium-slow, soft, shimmering. Preference given with music words. Proficient = 6+ of 8 dynamic/tempo labels correct + reason.

## PE 2: Kicking to a target

**Prepare:** Ball, cones for a goal and distance markers at 5, 10, 20 feet.

**Watch for:**
- Toe-kicking: use the inside of the foot for accuracy.
- Kicking every ball hard: match force to distance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plant and kick
*Student:* Learn cues: plant foot beside ball, kick with inside of foot, follow through to target.

*How to teach:* Teach: plant beside, inside of foot, follow through.

- **Q:** Where does your plant foot point?
  **A:** At the target.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rolling ball kick
*Student:* Kick a ball rolled slowly toward you back to the roller.

*How to teach:* Kick a slowly rolled ball back to the roller.

- **Q:** When do you plant your foot for a rolling ball?
  **A:** As the ball arrives beside you.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Force control
*Student:* Kick to cones at 5, 10 and 20 feet, using light, medium and strong kicks.

*How to teach:* Kick to cones at 5, 10, 20 feet with light, medium, strong kicks.

- **Q:** Which kick for the 20-foot cone?
  **A:** Strong kick.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mini goal
*Student:* Shoot at a 2-cone goal from 3 spots; count goals out of 9.

*How to teach:* Shoot at a 2-cone goal from 3 spots, 3 times each.

- **Q:** How many shots total?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Pass back and forth with a partner 10 times and name the 3 kick cues.

*How to teach:* Pass with a partner 10 times; name cues.

- **Q:** What is force?
  **A:** How hard you push or kick.

**Assessment:** Kick cue checklist and goal count.

**Answer key:** Kick cue checklist: plant foot beside the ball; kick with inside of foot; follow through toward target. Goal count out of 9 recorded. Proficient = all 3 cues + 5 or more goals.

## Health 2: Growing and changing

**Prepare:** Baby photo, measuring tape, paper for a timeline.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking everyone should be the same size at the same age: kids grow at different rates.
- Thinking older adults don't have needs: every life stage has needs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Then and now
*Student:* Compare a baby photo to now; list what you can do now.

*How to teach:* Compare a baby photo to now; list new abilities.

- **Q:** What can you do now that you couldn't as a baby?
  **A:** Walk, talk, read, ride a bike.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure me
*Student:* Measure height, hand span and foot; compare with family members.

*How to teach:* Measure height, hand span, foot; compare with family.

- **Q:** Who is tallest in your family?
  **A:** Answer from measurements.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Life stages
*Student:* Make a life-stage timeline (infant to older adult) and note needs at each.

*How to teach:* Make a life-stage timeline with needs.

- **Q:** Put in order: teen, infant, adult, child.
  **A:** Infant, child, teen, adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Everyone is unique
*Student:* Discuss how kids grow at different rates and that differences are normal.

*How to teach:* Discuss how kids grow at different rates.

- **Q:** Is it normal to be shorter or taller than a friend?
  **A:** Yes, everyone grows at their own rate.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present your timeline and one way you have grown.

*How to teach:* Present the timeline and one way you've grown.

- **Q:** What does unique mean?
  **A:** One of a kind.

**Assessment:** Life-stage timeline.

**Answer key:** Timeline: infant, toddler, child, teen, adult, older adult in order, each with a need (infant - feeding, diapers, care; toddler - safety, play; child - learning, healthy food; teen - independence, guidance; adult - work, caring for family; older adult - health care, help, company). Presentation names one way they've grown. Proficient = 6 stages in order with needs.
