# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 8

## ELA 2: Anansi tales and writing an original fable

**Prepare:** Get Anansi and the Moss-Covered Rock (Eric A. Kimmel) and preview it. Make a fable planning sheet (characters, traits, problem, ending, moral) and a short reader's theater script of 'The Lion and the Mouse' (Narrator, Lion, Mouse, Hunters). For Friday, pick a short folktale the child hasn't read.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a fable with no lesson. Have the child decide the moral first, then plan a story that teaches it.
- Forgetting quotation marks around dialogue. Highlight the exact words spoken and put marks around them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Anansi the trickster
*Student:* Read Anansi and the Moss-Covered Rock (West African folktale) and explain how Little Bush Deer teaches Anansi a lesson. Note how the beginning introduces Anansi's trick and the ending wraps it up.

*How to teach:* Read Anansi and the Moss-Covered Rock (West African). Discuss Anansi's trick and how Little Bush Deer turns it around. Notice how the beginning introduces the trick and the ending wraps it up.

- **Q:** What is Anansi's trick?
  **A:** Anyone who says 'Isn't this a strange moss-covered rock!' falls down asleep, so Anansi brings animals to the rock and steals their food while they are knocked out.
- **Q:** How does Little Bush Deer teach Anansi a lesson?
  **A:** She tricks Anansi into saying the magic words himself, so he falls asleep, and the animals take back their food.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan a fable
*Student:* Writing: choose two animal characters with opposite traits and a lesson; plan beginning, problem, and ending on a fable organizer. Phonics review: vowel-team word hunt in the Anansi book.

*How to teach:* Pick two animals with opposite traits (e.g., a boastful rabbit and a patient snail) and a lesson. Fill in the fable organizer. Hunt for vowel-team words in the Anansi book.

- **Q:** What should you decide first when planning a fable?
  **A:** The lesson (moral) the story will teach.
- **Q:** Why give the characters opposite traits?
  **A:** So the difference causes the problem and shows the lesson.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft the fable
*Student:* Draft the original fable with dialogue and a moral as the last line. Read it to a listener and ask 'Was my lesson clear?'

*How to teach:* Draft the fable with dialogue and a moral as the last line. Read it to a listener and ask 'Was my lesson clear?'

- **Q:** Where does the moral go in a fable?
  **A:** At the end, usually as the last line.
- **Q:** Punctuate: Hurry up said the rabbit
  **A:** "Hurry up," said the rabbit.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit with a checklist (capitals, end marks, spelling of vowel-team words) and publish with illustrations. Rehearse a reader's theater script of 'The Lion and the Mouse.'

*How to teach:* Edit with a checklist and publish with illustrations. Rehearse 'The Lion and the Mouse' reader's theater with expression.

- **Q:** What does the mouse say when the lion catches him?
  **A:** He begs to be let go and promises to help the lion someday.
- **Q:** Name 2 things on the editing checklist.
  **A:** Capitals, end marks, spelling of vowel-team words.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Perform the reader's theater and read your fable aloud. Unit test: read a new folktale, write its moral, sequence 4 events, and spell 12 long-vowel words.

*How to teach:* Perform the reader's theater and read the fable aloud. Give the unit test on a new folktale: moral, sequence of 4 events, and 12 spelling words.

- **Q:** What is a moral?
  **A:** The lesson a story teaches.
- **Q:** Spell 'team' and 'grow.'
  **A:** t-e-a-m; g-r-o-w.

**Assessment:** Original fable (rubric: characters, problem, moral, conventions) and unit test on a cold-read folktale.

**Answer key:** Original fable rubric (3 = proficient): Characters — two animals with opposite traits; Problem — clear problem caused by a trait; Moral — stated as the last line and matches the story; Conventions — capitals, end marks, vowel-team spelling mostly correct. Unit test on a cold-read folktale: moral written in one sentence that fits the story; 4 events sequenced correctly; spelling (suggested 12): rain, play, tree, beach, boat, snow, toe, night, pie, fly, team, grow.

## Math 2: Comparing three-digit numbers

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks and digit cards 0–9 (two sets). For Wednesday, look up two or three landmark heights in feet or town populations under 1,000 (or use family heights in inches). Prepare the 20-item Unit 2 test.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing by the ones digit first (thinking 329 > 412 because 9 > 2). Always compare hundreds first, then tens, then ones.
- Mixing up > and <. Teach 'the open mouth faces the bigger number' and have the child read the sentence aloud ('452 is greater than 425').

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare with blocks
*Student:* Build two numbers (452 and 425) with blocks and compare hundreds first, then tens; record with > or <.

*How to teach:* Build 452 and 425 with blocks. Compare hundreds (both 4), then tens (5 tens > 2 tens), so 452 > 425. The child records with a symbol and reads it aloud.

- **Q:** Compare 452 and 425.
  **A:** 452 > 425; same hundreds, but 5 tens is more than 2 tens.
- **Q:** Compare 610 and 601.
  **A:** 610 > 601; same hundreds, and 1 ten is more than 0 tens.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Symbols
*Student:* Compare 15 pairs of numbers with >, =, < and explain one comparison in words ('362 > 326 because 6 tens is more than 2 tens').

*How to teach:* The child compares 15 pairs with >, =, < and writes one explanation sentence. Check that the sentence names the place that decides it.

- **Q:** Compare 362 and 326 and explain.
  **A:** 362 > 326 because 6 tens is more than 2 tens (the hundreds are equal).
- **Q:** Compare 198 and 201.
  **A:** 198 < 201 because 1 hundred is less than 2 hundreds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Order
*Student:* Order sets of 4 numbers from least to greatest; compare city populations or heights of landmarks in feet.

*How to teach:* Write 4 numbers on cards; the child orders them least to greatest by sorting on hundreds first, then tens. Then order the real numbers you looked up.

- **Q:** Order 532, 253, 325, 523 from least to greatest.
  **A:** 253, 325, 523, 532.
- **Q:** Which is tallest: a 305-foot tower, a 350-foot tower, or a 299-foot tower?
  **A:** The 350-foot tower.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Greatest number game
*Student:* Draw 3 digit cards, make the greatest and least possible numbers, and compare with a partner — 10 rounds.

*How to teach:* Each player draws 3 digit cards and makes the greatest and least numbers possible; the greater number wins the round. Play 10 rounds and record each comparison with a symbol.

- **Q:** Make the greatest and least numbers with 4, 7, 1.
  **A:** Greatest 741; least 147.
- **Q:** With 0, 5, 3, what is the least three-digit number?
  **A:** 305 (0 cannot go in the hundreds place).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Test on place value, counting patterns, number forms, and comparing three-digit numbers (20 items).

*How to teach:* Give the 20-item Unit 2 test independently. Read directions aloud if needed, but not the answers. Afterward, reteach any missed place with blocks.

- **Q:** What is 100 more than 285?
  **A:** 385.
- **Q:** Compare 845 and 799.
  **A:** 845 > 799.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test (suggested 20 items with answers): 1) 3 flats, 4 rods, 2 units = 342 2) value of 6 in 567 = 60 3) 100 more than 285 = 385 4) 10 less than 400 = 390 5) skip by 5s: 35, 40, 45, 50 6) skip by 100s: 120, 220, 320, 420 7) 700 + 50 + 9 = 759 8) 806 in words = eight hundred six 9) two hundred thirteen = 213 10) 914 expanded = 900 + 10 + 4 11) 452 > 425 12) 309 < 390 13) 671 = 671 14) 198 < 201 15) 845 > 799 16) order 532, 253, 325, 523 least to greatest → 253, 325, 523, 532 17) 18 tens = 180 18) count on: 398, 399, 400, 401 19) greatest number with 4, 7, 1 = 741 20) least = 147. Proficient: 17/20.

## Science 2: Pieces make a whole

**Prepare:** Gather 20 building blocks or LEGO bricks, craft sticks, paper and pencils, and photos (from books or online) of reused materials, like a table made from old fence boards or a building made from reused bricks.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the pieces become different materials when rebuilt. The same 20 pieces are still the same pieces — only the shape changes.
- Losing count of pieces. Count and record each type before and after.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build a house
*Student:* Build a small house from 20 blocks and draw it; count pieces of each type.

*How to teach:* Build a small house from 20 blocks and draw it. Count pieces of each type.

- **Q:** How many blocks did you use?
  **A:** 20.
- **Q:** Why count each type of piece?
  **A:** So you can check you use the same pieces later.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Take apart
*Student:* Take the house apart and build a new object (car or bridge) using the same 20 pieces; draw it.

*How to teach:* Take the house apart and build a new object using the same 20 pieces. Draw it.

- **Q:** Are these the same pieces?
  **A:** Yes — the same 20 blocks in a new shape.
- **Q:** Did the blocks change?
  **A:** No, only how they are arranged.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Same pieces, new shape
*Student:* Compare the two drawings and explain how the same pieces made different objects.

*How to teach:* Compare the drawings and explain how the same pieces made different objects.

- **Q:** How can the same pieces make a house and a car?
  **A:** By putting them together in a different arrangement.
- **Q:** Did you have any pieces left over?
  **A:** Answer should be no if all 20 were used.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Real world
*Student:* Look at pictures of a wooden fence turned into a table or bricks reused in a building; discuss.

*How to teach:* Look at pictures of reused materials (old fence wood turned into a table, reused bricks). Discuss.

- **Q:** How is a table made from old fence boards like your block build?
  **A:** The same pieces (boards) were taken apart and put together to make a new object.
- **Q:** Why do people reuse materials?
  **A:** It saves resources and reduces waste.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write an evidence-based account: 'I know an object made of pieces can become a new object because…'

*How to teach:* The child writes an evidence-based account with drawings.

- **Q:** Finish: 'I know an object made of pieces can become a new object because…'
  **A:** …I used the same 20 blocks to build a house and then a car.
- **Q:** What is your evidence?
  **A:** My two drawings and my piece count.

**Assessment:** Written evidence account with drawings.

**Answer key:** Written account (3 = proficient): 'I know an object made of pieces can become a new object because I built a house from 20 blocks and then used the same 20 blocks to build a car.' Includes drawings of both objects and the piece count matching (20 = 20).

## People Who Make a Difference: North America: countries and oceans

**Prepare:** Globe; blank North America outline map; colored pencils; atlas.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Gulf of Mexico is an ocean: it is a gulf (part of the ocean partly surrounded by land), connected to the Atlantic.
- Thinking Mexico is in South America: Mexico is in North America.

### Day 1 (Monday): Continents
*Student:* Use a globe to find the 7 continents and 5 oceans; locate North America and the United States.

*How to teach:* Use a globe to find the 7 continents and 5 oceans; locate North America and the U.S.

- **Q:** Name the 7 continents.
  **A:** North America, South America, Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia, Antarctica.
- **Q:** Name the 5 oceans.
  **A:** Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Countries
*Student:* Label Canada, the United States, and Mexico on a blank map and color each; locate California.

*How to teach:* Label Canada, the U.S. and Mexico; color each; locate California.

- **Q:** Which country is north of the U.S.?
  **A:** Canada.
- **Q:** Which country is south of the U.S.?
  **A:** Mexico.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Oceans
*Student:* Label the Pacific, Atlantic, and Arctic Oceans and the Gulf of Mexico.

*How to teach:* Label the Pacific, Atlantic, Arctic Oceans and the Gulf of Mexico.

- **Q:** Which ocean is west of California?
  **A:** The Pacific Ocean.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Map practice
*Student:* Label the map from memory, check against an atlas, and fix mistakes.

*How to teach:* Label from memory; check against an atlas; fix mistakes.

- **Q:** Which ocean is north of Canada?
  **A:** The Arctic Ocean.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Map quiz: label 3 countries and 3 oceans from memory.

*How to teach:* Give the map quiz.

- **Q:** What is a border?
  **A:** A line where one country or state ends and another begins.

**Assessment:** North America countries and oceans map quiz.

**Answer key:** Map quiz from memory: Canada (north), United States (middle), Mexico (south); Pacific Ocean (west), Atlantic Ocean (east), Arctic Ocean (north). Proficient = 6/6; developing = 4-5; beginning = 3 or fewer.

## Art & Music 2: Meter and rhythm composition

**Prepare:** Index cards, Chrome Music Lab Rhythm, homemade shaker or pot drum, pencil.

**Watch for:**
- Uneven measures: in 4/4, every measure must have exactly 4 beats.
- Thinking bar lines are rests: bar lines just divide the music into measures.

### Day 1 (Monday): Measures
*Student:* Learn bar lines and measures in 4/4 and draw 4 measures with rhythms.

*How to teach:* Teach bar lines and measures in 4/4 (4 beats per measure). Draw 4 measures.

- **Q:** How many beats in each measure of 4/4?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** What divides measures?
  **A:** Bar lines.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compose
*Student:* Compose a 4-measure rhythm piece using ta, ti-ti, and rest and write it on cards.

*How to teach:* Compose a 4-measure piece with ta, ti-ti, rest on cards.

- **Q:** Does 'ta ti-ti rest ta' fill one 4/4 measure?
  **A:** Yes: 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4 beats.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Digital rhythm
*Student:* Recreate your piece in Chrome Music Lab Rhythm and listen to it.

*How to teach:* Recreate the piece in Chrome Music Lab Rhythm.

- **Q:** Did it sound the way you planned?
  **A:** Child compares.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice your piece on a homemade shaker or pot drum, circling and slowly repeating the trickiest measure until it is steady.

*How to teach:* Practice on shaker; repeat the trickiest measure slowly.

- **Q:** Why practice a tricky measure slowly?
  **A:** To get it correct, then speed up.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Perform your composition and explain why you chose your rhythms.

*How to teach:* Perform; explain rhythm choices.

- **Q:** What is meter?
  **A:** How beats are grouped, like in 4s (4/4).

**Assessment:** Rhythm composition performance and notation.

**Answer key:** Notation: 4 measures in 4/4, each with exactly 4 beats of ta, ti-ti and rest, with bar lines and a double bar at the end. Performance steady; explanation gives a reason for rhythm choices. Proficient = all measures correct + steady + reason.

## PE 2: Locomotor mix and relay

**Prepare:** Cones, a die, beanbag as baton, cue cards.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up slide and gallop: slide goes sideways; gallop goes forward.
- Giving only praise or only correction: give one strength and one cue.

### Day 1 (Monday): Locomotor dice
*Student:* Roll a die; each number is a skill (1 hop, 2 gallop, 3 slide, 4 skip, 5 leap, 6 jump) to travel to the next cone.

*How to teach:* Roll a die; 1 hop, 2 gallop, 3 slide, 4 skip, 5 leap, 6 jump to the next cone.

- **Q:** You rolled a 3. What skill?
  **A:** Slide.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pattern card
*Student:* Write a 4-skill pattern (skip-skip-leap-jump) and perform it 5 times without stopping.

*How to teach:* Write a 4-skill pattern and perform it 5 times.

- **Q:** What is a combination?
  **A:** Two or more skills done in a row.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Relay
*Student:* Run a family relay where each leg uses a different locomotor skill; pass a beanbag baton.

*How to teach:* Family relay, each leg a different skill; pass the beanbag baton.

- **Q:** How do you pass the baton safely?
  **A:** Hand it to the next person, who takes it and then goes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coach me
*Student:* Watch a partner skip and leap and tell them one thing they did well and one cue to try.

*How to teach:* Watch a partner skip and leap; give one strength and one cue.

- **Q:** Give a helpful cue for a skip.
  **A:** 'Swing your opposite arm' or 'step-hop, switch feet.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate all six locomotor skills in a row and name one cue for each.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate all 6 skills and a cue for each.

- **Q:** Name a cue for the slide.
  **A:** Sideways, step-together, don't cross your feet.

**Assessment:** Locomotor skill checklist (6 skills) and peer-feedback observation.

**Answer key:** Locomotor checklist with one cue each: hop - one foot, soft landing; gallop - lead foot stays front; slide - sideways, feet don't cross; skip - step-hop alternating; leap - one foot to the other, reach long; jump - two-foot take-off, arm swing. Proficient = all 6 skills with a correct cue each + one helpful peer comment.

## Health 2: Sleep, rest and screen time

**Prepare:** Sleep log (4 nights: bedtime, wake time), paper and crayons.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kids need the same sleep as adults: children ages 6-12 need 9-12 hours.
- Thinking screens help you fall asleep: bright screens and exciting content make it harder.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why sleep
*Student:* Learn that kids need 9–12 hours of sleep and how sleep helps the brain and growth.

*How to teach:* Explain kids need 9-12 hours; sleep helps the brain learn, the body grow, and you feel good.

- **Q:** How many hours of sleep do kids your age need?
  **A:** 9 to 12 hours.
- **Q:** Name one way sleep helps.
  **A:** Helps your brain remember, your body grow and heal, and gives energy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sleep log
*Student:* Start a 4-night sleep log noting bedtime and wake time.

*How to teach:* Start the 4-night sleep log.

- **Q:** If you sleep from 8:00 p.m. to 6:30 a.m., how long is that?
  **A:** 10 and a half hours.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bedtime routine
*Student:* Draw a picture schedule for a calm bedtime routine (bath, teeth, book, lights out).

*How to teach:* Draw a calm bedtime routine: bath, teeth, book, lights out.

- **Q:** What is a routine?
  **A:** Steps you do in the same order every day.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Screen limits
*Student:* Discuss how screens before bed affect sleep; make a family 'screens off' time.

*How to teach:* Discuss screens before bed; set a family 'screens off' time (about 1 hour before bed).

- **Q:** Why turn off screens before bed?
  **A:** Bright light and exciting shows keep your brain awake.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Total your sleep hours from the log and set a bedtime goal.

*How to teach:* Total sleep hours; set a bedtime goal.

- **Q:** What is your bedtime goal?
  **A:** Specific time.

**Assessment:** Sleep log and bedtime goal.

**Answer key:** Sleep log: 4 nights with bedtime, wake time and hours (e.g., 8:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m. = 11 hours). Total and average correct. Bedtime goal is specific (e.g., 'lights out by 8:00'). Proficient = accurate totals + specific goal.
