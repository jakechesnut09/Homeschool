# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 5

## ELA 2: Aesop's fables: the lesson of the story

**Prepare:** Find Aesop's Fables (Project Gutenberg or library) and preview 'The Lion and the Mouse,' 'The Tortoise and the Hare,' and 'The Fox and the Grapes.' Make 4 sequence cards for 'The Lion and the Mouse' and CVC/CVCe word cards (cap/cape, kit/kite, hop/hope, cub/cube, tap/tape).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the moral is a plot summary ('The tortoise won'). A moral is the lesson for people: 'Slow and steady wins the race.'
- Forgetting the silent e changes the vowel. Show that magic e makes the vowel say its name: cap → cape.

### Day 1 (Monday): The Lion and the Mouse
*Student:* Listen to 'The Lion and the Mouse' from Aesop's Fables and retell it with 4 sequence cards. Discuss the moral: little friends can be great friends.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Lion and the Mouse' aloud. The child orders 4 sequence cards and retells. Discuss the moral.

- **Q:** Why does the lion let the mouse go?
  **A:** The mouse promises to help him someday, and the lion is amused and lets him go.
- **Q:** How does the mouse help the lion later?
  **A:** The lion is caught in a hunter's net, and the mouse gnaws the ropes to free him.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Magic e
*Student:* Phonics: change CVC to CVCe words (cap/cape, kit/kite, hop/hope, cub/cube) and read 20 long-vowel words. Write 3 sentences using CVCe words.

*How to teach:* Show cap → cape: adding e makes the a say its name. Read 20 CVCe words and write 3 sentences with them.

- **Q:** What word do you make when you add e to 'kit'?
  **A:** kite.
- **Q:** Read: hop, hope. What changed?
  **A:** Adding e makes the o long: hope.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tortoise and the Hare
*Student:* Read 'The Tortoise and the Hare' and compare how each character responds to the race. Write the moral in your own words: 'Slow and steady wins the race' means…

*How to teach:* Read 'The Tortoise and the Hare.' Compare how each acts in the race and write the moral in your own words.

- **Q:** Why does the hare lose the race?
  **A:** He is so sure he will win that he stops to nap, and the tortoise keeps going and passes him.
- **Q:** What does 'slow and steady wins the race' mean?
  **A:** If you keep working and don't give up, you can succeed, even if you aren't the fastest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Proper nouns
*Student:* Grammar: capitalize names of people, pets, and places; fix 8 sentences with missing capitals. Retell a fable to a family member using first, next, then, last.

*How to teach:* Teach: names of people, pets, and places start with capitals. Fix 8 sentences. The child retells a fable with first, next, then, last.

- **Q:** Fix: 'my dog rex lives in ohio.'
  **A:** My dog Rex lives in Ohio.
- **Q:** Which words need capitals: 'aesop told a fable about a lion'?
  **A:** Aesop (a person's name) and the first word, so: 'Aesop told a fable about a lion.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 5 review
*Student:* Spelling check of 10 CVCe words, match 4 fables to their morals, and read a new fable ('The Fox and the Grapes') independently and write its lesson in one sentence.

*How to teach:* Give the CVCe spelling check, match 4 fables to their morals, and have the child read 'The Fox and the Grapes' alone and write its lesson.

- **Q:** In 'The Fox and the Grapes,' what does the fox say when he can't reach the grapes?
  **A:** That the grapes are probably sour anyway.
- **Q:** What is the lesson?
  **A:** People sometimes say they don't want what they can't have.

**Assessment:** Fable-moral matching, written moral for a new fable, and CVCe spelling check.

**Answer key:** Fable-moral matching: 'The Lion and the Mouse' → little friends can be great friends (kindness is repaid); 'The Tortoise and the Hare' → slow and steady wins the race; 'The Fox and the Grapes' → it is easy to dislike what you cannot have ('sour grapes'); 'The Boy Who Cried Wolf' → no one believes a liar, even when he tells the truth. Written moral for 'The Fox and the Grapes': proficient states the lesson in a sentence (e.g., 'People sometimes pretend they didn't want something they couldn't get'). CVCe spelling (suggested): cape, kite, hope, cube, tape, bike, home, cake, rope, mule.

## Math 2: Hundreds, tens, and ones

**Prepare:** Gather base-ten blocks (or cut paper: 1×1 squares, 1×10 strips, 10×10 flats), about 100 straws and rubber bands, and draw a place-value mat (hundreds | tens | ones). Learn quick pictures: square = hundred, line = ten, dot = one.

**Watch for:**
- Reading 305 as 'thirty-five' or writing 'three hundred five' as 3005. Build it with blocks and point out the empty tens column — the 0 holds that place.
- Thinking the digit 4 in 346 is worth 4. Ask 'What does the 4 stand for?' and show 4 ten-rods = 40.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bundling
*Student:* Bundle straws into tens with rubber bands, then 10 tens into a hundred; explain why 100 = 10 tens.

*How to teach:* Count out 10 straws and bundle with a rubber band — 'one ten.' Make 10 bundles, then bundle those into one big bundle — 'one hundred.' The child counts the tens by 10s to 100 and says why 100 = 10 tens.

- **Q:** How many tens are in 100?
  **A:** 10 tens.
- **Q:** How many ones are in 3 tens?
  **A:** 30 ones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build numbers
*Student:* Build 10 three-digit numbers on a place-value mat with base-ten blocks (e.g., 346 = 3 hundreds, 4 tens, 6 ones) and draw quick pictures.

*How to teach:* Say a number (346); the child builds it with 3 flats, 4 rods, 6 units on the mat, says 'three hundreds, four tens, six ones,' and draws a quick picture. Repeat for 10 numbers.

- **Q:** What blocks show 346?
  **A:** 3 hundreds (flats), 4 tens (rods), 6 ones (units).
- **Q:** Draw 218 as a quick picture.
  **A:** 2 squares, 1 line, 8 dots.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Zeros matter
*Student:* Build and compare 305, 350, and 503; explain what the zero means in each.

*How to teach:* Build 305, 350, and 503 side by side. Ask what the zero means in each (no tens; no ones; no tens). Have the child read each aloud correctly and tell which is greatest.

- **Q:** What does the 0 in 305 mean?
  **A:** There are no tens — 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones.
- **Q:** Which is greatest: 305, 350, or 503?
  **A:** 503, because it has 5 hundreds.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hundreds
*Student:* Count 100, 200, …, 900 with flats; show that 4 hundreds = 40 tens = 400 ones.

*How to teach:* Count flats by 100s to 900. Then show 4 flats and ask how many rods would cover them (40) and how many units (400). The child completes: 4 hundreds = __ tens = __ ones.

- **Q:** 4 hundreds = how many tens = how many ones?
  **A:** 40 tens = 400 ones.
- **Q:** How many hundreds are in 700?
  **A:** 7 hundreds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: write the number shown by 8 block pictures and give the value of the underlined digit in 8 numbers.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz: the child writes the number for 8 block pictures and the value of an underlined digit in 8 numbers. Have them build any missed item with blocks to self-correct.

- **Q:** What is the value of the 7 in 175?
  **A:** 70 (7 tens).
- **Q:** What number is 6 flats, 2 rods, 9 units?
  **A:** 629.

**Assessment:** Place-value quiz on block pictures and digit values.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): block pictures — 1) 2 flats, 3 rods, 5 units = 235 2) 4 flats, 0 rods, 7 units = 407 3) 1 flat, 5 rods = 150 4) 6 flats, 2 rods, 9 units = 629 5) 3 flats, 8 units = 308 6) 9 flats, 9 rods, 9 units = 999 7) 5 flats, 1 rod, 1 unit = 511 8) 7 flats, 4 rods, 2 units = 742. Underlined digit values: 9) the 4 in 346 = 40 10) the 3 in 346 = 300 11) the 8 in 812 = 800 12) the 0 in 506 = 0 13) the 7 in 175 = 70 14) the 2 in 932 = 2 15) the 6 in 460 = 60 16) the 5 in 581 = 500. Proficient: 14/16.

## Science 2: Testing materials: strength and flexibility

**Prepare:** Cut same-size strips (about 3 cm × 20 cm) of paper, foil, fabric, and plastic bag; gather a paper cup, string or a hole punch, pennies, cardboard, craft sticks, rubber bands, and a ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking thicker always means stronger. Test it — a thin plastic bag strip can hold more than a thicker paper strip.
- Stretching rubber bands toward faces. Safety: point away and stretch gently.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strength test
*Student:* Hang a paper cup from strips of different materials and add pennies until each breaks; record the number.

*How to teach:* Hang a paper cup from each strip and add pennies until it breaks. Record the count.

- **Q:** Which strip held the most pennies?
  **A:** Answer from data — often fabric or plastic.
- **Q:** Why add pennies one at a time?
  **A:** To get an exact count of how much it held.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flexibility test
*Student:* Bend strips of cardboard, plastic, wood craft sticks, and foil and rank them from most to least flexible.

*How to teach:* Bend cardboard, plastic, craft sticks, and foil; rank from most to least flexible.

- **Q:** Which is most flexible: foil or a craft stick?
  **A:** Foil.
- **Q:** What does flexible mean?
  **A:** Able to bend without breaking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stretch test
*Student:* Measure how far rubber bands and fabric strips stretch with a ruler.

*How to teach:* Stretch rubber bands and fabric strips; measure how far each stretches with a ruler.

- **Q:** Which stretched farther: rubber band or fabric?
  **A:** The rubber band.
- **Q:** What happens when you let go of a rubber band?
  **A:** It snaps back to its shape — it is elastic.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Best for the job
*Student:* Use data to pick the best material for a bag handle, a bridge deck, and a hair tie; explain with numbers.

*How to teach:* Use data to choose the best material for a bag handle, a bridge deck, and a hair tie; explain with numbers.

- **Q:** Why choose a rubber band for a hair tie?
  **A:** It stretches and goes back to its shape.
- **Q:** Why choose the strongest strip for a bag handle?
  **A:** It held the most pennies, so it can carry heavy things.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Present test results in a chart and answer 'Why do engineers test materials?'

*How to teach:* Present results in a chart and answer 'Why do engineers test materials?'

- **Q:** Why do engineers test materials?
  **A:** To find the best material for a job so things work and are safe.
- **Q:** What would happen if a bridge used a weak material?
  **A:** It could bend or break.

**Assessment:** Strength data chart and material-choice explanations.

**Answer key:** Strength chart: number of pennies each strip held before breaking (fabric and plastic bag usually hold the most; paper and foil fewer). Material choices: bag handle — strongest strip from data (often fabric or plastic) because it held the most pennies; bridge deck — a stiff, strong material such as craft sticks or cardboard; hair tie — rubber band because it stretches and snaps back. Explanations must use numbers from the data.

## People Who Make a Difference: Family history book and unit check

**Prepare:** Family history book, LOC photo of a one-room schoolhouse; cover materials; quiz cards (sources, timeline events).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all children long ago went to big schools: many rural children attended one-room schools.
- Reading the book word for word when presenting: tell the story and point to pages.

### Day 1 (Monday): School long ago
*Student:* Compare a Library of Congress photo of a one-room schoolhouse with your learning space today; list 4 differences.

*How to teach:* Compare a one-room schoolhouse photo with your learning space; list 4 differences.

- **Q:** What was a one-room schoolhouse?
  **A:** A school where children of all ages learned together in one room with one teacher.
- **Q:** Name a difference from today.
  **A:** Slates instead of paper/tablets, wood stove for heat, all ages together, walked far to school.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finish the book
*Student:* Add a cover, a page 'How my life is different from my grandparent's,' and a page with a favorite family tradition.

*How to teach:* Add a cover, a 'my life vs. my grandparent's' page and a family tradition page.

- **Q:** What is a tradition?
  **A:** Something a family or group does again and again, often passed down.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice presenting
*Student:* Practice telling your family story in 2 minutes using the book.

*How to teach:* Practice telling your family story in 2 minutes.

- **Q:** What will you tell first?
  **A:** Begins with family tree or earliest event.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the family history book to relatives (in person or by video).

*How to teach:* Present the family history book to relatives.

- **Q:** What is the most interesting thing in your book?
  **A:** Child explains.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Quiz: sort sources as primary/secondary, order events on a timeline, and name 2 ways life has changed.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz.

- **Q:** Is a photo taken in 1910 primary or secondary?
  **A:** Primary.

**Assessment:** Family history book presentation and unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 quiz: Sources - primary: diary, photo, letter, birth certificate; secondary: textbook, encyclopedia. Timeline: orders 4 events by year correctly. Two ways life changed (e.g., phones/technology, how people travel, school, how chores are done). Presentation: 2 minutes, in order, uses book pages. Proficient = 80% quiz + clear presentation.

## Art & Music 2: Steady beat

**Prepare:** Recordings: Sousa's 'The Stars and Stripes Forever' (march), Strauss's 'The Blue Danube' (waltz); wooden spoons or rhythm sticks; draw a chart of 8 hearts.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing beat and rhythm: beat is the steady pulse; rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds (often the words).
- Speeding up: a steady beat stays the same speed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heartbeat
*Student:* Feel your heartbeat and compare it to a steady beat; pat the beat to Sousa's 'The Stars and Stripes Forever.'

*How to teach:* Feel your heartbeat; pat a steady beat to 'The Stars and Stripes Forever.'

- **Q:** How is a beat like a heartbeat?
  **A:** Both are steady and keep going evenly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Beat chart
*Student:* Point to 8 hearts on a beat chart while chanting 'Engine, Engine Number Nine' and other rhymes.

*How to teach:* Point to 8 hearts on a beat chart while chanting 'Engine, Engine Number Nine.'

- **Q:** How many beats did you point to?
  **A:** 8 (one per heart).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Beat vs. rhythm
*Student:* Pat the beat while clapping the words of 'Twinkle, Twinkle' and explain the difference between beat and rhythm.

*How to teach:* Pat the beat while clapping the words of 'Twinkle, Twinkle.'

- **Q:** What is rhythm?
  **A:** The pattern of long and short sounds, like the words of a song.
- **Q:** Which stays even: beat or rhythm?
  **A:** The beat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Marches and waltzes
*Student:* March to music in 2 and sway to a waltz in 3 (Strauss's 'Blue Danube'); count 1-2 and 1-2-3.

*How to teach:* March to music in 2 (count 1-2); sway to 'The Blue Danube' in 3 (count 1-2-3).

- **Q:** Does a waltz feel in 2 or 3?
  **A:** In 3 (1-2-3).
- **Q:** Does a march feel in 2 or 3?
  **A:** In 2 (1-2, left-right).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Keep a steady beat on spoons with 3 songs while a parent checks; explain beat in one sentence.

*How to teach:* Keep a steady beat on spoons with 3 songs; explain beat in one sentence.

- **Q:** What is tempo?
  **A:** How fast or slow the beat is.

**Assessment:** Steady-beat performance check.

**Answer key:** Steady-beat check: keeps an even beat on spoons through 3 songs without rushing or dragging. One-sentence explanation: 'The beat is the steady pulse that keeps going through the music, like a heartbeat.' Proficient = 3 songs steady + correct sentence.

## PE 2: Skipping

**Prepare:** Chalk start/finish lines 20 meters apart (about 65 feet), music with a steady beat; a window or mirror.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping with the same arm and leg forward: swing the opposite arm.
- Galloping instead of skipping: a skip switches the lead foot every step-hop.

### Day 1 (Monday): Step-hop
*Student:* Break the skip into 'step-hop' slowly on one side, then the other, saying the cue out loud.

*How to teach:* Slowly say 'step-hop' on the right, then 'step-hop' on the left.

- **Q:** What two parts make a skip?
  **A:** A step and a hop.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add arms
*Student:* Skip across the yard swinging opposite arm to leg; practice in front of a window or mirror.

*How to teach:* Skip across the yard with opposite arm swinging; watch in a window.

- **Q:** Which arm swings when your right leg comes up?
  **A:** Left arm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Skip to music
*Student:* Skip to a steady song, changing direction each time the chorus starts.

*How to teach:* Skip to music; change direction when the chorus starts.

- **Q:** When did you change direction?
  **A:** When the chorus started.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Skip for height & distance
*Student:* Skip high (big lift) and then skip far (long steps); count how many skips it takes to cross the yard.

*How to teach:* Skip high (big lift), then far (long steps); count skips to cross the yard.

- **Q:** Did high skips or far skips take fewer skips to cross?
  **A:** Far (long) skips.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show a smooth skip for 20 meters and tell the 3 cues: step-hop, swing arms, land on toes.

*How to teach:* Skip 20 meters and say the 3 cues.

- **Q:** Say the skip cues.
  **A:** Step-hop, swing arms, land on toes.

**Assessment:** Skip checklist: step-hop pattern, opposition, rhythm.

**Answer key:** Skip checklist: step-hop pattern alternating feet; opposite arm swings with leg; smooth rhythm; lands on balls of feet. Proficient = all 4 for 20 meters; developing = 2-3; beginning = 0-1.

## Health 2: Families are different and special

**Prepare:** Family photos; 'The Family Book' by Todd Parr (library); paper for a chore chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking there is one 'right' kind of family: families come in many shapes and sizes.
- Thinking chores are only adults' jobs: everyone can help the family.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read aloud
*Student:* Read 'The Family Book' by Todd Parr and list ways families are alike and different.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Family Book'; list ways families are alike and different.

- **Q:** How are families different?
  **A:** Some are big or small, have one parent or two, two moms or two dads, are adopted, live with grandparents, etc.
- **Q:** How are all families alike in the book?
  **A:** They love each other, help each other and like to be together.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family portrait
*Student:* Draw your family and label how each person helps the family.

*How to teach:* Draw your family and label how each person helps.

- **Q:** How does one person in your family help?
  **A:** Specific job.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family jobs
*Student:* Make a chore chart and choose two jobs you will do this week.

*How to teach:* Make a chore chart; choose two jobs.

- **Q:** What does responsibility mean?
  **A:** Doing the jobs you are supposed to do.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family meeting
*Student:* Hold a short family meeting to share feelings and plan a fun activity together.

*How to teach:* Hold a short family meeting to share feelings and plan a fun activity.

- **Q:** Why have family meetings?
  **A:** To share feelings, solve problems, and plan together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Tell 3 ways your family supports you and 2 ways you support your family.

*How to teach:* Tell 3 ways your family supports you and 2 ways you support them.

- **Q:** What does 'support' mean?
  **A:** Helping and caring for someone.

**Assessment:** Family portrait and oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Family portrait: each person drawn and labeled with a way they help. Oral: 3 ways family supports the child (e.g., cooks meals, helps with homework, hugs when sad) and 2 ways the child helps (sets the table, feeds the pet). Proficient = all labeled + 5 specific examples.
