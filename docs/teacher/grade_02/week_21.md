# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 21

## ELA 2: Cinderella from France and China

**Prepare:** Get Perrault's Cinderella (Andrew Lang's The Blue Fairy Book, public domain) and Yeh-Shen (Ai-Ling Louie); read both first. Draw a large Venn diagram and make prefix cards (un-, re-, dis-).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every Cinderella story has a fairy godmother. Point out different magic helpers: a fairy godmother in Perrault, magic fish bones in Yeh-Shen.
- Thinking a prefix can go on any word. Check that the new word makes sense (unhappy, but not 'unrun').

### Day 1 (Monday): Perrault's Cinderella
*Student:* Read Perrault's Cinderella and list the story parts: kind girl, mean family, magic helper, event, lost item, happy ending. Phonics: prefix un- (unhappy, unlock).

*How to teach:* Read Perrault's Cinderella and list the story parts: kind girl, mean family, magic helper, event, lost item, happy ending. Teach un- (not).

- **Q:** Who helps Cinderella go to the ball?
  **A:** Her fairy godmother, who turns a pumpkin into a coach and mice into horses.
- **Q:** What must Cinderella remember at the ball?
  **A:** To leave before midnight, when the magic ends.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yeh-Shen
*Student:* Read Yeh-Shen, a Cinderella story from China, and use the illustrations to describe the setting. Phonics: prefix re- (reread, retell, rebuild).

*How to teach:* Read Yeh-Shen and use illustrations to describe the setting (long ago in China). Teach re- (again).

- **Q:** What is Yeh-Shen's magic helper?
  **A:** The bones of her fish friend, which grant wishes.
- **Q:** What does re- mean in 'retell'?
  **A:** Again — retell means tell again.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Fill a Venn diagram comparing Cinderella and Yeh-Shen (magic fish vs. fairy godmother, golden slipper vs. glass slipper). Phonics: prefix dis- (dislike, disagree).

*How to teach:* Fill the Venn diagram comparing Cinderella and Yeh-Shen. Teach dis- (not/opposite).

- **Q:** Name one way the two stories are alike.
  **A:** Both girls are mistreated by a stepmother, lose a slipper at a big event, and are found by it.
- **Q:** Name one difference.
  **A:** Cinderella's helper is a fairy godmother; Yeh-Shen's is magic fish bones (or glass slipper vs. golden slippers).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prefix meaning
*Student:* Use prefixes to define 12 new words and use 4 in sentences. Discuss in a partner talk which magic helper is more interesting and why.

*How to teach:* Use prefixes to define 12 words and use 4 in sentences. Partner talk: which magic helper is more interesting and why?

- **Q:** What does 'disagree' mean?
  **A:** To not agree.
- **Q:** What does 'unlock' mean?
  **A:** To open a lock (the opposite of lock).

### Day 5 (Friday): Week 21 review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences: one way the stories are alike and two ways they are different. Spelling check of 12 prefix words.

*How to teach:* The child writes 1 way the stories are alike and 2 ways they differ, then takes the prefix spelling check.

- **Q:** Write a contrast sentence.
  **A:** Example: Cinderella wore glass slippers, but Yeh-Shen wore golden slippers.
- **Q:** Spell 'unhappy' and 'reread.'
  **A:** u-n-h-a-p-p-y; r-e-r-e-a-d.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram and written compare/contrast sentences; prefix spelling check.

**Answer key:** Venn diagram (model): Cinderella only — France, fairy godmother, pumpkin coach, glass slipper, must leave by midnight, a ball at the palace. Yeh-Shen only — China, magic fish bones, golden slippers, a festival, a king finds her. Both — kind girl treated badly by a stepmother/stepfamily, a magic helper, a big event she goes to in secret, she loses a slipper, the slipper helps find her, happy ending. Written sentences: 1 alike, 2 different. Spelling (suggested 12): unhappy, unlock, undo, unkind, reread, retell, rebuild, redo, dislike, disagree, disappear, unfair.

## Math 2: Mental math with 10 and 100

**Prepare:** Number cards, open number line paper, hundreds flats, and a timer. Write 30 quick '+/− 10 or 100' oral items and a Friday quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Changing the ones digit when adding 10. Ask which digit is in the tens place and change only that digit (unless it crosses a hundred).
- Crossing hundreds incorrectly (395 + 10 = 3105 or 305). Use blocks: 9 tens + 1 ten = 10 tens = 1 hundred, so 405.

### Day 1 (Monday): 10 and 100 patterns
*Student:* Use a 'number path' to show 10 more/less and 100 more/less for 10 numbers and describe which digit changes.

*How to teach:* Draw a number path for 10 numbers: write 10 less/10 more and 100 less/100 more around each. Ask which digit changes each time.

- **Q:** What is 10 more and 100 more than 572?
  **A:** 582 and 672.
- **Q:** Which digit changes when you subtract 100 from 845?
  **A:** The hundreds digit (8 → 7): 745.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Number line jumps
*Student:* Add 345 + 230 by jumping 200, then 30 on an open number line; solve 6 more.

*How to teach:* Solve 345 + 230 on an open number line: 345 + 200 = 545, + 30 = 575. The child solves 6 more, labeling the jumps.

- **Q:** 345 + 230 = ?
  **A:** 575.
- **Q:** 520 + 140 = ?
  **A:** 660.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mental math race
*Student:* Answer 30 quick '+10, −10, +100, −100' problems orally with a timer; record time.

*How to teach:* Call out 30 quick '+10, −10, +100, −100' items while timing. Record the time and number correct; aim to beat it next time.

- **Q:** 690 + 10 = ?
  **A:** 700.
- **Q:** 207 − 10 = ?
  **A:** 197.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Missing number puzzles
*Student:* Solve chains like 472 → □ (+100) → □ (−10) → □ and create 2 puzzles.

*How to teach:* Solve chains: 472 → +100 → 572 → −10 → 562. The child creates 2 chain puzzles for a family member.

- **Q:** 472 → (+100) → □ → (−10) → □
  **A:** 572, then 562.
- **Q:** 300 → (−10) → □ → (−100) → □
  **A:** 290, then 190.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: 15 mental math items and 4 number-line problems.

*How to teach:* Give the 15 mental items and 4 number-line problems.

- **Q:** 899 + 100 = ?
  **A:** 999.
- **Q:** 680 − 250 on a number line?
  **A:** 430 (680 − 200 = 480, − 50 = 430).

**Assessment:** Mental math quiz.

**Answer key:** Mental math quiz (suggested 15): 1) 345 + 10 = 355 2) 345 + 100 = 445 3) 600 − 10 = 590 4) 218 − 100 = 118 5) 795 + 10 = 805 6) 903 − 100 = 803 7) 460 − 10 = 450 8) 899 + 100 = 999 9) 512 + 10 = 522 10) 130 − 100 = 30 11) 207 − 10 = 197 12) 690 + 10 = 700 13) 444 + 100 = 544 14) 381 − 10 = 371 15) 710 − 100 = 610. Number-line items: a) 345 + 230 = 575 b) 520 + 140 = 660 c) 680 − 250 = 430 d) 415 + 305 = 720. Proficient: 17/19.

## Science 2: Comparing land habitats: forest, desert, grassland

**Prepare:** Get library books or National Geographic Kids articles on redwood forests, the Mojave Desert (e.g., A Walk in the Desert by Rebecca L. Johnson), and grasslands, plus a short grassland video. Make a three-column chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking deserts have no life. Many plants and animals have adaptations to survive with little water.
- Thinking a habitat with bigger animals has more diversity. Diversity is the number of kinds, not size.

### Day 1 (Monday): Forest
*Student:* Read a library book or National Geographic Kids article about a redwood forest and list 8 plants and animals that live there, noting where each finds shelter.

*How to teach:* Read about a redwood forest; list 8 plants and animals and where each finds shelter.

- **Q:** Name 2 animals that live in a redwood forest.
  **A:** Examples: banana slug, owl, deer, woodpecker.
- **Q:** Where might an owl find shelter?
  **A:** In a hole in a tree.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Desert
*Student:* Read about California's Mojave Desert and list the living things (Joshua tree, tortoise, roadrunner, cactus); discuss how each survives with little water.

*How to teach:* Read about the Mojave Desert and list living things; discuss how each survives with little water.

- **Q:** How does a cactus survive with little water?
  **A:** It stores water in its thick stem and has spines instead of leaves.
- **Q:** How does a desert tortoise survive the heat?
  **A:** It stays in a burrow underground during the hottest times.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grassland
*Student:* Watch a short video about California grasslands or the prairie and list the plants and animals; tally how many kinds each source names.

*How to teach:* Watch a grassland video and list plants and animals; tally kinds named.

- **Q:** What plants are most common in a grassland?
  **A:** Grasses (and wildflowers).
- **Q:** Name a grassland animal.
  **A:** Examples: ground squirrel, hawk, coyote, bison (prairie).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare chart
*Student:* Make a three-column chart and a bar graph of how many kinds of living things each habitat has; describe the pattern you see.

*How to teach:* Make a three-column chart and a bar graph of the number of kinds; describe the pattern.

- **Q:** Which habitat had the most kinds on your chart?
  **A:** Usually the forest.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** More water and plants give more food and shelter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Answer in 3–4 sentences: 'Which habitat has the most diversity, and why?' using evidence from the chart (more water and plants support more animals).

*How to teach:* The child answers in 3–4 sentences which habitat has the most diversity and why.

- **Q:** What helps a habitat have more diversity?
  **A:** More water, plants, and shelter.
- **Q:** Why does the desert have fewer kinds?
  **A:** There is little water.

**Assessment:** Habitat comparison chart and written answer with evidence.

**Answer key:** Comparison chart: forest (e.g., redwood, fern, banana slug, owl, deer, squirrel, salamander, woodpecker), desert (Joshua tree, cactus, desert tortoise, roadrunner, lizard, kangaroo rat, jackrabbit), grassland (grasses, wildflowers, ground squirrel, hawk, coyote, gopher snake). Written answer (3 = proficient): names the habitat with the most kinds on the chart (usually the forest) and explains with evidence: more water and plants provide more food and shelter, so more animals can live there.

## People Who Make a Difference: Food long ago

**Prepare:** Calisphere photos of early-1900s California farms and modern farm photos; apples and an oven or dehydrator (adult help); Venn diagram paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking farms long ago had tractors like today: many used horses, mules and hand tools.
- Thinking preserving means freezing: long ago people canned, dried, salted and smoked food.

### Day 1 (Monday): Farms long ago
*Student:* Look at Calisphere photos of early California farms and list tools and animals used.

*How to teach:* Look at early California farm photos; list tools and animals.

- **Q:** What pulled plows long ago?
  **A:** Horses or mules.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Farms today
*Student:* Compare with photos of modern farms (tractors, irrigation).

*How to teach:* Compare with modern farm photos.

- **Q:** What machine replaced horses on farms?
  **A:** Tractors.
- **Q:** What is irrigation?
  **A:** Bringing water to crops through canals, pipes or sprinklers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Food memories
*Student:* Ask a grandparent how food was bought and cooked when they were young.

*How to teach:* Ask a grandparent how food was bought and cooked when young.

- **Q:** What did you learn?
  **A:** Fact from interview.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Preserving food
*Student:* Learn how food was preserved long ago (canning, drying) and make dried apple slices with an adult.

*How to teach:* Learn canning and drying; make dried apple slices with an adult.

- **Q:** Why did people preserve food long ago?
  **A:** To keep food from spoiling so they had food in winter.
- **Q:** What does drying do?
  **A:** Removes water so germs can't grow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Venn diagram of food production long ago and today.

*How to teach:* Venn diagram of food production long ago and today.

- **Q:** How is farming the same today?
  **A:** Farmers still need soil, water and sun to grow crops.

**Assessment:** Long ago/today Venn diagram.

**Answer key:** Venn diagram: Long ago - horses/mules, hand tools, more people working each farm, canning/drying at home, food grown closer to home; Today - tractors and machines, sprinkler/drip irrigation, refrigerated trucks, supermarkets; Both - farmers grow food, need water, sun and soil, harvest crops. Proficient = 2+ accurate items in each section.

## Art & Music 2: Instrument families

**Prepare:** Instrument picture cards; orchestra demo videos (BBC Ten Pieces or PBS); rubber bands and a box, straws, paper towel tube; 10 instrument sound clips.

**Watch for:**
- Calling the saxophone or flute 'brass' because they're metal: they're woodwinds.
- Thinking the piano is only a string instrument: hammers strike strings, so it's often grouped with percussion/keyboards.

### Day 1 (Monday): Four families
*Student:* Watch an orchestra instrument demonstration and sort picture cards into strings, woodwinds, brass, and percussion.

*How to teach:* Watch an orchestra demo; sort cards into strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion.

- **Q:** Name the four families.
  **A:** Strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): How sound is made
*Student:* Make a rubber-band guitar (string), a straw oboe (woodwind), and a tube trumpet (brass buzz) and compare how each makes sound.

*How to teach:* Make a rubber-band guitar, a straw oboe (flatten and snip a straw tip) and a tube trumpet (buzz lips into a tube). Compare.

- **Q:** How do brass players make sound?
  **A:** Buzzing their lips into a mouthpiece.
- **Q:** How do strings make sound?
  **A:** Vibrating strings (plucked or bowed).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timbre game
*Student:* Listen to 10 instrument sound clips and name the family of each.

*How to teach:* Listen to 10 clips; name each family.

- **Q:** What is timbre?
  **A:** The unique sound quality of an instrument or voice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Favorite instrument
*Student:* Research one instrument and draw it, labeling its parts and family.

*How to teach:* Research one instrument; draw and label it.

- **Q:** What family is the clarinet?
  **A:** Woodwinds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Identify instrument families from sound and picture (10 items).

*How to teach:* Identify families from sound and pictures (10 items).

- **Q:** What family is the timpani?
  **A:** Percussion.

**Assessment:** Instrument family identification check.

**Answer key:** 10-item check (example key): violin - strings; cello - strings; harp - strings; flute - woodwinds; clarinet - woodwinds; oboe - woodwinds; trumpet - brass; tuba - brass; French horn - brass; timpani/snare drum - percussion. Proficient = 8+/10.

## PE 2: Hand dribbling

**Prepare:** Playground ball or basketball, chalk paths, cones.

**Watch for:**
- Slapping with the palm: push with finger pads.
- Watching the ball: eyes up to see where you're going.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fingertip pads
*Student:* Dribble in place with the dominant hand using fingertips, ball at waist height, 20 times.

*How to teach:* Dribble in place 20 times, fingertips, waist height.

- **Q:** What part of the hand pushes the ball?
  **A:** Fingertips (finger pads).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Switch hands
*Student:* Dribble with the weaker hand, then alternate hands every 5 bounces.

*How to teach:* Dribble with the weaker hand; alternate every 5 bounces.

- **Q:** Why practice your weaker hand?
  **A:** To dribble both ways and keep the ball away from defenders.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Walking dribble
*Student:* Dribble while walking a straight line, then a curved chalk path.

*How to teach:* Walk a straight and a curved chalk path while dribbling.

- **Q:** Where should the ball be while walking?
  **A:** Slightly in front and to the side.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Eyes up
*Student:* Dribble while calling out the number of fingers a parent holds up.

*How to teach:* Dribble while calling out fingers a parent holds up.

- **Q:** Why keep eyes up?
  **A:** To see where you're going and other players.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Dribble while walking around 4 cones and back, switching hands once.

*How to teach:* Dribble around 4 cones and back, switching hands once.

- **Q:** How high should the dribble be?
  **A:** Waist high.

**Assessment:** Dribble checklist: fingertips, waist height, eyes up.

**Answer key:** Dribble checklist: fingertips push; ball at waist height; eyes up; can switch hands; walks around 4 cones and back. Proficient = all.

## Health 2: Digestion

**Prepare:** Zip-top bag, crackers, water (a little orange juice optional), a clean stocking or knee-high, scissors, tray.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking food goes straight from mouth to stomach without a tube: the esophagus carries it.
- Thinking the stomach does all digestion: most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food journey
*Student:* Learn the path: mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines.

*How to teach:* Teach the path: mouth, esophagus, stomach, small and large intestines.

- **Q:** What carries food from the mouth to the stomach?
  **A:** The esophagus.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stomach model
*Student:* Mash crackers and water in a zip bag to model the stomach.

*How to teach:* Mash crackers and water in a zip bag like the stomach.

- **Q:** What does the stomach do?
  **A:** Squeezes and mixes food with digestive juices into mush.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Intestine model
*Student:* Squeeze mush through a stocking to model the intestines.

*How to teach:* Squeeze the mush through a stocking like the intestines.

- **Q:** What does the small intestine do?
  **A:** Absorbs nutrients into the blood.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fiber & water
*Student:* Discuss why fiber and water help digestion.

*How to teach:* Discuss fiber and water helping digestion.

- **Q:** Why are fiber and water important?
  **A:** They help food move smoothly through your intestines.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw and label the digestive system.

*How to teach:* Draw and label the digestive system.

- **Q:** What is digestion?
  **A:** Breaking food down so the body can use its nutrients.

**Assessment:** Labeled diagram.

**Answer key:** Labeled diagram: mouth (teeth chew, saliva) → esophagus (tube to stomach) → stomach (mashes and mixes with juices) → small intestine (absorbs nutrients into blood) → large intestine (absorbs water, forms waste). Proficient = 5 parts in order with jobs.
