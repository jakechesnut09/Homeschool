# Teacher's Guide — 2nd Grade, Week 16

## ELA 2: Ira Sleeps Over; revising and publishing fiction

**Prepare:** Get Ira Sleeps Over (Bernard Waber). Make a revision checklist (capitals, end marks, quotation marks, past-tense verbs, unit spelling patterns), construction paper and a stapler. Choose an on-level passage (about 100 words) for the fluency check and a short new story with questions for the unit test.

**Watch for:**
- Giving vague feedback ('It's good'). Teach 'star and wish': one specific good thing and one specific thing to improve.
- Placing quotation marks around the speaker tag ('"said Mom"'). Only the spoken words go inside.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ira's choice
*Student:* Read Ira Sleeps Over and discuss how Ira responds to his sister's teasing and how he decides about his teddy bear. Practice irregular words (could, would, should, through, though).

*How to teach:* Read Ira Sleeps Over. Discuss how Ira responds to his sister's teasing and how he decides about his teddy bear. Practice irregular words (could, would, should, through, though).

- **Q:** Why does Ira decide not to take his teddy bear at first?
  **A:** His sister says Reggie will laugh at him and think he's a baby.
- **Q:** What changes Ira's mind?
  **A:** Reggie gets out his own teddy bear (Foo Foo) at bedtime, so Ira goes home to get his bear, Tah Tah.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Feedback
*Student:* Read your story draft to a family member who gives one 'star' (something good) and one 'wish' (something to improve). Revise based on the wish.

*How to teach:* The child reads the story draft to a family member who gives one star and one wish. Revise based on the wish.

- **Q:** Give an example of a helpful 'wish.'
  **A:** Example: 'I wish you told how the bear felt when it was lost.'
- **Q:** What do you do with a wish?
  **A:** Use it to revise — add or change that part.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Edit with a checklist: capitals, end marks, quotation marks for dialogue, and spelling of this unit's patterns. Fix past-tense verbs.

*How to teach:* Edit with the checklist: capitals, end marks, quotation marks, unit spelling patterns, past-tense verbs.

- **Q:** Punctuate: Where is my bear asked Ira
  **A:** "Where is my bear?" asked Ira.
- **Q:** Fix: 'He goed home.'
  **A:** He went home.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish the book
*Student:* Publish the story as a stapled book with a cover, title page, and illustrations. Practice reading it aloud smoothly three times.

*How to teach:* Publish the story as a stapled book with cover, title page, and illustrations. Practice reading it aloud 3 times.

- **Q:** What goes on a title page?
  **A:** The title and the author's (and illustrator's) name.
- **Q:** Why practice reading aloud 3 times?
  **A:** To read smoothly with expression.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Fluency check: one-minute read of an on-level passage (goal about 70–90 words correct) with expression. Unit test on a new story: character response, beginning/ending, and adjective/adverb use.

*How to teach:* Give the one-minute fluency check and the unit test on a new story.

- **Q:** How do you count words correct per minute?
  **A:** Count the words read in one minute and subtract the errors.
- **Q:** How did Ira feel at the end of Ira Sleeps Over?
  **A:** Happy and comfortable — he and Reggie both had their bears and fell asleep.

**Assessment:** Published fiction book (narrative rubric), fluency WCPM record, and unit test.

**Answer key:** Fluency: count words correct per minute on the passage (goal about 70–90 WCPM mid-grade 2); note expression (1–3). Narrative rubric (3 = proficient): beginning introduces character/setting; problem and events in order; solution and ending; descriptive words and dialogue; mostly correct conventions. Unit test: character response explained with text evidence; beginning/ending described; adjectives and adverbs correctly identified.

## Math 2: Fluency within 100 and unit review

**Prepare:** Make a strategy anchor chart (blocks, number line, hundreds chart, compensation, mental) and gather digit cards 0–9. Prepare the Unit 4 test and 5 worked problems containing mistakes for Thursday.

**Watch for:**
- In compensation, adjusting the wrong way (72 − 29 → 72 − 30 = 42, then subtract 1 = 41). Explain: you took away 1 too many, so give 1 back: 43.
- Regrouping in subtraction when it isn't needed (or never). Check the ones first: 'Do I have enough ones to take away?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Compensation
*Student:* Solve 39 + 26 as 40 + 25 and 72 − 29 as 72 − 30 + 1; explain why changing numbers works.

*How to teach:* Show 39 + 26 as 40 + 25 (move 1 from 26 to 39) = 65. Show 72 − 29 as 72 − 30 = 42, then add back 1 = 43, because you subtracted 1 too many.

- **Q:** Solve 39 + 26 with compensation.
  **A:** 65 — 40 + 25 = 65.
- **Q:** Solve 72 − 29 with compensation.
  **A:** 43 — 72 − 30 = 42, add back 1 = 43.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strategy choice
*Student:* Sort 12 problems by best strategy (mental, number line, blocks, compensation) and solve them.

*How to teach:* Sort 12 problems by best strategy (e.g., 50 + 30 mental; 48 + 37 number line; 99 + 24 compensation). Solve them, then discuss why each strategy fits.

- **Q:** What strategy is quick for 99 + 24 and what is the answer?
  **A:** Compensation: 100 + 23 = 123.
- **Q:** Solve 50 + 30 mentally.
  **A:** 80.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Target 100 game
*Student:* Make two 2-digit numbers from cards whose sum is closest to 100; play 8 rounds and record equations.

*How to teach:* Target 100: draw 4 digit cards and make two 2-digit numbers with a sum as close to 100 as possible. Record each equation and how far it is from 100. Play 8 rounds.

- **Q:** With cards 4, 7, 5, 2, make two numbers whose sum is closest to 100.
  **A:** 47 + 52 = 99 (or 42 + 57 = 99) — only 1 away from 100.
- **Q:** How far from 100 is a sum of 104?
  **A:** 4 away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Find and fix mistakes in 5 worked problems (e.g., forgot to regroup) and explain each error.

*How to teach:* Show 5 worked problems with mistakes. The child finds and fixes each and explains the error in a sentence.

- **Q:** Someone wrote 46 + 38 = 74. What went wrong?
  **A:** They didn't add the regrouped ten from 14 ones; the correct answer is 84.
- **Q:** Someone wrote 53 − 27 = 34. What went wrong?
  **A:** They did 7 − 3 instead of trading a ten for 13 − 7; the correct answer is 26.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Add and subtract within 100 (16 items), add up to four two-digit numbers, and solve 3 two-step word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test: 16 add/subtract items, multi-addend items, and 3 two-step word problems with models.

- **Q:** 38 fish in a tank, 15 added, then 20 sold. How many now?
  **A:** 33 fish.
- **Q:** 22 + 15 + 18 + 25 = ?
  **A:** 80.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test (suggested 16 items): 1) 39 + 26 = 65 2) 72 − 29 = 43 3) 54 + 38 = 92 4) 81 − 45 = 36 5) 66 + 27 = 93 6) 90 − 58 = 32 7) 47 + 47 = 94 8) 63 − 37 = 26 9) 28 + 35 = 63 10) 100 − 45 = 55 11) 75 − 38 = 37 12) 19 + 64 = 83 13) 22 + 15 + 18 + 25 = 80 14) 31 + 14 + 26 = 71 15) 13 + 27 + 30 + 9 = 79 16) 56 − 21 = 35. Two-step: a) 38 fish, 15 added, 20 sold → 33. b) Had 50¢, spent 18¢ and 25¢ → 7¢ left. c) 27 boys, 6 more girls than boys → 33 girls, 60 children in all. Proficient: 16/19.

## Science 2: Seeds on the move

**Prepare:** Collect assorted seeds (maple 'helicopters,' dandelion, burrs, acorns, apple and other fruit seeds), an old sock, a fan, and a tape measure. Check the walking area for poison oak or foxtails.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking seeds move by themselves. They are carried by wind, water, animals, or fur, or pop out of pods.
- Thinking animals plant seeds on purpose. Most dispersal is accidental (seeds stick or pass through).

### Day 1 (Monday): Seed collection
*Student:* Collect seeds outside or from fruits and sort them by how you think they travel (wind, water, animals, fur).

*How to teach:* Collect seeds and sort by how they might travel.

- **Q:** How do dandelion seeds travel?
  **A:** By wind — they have fluffy parachutes.
- **Q:** How do burrs travel?
  **A:** They hook onto animal fur or clothes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sock walk
*Student:* Walk through a grassy area wearing an old sock over a shoe; observe and count seeds that stick.

*How to teach:* Walk through grass with a sock over a shoe; count seeds that stick.

- **Q:** Why did seeds stick to the sock?
  **A:** They have hooks or stickers that grab onto fur and cloth.
- **Q:** How does this help plants?
  **A:** Seeds get carried to new places to grow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Wind test
*Student:* Use a fan to test which seeds travel farthest; measure distances.

*How to teach:* Use a fan to test which seeds travel farthest; measure distances.

- **Q:** Which seed traveled farthest?
  **A:** Usually a light, fluffy seed like dandelion.
- **Q:** Why do heavy seeds not go far in wind?
  **A:** They are too heavy for the wind to carry.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Animals eat fruit
*Student:* Read about birds eating berries and dropping seeds; draw a seed's journey from fruit to new plant.

*How to teach:* Read about birds eating berries and dropping seeds; draw a seed's journey.

- **Q:** How do birds spread seeds?
  **A:** They eat berries and the seeds pass through and drop in new places.
- **Q:** Why do some plants have tasty fruit?
  **A:** Animals eat the fruit and carry the seeds away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Match 8 seeds to their dispersal method with reasons.

*How to teach:* Match 8 seeds to their dispersal method with reasons.

- **Q:** How does a maple seed travel?
  **A:** Wind — its wings spin like a helicopter.
- **Q:** How does a coconut travel?
  **A:** It floats on water.

**Assessment:** Seed dispersal matching and journey drawing.

**Answer key:** Matching (suggested 8): dandelion — wind (fluffy parachute); maple — wind (wings spin); burr — animal fur (hooks); coconut — water (floats); berry — animals eat and drop seeds; acorn — squirrels bury them; apple seed — animals eat fruit; cocklebur/foxtail — sticks to fur or socks. Journey drawing: fruit eaten → seed carried → dropped → sprouts into new plant.

## People Who Make a Difference: Trade between nations

**Prepare:** 10 household items with 'Made in' labels; world map; stickers; trade-game cards (e.g., different colored cards per player).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking imports and exports are the same: imports come into a country; exports are sent out.
- Thinking a country can make everything it needs: climate, land and skills differ, so nations trade.

### Day 1 (Monday): Label hunt
*Student:* Look at labels on 10 household items to find where they were made; mark the countries on a world map.

*How to teach:* Find where 10 household items were made; mark countries on a map.

- **Q:** Which country made the most items in our house?
  **A:** Answer from labels (often China or the U.S.).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Why trade
*Student:* Learn that nations trade to get things they cannot make or grow; discuss bananas from Central America and almonds exported from California.

*How to teach:* Learn nations trade for what they can't make or grow: bananas from Central America; almonds exported from California.

- **Q:** Why does the U.S. import bananas?
  **A:** Bananas need a warm, tropical climate, so most are grown in Central and South America.
- **Q:** What is an export?
  **A:** A good sent out of a country to sell.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trade game
*Student:* Play a trading game where each player has different goods and must trade to make a complete set.

*How to teach:* Play a trading game to complete sets.

- **Q:** Why did you need to trade?
  **A:** You didn't have all the goods you needed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): California exports
*Student:* Read about California products sent around the world (almonds, movies, technology).

*How to teach:* Read about California exports (almonds, movies, technology).

- **Q:** Name a California export.
  **A:** Almonds, movies, technology, wine, dairy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write 3 sentences about why trade helps nations.

*How to teach:* Write 3 sentences about why trade helps nations.

- **Q:** What is an import?
  **A:** A good brought into a country from another.

**Assessment:** Trade map and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Trade map: countries from 10 labels marked correctly. Written sentences (any 3 accurate): nations trade to get goods they can't make or grow (bananas grow in warm Central America); trade lets nations sell what they make well (California almonds); trade gives people more choices and jobs; trade helps nations work together. Proficient = accurate map + 3 sentences.

## Art & Music 2: Compose a melody

**Prepare:** Chrome Music Lab Song Maker, staff paper, xylophone or piano app.

**Watch for:**
- Composing with too many notes: a short melody with a clear shape is better.
- Ignoring feedback: one 'wish' can make the song better.

### Day 1 (Monday): Song Maker
*Student:* Explore Chrome Music Lab Song Maker and make 3 short melodies that go up, go down, and repeat.

*How to teach:* Make 3 Song Maker melodies: one going up, one going down, one repeating.

- **Q:** How does a melody that goes up look on Song Maker?
  **A:** The colored blocks climb higher left to right.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compose
*Student:* Compose an 8-beat melody using sol, mi, la, and do on Song Maker or a xylophone; write it on staff paper or with iconic notes.

*How to teach:* Compose an 8-beat melody using sol, mi, la and do; write it.

- **Q:** Which is lowest: do, mi, sol or la?
  **A:** Do.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add words
*Student:* Add words about a pet or favorite place to your melody.

*How to teach:* Add words about a pet or favorite place.

- **Q:** Do your words fit the beats?
  **A:** Each syllable matches a note.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Play it for a family member, ask for one 'star' and one 'wish,' and revise one part.

*How to teach:* Play for a family member; get a star and a wish; revise one part.

- **Q:** What is a 'star and a wish'?
  **A:** One thing they liked and one idea to improve.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Perform the final melody and explain why you chose its up and down shape.

*How to teach:* Perform; explain the up/down shape.

- **Q:** What is a phrase?
  **A:** A short musical sentence.

**Assessment:** Composed melody (notation or recording) and performance.

**Answer key:** Composed melody: 8 beats using do, mi, sol, la, written in staff or iconic notation or recorded; words fit the rhythm; one revision made from a star-and-wish. Explanation of the up/down shape (e.g., 'goes up when the dog jumps'). Proficient = all.

## PE 2: Flexibility and fitness review

**Prepare:** Mat, ruler and a box, a kids yoga video, fitness log.

**Watch for:**
- Bouncing when stretching: hold still for 15 seconds.
- Thinking fitness is only running: cardio, strength and flexibility are all parts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stretch routine
*Student:* Learn 6 stretches (hamstring, quad, calf, shoulder, side, butterfly), holding each for 15 seconds.

*How to teach:* Teach hamstring, quad, calf, shoulder, side and butterfly stretches, 15 seconds each.

- **Q:** Where is the hamstring?
  **A:** The back of the thigh.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sit and reach
*Student:* Measure sit-and-reach with a ruler by a box and record the result.

*How to teach:* Measure sit-and-reach at a box with a ruler; record.

- **Q:** What does sit-and-reach test?
  **A:** Flexibility of the hamstrings and lower back.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Yoga for kids
*Student:* Follow a kid yoga video (tree, cat-cow, downward dog, cobra) and name which muscles stretch.

*How to teach:* Follow a kids yoga video (tree, cat-cow, downward dog, cobra); name stretched muscles.

- **Q:** What does downward dog stretch?
  **A:** Hamstrings, calves and shoulders.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fitness circuit
*Student:* Do a 6-station circuit: jog, push-ups, curl-ups, jumping jacks, stretch, balance.

*How to teach:* 6-station circuit: jog, push-ups, curl-ups, jumping jacks, stretch, balance.

- **Q:** Which station builds cardio?
  **A:** Jogging and jumping jacks.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how cardio, strength and flexibility each help your body; share your fitness log.

*How to teach:* Explain cardio, strength, flexibility; share the log.

- **Q:** How does flexibility help you?
  **A:** Lets you move freely and helps prevent injuries.

**Assessment:** Fitness log complete; oral explanation of 3 fitness parts.

**Answer key:** Explanation: cardio (heart/lung endurance) makes your heart and lungs strong so you can play longer; strength makes muscles able to push, pull and lift; flexibility lets joints move through full range and helps prevent injury. Fitness log complete (endurance, strength, sit-and-reach). Proficient = 3 parts explained + log complete.

## Health 2: Food safety and meal planning

**Prepare:** Kitchen, fridge thermometer, recipe card.

**Watch for:**
- Using the same cutting board for raw meat and veggies: keep them separate.
- Leaving food out a long time: chill leftovers within 2 hours.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clean, separate, cook, chill
*Student:* Learn the 4 food safety steps and find examples in your kitchen.

*How to teach:* Teach Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill; find examples.

- **Q:** What are the 4 food safety steps?
  **A:** Clean, separate, cook, chill.
- **Q:** What is cross-contamination?
  **A:** Germs moving from one food (like raw meat) to another.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fridge check
*Student:* Check fridge temperature with a parent and see where foods are stored.

*How to teach:* Check fridge temperature with a parent.

- **Q:** How cold should the fridge be?
  **A:** 40°F or colder.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a meal
*Student:* Plan a balanced family dinner using MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Plan a balanced dinner using MyPlate.

- **Q:** Does your meal have all 5 groups?
  **A:** Checks plan.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cook together
*Student:* Help cook the planned meal, washing hands and produce first.

*How to teach:* Help cook, washing hands and produce first.

- **Q:** Why wash hands before cooking?
  **A:** To keep germs out of the food.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Unit quiz on food groups, snacks, labels and food safety.

*How to teach:* Give the nutrition unit quiz.

- **Q:** Where should raw meat go in the fridge?
  **A:** On the bottom shelf, so juices can't drip on other foods.

**Assessment:** Nutrition unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Unit quiz (sample answers): 5 food groups = fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy; go/slow/whoa examples correct; serving size must be checked before comparing; ads use tricks; food safety steps = clean, separate, cook, chill; fridge at 40°F or colder. Proficient = 80% correct.
