# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 32

## English Language Arts 6: Literary argument essay

**Prepare:** Gather: Secret Garden notes, computer. Gather Secret Garden notes and quote list.

**Watch for:**
- Dropping a quote in as its own sentence. Embed it inside your own sentence with a lead-in phrase and follow it with analysis.
- Switching to informal tone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Claim
*Student:* Write a claim answering: Who changes more, Mary or Colin, and what causes the change? Gather five pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* The child writes a claim and gathers five pieces of evidence.

- **Q:** Write a claim about who changes more.
  **A:** Example: 'Mary changes more because she transforms from a sour, selfish girl into someone who heals both Colin and the garden.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Embedding quotes
*Student:* Lesson on embedding quotations smoothly; draft two body paragraphs with embedded quotes and analysis.

*How to teach:* Teach embedding; the child drafts two body paragraphs.

- **Q:** How is an embedded quotation different from a dropped-in quotation?
  **A:** An embedded quote is woven into your own sentence with a lead-in (e.g., 'When Mary first meets Colin, Burnett describes him as…') so it reads smoothly, and it is followed by analysis; a dropped quote stands alone as its own sentence with no introduction.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish draft
*Student:* Draft the introduction, a counterclaim paragraph and conclusion.

*How to teach:* Draft introduction, counterclaim and conclusion.

- **Q:** Give a counterclaim to 'Mary changes more.'
  **A:** 'Some readers argue Colin changes more because he goes from bedridden to running.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise for sentence variety and consistent formal tone; edit and type the final essay.

*How to teach:* Revise and edit; type the final.

- **Q:** Make formal: 'Colin was totally being a brat.'
  **A:** 'Colin behaved in a spoiled, demanding way.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Submit the essay with a self-assessment and take the unit vocabulary test.

*How to teach:* Submit with self-assessment; vocabulary test.

- **Q:** What is a symbol?
  **A:** An object that stands for a bigger idea.

**Assessment:** Literary argument essay rubric.

**Answer key:** Literary argument rubric — 4: arguable claim (e.g., 'Colin changes more, because he moves from believing he will die to running'), 3+ embedded quotes with analysis, counterclaim and rebuttal, formal tone, varied sentences; 3: some analysis thin; 2: summary; 1: incomplete. Vocabulary test: novel and unit terms (symbol, theme, omniscient, dialect, characterization).

## Mathematics 6: Mean and mean absolute deviation

**Prepare:** Gather: snap cubes, ruler and pencil for balance. Have 20–30 snap cubes; a ruler balanced on a pencil helps show 'balance point'.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to use absolute values for MAD, so deviations cancel to 0.
- Thinking the mean must be one of the data values.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fair share
*Student:* Use stacks of snap cubes to level out unequal towers and connect the result to adding and dividing.

*How to teach:* Build towers of 3, 5, 7 and 9 cubes; move cubes until all are equal. Connect to adding (24) and dividing by 4.

- **Q:** Towers of 3, 5, 7 and 9 cubes are leveled. How tall is each?
  **A:** 6 cubes (24 ÷ 4).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Balance point
*Student:* Show on a dot plot that the mean is the point where distances above and below balance, using 4 small data sets.

*How to teach:* Plot 2, 4, 6, 8 and mark the mean 5. Show distances below (3 + 1) equal distances above (1 + 3).

- **Q:** Show that 5 is the balance point of 2, 4, 6, 8.
  **A:** Distances below: 3 + 1 = 4; above: 1 + 3 = 4. They balance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mean absolute deviation
*Student:* Compute the MAD for two basketball players' points per game and decide who is more consistent.

*How to teach:* Model MAD: find the mean, find each distance from it, average the distances.

- **Q:** Player A scored 10, 12, 14, 16, 18; Player B scored 6, 10, 14, 18, 22. Find each MAD. Who is more consistent?
  **A:** Both means are 14. A's MAD = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4; B's = 24 ÷ 5 = 4.8. A is more consistent.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing groups
*Student:* Compare two data sets (e.g., plant heights with and without fertilizer) using mean and MAD.

*How to teach:* Give two data sets: fertilized plants 12, 14, 15, 16, 18 cm and unfertilized 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 cm.

- **Q:** Compare the groups using mean and MAD.
  **A:** Fertilized mean 15, MAD 1.6; unfertilized mean 11, MAD 1.2. Fertilized plants were about 4 cm taller on average; both groups had small variability.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mean and MAD.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** The mean of five numbers is 8; four are 6, 7, 9, 10. Find the fifth.
  **A:** 8 (total 40 − 32).

**Assessment:** Mean and MAD quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) mean of 4, 7, 9, 10, 15 = 9 2) MAD = (5 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 6) ÷ 5 = 2.8 3) 3, 3, 6, 8: mean 5, MAD = (2 + 2 + 1 + 3) ÷ 4 = 2 4) a team with MAD 1.5 is more consistent than one with MAD 3.2 5) mean of five numbers is 8 and four are 6, 7, 9, 10 → fifth is 8.

## Science 6 (Integrated): The nervous system and senses

**Prepare:** Gather: ruler, paper clips, blindfold, jelly beans or flavored candy. Jelly beans or flavored candy; check for allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking reflexes go through the brain first. Many reflexes route through the spinal cord.
- Thinking taste alone gives flavor. Smell supplies much of it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Neurons and messages
*Student:* Diagram a neuron and the pathway from receptor to spinal cord to brain to muscle.

*How to teach:* Diagram a neuron; trace receptor → nerve → spinal cord → brain → muscle.

- **Q:** Name three parts of a neuron.
  **A:** Dendrites, cell body, axon.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reaction time
*Student:* Run the ruler-drop reaction test 10 times with each hand and compare averages.

*How to teach:* Run the ruler drop 10 times per hand; average.

- **Q:** Why might the dominant hand be faster?
  **A:** It's practiced more, so the nerve pathways are used more.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Touch receptors
*Student:* Use a two-point test with paper clips on fingertip, palm and forearm to map receptor density.

*How to teach:* Two-point test on fingertip, palm and forearm.

- **Q:** Where can you feel two points closest together?
  **A:** Fingertips — they have the most touch receptors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Taste and smell
*Student:* Blindfolded, taste jelly beans with and without holding your nose and record identification accuracy.

*How to teach:* Blindfolded jelly bean test with and without nose held.

- **Q:** Predict the effect of holding your nose.
  **A:** Fewer correct identifications.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER explaining why smell affects taste, using your data.

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER.

- **Q:** What part of the nervous system processes signals?
  **A:** The brain.

**Assessment:** Sense investigation data and CER.

**Answer key:** Data: reaction averages each hand, two-point thresholds (fingertip smallest), jelly bean accuracy with and without nose held. CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: 'Smell affects taste: I identified 9 of 10 flavors normally but only 3 of 10 holding my nose, because much of flavor comes from smell receptors in the nose.'

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: From republic to empire

**Prepare:** Gather: map of Roman expansion, Plutarch's Life of Caesar excerpt, Res Gestae of Augustus excerpt. Print excerpts from Plutarch's Life of Caesar and the Res Gestae; a map of Roman expansion.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Augustus called himself king. He used titles like princeps ('first citizen') while holding real power.
- Thinking Caesar was emperor. He was dictator; Augustus was the first emperor.

### Day 1 (Monday): Punic Wars
*Student:* Map the Punic Wars and Hannibal's route and explain how victory made Rome a Mediterranean power.

*How to teach:* Map the Punic Wars and Hannibal's route.

- **Q:** What did Rome gain by defeating Carthage?
  **A:** Control of the western Mediterranean.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Crisis in the Republic
*Student:* Read about the gap between rich and poor, the Gracchi and Cicero's defense of the Republic.

*How to teach:* Read about the Gracchi and Cicero.

- **Q:** What did the Gracchi try to do?
  **A:** Give land to poor citizens; they were killed by opponents in the Senate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Julius Caesar
*Student:* Read Plutarch's account of Caesar crossing the Rubicon and his assassination; debate whether he was a hero or threat.

*How to teach:* Read Plutarch; debate Caesar.

- **Q:** Why did crossing the Rubicon matter?
  **A:** Bringing an army into Italy broke the law and started civil war.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Augustus
*Student:* Read excerpts of the Res Gestae and identify how Augustus presented his power while keeping republican forms.

*How to teach:* Read the Res Gestae.

- **Q:** How did Augustus present his power?
  **A:** As restoring the Republic and being 'first citizen', while controlling the army and government.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a timeline from 509 BCE to 27 CE and take a quiz on the republic's fall.

*How to teach:* Make the timeline; quiz.

- **Q:** Who was the first Roman emperor?
  **A:** Augustus (27 BCE).

**Assessment:** Timeline and quiz.

**Answer key:** Timeline: 509 BCE Republic founded; 264–146 BCE Punic Wars; 133 BCE Tiberius Gracchus; 49 BCE Caesar crosses the Rubicon; 44 BCE Caesar assassinated; 27 BCE Augustus becomes the first emperor (beginning of the Pax Romana). Quiz: Hannibal crossed the Alps with elephants; Carthage destroyed 146 BCE; the gap between rich and poor and powerful generals weakened the Republic.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Choreography

**Prepare:** Music without lyrics, a poem or natural phenomenon (water cycle, storm) as stimulus, and a recording device.

**Watch for:**
- Making a dance of random moves. Strong choreography develops a few motifs.
- Thinking canon and unison are the same. Unison = together; canon = staggered starts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stimulus
*Student:* Choose a stimulus (a poem, the water cycle, a storm) and brainstorm movements.

*How to teach:* Choose a stimulus; brainstorm movements. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a stimulus in choreography?
  **A:** The idea or source that inspires the dance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Motifs
*Student:* Create three movement motifs and vary them in level and speed.

*How to teach:* Create three motifs; vary level and speed. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is a movement motif?
  **A:** A short movement phrase that is repeated and varied.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Structure
*Student:* Arrange motifs into a 1-minute dance with a beginning, middle and end.

*How to teach:* Arrange motifs into a 1-minute dance with beginning, middle and end. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why have a clear beginning and ending?
  **A:** So the audience knows when the dance starts and ends and can follow its shape.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse with a partner using unison and canon.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with a partner using unison and canon. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is canon in dance?
  **A:** Dancers perform the same movement starting at different times.

### Day 5 (Friday): Perform
*Student:* Perform and record the dance, then explain how it connects to the stimulus.

*How to teach:* Perform, record and explain the connection to the stimulus. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** How does your dance connect to the stimulus?
  **A:** Answer varies; should link specific motifs to parts of the stimulus.

**Assessment:** Choreography performance.

**Answer key:** Choreography rubric (1–4 each): three motifs developed, clear structure, unison and canon, connection to stimulus explained. Proficient = 3+ each.

## Physical Education 6: Designing a personal fitness plan

**Prepare:** Gather: fitness log, planning template. Print a 4-week planning template. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Planning too much too soon.
- Forgetting rest and sleep.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review data
*Student:* Review baseline and mid-year scores to choose focus areas.

*How to teach:* Review data; choose focus.

- **Q:** How do you choose focus areas?
  **A:** Look for tests below the Healthy Fitness Zone or where goals weren't met.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write plan
*Student:* Write a 4-week plan with FITT for cardio, strength and flexibility.

*How to teach:* Write the 4-week plan.

- **Q:** How much activity do kids need daily?
  **A:** At least 60 minutes, with muscle- and bone-strengthening 3 days a week.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Week 1 workout
*Student:* Complete day 1 of your plan and log it.

*How to teach:* Complete day 1; log.

- **Q:** What should a log include?
  **A:** Date, activity, FITT and how you felt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nutrition and rest
*Student:* Add hydration, nutrition and sleep goals to your plan and complete day 2.

*How to teach:* Add nutrition, hydration and sleep; day 2.

- **Q:** How much sleep do 6th graders need?
  **A:** About 9–12 hours.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete day 3 and have a parent sign off on the plan.

*How to teach:* Day 3; parent signs.

- **Q:** Why include rest days?
  **A:** Muscles recover and get stronger.

**Assessment:** Written personal fitness plan.

**Answer key:** Personal fitness plan rubric — 4: focus areas from data; 4-week FITT for cardio, strength and flexibility with progression; hydration, nutrition and sleep goals (9–12 hours for this age); parent signature; 3: one area thin; 2: missing FITT; 1: incomplete.

## Health 6: Consumer health: products and advertising

**Prepare:** Gather two versions (name-brand and generic/store-brand) of a product like shampoo, toothpaste or sunscreen with price tags. Find 4 health-product ads. Unit price = price ÷ number of units (ounces).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking name brands are always better. Generic products often have the same active ingredients for less money.
- Believing testimonials prove a product works. Personal stories aren't scientific evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ad claims
*Student:* Analyze 4 health-product ads for claims, techniques and missing information.

*How to teach:* Analyze 4 health-product ads for claims, techniques and missing information. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What questions should you ask about an ad claim?
  **A:** Who says so? What evidence? What's left out? Who profits?

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparison shopping
*Student:* Compare name-brand and generic versions of a product by ingredients and unit price.

*How to teach:* Compare name-brand and generic by ingredients and unit price. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** A 10-oz bottle costs $5.00 and a 16-oz bottle costs $6.40. Which is the better unit price?
  **A:** The 16-oz bottle: $0.40 per ounce vs. $0.50 per ounce.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Quackery
*Student:* Identify red flags of health fraud (miracle cure, secret formula, testimonials only).

*How to teach:* Identify red flags of health fraud (miracle cure, secret formula, testimonials only, 'act now').

- **Q:** What is quackery?
  **A:** Selling health products or treatments that don't work, using false or unproven claims.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decide
*Student:* Use DECIDE to choose a sunscreen or toothpaste and justify the choice.

*How to teach:* Use DECIDE to choose a sunscreen or toothpaste and justify. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What should you look for in a toothpaste?
  **A:** Fluoride and the ADA Seal of Acceptance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a consumer-health worksheet.

*How to teach:* Complete the consumer-health worksheet. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a generic product?
  **A:** A product without a brand name, usually with the same active ingredients and lower price.

**Assessment:** Consumer worksheet.

**Answer key:** Worksheet key: identifies ad techniques and missing info; computes unit prices correctly; names three quackery red flags; justifies a product choice using ingredients, evidence and price. Proficient = 80%.
