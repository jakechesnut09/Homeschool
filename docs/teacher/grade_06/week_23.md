# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 23

## English Language Arts 6: The Story of My Life, selected chapters 5-23

**Prepare:** Gather: The Story of My Life, Braille alphabet chart. Print a Braille alphabet chart; gather glue (dots) and cardstock.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking an objective summary includes your reaction. Keep it neutral.
- Thinking learning to speak was easy once Helen could spell. Chapter 13 shows it took enormous persistence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Learning language
*Student:* Read chapters 5-7 and trace how Anne Sullivan teaches Keller, listing each method.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5–7; list Sullivan's methods.

- **Q:** Name two methods Anne Sullivan used.
  **A:** Spelling words into Helen's hand connected to real objects; teaching outdoors through nature; letting her learn through experience and conversation rather than drills.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Braille and reading
*Student:* Read about Keller learning to read and write; use a Braille chart to write your name with glue dots on cardstock.

*How to teach:* Read about reading and writing; the child writes their name in Braille with glue dots.

- **Q:** How many dots are in a Braille cell?
  **A:** Six (two columns of three).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Learning to speak
*Student:* Read chapter 13 on learning to speak and explain how this section contributes to the central idea.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 13; explain how it develops the central idea.

- **Q:** How does learning to speak contribute to the central idea?
  **A:** It shows her determination and that communication kept expanding her world; she worked hard with a teacher (Sarah Fuller) to speak aloud.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Objective summary
*Student:* Write a 150-word objective summary of the selections read so far.

*How to teach:* The child writes a 150-word objective summary.

- **Q:** What should an objective summary's first sentence do?
  **A:** State the title, author and central idea.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on central idea and structure; discuss with a parent what surprised you most.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; discuss what surprised the child.

- **Q:** What text structure does Keller mainly use?
  **A:** Chronological order (time order), with reflection from her adult point of view.

**Assessment:** Objective summary and quiz.

**Answer key:** Objective summary rubric (150 words): 4 = central idea (language and a devoted teacher transformed Keller's life), key events in order (water, learning words, reading/Braille, learning to speak), neutral, own words; 3 = minor omission; 2 = opinion or copying; 1 = off-topic. Quiz: central idea = learning language opened Keller's world; structure = chronological autobiography with adult reflection; Sullivan spelled words into her hand, taught outdoors from nature, and connected words to experiences.

## Mathematics 6: Exponents and order of operations

**Prepare:** Gather: sheet of paper for folding, calculator. Have a sheet of paper for folding and a calculator for checking.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 2⁵ means 2 × 5. It means 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32.
- Working strictly left to right. Order: parentheses, exponents, multiply/divide left to right, add/subtract left to right.

### Day 1 (Monday): Paper-folding powers
*Student:* Fold a sheet of paper in half repeatedly, record layers as 2^1, 2^2 ... and predict layers after 10 folds.

*How to teach:* Fold the paper in half and count layers after each fold; record 2, 4, 8… as powers of 2. Ask the child to predict 10 folds.

- **Q:** How many layers after 10 folds?
  **A:** 2¹⁰ = 1,024 layers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluating powers
*Student:* Evaluate 15 expressions such as 3^4, (1/2)^3 and 0.2^2 and compare 2^5 with 5^2.

*How to teach:* Write 3⁴ as 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 before computing. The child evaluates 15 expressions.

- **Q:** Evaluate 3⁴, (½)³ and 0.2².
  **A:** 81; ⅛; 0.04.
- **Q:** Which is larger, 2⁵ or 5²?
  **A:** 2⁵ = 32 is larger than 5² = 25.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Order of operations
*Student:* Evaluate 15 expressions like 2 + 3 x 4^2 and (2 + 3)^2 and find the errors in three worked examples.

*How to teach:* Model 2 + 3 × 4² step by step, then (2 + 3)². The child finds the errors in three worked examples.

- **Q:** Evaluate 2 + 3 × 4².
  **A:** 50 (4² = 16; 3 × 16 = 48; 48 + 2 = 50).
- **Q:** Evaluate (2 + 3)².
  **A:** 25.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Formulas
*Student:* Evaluate V = s^3, SA = 6s^2 and A = 1/2 bh for given values, and choose a formula to solve a packaging problem.

*How to teach:* Substitute into formulas, writing the formula, the substitution, then the answer.

- **Q:** A cube box has edge 1.5 ft. Find its volume and surface area.
  **A:** V = 1.5³ = 3.375 cu ft; SA = 6 × 1.5² = 13.5 sq ft.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on exponents, order of operations and formulas.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Evaluate 4 + 2³ × 5.
  **A:** 44.

**Assessment:** Exponent and order of operations quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 3⁴ = 81 2) (⅔)² = 4/9 3) 0.5³ = 0.125 4) 10 − 2 × 3 = 4 5) (10 − 2) × 3 = 24 6) 4 + 2³ × 5 = 44 7) 3 × (2 + 4)² ÷ 9 = 12 8) volume of a cube with s = 4: 64 9) surface area of a cube with s = ½: 1.5 10) 18 − 6 ÷ 3 + 1 = 17.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Energy transfer and conservation

**Prepare:** Gather: foam pipe insulation cut in half, marbles, tape, rubber bands. Buy foam pipe insulation and cut it lengthwise into channels.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking energy is used up. It transfers or transforms; it is conserved.
- Starting the marble at exactly the loop's height. It must start higher because of friction.

### Day 1 (Monday): Where does energy go?
*Student:* Rub hands together and bounce a ball until it stops; explain the energy transfers to thermal energy and sound.

*How to teach:* Rub hands; bounce a ball until it stops.

- **Q:** Why does a bouncing ball stop?
  **A:** Each bounce transfers some energy to thermal energy and sound, so it rises less each time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Roller coaster design
*Student:* Design a marble roller coaster from foam pipe insulation with a loop, defining criteria and constraints.

*How to teach:* Define criteria (completes a loop) and constraints (materials).

- **Q:** Give one criterion for the coaster.
  **A:** The marble must complete one loop and stop at the end.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build and test
*Student:* Build and test the coaster, adjusting starting height so the marble completes the loop.

*How to teach:* Build and test, adjusting start height.

- **Q:** Why must the start be higher than the loop?
  **A:** Friction converts some energy to heat, so extra potential energy is needed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Energy argument
*Student:* Write an argument explaining energy transfers at each part of your coaster, including friction.

*How to teach:* The child writes the energy argument.

- **Q:** Where is the marble's speed lowest on the loop?
  **A:** At the top of the loop.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on forces, fields and energy.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** State the law of conservation of energy.
  **A:** Energy is not created or destroyed; it only changes form or moves.

**Assessment:** Coaster argument and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Argument rubric — 4: describes energy at release (PE), descent (PE→KE), loop (KE→PE→KE) and end, includes friction converting energy to thermal energy and sound, uses test data on starting height; 3: one section thin; 2: no friction; 1: incomplete. Unit 6 test sample: electric/magnetic forces and distance; electromagnet variables; gravity depends on mass and distance; KE depends on mass and speed (speed matters more); types of PE; energy conservation.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Indo-Aryan migrations, Brahmanism and early Hinduism

**Prepare:** Gather: Rig Veda Hymn of Creation excerpt, Bhagavad Gita excerpt. Print the Rig Veda 'Hymn of Creation' (10.129) and a short Bhagavad Gita excerpt. Teach respectfully — Hinduism is a living faith.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Hinduism has one founder. It developed over centuries from Vedic traditions.
- Thinking the varna system is unchanged today. Caste discrimination is illegal in modern India, though effects remain.

### Day 1 (Monday): Indo-Aryan migrations
*Student:* Read about the Indo-Aryan migrations and the Vedas and explain their effect on language and religion.

*How to teach:* Read about Indo-Aryan migrations and the Vedas.

- **Q:** In what language were the Vedas composed?
  **A:** Sanskrit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brahmanism
*Student:* Read the Rig Veda 'Hymn of Creation' and discuss the role of Brahmin priests and rituals.

*How to teach:* Read the Hymn of Creation.

- **Q:** What makes the Hymn of Creation unusual?
  **A:** It asks questions about how the universe began and ends by wondering whether anyone truly knows.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hindu beliefs
*Student:* Create a graphic organizer explaining Brahman, karma, dharma, reincarnation and moksha.

*How to teach:* Make the beliefs organizer.

- **Q:** What is karma?
  **A:** The idea that one's actions affect one's future lives.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Social structure
*Student:* Diagram the four varnas and jati system, and discuss how the system shaped daily life and limited mobility.

*How to teach:* Diagram varnas and jatis.

- **Q:** Name the four varnas.
  **A:** Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (rulers/warriors), Vaishyas (merchants/farmers), Shudras (laborers/servants).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how Brahmanism evolved into Hinduism with two pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* The child writes the paragraph.

- **Q:** What is moksha?
  **A:** Release from the cycle of rebirth, uniting with Brahman.

**Assessment:** Organizer and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Organizer: Brahman (universal spirit), karma (actions have consequences), dharma (duty), reincarnation (rebirth), moksha (release from the cycle). Paragraph rubric — 4: clear topic sentence answering the question, at least two specific accurate pieces of evidence (named source, artifact or fact), explanation linking each to the claim, concluding sentence; 3: accurate but only one piece of evidence explained; 2: general statements without evidence; 1: inaccurate or off-topic. Expected: Vedic rituals led by Brahmin priests developed, through the Upanishads and later texts like the Bhagavad Gita, into Hinduism with beliefs in Brahman, karma, dharma and moksha.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Theatre games and improvisation

**Prepare:** Clear an open space; gather a few props (scarf, chair, hat). Recruit family members. Learn 'Yes, and' (accept an idea and add to it) and 'Freeze' (call freeze, tap someone out, start a new scene from the frozen pose).

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'no' to scene partners' ideas. Accept ('yes') and build ('and') to keep scenes going.
- Thinking tableaux are silly poses. Tableaux are frozen pictures that tell a story through levels, facial expression and focus.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm-ups
*Student:* Do vocal and physical warm-ups (tongue twisters, mirror game) and learn theatre etiquette.

*How to teach:* Do vocal and physical warm-ups; learn etiquette (listen, support others, no put-downs). Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why warm up before theatre?
  **A:** To prepare voice and body and focus the group.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yes, and
*Student:* Play 'Yes, and' storytelling and 'Freeze' improv with family members.

*How to teach:* Play 'Yes, and' storytelling and 'Freeze' improv. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What does 'Yes, and' mean?
  **A:** Accept your partner's idea and add something new to it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Objectives
*Student:* Improvise scenes where each character wants something different and identify the objective.

*How to teach:* Improvise scenes where characters want different things; name objectives. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is a character's objective?
  **A:** What the character wants in the scene.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tableaux
*Student:* Create frozen tableaux of three moments from a myth studied in history.

*How to teach:* Create tableaux of three moments from a myth. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What makes a strong tableau?
  **A:** Clear story, different levels, facial expressions and everyone focused on the action.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Write about what made an improv scene work and what you would change.

*How to teach:* Write about what made a scene work and what to change. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why does conflict make scenes interesting?
  **A:** When characters want different things, there is tension and something at stake.

**Assessment:** Participation rubric and reflection.

**Answer key:** Participation rubric (1–4): commits to scenes, accepts and builds ideas, clear objectives, supports others. Reflection proficient if it names a specific successful moment and why.

## Physical Education 6: Rhythm and line dances

**Prepare:** Gather: music player, GoNoodle videos. Queue music at several tempos; Cha Cha Slide or Cupid Shuffle. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Starting off the beat. Count '5, 6, 7, 8' before starting.
- Looking at feet. Look up and listen.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finding the beat
*Student:* Step to the beat of songs at different tempos and count 8-counts.

*How to teach:* Step to the beat; count 8-counts.

- **Q:** What is an 8-count?
  **A:** A phrase of eight beats used to organize dance steps.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Basic steps
*Student:* Learn grapevine, step-touch, pivot turn and kick-ball-change.

*How to teach:* Learn grapevine, step-touch, pivot turn, kick-ball-change.

- **Q:** Describe a grapevine to the right.
  **A:** Step right, cross left behind, step right, touch left (or tap).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Line dance
*Student:* Learn the Cha Cha Slide or Cupid Shuffle with correct counts.

*How to teach:* Learn the line dance.

- **Q:** In the Cupid Shuffle, what do you do after 'to the right' and 'to the left'?
  **A:** Kick (heel taps), then 'walk it by yourself' turning a quarter.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heart rate
*Student:* Dance for 10 minutes continuously and check heart rate.

*How to teach:* Dance 10 minutes; check heart rate.

- **Q:** Is 10 minutes of continuous dancing aerobic exercise?
  **A:** Yes — it raises heart rate into a moderate-to-vigorous range.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform the line dance from memory for a family member.

*How to teach:* Perform from memory.

- **Q:** What helps you stay on beat?
  **A:** Counting and listening for the strong beat.

**Assessment:** Line dance performance.

**Answer key:** Line dance performance: 4 = all steps from memory on the beat with energy; 3 = minor errors; 2 = frequent cues needed; 1 = can't complete.

## Health 6: Calling 911 and emergency resources

**Prepare:** Use a phone that is OFF or unplugged for mock calls (never dial 911 to practice). Make a contact card template. Discuss home-alone rules ahead of time with other caregivers.

**Watch for:**
- Hanging up before the dispatcher says to. Stay on the line and answer questions; the dispatcher may give instructions.
- Thinking 911 is for any problem. It's for emergencies where life, health or property is in immediate danger.

### Day 1 (Monday): 911 calls
*Student:* Learn what dispatchers ask and practice a mock 911 call with a parent playing dispatcher.

*How to teach:* Learn what dispatchers ask: location, what happened, how many people, is the person breathing. Practice a mock call with you as dispatcher.

- **Q:** What is the most important information to give a 911 dispatcher?
  **A:** Your exact location/address, then what the emergency is.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Know your info
*Student:* Memorize your address and parents' numbers and set up an ICE contact.

*How to teach:* Memorize address and parents' numbers; set up an ICE contact. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is an ICE contact?
  **A:** An 'In Case of Emergency' contact saved in a phone so responders know whom to call.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Local resources
*Student:* Locate nearest fire station, hospital and police station on a map.

*How to teach:* Locate the nearest fire station, hospital and police station on a map. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why know where the nearest hospital is?
  **A:** So you can get help faster in an emergency.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Home alone safety
*Student:* Write home-alone rules with a parent.

*How to teach:* Write home-alone rules with a parent (don't open the door, don't tell callers you're alone, check-in times).

- **Q:** If someone knocks while you're home alone, what do you do?
  **A:** Don't open the door; follow family rules and call a parent if worried.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a mock-call rubric and contact card.

*How to teach:* Complete the mock-call rubric and contact card. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Should you hang up after giving your address?
  **A:** No — stay on the line until the dispatcher says you can hang up.

**Assessment:** Mock call rubric.

**Answer key:** Mock call rubric (✓ each): states address clearly, describes emergency, answers questions calmly, follows instructions, stays on the line. Contact card: address, parent numbers, ICE contact. Proficient = all.
