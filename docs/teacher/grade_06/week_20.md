# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 20

## English Language Arts 6: Speaker, tone and theme

**Prepare:** Gather: 'The Road Not Taken' by Robert Frost, 'Who Has Seen the Wind?' by Christina Rossetti, 'The Land of Counterpane' by Robert Louis Stevenson. Print 'The Road Not Taken', 'Who Has Seen the Wind?' and 'The Land of Counterpane'; choose a free-verse poem from CommonLit.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming the poet and speaker are the same person. The speaker is the voice in the poem.
- Reading 'The Road Not Taken' as simply 'be a nonconformist'. The speaker admits the roads were 'really about the same'.

### Day 1 (Monday): The speaker
*Student:* Read 'The Road Not Taken' and describe the speaker's situation and attitude with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read the Frost poem twice. Ask who is speaking and what situation they're in.

- **Q:** Describe the speaker's situation in 'The Road Not Taken'.
  **A:** Standing in a yellow wood where two roads split, the speaker must choose one and knows he probably won't come back to try the other.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Theme in Frost
*Student:* Debate the poem's theme (is it really about choosing the 'less traveled' road?) and write a theme paragraph.

*How to teach:* Point out lines saying the roads were 'worn… really about the same'; debate the theme. The child writes a theme paragraph.

- **Q:** Is the poem really about choosing the 'less traveled' road? Use evidence.
  **A:** Not simply: the speaker says the roads were worn 'really about the same,' and predicts he will tell the story 'with a sigh' 'ages and ages hence.' A theme: people later give their choices meaning when telling their life stories.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tone words
*Student:* Read Rossetti and Stevenson poems, choose precise tone words for each, and cite the lines that create that tone.

*How to teach:* Read Rossetti and Stevenson; choose tone words with line evidence.

- **Q:** Give a tone word for 'Who Has Seen the Wind?' and evidence.
  **A:** Gentle/wondering: we can't see the wind, but 'when the leaves hang trembling, / The wind is passing through.'
- **Q:** Give a tone word for 'The Land of Counterpane'.
  **A:** Cheerful/imaginative: a sick child plays on the bedclothes as 'the giant great and still / That sits upon the pillow-hill.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Free verse
*Student:* Read a free-verse poem from CommonLit and compare its structure with Frost's rhymed stanzas.

*How to teach:* Compare a free-verse poem's structure with Frost's rhymed five-line stanzas.

- **Q:** What is the rhyme scheme of each stanza of 'The Road Not Taken'?
  **A:** ABAAB, in four five-line stanzas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Analyze an unseen poem independently for speaker, tone and theme as a quiz.

*How to teach:* The child analyzes an unseen poem independently.

- **Q:** What is the speaker of a poem?
  **A:** The voice telling the poem, which may not be the poet.

**Assessment:** Independent poetry analysis.

**Answer key:** Independent analysis rubric (any unseen poem): 4 = identifies speaker and situation, names precise tone words with lines that create them, states a theme as a sentence with evidence; 3 = two of three strong; 2 = mostly summary; 1 = off-topic.

## Mathematics 6: Variables and writing expressions

**Prepare:** Gather: index cards. Make index cards: 8 phrases, 8 matching expressions and 8 tape diagrams for the Day 3 game.

**Watch for:**
- Reversing subtraction: '7 less than y' is y − 7, not 7 − y. Test with a number: 7 less than 10 is 3.
- Reading 3x as 'three plus x'. 3x means 3 times x.

### Day 1 (Monday): Letters for numbers
*Student:* Write expressions for situations such as 'x more than 5' and 'a number divided by 3'; translate 15 phrases into expressions.

*How to teach:* Model translating with a real number first ('5 more than 3' → 3 + 5) then a variable. The child translates 15 phrases.

- **Q:** Write expressions: 'x more than 5', 'a number divided by 3', '7 less than y'.
  **A:** 5 + x; n ÷ 3 (n/3); y − 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Parts of expressions
*Student:* Label terms, coefficients and constants in 10 expressions and describe 2(x + 5) as 'a product of two factors'.

*How to teach:* Label the terms, coefficients and constants in 3x + 5y + 8 together; then the child labels 10 expressions.

- **Q:** In 3x + 5y + 8, name the terms, coefficients and constant.
  **A:** Terms: 3x, 5y, 8. Coefficients: 3 and 5. Constant: 8.
- **Q:** Describe 2(x + 5) in words.
  **A:** The product of 2 and the sum of x and 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Expression match
*Student:* Play a card-matching game linking verbal descriptions, expressions and tape diagrams.

*How to teach:* Play memory or matching with the cards. When a match is made, the child explains why it fits.

- **Q:** Which matches 'add 1 to a number, then divide by 4': n/4 + 1 or (n + 1)/4?
  **A:** (n + 1)/4 — the parentheses show the adding happens first.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluating expressions
*Student:* Evaluate 12 expressions for given variable values, e.g., 3x + 4 when x = 2.5.

*How to teach:* Model substituting: replace the letter with the number in parentheses, then compute.

- **Q:** Evaluate 3x + 4 when x = 2.5.
  **A:** 11.5.
- **Q:** Evaluate 5(y − 2) when y = 6.
  **A:** 20.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing, reading and evaluating expressions.

*How to teach:* Give the expression quiz.

- **Q:** Write an expression for the cost of t tickets at $12 each plus a $5 fee.
  **A:** 12t + 5.

**Assessment:** Expression quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 8 more than n → n + 8 2) the product of 6 and a number → 6n 3) a number divided by 5 → n/5 4) 3 less than twice x → 2x − 3 5) In 4x + 9: coefficient 4, constant 9, terms 4x and 9 6) 3x + 4 when x = 2.5 → 11.5 7) 10 − 2y when y = 3 → 4 8) (m + 6)/2 when m = 8 → 7 9) cost of t tickets at $12 plus a $5 fee → 12t + 5.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Gravity

**Prepare:** Gather: PhET Gravity and Orbits simulation, ball on a string, objects of different masses. Bookmark PhET Gravity and Orbits. Swing the ball outdoors away from people.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster. Without air resistance, all objects fall at the same rate; air resistance slows light, spread-out objects.
- Thinking mass changes on other planets. Mass stays the same; weight changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Falling objects
*Student:* Drop objects of different masses (a book and a crumpled paper) and test whether they fall at the same rate.

*How to teach:* Drop a book and a flat sheet, then a book and a crumpled ball of paper, from the same height.

- **Q:** Why do the book and crumpled paper land together, but a flat sheet falls slower?
  **A:** Gravity accelerates both equally; the flat sheet has more air resistance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mass vs. weight
*Student:* Calculate your weight on the Moon, Mars and Jupiter using gravity ratios and explain why mass stays the same.

*How to teach:* Use ratios (Moon ≈ 0.17, Mars ≈ 0.38, Jupiter ≈ 2.5) to compute weights.

- **Q:** An 80-lb student weighs about how much on the Moon and on Mars?
  **A:** About 13 lb on the Moon and about 30 lb on Mars; mass is unchanged.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Orbits simulation
*Student:* Use PhET Gravity and Orbits to change the Sun's and Earth's masses and record effects on the orbit.

*How to teach:* Change masses in PhET; record orbit changes.

- **Q:** What happens to Earth's orbit if the Sun's mass increases?
  **A:** Stronger gravity pulls Earth into a tighter (smaller) orbit.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ball on a string
*Student:* Swing a ball on a string to model an orbit and explain what happens when the string (gravity) is removed.

*How to teach:* Swing the ball, then let go of the string (safely).

- **Q:** What happens when you let go, and what does it model?
  **A:** The ball flies off in a straight line — like a planet if gravity disappeared.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an argument with evidence that gravitational force depends on mass and distance.

*How to teach:* The child writes the argument.

- **Q:** What two factors determine gravitational force?
  **A:** The masses of the objects and the distance between them.

**Assessment:** Written argument.

**Answer key:** Argument rubric — 4: claim that gravitational force increases with greater mass and decreases with greater distance; evidence from the PhET simulation (doubling the Sun's mass pulls Earth into a tighter, faster orbit; removing gravity sends Earth off in a straight line) and planet weight calculations; reasoning connecting evidence; 3: evidence thin; 2: claim only; 1: incorrect.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Greek myths, literature and the arts

**Prepare:** Gather: Aesop's Fables (Project Gutenberg), photos of Doric, Ionic and Corinthian columns. Download Aesop's Fables; walk your neighborhood or look online for columns.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Olympics honored athletes alone. They honored Zeus.
- Mixing up column styles.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gods and daily life
*Student:* Read about the Olympian gods, festivals and the Olympic Games and explain how religion shaped Greek life.

*How to teach:* Read about gods and festivals.

- **Q:** Whom did the ancient Olympic Games honor, and when did they begin?
  **A:** Zeus; traditionally 776 BCE at Olympia.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Aesop's Fables
*Student:* Read five of Aesop's Fables and write the moral of each, then write your own fable.

*How to teach:* Read five fables; write morals and a new fable.

- **Q:** What is the moral of 'The Tortoise and the Hare'?
  **A:** Slow and steady wins the race.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Greek theater
*Student:* Learn the parts of a Greek theater, tragedy vs. comedy, and make a Greek drama mask.

*How to teach:* Learn theater parts; make a mask.

- **Q:** What is the difference between tragedy and comedy?
  **A:** Tragedy shows a hero's downfall; comedy is humorous with a happy ending.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Columns hunt
*Student:* Identify Doric, Ionic and Corinthian columns and find examples on buildings in your community (courthouses, banks).

*How to teach:* Identify columns.

- **Q:** How can you tell Doric, Ionic and Corinthian columns apart?
  **A:** Doric — plain top; Ionic — scroll-shaped top; Corinthian — decorated with leaves.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* List 10 modern words, brands or symbols from Greek mythology and explain their origins.

*How to teach:* List 10 Greek-origin words/brands.

- **Q:** Where does the brand name Nike come from?
  **A:** Nike, the Greek goddess of victory.

**Assessment:** Fable and Greek legacy list.

**Answer key:** Fable rubric — 4: animal characters, brief plot, clear stated moral; 3: moral unclear; 2: no moral; 1: incomplete. Legacy list examples: Nike (goddess of victory), marathon (battle of Marathon), Olympics, panic (Pan), echo, Atlas (maps), Amazon, Midas touch, Achilles' heel/tendon, Pandora, Trident, Hercules, Apollo program.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Form in music

**Prepare:** Find recordings of Mozart's 'Twelve Variations on Ah vous dirai-je, Maman' (K. 265) and Beethoven's 'Für Elise.' Cut colored shapes for sections.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every return of A must be identical. Returning sections may vary slightly.
- Confusing theme and variations with rondo. Variations change one theme repeatedly; rondo alternates a returning A with new sections.

### Day 1 (Monday): AB and ABA
*Student:* Listen to songs and label AB and ABA sections using shapes and colors.

*How to teach:* Label AB and ABA sections in songs. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is ternary form?
  **A:** ABA — A, contrasting B, A returns.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rondo
*Student:* Map the ABACA form of Beethoven's 'Fur Elise'.

*How to teach:* Map Für Elise as ABACA. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Which section of 'Für Elise' keeps returning?
  **A:** The famous opening A theme.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Theme and variations
*Student:* Listen to Mozart's 'Twelve Variations on Ah vous dirai-je, Maman' and describe how three variations change the theme.

*How to teach:* Listen to Mozart's variations; describe three. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name two ways Mozart varies the 'Twinkle' theme.
  **A:** Adding fast running notes, changing to minor, changing rhythm, moving the melody to the left hand.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Choose two contrasting pieces you like and compare their form, purpose and mood in writing.

*How to teach:* Compare form, purpose and mood of two pieces you like. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why does form matter in music?
  **A:** It organizes the music, creating repetition and contrast so listeners can follow it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a listening quiz on musical forms.

*How to teach:* Listening quiz on forms. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** What is binary form?
  **A:** AB — two contrasting sections.

**Assessment:** Form listening quiz.

**Answer key:** Form quiz: identifies AB, ABA, rondo and theme and variations in examples. Proficient = 80%. Written comparison addresses form, purpose and mood with specific details.

## Physical Education 6: Batting

**Prepare:** Gather: bat or foam bat, tee or cone, whiffle balls. Use foam or plastic bats and whiffle balls; keep others well back from the swing area. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Taking eyes off the ball. Watch it to the bat.
- Hands apart on the grip. Hands together, knuckles lined up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stance and grip
*Student:* Learn batting stance and grip and practice dry swings.

*How to teach:* Teach stance and grip; dry swings.

- **Q:** How should hands grip a bat?
  **A:** Hands together, dominant hand on top, knuckles lined up.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tee hitting
*Student:* Hit 30 whiffle balls off a tee aiming for line drives.

*How to teach:* Tee hitting for line drives.

- **Q:** What swing path makes line drives?
  **A:** A level swing through the ball.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Soft toss
*Student:* Hit soft-tossed balls from a partner.

*How to teach:* Soft-toss hitting.

- **Q:** Where should your eyes be at contact?
  **A:** On the ball.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Placement
*Student:* Practice hitting to left, center and right field targets.

*How to teach:* Hit to field targets.

- **Q:** How do you hit to the opposite field?
  **A:** Let the ball travel deeper and contact it later.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Hit 10 pitched balls and record contacts.

*How to teach:* Contact check.

- **Q:** Why rotate the hips?
  **A:** They generate power for the swing.

**Assessment:** Batting contact check.

**Answer key:** Contact check: 10 pitched balls, record contacts (5+ proficient). Cues: knocking knuckles grip, side stance, eyes on the ball, step and swing level, rotate hips, follow through.

## Health 6: Pedestrian, bike and passenger safety

**Prepare:** Gather a bicycle, helmet, and a neighborhood map. California law requires helmets for bicyclists, skateboarders and scooter riders under 18. Cyclists ride with traffic, obey signs and signals, and use lights at night.

**Watch for:**
- Riding against traffic to see cars. Ride in the same direction as traffic so drivers expect you.
- Thinking headphones are fine while walking near traffic. They block warning sounds like horns and sirens.

### Day 1 (Monday): Road rules
*Student:* Learn CA rules for cyclists and pedestrians, including helmet law under 18.

*How to teach:* Learn CA rules for cyclists and pedestrians. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Which direction should cyclists ride?
  **A:** With traffic, on the right side of the road.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bike check
*Student:* Do an ABC quick check (air, brakes, chain) and ride a safe route with a parent.

*How to teach:* Do the ABC quick check (Air, Brakes, Chain) and ride a safe route with a parent. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does the ABC check stand for?
  **A:** Air (tire pressure), Brakes (working), Chain (oiled, on the gears).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distractions
*Student:* Discuss phone and headphone distraction while walking or riding.

*How to teach:* Discuss phone and headphone distraction. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why is texting while walking dangerous?
  **A:** You don't see cars, curbs or obstacles and react more slowly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Risk influences
*Student:* Analyze stunt videos and peer dares that encourage risky behavior.

*How to teach:* Analyze stunt videos and peer dares. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why do stunt videos encourage risky behavior?
  **A:** They show only successes, not injuries, and reward attention with views.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Map a safe route to a local destination and explain hazards.

*How to teach:* Map a safe route and explain hazards. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name two hazards on a route and how to handle them.
  **A:** Busy intersections (use crosswalks/signals), driveways (watch for backing cars), no sidewalk (walk facing traffic).

**Assessment:** Safe route map.

**Answer key:** Safe route map: marked route, crosswalks/signals used, at least three hazards labeled with safety strategies. Proficient = all three hazards with strategies.
