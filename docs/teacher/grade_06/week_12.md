# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 12

## English Language Arts 6: The Odyssey, part 2: homecoming

**Prepare:** Gather: The Children's Homer, audio or animated clip of the Sirens episode. Find a short audio or animated retelling of the Sirens episode (preview for scariness).

**Watch for:**
- Writing a theme as a topic ('loyalty'). Make it a statement: 'True loyalty endures through long separation.'
- Assuming a film version is 'the same story'. List specifically what was added, cut or changed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis
*Student:* Read the Sirens and Scylla episodes and discuss what they suggest about temptation and leadership.

*How to teach:* Read Sirens and Scylla. Discuss temptation and the hard choices of a leader.

- **Q:** How does Odysseus get past the Sirens?
  **A:** He plugs the crew's ears with wax and has himself tied to the mast so he can hear but not steer toward them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Return to Ithaca
*Student:* Read Odysseus's return in disguise and trace how Penelope tests him.

*How to teach:* Read the return to Ithaca and Penelope's test.

- **Q:** How does Penelope test Odysseus?
  **A:** She orders the bed moved; he angrily says it can't be, since he built it around a living olive tree — a secret only he would know.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Themes across the epic
*Student:* Write two theme statements for the Odyssey (e.g., about loyalty or pride) with evidence.

*How to teach:* The child writes two theme statements with evidence.

- **Q:** Write a theme about loyalty in the Odyssey.
  **A:** Example: 'True loyalty lasts through long hardship' — Penelope waits twenty years and refuses the suitors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Text vs. media
*Student:* Watch or listen to a retelling of the Sirens episode and compare it with the text in a chart of what was added, cut or changed.

*How to teach:* Watch/listen to the retelling; complete the added/cut/changed chart.

- **Q:** Why might a film add or change details?
  **A:** To make it more visual or dramatic, fit time limits or suit its audience.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a comparison of how a Greek myth and the Odyssey both treat pride (RL.6.9) and take the Odyssey quiz.

*How to teach:* The child writes the comparison and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** What is Charybdis?
  **A:** A whirlpool that swallows the sea.

**Assessment:** Comparison response and quiz.

**Answer key:** Comparison rubric: 4 = explains how both texts treat pride (e.g., Midas's pride/greed or Arachne's boasting is punished; Odysseus's boasting to the Cyclops prolongs his journey) with evidence from each and a shared theme; 3 = both texts discussed but thin; 2 = one text; 1 = off-topic. Quiz: 1) crew's ears stopped with wax; Odysseus tied to the mast 2) Scylla is a six-headed monster, Charybdis a whirlpool 3) Odysseus returns disguised as a beggar 4) he strings the great bow and shoots through the axes 5) Penelope tests him about their bed, which he built from a living olive tree, so it cannot be moved.

## Mathematics 6: Finding the whole and percent problems

**Prepare:** Gather: phone or tablet battery indicator, nutrition labels. Pick 2–3 food packages whose labels list total carbohydrates and sugars.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying the part by the percent instead of finding the whole. Use a tape: if 30% is 12, then 10% is 4 and 100% is 40.
- Writing 'what percent' answers as a fraction or decimal without converting to per 100.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finding the whole
*Student:* Solve problems like '12 is 30% of what number?' with tape diagrams and tables (10 problems).

*How to teach:* Model '12 is 30% of what?' with a tape of 10 boxes (10% each): 3 boxes = 12, so 1 box = 4 and 10 boxes = 40. The child solves 10 problems.

- **Q:** 12 is 30% of what number?
  **A:** 40.
- **Q:** 45 is 75% of what number?
  **A:** 60 (25% = 15, so 100% = 60).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): What percent?
*Student:* Find what percent one quantity is of another using nutrition labels (e.g., grams of sugar out of total carbohydrates).

*How to teach:* Pick a label and find, e.g., sugar as a percent of total carbohydrates: part ÷ whole × 100.

- **Q:** A snack has 9 g sugar out of 36 g carbohydrates. What percent is sugar?
  **A:** 25%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Battery and progress bars
*Student:* Use a device battery percentage and download bars to solve find-the-part, find-the-percent and find-the-whole problems.

*How to teach:* Use the device: 'If 40% battery lasts 8 hours, how long would 100% last?' and download bars 'how many MB at 25%?'

- **Q:** A phone at 40% has 8 hours left. How long would a full charge last at that rate?
  **A:** 20 hours.
- **Q:** A 600 MB download is 25% done. How many MB have downloaded?
  **A:** 150 MB.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of unit rates, conversions, speed and percents.

*How to teach:* Assign the mixed review and check the child labels units on every answer.

- **Q:** A runner goes 3 miles in 27 minutes. Minutes per mile?
  **A:** 9 minutes per mile.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 end-of-unit assessment and correct missed problems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test; the child corrects missed problems.

- **Q:** 18 is what percent of 72?
  **A:** 25%.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 end-of-unit assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample key: 1) 12 is 30% of 40 2) 45 is 75% of 60 3) 9 is what percent of 36? 25% 4) 5 lb for $7.50 → $1.50/lb 5) 6 ft = 72 in 6) 300 miles in 5 hours = 60 mph; 210 miles at that speed takes 3.5 h 7) 40% of 85 = 34 8) a $60 jacket at 25% off = $45 9) 16 oz for $4 vs 24 oz for $5.52: $0.25 vs $0.23 per oz → 24 oz better.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Forecasting and severe weather

**Prepare:** Gather: weather log, National Weather Service website. Bookmark weather.gov and a satellite viewer (NOAA GOES).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking forecasts are guesses. They use data and patterns, though they give probabilities.
- Thinking atmospheric rivers are rivers on land. They're bands of water vapor in the sky.

### Day 1 (Monday): Make a forecast
*Student:* Use your barometer, cloud and wind observations to forecast tomorrow's weather and check the next day.

*How to teach:* Forecast using the barometer and clouds; check tomorrow.

- **Q:** Falling pressure and thickening clouds suggest what?
  **A:** Rain or a storm likely soon.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Thunderstorms
*Student:* Diagram the stages of a thunderstorm and explain the roles of rising warm air and condensation.

*How to teach:* Diagram thunderstorm stages.

- **Q:** Name the three stages of a thunderstorm.
  **A:** Cumulus (developing, updrafts), mature (updrafts and downdrafts, heavy rain, lightning), dissipating (downdrafts).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Atmospheric rivers
*Student:* Read about atmospheric rivers that bring California winter storms and trace one on a satellite image.

*How to teach:* Read about atmospheric rivers; trace one on satellite imagery.

- **Q:** What is an atmospheric river?
  **A:** A long, narrow band of water vapor in the atmosphere that can bring heavy rain and snow when it reaches land.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Weather report
*Student:* Write and record a 2-minute TV-style weather report for your town using real data.

*How to teach:* The child writes and records a 2-minute report.

- **Q:** What data should a weather report include?
  **A:** Temperature, precipitation chance, wind, pressure trend and a forecast.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on water cycle, air pressure, air masses and forecasting.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** What drives a thunderstorm's growth?
  **A:** Rising warm, moist air that cools and condenses, releasing energy.

**Assessment:** Weather report and Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample: water cycle processes; air pressure decreases with altitude; jar-barometer reading; dew point; cloud types; air mass types; front symbols; thunderstorm stages (cumulus, mature, dissipating); atmospheric rivers carry moisture from the tropics and can bring a large share of California's yearly rain and snow. Weather report rubric — 4: real data (temperature, pressure, wind, humidity), map/front explanation, forecast with reasoning, clear delivery; 3: minor gaps; 2: forecast without data; 1: incomplete.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Trade and the Kingdom of Kush

**Prepare:** Gather: map of Egypt and Kush, images of Meroe pyramids. Print a map of Egypt and Kush and images of Meroë's pyramids.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Kush was just a part of Egypt. It was a powerful independent kingdom that even ruled Egypt.
- Thinking all pyramids are in Egypt. Kush built more pyramids (at Meroë and Napata) than Egypt.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trade routes
*Student:* Map Egyptian trade with Punt, Nubia, the Levant and Crete, listing goods exchanged.

*How to teach:* Map trade with Punt, Nubia, the Levant and Crete.

- **Q:** What did Egypt get from Nubia?
  **A:** Gold, ivory, ebony and other goods.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Kush rises
*Student:* Read about Kush and its resources (gold, ivory, ebony) and its conquest of Egypt under Piye.

*How to teach:* Read about Kush and Piye.

- **Q:** Who was Piye?
  **A:** A king of Kush who conquered Egypt and founded the 25th Dynasty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Meroe
*Student:* Compare the pyramids of Meroe with Giza and explain Meroe's ironworking industry.

*How to teach:* Compare Meroë and Giza pyramids.

- **Q:** Why was Meroë a strong economic center?
  **A:** It had iron ore and wood for fuel, making it a major ironworking and trading center.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kush and Egypt
*Student:* Create a timeline showing changing relations between Egypt and Kush (trade, conquest, cultural borrowing).

*How to teach:* Make the Egypt–Kush timeline.

- **Q:** Give an example of cultural borrowing between Kush and Egypt.
  **A:** Kush built pyramids and worshipped Egyptian gods like Amun.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on Egypt and Kush.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** Where was Kush located?
  **A:** South of Egypt along the Nile, in Nubia (modern Sudan).

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample: Nile geography; Narmer; pharaoh; afterlife beliefs; Great Pyramid; Rosetta Stone; Hatshepsut; Ramses II; Kush in Nubia south of Egypt, rich in gold, ivory and ebony; Piye conquered Egypt (~728 BCE, 25th Dynasty); Meroë famous for ironworking; Meroë pyramids smaller and steeper than Giza.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Design for people: architecture

**Prepare:** Graph paper, cardboard, tape, scissors. Prepare interview questions for a family member (what activities, how many people, comfort, light, accessibility needs).

**Watch for:**
- Designing for yourself only. Good design starts with the user's needs.
- Forgetting accessibility. Spaces should work for people with different abilities.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interview a user
*Student:* Interview a family member about what they would need in a dream reading nook or community space.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about a reading nook or community space. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why interview users first?
  **A:** So the design solves their real needs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Floor plan
*Student:* Draw a floor plan to scale that meets those needs, including accessibility features.

*How to teach:* Draw a floor plan to scale with accessibility features. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Name two accessibility features.
  **A:** Ramps, wide doorways (at least 32 inches), lever handles, reachable shelves, good lighting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Perspective view
*Student:* Draw a perspective view of your design.

*How to teach:* Draw a perspective view of the design. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What does a scale like 1 square = 1 foot mean?
  **A:** Each square on the plan represents one foot in real life.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Model
*Student:* Build a small cardboard model of the space.

*How to teach:* Build a small cardboard model. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why build a model?
  **A:** To see the space in 3D and test the design.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Present your design and explain how it meets the user's needs.

*How to teach:* Present the design; explain how it meets needs. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is an elevation drawing?
  **A:** A flat side or front view of a building or wall.

**Assessment:** Design presentation.

**Answer key:** Design presentation: 4 = floor plan to scale, perspective view, model, clear link to user needs including accessibility; 3 = one element weak; 2 = several missing; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Basketball: passing and shooting

**Prepare:** Gather: basketball, wall target, hoop. Make a wall target with tape. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Shooting with two hands pushing from the chest. Use one shooting hand with guide hand on side.
- Jumping off the wrong foot on a layup. Right-hand layup: step right, jump off left.

### Day 1 (Monday): Passing
*Student:* Practice chest, bounce and overhead passes to a wall target, 20 each.

*How to teach:* Chest, bounce and overhead passes.

- **Q:** Where should a bounce pass hit the floor?
  **A:** About two-thirds of the way to the receiver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shooting form
*Student:* Practice form shooting close to the hoop using BEEF (balance, eyes, elbow, follow-through).

*How to teach:* Form shooting with BEEF.

- **Q:** What does BEEF stand for?
  **A:** Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Layups
*Student:* Practice right- and left-hand layups with the correct footwork.

*How to teach:* Layups both sides.

- **Q:** Footwork for a right-hand layup?
  **A:** Step right, then left, jump off the left foot and lift the right knee.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Video analysis
*Student:* Record your shot, compare it with the BEEF checklist and set a correction goal.

*How to teach:* Record and compare to BEEF.

- **Q:** Your shots keep falling short. What might help?
  **A:** More leg power (bend knees) and a full follow-through.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Shoot 10 shots from 3 spots and record your percentage.

*How to teach:* Shooting check.

- **Q:** You made 12 of 30 shots. Percentage?
  **A:** 40%.

**Assessment:** Shooting percentage and form checklist.

**Answer key:** Shooting check: 10 shots from 3 spots (30 total); percentage = makes ÷ 30 × 100 (e.g., 12/30 = 40%). Form checklist (BEEF): Balance, Eyes on the rim, Elbow under the ball, Follow-through.

## Health 6: Respecting differences

**Prepare:** Find picture books or articles about inclusion (e.g., 'Wonder' excerpt, 'The Day You Begin'). Gather 5 media examples showing stereotypes.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stereotypes are harmless if they're 'positive.' Any stereotype ignores individuals and can hurt.
- Thinking being 'nice' is enough. Inclusion means actively welcoming people.

### Day 1 (Monday): Identity map
*Student:* Create an identity map (culture, interests, family, abilities) and compare with a family member's.

*How to teach:* Create an identity map; compare with a family member's. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is identity?
  **A:** The qualities, background and beliefs that make you who you are.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stereotypes
*Student:* Identify stereotypes in 5 media examples and explain their harm.

*How to teach:* Identify stereotypes in 5 media examples; explain harm. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is a stereotype?
  **A:** An oversimplified belief that all members of a group are the same.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inclusion
*Student:* Read a story about exclusion and rewrite the ending to show inclusion.

*How to teach:* Read a story about exclusion; rewrite the ending to show inclusion. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What does inclusion look like?
  **A:** Inviting others, making room, listening and valuing differences.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Respect in action
*Student:* List ways to include people with disabilities, new students or different family structures.

*How to teach:* List ways to include people with disabilities, new students or different family structures.

- **Q:** How can you include a new student?
  **A:** Introduce yourself, invite them to sit with you, show them around.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on how respect supports mental health; complete a check.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph on how respect supports mental health; complete the check. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What's the difference between prejudice and discrimination?
  **A:** Prejudice is an unfair judgment or attitude; discrimination is unfair treatment or action.

**Assessment:** Paragraph and check.

**Answer key:** Check key: diversity, stereotype, prejudice (attitude), discrimination (action), inclusion. Paragraph rubric: 4 = explains how respect/inclusion improves belonging and mental health with examples; 3 = explanation without examples; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.
