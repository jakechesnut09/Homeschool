# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 30

## English Language Arts 6: The Secret Garden, chapters 8-15

**Prepare:** Gather: The Secret Garden. Keep reading; let the child prepare three discussion questions.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the omniscient narrator is Mary. The narrator stands outside and sometimes comments directly.
- Assuming Colin is simply sick. His fear and being treated as an invalid keep him weak.

### Day 1 (Monday): Inside the garden
*Student:* Read chapters 8-10 and note sensory images of the garden coming alive; compare to Mary's changes.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 8–10; collect sensory images of the garden.

- **Q:** How do the garden's changes mirror Mary's?
  **A:** As green shoots appear and she clears space, Mary grows stronger, healthier and kinder.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dickon
*Student:* Read chapters 11-12 and analyze Dickon's role and how others respond to him.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 11–12; analyze Dickon.

- **Q:** What is special about Dickon?
  **A:** He is gentle, cheerful and at ease with nature; animals trust him, and people feel happy around him.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Colin
*Student:* Read chapters 13-14 and compare Colin with Mary at the beginning of the book.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 13–14; compare Colin and early Mary.

- **Q:** How is Colin like Mary at the beginning?
  **A:** Both are spoiled, unloved, lonely children used to giving orders and having their way.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Narrator
*Student:* Read chapter 15; find places where the omniscient narrator comments directly, and discuss how that shapes our view.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 15; find narrator comments.

- **Q:** What does an omniscient narrator do?
  **A:** Knows all characters' thoughts and can comment on events directly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Book-club style discussion of three questions you prepared; quiz on chapters 8-15.

*How to teach:* Hold a book-club discussion; give the quiz.

- **Q:** What does Mary ask her uncle for?
  **A:** 'A bit of earth' to plant seeds in.

**Assessment:** Discussion rubric and quiz.

**Answer key:** Discussion rubric (4/3/2/1): prepared questions; cites text; listens and builds on others; respectful. Quiz: 1) Mary finds the door under the ivy 2) Dickon is Martha's brother who charms animals 3) Mary asks her uncle for 'a bit of earth' 4) Colin is Mr. Craven's son, who believes he will become a hunchback and die 5) Colin and Mary are alike: spoiled, lonely, demanding.

## Mathematics 6: Statistical questions and dot plots

**Prepare:** Gather: family survey sheet, graph paper. Write the survey question with your child before Day 2 and line up 20+ people to ask (family, friends, co-op, by phone).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any question with a number answer is statistical. It must expect variability across many answers.
- Spacing dot-plot values unevenly or skipping values with no data. The number line must be evenly scaled.

### Day 1 (Monday): Statistical questions
*Student:* Sort 12 questions as statistical or not (e.g., 'How old am I?' vs 'How old are students at my co-op?') and write 3 of your own.

*How to teach:* Contrast 'How old am I?' (one answer) with 'How old are students at my co-op?' (many varying answers). The child sorts 12 and writes 3.

- **Q:** Is 'How many pets does each family on our street have?' statistical?
  **A:** Yes — answers vary from family to family.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Collect data
*Student:* Survey 20 or more people (family, friends, co-op) with your statistical question and record results in a frequency table.

*How to teach:* The child surveys 20+ people and tallies a frequency table.

- **Q:** Why survey at least 20 people?
  **A:** More data gives a clearer picture of the variability and typical values.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dot plots
*Student:* Make a dot plot of your survey data and of a provided data set on daily screen time.

*How to teach:* Model a dot plot: an evenly scaled number line with one dot per response stacked above its value.

- **Q:** In a dot plot of screen time, a stack of 5 dots above 2 means what?
  **A:** Five people reported 2 hours of screen time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Describing distributions
*Student:* Describe the center, spread and shape (symmetric, skewed, clusters, gaps) of three dot plots in writing.

*How to teach:* Teach the vocabulary: center (typical value), spread (how far values range), shape (symmetric, skewed, clusters, gaps, outliers).

- **Q:** Describe 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 9.
  **A:** Center about 3; most values between 1 and 5; 9 is an outlier; skewed right.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on statistical questions and dot plots.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the survey dot plot.

- **Q:** Rewrite 'What is my favorite food?' as a statistical question.
  **A:** Example: 'What are the favorite foods of the people in my family?' (or any question expecting varied answers).

**Assessment:** Quiz plus survey dot plot.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 'How tall is the Eiffel Tower?' — not statistical (one answer) 2) 'How many hours do 6th graders sleep on school nights?' — statistical 3) For data 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 9: center about 3, most values from 1 to 5, 9 is an outlier, shape skewed right. Survey dot-plot rubric: 4 = statistical question, 20+ responses, frequency table, evenly scaled labeled dot plot, written description of center/spread/shape; 3 = one element weak; 2 = two missing; 1 = mostly incomplete.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Circulatory and respiratory systems

**Prepare:** Gather: stopwatch, plastic bottle, balloons, straw, clay. Find the pulse at the wrist or neck; count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the heart pumps blood straight to the body. Blood goes to the lungs and back first.
- Thinking lungs pull air in on their own. The diaphragm contracts and moves down to make room.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heart rate investigation
*Student:* Measure resting heart rate and heart rate after 1 minute of jumping jacks, three trials each, and graph.

*How to teach:* Measure resting heart rate and after 1 minute of jumping jacks, 3 trials.

- **Q:** You count 22 beats in 15 seconds. Beats per minute?
  **A:** 88 beats per minute.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Path of blood
*Student:* Trace the path of blood through the heart, lungs and body on a diagram.

*How to teach:* Trace blood through the heart.

- **Q:** Trace blood from the body back to the body.
  **A:** Right atrium → right ventricle → lungs (pick up oxygen) → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lung model
*Student:* Build a bottle-and-balloon lung model and explain how the diaphragm moves air.

*How to teach:* Build the bottle lung model.

- **Q:** What happens when the diaphragm moves down?
  **A:** Chest space increases, pressure drops and air rushes into the lungs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Systems interact
*Student:* Explain in writing how the circulatory and respiratory systems interact during exercise using your heart rate data.

*How to teach:* Write how the systems interact.

- **Q:** Why does heart rate rise during exercise?
  **A:** Muscles need more oxygen and must get rid of more CO₂.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER arguing that the body is a system of interacting subsystems using exercise data.

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER.

- **Q:** What gas do lungs remove from the body?
  **A:** Carbon dioxide.

**Assessment:** Heart rate graph and CER.

**Answer key:** Graph: resting vs. after-exercise heart rate (3 trials each). CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: during exercise muscles need more oxygen; breathing rate and heart rate rise so the respiratory system brings in oxygen and the circulatory system delivers it and removes CO₂.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Founding of Rome

**Prepare:** Gather: map of Italy, Livy's account of Romulus and Remus (excerpt). Print a map of Italy and Livy's account of Romulus and Remus.

**Watch for:**
- Treating legends as history. Romulus and Remus is a founding myth; archaeology shows small villages on the hills.
- Thinking Rome invented everything. It borrowed heavily from Etruscans and Greeks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Italy's geography
*Student:* Map the Italian peninsula, Tiber River, seven hills and Mediterranean location, and explain advantages for Rome.

*How to teach:* Map Italy, the Tiber and the seven hills.

- **Q:** Why was Rome's location good?
  **A:** Hills for defense, the Tiber for water and trade, and central position in the Mediterranean.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Legends
*Student:* Read Livy's account of Romulus and Remus and the Aeneas legend; separate legend from archaeological evidence.

*How to teach:* Read Livy; separate legend from evidence.

- **Q:** What part of the Romulus story is legend, and what does archaeology show?
  **A:** Twins raised by a she-wolf is legend; archaeology shows early villages on the Palatine Hill around the 700s BCE.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Etruscans and Greeks
*Student:* Identify what Rome borrowed from Etruscans and Greeks (arches, alphabet, gods).

*How to teach:* List borrowings.

- **Q:** What did Rome borrow from the Greeks?
  **A:** Gods (renamed), architecture, the alphabet (through the Etruscans), ideas in literature and philosophy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cincinnatus
*Student:* Read the story of Cincinnatus and explain the Roman value of civic duty; connect to George Washington.

*How to teach:* Read about Cincinnatus.

- **Q:** Why is Cincinnatus compared to George Washington?
  **A:** Both gave up power voluntarily and returned to their farms.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on early Rome and legend vs. history.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What river runs through Rome?
  **A:** The Tiber.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Tiber River and seven hills 2) central Mediterranean location aided trade 3) legend: Romulus and Remus raised by a she-wolf; Romulus founded Rome (753 BCE traditionally) 4) Aeneas fled Troy 5) Etruscans: arches, engineering 6) Greeks: gods, alphabet (via Etruscans), architecture 7) Cincinnatus left his farm to lead Rome, then gave up power.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Stop-motion animation: production

**Prepare:** Phone on a stand (or taped to a stack of books), stop-motion app, the set and characters. Use steady lamp light (not window light, which changes).

**Watch for:**
- Changing lighting between frames, causing flicker. Use constant lamp light and close curtains.
- Bumping the camera. Use a stand and a remote or timer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Test shots
*Student:* Shoot a 2-second test and adjust lighting and camera stability.

*How to teach:* Shoot a 2-second test; adjust lighting and stability. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Why shoot a test first?
  **A:** To fix lighting, focus and stability before the full shoot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shoot part 1
*Student:* Shoot the first half of the animation, moving figures in small increments.

*How to teach:* Shoot part 1, moving figures in small increments. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is easing in animation?
  **A:** Starting and stopping movements gradually with smaller moves for natural motion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoot part 2
*Student:* Shoot the second half.

*How to teach:* Shoot part 2. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What causes flicker?
  **A:** Changing light between frames.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit and sound
*Student:* Edit timing, add titles, credits and sound or music.

*How to teach:* Edit timing; add titles, credits and sound. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why add credits?
  **A:** To recognize everyone who helped and the music used.

### Day 5 (Friday): Screening
*Student:* Screen the animation for your family and reflect on what you would improve.

*How to teach:* Screen for family; reflect. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What would you improve next time?
  **A:** Answer varies; should be specific (e.g., smaller moves).

**Assessment:** Completed stop-motion animation.

**Answer key:** Animation rubric (1–4 each): smooth motion, clear story, stable camera/lighting, sound and credits. Proficient = 3+ each.

## Physical Education 6: Orienteering

**Prepare:** Gather: compass or phone compass, hand-drawn park map, control markers. Draw a yard/park map; place control markers (bandanas) before Days 3 and 5. Stay within sight of the child. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Holding the map in any direction. Orient it to north.
- Rushing without checking the map.

### Day 1 (Monday): Map reading
*Student:* Draw a map of your yard or a park with a legend and north arrow.

*How to teach:* Draw the map with legend and north arrow.

- **Q:** What is a map legend?
  **A:** A key explaining symbols.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compass
*Student:* Learn to take a bearing and walk to a target 20 paces away.

*How to teach:* Take a bearing; walk 20 paces.

- **Q:** How do you follow a bearing?
  **A:** Set the bearing, turn until the needle sits in the north arrow ('red in the shed'), walk toward the direction-of-travel arrow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Score-O
*Student:* Find as many control points as possible in 20 minutes on a parent-made course.

*How to teach:* Score-O for 20 minutes.

- **Q:** What is orienting the map?
  **A:** Turning the map so its north matches real north.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design a course
*Student:* Design an orienteering course for a family member.

*How to teach:* Design a course.

- **Q:** What should a course include?
  **A:** A start, clear controls in order, a map and a finish.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a timed orienteering course and reflect on navigation strategy.

*How to teach:* Timed course; reflect.

- **Q:** What is a 'handrail' in orienteering?
  **A:** A linear feature like a path or fence to follow.

**Assessment:** Course completion time.

**Answer key:** Course time recorded; all controls found; reflection on navigation (orienting the map, using landmarks, pacing distance).

## Health 6: Communicable diseases and the immune system

**Prepare:** Gather glitter (or flour), soap, a timer. Prepare a chart: bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking antibiotics cure colds and flu. Antibiotics kill bacteria, not viruses.
- Thinking a quick water rinse cleans hands. Scrub with soap for at least 20 seconds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pathogens
*Student:* Compare bacteria, viruses, fungi and protozoa and give a disease example for each.

*How to teach:* Compare pathogens and give examples. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Give a disease caused by a virus, a bacterium, a fungus and a protozoan.
  **A:** Virus: flu, cold, COVID-19; bacterium: strep throat; fungus: athlete's foot/ringworm; protozoan: malaria or giardia.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): How germs spread
*Student:* Model germ spread with glitter on hands and objects around the house.

*How to teach:* Model germ spread with glitter on hands and objects. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What did the glitter show?
  **A:** Germs spread easily from hands to objects and other people.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Handwashing test
*Student:* Test glitter removal with water only vs. 20-second soap wash and record results.

*How to teach:* Test glitter removal: water only vs. 20-second soap wash. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Why does soap work better than water alone?
  **A:** Soap breaks up oils and lifts germs off skin so they rinse away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Immune defenses
*Student:* Draw the body's lines of defense (skin, mucus, white blood cells, antibodies) and explain how vaccines work.

*How to teach:* Draw lines of defense: skin and mucus, white blood cells, antibodies; explain vaccines. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** How does a vaccine protect you?
  **A:** It shows the immune system a harmless form or piece of a germ, so the body makes antibodies and memory cells ready to fight the real germ.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a communicable disease quiz.

*How to teach:* Communicable disease quiz. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Are antibiotics effective against viruses?
  **A:** No — they only work on bacteria.

**Assessment:** Disease quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: four pathogen types with examples; spread by contact, droplets, contaminated food/water, vectors; handwashing 20 seconds; lines of defense; antibodies; vaccines; antibiotics only for bacteria. Proficient = 80%.
