# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 34

## English Language Arts 6: Drafting the research report

**Prepare:** Gather: note cards, computer. Keep note cards organized by subtopic.

**Watch for:**
- Copying sentences and changing a few words. That's still plagiarism; read, cover, write from memory, then cite.
- Using one source per section. Synthesize at least two.

### Day 1 (Monday): Outline
*Student:* Sort note cards into 3-4 subtopics and build an outline with headings.

*How to teach:* Sort cards into subtopics; build an outline with headings.

- **Q:** Why use headings in a report?
  **A:** They organize information and help readers find sections.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft sections
*Student:* Draft two sections, synthesizing information from at least two sources in each.

*How to teach:* The child drafts two sections synthesizing two sources each.

- **Q:** What does 'synthesize' mean?
  **A:** Combine information from several sources into one explanation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paraphrase practice
*Student:* Practice paraphrasing vs. plagiarism with three passages, then draft the remaining sections with in-text citations.

*How to teach:* Practice paraphrasing three passages; then draft the remaining sections with citations.

- **Q:** How do you avoid plagiarism?
  **A:** Use your own words and sentence structure, quote exact words in quotation marks, and cite every source.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Text features
*Student:* Add an introduction, conclusion and at least one graphic (map, diagram or timeline) with a caption.

*How to teach:* Add intro, conclusion and a captioned graphic.

- **Q:** What should a caption do?
  **A:** Explain what the graphic shows and how it relates to the text.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check-in
*Student:* Type the full draft (at least 3 pages in one sitting) and conference with a parent using the research rubric.

*How to teach:* Type the full draft; conference with the rubric.

- **Q:** What is an in-text citation?
  **A:** A short reference in the sentence or parentheses, e.g., (Smith), showing the source of the information.

**Assessment:** Complete typed draft.

**Answer key:** Draft check (research rubric): focused question answered; 3–4 sections with headings; each section uses 2+ sources with in-text citations; paraphrased; intro and conclusion; one captioned graphic; 3+ typed pages. 4 = all; 3 = one missing; 2 = several; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Statistics project

**Prepare:** Gather: survey or measurement tools, poster board or slides. Help the child choose a doable project question (e.g., 'How long do family members sleep?') and tools to collect 30 data points.

**Watch for:**
- Using mean/MAD for strongly skewed data or data with outliers; median/IQR is better there.
- Collecting too little data or a biased sample (only asking siblings).

### Day 1 (Monday): Mean or median?
*Student:* Decide for 6 data sets whether mean/MAD or median/IQR better describes them, based on shape and outliers.

*How to teach:* Show a symmetric set and a skewed set with an outlier; decide which measures fit each.

- **Q:** House prices in a neighborhood include one mansion. Mean or median?
  **A:** Median (and IQR), because the outlier pulls the mean.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan an investigation
*Student:* Write a statistical question, plan how to collect at least 30 data points, and begin collecting.

*How to teach:* The child writes the question, plans 30+ data points and starts collecting.

- **Q:** Is 'What is my dog's weight?' a good project question?
  **A:** No — one answer, no variability. Better: 'What do dogs at the park weigh?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Analyze
*Student:* Display your data with two plot types and calculate center and spread measures.

*How to teach:* The child makes two displays and computes center and spread.

- **Q:** Which two displays fit 30 measurements well?
  **A:** A dot plot or histogram plus a box plot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Create a poster or slides answering your question with data, and present it to your family.

*How to teach:* The child builds a poster or slides and presents; ask follow-up questions.

- **Q:** What should the conclusion include?
  **A:** An answer to the question using a center and spread value, and a note about shape or outliers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 end-of-unit assessment on data and distributions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** Mean and MAD of 2, 4, 4, 5, 10?
  **A:** Mean 5; MAD 2.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 assessment and statistics project rubric.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test sample key: 1) 'How many books did each student read this summer?' is statistical 2) mean of 2, 4, 4, 5, 10 = 5 3) MAD = (3 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 5) ÷ 5 = 2 4) median = 4 5) 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 → min 1, Q1 3, median 6, Q3 8.5, max 12, IQR 5.5 6) for skewed data use the median. Project rubric: 4 = statistical question, 30+ data points, two correct displays, correct center and spread, a conclusion that answers the question; 3 = minor calculation errors; 2 = one display or measure missing; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Solar oven: define and design

**Prepare:** Gather: pizza box, foil, black paper, plastic wrap, tape, newspaper. Get a pizza box, foil, black paper, plastic wrap.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking more foil always helps. Foil reflects light in; black paper absorbs; insulation keeps heat in.
- Skipping the decision matrix.

### Day 1 (Monday): The problem
*Student:* Research how solar ovens are used around the world and define criteria (reach 60 C) and constraints (materials, cost).

*How to teach:* Research solar ovens; define criteria and constraints.

- **Q:** Why are solar ovens useful in some places?
  **A:** They cook without fuel, saving wood and reducing smoke.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Science ideas
*Student:* List how reflection, absorption, insulation and the greenhouse effect could each help the oven.

*How to teach:* List how each science idea helps.

- **Q:** Why line the bottom with black paper?
  **A:** Black absorbs light, converting it to thermal energy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sketch designs
*Student:* Sketch three designs and evaluate them with a decision matrix.

*How to teach:* Sketch three designs; use a matrix.

- **Q:** Why does plastic wrap help?
  **A:** It traps warm air inside (greenhouse effect) and reduces convection losses.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build prototype
*Student:* Build the chosen solar oven from a pizza box and household materials.

*How to teach:* Build the prototype.

- **Q:** Why angle the reflector flap?
  **A:** To reflect more sunlight into the box.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check-in
*Student:* Write a design brief explaining your choices and predict the maximum temperature.

*How to teach:* Write the design brief; predict max temperature.

- **Q:** What does newspaper insulation do?
  **A:** Slows heat loss by conduction.

**Assessment:** Design brief and decision matrix.

**Answer key:** Design brief rubric — 4: problem, criteria (reach 60°C), constraints, science justification (reflection, absorption, insulation, greenhouse effect), chosen design with matrix scores, prediction with reasoning; 3: one gap; 2: no science reasoning; 1: incomplete.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Judea under Rome and the origins of Christianity

**Prepare:** Gather: Sermon on the Mount excerpt (Matthew 5-7), map of Paul's journeys. Print Matthew 5–7 excerpts and a map of Paul's journeys; present religious content historically and respectfully.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Christianity was immediately accepted. It was persecuted at times before becoming legal.
- Confusing the Edict of Milan with making Christianity official. Milan (313 CE) allowed it; Theodosius made it official (380 CE).

### Day 1 (Monday): Judea and Rome
*Student:* Read about Roman rule in Judea, the Jewish revolts, and Rome's ban on Jews living in Jerusalem; map Jewish communities around the Mediterranean.

*How to teach:* Read about Roman rule in Judea and the revolts.

- **Q:** What happened after the Jewish revolts?
  **A:** Rome destroyed the Temple (70 CE) and later banned Jews from Jerusalem after the Bar Kokhba revolt.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jesus of Nazareth
*Student:* Read the Sermon on the Mount and summarize key teachings of Jesus as described in the New Testament.

*How to teach:* Read the Sermon on the Mount.

- **Q:** Name two teachings from the Sermon on the Mount.
  **A:** Love your enemies; the Beatitudes ('Blessed are the meek'); the Golden Rule.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paul's journeys
*Student:* Map Paul's missionary journeys and explain his role in spreading Christian beliefs to non-Jews.

*How to teach:* Map Paul's journeys.

- **Q:** What was Paul's role?
  **A:** He traveled and wrote letters spreading Christianity, especially to non-Jews.

### Day 4 (Thursday): From persecution to official religion
*Student:* Create a timeline from early persecution to Constantine's Edict of Milan and Theodosius, and explain why Christianity spread.

*How to teach:* Make the timeline.

- **Q:** What was the Edict of Milan?
  **A:** Constantine's 313 CE decree allowing Christians to worship freely.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short explanation of three factors that helped Christianity spread through the Roman Empire.

*How to teach:* The child writes the explanation.

- **Q:** Which emperor made Christianity the official religion?
  **A:** Theodosius (380 CE).

**Assessment:** Explanation paragraph and map.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric — 4: clear topic sentence answering the question, at least two specific accurate pieces of evidence (named source, artifact or fact), explanation linking each to the claim, concluding sentence; 3: accurate but only one piece of evidence explained; 2: general statements without evidence; 1: inaccurate or off-topic. Expected factors: Roman roads and peace made travel easy; common languages (Greek, Latin); Paul's missionary journeys and letters; the message's appeal to the poor and enslaved; Constantine's support and the Edict of Milan; later official status under Theodosius.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Museums and curation

**Prepare:** Plan a local or virtual museum visit (Getty or LACMA online). Get graph paper for the display plan.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking museums show everything they own. Curators select and arrange works to tell stories.
- Thinking display order doesn't matter. Flow guides how viewers experience the show.

### Day 1 (Monday): Museum visit
*Student:* Visit a local or virtual museum (e.g., the Getty or LACMA online) and note how works are grouped and labeled.

*How to teach:* Visit a museum; note how works are grouped and labeled. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name two ways museums group artworks.
  **A:** By time period, culture, artist, theme or medium.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Community values
*Student:* Write how the museum's choices reflect its community's history and values.

*How to teach:* Write how the museum's choices reflect community values. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How can a museum reflect its community?
  **A:** By collecting and showing art from local cultures and histories.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Select works
*Student:* Select 8-10 of your works from the year across visual art and media arts.

*How to teach:* Select 8–10 works across visual and media arts. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What should guide your selection?
  **A:** Growth, variety, best quality and a theme.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Display plan
*Student:* Draw a display plan for your exhibition showing placement and flow.

*How to teach:* Draw a display plan with placement and flow. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why plan flow?
  **A:** To lead viewers through the exhibition in a meaningful order.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share the plan and revise based on feedback.

*How to teach:* Share the plan; revise based on feedback. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is a wall label?
  **A:** A small sign with title, artist, media, date and a short description.

**Assessment:** Museum reflection and display plan.

**Answer key:** Museum reflection: 4 = describes grouping/labels and connects choices to community values with examples; 3 = some; 2 = vague; 1 = missing. Display plan: 8–10 works placed with clear flow.

## Physical Education 6: Following the plan: week 3

**Prepare:** Gather: fitness log. Fitness log. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Sacrificing form for more push-ups.
- Ignoring pain. Stop if something hurts sharply.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cardio intervals
*Student:* Complete interval training with increased intensity.

*How to teach:* Intervals with increased intensity.

- **Q:** How can you increase intensity?
  **A:** Run faster, add hills, shorten rest.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength
*Student:* Complete strength workout and test max push-ups in 1 minute.

*How to teach:* Strength and 1-minute push-up test.

- **Q:** Why keep good form?
  **A:** It prevents injury and makes the test valid.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Team game
*Student:* Play a team game (ultimate or soccer) with family or co-op.

*How to teach:* Team game.

- **Q:** Name a fitness benefit of team games.
  **A:** Cardio endurance, agility, teamwork.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flexibility and yoga
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute yoga flow.

*How to teach:* 20-minute yoga flow.

- **Q:** What is a benefit of yoga?
  **A:** Flexibility, balance, strength and relaxation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Adjust
*Student:* Adjust next week's plan based on results and how you felt.

*How to teach:* Adjust the plan.

- **Q:** If a workout felt too easy, what do you change?
  **A:** Increase intensity, time or frequency a little.

**Assessment:** Plan adjustment notes.

**Answer key:** Plan adjustment notes: results (e.g., max push-ups in 1 minute), how workouts felt, and specific changes for next week (increase, keep or reduce F/I/T/T) with reasons.

## Health 6: Environmental and community health

**Prepare:** Bookmark AirNow.gov for daily AQI. Print a trash audit sheet (categories: recyclable, compostable, landfill). Find your county public health department website.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking environmental health is someone else's job. Individual and family choices affect air, water and waste.
- Thinking clear water is always safe. Unsafe water can look clear; public systems test it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Air quality
*Student:* Check the local AQI for a week and graph it.

*How to teach:* Check the local AQI daily for a week and graph it. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What color and number range is 'good' air quality?
  **A:** Green, 0–50.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water and noise
*Student:* Learn about safe drinking water and noise pollution in your community.

*How to teach:* Learn about safe drinking water (testing, boil-water notices) and noise pollution. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is a boil-water notice?
  **A:** An alert that tap water may be contaminated; boil it before drinking or cooking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trash audit
*Student:* Conduct a one-day household trash audit and plan reduce/reuse/recycle steps.

*How to teach:* Conduct a one-day household trash audit; plan reduce/reuse/recycle steps. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Which is most effective: reduce, reuse or recycle?
  **A:** Reduce — creating less waste in the first place.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Public health
*Student:* Research what your county public health department does.

*How to teach:* Research what your county public health department does. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name two services of a county public health department.
  **A:** Vaccination clinics, disease tracking, restaurant inspections, health education, emergency preparedness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Choose a community health issue for your advocacy project and justify it.

*How to teach:* Choose a community health issue for the advocacy project and justify it. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What makes a good advocacy topic?
  **A:** It affects many people, has a clear healthy action, and can be supported with facts.

**Assessment:** Project proposal.

**Answer key:** Project proposal: names a community health issue, explains why it matters with at least one fact, identifies an audience, and proposes a healthy action. Proficient = all four.
