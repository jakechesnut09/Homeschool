# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 26

## English Language Arts 6: Shakespeare's world and A Midsummer Night's Dream, Act 1-2

**Prepare:** Gather: 'Tales from Shakespeare' by Charles and Mary Lamb (Project Gutenberg), Folger Shakespeare text of A Midsummer Night's Dream (free online). Bookmark the Folger text of A Midsummer Night's Dream and the Lambs' retelling. Watch a short Globe Theatre video.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming 'thou/thee' is fancy talk. It was informal 'you' in Shakespeare's time.
- Confusing the four lovers. Keep a chart: Hermia ♥ Lysander; Demetrius wants Hermia; Helena ♥ Demetrius.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shakespeare's theater
*Student:* Learn about the Globe Theatre and Elizabethan stage; read the Lambs' prose retelling of the play for an overview.

*How to teach:* Show pictures of the Globe (open-air, groundlings, no sets, men played women's roles). Read the Lambs' retelling for an overview.

- **Q:** Name two features of the Globe Theatre.
  **A:** Open-air round theater; standing 'groundlings' near the stage; little scenery; all-male actors.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The lovers
*Student:* Read Act 1 Scene 1 in the original (with glosses); chart the four lovers and their conflicts.

*How to teach:* Read Act 1 Scene 1 with glosses; chart the lovers.

- **Q:** What is Hermia's conflict in Act 1 Scene 1?
  **A:** Her father Egeus orders her to marry Demetrius, but she loves Lysander; Athenian law threatens death or life in a convent if she disobeys, so she plans to run away with Lysander.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The mechanicals
*Student:* Read Act 1 Scene 2 aloud in parts and identify what makes the actors' rehearsal funny.

*How to teach:* Read Act 1 Scene 2 aloud in parts.

- **Q:** What makes the mechanicals' scene funny?
  **A:** Bottom wants to play every role (even the lion, promising to roar 'as gently as any sucking dove'), Flute doesn't want to play a woman because he has 'a beard coming,' and Snug worries he's 'slow of study.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): The fairies
*Student:* Read key speeches from Act 2 (Puck, Oberon) and paraphrase three passages into modern English.

*How to teach:* Read Puck's and Oberon's speeches; paraphrase three passages.

- **Q:** Paraphrase Puck's 'I am that merry wanderer of the night.'
  **A:** 'I'm the fun-loving spirit who roams around at night (playing tricks).'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a plotline web connecting the lovers, fairies and mechanicals; quiz on drama terms.

*How to teach:* The child completes the plot web and takes the terms quiz.

- **Q:** What are the three groups of characters?
  **A:** The lovers, the fairies and the mechanicals (amateur actors).

**Assessment:** Drama terms quiz and plot web.

**Answer key:** Drama terms quiz: act, scene, dialogue, stage directions, monologue/soliloquy, aside, cast of characters, comedy. Plot web: three groups — lovers (Hermia, Lysander, Helena, Demetrius), fairies (Oberon, Titania, Puck), mechanicals (Bottom, Quince, Flute, Snout, Snug, Starveling) — linked by the woods outside Athens and Theseus and Hippolyta's wedding. 4 = all connections; 3 = one missing; 2 = groups only; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Comparing and absolute value

**Prepare:** Gather: index cards with rational numbers. Write about 12 rational numbers (integers, fractions, decimals, positive and negative) on index cards.

**Watch for:**
- Ordering negatives by their size without the sign (−1.5 < −2). Use the number line: farther left is less.
- Thinking absolute value 'just makes things positive' without meaning. Stress it is distance from 0.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparing signed numbers
*Student:* Write inequality statements comparing pairs like -7 and -3, and explain them as positions on a number line.

*How to teach:* Place −7 and −3 on the number line and model reading '−7 < −3' as '−7 is to the left of −3'.

- **Q:** Compare −7 and −3 with < or >.
  **A:** −7 < −3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ordering
*Student:* Order sets of rational numbers from least to greatest using a card sort, including fractions and decimals.

*How to teach:* Shuffle the cards; the child orders them on the table, converting fractions to decimals when unsure.

- **Q:** Order 0.3, −2.5, 3/2, −½, 1 from least to greatest.
  **A:** −2.5, −½, 0.3, 1, 3/2.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Absolute value
*Student:* Find |x| for 12 numbers and interpret absolute value as distance, e.g., a debt of $30 means balance -30.

*How to teach:* Show |−8| as 8 steps from 0. Connect to debt: balance −30 means a debt of |−30| = $30.

- **Q:** Find |−8| and |3.5|.
  **A:** 8 and 3.5.
- **Q:** An account balance is −30 dollars. What is the size of the debt?
  **A:** $30 (|−30| = 30).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing statements
*Student:* Distinguish statements about order and about absolute value (e.g., -24 < -14 but |-24| > |-14|) in 8 contexts.

*How to teach:* Contrast 'which is less?' with 'which is farther from 0?' in contexts like debts and depths.

- **Q:** Is −24 < −14? Is |−24| > |−14|? Explain with debt.
  **A:** Both true: a balance of −24 is lower, and a debt of $24 is larger than a debt of $14.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on ordering and absolute value.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** True or false: |−9| < |4|.
  **A:** False; 9 > 4.

**Assessment:** Ordering and absolute value quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) −7 < −3 2) −1.5 > −2 3) order {2, −3.5, −¼, 0, −1} → −3.5, −1, −¼, 0, 2 4) |−12| = 12 5) |5| = 5 6) balances −40 and −25: −40 is the greater debt ($40) 7) |−9| < |4| is false (9 > 4) 8) −120 m is farther below sea level than −86 m.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Cell parts and their functions

**Prepare:** Gather: plastic bag, gelatin or cornstarch, small food items as organelles. Prepare gelatin (or cornstarch goo) in a bag; choose food items as organelles.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only plant cells have mitochondria. Both plant and animal cells do.
- Confusing the cell wall and membrane. Plants have both; animals only a membrane.

### Day 1 (Monday): Organelles
*Student:* Learn the function of each major organelle and complete a labeled diagram of an animal cell.

*How to teach:* Teach organelle functions; the child labels an animal cell.

- **Q:** What does the mitochondrion do?
  **A:** Releases energy from food (cellular respiration).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plant vs. animal
*Student:* Compare plant and animal cells in a Venn diagram and relate differences to how plants make food.

*How to teach:* Make a Venn diagram of plant vs. animal cells.

- **Q:** Which structures do plant cells have that animal cells lack?
  **A:** Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edible cell model
*Student:* Build a cell model in a bag of gelatin using food items for organelles, with a key explaining each choice.

*How to teach:* Build the edible model with a key.

- **Q:** Why is a grape a good model for a nucleus?
  **A:** Any reasoned answer: round, central and enclosed, like the nucleus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cell analogy
*Student:* Write a 'cell is like a city' analogy explaining the role of at least 7 organelles.

*How to teach:* Write a 'cell is like a city' analogy.

- **Q:** What would the cell membrane be in a city?
  **A:** The city border or gates controlling what comes in and out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on organelle functions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Which organelle carries out photosynthesis?
  **A:** The chloroplast.

**Assessment:** Cell model, analogy and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: nucleus — controls cell, holds DNA; cell membrane — controls what enters/leaves; cytoplasm — gel where organelles sit; mitochondria — release energy from food; ribosomes — make proteins; endoplasmic reticulum — transports materials; Golgi — packages and ships; vacuole — storage; lysosome — breaks down wastes; chloroplast (plant) — photosynthesis; cell wall (plant) — support. Model and analogy rubric: 7+ organelles with accurate functions and fitting analogies.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Geography and the Shang Dynasty

**Prepare:** Gather: map of China, images of oracle bones and Shang bronzes. Print a China map with deserts, mountains and rivers; find oracle bone images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking China was isolated by choice. Natural barriers limited contact.
- Thinking oracle bones were just decoration. They were used to ask ancestors questions — and are the earliest Chinese writing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Barriers and rivers
*Student:* Label China's natural barriers (Himalayas, Gobi, Taklamakan, Pacific) and the Huang He and Chang Jiang rivers.

*How to teach:* Label barriers and rivers.

- **Q:** How did geography isolate China?
  **A:** Mountains, deserts and the ocean surrounded it, limiting contact.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): River of sorrow
*Student:* Explain why the Huang He was called 'China's Sorrow' and how loess soil helped farming.

*How to teach:* Discuss 'China's Sorrow'.

- **Q:** Why was the Huang He called 'China's Sorrow'?
  **A:** Its floods killed many people and destroyed farms.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Oracle bones
*Student:* Study images of Shang oracle bones, write a question in the oracle-bone style, and connect them to Chinese writing.

*How to teach:* Study oracle bones; write a question.

- **Q:** How were oracle bones used?
  **A:** Questions were carved on bones or shells; heated until they cracked, and priests read the cracks as answers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shang bronzes
*Student:* Analyze Shang bronze vessels and explain what they reveal about technology and ancestor worship.

*How to teach:* Analyze bronzes.

- **Q:** What do Shang bronzes show?
  **A:** Advanced metalworking skill and the importance of ancestor rituals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Chinese geography and the Shang.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What is loess?
  **A:** Fine, fertile yellow soil.

**Assessment:** Map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Map: Himalayas, Gobi and Taklamakan deserts, Pacific Ocean, Huang He and Chang Jiang. Quiz: 1) barriers isolated China 2) Huang He called 'China's Sorrow' for deadly floods 3) loess = fertile yellow soil 4) Shang oracle bones were heated until they cracked and the cracks interpreted 5) bronze vessels used in ancestor rituals.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Rehearsal and performance

**Prepare:** Script, simple costumes and props, a video camera. Mark stage areas with tape (upstage = away from audience, downstage = toward audience).

**Watch for:**
- Turning your back to the audience. 'Cheat out' so the audience sees your face.
- Stopping when you forget a line. Stay in character and improvise or let a partner help.

### Day 1 (Monday): Blocking
*Student:* Block the play: mark entrances, exits and positions.

*How to teach:* Block entrances, exits and positions. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What is downstage?
  **A:** The part of the stage closest to the audience.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse with scripts, then off book for the first half.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with scripts, then off book for the first half. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does 'off book' mean?
  **A:** Performing without the script, from memory.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Hold a dress rehearsal with costumes and props.

*How to teach:* Dress rehearsal with costumes and props. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** Why have a dress rehearsal?
  **A:** To practice everything exactly like the performance and fix problems.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Performance
*Student:* Perform the play for an audience and record it.

*How to teach:* Perform for an audience and record it. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is a cue?
  **A:** A signal (a line, action or sound) for an actor or crew to do something.

### Day 5 (Friday): Response
*Student:* Watch the recording and write a critique of artistic choices using theatre vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Watch the recording; write a critique using theatre vocabulary. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name two theatre terms to use in a critique.
  **A:** Blocking, projection, objective, character, cue, tableau.

**Assessment:** Performance and written critique.

**Answer key:** Performance: memorized, projected, in character, follows blocking. Critique rubric: 4 = evaluates specific choices with theatre terms and evidence; 3 = some terms; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## Physical Education 6: Sprinting and relays

**Prepare:** Gather: stopwatch, cones, baton or paper towel tube. Mark 40 m and 50 m; use a paper towel tube as a baton. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging arms across the body. Drive arms forward and back.
- Looking back during a baton exchange. The outgoing runner looks ahead after starting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sprint form
*Student:* Practice sprint drills (A-skips, high knees, arm drive).

*How to teach:* Sprint drills.

- **Q:** What is correct sprint arm action?
  **A:** Arms bent about 90°, driving forward and back from the shoulders, not across the body.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Starts
*Student:* Practice standing and three-point starts and time 40-meter dashes.

*How to teach:* Standing and three-point starts; time 40 m dashes.

- **Q:** Why lean forward at the start?
  **A:** It helps accelerate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Baton exchange
*Student:* Practice upsweep baton exchanges in an exchange zone.

*How to teach:* Upsweep exchanges in the zone.

- **Q:** Describe the upsweep pass.
  **A:** The receiver's hand is back, palm down/open; the passer sweeps the baton up into it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relay races
*Student:* Run shuttle and 4x50 m relays.

*How to teach:* Shuttle and 4×50 m relays.

- **Q:** Where must the baton pass happen?
  **A:** Within the exchange zone.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Time a final 50 m sprint and compare with day 2.

*How to teach:* Time the 50 m.

- **Q:** What helps improve sprint time?
  **A:** Good starts, arm drive, high knees and relaxing the face and shoulders.

**Assessment:** Sprint times.

**Answer key:** Sprint times: Day 2 40 m and Day 5 50 m recorded; improvement noted with form cues (high knees, arm drive, forward lean at start).

## Health 6: Violence prevention

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards for warning signs and de-escalation. PARENT PREVIEW: this week discusses weapons and gangs; decide how to discuss any firearms in your home and how they are stored.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking violence is only physical. Verbal threats and emotional abuse are violence too.
- Thinking you should handle a weapon to make it safe. Never touch it — leave and tell an adult.

### Day 1 (Monday): Types of violence
*Student:* Discuss physical, verbal and emotional violence and their effects.

*How to teach:* Discuss physical, verbal and emotional violence and effects. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Give an example of emotional violence.
  **A:** Threats, constant insults, humiliation or controlling someone through fear.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Warning signs
*Student:* List warning signs of potential violence and why to tell an adult.

*How to teach:* List warning signs of potential violence (threats, talk of weapons, uncontrolled anger, posting threats online) and why to tell an adult.

- **Q:** If a classmate posts a threat online, what should you do?
  **A:** Take it seriously, screenshot it, and tell a trusted adult or school official right away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weapons
*Student:* Learn the rule if you see a gun: don't touch, leave, tell an adult.

*How to teach:* Learn the rule if you see a gun: don't touch, leave the area, tell an adult. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What three steps do you follow if you find a gun?
  **A:** Don't touch it, leave the area, tell an adult right away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gangs
*Student:* Read about gang recruitment and list positive alternatives.

*How to teach:* Read about gang recruitment (promises of belonging, protection, money) and list positive alternatives.

- **Q:** Name two positive alternatives to gangs.
  **A:** Sports teams, clubs, youth groups, volunteering, mentoring programs, jobs/arts programs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Role-play de-escalating a heated argument; complete a check.

*How to teach:* Role-play de-escalating a heated argument; complete the check. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Name two de-escalation strategies.
  **A:** Stay calm, speak softly, give space, listen, avoid insults, walk away and get an adult.

**Assessment:** Role-play and check.

**Answer key:** Role-play rubric: 4 = calm voice, gives space, listens, uses I-messages, walks away/gets help if needed; 3 = most; 2 = some; 1 = escalates. Check key: types of violence; warning signs; don't touch-leave-tell; gang alternatives.
