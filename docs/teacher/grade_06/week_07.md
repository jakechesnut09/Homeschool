# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 7

## English Language Arts 6: Hatchet chapters 10-13: mistakes and growth

**Prepare:** Gather: Hatchet, character-change chart. Parent preview (important): after the rescue plane passes without seeing him, Brian sinks into despair and attempts to take his own life before deciding to keep living (the 'new Brian'). Read this section first and be ready to talk about it; reassure your child and share where help is available (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline).

**Watch for:**
- Stating a theme as one word ('survival'). A theme is a full sentence about life: 'Hope and patience help people survive hardship.'
- Pronoun shifts ('If a person gets lost, you should…'). Keep the same person/number throughout.

### Day 1 (Monday): Foolbirds and fish
*Student:* Read chapters 10-11 and record what Brian learns from failure while hunting; connect to the idea of 'tough hope'.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 10–11. The child records each failure and what Brian learns.

- **Q:** What does 'tough hope' mean?
  **A:** Hope that is realistic and lasting — based on Brian's own skill and effort rather than wishing for rescue.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The plane passes
*Student:* Read chapter 12 and write a journal entry analyzing how the missed rescue plane is a turning point.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 12. Discuss the turning point gently; the child writes a journal entry analyzing it.

- **Q:** Why is the missed rescue plane a turning point?
  **A:** Brian's hope of quick rescue is crushed; after his lowest moment he decides to survive on his own and becomes tougher and more determined.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): New Brian
*Student:* Read chapter 13 and complete a 'Brian then vs. now' character-change chart with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 13. The child completes a then/now chart with evidence.

- **Q:** Give one difference between Brian at the start and the 'new Brian.'
  **A:** Then: panicked and helpless. Now: observant and patient, learning from mistakes, hunting with a bow and getting food on his own.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pronoun shifts
*Student:* Lesson on shifts in pronoun number and person; correct 12 sentences such as 'If a person gets lost, you should stay calm'.

*How to teach:* Teach pronoun shifts; the child corrects 12 sentences.

- **Q:** Correct: 'If a person gets lost, you should stay calm.'
  **A:** 'If a person gets lost, he or she should stay calm.' or 'If you get lost, you should stay calm.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draft a theme statement for Hatchet (not just a topic word) and support it with three details; quiz on chapters 10-13.

*How to teach:* The child drafts a theme statement with three details and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** Write a theme for Hatchet in one sentence.
  **A:** Example: 'Patience and learning from mistakes help people survive hardship.'

**Assessment:** Theme statement paragraph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Theme paragraph rubric: 4 = full-sentence theme (e.g., 'Learning from mistakes and holding on to hope can help a person survive'), three accurate supporting details (foolbird hunting failures, making a bow and fish spear, the 'tough hope' after the plane passes), explained; 3 = theme plus two details; 2 = topic word only; 1 = no support. Quiz (ch. 10–13): 1) foolbirds are hard to see because of camouflage; he learns to look for shapes 2) he makes a fish spear and later a bow 3) a search plane passes without seeing him 4) after despair, Brian becomes the 'new Brian' who has tough hope 5) he learns that mistakes are teachers.

## Mathematics 6: Tables of equivalent ratios

**Prepare:** Gather: graph paper, Desmos (optional). Desmos is optional for plotting on Day 2.

**Watch for:**
- Filling a table by adding across rows inconsistently. Check each row by asking 'what did I multiply by?'
- Comparing ratios by looking at only one number. Build a common row (same amount of one quantity) before comparing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ratio tables
*Student:* Convert three double number lines into ratio tables, and fill in missing values in 8 tables using multiplication and scaling.

*How to teach:* Turn a double number line into a two-column table. Show scaling up (×3) and down (÷4) in a table, then the child fills the 8 tables.

- **Q:** Flour : sugar is 4 : 3. How much sugar with 12 cups flour?
  **A:** 9 cups sugar (×3).
- **Q:** How much flour with 5 cups sugar?
  **A:** 6⅔ cups (each cup of sugar goes with 4/3 cup flour; 5 × 4/3 = 20/3).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tables and graphs
*Student:* Plot ratio-table pairs (e.g., cups of rice to cups of water) on the first quadrant and notice that the points line up through (0,0).

*How to teach:* Plot rice:water pairs (1,2), (2,4), (3,6) with rice on the x-axis. Ask what the points have in common; extend the line to (0,0).

- **Q:** Rice to water is 1:2. Name another point on the line and explain why it passes through (0,0).
  **A:** (5, 10), or any (x, 2x). Zero cups of rice needs zero cups of water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Which is faster?
*Student:* Compare two runners and two recipes using tables with a common row; justify which is faster or stronger in writing.

*How to teach:* Present two runners: A runs 400 m in 80 s, B runs 300 m in 50 s. Help the child scale both to a common row (same distance or 1 second), then write a justification.

- **Q:** Which runner is faster?
  **A:** B. A runs 5 m/s; B runs 6 m/s. (At 1,200 m: A takes 240 s, B 200 s.)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Problem solving
*Student:* Solve 6 multi-step ratio word problems from Illustrative Math Grade 6 Unit 2, showing a table for each.

*How to teach:* Let the child work the 6 multi-step problems, requiring a labeled table for each. Ask them to explain one table aloud.

- **Q:** Lemonade uses 3 cups lemon juice for every 5 cups water. With 20 cups water, how much juice and how much lemonade total?
  **A:** 12 cups juice; 32 cups total.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on completing tables, graphing ratio pairs and comparing ratios.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently, then check the graph for labeled axes and evenly spaced scales.

- **Q:** Fill in: 2:7 = 6:__ = __:35.
  **A:** 6:21 and 10:35.

**Assessment:** Quiz on ratio tables and graphs.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) table for 2:7 — 4:14, 6:21, 10:35, 1:3.5 2) points (2,7), (4,14), (6,21) lie on a line through (0,0) 3) Paint A: 3 cups blue to 5 cups yellow; Paint B: 4 cups blue to 6 cups yellow. Blue per cup of yellow: A = 0.6, B ≈ 0.67, so B is bluer 4) Runner who goes 100 m in 20 s (5 m/s) is slower than one who goes 150 m in 25 s (6 m/s) 5) 3 cups lemon juice per 5 cups water: 20 cups water → 12 cups juice.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Design challenge: keep it cold

**Prepare:** Gather: small boxes or cups, foil, cotton balls, bubble wrap, newspaper, ice cubes, kitchen scale. Pre-freeze same-size ice cubes; a kitchen scale reading grams is required.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking insulation 'adds cold'. It slows energy flow into the ice.
- Skipping the control cup. Without it you can't tell how much the device helped.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define the problem
*Student:* Define criteria (an ice cube loses the least mass in 60 minutes) and constraints (materials list, size) for an ice cube keeper.

*How to teach:* Define criteria (least mass lost in 60 min) and constraints.

- **Q:** What is the measurable criterion?
  **A:** The ice cube loses the least mass (grams) in 60 minutes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Design and build
*Student:* Sketch two designs, choose one using science ideas about conduction, convection and radiation, and build it.

*How to teach:* Sketch two designs; choose one using heat-transfer ideas; build.

- **Q:** How does foil help?
  **A:** Shiny foil reflects radiant energy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Weigh an ice cube, keep it in your device for 60 minutes alongside a control cup, then weigh the remaining ice.

*How to teach:* Weigh ice, test for 60 minutes beside a control, reweigh.

- **Q:** Ice started at 20 g and ended at 14 g. How much melted?
  **A:** 6 g.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Redesign
*Student:* Analyze which features worked, modify the design and retest.

*How to teach:* Analyze and redesign one feature; retest.

- **Q:** Why change only one feature?
  **A:** So you know which change caused any improvement.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present and check
*Student:* Present your data and final design, explaining how it reduces each type of heat transfer; take the unit test.

*How to teach:* Present; take the unit test.

- **Q:** Why do bubble wrap and cotton insulate well?
  **A:** They trap air, a poor conductor, and limit convection.

**Assessment:** Design challenge rubric and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Design rubric — 4: criteria/constraints stated; design justified with conduction (insulators like cotton/bubble wrap), convection (sealed air) and radiation (foil reflects); mass data for device and control; redesign explained by data; 3: minor gaps; 2: no control or redesign; 1: incomplete. Unit 2 test sample: temperature vs. thermal energy; heating curve plateaus; conduction/convection/radiation examples; energy flows hot to cold; water heats slower than sand; mass affects temperature change.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Writing and the Epic of Gilgamesh

**Prepare:** Gather: air-dry clay or play dough, chopstick or craft stick, excerpts of the Epic of Gilgamesh (Fordham Sourcebook). Print the Gilgamesh flood excerpt (Fordham Sourcebook); prepare clay.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cuneiform is an alphabet. It used hundreds of signs for words and syllables.
- Thinking writing began for literature. It began for record-keeping (trade and taxes).

### Day 1 (Monday): From tokens to writing
*Student:* Read how clay tokens and pictographs evolved into cuneiform and make a three-stage chart.

*How to teach:* Show tokens, pictographs and cuneiform; make the chart.

- **Q:** Why did writing first develop in Sumer?
  **A:** To keep records of goods, trade and taxes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write cuneiform
*Student:* Press your name and a short message in cuneiform wedges into a clay tablet using a stick.

*How to teach:* Press cuneiform into clay with a stick.

- **Q:** What was cuneiform written with?
  **A:** A reed stylus pressed into wet clay, making wedge shapes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scribe school
*Student:* Read about scribal schools and write a letter from a student scribe describing a day.

*How to teach:* Read about scribe schools; write the letter.

- **Q:** Why were scribes important?
  **A:** Few people could write; scribes kept records for temples, rulers and merchants.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gilgamesh
*Student:* Read the flood episode of the Epic of Gilgamesh and discuss what it reveals about Mesopotamian beliefs.

*How to teach:* Read the Gilgamesh flood episode.

- **Q:** What does the flood story suggest about Mesopotamian beliefs?
  **A:** The gods were powerful and unpredictable and could destroy humans; floods were a real fear.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing and short response on why writing mattered for government and trade.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and short response.

- **Q:** Why did writing matter for government?
  **A:** Rulers could record laws, collect taxes and keep track of resources.

**Assessment:** Clay tablet and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) tokens → pictographs → cuneiform (wedge-shaped marks) 2) written with a reed stylus on wet clay 3) scribes trained for years in schools (edubba, 'tablet house') 4) Gilgamesh was king of Uruk 5) in the flood story Utnapishtim builds a boat after a god warns him. Short response: writing let rulers record laws, taxes and trade, and keep records over time. Tablet: recognizable wedge marks.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Greek pottery

**Prepare:** Find black-figure and red-figure vases on the Metropolitan Museum website (e.g., works attributed to Exekias for black-figure; a red-figure krater). Gather orange and black paper, a white pencil, scissors, glue, and optional air-dry clay.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up black-figure and red-figure. Black-figure = black figures on orange clay; red-figure = orange figures on a black background.
- Thinking vases were only decorative. They had everyday uses: storing, mixing and drinking.

### Day 1 (Monday): Vase shapes
*Student:* Learn the shapes and uses of the amphora, krater and kylix and sketch each.

*How to teach:* Learn amphora (two-handled storage jar), krater (wide bowl for mixing wine and water), kylix (shallow drinking cup); sketch each.

- **Q:** What was a krater used for?
  **A:** Mixing wine with water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Black vs. red figure
*Student:* Compare black-figure and red-figure vases from the Met's collection and list differences.

*How to teach:* Compare black-figure and red-figure vases; list differences. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why did red-figure allow more detail?
  **A:** Artists painted details with a brush on the orange figures instead of scratching them into black slip.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design
*Student:* Design a vase showing a myth or a modern event with a Greek key border.

*How to teach:* Design a vase showing a myth or modern event with a Greek key border. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is a Greek key (meander)?
  **A:** A continuous border pattern of interlocking right-angle lines.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Create
*Student:* Create the vase in black-figure style with cut paper or on an air-dry clay pot.

*How to teach:* Create the vase in black-figure style with cut paper or on clay. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** In black-figure, how were details added?
  **A:** Scratched (incised) through the black to show the orange clay.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Present the story on your vase and evaluate your use of border patterns.

*How to teach:* Present the story and evaluate the border. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is a frieze?
  **A:** A horizontal band of decoration or figures.

**Assessment:** Greek vase project.

**Answer key:** Greek vase rubric: 4 = correct vase shape, clear narrative scene in black-figure style, neat Greek key border, explanation of story; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = style or border missing; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Soccer: dribbling and passing

**Prepare:** Gather: soccer ball, cones. Set up a cone course and a 1 m cone gate. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking with the toe. Pass with the inside of the foot.
- Looking only at the ball. Look up between touches.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dribbling
*Student:* Dribble through a cone course using inside, outside and sole touches.

*How to teach:* Dribble the course with small touches.

- **Q:** Name three parts of the foot used to dribble.
  **A:** Inside, outside and sole.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Passing
*Student:* Practice inside-foot passes with a partner or against a wall at 5 and 10 meters.

*How to teach:* Pass inside-foot at 5 and 10 m.

- **Q:** Cues for an inside-foot pass?
  **A:** Plant foot beside the ball pointing at the target, ankle locked, strike with the inside of the foot, follow through.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Receiving
*Student:* Trap balls with the inside of the foot and thigh, then pass in one motion.

*How to teach:* Trap with inside foot and thigh, then pass.

- **Q:** How do you 'cushion' a ball when receiving?
  **A:** Give with the foot or thigh as the ball arrives to absorb its speed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Small-sided game
*Student:* Play 2v1 keep-away to practice passing into open space.

*How to teach:* Play 2v1 keep-away.

- **Q:** Where should the receiver move?
  **A:** Into open space away from the defender, creating a passing lane.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Complete a skill check: 10 passes through a 1-meter cone gate and a timed dribble course.

*How to teach:* Run the skill check.

- **Q:** Why keep the ball close when dribbling?
  **A:** So you keep control and defenders can't steal it.

**Assessment:** Soccer skill check.

**Answer key:** Skill check: 10 inside-foot passes through a 1 m gate (score successes; 7+ = proficient) and a timed dribble course with close control (ball within about 1 m) using inside, outside and sole touches.

## Health 6: Media and social media influences on emotions

**Prepare:** Print a screen-time and mood log (time, app, minutes, mood before/after). Gather magazines or screenshots showing filtered images. Read about how recommendation algorithms work (Common Sense Media has teen-friendly explanations). Find the AAP Family Media Plan tool (healthychildren.org). PARENT PREVIEW: read the materials first; if your child shares feelings that worry you (hopelessness, self-harm, not wanting to live), stay calm, listen, and contact your pediatrician or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking social media shows real life. Posts are curated highlights, often edited or filtered.
- Thinking you're in full control of how long you scroll. Apps are designed (infinite scroll, autoplay, notifications) to keep you using them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparison trap
*Student:* Discuss how curated posts lead to comparison and list three edited-reality examples.

*How to teach:* Discuss curated posts and the comparison trap; your child lists three edited-reality examples (filters, posed photos, only showing highlights).

- **Q:** Why can scrolling social media make people feel worse about themselves?
  **A:** They compare their real everyday life to others' edited highlight reels.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Screen-time log
*Student:* Log screen use and mood for two days and look for patterns.

*How to teach:* Your child logs screen use and mood for two days and looks for patterns. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What pattern might you find in a screen-time and mood log?
  **A:** Example: feeling more tired or down after long scrolling, or better after video-chatting a friend.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Algorithms
*Student:* Learn how algorithms keep users scrolling and identify design features that do this.

*How to teach:* Explain that algorithms track what you watch and show more of it; identify design features (infinite scroll, autoplay, likes, streaks, notifications).

- **Q:** Name two design features that keep users scrolling.
  **A:** Infinite scroll, autoplay, notifications, streaks, likes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Media plan
*Student:* Write a family media plan with screen-free times and zones.

*How to teach:* Write a family media plan with screen-free times (meals, an hour before bed) and zones (bedrooms).

- **Q:** Why keep devices out of bedrooms at night?
  **A:** Screens and notifications delay sleep and reduce sleep quality.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Emotions and Stress unit test.

*How to teach:* Give the Emotions and Stress unit test. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What does FOMO stand for, and how can you manage it?
  **A:** Fear of missing out; take breaks from social media, focus on real-life activities and remember posts are highlights.

**Assessment:** Unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: 1) feelings wheel/naming emotions 2) triggers and body cues 3) healthy vs. unhealthy expression 4) fight-or-flight (adrenaline, cortisol) 5) eustress vs. distress 6) two breathing techniques 7) coping strategies 8) growth mindset and resilience 9) positive self-talk 10) social media comparison/algorithms. Proficient = 80%.
