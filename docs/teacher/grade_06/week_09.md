# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 9

## English Language Arts 6: Explanatory essay: how Brian survives

**Prepare:** Gather: Hatchet notes, survival article, essay organizer. Gather Hatchet notes and the Week 6 survival article; print an essay organizer.

**Watch for:**
- Retelling the plot in body paragraphs. Each paragraph needs a topic sentence, evidence and explanation of how it shows survival.
- Citing the article without naming it. Teach 'According to…'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Thesis and plan
*Student:* Write a thesis explaining three ways Brian survives (mental and physical) and fill an essay organizer with evidence.

*How to teach:* Model a thesis: 'Brian survives because he masters fire, learns to find food and develops tough hope.' The child writes theirs and fills the organizer.

- **Q:** What makes a thesis strong?
  **A:** It answers the prompt directly and previews the main points.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body paragraphs
*Student:* Draft three body paragraphs, each with a quote from Hatchet and one fact from the survival article.

*How to teach:* The child drafts three body paragraphs with a Hatchet quote and an article fact in each.

- **Q:** What should come right after a quotation?
  **A:** Explanation of how it supports the paragraph's point.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Intro and conclusion
*Student:* Draft an introduction with a hook and a conclusion that explains what Brian's survival teaches readers.

*How to teach:* Discuss hooks (question, surprising fact, quote). The child drafts intro and conclusion.

- **Q:** What should the conclusion do?
  **A:** Restate the thesis in new words and explain what Brian's survival teaches readers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise for transitions and precise words; edit for pronoun shifts and intensive pronouns, then type the final essay.

*How to teach:* Revise for transitions; edit for pronoun shifts and intensive pronouns; type final.

- **Q:** Name three transitions for explanatory writing.
  **A:** First, in addition, for example, finally, as a result.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Submit the essay, self-score with the explanatory rubric, and take the unit vocabulary test.

*How to teach:* Child submits, self-scores and takes the vocabulary test.

- **Q:** Define 'epilogue.'
  **A:** A section after the main story that tells what happened later.

**Assessment:** Explanatory essay scored on rubric; vocabulary test.

**Answer key:** Explanatory essay rubric — 4: clear thesis naming three ways Brian survives (e.g., making fire, finding food, keeping a positive mental attitude/'tough hope'); each body paragraph has a quote from Hatchet and a fact from the article, explained; hook intro and meaningful conclusion; transitions; few errors. 3: all parts but some evidence unexplained. 2: missing evidence or a paragraph. 1: incomplete. Vocabulary test covers unit words (theme, personification, objective summary, intensive pronoun, epilogue, turning point).

## Mathematics 6: Units of measure and unit rates

**Prepare:** Gather: ruler with inches and cm, measuring cups and a liter bottle, bathroom scale. Have a phone timer ready for Day 4; know that 1 in = 2.54 cm exactly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a bigger number always means a bigger length. In smaller units (cm), the same object gets a bigger number.
- Multiplying when you should divide in a conversion. Ask: 'Should the answer be more or fewer units?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Measuring with different units
*Student:* Measure the same objects in inches and centimeters and in cups and milliliters, and record why the numbers differ.

*How to teach:* Measure a book in inches and centimeters and a mug in cups and milliliters. Ask why the cm number is bigger; the child records and explains.

- **Q:** A pencil is about 7 inches long. Will its length in centimeters be a bigger or smaller number? Why?
  **A:** Bigger (about 17.8 cm), because centimeters are smaller units, so more of them fit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Converting units
*Student:* Use the ratio 1 in : 2.54 cm and 1 ft : 12 in to convert 12 measurements with ratio tables.

*How to teach:* Set up a ratio table with inches and cm (1 : 2.54). The child converts the 12 measurements, scaling the table up and down.

- **Q:** Convert 12 inches to cm.
  **A:** 30.48 cm (12 × 2.54).
- **Q:** Convert 5 feet to inches, then to cm.
  **A:** 60 inches; 152.4 cm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two unit rates
*Student:* For 'every 4 cups of water makes 3 servings' find both unit rates (cups per serving, servings per cup) and explain when each is useful.

*How to teach:* Ask: 'If 4 cups make 3 servings, how much water per serving? How many servings per cup?' Divide both ways and discuss when each helps.

- **Q:** Find both unit rates for 4 cups of water : 3 servings.
  **A:** 1⅓ cups per serving, and ¾ serving per cup.
- **Q:** How much water for 12 servings?
  **A:** 16 cups.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rates at home
*Student:* Time how many seconds it takes to fill a 1-liter bottle at the faucet and calculate liters per minute and minutes per liter.

*How to teach:* Time filling the liter bottle. The child converts seconds to minutes and finds both unit rates.

- **Q:** A 1-liter bottle fills in 15 seconds. Find liters per minute and minutes per liter.
  **A:** 4 liters per minute; 0.25 (¼) minute per liter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on unit conversion and finding unit rates.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently and review any conversion where they multiplied instead of divided.

- **Q:** Convert 50.8 cm to inches.
  **A:** 20 inches (50.8 ÷ 2.54).

**Assessment:** Quiz on conversions and unit rates.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 3 ft = 36 in 2) 10 in = 25.4 cm 3) 50.8 cm = 20 in 4) $6 for 4 lb → $1.50 per lb and ⅔ lb per dollar 5) 150 miles in 3 hours → 50 mph 6) 2 yd = 6 ft = 72 in 7) 8 cups = 2 quarts (4 cups per quart).

## Science 6 (Integrated): The atmosphere and air pressure

**Prepare:** Gather: balloon, jar, straw, tape, empty plastic bottle, hot water. Do the index-card trick over a sink. Adult pours hot water into the bottle.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking air has no weight or pressure. Air pushes in all directions.
- Thinking the bottle crushes because the air 'sucks'. Outside air pressure pushes in when inside pressure drops.

### Day 1 (Monday): Layers of air
*Student:* Create a scaled drawing of atmospheric layers and note where weather happens.

*How to teach:* Draw layers to scale with the troposphere at the bottom.

- **Q:** In which layer does weather happen?
  **A:** The troposphere.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Air has pressure
*Student:* Demonstrate air pressure with the index-card-on-a-cup-of-water trick and the crushed bottle (warm air cooling inside a capped bottle).

*How to teach:* Do the card trick and the crushed bottle.

- **Q:** Why does the capped bottle crush as it cools?
  **A:** The air inside cools, its pressure drops, and higher outside air pressure pushes the bottle in.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build a barometer
*Student:* Build a jar-and-balloon barometer with a straw pointer and begin recording daily readings.

*How to teach:* Build the jar barometer; begin daily readings.

- **Q:** If the straw pointer rises, what happened to air pressure?
  **A:** It increased (pushing the balloon down, lifting the pointer).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Warm air rises
*Student:* Hold a paper spiral above a warm lamp or radiator and explain the motion using density.

*How to teach:* Hold the paper spiral above a warm lamp.

- **Q:** Why does the spiral spin?
  **A:** Warm air is less dense and rises, pushing the spiral.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on atmosphere and air pressure; compare your barometer readings with the weather.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; compare barometer readings with weather.

- **Q:** Does falling pressure usually bring fair or stormy weather?
  **A:** Stormy/cloudy.

**Assessment:** Quiz and barometer log.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) troposphere — where weather happens (lowest layer) 2) stratosphere — ozone layer 3) mesosphere, thermosphere above 4) air pressure decreases with altitude 5) the bottle crushes because cooling air inside lowers pressure and outside air pushes in 6) warm air rises because it is less dense. Barometer log: daily readings with weather notes; falling pressure often comes before clouds/rain, rising before clear weather.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The gift of the Nile

**Prepare:** Gather: map of the Nile, Herodotus 'Egypt is the gift of the Nile' quote, 'Hymn to the Nile' (Fordham Sourcebook). Print the 'Hymn to the Nile' (Fordham) and a Nile map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Upper Egypt is in the north. The Nile flows north, so Upper Egypt is south and Lower Egypt is the delta in the north.
- Thinking the Nile's floods were destructive like Mesopotamia's. They were regular and predictable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mapping the Nile
*Student:* Label the Nile, delta, cataracts, Upper and Lower Egypt, and surrounding deserts on a map.

*How to teach:* Label the Nile map.

- **Q:** Which direction does the Nile flow?
  **A:** North, into the Mediterranean Sea.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hymn to the Nile
*Student:* Read the 'Hymn to the Nile' and list what Egyptians thanked the river for.

*How to teach:* Read the hymn; list thanks.

- **Q:** What did Egyptians thank the Nile for?
  **A:** Water, food and crops, the flood that renewed the fields, and life itself.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Farming calendar
*Student:* Make a circular calendar of the flood, planting and harvest seasons and explain the shaduf.

*How to teach:* Make a circular farming calendar; explain the shaduf.

- **Q:** What is a shaduf?
  **A:** A lever with a bucket on one end and a weight on the other, used to lift water from the river.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unification
*Student:* Read about Narmer (Menes) and the double crown and explain how the Nile helped unify Egypt.

*How to teach:* Read about Narmer and the double crown.

- **Q:** How did the Nile help unify Egypt?
  **A:** It linked communities by travel and trade along one river, making it easier to rule as one kingdom.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare the Nile with the Tigris-Euphrates in a Venn diagram; quiz on Egyptian geography.

*How to teach:* Make the Venn diagram; quiz.

- **Q:** What did Herodotus mean by 'the gift of the Nile'?
  **A:** Without the Nile's water and fertile silt, Egypt would be desert.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram and quiz.

**Answer key:** Venn: both rivers deposited silt and supported irrigation and civilization; the Nile flooded predictably and gently and flows north, protected by deserts; the Tigris-Euphrates flooded unpredictably, with fewer natural barriers. Quiz: 1) Herodotus: 'Egypt is the gift of the Nile' 2) delta in the north 3) cataracts = rapids 4) Upper Egypt south, Lower Egypt north 5) deserts protected Egypt 6) Narmer (Menes) united Upper and Lower Egypt around 3100 BCE.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Roman mosaics and art preservation

**Prepare:** Find Pompeii mosaics such as 'Cave Canem' (House of the Tragic Poet). Gather colored paper squares (about 1 cm) or dried beans, glue, cardboard, and grid paper. Find a short museum conservation video (e.g., Getty or the Met).

**Watch for:**
- Gluing tesserae touching each other. Leave small even gaps (grout lines).
- Thinking conservation means repainting old art. Conservators stabilize and protect art and try not to change the original.

### Day 1 (Monday): Roman mosaics
*Student:* Study Pompeii mosaics (e.g., 'Cave Canem') and identify how tesserae create shading.

*How to teach:* Study Pompeii mosaics; identify how tesserae create shading. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is a tessera?
  **A:** One small tile piece in a mosaic.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan a mosaic
*Student:* Sketch a mosaic design and plan colors on a grid.

*How to teach:* Sketch a mosaic design and plan colors on a grid. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why plan colors on a grid first?
  **A:** To see how pieces will fit and show shading before gluing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build the mosaic
*Student:* Glue paper or bean tesserae onto cardboard, leaving even grout lines.

*How to teach:* Glue tesserae onto cardboard with even grout lines. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** How do mosaics show shading?
  **A:** By placing lighter and darker tiles in rows that follow the shape.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Preservation
*Student:* Watch a museum conservation video and compare how 2D, 3D and digital works are preserved.

*How to teach:* Watch a conservation video; compare how 2D, 3D and digital works are preserved. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** How are digital artworks preserved?
  **A:** Backing up files in multiple places and updating them to new formats.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & critique
*Student:* Display your ancient-art pieces together and write a critique of your strongest work using your criteria.

*How to teach:* Display ancient-art pieces; critique your strongest using criteria. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What does a curator do?
  **A:** Chooses, organizes and cares for artworks in a collection or exhibition.

**Assessment:** Mosaic and unit critique.

**Answer key:** Mosaic: clear design, even grout lines, shading with tesserae. Critique rubric: 4 = uses own criteria with evidence about strongest work; 3 = criteria with little evidence; 2 = opinion only; 1 = missing.

## Physical Education 6: Ultimate: throwing and catching a disc

**Prepare:** Gather: flying disc, cones. Use a soft flying disc; cones for targets. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing with an angled release. Keep the disc flat.
- Catching with one hand while learning. Use two hands (pancake).

### Day 1 (Monday): Backhand
*Student:* Practice backhand throws to a partner, focusing on a flat release.

*How to teach:* Practice backhand.

- **Q:** What causes a disc to curve or dive?
  **A:** Releasing it tilted; release flat and level.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Catching
*Student:* Practice pancake and rim catches at different heights.

*How to teach:* Practice pancake and rim catches.

- **Q:** When do you use a pancake catch?
  **A:** For a disc between chest and waist — clap hands together on it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Forehand
*Student:* Learn the forehand (flick) throw and practice 30 throws.

*How to teach:* Practice the forehand flick.

- **Q:** What powers a forehand flick?
  **A:** A quick snap of the wrist.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pivot and pass
*Student:* Practice pivoting to avoid a marker and passing to a moving receiver.

*How to teach:* Pivot and pass to a moving receiver.

- **Q:** Why throw ahead of a moving receiver?
  **A:** So the disc arrives where they will be, not where they were.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Complete 10 backhand throws to a target 10 meters away and record successes.

*How to teach:* Run the accuracy check.

- **Q:** What is a key cue for the backhand?
  **A:** Step toward the target and keep the disc flat.

**Assessment:** Disc throwing accuracy check.

**Answer key:** Accuracy check: 10 backhand throws to a target/partner 10 m away; record catches or target hits (6+ = proficient). Cue checklist: sideways stance, step toward target, flat release, follow through.

## Health 6: Communication skills

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards (someone cuts in line; a sibling borrows something without asking). Have a mirror. I-message formula: I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking assertive means aggressive. Assertive is firm and respectful; aggressive attacks or blames.
- Thinking listening means waiting to talk. Active listening means focusing, then paraphrasing what you heard.

### Day 1 (Monday): Three styles
*Student:* Watch or act out passive, aggressive and assertive responses to the same situation and compare.

*How to teach:* Act out passive, aggressive and assertive responses to the same situation; compare. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Someone cuts in line. Give an assertive response.
  **A:** 'Excuse me, I was in line first. Please go to the back.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): I-messages
*Student:* Rewrite 6 blaming statements as I-messages (I feel... when... because... I need...).

*How to teach:* Rewrite 6 blaming statements as I-messages. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Rewrite: 'You always ruin everything!'
  **A:** 'I feel frustrated when my project gets knocked over because I worked hard on it. I need you to be careful around my desk.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Active listening
*Student:* Practice paraphrasing in a 5-minute conversation with a family member.

*How to teach:* Practice paraphrasing in a 5-minute conversation. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is paraphrasing?
  **A:** Restating what someone said in your own words to show you understood.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nonverbal
*Student:* Practice assertive body language and tone in a mirror.

*How to teach:* Practice assertive body language and tone in a mirror. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Describe assertive body language.
  **A:** Stand tall, eye contact, calm steady voice, relaxed hands.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform an assertive communication role-play scored with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Perform an assertive role-play scored with a rubric. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** Why do I-messages reduce conflict?
  **A:** They express feelings without blaming, so the other person is less defensive.

**Assessment:** Communication rubric.

**Answer key:** Communication rubric (1–4 each): uses an I-message with all parts; assertive tone and body language; listens and paraphrases; reaches a respectful outcome. Proficient = 3+ each.
