# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 29

## English Language Arts 6: The Secret Garden, chapters 1-7

**Prepare:** Gather: 'The Secret Garden' by Frances Hodgson Burnett (Project Gutenberg or library). Get The Secret Garden. Preview: chapter 1 opens with Mary's parents dying in a cholera outbreak in India; Burnett uses some colonial-era attitudes worth discussing.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Mary is simply 'bad'. Ask why: neglectful parents and servants who obeyed her every whim.
- Skipping dialect. Read Martha's lines aloud — it gets easier.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mistress Mary
*Student:* Read chapters 1-2 and list Mary's traits and their causes with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2; list traits and causes.

- **Q:** Why is Mary so disagreeable at the start?
  **A:** Her parents ignored her and servants gave her whatever she wanted, so she never learned to care for others.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Across the moor
*Student:* Read chapters 3-4 and describe Misselthwaite Manor and the moor; explain how the setting reflects Mary's mood.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 3–4; describe the manor and moor.

- **Q:** How does the setting reflect Mary's mood on arrival?
  **A:** The dark, gloomy manor and the bare, endless moor match her lonely, sour, cold feelings.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Yorkshire dialect
*Student:* Read chapter 5; translate five lines of Martha's dialect and discuss why Burnett uses dialect.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 5; translate dialect words.

- **Q:** Translate Yorkshire dialect: tha', nowt, summat.
  **A:** you; nothing; something.

### Day 4 (Thursday): The key
*Student:* Read chapters 6-7 and predict how finding the garden will change Mary.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 6–7; predict.

- **Q:** How does Mary find the key?
  **A:** A robin leads her to the spot where the buried key is in the earth.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a character sketch of Mary with three quotations; reading quiz on chapters 1-7.

*How to teach:* Write the character sketch; give the quiz.

- **Q:** Why was the garden locked?
  **A:** Mr. Craven locked it after his wife died following an accident in it, ten years ago.

**Assessment:** Character sketch and quiz.

**Answer key:** Character sketch rubric — 4: Mary's traits (sour, spoiled, lonely, curious) with three quotations, explanations of causes (neglect in India), and early signs of change; 3: two quotes; 2: list of traits; 1: off-topic. Quiz: 1) Mary's parents die of cholera in India 2) she is sent to her uncle Archibald Craven at Misselthwaite Manor in Yorkshire 3) Mrs. Medlock brings her 4) Martha is the maid 5) the garden has been locked for ten years 6) she hears crying in the corridors 7) a robin helps her find the buried key.

## Mathematics 6: Polygons in the coordinate plane

**Prepare:** Gather: graph paper, Desmos. Graph paper; optionally set up Desmos for the Day 4 design.

**Watch for:**
- Finding side length by subtracting a negative incorrectly (4 − (−3) = 1). Count on the grid or use |4| + |−3| = 7.
- Using perimeter when area is asked.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drawing polygons
*Student:* Plot and connect vertices to draw rectangles, triangles and trapezoids in all four quadrants.

*How to teach:* Give vertex lists; the child plots and connects in order. Check that rectangles have square corners.

- **Q:** Plot (−3, 2), (4, 2), (4, −1), (−3, −1). What shape is it?
  **A:** A rectangle 7 units wide and 3 tall.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Side lengths
*Student:* Use coordinates to find horizontal and vertical side lengths and perimeters of 8 polygons.

*How to teach:* Find horizontal and vertical side lengths using coordinates, then perimeters.

- **Q:** Find the perimeter of that rectangle.
  **A:** 20 units (7 + 3 + 7 + 3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Area on the grid
*Student:* Find areas of coordinate polygons by decomposing or enclosing in rectangles.

*How to teach:* Decompose or enclose in a rectangle and subtract corner triangles.

- **Q:** Area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (6, 0), (2, 4)?
  **A:** 12 square units (½ × 6 × 4).
- **Q:** Area of the rectangle from Day 1?
  **A:** 21 square units.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design challenge
*Student:* Design a garden plot with 4-6 vertices on Desmos having area exactly 48 square units and list its coordinates.

*How to teach:* Challenge the child to design a garden with area exactly 48. Rectangles are fine; encourage an L-shape for extra challenge.

- **Q:** Give rectangle vertices with area 48.
  **A:** Example: (−4, −3), (4, −3), (4, 3), (−4, 3) — 8 × 6 = 48.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 end-of-unit assessment on rational numbers, inequalities and the coordinate plane.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Distance from (−2, 5) to (−2, −3)?
  **A:** 8 units.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 end-of-unit assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test sample key: 1) −3 < −1.5 2) order −2, −⅓, 0.5, 1¼ 3) |−15| = 15 4) at least 12 → x ≥ 12 5) x < 4: open circle at 4, shade left 6) (−2, 5) Quadrant II 7) distance (−2, 5) to (−2, −3) = 8 8) rectangle (−3, 2), (4, 2), (4, −1), (−3, −1): perimeter 20, area 21 9) (4, −3) reflected over the y-axis → (−4, −3) 10) −8°F is colder than −3°F.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Skeletal and muscular systems

**Prepare:** Gather: cardboard, straws, string, chicken wing (cooked or raw with gloves). Handle raw chicken with gloves and wash hands and surfaces afterward (or use cooked).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking muscles push. Muscles only pull; they work in pairs.
- Confusing tendons and ligaments. Tendons connect muscle to bone; ligaments connect bone to bone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bones
*Student:* Identify 15 major bones on a diagram and feel the bones of your hand and arm.

*How to teach:* Identify 15 bones on a diagram and feel them.

- **Q:** Name the three bones in the arm.
  **A:** Humerus (upper arm), radius and ulna (forearm).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Joints
*Student:* Test range of motion of hinge, ball-and-socket and pivot joints on your body and record findings.

*How to teach:* Test joint motion.

- **Q:** Which joint type lets the shoulder move in circles?
  **A:** Ball-and-socket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Muscle pairs
*Student:* Build a cardboard arm model with string 'muscles' to show biceps and triceps working in pairs.

*How to teach:* Build the cardboard arm.

- **Q:** Which muscle contracts to bend the elbow?
  **A:** The biceps (the triceps relaxes).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chicken wing
*Student:* With gloves, examine a chicken wing to find muscles, tendons and bone and sketch them.

*How to teach:* Examine a chicken wing.

- **Q:** What connects muscle to bone?
  **A:** Tendons.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on bones, joints and muscle pairs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** How many bones in an adult skeleton?
  **A:** 206.

**Assessment:** Arm model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) adult skeleton has 206 bones 2) skull protects the brain; ribs protect heart and lungs 3) hinge joint — elbow/knee; ball-and-socket — shoulder/hip; pivot — neck (top vertebrae) 4) biceps contracts to bend the elbow; triceps to straighten 5) tendon connects muscle to bone. Arm model rubric: shows muscle pairs pulling.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The Han Dynasty and the Silk Roads

**Prepare:** Gather: map of Eurasia, paper-making supplies (scrap paper, blender, screen). Gather scrap paper, a blender (adult), and a screen for papermaking.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Silk Road was one road. It was a network of routes.
- Thinking only goods traveled. Ideas, religions and diseases did too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Han government
*Student:* Read about the Han civil service exams and explain how Confucian ideas shaped the bureaucracy.

*How to teach:* Read about Han exams.

- **Q:** How did Confucianism shape Han government?
  **A:** Officials were chosen partly by exams on Confucian classics, valuing education and virtue.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Han inventions
*Student:* Make recycled paper with scrap paper, a blender and a screen, and list other Han inventions (seismograph, wheelbarrow).

*How to teach:* Make paper; list inventions.

- **Q:** Name three Han inventions.
  **A:** Paper, seismograph, wheelbarrow (also the rudder).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Silk Roads
*Student:* Map the Silk Roads from Chang'an to Rome and list goods and ideas exchanged in each direction.

*How to teach:* Map the Silk Roads.

- **Q:** Name goods traveling east and west.
  **A:** West: silk, paper; east: horses, glass, wool, gold.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Buddhism comes to China
*Student:* Trace how Buddhism traveled to China along the Silk Roads and how it blended with Chinese traditions.

*How to teach:* Trace Buddhism to China.

- **Q:** How did Buddhism reach China?
  **A:** Merchants and monks along the Silk Roads.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on ancient China.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** What was Chang'an?
  **A:** The Han capital and eastern starting point of the Silk Roads.

**Assessment:** Silk Roads map and Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Silk Roads map: Chang'an west through Central Asia to the Mediterranean; goods east→west: silk, paper, spices; west→east: horses, glass, wool, gold/silver. Unit 7 test sample: geography; Shang; Mandate of Heaven; Confucius and Laozi; Qin Legalism and standardization; Han civil service exams based on Confucian learning; Han inventions — paper, seismograph, wheelbarrow; Silk Roads; Buddhism entered China via the Silk Roads.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Stop-motion animation: planning

**Prepare:** Sticky notes (a pad), clay or LEGO figures, a free stop-motion app (e.g., Stop Motion Studio), and videos of a zoetrope and Muybridge's 'The Horse in Motion' (1878, filmed in Palo Alto, California).

**Watch for:**
- Moving figures too much between frames. Small increments make smooth motion.
- Thinking animation is one drawing that moves. It's many still images shown quickly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Flip book
*Student:* Make a 20-page flip book of a bouncing ball on sticky notes.

*How to teach:* Make a 20-page flip book of a bouncing ball (squash on landing, stretch while falling). Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why does a flip book appear to move?
  **A:** Images shown in quick succession blend together in our perception.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Animation history
*Student:* Watch early animation (zoetrope, Muybridge's horse) and explain persistence of vision.

*How to teach:* Watch early animation; explain persistence of vision. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What did Muybridge's horse photos prove?
  **A:** That all four of a galloping horse's hooves leave the ground at the same time.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story idea
*Student:* Develop a 20-30 second animation idea with a character, problem and solution.

*How to teach:* Develop a 20–30 second idea with character, problem, solution. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** At 12 frames per second, how many photos for 20 seconds?
  **A:** 240 frames.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Storyboard
*Student:* Draw an 8-panel storyboard with camera angles.

*How to teach:* Draw an 8-panel storyboard with camera angles. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why plan camera angles?
  **A:** To show action clearly and add variety.

### Day 5 (Friday): Build set
*Student:* Build characters and a simple set from clay or LEGO.

*How to teach:* Build characters and set. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why should a set be stable?
  **A:** Bumping it between frames causes jumps in the animation.

**Assessment:** Flip book and storyboard.

**Answer key:** Flip book: 20 pages with smooth bounce. Storyboard: 8 panels with character, problem, solution and camera angles. Proficient = both complete.

## Physical Education 6: Jump rope fitness

**Prepare:** Gather: jump rope. Size the jump rope: standing on the middle, handles should reach the armpits. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Jumping too high. Small, quick hops.
- Swinging from the shoulders. Turn the rope with the wrists.

### Day 1 (Monday): Basic skills
*Student:* Practice basic bounce, alternate foot step and skier for 1-minute intervals.

*How to teach:* Basic bounce, alternate step, skier.

- **Q:** How should the rope be sized?
  **A:** Standing on the middle, handles reach about the armpits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tricks
*Student:* Learn criss-cross and side swing; attempt double unders.

*How to teach:* Criss-cross and side swing; try double unders.

- **Q:** What is a double under?
  **A:** The rope passes under the feet twice in one jump.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Endurance
*Student:* Jump in 30-second intervals for 10 rounds and record total jumps.

*How to teach:* 10 rounds of 30-second intervals.

- **Q:** Why rest between intervals?
  **A:** To recover so you can keep good form.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Routine
*Student:* Create a 1-minute jump rope routine with at least five skills.

*How to teach:* Create a routine.

- **Q:** Name five jump rope skills.
  **A:** Basic bounce, alternate step, skier, criss-cross, side swing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform your routine and count jumps in 1 minute.

*How to teach:* Perform; count 1 minute.

- **Q:** Which body part turns the rope?
  **A:** The wrists.

**Assessment:** Jump rope routine and count.

**Answer key:** Routine: 1 minute, 5+ skills (basic bounce, alternate step, skier, criss-cross, side swing) and a count of jumps in 1 minute (record and compare with the endurance day).

## Health 6: Sleep, screens, eyes and hearing

**Prepare:** Print a 3-night sleep log. Install a free sound meter app. Get a sunscreen bottle. Know: preteens 6–12 need 9–12 hours of sleep (13–18: 8–10); the 60/60 rule for headphones (no more than 60% volume for 60 minutes at a time); prolonged noise above about 85 decibels can damage hearing; 20-20-20 eye rule.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you can catch up on all lost sleep on weekends. Sleep debt builds up; regular schedules work best.
- Thinking sunscreen is only for beach days. UV rays reach you on cloudy days and in everyday outdoor time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sleep needs
*Student:* Learn why 9–12 hours matter for preteens and keep a 3-night sleep log.

*How to teach:* Learn why 9–12 hours matter; keep a 3-night sleep log. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name two effects of sleep debt.
  **A:** Trouble concentrating, irritability, weaker immune system, slower reaction time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Screens and sleep
*Student:* Discuss blue light and phones in bedrooms and plan a device curfew.

*How to teach:* Discuss blue light and phones in bedrooms; plan a device curfew. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why can screens before bed disturb sleep?
  **A:** Blue light and stimulating content delay the release of melatonin and keep the brain alert.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hearing
*Student:* Use a sound meter app to measure common noises and learn safe headphone volume (60/60 rule).

*How to teach:* Measure common noises with the app; learn the 60/60 rule. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is the 60/60 rule?
  **A:** Listen at no more than 60% volume for no more than 60 minutes at a time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sun and eyes
*Student:* Read a sunscreen label, check the UV index and learn the 20-20-20 eye rule.

*How to teach:* Read a sunscreen label (SPF 30+, broad spectrum, reapply every 2 hours); check the UV index; learn 20-20-20.

- **Q:** What is the 20-20-20 rule?
  **A:** Every 20 minutes of screen time, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a personal health practice goal and take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Write a personal health practice goal; take the quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What does 'broad spectrum' mean on sunscreen?
  **A:** It protects against both UVA and UVB rays.

**Assessment:** Goal and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 9–12 hours sleep; sleep debt; blue light; 60/60 rule; 85 dB; SPF 30+ broad spectrum, reapply every 2 hours; 20-20-20. Goal: SMART and tied to sleep, hearing, sun or eye health.
