# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 5

## English Language Arts 6: Hatchet chapters 1-4: the crash

**Prepare:** Gather: Hatchet by Gary Paulsen (library copy), map of Canada. Get Hatchet (library). Parent preview: Brian's parents are divorcing and he carries 'the Secret' (he saw his mother with another man); the pilot has a fatal heart attack during the flight. Discuss these calmly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the hatchet is just a tool. Point out it is a gift from his mother — tied to his feelings about her and the Secret.
- Using 'myself' as a subject or object ('Mom and myself went'). Intensive pronouns only emphasize a noun already in the sentence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Before the crash
*Student:* Read chapter 1, locate the flight route on a map of Canada, and note what we learn about Brian and the Secret.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 1 aloud together, then trace a route from New York to northern Canada on the map. Ask what we know about Brian and the Secret.

- **Q:** Where is Brian going and why?
  **A:** To visit his father in the north woods of Canada (where his father works in the oil fields) — his parents are divorced.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Crash landing
*Student:* Read chapters 2-3; list sensory words Paulsen uses during the crash and explain how short sentences build tension.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 2–3. The child lists sensory words and finds a run of short sentences, reading them aloud to feel the pace.

- **Q:** Why does Paulsen use short sentences and fragments during the crash?
  **A:** They speed up the pace and copy Brian's panicked, choppy thoughts, building tension.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): First night
*Student:* Read chapter 4; make a T-chart of Brian's physical and emotional problems and discuss which is worse.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 4. The child makes a T-chart (physical: mosquitoes, hunger, injuries; emotional: fear, loneliness, the Secret).

- **Q:** Which of Brian's problems is worse at first, physical or emotional? Use evidence.
  **A:** Accept either with evidence; e.g., emotional — memories of the Secret and fear overwhelm him and he cries, while physical problems like hunger and mosquitoes are painful but manageable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Intensive pronouns
*Student:* Lesson on intensive pronouns (himself, myself); write 8 sentences about Brian using them correctly and fix 6 misuse errors.

*How to teach:* Teach that intensive pronouns emphasize ('Brian himself built the fire') and can be removed without changing meaning. The child writes 8 and fixes 6.

- **Q:** Correct: 'The pilot and myself flew north.'
  **A:** 'The pilot and I flew north.'
- **Q:** Write a sentence with an intensive pronoun about Brian.
  **A:** Example: 'Brian himself had to find food.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Reading check quiz on chapters 1-4 and a journal entry from Brian's point of view after the first night.

*How to teach:* Give the reading check; the child writes a journal entry as Brian after the first night.

- **Q:** What gift does Brian receive from his mother?
  **A:** A hatchet, worn on his belt.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and point-of-view journal entry.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz (chapters 1–4): 1) Brian is 13 and flying in a small bush plane to visit his father in the Canadian north woods (oil fields) 2) His mother gave him a hatchet 3) The Secret: he saw his mother kissing a man who was not his father 4) The pilot has a heart attack 5) Brian crash-lands in a lake. Journal rubric: 4 = first-person voice, accurate events, physical and emotional details (mosquitoes, hunger, fear, memories of the Secret); 3 = accurate but less detail; 2 = errors; 1 = off-topic.

## Mathematics 6: What are ratios?

**Prepare:** Gather: colored counters or beans, measuring cups, drink mix or food coloring. Clear cups help for color mixing; use tablespoons of water as the 'part' unit.

**Watch for:**
- Reversing the order of a ratio. 'Red to blue' means red first; have the child say the words while writing the numbers.
- Confusing part-to-part with part-to-whole. For 3 red and 2 blue, red:blue is 3:2 but red:total is 3:5.

### Day 1 (Monday): Introducing ratios
*Student:* Describe collections of beans or counters with ratio language in three forms (3:2, 3 to 2, 3 for every 2) and write 10 ratio sentences.

*How to teach:* Put out 5 red and 3 blue beans. Model saying the ratio three ways ('5 to 3', '5:3', 'for every 5 red there are 3 blue'), then the child writes 10 ratio sentences about different collections.

- **Q:** With 5 red and 3 blue beans, write the ratio of blue to red and red to all beans.
  **A:** Blue to red = 3:5; red to all = 5:8.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ratio diagrams
*Student:* Draw discrete diagrams for recipe ratios (e.g., 2 cups flour for every 3 eggs) and answer which-ratio-matches questions.

*How to teach:* Model drawing 2 flour circles and 3 egg squares for one batch, then repeat the group to show two batches. The child draws diagrams and answers the matching questions.

- **Q:** A recipe uses 2 cups flour for every 3 eggs. How much flour for 6 eggs?
  **A:** 4 cups — two batches of (2 flour, 3 eggs).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recipes and batches
*Student:* Make one batch and a double batch of a drink mix (e.g., 2 scoops per 1 cup water), taste-test that they match, and explain why the ratio is equivalent.

*How to teach:* Mix one batch (2 scoops : 1 cup) and a double batch (4 scoops : 2 cups); taste both. Ask why they taste the same.

- **Q:** Is 4 scoops to 2 cups equivalent to 2 scoops to 1 cup? Why?
  **A:** Yes — both quantities were multiplied by 2, so every cup has the same amount of mix.
- **Q:** How many scoops for 3 cups?
  **A:** 6 scoops.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Color mixing
*Student:* Mix food coloring drops with water in 1:5 and 2:10 ratios to check that equivalent ratios make the same color; record observations in a table.

*How to teach:* In clear cups, mix 1 drop : 5 tbsp water and 2 drops : 10 tbsp; then 2 drops : 5 tbsp. The child records colors in a table and explains which match.

- **Q:** Will 2 drops in 5 tbsp look the same as 1 drop in 5 tbsp?
  **A:** No — it will be darker, because there is more coloring per tablespoon of water (2:5 is not equivalent to 1:5).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: write and interpret ratios from pictures and stories; explain the difference between a part-to-part and part-to-whole ratio.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently, then discuss part-to-part vs. part-to-whole using the family as an example.

- **Q:** A class has 7 girls and 5 boys. Write a part-to-part and a part-to-whole ratio.
  **A:** Part-to-part: girls to boys 7:5. Part-to-whole: girls to all students 7:12.

**Assessment:** Ratio language quiz and color-mixing lab write-up.

**Answer key:** Quiz sample: picture of 4 triangles and 6 circles. 1) triangles to circles = 4:6 (or 2:3) 2) circles to triangles = 6:4 (3:2) 3) circles to all shapes = 6:10 (3:5) 4) 'For every 3 cups flour there are 2 cups sugar' — sugar to flour = 2:3 5) part-to-part compares two parts (boys:girls); part-to-whole compares a part to the total (boys:all students). Lab rubric: 4 = data table with both ratios, observation that 1:5 and 2:10 match in color, explanation that both quantities were doubled; 3 = table and match noted, explanation thin; 2 = incomplete data; 1 = no explanation.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Energy transfer: conduction, convection, radiation

**Prepare:** Gather: metal, wooden and plastic spoons, butter, hot water, lamp, black and white paper. Adult handles hot water. Use thermometers that fit under paper; a desk lamp works if not sunny.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cold flows into things. Energy flows from warmer to cooler; 'cold' is lack of energy.
- Mixing up convection (fluid moving) and conduction (touching).

### Day 1 (Monday): Spoon conduction race
*Student:* Put a dab of butter on metal, wooden and plastic spoons standing in hot water and record which melts first.

*How to teach:* Stand spoons in hot water with butter dabs at equal heights; record melting order.

- **Q:** Which spoon's butter melts first and why?
  **A:** Metal — metals are good conductors, transferring energy quickly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Convection currents
*Student:* Drop cold blue-dyed water into warm water in a clear container and sketch the convection current.

*How to teach:* Drop blue cold water into warm water; sketch the current.

- **Q:** Why does the cold blue water sink?
  **A:** Cold water is denser than warm water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Radiation
*Student:* Place thermometers under black and white paper in sunlight or under a lamp and record temperatures every 2 minutes for 20 minutes.

*How to teach:* Record temperature under black and white paper every 2 minutes.

- **Q:** Which paper gets warmer and why?
  **A:** Black — it absorbs more light energy; white reflects more.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reaching equilibrium
*Student:* Mix equal amounts of hot and cold water and measure the final temperature; explain energy flow from hot to cold.

*How to teach:* Mix equal hot and cold water; measure.

- **Q:** If you mix equal amounts of 60°C and 20°C water, what final temperature do you expect?
  **A:** About 40°C (a little less, since some energy escapes).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label conduction, convection and radiation in a diagram of a campfire and a kitchen; quiz on transfer methods.

*How to teach:* The child labels the campfire and kitchen diagrams and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** Name the transfer: feeling the sun on your face.
  **A:** Radiation.

**Assessment:** Transfer diagram and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) a metal spoon getting hot in soup — conduction 2) warm air rising from a heater — convection 3) feeling sun's warmth — radiation 4) black paper warms more because it absorbs more radiation 5) mixing equal amounts of 60°C and 20°C water gives about 40°C. Diagram: campfire — hands warmed by radiation, smoke/hot air rising by convection, a metal poker heating by conduction; kitchen — pot handle (conduction), boiling water circulating (convection), toaster glow (radiation).

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Geography of the Fertile Crescent

**Prepare:** Gather: baking tray, soil, water, map of Mesopotamia. Prepare a tray with soil for the irrigation model (outside).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking floods were always helpful. Tigris-Euphrates floods were unpredictable and destructive, unlike the Nile's.
- Thinking Mesopotamia is a country. It's a region, mostly modern Iraq.

### Day 1 (Monday): Land between rivers
*Student:* Label a map of the Fertile Crescent with rivers, deserts, mountains and modern countries.

*How to teach:* Label the Fertile Crescent map.

- **Q:** What does 'Mesopotamia' mean?
  **A:** 'Land between the rivers' (the Tigris and Euphrates).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Irrigation model
*Student:* Build an irrigation model in a tray of soil with a canal and test how water reaches 'fields'.

*How to teach:* Dig a canal in the tray; pour water to test.

- **Q:** Why did Sumerians need irrigation?
  **A:** Rainfall was low, so canals carried river water to fields.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Floods and silt
*Student:* Read about unpredictable Tigris-Euphrates floods and how Sumerians managed them with levees and canals.

*How to teach:* Read about floods, levees and canals.

- **Q:** Why were Tigris-Euphrates floods a problem?
  **A:** They were unpredictable and could be violent, destroying crops and villages.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Surplus and cities
*Student:* Draw a cause-and-effect chain from irrigation to surplus to specialization to cities.

*How to teach:* Draw the cause-and-effect chain.

- **Q:** How did irrigation lead to cities?
  **A:** Irrigation → more food (surplus) → people could specialize → trade and population grew → cities.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Mesopotamian geography and agriculture.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What is silt?
  **A:** Fine, rich soil left by floodwaters.

**Assessment:** Map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Map: Tigris and Euphrates, Persian Gulf, Zagros Mountains, Syrian/Arabian Desert, modern Iraq, Syria, Turkey, Kuwait. Quiz: 1) Mesopotamia means 'land between the rivers' 2) silt from floods made soil fertile 3) irrigation canals brought water to fields 4) levees held back floodwater 5) surplus → specialization → trade → cities.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Prehistoric and Mesopotamian art

**Prepare:** Find images of the Lascaux cave paintings (France, about 17,000 years old), the Ishtar Gate (Babylon, c. 575 BCE, Pergamon Museum) and Assyrian lion-hunt reliefs (Nineveh, British Museum). Gather a brown paper bag, charcoal, earth-tone chalk pastels, and air-dry clay.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking prehistoric art was just decoration. It may have had ritual, hunting or storytelling purposes.
- Thinking cylinder seals were jewelry only. They were rolled on clay to sign or seal documents and goods.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cave painting study
*Student:* Study images of Lascaux and discuss what the animals may have meant to the artists.

*How to teach:* Study Lascaux; discuss what the animals may have meant. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What animals appear in Lascaux?
  **A:** Horses, bulls/aurochs, deer, bison and others.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cave art
*Student:* Crumple a brown paper bag for a rock texture and draw animals with charcoal and earth-tone pastels.

*How to teach:* Crumple a paper bag; draw animals with charcoal and earth-tone pastels. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What did prehistoric artists use for paint?
  **A:** Natural pigments like ochre (earth), charcoal and minerals mixed with water or fat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mesopotamian relief
*Student:* Study the Ishtar Gate and Assyrian lion reliefs and identify how they show power.

*How to teach:* Study the Ishtar Gate and Assyrian reliefs; identify how they show power. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How do the Assyrian lion reliefs show the king's power?
  **A:** The king is shown defeating powerful lions, proving his strength and right to rule.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cylinder seal
*Student:* Carve a design into a clay cylinder and roll it onto flat clay to make an impression.

*How to teach:* Carve a design into a clay cylinder; roll it onto flat clay. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What was a cylinder seal used for?
  **A:** To sign or mark ownership by rolling an impression into wet clay.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Write how your cave art and seal connect to the purposes of ancient art.

*How to teach:* Write how the cave art and seal connect to ancient art's purposes. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Name two purposes of ancient art.
  **A:** Ritual/religion, recording events, showing power, marking ownership, storytelling.

**Assessment:** Cave art and seal with reflection.

**Answer key:** Cave art: animals drawn with natural colors on textured paper. Seal: carved design leaves a clear rolled impression. Reflection rubric: 4 = connects both works to specific purposes (ritual, power, ownership) with examples; 3 = one; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## Physical Education 6: Group problem solving

**Prepare:** Gather: poly spots or paper plates, ball, rope. Gather poly spots or paper plates and soft balls. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing in without planning. Plan for 2 minutes first.
- Thinking modifications make games 'easier'. They make games fair and include everyone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lava river
*Student:* Cross a 'lava river' using a limited number of poly spots as a group, planning before trying.

*How to teach:* Plan first, then cross the 'lava' using limited spots.

- **Q:** Why plan before trying?
  **A:** Planning lets everyone understand roles and avoid repeated mistakes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Group juggle
*Student:* Pass several balls in a set pattern around a circle and add balls as success grows.

*How to teach:* Group juggle in a set pattern; add balls.

- **Q:** What helps the group juggle?
  **A:** Saying the receiver's name before tossing and using gentle underhand tosses.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Keep it up
*Student:* Keep a balloon or beach ball in the air as long as possible with each person touching it in order.

*How to teach:* Keep a balloon up, each person in order.

- **Q:** What is a good strategy for keeping the balloon up?
  **A:** Gentle, high touches and calling the next person.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modify for all
*Student:* Redesign one challenge so a person using a wheelchair or with limited vision could fully take part.

*How to teach:* Redesign a challenge for full inclusion.

- **Q:** How could you adapt 'lava river' for a player who uses a wheelchair?
  **A:** Use larger spots or tape zones reachable by wheels, allow a partner to place spots, keep the path wide and flat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Record your group's best times and explain which strategy change helped most.

*How to teach:* Record times and explain the best strategy.

- **Q:** What should you do when a strategy fails?
  **A:** Talk as a group, identify the problem and try one change.

**Assessment:** Strategy reflection.

**Answer key:** Strategy reflection rubric — 4: records best times, names the specific strategy change that helped most and explains why with data; 3: names a strategy without data; 2: vague; 1: missing.

## Health 6: Coping and stress management

**Prepare:** Have a timer, planner, and a progressive muscle relaxation script (many free scripts online). Box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. 4-7-8 breathing: in 4, hold 7, out 8.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking coping means avoiding problems. Healthy coping manages stress so you can face the problem.
- Using only one strategy. Different situations need different tools.

### Day 1 (Monday): Breathing
*Student:* Practice box breathing and 4-7-8 breathing; record pulse before and after.

*How to teach:* Take pulse; practice box breathing and 4-7-8 breathing for 2 minutes each; retake pulse. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why does slow breathing lower stress?
  **A:** It activates the body's relaxation response, slowing heart rate and calming the nervous system.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Muscle relaxation
*Student:* Follow a progressive muscle relaxation script and journal how you feel.

*How to teach:* Follow a progressive muscle relaxation script: tense each muscle group for 5 seconds, then release.

- **Q:** How does progressive muscle relaxation work?
  **A:** Tensing then releasing muscles helps you notice and let go of tension.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Time management
*Student:* Plan the coming week in a planner, blocking schoolwork, activity, chores and rest.

*How to teach:* Plan the coming week in a planner, blocking schoolwork, activity, chores and rest. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How does time management reduce stress?
  **A:** Planning prevents last-minute rushing and shows when there's time for rest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coping toolbox
*Student:* Build a coping toolbox of 8 strategies (exercise, music, talking, art, nature, etc.).

*How to teach:* Build a coping toolbox of 8 strategies. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Name four healthy coping strategies.
  **A:** Exercise, music, talking to someone, art, time in nature, deep breathing, journaling.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a personal stress-management plan and use one strategy daily for a week.

*How to teach:* Write a personal stress-management plan; use one strategy daily for a week. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What should a stress-management plan include?
  **A:** Top stressors, specific coping strategies for each, and a trusted adult to talk to.

**Assessment:** Stress-management plan.

**Answer key:** Plan rubric: 4 = names top stressors, matches specific strategies to each, includes time management and a support person, plus a daily log; 3 = missing one; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.
