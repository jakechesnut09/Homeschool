# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 13

## English Language Arts 6: Writing: modern myth or hero's journey story

**Prepare:** Gather: hero's journey diagram, computer. Print a hero's journey diagram (ordinary world, call to adventure, trials, climax, return).

**Watch for:**
- Making the story all action with no character change. A hero's journey shows the hero changing.
- Shifting tense (past to present) mid-story. Choose one tense and keep it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hero's journey
*Student:* Map Odysseus's and Brian's stories onto the hero's journey and plan your own hero's journey story or modern myth.

*How to teach:* Map Odysseus and Brian onto the hero's journey together; the child plans their own.

- **Q:** Map Brian onto two stages of the hero's journey.
  **A:** Call to adventure: the plane crash; trials: hunger, the porcupine, the moose and tornado; return: rescue, changed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft beginning
*Student:* Draft your story's opening and call to adventure using vivid setting details.

*How to teach:* The child drafts the opening and call to adventure with vivid setting.

- **Q:** What is a call to adventure?
  **A:** The event that pulls the hero out of ordinary life into the challenge.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft trials
*Student:* Draft two trials your hero faces, using dialogue and transitions that show time shifts.

*How to teach:* The child drafts two trials with dialogue and time-shift transitions.

- **Q:** Give three transitions showing time shifts.
  **A:** Later that night; three days passed; meanwhile.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise the climax and return; edit for pronoun case and consistent verb tense, then type the final draft.

*How to teach:* Revise the climax and return; edit for pronoun case and verb tense; type final.

- **Q:** Correct the tense shift: 'She ran to the cave and grabs the torch.'
  **A:** 'She ran to the cave and grabbed the torch.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Share and check
*Student:* Read your story aloud and score it with the narrative rubric; take the unit test on myths and the Odyssey.

*How to teach:* The child reads aloud, self-scores, and takes the unit test.

- **Q:** What is hubris?
  **A:** Excessive pride, which often leads to a hero's downfall in Greek stories.

**Assessment:** Narrative scored on rubric; unit test.

**Answer key:** Narrative 4-point rubric — 4: focused small moment, engaging lead, well-paced sequence, correctly punctuated dialogue, vivid sensory detail and a reflective ending, few errors; 3: all elements present but uneven detail or several errors; 2: summary-like, little dialogue or detail, errors interfere; 1: incomplete or off-task. Unit test sample: 1) myth = traditional story explaining nature or teaching values 2) epic hero = larger-than-life hero on a long journey 3) Prometheus stole fire for humans 4) Pandora's box left Hope 5) Polyphemus is a Cyclops, son of Poseidon 6) Odysseus's flaw = pride 7) hubris = excessive pride.

## Mathematics 6: Meanings of division

**Prepare:** Gather: fraction strips, measuring cups. Check your measuring-cup set includes ¼ cup; a ¾ cup can be made with ½ + ¼.

**Watch for:**
- Believing division always makes a number smaller. Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a quotient larger than the dividend.
- Ignoring the remainder as a fraction of a group: in 2 ÷ ¾ the leftover ½ is ⅔ of a ¾-group, not ½.

### Day 1 (Monday): Size of divisor and quotient
*Student:* Predict and then compute what happens to quotients like 12 / 6, 12 / 1, 12 / 1/2, and write a rule about divisors less than 1.

*How to teach:* Write 12 ÷ 6, 12 ÷ 3, 12 ÷ 1, 12 ÷ ½. Have the child predict, compute, and state the pattern.

- **Q:** What is 12 ÷ ½? Why is it bigger than 12?
  **A:** 24. There are two halves in each whole, so 24 halves in 12. Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a quotient greater than the dividend.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Two meanings
*Student:* Write two different story problems for 6 / 2 (how many groups vs. how much per group) and draw a diagram for each.

*How to teach:* Model two stories for 6 ÷ 2: '6 cookies, 2 per bag — how many bags?' and '6 cookies into 2 bags — how many per bag?'. The child writes and draws both.

- **Q:** Write a 'how many groups' story for 6 ÷ 2.
  **A:** Example: 6 feet of rope cut into 2-foot pieces — how many pieces? 3.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): How many groups?
*Student:* Use measuring cups to find how many 1/4 cups fit in 3 cups and how many 3/4 cups fit in 3 cups; record as division equations.

*How to teach:* Fill a 3-cup container with ¼-cup scoops, counting; then with ¾-cup scoops.

- **Q:** How many ¼ cups are in 3 cups? How many ¾ cups?
  **A:** 3 ÷ ¼ = 12 and 3 ÷ ¾ = 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Using diagrams
*Student:* Draw tape diagrams to solve 8 'how many groups' fraction problems, including answers that are not whole numbers.

*How to teach:* Draw a tape of 2 wholes split into fourths; circle groups of ¾. Show 2 full groups and ⅔ of another.

- **Q:** 2 ÷ ¾ = ?
  **A:** 2⅔ — two full groups of ¾ use 1½, and the leftover ½ is ⅔ of a ¾-group.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: match equations, diagrams and stories for fraction division.

*How to teach:* Give the matching quiz independently.

- **Q:** How many ⅓-cup scoops are in 2 cups?
  **A:** 6.

**Assessment:** Quiz on interpreting division and fraction diagrams.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (match equation–diagram–story): 1) 3 ÷ ¼ = 12 2) 3 ÷ ¾ = 4 3) 2 ÷ ¾ = 2⅔ 4) 6 ÷ ½ = 12 5) 'How many ⅓-cup scoops in 2 cups?' → 2 ÷ ⅓ = 6 6) '5 lb shared equally into 2 bags' → 5 ÷ 2 = 2½ lb each 7) 1½ ÷ ½ = 3.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Uneven heating and global winds

**Prepare:** Gather: flashlight, graph paper, globe or ball, lazy Susan or paper plate, marker. Darken the room for the flashlight activity.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the equator is warmer because it's closer to the sun. It's because sunlight hits more directly, concentrating energy.
- Thinking winds blow straight from poles to equator. Earth's rotation curves them (Coriolis).

### Day 1 (Monday): Flashlight angles
*Student:* Shine a flashlight on graph paper straight down and at an angle; count squares lit to model why the equator is warmer.

*How to teach:* Shine straight down vs. angled; count lit squares.

- **Q:** Which beam lights more squares, and what does it model?
  **A:** The angled beam spreads over more squares — energy is less concentrated, like sunlight near the poles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Convection cells
*Student:* Draw global convection cells (Hadley, Ferrel, polar) and explain how they form from uneven heating.

*How to teach:* Draw the three cells per hemisphere.

- **Q:** Where does air rise in the Hadley cell?
  **A:** At the equator, where heating is strongest.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coriolis effect
*Student:* Draw a straight line on a spinning paper plate to model the Coriolis effect and explain curved winds.

*How to teach:* Draw a line across a spinning plate.

- **Q:** Why does the line curve?
  **A:** The plate turns underneath, like Earth rotating; winds appear deflected (right in the Northern Hemisphere).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Global wind map
*Student:* Label trade winds, westerlies and polar easterlies on a world map and explain which affects California.

*How to teach:* Label wind belts on a map.

- **Q:** Which wind belt affects California?
  **A:** The westerlies (blowing from west to east).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on uneven heating and global winds.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Why is the equator warmer than the poles?
  **A:** Sunlight strikes it more directly, concentrating energy.

**Assessment:** Global wind map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) direct (vertical) light covers less area, so energy is concentrated 2) Hadley cells: air rises at the equator and sinks near 30° 3) Coriolis effect deflects winds right in the Northern Hemisphere 4) trade winds (0–30°) blow from east; westerlies (30–60°) from west; polar easterlies 5) California lies in the prevailing westerlies. Map rubric: all wind belts labeled with arrows and latitudes.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Origins of the ancient Hebrews

**Prepare:** Gather: map of ancient Canaan and Egypt, Hebrew Bible excerpts: Genesis 12 and Exodus 20. Print Genesis 12 and Exodus 20. Approach religious texts as historical and cultural sources; respect your family's beliefs while noting what is tradition vs. archaeology.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking monotheism was common. Most ancient peoples were polytheistic.
- Confusing Hebrews, Israelites and Jews. Explain how the names developed over time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Geography of Canaan
*Student:* Map Abraham's journey from Ur to Canaan as described in Genesis and the location of ancient Israel.

*How to teach:* Map Abraham's journey.

- **Q:** Where did Abraham's journey begin and end according to Genesis?
  **A:** From Ur in Mesopotamia to Canaan.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Monotheism
*Student:* Compare monotheism and polytheism and explain why historians consider Judaism's belief in one God significant.

*How to teach:* Compare monotheism and polytheism.

- **Q:** Why is Judaism's monotheism historically significant?
  **A:** It was one of the earliest lasting monotheistic faiths and influenced Christianity and Islam.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Exodus
*Student:* Read the Exodus account and map the route; explain the significance of the Exodus in Jewish tradition and Passover.

*How to teach:* Read the Exodus account; map the route.

- **Q:** What does Passover commemorate?
  **A:** The Exodus — the Israelites' liberation from slavery in Egypt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): The Ten Commandments
*Student:* Read Exodus 20 and categorize the commandments as duties to God and to other people; connect to later legal traditions.

*How to teach:* Categorize the commandments.

- **Q:** Give one commandment about duties to people.
  **A:** Honor your father and mother; do not murder; do not steal; do not bear false witness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on early Hebrew history and beliefs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What is monotheism?
  **A:** Belief in one God.

**Assessment:** Map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Map: Ur to Haran to Canaan; Egypt and the Sinai. Quiz: 1) Abraham is considered the founder of the Hebrew people (journey from Ur to Canaan in Genesis) 2) monotheism = belief in one God 3) Moses led the Exodus from Egypt 4) Passover commemorates the Exodus 5) Ten Commandments: duties to God (first commandments) and to people (honor parents, do not kill, steal, lie or covet).

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Visual persuasion: posters

**Prepare:** Find WPA National Park posters (Library of Congress) and two modern public service posters. Gather poster paper, markers, a ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Using too many colors and fonts. A limited palette and one or two lettering styles make a clearer message.
- Writing too much text. Posters should be read in seconds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analyze posters
*Student:* Analyze three public service posters (e.g., WPA National Park posters) for focal point, color and text.

*How to teach:* Analyze three posters for focal point, color and text. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What makes WPA posters easy to read from a distance?
  **A:** Bold simple shapes, a limited color palette and large, clear lettering.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan a message
*Student:* Choose a message (water conservation, reading) and sketch three thumbnail layouts.

*How to teach:* Choose a message; sketch three thumbnail layouts. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why sketch thumbnails?
  **A:** To try different layouts quickly before choosing the best one.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lettering
*Student:* Practice block and decorative lettering for your headline.

*How to teach:* Practice block and decorative lettering for the headline. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is typography?
  **A:** The style and arrangement of letters.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final poster
*Student:* Create the final poster with a clear focal point and limited color palette.

*How to teach:* Create the final poster with a clear focal point and limited palette. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How can you create a focal point?
  **A:** Make it largest, highest contrast, or place it where lines lead the eye.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & critique
*Student:* Ask two viewers what the message is and revise if they misread it.

*How to teach:* Ask two viewers what the message is; revise if misread. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why test your poster on viewers?
  **A:** To check that the message is communicated clearly.

**Assessment:** Persuasive poster and viewer feedback.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric: 4 = message understood by both viewers, strong focal point, limited palette, readable lettering; 3 = minor issues; 2 = message unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Basketball: offense and defense

**Prepare:** Gather: basketball, hoop. Hoop or target. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Standing still after passing. Cut after the pass.
- Crossing feet on defense. Slide.

### Day 1 (Monday): Give-and-go
*Student:* Practice the give-and-go with a partner and cut to the basket.

*How to teach:* Practice give-and-go.

- **Q:** What is a give-and-go?
  **A:** Pass to a teammate, cut toward the basket, and receive the ball back.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Defense
*Student:* Practice defensive slides and staying between your player and the basket.

*How to teach:* Defensive slides.

- **Q:** Where should a defender stay?
  **A:** Between their player and the basket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rebounding
*Student:* Practice boxing out and rebounding in a 1v1 drill.

*How to teach:* Box out and rebound.

- **Q:** What does boxing out mean?
  **A:** Putting your body between your opponent and the basket to get the rebound.

### Day 4 (Thursday): 2v2 games
*Student:* Play 2v2 half-court games applying offensive and defensive strategies.

*How to teach:* 2v2 games.

- **Q:** Why spread out on offense?
  **A:** To create space for drives and passes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Diagram one play you used and explain why it worked.

*How to teach:* Diagram a play.

- **Q:** Why does the give-and-go work?
  **A:** The defender watches the ball, letting the cutter get free.

**Assessment:** Diagrammed play and observation.

**Answer key:** Diagrammed play rubric: shows players, passes, cuts and explains why it works (e.g., give-and-go: pass, cut past defender, receive return pass near the basket). Observation: uses defensive slides, boxes out, moves after passing.

## Health 6: Bullying and harassment

**Prepare:** Print materials from StopBullying.gov. Write 8 scenarios mixing bullying and ordinary conflict. PARENT PREVIEW: ask whether your child has experienced or witnessed bullying; listen without overreacting.

**Watch for:**
- Calling every disagreement bullying. Bullying is unwanted aggressive behavior with a power imbalance that is repeated or likely to be repeated; a one-time argument between equals is conflict.
- Thinking bystanders have no role. Bystanders can become upstanders by safely helping or reporting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Defining bullying
*Student:* Distinguish bullying (repeated, power imbalance) from conflict in 8 scenarios.

*How to teach:* Your child classifies 8 scenarios as bullying or conflict, citing repetition and power imbalance.

- **Q:** Two friends argue once about a game. Bullying or conflict?
  **A:** Conflict — it's a one-time disagreement between equals.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forms
*Student:* Chart physical, verbal, social/relational and cyber bullying with examples and effects.

*How to teach:* Chart physical, verbal, social/relational and cyber bullying with examples and effects. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Give an example of social (relational) bullying.
  **A:** Spreading rumors, deliberately excluding someone, or turning friends against them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bystander roles
*Student:* Learn bystander, upstander and reporter roles and practice safe upstander responses.

*How to teach:* Learn bystander, upstander and reporter roles; practice safe upstander responses. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name two safe ways to be an upstander.
  **A:** Don't join in or laugh, invite the targeted person to leave with you, say 'That's not cool,' or tell a trusted adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reporting
*Student:* Write a script for reporting bullying to a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Write a script for reporting bullying to a trusted adult. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What should you include when reporting bullying?
  **A:** Who was involved, what happened, when and where, how often, and any evidence such as screenshots.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a bullying scenario quiz.

*How to teach:* Complete the bullying scenario quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What is a power imbalance?
  **A:** When one person has more power (size, popularity, numbers, information) than the other.

**Assessment:** Scenario quiz.

**Answer key:** Scenario quiz key: bullying = unwanted, aggressive, power imbalance, repeated; four forms: physical, verbal, social, cyber; upstander actions; reporting to a trusted adult; telling is not tattling when someone is being hurt. Proficient = 80%.
