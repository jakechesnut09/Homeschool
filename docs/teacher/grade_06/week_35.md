# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 35

## English Language Arts 6: Revising and presenting research

**Prepare:** Gather: draft, slides or poster software. Set up slide or poster software.

**Watch for:**
- Putting paragraphs on slides. Use images and key words; speak the details.
- Rushing the talk. Practice with a timer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for precise domain-specific vocabulary and logical order of ideas.

*How to teach:* Revise for domain vocabulary and logical order.

- **Q:** What is domain-specific vocabulary?
  **A:** Words specific to a subject, such as 'quarry' and 'limestone' for pyramids.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit for conventions, check every citation, and publish the final report.

*How to teach:* Edit, check citations, publish.

- **Q:** What should you check in every citation?
  **A:** That it matches a works-cited entry and the information really came from that source.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build slides
*Student:* Create 6-8 slides with images and minimal text that clarify your key findings.

*How to teach:* Build 6–8 slides with images and minimal text.

- **Q:** How much text should a slide have?
  **A:** A title and a few key words or a short phrase; the speaker explains.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse a 5-minute presentation focusing on eye contact, volume and clear pronunciation.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a 5-minute talk.

- **Q:** Name three speaking skills to practice.
  **A:** Eye contact, volume, clear pronunciation, pace.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present to family or co-op and answer audience questions.

*How to teach:* Present and answer questions.

- **Q:** How should you answer a question you don't know?
  **A:** Say so honestly and offer to find out.

**Assessment:** Research report and presentation rubrics.

**Answer key:** Report rubric — 4: accurate, well-organized, domain vocabulary, correct citations, few errors; 3: minor issues; 2: weak organization or citations; 1: incomplete. Presentation rubric — 4: 6–8 clear slides, 5-minute talk with eye contact, volume and clear pronunciation, answers questions with knowledge; 3: minor issues; 2: reads slides; 1: incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Modeling project: plan a trip

**Prepare:** Gather: map of California, online prices, spreadsheet or paper. Let the child pick a California destination and look up the driving distance and prices together.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the return trip when computing fuel.
- Adding tax as 8 instead of 8% (0.08 × total).

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a destination
*Student:* Pick a California destination, find the distance, and use a car's miles-per-gallon rate to calculate fuel needed.

*How to teach:* Find distance and mpg; compute gallons and fuel cost.

- **Q:** A 380-mile trip in a car getting 28 mpg needs how much gas? At $4.80/gal, cost?
  **A:** About 13.6 gallons; about $65.14 (one way).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Budget
*Student:* Build a budget with lodging, food and tickets, compute totals with decimals and add 8% sales tax.

*How to teach:* Build a budget table; add 8% tax by multiplying by 0.08 (or the total by 1.08).

- **Q:** A $412.50 budget plus 8% tax?
  **A:** $445.50.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Time and speed
*Student:* Use average speed to plan driving time and a schedule of stops.

*How to teach:* Use average speed to estimate driving time and plan stops.

- **Q:** 380 miles at 60 mph takes how long?
  **A:** 6⅓ hours = 6 hours 20 minutes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Packing volume
*Student:* Determine whether your luggage (measure boxes or bags) fits a car trunk of given volume, using fractional dimensions.

*How to teach:* Measure bags; compute volumes and compare with the trunk.

- **Q:** A trunk holds 14 cu ft. Do four 2 × 1½ × ¾ ft suitcases fit by volume?
  **A:** Each is 2.25 cu ft; four = 9 cu ft, so yes by volume (shape matters too).

### Day 5 (Friday): Present the plan
*Student:* Present your trip plan with all calculations and answer family questions about your choices.

*How to teach:* The child presents the plan and answers questions.

- **Q:** How would doubling the trip distance change fuel cost?
  **A:** It would double (fuel is proportional to distance).

**Assessment:** Trip-planning project rubric (accuracy, representations, explanation).

**Answer key:** Project rubric: 4 = all calculations correct and shown (fuel = distance ÷ mpg, cost, budget with 8% tax, time = distance ÷ speed, volume comparison), clear tables, confident explanation; 3 = one or two calculation errors; 2 = several sections incomplete; 1 = little math shown. Sample check: 380 mi at 28 mpg ≈ 13.6 gal; at $4.80/gal ≈ $65.14 one way; $412.50 budget + 8% tax = $445.50; 380 mi at 60 mph ≈ 6 h 20 min.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Solar oven: test and iterate

**Prepare:** Gather: solar oven, oven thermometer, s'mores ingredients. Use an oven thermometer; check the forecast for sun.

**Watch for:**
- Changing several features at once.
- Opening the lid often, letting heat out.

### Day 1 (Monday): First test
*Student:* Test the oven in direct sun, recording internal temperature every 5 minutes for 45 minutes.

*How to teach:* Test in direct sun; record every 5 minutes.

- **Q:** Why record every 5 minutes?
  **A:** To see the heating pattern over time, not just the final temperature.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Analyze
*Student:* Graph results and compare with a classmate's, family member's or online design data to find the best features.

*How to teach:* Graph and compare designs.

- **Q:** What does a flat section on the graph mean?
  **A:** Energy coming in equals energy escaping.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Modify
*Student:* Make one change (extra reflector, insulation, angle) and record why you chose it.

*How to teach:* Make one modification and explain.

- **Q:** Why change only one feature?
  **A:** So any change in performance can be attributed to it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Second test
*Student:* Retest and try to melt a s'more; compare the two graphs.

*How to teach:* Retest; try a s'more.

- **Q:** How did the graph change?
  **A:** Answers vary; expect a faster rise or higher max if the change improved transfer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check-in
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how the modification affected thermal energy transfer.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph about the modification.

- **Q:** Which heat transfer did more insulation reduce?
  **A:** Conduction.

**Assessment:** Test data and iteration analysis.

**Answer key:** Analysis rubric — 4: two graphs compared, one change explained by heat transfer (e.g., extra insulation reduced conduction loss, raising maximum temperature from 52°C to 63°C); 3: comparison without explanation; 2: one test only; 1: incomplete.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Roman legacy and the decline of the West

**Prepare:** Gather: Latin roots list, images of the Pantheon and Colosseum. Print a Latin roots list and Pantheon/Colosseum images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Rome 'fell' in one day. The decline took centuries; the West ended in 476 CE, the East continued.
- Thinking 'Eureka' is Latin. It's Greek.

### Day 1 (Monday): Architecture and engineering
*Student:* Compare the Pantheon and Colosseum with modern buildings and identify Roman arches, domes and concrete.

*How to teach:* Compare the Pantheon and Colosseum with modern buildings.

- **Q:** Name three Roman building innovations.
  **A:** Arches, domes, concrete.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Latin everywhere
*Student:* Find 20 English words with Latin roots and translate common Latin mottos (e.g., California's 'Eureka' is Greek; the U.S. 'E pluribus unum' is Latin).

*How to teach:* Find Latin roots; translate mottos.

- **Q:** What does 'E pluribus unum' mean?
  **A:** 'Out of many, one.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Roman law
*Student:* Identify Roman legal principles still used today (innocent until proven guilty, rights of citizens).

*How to teach:* Identify legal principles.

- **Q:** Name a Roman legal principle used today.
  **A:** Innocent until proven guilty.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decline and fall
*Student:* Read about the division of the empire and the fall of the West in 476 CE and rank causes by importance.

*How to teach:* Read about the fall; rank causes.

- **Q:** When did the Western Roman Empire fall?
  **A:** 476 CE.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit review
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on Ancient Rome.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** What does California's motto 'Eureka' mean?
  **A:** 'I have found it' (Greek).

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test sample: geography; legends; Republic structure; Twelve Tables; Punic Wars; Caesar; Augustus; Pax Romana; roads, aqueducts, concrete, arches, domes; Latin roots; spread of Christianity; 476 CE fall of the West (Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus); causes: invasions, economic trouble, political instability, an overextended empire.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Portfolio and artist statement

**Prepare:** Selected artworks, construction paper for mats, labels, and a camera for the digital portfolio.

**Watch for:**
- Writing an artist statement that only lists projects. It should explain growth, interests and ideas.
- Leaving out media arts and music. A complete portfolio includes animation and recordings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Artist statement
*Student:* Write a one-paragraph artist statement about your growth and interests.

*How to teach:* Write a one-paragraph artist statement. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What belongs in an artist statement?
  **A:** Your interests, ideas, techniques you used and how you grew.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wall labels
*Student:* Write wall labels (title, media, year, one sentence) for each piece.

*How to teach:* Write wall labels for each piece. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Write a sample wall label.
  **A:** 'City Corner, colored pencil, 2027. Two-point perspective shows a busy street.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mat and mount
*Student:* Mat and mount 2D works and prepare stands for 3D works.

*How to teach:* Mat and mount 2D works; prepare stands for 3D. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why mat artwork?
  **A:** It frames and protects the work and looks professional.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Digital portfolio
*Student:* Photograph all works and assemble a digital portfolio including your animation and music recordings.

*How to teach:* Photograph works and assemble a digital portfolio with animation and music. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why include a digital portfolio?
  **A:** It preserves work and is easy to share.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Evaluate portfolio completeness with a checklist.

*How to teach:* Evaluate completeness with a checklist. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What should the checklist include?
  **A:** Artworks, labels, artist statement, digital portfolio, recordings.

**Assessment:** Portfolio and artist statement.

**Answer key:** Portfolio checklist: 8–10 works, labels, matting, digital portfolio including animation and music. Artist statement rubric: 4 = growth, interests and specific techniques; 3 = most; 2 = list only; 1 = missing.

## Physical Education 6: End-of-year fitness reassessment

**Prepare:** Gather: PACER audio or measured course, mat, sit-and-reach box. Same test setups as Weeks 2–3. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Judging success only by final scores.
- Comparing with others.

### Day 1 (Monday): PACER or mile
*Student:* Retake the PACER or mile run.

*How to teach:* Retake PACER/mile.

- **Q:** Why use the same test each time?
  **A:** To compare fairly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Curl-ups and push-ups
*Student:* Retake curl-up and push-up tests.

*How to teach:* Retake curl-ups and push-ups.

- **Q:** What does curl-up improvement show?
  **A:** Stronger abdominal muscular strength/endurance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility
*Student:* Retake sit-and-reach and trunk-lift tests.

*How to teach:* Retake flexibility tests.

- **Q:** What component does sit-and-reach measure?
  **A:** Flexibility.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analyze
*Student:* Graph baseline, mid-year and final scores and check whether goals were met.

*How to teach:* Graph and analyze.

- **Q:** Baseline 8 push-ups, mid-year 11, final 15. Total improvement?
  **A:** 7 push-ups.

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebrate
*Student:* Play a favorite game and share one fitness accomplishment.

*How to teach:* Play; share an accomplishment.

- **Q:** Why celebrate effort, not just scores?
  **A:** It builds lifelong motivation.

**Assessment:** Fitness growth graph.

**Answer key:** Fitness growth graph: baseline, mid-year and final for PACER/mile, curl-ups, push-ups, sit-and-reach and trunk lift; notes on which goals were met.

## Health 6: Advocacy project

**Prepare:** Gather poster paper, computer, camera. Print an advocacy rubric: clear message, accurate facts from valid sources (cited), audience fit, persuasive design, call to action, presentation delivery.

**Watch for:**
- Using facts without sources. Every fact should be cited from a valid source.
- Leaving out a call to action. Audiences need a specific action.

### Day 1 (Monday): Research
*Student:* Gather 4 facts from valid sources about your chosen issue (kindness, emergency preparedness, sun safety, clean air).

*How to teach:* Gather 4 facts from valid sources. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name two valid sources for your topic.
  **A:** CDC.gov, Ready.gov, AirNow.gov, KidsHealth, county public health department.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Identify audience, message and call to action.

*How to teach:* Identify audience, message and call to action. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Give an example of a call to action.
  **A:** 'Check your smoke alarms this weekend!'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Produce a poster, brochure, slide show or short video.

*How to teach:* Produce a poster, brochure, slide show or video. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How can design make the message more persuasive?
  **A:** Clear headlines, strong images, simple facts and a bold call to action.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice a 3-minute presentation and get feedback.

*How to teach:* Practice a 3-minute presentation; get feedback. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What makes a presentation effective?
  **A:** Eye contact, clear voice, accurate facts and a confident call to action.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Revise using an advocacy rubric.

*How to teach:* Revise using the rubric. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why revise before presenting?
  **A:** To fix weak areas the rubric or feedback revealed.

**Assessment:** Advocacy rubric draft score.

**Answer key:** Advocacy rubric (1–4 each): clear health-enhancing message; at least 4 accurate, cited facts; audience fit; persuasive design; call to action. Proficient = 3+ each.
