# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 36

## English Language Arts 6: Year-end reflection and assessment

**Prepare:** Gather: writing portfolio, summer reading list. Choose an unfamiliar short story and article (CommonLit) and a writing prompt with two short sources; gather the year's portfolio.

**Watch for:**
- Answering constructed responses without evidence. Use the RACE frame: Restate, Answer, Cite, Explain.
- Not planning timed essays. Spend 5 minutes outlining.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading assessment
*Student:* Read an unfamiliar story and article and answer evidence-based constructed-response questions.

*How to teach:* The child reads an unfamiliar story and article and answers constructed responses using RACE.

- **Q:** What does RACE stand for?
  **A:** Restate the question, Answer it, Cite evidence, Explain the evidence.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): On-demand writing
*Student:* Write a timed argument or explanatory essay from a prompt with two short sources.

*How to teach:* Give a timed essay (about 45–60 minutes) with two sources.

- **Q:** How should time be used in an on-demand essay?
  **A:** About 5–10 minutes planning, most time drafting, last 5 minutes rereading and fixing errors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar and vocabulary
*Student:* Take a cumulative test on pronouns, punctuation, roots and figurative language.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative grammar and vocabulary test.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Me and her read Hatchet.'
  **A:** 'She and I read Hatchet.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Portfolio
*Student:* Choose your best three pieces from the year and write a cover letter explaining your growth.

*How to teach:* The child picks three pieces and writes a cover letter about growth.

- **Q:** What makes a strong portfolio letter?
  **A:** Specific examples from the pieces showing growth, plus goals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Looking ahead
*Student:* Create a summer reading list of 6 books and share your favorite read of the year in a book talk.

*How to teach:* The child makes a summer list of six books and gives a book talk.

- **Q:** What should a book talk include?
  **A:** Title, author, a hook about the plot without spoilers, and why you'd recommend it.

**Assessment:** Final reading, writing and language assessments; portfolio letter.

**Answer key:** Reading assessment: score each constructed response 2 = correct answer with text evidence explained, 1 = answer without evidence, 0 = incorrect. On-demand essay: use the argument or explanatory rubric (4–1). Grammar/vocabulary test sample: 1) 'between you and me' 2) 'he and I' 3) intensive pronoun: 'herself' 4) fix pronoun shift 5) fix vague pronoun 6) nonrestrictive commas 7) chron = time 8) metaphor vs simile 9) personification 10) connotation. Portfolio letter: 4 = three pieces with specific evidence of growth and goals.

## Mathematics 6: Cumulative review and final assessment

**Prepare:** Gather: review packets, Khan Academy course challenge. Print review sets; set up the Khan Academy 6th grade course challenge.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing review — have the child star problems they are unsure of and revisit them.
- Rounding too early in multi-step problems.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: ratios and number
*Student:* Complete a review set on ratios, percents, fraction division and decimals; star problems to revisit.

*How to teach:* The child works the ratio/number review set, starring uncertain problems; review starred ones together.

- **Q:** Find 40% of 65 and compute 2½ ÷ ⅚.
  **A:** 26 and 3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: expressions and geometry
*Student:* Complete a review set on equations, expressions, area, surface area and volume.

*How to teach:* The child works the expressions/geometry review set; review stars.

- **Q:** Solve ⅔x = 18. Find the volume of a 2 × 3 × 4.5 prism.
  **A:** x = 27; 27 cubic units.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: rational numbers and data
*Student:* Complete a review set on negatives, the coordinate plane and statistics; then take the Khan Academy 6th grade course challenge.

*How to teach:* The child works the rational numbers/data set, then takes the Khan Academy course challenge.

- **Q:** Distance from (−4, 3) to (6, 3)? Median of 3, 9, 4, 7, 12, 5?
  **A:** 10 units; 6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam
*Student:* Take a 40-problem cumulative final assessment covering all grade 6 domains.

*How to teach:* Give the 40-problem final in one or two sittings without help.

- **Q:** Evaluate 5 + 2 × 3².
  **A:** 23.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection and goals
*Student:* Correct the final, write a reflection on your growth this year, and set goals for Math 7.

*How to teach:* Correct the final together; the child writes a reflection and 2–3 goals for Math 7.

- **Q:** Which topic grew most this year and what's a goal for next year?
  **A:** Answers vary; a strong reflection names a specific skill with evidence (e.g., 'I can now divide fractions using reciprocals') and a concrete goal.

**Assessment:** Cumulative final exam.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Capstone presentation and year review

**Prepare:** Gather: science notebook, slides or poster. Gather the science notebook and photos for the presentation.

**Watch for:**
- Presenting results without explaining the science.
- Treating units as separate. Energy connects them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Prepare presentation
*Student:* Create a presentation of your solar oven design process with photos, graphs and conclusions.

*How to teach:* Build the presentation.

- **Q:** What should a capstone presentation include?
  **A:** Problem, criteria/constraints, designs, data, modifications and conclusions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Present
*Student:* Present to your family or co-op and answer questions about the science behind your design.

*How to teach:* Present and answer questions.

- **Q:** Explain how your oven converts sunlight to thermal energy.
  **A:** Light passes through the plastic, is absorbed by black surfaces and becomes thermal energy, which is trapped by the wrap and insulation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Year review: energy
*Student:* Make a concept map showing how energy connects thermal energy, weather, forces and living systems.

*How to teach:* Make an energy concept map.

- **Q:** How does energy connect weather and the water cycle?
  **A:** The sun's energy evaporates water and unevenly heats Earth, driving winds, currents and weather.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take a cumulative assessment covering all units.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative assessment.

- **Q:** What do cells use energy from food for?
  **A:** To carry out life processes (mitochondria release the energy).

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on your favorite investigation and a question you want to explore in grade 7.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection.

- **Q:** What makes a good scientific question for next year?
  **A:** A testable question with measurable variables.

**Assessment:** Capstone presentation rubric and cumulative assessment.

**Answer key:** Capstone rubric — 4: clear problem, design process, data graphs, science explanations, answers questions accurately; 3: minor gaps; 2: limited science; 1: incomplete. Cumulative assessment sample: independent/dependent variables; particle motion and temperature; conduction/convection/radiation; water cycle; fronts; global winds; rain shadow; greenhouse effect; earthquakes along faults; magnetic and electric forces; gravity; KE and PE; cell theory and organelles; body systems.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Legacies of the ancient world: final project

**Prepare:** Gather: year-long timeline, poster board or slides. Gather the year's timeline and notes; poster board or slides.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a legacy without tracing it across civilizations.
- Not connecting to life today.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a legacy
*Student:* Choose one ancient legacy (writing, law, democracy, mathematics, religion, engineering) to trace across at least three civilizations.

*How to teach:* The child chooses a legacy and plans.

- **Q:** Trace 'law' across three civilizations.
  **A:** Hammurabi's Code (Babylon), Ten Commandments (Hebrews), Twelve Tables (Rome).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Gather evidence including at least two primary sources from the year and take notes.

*How to teach:* Gather evidence including two primary sources.

- **Q:** Name a primary source for democracy.
  **A:** Pericles' Funeral Oration.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Create a museum exhibit poster or slide show connecting the legacy to life in California today.

*How to teach:* Create the exhibit.

- **Q:** Connect Roman engineering to California today.
  **A:** Aqueducts → California Aqueduct; arches and concrete in buildings.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present your exhibit to family or co-op as a museum docent and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present as a docent.

- **Q:** What makes a good docent?
  **A:** Clear explanations, knowledge and answering visitors' questions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final assessment
*Student:* Complete the year-end timeline and take a cumulative final assessment on all seven civilizations.

*How to teach:* Complete the timeline; give the final.

- **Q:** Which came first: Hammurabi or Alexander?
  **A:** Hammurabi (~1750 BCE vs. ~330 BCE).

**Assessment:** Legacy project rubric and cumulative final.

**Answer key:** Legacy project rubric — 4: one legacy traced across 3+ civilizations, two primary sources, accurate details, clear connection to California today, confident docent presentation; 3: two civilizations or one source; 2: limited evidence; 1: incomplete. Cumulative final sample: one question per civilization — Mesopotamia (cuneiform, Hammurabi), Egypt (Nile, pharaohs), Kush (Meroë), Hebrews (monotheism), Greece (democracy), India (Ashoka, zero), China (Confucius, Silk Roads), Rome (Republic, law).

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Arts showcase

**Prepare:** Exhibition space, instrument, audience invitations, and comment cards for visitors.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a docent only reads labels. A docent explains, answers questions and engages visitors.
- Ignoring visitor feedback. Comments help you grow.

### Day 1 (Monday): Install
*Student:* Install your exhibition following your display plan.

*How to teach:* Install the exhibition following the plan. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How high should 2D art hang?
  **A:** Center at about eye level (around 57–60 inches).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse a short music performance and dance or monologue for the showcase.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a music piece and a dance or monologue. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What should you rehearse besides the piece itself?
  **A:** Introductions, transitions and bows.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Showcase
*Student:* Host the showcase: guide visitors as a docent and perform.

*How to teach:* Host the showcase as docent; perform. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is a docent?
  **A:** A guide who leads visitors and explains artworks.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Collect written comments from visitors and summarize them.

*How to teach:* Collect and summarize visitor comments. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What patterns did you notice in comments?
  **A:** Answer varies; should summarize common themes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final reflection
*Student:* Write a final reflection on your favorite project and set an arts goal for grade 7.

*How to teach:* Write a final reflection; set a grade 7 arts goal. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What was your favorite project and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name the project and a specific reason.

**Assessment:** Showcase rubric and final reflection.

**Answer key:** Showcase rubric (1–4 each): installation quality, docent talk, performance, response to feedback. Reflection proficient if it names a favorite project with reasons, growth evidence, and a specific grade 7 goal.

**Watch for:**

*How to teach:* Run field day.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection.

*Student:* Write a summer activity plan with at least 60 minutes of activity per day.

*How to teach:* Write a summer plan.

*Student:* Complete a year-end quiz on fitness concepts and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Year-end quiz.

## Health 6: Presentation and year review

**Prepare:** Arrange an audience (family, co-op, community group). Prepare a review game on mental health, safety and personal health. Print the end-of-year assessment and the week 1 wellness wheel.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking health learning ends with the course. Skills like decision making and communication are used for life.
- Thinking summer means no safety planning. Water, heat, sun and bike safety matter most in summer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Present your advocacy project to family, co-op or a community group.

*How to teach:* Present the advocacy project. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What was your call to action?
  **A:** Answer varies; should be specific.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review game
*Student:* Play a review game on mental health, safety and personal health.

*How to teach:* Play the review game. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name three steps of DECIDE.
  **A:** Any three of: Define, Explore, Consider, Identify values, Decide, Evaluate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year health assessment.

*How to teach:* Take the end-of-year assessment. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What number do you call or text for mental health crisis support?
  **A:** 988.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Goal review
*Student:* Review your wellness wheel from week 1 and re-rate each area.

*How to teach:* Re-rate the wellness wheel and compare with week 1. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Which area improved most and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; should cite specific habits.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on your growth and a summer health and safety plan.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection and summer health and safety plan. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name two summer safety practices.
  **A:** Sunscreen SPF 30+, swimming with a buddy/life jacket, helmet when biking, hydration in heat.

**Assessment:** Presentation and final assessment.

**Answer key:** Final assessment key: wellness dimensions; DECIDE; CRAAP; stress response and coping; I-messages; conflict steps; bullying definition; stop-screenshot-block-tell; 988; Check–Call–Care; Hands-Only CPR; Drop, Cover, Hold On; go-bag; gun safety rule; No-Go-Tell; pathogens and vaccines; unit price. Presentation rubric as in week 35. Proficient = 80%.
