# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 27

## English Language Arts 6: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 3-5

**Prepare:** Gather: Folger text of the play, Lambs' Tales. Choose Act 3 scenes to read (Folger text). Keep the Week 26 lovers chart.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting why the love changes. The love-in-idleness flower's juice causes it.
- Thinking the play-within-a-play is serious. 'Pyramus and Thisbe' is a tragedy performed so badly it becomes comedy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Love potion chaos
*Student:* Read selected scenes from Act 3 and chart who loves whom before and after the potion.

*How to teach:* Read selected Act 3 scenes; chart who loves whom before and after.

- **Q:** After both men are anointed, whom do Lysander and Demetrius love?
  **A:** Both love Helena; Hermia is left out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bottom's transformation
*Student:* Read the Bottom and Titania scene aloud and identify puns and wordplay.

*How to teach:* Read the Bottom/Titania scene aloud; find puns.

- **Q:** What happens to Bottom, and what is funny about Titania's reaction?
  **A:** Puck gives him a donkey's head; enchanted, Titania falls in love with him and has fairies wait on him — the queen adoring a fool with a donkey head.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Resolution
*Student:* Read Act 4 and the Pyramus and Thisbe performance in Act 5; explain how each plotline resolves.

*How to teach:* Read Act 4 and the play-within-the-play in Act 5.

- **Q:** How are the lovers' problems solved?
  **A:** Oberon has the spell removed from Lysander but leaves Demetrius loving Helena; Theseus overrules Egeus, and the two couples marry with Theseus and Hippolyta.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Play vs. retelling
*Student:* Compare the Lambs' prose version with Shakespeare's play: what is lost and gained? Write two paragraphs.

*How to teach:* Compare the Lambs' prose with the play; write two paragraphs.

- **Q:** What is lost in a prose retelling?
  **A:** Shakespeare's poetry, wordplay, jokes and stage action — the mechanicals' comedy especially.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a comprehension quiz and write a short analysis of how one scene contributes to the play's comedy.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and scene analysis.

- **Q:** Who says 'Lord, what fools these mortals be!'?
  **A:** Puck.

**Assessment:** Quiz and scene analysis.

**Answer key:** Comprehension quiz: 1) Puck anoints Lysander by mistake, so he loves Helena 2) Puck gives Bottom a donkey's head 3) Titania, under the potion, falls in love with Bottom 4) Helena believes both men are mocking her 5) 'Lord, what fools these mortals be!' — Puck 6) Oberon releases Titania; lovers paired Lysander–Hermia, Demetrius–Helena 7) Theseus overrules Egeus 8) the mechanicals perform 'Pyramus and Thisbe' at the wedding. Scene analysis rubric: 4 = names the comic techniques (mistaken identity, wordplay, slapstick, dramatic irony) with quotes; 3 = one technique; 2 = summary; 1 = off-topic.

## Mathematics 6: Inequalities

**Prepare:** Gather: number line strips. Make number-line strips the child can draw on.

**Watch for:**
- Using an open circle for ≥ or ≤. Closed (filled) circle means the number itself is included.
- Reading 'at least' as <. 'At least 48' means 48 or more: h ≥ 48.

### Day 1 (Monday): Writing inequalities
*Student:* Write inequalities for constraints like 'you must be at least 48 inches to ride' and 'fewer than 30 students' (10 situations).

*How to teach:* Translate key phrases: at least (≥), at most/no more than (≤), more than (>), fewer than (<). The child writes 10.

- **Q:** Write inequalities: at least 48 inches to ride; fewer than 30 students.
  **A:** h ≥ 48; s < 30.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Graphing solutions
*Student:* Graph x > c, x < c, x >= c and x <= c on number lines and test values with substitution.

*How to teach:* Graph x > 3 (open circle at 3, shade right) and x ≤ −1 (closed at −1, shade left). The child tests values by substitution.

- **Q:** Describe the graph of x ≤ −1.
  **A:** A closed circle at −1 with shading to the left.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Infinitely many solutions
*Student:* Explain why x > 3 has infinitely many solutions and identify whether given values are solutions.

*How to teach:* Ask the child to name solutions of x > 3 that get closer and closer to 3 (3.1, 3.01, 3.001). Discuss why there are infinitely many.

- **Q:** Is 3 a solution of x > 3? Is 3.001?
  **A:** 3 is not; 3.001 is.
- **Q:** Why does x > 3 have infinitely many solutions?
  **A:** Every number greater than 3, including all decimals and fractions, makes it true.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Inequalities in context
*Student:* Solve real problems like saving at least $120 and decide which values make sense in context (whole people, decimals of money).

*How to teach:* Discuss when only whole numbers make sense (people) vs decimals (money).

- **Q:** A van holds at most 7 people. Write an inequality and list solutions that make sense.
  **A:** p ≤ 7; p = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7 (whole numbers only).
- **Q:** You need to save at least $120. Write an inequality.
  **A:** s ≥ 120.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing, graphing and interpreting inequalities.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Write: no more than $20.
  **A:** c ≤ 20.

**Assessment:** Inequality quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) at least 48 inches → h ≥ 48 2) fewer than 30 students → s < 30 3) no more than $20 → c ≤ 20 4) more than 5 → x > 5 5) x < −2: open circle at −2, shaded left 6) Is 4 a solution of x ≥ 4? Yes 7) Is −1 a solution of x > 0? No 8) An elevator holds at most 1,500 lb → w ≤ 1,500.

## Science 6 (Integrated): The cell membrane and getting materials

**Prepare:** Gather: raw egg, vinegar, corn syrup, water, potato slices, salt. Start the vinegar soak 1–2 days before Day 2. Handle raw egg with clean hands.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking osmosis moves salt or sugar. In osmosis, water moves across the membrane.
- Thinking the egg grew in vinegar because of vinegar alone. Water from the vinegar moved in.

### Day 1 (Monday): Naked egg
*Student:* Soak a raw egg in vinegar to dissolve the shell, measuring circumference before and after.

*How to teach:* Measure the egg; soak in vinegar.

- **Q:** What are the bubbles on the egg in vinegar?
  **A:** Carbon dioxide from the acid reacting with the calcium carbonate shell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg osmosis
*Student:* Move the shell-less egg into corn syrup and later water, measuring and explaining size changes with osmosis.

*How to teach:* Move the egg to corn syrup, then water; measure.

- **Q:** Why does the egg shrink in corn syrup?
  **A:** Water moves out of the egg toward the area with less water (the syrup).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Potato osmosis
*Student:* Soak potato slices in plain and salty water and compare their mass and stiffness after an hour.

*How to teach:* Soak potato slices in plain and salt water.

- **Q:** Which slice gets limp and loses mass?
  **A:** The one in salt water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Diffusion
*Student:* Place a scented extract in a balloon and detect diffusion through the membrane; model particle movement.

*How to teach:* Put extract in a balloon; smell outside.

- **Q:** What is diffusion?
  **A:** Particles spreading from where they're more concentrated to less concentrated.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a lab report explaining how the egg membrane acts like a cell membrane.

*How to teach:* The child writes the lab report.

- **Q:** How is the egg membrane like a cell membrane?
  **A:** Both are semi-permeable: they let water through but block larger molecules.

**Assessment:** Osmosis lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric — 4: measurements before and after each soak; explains the vinegar dissolves the calcium carbonate shell (bubbles are CO₂), the egg swelled in water (water moved in) and shrank in corn syrup (water moved out); connects to a semi-permeable cell membrane; 3: one explanation missing; 2: data only; 1: incomplete.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Zhou, Mandate of Heaven, Confucius and Laozi

**Prepare:** Gather: Analects of Confucius excerpts, Dao De Jing excerpts. Print 10 Analects sayings and Dao De Jing passages.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Mandate of Heaven was permanent. It could be lost by bad rulers.
- Thinking Confucianism and Daoism agree on government. Confucians wanted active virtuous leaders; Daoists wanted minimal government.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mandate of Heaven
*Student:* Draw the dynastic cycle and explain how the Zhou used the Mandate of Heaven to justify rule.

*How to teach:* Draw the dynastic cycle.

- **Q:** What was the Mandate of Heaven?
  **A:** The belief that heaven grants the right to rule to a just ruler and takes it away from an unjust one.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Warring States
*Student:* Read about disorder in the Warring States period and list problems Confucius saw in society.

*How to teach:* Read about the Warring States.

- **Q:** What problems did Confucius see?
  **A:** Disorder, warfare, corrupt rulers and people neglecting duties.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Analects
*Student:* Read 10 sayings from the Analects and explain the five relationships and filial piety.

*How to teach:* Read Analects sayings.

- **Q:** Name the five relationships.
  **A:** Ruler–subject, father–son, husband–wife, older brother–younger brother, friend–friend.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Daoism
*Student:* Read passages of the Dao De Jing and compare Daoist ideas about nature and government with Confucian ideas.

*How to teach:* Read the Dao De Jing.

- **Q:** How does Daoism view government?
  **A:** The best government does little and lets things follow their natural course.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a dialogue between Confucius and Laozi about how to fix a troubled kingdom.

*How to teach:* The child writes the dialogue.

- **Q:** What is filial piety?
  **A:** Respect and duty toward parents and ancestors.

**Assessment:** Philosophers' dialogue.

**Answer key:** Dialogue rubric — 4: Confucius stresses virtuous rulers, proper relationships, filial piety and education; Laozi stresses harmony with nature, simplicity and governing little (wu wei); both use accurate ideas and respond to each other; 3: accurate, one-sided; 2: inaccurate in places; 1: off-topic.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Photography composition

**Prepare:** Phone or tablet camera; turn on the grid overlay. Find example photos showing rule of thirds, leading lines and framing.

**Watch for:**
- Always centering the subject. Placing it on a rule-of-thirds intersection is often more interesting.
- Taking every photo from standing eye level. Changing angle changes the mood and story.

### Day 1 (Monday): Composition rules
*Student:* Study example photos for rule of thirds, leading lines and framing.

*How to teach:* Study examples of rule of thirds, leading lines and framing. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is the rule of thirds?
  **A:** Dividing the frame into a 3×3 grid and placing important subjects along the lines or intersections.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rule of thirds
*Student:* Take 10 photos using the rule-of-thirds grid and choose the best two.

*How to teach:* Take 10 photos using the grid; choose the best two. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why is an off-center subject often more interesting?
  **A:** It creates balance with space and leads the eye through the image.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Angles
*Student:* Photograph the same object from bird's-eye, eye-level and worm's-eye views.

*How to teach:* Photograph an object from bird's-eye, eye-level and worm's-eye views. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** How does a worm's-eye view change a subject?
  **A:** It makes the subject look tall, powerful or dramatic.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Photo walk
*Student:* Take a neighborhood photo walk and capture leading lines, patterns and textures.

*How to teach:* Neighborhood photo walk: leading lines, patterns, textures. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What are leading lines?
  **A:** Lines (roads, fences, rails) that draw the viewer's eye toward the subject.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & critique
*Student:* Select your best 5 photos and explain the composition technique in each.

*How to teach:* Select 5 best photos; caption the technique in each. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What is framing in photography?
  **A:** Using objects in the scene (doorway, branches) to surround the subject.

**Assessment:** Curated 5-photo set with captions.

**Answer key:** 5-photo set: each uses an identified technique correctly with a caption explaining it. 4 = all 5; 3 = 4; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.

## Physical Education 6: Distance running and pacing

**Prepare:** Gather: stopwatch, measured loop. Measure a loop (e.g., 400 m). Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Starting too fast. Even splits are faster overall.
- Thinking walking breaks are failure. Run-walk builds endurance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Target heart rate
*Student:* Calculate your target heart-rate zone and check it during a 10-minute jog.

*How to teach:* Calculate target zone; jog 10 minutes; check.

- **Q:** Calculate the target zone for a 12-year-old (60–85% of 220 − age).
  **A:** Max ≈ 208 bpm; target ≈ 125–177 bpm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pacing
*Student:* Run four laps trying to keep each lap within 5 seconds of the others.

*How to teach:* Run four even laps.

- **Q:** Laps of 2:10, 2:12, 2:08, 2:11. Is pacing even?
  **A:** Yes — all within 5 seconds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interval run
*Student:* Alternate 1 minute fast and 1 minute easy for 16 minutes.

*How to teach:* Run 1-minute intervals for 16 minutes.

- **Q:** Why do intervals?
  **A:** They build speed and aerobic fitness by alternating hard and easy efforts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Long run
*Student:* Run or run-walk 20 continuous minutes and record distance.

*How to teach:* Run or run-walk 20 minutes.

- **Q:** What is a good pace for a long run?
  **A:** One where you can talk in short sentences.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Run a timed mile and compare with baseline.

*How to teach:* Time the mile; compare.

- **Q:** Baseline mile 11:30, now 10:45. Improvement?
  **A:** 45 seconds faster.

**Assessment:** Mile time and pacing splits.

**Answer key:** Mile time vs. baseline and pacing splits (laps within 5 seconds of each other = proficient). Target zone formula: max HR ≈ 220 − age; target 60–85%. For age 12: max ≈ 208; zone ≈ 125–177 bpm.

## Health 6: Personal safety and boundaries

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards for 'uh-oh' feelings, boundaries and grooming warning signs. Choose a family code word. PARENT PREVIEW: grooming and unwanted touch are discussed; reassure your child they can always tell you, and that it is never a child's fault.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking abusers are always strangers. Most people who harm children are people they know; warning signs matter more than who the person is.
- Thinking you must obey every adult. You can say no to anything that feels unsafe, even to adults you know.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trust your gut
*Student:* Discuss intuition and practice identifying uh-oh feelings in scenarios.

*How to teach:* Discuss intuition; identify 'uh-oh' feelings in scenarios. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What should you do when something gives you an 'uh-oh' feeling?
  **A:** Trust it, get away from the situation, and tell a trusted adult.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Boundaries
*Student:* Learn personal-boundary rights and practice saying no to unwanted touch or requests.

*How to teach:* Learn personal-boundary rights; practice saying no to unwanted touch or requests. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is consent?
  **A:** Freely given permission; you have the right to say no to touch or requests, and others must respect it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grooming warning signs
*Student:* Learn warning signs such as secrets, gifts and isolation from adults or online contacts.

*How to teach:* Learn grooming warning signs: secrets, special gifts, isolating you, testing boundaries, online flattery.

- **Q:** An adult gives you gifts and says, 'Don't tell your parents.' Is that a warning sign?
  **A:** Yes — secrets and gifts are grooming warning signs; tell a trusted adult right away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Escape plan
*Student:* Practice No-Go-Tell and create a code word with your family.

*How to teach:* Practice No-Go-Tell and create a family code word. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What does No-Go-Tell mean?
  **A:** Say no, get away, and tell a trusted adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Violence Prevention and Personal Safety unit quiz.

*How to teach:* Take the Violence Prevention and Personal Safety unit quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What is a family code word used for?
  **A:** A secret word you or a trusted ride must know, or a signal to ask to be picked up when you feel unsafe.

**Assessment:** Unit quiz.

**Answer key:** Unit quiz key: types of violence; warning signs; gun rule (don't touch, leave, tell); gang alternatives; de-escalation; boundaries and consent; grooming signs (secrets, gifts, isolation); No-Go-Tell; code word; never the child's fault. Proficient = 80%.
