# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 11

## English Language Arts 6: The Odyssey, part 1

**Prepare:** Gather: 'The Children's Homer' by Padraic Colum (Project Gutenberg), map of the Mediterranean. Download The Children's Homer (Colum) and print a Mediterranean map. Preview: the Cyclops episode includes the giant eating crew members.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking an epic hero is perfect. Odysseus is brave and clever but also proud and boastful.
- Assuming the crew's problems are only bad luck. Choices (opening the winds bag, taunting the Cyclops) cause them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Meet Odysseus
*Student:* Read the opening of the Odysseus section of The Children's Homer, map the start of his journey, and list epic hero traits.

*How to teach:* Read the opening; map Troy to Ithaca. The child lists epic-hero traits.

- **Q:** What is Odysseus trying to do?
  **A:** Return home to Ithaca after the Trojan War, to his wife Penelope and son Telemachus.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Cyclops
*Student:* Read the Polyphemus episode and cite evidence that Odysseus is both clever and proud.

*How to teach:* Read the Cyclops episode; the child cites evidence for clever and proud.

- **Q:** Give evidence Odysseus is clever and proud in the Cyclops episode.
  **A:** Clever: he calls himself 'Noman' and escapes by hiding his men under the rams. Proud: he shouts his real name as they sail away, so Polyphemus can ask Poseidon for revenge.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Aeolus and Circe
*Student:* Read the bag of winds and Circe episodes and explain how the crew's choices cause new problems.

*How to teach:* Read the Aeolus and Circe episodes; chart choice → consequence.

- **Q:** What happens when the crew opens the bag of winds?
  **A:** The winds burst out and blow the ships away from Ithaca, just when home was in sight.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Root review
*Student:* Find 10 words in the reading with Greek or Latin parts (e.g., 'journey', 'monstrous') and analyze them.

*How to teach:* The child finds 10 words with Greek/Latin parts and analyzes them.

- **Q:** Analyze 'monstrous.'
  **A:** Monster + -ous (full of/having the qualities of): like a monster.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph analyzing which episode best shows Odysseus's greatest flaw; reading quiz.

*How to teach:* The child writes the analytical paragraph and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** Why is Poseidon angry with Odysseus?
  **A:** Odysseus blinded Poseidon's son Polyphemus.

**Assessment:** Analytical paragraph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = names a flaw (usually pride/hubris), chooses an episode (e.g., shouting his real name to Polyphemus, which brings Poseidon's anger) with evidence and explanation; 3 = adequate; 2 = retelling; 1 = off-topic. Reading quiz: 1) Odysseus fought at Troy and is trying to get home to Ithaca 2) he tells the Cyclops his name is 'Noman' 3) blinds Polyphemus with a sharpened stake 4) the men escape clinging under the rams 5) Aeolus gives a bag of winds that the crew opens near Ithaca 6) Circe turns the men into swine.

## Mathematics 6: Percentages

**Prepare:** Gather: 10 x 10 grids, store sale ads. Print several 10 × 10 grids; clip 2–3 sale ads showing percent-off deals.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking percents can't exceed 100. 120% means 1.2 times the whole — show two grids.
- Finding the discount but forgetting to subtract it to get the sale price.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is a percent?
*Student:* Shade 10 x 10 grids to show 25%, 40%, 7% and 120%, and connect percents to fractions and decimals out of 100.

*How to teach:* Shade 25 squares of a 10 × 10 grid and say '25 out of 100 = 25% = 0.25 = ¼'. The child shades 40%, 7% and 120% (one full grid plus 20 squares).

- **Q:** Write 25% as a fraction and a decimal.
  **A:** 25/100 = ¼ = 0.25.
- **Q:** What does 120% of a quantity mean?
  **A:** 1.2 times it — the whole plus 20% more.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Benchmark percents
*Student:* Find 10%, 25%, 50% and 75% of 8 different quantities using mental math and double number lines.

*How to teach:* Teach 10% by dividing by 10, 50% by halving, 25% by halving twice, 75% as 3 × 25%. Practice with a double number line 0–100%.

- **Q:** Find 10% of 80, 25% of 60 and 75% of 40.
  **A:** 8, 15 and 30.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Percent of a quantity
*Student:* Use tables to find percents such as 35% of $80 and 15% of 60 minutes (10 problems).

*How to teach:* Model 35% of $80 = 10% ($8) × 3 + 5% ($4) = $28, and with a table. The child solves the 10 problems.

- **Q:** Find 35% of $80 and 15% of 60 minutes.
  **A:** $28 and 9 minutes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sales and tips
*Student:* Calculate sale prices from real ads (20% off, 30% off) and a 15% tip on three restaurant bills.

*How to teach:* Use real ads: find the discount, then subtract. Then compute a 15% tip as 10% + 5%.

- **Q:** A $45 shirt is 20% off. Sale price?
  **A:** $36 (discount $9).
- **Q:** 15% tip on a $32 bill?
  **A:** $4.80, total $36.80.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on percent meaning and finding a percent of a quantity.

*How to teach:* Give the percent quiz independently.

- **Q:** What is 120% of 50?
  **A:** 60.

**Assessment:** Percent quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 40% = 40/100 = 2/5 = 0.4 2) 7% = 0.07 3) 10% of 250 = 25 4) 25% of 64 = 16 5) 75% of 40 = 30 6) 35% of $80 = $28 7) $45 item 20% off → $36 8) 15% tip on $32 = $4.80 9) 120% of 50 = 60.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Air masses and fronts

**Prepare:** Gather: two containers, warm water, cold blue-dyed water, cardboard divider, weather maps from NOAA. Print three NOAA/NWS surface weather maps; prepare red warm water and blue cold water.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking air masses are named only by temperature. They're named by humidity (maritime/continental) and temperature (tropical/polar).
- Thinking 'H' on a map means hot. H = high pressure.

### Day 1 (Monday): Air mass types
*Student:* Learn the four air mass types (maritime/continental, tropical/polar) and identify which ones affect California.

*How to teach:* Teach the four types; identify those affecting California.

- **Q:** What does 'maritime polar' mean, and does it affect California?
  **A:** Moist and cold, forming over northern oceans; yes — it brings winter storms from the North Pacific.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Front model
*Student:* Model a front with warm red water and cold blue water separated by a divider; remove it and sketch what happens.

*How to teach:* Remove the divider; sketch the cold water sliding under warm.

- **Q:** What happens at a cold front?
  **A:** Cold, dense air pushes under warm air, forcing it up quickly — often causing storms and a temperature drop.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading weather maps
*Student:* Interpret front symbols, highs and lows on three daily NOAA weather maps.

*How to teach:* Interpret three maps.

- **Q:** What does a line of blue triangles mean?
  **A:** A cold front, moving in the direction the triangles point.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Weather data
*Student:* Collect a week of local temperature, pressure, wind and humidity data and connect changes to fronts on maps.

*How to teach:* Collect a week of local data and connect to maps.

- **Q:** What typically happens to pressure before a storm?
  **A:** It falls.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER explaining a weather change you observed using air mass and front data.

*How to teach:* The child writes a CER on an observed change.

- **Q:** What does 'L' on a weather map mean?
  **A:** An area of low pressure.

**Assessment:** Weather data analysis CER.

**Answer key:** CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: e.g., 'A cold front passed Tuesday: temperature dropped 10°F, pressure fell then rose, winds shifted, and showers occurred.' Map key: blue triangles = cold front; red half-circles = warm front; alternating = stationary; purple = occluded; H = high (usually fair); L = low (clouds, precipitation).

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Art, writing and great rulers

**Prepare:** Gather: hieroglyph alphabet chart, paper bag (for papyrus effect), images of Abu Simbel and Deir el-Bahri. Print a hieroglyph alphabet; find Abu Simbel and Deir el-Bahri images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking hieroglyphs are just pictures. They combine sound signs and meaning signs.
- Accepting royal monuments as unbiased. They are often propaganda.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hieroglyphs and the Rosetta Stone
*Student:* Learn how the Rosetta Stone was deciphered and write your name in hieroglyphs in a cartouche.

*How to teach:* Learn how the Rosetta Stone was deciphered; write name in a cartouche.

- **Q:** Why was the Rosetta Stone the key to hieroglyphs?
  **A:** It had the same text in hieroglyphs, Demotic and Greek; scholars like Champollion used the Greek to decipher the hieroglyphs (1822).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egyptian art rules
*Student:* Analyze tomb paintings for composite view, hierarchical scale and symbolism, then draw a figure in Egyptian style.

*How to teach:* Analyze art rules; draw in Egyptian style.

- **Q:** What is hierarchical scale?
  **A:** Showing more important people larger.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hatshepsut
*Student:* Read about Hatshepsut's reign, trade expedition to Punt and temple, and why her images were later erased.

*How to teach:* Read about Hatshepsut.

- **Q:** What was notable about Hatshepsut's reign?
  **A:** She ruled as pharaoh, sent a trading expedition to Punt and built a great temple at Deir el-Bahri; later rulers erased many of her images.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ramses the Great
*Student:* Examine Abu Simbel and the Battle of Kadesh accounts and decide what is history and what is propaganda.

*How to teach:* Examine Kadesh accounts.

- **Q:** Why is Ramses II's Kadesh account considered propaganda?
  **A:** He presented the battle as a great victory, but it was likely a draw and later ended with a peace treaty with the Hittites.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph comparing the legacies of Hatshepsut and Ramses II.

*How to teach:* The child writes the comparison.

- **Q:** What is a cartouche?
  **A:** An oval around a royal name in hieroglyphs.

**Assessment:** Comparison paragraph.

**Answer key:** Comparison paragraph — 4: compares Hatshepsut (female pharaoh, peaceful trade expedition to Punt, temple at Deir el-Bahri; images later erased) and Ramses II (long reign, building projects like Abu Simbel, military campaigns like Kadesh presented as a great victory, later peace treaty with the Hittites), with evidence and an evaluation of legacy; 3: both described, little comparison; 2: one ruler; 1: inaccurate.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Two-point perspective

**Prepare:** Ruler, long paper (tape two sheets), colored pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Placing vanishing points too close together. Spread them far apart (at the paper edges) to avoid distorted boxes.
- Forgetting the leading edge. Start each box with a vertical line (the nearest corner).

### Day 1 (Monday): Two-point boxes
*Student:* Draw boxes using two vanishing points on a long sheet of paper.

*How to teach:* Draw boxes using two vanishing points. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is the leading edge?
  **A:** The vertical edge closest to the viewer, from which lines go to both vanishing points.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Buildings
*Student:* Turn boxes into buildings with doors, windows and roofs.

*How to teach:* Turn boxes into buildings with doors, windows and roofs. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Where do window lines go?
  **A:** To the same vanishing point as the wall they're on.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cityscape
*Student:* Draw a street corner cityscape in two-point perspective.

*How to teach:* Draw a street-corner cityscape. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** How is two-point different from one-point perspective?
  **A:** Two-point uses two vanishing points and shows objects from a corner; one-point shows them straight on.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Atmospheric perspective
*Student:* Color the cityscape using brighter, sharper colors in front and lighter, cooler colors far away.

*How to teach:* Color using atmospheric perspective. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is atmospheric perspective?
  **A:** Distant objects look lighter, cooler and less detailed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Revise one area of your cityscape based on a parent's critique.

*How to teach:* Revise one area after a parent's critique. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why get a critique?
  **A:** Others spot errors and suggest improvements.

**Assessment:** Two-point cityscape.

**Answer key:** Cityscape: 4 = two vanishing points on the horizon, accurate converging lines, at least three buildings with details, atmospheric color; 3 = minor errors; 2 = inconsistent; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Basketball: ball handling and dribbling

**Prepare:** Gather: basketball, cones, hoop if available. Pump up a basketball; set cones. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Slapping the ball with the palm. Dribble with fingertips.
- Watching the ball. Eyes up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ball handling
*Student:* Complete ball-handling drills (around the waist, figure 8, finger taps).

*How to teach:* Ball-handling drills.

- **Q:** Why use fingertips, not the palm?
  **A:** Fingertips give more control.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dribbling
*Student:* Practice control and speed dribbles with each hand through cones.

*How to teach:* Control vs. speed dribbles.

- **Q:** How is a speed dribble different from a control dribble?
  **A:** Speed: ball pushed out ahead at waist height while running; control: lower, closer to the body.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crossover
*Student:* Learn the crossover dribble and practice it against a partner defender.

*How to teach:* Crossover against a defender.

- **Q:** When do you use a crossover?
  **A:** To change direction quickly and get past a defender.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peer feedback
*Student:* Use a critical-elements checklist to give a partner feedback on dribbling.

*How to teach:* Peer checklist feedback.

- **Q:** Give one dribbling cue.
  **A:** Eyes up; fingertips; keep the ball low; protect it with your body.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Complete a timed dribble course with both hands.

*How to teach:* Timed course.

- **Q:** Why practice with both hands?
  **A:** So you can dribble away from defenders on either side.

**Assessment:** Dribble course time and checklist.

**Answer key:** Dribble course time (both hands) with checklist: fingertip control, ball at waist height or lower, eyes up, body protects the ball. Proficient = completes course without losing control using both hands.

## Health 6: Peer pressure and family relationships

**Prepare:** Write peer-pressure scenario cards (direct: 'Come on, just try it'; indirect: everyone wearing a brand; online: a group chat dare). Prepare interview questions about family roles.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking peer pressure is always negative. Positive peer pressure encourages good choices.
- Thinking family communication just happens. Family meetings and I-messages help.

### Day 1 (Monday): Types of pressure
*Student:* Identify direct, indirect and online peer pressure in scenarios.

*How to teach:* Identify direct, indirect and online pressure. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Give an example of indirect peer pressure.
  **A:** Feeling you must buy certain shoes because everyone has them, though no one asked.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Positive pressure
*Student:* Write examples of positive peer pressure you have experienced or given.

*How to teach:* Write examples of positive peer pressure. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Give an example of positive peer pressure.
  **A:** A friend encouraging you to join a team or study for a test.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family roles
*Student:* Interview a family member about responsibilities and how communication works at home.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about responsibilities and communication. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why do families have roles and responsibilities?
  **A:** To share work, support each other and keep the household running.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family meeting
*Student:* Lead a short family meeting using I-messages to plan an activity.

*How to teach:* Lead a family meeting using I-messages to plan an activity. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What makes a family meeting effective?
  **A:** Everyone gets a turn, uses respectful language, and agrees on a plan.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Relationships unit test.

*How to teach:* Take the Relationships unit test. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What is active listening?
  **A:** Focusing fully on the speaker and paraphrasing what they said.

**Assessment:** Unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: healthy vs. unhealthy friendship traits; boundaries; passive/aggressive/assertive; I-message parts; active listening; conflict-resolution steps; mediator; win-win; types of peer pressure (direct, indirect, online); positive peer pressure. Proficient = 80%.
