# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 15

## English Language Arts 6: Planning an argument essay

**Prepare:** Gather: two to three articles on a chosen issue (e.g., Should zoos exist? Should kids have phones?), note cards. Choose 2–3 balanced articles on one issue the child cares about.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a non-debatable topic ('Dogs are animals'). The question must have reasonable sides.
- Vague pronouns: 'they say' or 'this' with no clear noun. Replace with the specific noun.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a question
*Student:* Choose a debatable question from a list, read two articles on it, and take a position.

*How to teach:* The child chooses a question, reads two articles and takes a position.

- **Q:** Is 'Should kids have phones?' debatable? Why?
  **A:** Yes — reasonable people disagree and evidence exists on both sides.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evidence notes
*Student:* Take notes on cards, separating quotations and paraphrases, with source titles.

*How to teach:* Model a quote card and a paraphrase card with source title.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a quotation and a paraphrase?
  **A:** A quotation uses the author's exact words in quotation marks; a paraphrase restates the idea in your own words (still cited).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Vague pronouns
*Student:* Lesson on vague pronouns ('they say', 'this'); fix 12 sentences with unclear antecedents.

*How to teach:* Teach vague pronouns; the child fixes 12.

- **Q:** Fix: 'When Sam called Leo, he was upset.'
  **A:** Name who was upset: 'Leo was upset when Sam called him.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Outline
*Student:* Outline an essay with a claim, two or three reasons, evidence for each and a counterclaim with rebuttal.

*How to teach:* The child completes the outline including counterclaim and rebuttal.

- **Q:** What is a rebuttal?
  **A:** Your response showing why the opposing claim is weaker or wrong.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check-in
*Student:* Explain your outline orally to a parent in 3 minutes and revise based on their questions; quiz on vague pronouns.

*How to teach:* The child explains the outline orally; ask questions; give the quiz.

- **Q:** Fix: 'This is why we should go.'
  **A:** Replace 'this' with a specific noun phrase, e.g., 'The cheaper ticket price is why we should go.'

**Assessment:** Completed outline and pronoun quiz.

**Answer key:** Outline check: claim; 2–3 reasons; evidence (with source) for each; a counterclaim; rebuttal. 4 = complete and logical; 3 = one weak area; 2 = missing counterclaim or evidence; 1 = incomplete. Vague pronoun quiz sample answers: 'They say phones distract' → 'Researchers at ___ found…'; 'This is a problem' → 'This lack of sleep is a problem'; 'Mom told Aunt Jo that she was late' → 'Mom told Aunt Jo, "I am late."'

## Mathematics 6: Fractions in real-world problems

**Prepare:** Gather: ribbon or string, ruler, recipe card. Mark a 4½-yard length of ribbon or string for Day 1 (a yardstick helps).

**Watch for:**
- Writing the division backwards (⅜ ÷ 4½). Ask: 'What is being split up (the total), and what is the size of each piece?' Total goes first.
- Rounding a 'how many pieces' answer up when partial pieces don't count.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fractional lengths
*Student:* Measure a ribbon and find how many 3/8-yard pieces can be cut from 4 1/2 yards; solve 6 similar length problems.

*How to teach:* Measure ribbon to show the problem concretely, then compute 4½ ÷ ⅜. The child solves 6 similar problems.

- **Q:** How many ⅜-yard pieces can be cut from 4½ yards?
  **A:** 12 (9/2 × 8/3 = 12).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rectangles with fractional sides
*Student:* Find a missing side when area and one side are fractions (e.g., area 2 1/2 sq ft, width 3/4 ft).

*How to teach:* Review A = l × w, so w = A ÷ l. Model area 2½, width ¾.

- **Q:** A rectangle has area 2½ sq ft and width ¾ ft. Find the length.
  **A:** 3⅓ ft (5/2 × 4/3 = 10/3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recipes
*Student:* Scale a recipe calling for 2/3 cup sugar to fit a 1/2-cup measuring scoop and solve 5 recipe-division problems.

*How to teach:* Ask: 'If the recipe needs ⅔ cup and we only have a ½-cup scoop, how many scoops?' The child solves 5 recipe problems.

- **Q:** How many ½-cup scoops make ⅔ cup?
  **A:** 1⅓ scoops.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write your own
*Student:* Write and solve three fraction-division story problems and trade with a family member to solve.

*How to teach:* The child writes three fraction-division stories and an answer key; a family member solves them and the child checks.

- **Q:** Solve: 5 cups of soup poured into ⅔-cup bowls. How many bowls?
  **A:** 7½ bowls (5 × 3/2).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on fraction word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the word-problem quiz.

- **Q:** Jo walked ¾ mile, which is ⅗ of her route. How long is the route?
  **A:** 1¼ miles.

**Assessment:** Word problem quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 6 ÷ ¾ = 8 2) 3¾ ÷ ¼ = 15 3) ⅝ ÷ ¼ = 2½ 4) area 4½ sq ft, length 1½ ft → width 3 ft 5) 2¼ lb shared by 3 people → ¾ lb each 6) Jo walked ¾ mile, which is ⅗ of her route → route = ¾ ÷ ⅗ = 1¼ miles.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Global temperature rise

**Prepare:** Gather: two jars, thermometers, plastic wrap, lamp, NASA Climate Kids graphs. Print NASA Climate Kids global temperature and CO₂ graphs. Discuss climate change calmly and focus on solutions.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing weather (daily) and climate (long-term averages).
- Thinking the jar model is exactly the atmosphere. It models trapping heat; the real greenhouse effect involves gases absorbing infrared.

### Day 1 (Monday): Greenhouse model
*Student:* Place thermometers in a covered and an uncovered jar under a lamp and record temperatures every 2 minutes for 20 minutes.

*How to teach:* Run the jar experiment; record every 2 minutes.

- **Q:** Which jar warms more?
  **A:** The covered jar — the plastic traps warm air, modeling greenhouse gases.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading the graphs
*Student:* Analyze NASA global temperature and atmospheric CO2 graphs and describe trends since 1900.

*How to teach:* Analyze NASA graphs since 1900.

- **Q:** What trend do the graphs show?
  **A:** Both atmospheric CO₂ and global average temperature have risen, especially since about 1950.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Asking questions
*Student:* Write 8 questions to clarify evidence about causes of warming (solar output, volcanoes, CO2) and research answers on NASA Climate Kids.

*How to teach:* Write and research 8 questions.

- **Q:** Do changes in the sun's output explain recent warming?
  **A:** No — measurements show solar output has been fairly steady while temperatures rose; the main cause is increased greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels.

### Day 4 (Thursday): California impacts
*Student:* Read about changes to California's snowpack and wildfire seasons and discuss solutions families and communities use.

*How to teach:* Read about snowpack and wildfire; discuss solutions.

- **Q:** Name a community solution to wildfire risk.
  **A:** Clearing brush (defensible space), controlled burns, fire-resistant building, early warning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test on climate, circulation and global temperature change.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** What is the difference between weather and climate?
  **A:** Weather is short-term conditions; climate is long-term patterns averaged over decades.

**Assessment:** Greenhouse lab write-up and Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Lab write-up: covered jar warms more and stays warmer, modeling how greenhouse gases trap heat. Unit 4 test sample: uneven heating; convection cells; Coriolis; wind belts; California Current; rain shadow; greenhouse effect; CO₂ has risen from about 280 ppm before industrialization to over 400 ppm today; average global temperature has risen about 1°C (about 2°F) since the late 1800s mainly due to burning fossil fuels.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Exile and diaspora

**Prepare:** Gather: map of the Mediterranean and Near East, image of the Arch of Titus. Print a Near East/Mediterranean map and the Arch of Titus relief image.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Jewish people disappeared after 70 CE. They maintained their faith through study, synagogues and communities across the Diaspora.
- Confusing the First and Second Temples.

### Day 1 (Monday): Babylonian exile
*Student:* Read about the destruction of the First Temple and exile to Babylon and map the movement.

*How to teach:* Read about the Babylonian exile; map.

- **Q:** Who destroyed the First Temple, and when?
  **A:** The Babylonians under Nebuchadnezzar, in 586 BCE.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Return and Second Temple
*Student:* Read about Cyrus of Persia permitting return and the rebuilding of the Temple.

*How to teach:* Read about Cyrus and the Second Temple.

- **Q:** Why did Cyrus let the Jews return?
  **A:** Persian policy tolerated local religions; Cyrus allowed exiles to return and rebuild.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Roman destruction
*Student:* Analyze the Arch of Titus relief showing the Temple's treasures being carried away in 70 CE.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Arch of Titus.

- **Q:** What does the Arch of Titus show?
  **A:** Roman soldiers carrying treasures, including the menorah, from the Temple after its destruction in 70 CE.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Yavneh and survival
*Student:* Read about Yohanan ben Zaccai's academy at Yavneh and explain how study, synagogues and rabbis preserved Judaism.

*How to teach:* Read about Yavneh.

- **Q:** How did Judaism survive without the Temple?
  **A:** Through study of Torah, synagogues, rabbis and communities across the Diaspora; Yavneh became a center of learning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test with a map of the Diaspora.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** What is the Diaspora?
  **A:** The scattering of Jewish communities outside the land of Israel.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test sample: Abraham; monotheism; Exodus; Ten Commandments; David and Solomon; Babylonians destroyed the First Temple (586 BCE) and exiled many Jews; Cyrus of Persia allowed return (539 BCE); Second Temple rebuilt; Romans destroyed it in 70 CE; Arch of Titus shows the menorah taken; Yohanan ben Zakkai's academy at Yavneh; Diaspora = scattering of Jews outside their homeland (map shows communities in Babylon, Egypt, Asia Minor, Rome).

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Reading pitch on the staff

**Prepare:** Recorder (or keyboard/ukulele) and note-naming flashcards. Treble lines E G B D F, spaces F A C E; bass lines G B D F A ('Good Boys Do Fine Always'), spaces A C E G ('All Cows Eat Grass').

**Watch for:**
- Using treble-clef names on bass clef. Each clef has its own line and space names.
- Counting ledger lines incorrectly. Each ledger line continues the line-space pattern.

### Day 1 (Monday): Treble clef
*Student:* Learn line and space notes (EGBDF, FACE) and complete a note-naming drill.

*How to teach:* Learn treble line and space notes; do a note-naming drill. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name the treble clef spaces.
  **A:** F, A, C, E.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): First notes
*Student:* Learn to play B, A and G (recorder) or C-D-E (keyboard) and play 'Hot Cross Buns' from notation.

*How to teach:* Play B, A, G (recorder) or C-D-E (keyboard) and 'Hot Cross Buns.' Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** On recorder, which note uses only the thumb and first finger?
  **A:** B.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bass clef
*Student:* Learn bass clef notes and name 20 notes in a timed drill.

*How to teach:* Learn bass clef notes; name 20 in a timed drill. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name the bass clef lines from bottom to top.
  **A:** G, B, D, F, A.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Expanding range
*Student:* Add two new notes and play 'Mary Had a Little Lamb' and 'Ode to Joy' (first phrase).

*How to teach:* Add two notes; play 'Mary Had a Little Lamb' and 'Ode to Joy' (first phrase). Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is a ledger line?
  **A:** A short line added above or below the staff for notes outside it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a note-naming quiz and play a short melody for a family member.

*How to teach:* Note-naming quiz; play a short melody. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What note is on the middle line of the treble staff?
  **A:** B.

**Assessment:** Note-naming quiz and playing check.

**Answer key:** Note-naming quiz: 20 notes in treble and bass (proficient 90%). Playing check: correct notes and steady beat in a short melody.

## Physical Education 6: Volleyball: forearm pass and set

**Prepare:** Gather: volleyball or beach ball, net or rope. Use a volleyball or beach ball; string a rope as a net. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging the arms on a forearm pass. Keep a flat platform and use the legs.
- Setting with palms. Use fingertips in a triangle shape.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ready position
*Student:* Learn ready position and footwork; practice forearm passes off a self-toss.

*How to teach:* Teach ready position; self-toss passes.

- **Q:** Describe the ready position.
  **A:** Feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight forward, arms out in front.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Partner bumping
*Student:* Bump with a partner and count consecutive passes.

*How to teach:* Partner bump; count passes.

- **Q:** Where should the ball contact on a forearm pass?
  **A:** On the flat forearms above the wrists.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Setting
*Student:* Learn the overhead set with a triangle hand shape and set to yourself 20 times.

*How to teach:* Teach the overhead set.

- **Q:** How do the hands form for a set?
  **A:** A triangle/window above the forehead, contacting with fingertips.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pass to set
*Student:* Practice pass-to-set sequences with a partner.

*How to teach:* Pass to set.

- **Q:** What is the usual order of contacts?
  **A:** Pass (bump), set, then attack/hit.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Complete 10 forearm passes to a target zone and record successes.

*How to teach:* Passing check.

- **Q:** Why use your legs on a pass?
  **A:** Legs provide power and control instead of swinging arms.

**Assessment:** Passing accuracy check.

**Answer key:** Passing check: 10 forearm passes from a toss to a target zone; record successes (6+ proficient). Cues: ready position, platform (hands together, thumbs parallel, arms straight), contact on forearms, lift with legs.

## Health 6: Cyberbullying and advocacy for kindness

**Prepare:** Gather poster paper, markers or a video app. Review the Stop–Screenshot–Block–Tell response.

**Watch for:**
- Replying to a cyberbully to defend yourself. Replying often escalates it; save evidence, block and tell.
- Thinking online cruelty is less harmful. Cyberbullying can reach a large audience, is permanent and can happen anytime.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cyberbullying
*Student:* Analyze how cyberbullying differs from in-person bullying (audience, permanence, anonymity).

*How to teach:* Analyze how cyberbullying differs: larger audience, permanence, anonymity, 24/7. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name two ways cyberbullying differs from in-person bullying.
  **A:** It can reach a huge audience, stays online permanently, can be anonymous, and happens any time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stop, block, tell
*Student:* Practice responses: don't reply, screenshot, block, tell a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Practice: don't reply, screenshot, block, tell a trusted adult. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why take a screenshot before blocking?
  **A:** It saves evidence to show adults or report to the platform.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kindness campaign
*Student:* Design a kindness or anti-bullying poster or short video.

*How to teach:* Design a kindness or anti-bullying poster or short video. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What makes an anti-bullying message effective?
  **A:** A clear positive message, specific actions people can take, and an appealing design.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Share the campaign with family or co-op.

*How to teach:* Share the campaign with family or co-op. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is advocacy?
  **A:** Speaking up to encourage healthy behaviors or support a cause.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Respect and Bullying Prevention unit test.

*How to teach:* Take the Respect and Bullying Prevention unit test. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What should you do if you see someone cyberbullied?
  **A:** Don't share or like it, support the target, report it, and tell a trusted adult.

**Assessment:** Campaign and unit test.

**Answer key:** Campaign: clear message, accurate info, call to action. Unit test key: stereotype/prejudice/discrimination; inclusion; bullying definition and forms; upstander; digital footprint; scam red flags; stop-screenshot-block-tell. Proficient = 80%.
