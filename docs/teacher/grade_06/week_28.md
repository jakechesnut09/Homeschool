# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 28

## English Language Arts 6: Performing a scene

**Prepare:** Gather: scene script, simple costumes and props, filmed production clip. Find a filmed production clip (Globe on Screen or a free school production); collect simple costumes and props.

**Watch for:**
- Reading Shakespeare's lines in a sing-song. Speak for meaning; let the rhythm support it.
- Standing with back to audience; mark blocking.

### Day 1 (Monday): View a performance
*Student:* Watch a filmed scene of the play and compare the director's choices with your mental picture from reading.

*How to teach:* Watch a filmed scene and compare with the child's mental picture.

- **Q:** What is a director's choice?
  **A:** Decisions about staging, costume, tone and movement that interpret the script.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cut and cast
*Student:* Choose and cut a 2-3 minute scene (e.g., the mechanicals' rehearsal), assign roles with family members, and mark blocking.

*How to teach:* Cut a 2–3 minute scene, cast family and mark blocking.

- **Q:** What is blocking?
  **A:** The planned movement and positions of actors on stage.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse lines with attention to iambic rhythm, projection and gestures.

*How to teach:* Rehearse, tapping iambic rhythm; practice projection.

- **Q:** What is iambic pentameter?
  **A:** A line of ten syllables in five pairs, each unstressed then stressed (da-DUM).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform and record the scene for an audience.

*How to teach:* Perform and record.

- **Q:** Why face the audience when speaking?
  **A:** So they can hear and see expressions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect and check
*Student:* Write a reflection explaining your interpretive choices with textual evidence; take the unit test.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection and takes the unit test.

- **Q:** Who is the queen of the fairies?
  **A:** Titania.

**Assessment:** Performance rubric, reflection and unit test.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric (4/3/2/1): lines learned; meaning clear; projection; gestures and blocking; teamwork. Reflection rubric: explains at least two choices with quotes. Unit test sample: Theseus/Hippolyta, the lovers' chart, Puck's mistake, Bottom's transformation, Pyramus and Thisbe, drama terms, iambic pentameter (ten syllables, da-DUM ×5).

## Mathematics 6: The coordinate plane

**Prepare:** Gather: graph paper, Desmos. Draw a large four-quadrant grid; plan a simple neighborhood map for Day 4.

**Watch for:**
- Plotting (x, y) as (y, x). Remind: 'walk before you climb' — x is horizontal first.
- Counting tick marks instead of spaces when finding distance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Four quadrants
*Student:* Plot 20 points including negative and fractional coordinates and name each point's quadrant.

*How to teach:* Review quadrant numbering (I top-right, counterclockwise). The child plots 20 points and names quadrants.

- **Q:** Name the quadrant of (−3, 4), (2, −5) and (−1.5, −2).
  **A:** II, IV and III.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reflections
*Student:* Reflect points over each axis and describe how the signs of the coordinates change.

*How to teach:* Plot (3, −2) and its reflections over each axis. Ask which coordinate changed sign.

- **Q:** Reflect (3, −2) over the x-axis and over the y-axis.
  **A:** (3, 2) and (−3, −2). Over the x-axis the y-sign flips; over the y-axis the x-sign flips.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distances
*Student:* Find distances between points like (-3, 4) and (5, 4) using absolute value, in 10 problems.

*How to teach:* For points sharing a y-coordinate, subtract x-values or add absolute values when on opposite sides of 0.

- **Q:** Distance from (−3, 4) to (5, 4)?
  **A:** 8 units (|−3| + |5|).
- **Q:** Distance from (2, −6) to (2, 3)?
  **A:** 9 units.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coordinate map
*Student:* Draw a neighborhood map on a coordinate grid with home at the origin and solve walking-distance problems along the streets.

*How to teach:* Place home at (0,0), each block = 1 unit. The child marks 5 places and finds street distances (only horizontal and vertical moves).

- **Q:** School is at (4, −3). How many blocks does the child walk along streets from home?
  **A:** 7 blocks (4 east + 3 south).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on plotting, reflecting and distance.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the map.

- **Q:** Reflect (−6, 1) over the y-axis.
  **A:** (6, 1).

**Assessment:** Coordinate plane quiz and map project.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) (−4, 2) Quadrant II 2) (5, −1) Quadrant IV 3) (−2.5, −3) Quadrant III 4) (−6, 1) reflected over the x-axis → (−6, −1) 5) reflected over the y-axis → (6, 1) 6) distance from (−3, 4) to (5, 4) = 8 7) (1, −7) to (1, 2) = 9 8) (−5, −2) to (−5, −9) = 7. Map project rubric: 4 = home at origin, at least 5 labeled places, correct coordinates and street distances; 3 = minor errors; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Cells to organisms

**Prepare:** Gather: images of specialized cells, paper for flip book. Print images of nerve, muscle, red blood and skin cells.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all cells look alike.
- Thinking cells grow huge instead of dividing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Specialized cells
*Student:* Compare nerve, muscle, red blood and skin cells and explain how shape fits function.

*How to teach:* Compare specialized cells.

- **Q:** How does a nerve cell's shape fit its function?
  **A:** Long branches carry messages over long distances.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Levels of organization
*Student:* Create a nested diagram from cell to tissue to organ to organ system to organism with examples.

*How to teach:* Create the nested diagram.

- **Q:** Give an example of each level from cell to organism.
  **A:** Muscle cell → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → human.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cell division
*Student:* Make a flip book showing a cell dividing and explain why cells divide for growth and repair.

*How to teach:* Make the cell division flip book.

- **Q:** Why do cells divide?
  **A:** For growth and to repair or replace damaged cells.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review cells with a concept map linking all unit vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Build a concept map.

- **Q:** How are tissues different from organs?
  **A:** A tissue is a group of similar cells; an organ is made of several tissues working together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on cells and their parts.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Why are red blood cells disk-shaped?
  **A:** Their shape gives a large surface for carrying oxygen and lets them squeeze through tiny vessels.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test sample: cell theory; microscope use; living characteristics; organelle functions; plant vs. animal cells; osmosis and diffusion; specialized cells; levels of organization cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism; cells divide for growth and repair.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The Qin Dynasty and Shi Huangdi

**Prepare:** Gather: images of the terracotta army, clay. Find terracotta army images; have air-dry clay.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Great Wall was built all at once. Qin linked earlier walls; most of today's wall is from the Ming dynasty.
- Seeing Shi Huangdi as only good or only bad.

### Day 1 (Monday): Unifying China
*Student:* Read about Qin Shi Huangdi's unification and list his standardization policies (writing, coins, weights, axles).

*How to teach:* Read about unification; list standardizations.

- **Q:** Name three things Shi Huangdi standardized.
  **A:** Writing, coins, weights and measures, axle widths.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Legalism
*Student:* Compare Legalist ideas with Confucianism and discuss the burning of books.

*How to teach:* Compare Legalism and Confucianism.

- **Q:** What did Legalists believe?
  **A:** People are selfish, so strict laws and harsh punishments are needed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Great Wall
*Student:* Map the early walls and explain the costs to the workers who built them.

*How to teach:* Map early walls; discuss workers.

- **Q:** What was the cost of the Great Wall?
  **A:** Forced labor; many workers died.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Terracotta army
*Student:* Study the terracotta army and sculpt a small clay soldier showing individual features.

*How to teach:* Study the terracotta army; sculpt a soldier.

- **Q:** What is the terracotta army?
  **A:** Thousands of life-size clay soldiers buried near Shi Huangdi's tomb (found 1974).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an evaluation: was Shi Huangdi a great leader or a tyrant? Use evidence for both sides.

*How to teach:* The child writes the evaluation.

- **Q:** What was the 'burning of books'?
  **A:** The Qin ordered many books (especially Confucian) burned to control ideas.

**Assessment:** Evidence-based evaluation.

**Answer key:** Evaluation rubric — 4: uses evidence for 'great leader' (unified China, standardized writing, coins, weights and axle widths, built roads and walls) and 'tyrant' (Legalist harsh punishments, burned books, forced labor) and reaches a supported judgment; 3: both sides, weak conclusion; 2: one side; 1: inaccurate.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Photo story and editing

**Prepare:** Camera and a free photo editor (e.g., Snapseed, phone editing tools). Find a photo essay (e.g., a museum or news photo story).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking photo edits are never misleading. Removing or adding objects can change the truth.
- Shooting without a plan. A storyboard ensures a clear beginning, middle and end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Visual storytelling
*Student:* Analyze a photo essay and identify beginning, middle and end.

*How to teach:* Analyze a photo essay; identify beginning, middle and end. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What makes a photo essay tell a story?
  **A:** A sequence of images with a clear beginning, middle and end.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Storyboard
*Student:* Storyboard a 6-photo story about a family routine or recipe.

*How to teach:* Storyboard a 6-photo story about a routine or recipe. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is a storyboard?
  **A:** A sequence of sketches planning each shot.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoot
*Student:* Take the photos following your storyboard.

*How to teach:* Take the photos following the storyboard. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why vary shots (close-up, wide)?
  **A:** To show details and context and keep the story interesting.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit
*Student:* Crop and adjust contrast; discuss when photo editing becomes misleading manipulation.

*How to teach:* Crop and adjust contrast; discuss manipulation. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** When does editing become misleading manipulation?
  **A:** When it changes what actually happened—adding, removing or altering content to deceive.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & share
*Student:* Present your photo story and gather feedback.

*How to teach:* Present the photo story; gather feedback. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is saturation?
  **A:** The intensity of colors.

**Assessment:** Photo story.

**Answer key:** Photo story rubric: 4 = 6 photos follow storyboard with clear beginning/middle/end, varied shots, honest edits; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = unclear story; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Jumping and throwing events

**Prepare:** Gather: measuring tape, soft shot (bean bag or softball), foam noodle as bar. Measure a sand/grass landing area; use a pool noodle bar resting on cones (it falls safely). Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Not using the arms in a jump. Swing arms forward to add distance.
- Taking off from two feet in the running long jump. Use one foot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Standing long jump
*Student:* Practice arm swing and two-foot takeoff; measure three jumps.

*How to teach:* Standing long jump; measure three.

- **Q:** How do arms help the standing long jump?
  **A:** Swinging them forward adds momentum.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Running long jump
*Student:* Practice approach run and one-foot takeoff into grass or sand.

*How to teach:* Running long jump approach and one-foot takeoff.

- **Q:** Where is a long jump measured from?
  **A:** The takeoff line to the closest mark in the landing area.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scissor high jump
*Student:* Practice the scissor-kick high jump over a pool noodle.

*How to teach:* Scissor high jump over a noodle.

- **Q:** In the scissor jump, which foot takes off and which leg leads over the bar?
  **A:** Take off from the foot farther from the bar and swing the leg nearest the bar up and over first, then scissor the other leg over.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Softball throw
*Student:* Throw for distance using a step-and-throw and measure the best of three.

*How to teach:* Softball throw for distance.

- **Q:** How do you get more distance?
  **A:** Step and rotate, release at about a 45° angle.

### Day 5 (Friday): Mini track meet
*Student:* Hold a family track meet with sprint, jump and throw events.

*How to teach:* Hold the mini track meet.

- **Q:** Why measure the best of three?
  **A:** It gives a fair measure of ability.

**Assessment:** Track meet results.

**Answer key:** Track meet results recorded for sprint, standing and running long jump, high jump and softball throw (best of three). Scored on effort, correct technique and personal bests.

## Health 6: Personal hygiene and dental care for adolescents

**Prepare:** Toothbrush, floss, a hygiene checklist, and KidsHealth articles on acne and hygiene. Gather a sports bag to talk about gear hygiene.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking acne comes from dirt or chocolate. Acne mainly comes from hormones increasing oil that clogs pores with dead skin and bacteria.
- Thinking bleeding gums mean you should stop flossing. Bleeding is a sign of gingivitis; gentle daily flossing helps gums heal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hygiene review
*Student:* Review skin, hair and body odor care and update your hygiene routine for middle school.

*How to teach:* Review skin, hair and body odor care; update the routine. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why does body odor increase in middle school?
  **A:** Puberty hormones activate sweat glands; bacteria break down sweat and cause odor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Acne myths
*Student:* Sort acne myths and facts using KidsHealth and plan a gentle skin routine.

*How to teach:* Sort acne myths and facts; plan a gentle skin routine. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Should you pop pimples?
  **A:** No — popping can spread bacteria, cause infection and scars.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dental health
*Student:* Learn about gingivitis and practice proper flossing.

*How to teach:* Learn about gingivitis; practice proper flossing (C-shape around each tooth, below the gumline).

- **Q:** What is gingivitis?
  **A:** Early gum disease from plaque buildup; signs are red, swollen, bleeding gums.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sports hygiene
*Student:* List hygiene rules for sports gear, showers and preventing athlete's foot.

*How to teach:* List sports hygiene rules (shower after practice, wash uniforms, don't share towels, dry feet, wear flip-flops in locker rooms).

- **Q:** How do you prevent athlete's foot?
  **A:** Keep feet clean and dry, change socks, wear sandals in locker rooms, don't share towels.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a hygiene self-assessment and quiz.

*How to teach:* Complete a hygiene self-assessment and quiz. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** How often should you brush and floss?
  **A:** Brush twice a day for two minutes; floss once a day.

**Assessment:** Self-assessment and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: hormones cause more sweat/oil; acne myths; gingivitis signs; brush 2×2, floss daily; athlete's foot prevention; deodorant vs antiperspirant. Self-assessment complete and honest. Proficient = 80%.
