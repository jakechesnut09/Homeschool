# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 18

## English Language Arts 6: Figurative language in poetry

**Prepare:** Gather: 'Fog' by Carl Sandburg, 'Hope is the thing with feathers' by Emily Dickinson, 'Dreams' by Langston Hughes. Print 'Fog', 'Hope is the thing with feathers', 'Dreams' and 'Mother to Son' (Poetry Foundation or library).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing simile and metaphor. A simile uses like/as; a metaphor says one thing is another.
- Thinking connotation is the dictionary meaning. Connotation is the feeling a word carries.

### Day 1 (Monday): Images
*Student:* Read 'Fog' and sketch the image it creates; identify the metaphor and explain what it suggests about fog.

*How to teach:* Read 'Fog' twice; the child sketches the image and explains the metaphor.

- **Q:** What is fog compared to in 'Fog', and what does that suggest?
  **A:** A cat — it arrives silently ('little cat feet'), sits watching the harbor and city 'on silent haunches,' and then moves on, suggesting fog is quiet and changeable.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Extended metaphor
*Student:* Read Dickinson's 'Hope is the thing with feathers' and annotate each stanza's development of the bird metaphor.

*How to teach:* Read Dickinson's poem; annotate stanza by stanza.

- **Q:** How does Dickinson develop the bird metaphor?
  **A:** Hope perches in the soul and sings without words and never stops; it sings sweetest in the storm and keeps many warm; and it never asks 'a crumb' in return.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hughes's dreams
*Student:* Read Hughes's 'Dreams' and 'Mother to Son'; compare their metaphors and tones.

*How to teach:* Read 'Dreams' and 'Mother to Son'; compare metaphors and tones.

- **Q:** Compare the metaphors in 'Dreams' and 'Mother to Son.'
  **A:** 'Dreams' compares life without dreams to a broken-winged bird and a barren, frozen field (urgent, warning tone); 'Mother to Son' compares life to a stair that 'ain't been no crystal stair' with tacks and splinters (determined, encouraging tone: keep climbing).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Connotation
*Student:* Sort word pairs by connotation (thrifty/stingy, slender/skinny) and rewrite a stanza changing connotations to alter tone.

*How to teach:* Sort connotation pairs; the child rewrites a stanza changing tone.

- **Q:** Which word has a more positive connotation: thrifty or stingy?
  **A:** Thrifty (careful with money); stingy suggests selfishness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write your own poem using an extended metaphor; quiz on figurative language identification.

*How to teach:* The child writes an extended-metaphor poem and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** What is an extended metaphor?
  **A:** A metaphor carried through several lines or a whole poem.

**Assessment:** Figurative language quiz and original poem.

**Answer key:** Figurative-language quiz sample: 1) 'The fog comes on little cat feet' — metaphor (fog as a cat) 2) 'Hope is the thing with feathers' — metaphor (hope as a bird) 3) 'Life for me ain't been no crystal stair' — metaphor 4) 'as busy as a bee' — simile 5) 'the wind whispered' — personification 6) 'I've told you a million times' — hyperbole. Poem rubric: 4 = one metaphor developed across the poem, vivid images, deliberate line breaks; 3 = metaphor not fully extended; 2 = scattered images; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Long division and dividing decimals

**Prepare:** Gather: graph paper for aligning digits. Day 5 is the semester-1 review — glance back at the weeks 1–17 tests to see which skills need refreshing.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to write a 0 in the quotient when a divisor doesn't go into a partial dividend.
- Moving the decimal in the divisor but not the dividend. Both must be multiplied by the same power of 10.

### Day 1 (Monday): Partial quotients
*Student:* Divide 3- and 4-digit numbers by 2-digit divisors using partial quotients, then compare to the standard algorithm.

*How to teach:* Model 864 ÷ 24 with partial quotients (24 × 30 = 720, remainder 144 = 24 × 6 → 36), then the standard algorithm side by side.

- **Q:** Compute 864 ÷ 24.
  **A:** 36.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Long division
*Student:* Complete 12 long-division problems with the standard algorithm, checking each by multiplication.

*How to teach:* Practice divide–multiply–subtract–bring down, checking each by multiplying.

- **Q:** Compute 7,392 ÷ 16.
  **A:** 462 (check: 16 × 462 = 7,392).
- **Q:** Compute 1,035 ÷ 23.
  **A:** 45.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dividing decimals
*Student:* Divide decimals by whole numbers and by decimals by multiplying both numbers by powers of 10 (12 problems).

*How to teach:* Show 4.56 ÷ 1.2 becomes 45.6 ÷ 12 by multiplying both by 10. The child solves 12 problems.

- **Q:** Compute 7.2 ÷ 0.08.
  **A:** 90 (720 ÷ 8).
- **Q:** Compute 4.56 ÷ 1.2.
  **A:** 3.8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decimal word problems
*Student:* Solve problems such as splitting a $47.85 bill among 3 people and finding miles per gallon.

*How to teach:* Work the bill problem together, then the child solves the rest and checks reasonableness by estimating.

- **Q:** Split $47.85 equally among 3 people.
  **A:** $15.95 each.
- **Q:** 312.6 miles on 12 gallons — miles per gallon?
  **A:** 26.05 mpg.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Semester 1 cumulative review quiz covering area, ratios, percents, fraction division and decimals.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative quiz; review weak areas next week.

- **Q:** Compute 9.12 ÷ 0.24.
  **A:** 38.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 cumulative quiz.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 cumulative quiz key: 1) triangle b = 10, h = 7 → 35 2) boys:girls 3:5, 40 students → 15 boys, 25 girls 3) 30% of 90 = 27 4) 18 is 40% of 45 5) ¾ ÷ ⅛ = 6 6) 2⅖ ÷ ⅗ = 4 7) 6.3 × 0.4 = 2.52 8) 9.12 ÷ 0.24 = 38 9) $3.60 for 8 oz → $0.45/oz 10) prism 2 × 3 × 4: SA = 52 sq units, V = 24 cubic units.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Magnetic forces and fields

**Prepare:** Gather: bar magnets, paper clips, iron filings or compass, ruler, paper. Keep magnets away from phones and computers. Iron filings go in a sealed bag.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking magnets attract all metals. Only iron, nickel, cobalt (and steel) are strongly attracted; not aluminum or copper.
- Thinking magnetic force needs touching. It acts at a distance through a field.

### Day 1 (Monday): Magnet exploration
*Student:* Test which household objects are attracted to a magnet and how poles interact; record results.

*How to teach:* Test objects; record attracted/not and pole interactions.

- **Q:** Which is attracted: aluminum foil or a steel paper clip?
  **A:** The steel paper clip.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Distance and strength
*Student:* Measure how many paper clips a magnet holds at different distances through layers of paper; graph the data.

*How to teach:* Hold clips through more paper layers; graph.

- **Q:** What pattern do you expect?
  **A:** Fewer clips as distance increases.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mapping fields
*Student:* Map the magnetic field around a bar magnet using a compass or iron filings under paper and draw field lines.

*How to teach:* Map the field with compass or filings.

- **Q:** Where is a bar magnet's field strongest?
  **A:** At the poles, where field lines are closest together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Earth's field
*Student:* Make a compass by magnetizing a needle and floating it on a cork; explain Earth's magnetic field.

*How to teach:* Magnetize a needle; float on cork.

- **Q:** Why does a compass point north?
  **A:** Earth acts like a giant magnet; the needle lines up with Earth's magnetic field.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER on how distance affects magnetic force using your graph.

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER.

- **Q:** Do like poles attract or repel?
  **A:** Repel.

**Assessment:** Lab data graph and CER.

**Answer key:** CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: 'Magnetic force decreases as distance increases — at 0 sheets the magnet held 12 clips but at 10 sheets only 3.'

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Athens, Sparta and the Persian Wars

**Prepare:** Gather: map of the Persian Empire, Herodotus excerpts on Marathon and Thermopylae. Print a Persian Empire map and Herodotus excerpts.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Herodotus is perfectly reliable. He was Greek, so he may favor Greeks and exaggerate Persian numbers.
- Thinking the 300 Spartans fought alone at Thermopylae. Thousands of allied Greeks fought there too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sparta vs. Athens
*Student:* Read about education, women's roles and government in Sparta and Athens and complete a comparison chart.

*How to teach:* Complete the Athens–Sparta chart.

- **Q:** How did Spartan education differ from Athenian?
  **A:** Spartan boys trained for the military from age 7; Athenian boys studied subjects like reading, music and athletics.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Persian Empire
*Student:* Map the Persian Empire under Cyrus and Darius and describe satrapies, the Royal Road and tolerance policies.

*How to teach:* Map the Persian Empire.

- **Q:** What was the Royal Road?
  **A:** A long road (about 1,600 miles from Sardis to Susa) with relay stations to speed messages and trade.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marathon and Thermopylae
*Student:* Read Herodotus's accounts and evaluate him as a source (what bias might he have?).

*How to teach:* Read Herodotus; evaluate bias.

- **Q:** Why might Herodotus be biased?
  **A:** He was Greek writing about Greek victories and may have exaggerated Persian numbers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Salamis
*Student:* Explain how Athenian naval power won at Salamis and diagram the battle.

*How to teach:* Diagram Salamis.

- **Q:** How did the Greeks win at Salamis?
  **A:** Themistocles lured the large Persian fleet into a narrow strait where faster Greek triremes could ram them.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Semester 1 cumulative review quiz covering early humans through the Persian Wars.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative quiz.

- **Q:** Who won the Battle of Marathon?
  **A:** The Athenians (490 BCE).

**Assessment:** Semester 1 cumulative quiz.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 cumulative quiz sample: 1) primary vs. secondary 2) Beringia 3) farming → surplus → specialization 4) cuneiform 5) Hammurabi's Code 6) Nile flows north 7) Rosetta Stone 8) Kush/Meroë iron 9) monotheism 10) polis 11) Cleisthenes 12) Marathon 490 BCE, Thermopylae and Salamis 480 BCE.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Singing and ensemble

**Prepare:** Print lyrics for 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' and 'Dona Nobis Pacem' ('Grant us peace,' a traditional round in three parts). Find a recording of 'Dona Nobis Pacem.' Plan a mid-year family concert.

**Watch for:**
- Singing from the throat. Use breath support from the diaphragm and good posture.
- Listening to the other part and losing your own. Focus on your part; cover one ear if needed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Vocal warm-ups
*Student:* Learn breathing and vocal warm-ups and sing a major scale on solfege.

*How to teach:* Learn breathing and vocal warm-ups; sing do-re-mi-fa-sol-la-ti-do. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name the solfege syllables of a major scale.
  **A:** Do, re, mi, fa, sol, la, ti, do.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rounds
*Student:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' as a round with family members.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' as a round. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is a round?
  **A:** A song where voices sing the same melody starting at different times, creating harmony.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two-part harmony
*Student:* Learn 'Dona Nobis Pacem' and sing one part while a recording or family member sings another.

*How to teach:* Learn 'Dona Nobis Pacem'; sing one part with another. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What does 'Dona Nobis Pacem' mean?
  **A:** 'Grant us peace' (Latin).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearsal
*Student:* Rehearse a song and an instrumental piece for a mini-concert using performance criteria.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a song and instrumental piece with performance criteria. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is good singing posture?
  **A:** Stand tall, shoulders relaxed, feet apart, chin level.

### Day 5 (Friday): Mid-year concert
*Student:* Perform a mid-year family concert (one song, one instrumental piece) and self-evaluate.

*How to teach:* Mid-year concert; self-evaluate. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is harmony?
  **A:** Two or more different pitches sounding at the same time.

**Assessment:** Mid-year concert rubric.

**Answer key:** Concert rubric (1–4 each): accurate pitch/notes, steady rhythm, tone and breath support, holds own part in a round, stage presence. Proficient = 3+ each.

## Physical Education 6: Net games tournament and mid-year check

**Prepare:** Gather: volleyball, badminton set, fitness log. Fitness log with baseline scores ready. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing scores with others. Compare with your own baseline.
- Giving up on a goal not yet met. Adjust the plan.

### Day 1 (Monday): Four square and paddle games
*Student:* Play four square and paddle games to practice placement.

*How to teach:* Play four square and paddle games.

- **Q:** What is placement in net games?
  **A:** Sending the ball where the opponent can't easily return it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mid-year fitness check
*Student:* Retake the PACER or mile and the push-up test and compare to baseline.

*How to teach:* Retest PACER/mile and push-ups.

- **Q:** Baseline 18 laps, now 25. Improvement?
  **A:** 7 laps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tournament day 1
*Student:* Play a family volleyball or badminton tournament round.

*How to teach:* Tournament round 1.

- **Q:** How do you show good sportsmanship after losing?
  **A:** Shake hands and congratulate the winner.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tournament day 2
*Student:* Play final rounds and practice handshakes and positive comments.

*How to teach:* Tournament finals.

- **Q:** Give a positive comment to an opponent.
  **A:** 'Great serve!' 'Good game!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Goal check
*Student:* Update your SMART goals based on mid-year results.

*How to teach:* Update SMART goals.

## Health 6: Injury prevention at home

**Prepare:** Print a home safety checklist (smoke and CO alarms on each level and near bedrooms, tested monthly; cords; chemicals stored high and locked; stairs clear; medicines secured). Find CDC injury data for ages 10–14 (WISQARS). Have a flashlight. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking injuries are 'accidents' that can't be prevented. Most injuries are predictable and preventable.
- Mixing cleaning chemicals to clean better. Mixing bleach and ammonia creates toxic gas.

### Day 1 (Monday): Injury facts
*Student:* Read data on the leading causes of injury for kids 10–14 and graph the top five.

*How to teach:* Read injury data; graph the top five causes. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is the leading cause of injury death for kids 10–14?
  **A:** Motor vehicle crashes (falls and being struck by objects cause many non-fatal injuries).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Home audit
*Student:* Complete a home safety audit with a parent (smoke and CO alarms, cords, chemicals, stairs).

*How to teach:* Do a home safety audit with a parent. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** How often should smoke alarms be tested?
  **A:** Monthly (and batteries replaced as needed).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kitchen safety
*Student:* List rules for preventing burns and cuts and practice safe stove use with an adult.

*How to teach:* List rules for preventing burns and cuts; practice safe stove use. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why turn pot handles inward?
  **A:** So no one bumps or grabs them and spills hot food.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poison prevention
*Student:* Check chemical storage and post the Poison Control number.

*How to teach:* Check chemical storage; post Poison Control. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why should chemicals stay in original containers?
  **A:** So they're labeled with ingredients and warnings and not mistaken for drinks or food.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a home safety action plan and complete a check.

*How to teach:* Write a home safety action plan. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What does carbon monoxide do and why need a CO alarm?
  **A:** CO is an invisible, odorless poison gas that stops blood carrying oxygen; an alarm detects it.

**Assessment:** Home safety plan.

**Answer key:** Home safety plan: lists hazards found, specific fixes, who will do them, when. Check key: smoke/CO alarms tested monthly; chemicals stored safely; never mix bleach and ammonia; Poison Control 1-800-222-1222; pot handles in. Proficient = 4/5.
