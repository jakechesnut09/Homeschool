# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 25

## English Language Arts 6: Writing a memoir chapter

**Prepare:** Gather: notebook, computer. Have the child think ahead about something hard they learned (riding a bike, a skill, a challenge).

**Watch for:**
- Writing only events without reflection. Keller mixes narration with what it meant.
- Overusing dashes; vary with commas and parentheses.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Brainstorm moments when you learned something difficult and plan a memoir chapter with a turning point.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm learning moments; the child picks one and plans a turning point.

- **Q:** What is a turning point in a memoir?
  **A:** The moment the writer's understanding or situation changes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the chapter, combining narration of events with adult-like reflection, as Keller does.

*How to teach:* The child drafts, mixing events with reflection.

- **Q:** Write a reflection sentence like Keller's.
  **A:** Example: 'Looking back, I realize that falling was what finally taught me balance.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for voice and add at least three nonrestrictive elements punctuated with commas, parentheses or dashes.

*How to teach:* Revise for voice; add three nonrestrictive elements.

- **Q:** Punctuate: My coach a patient man never yelled.
  **A:** 'My coach, a patient man, never yelled.' (or parentheses/dashes)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit for conventions and publish the chapter typed.

*How to teach:* Edit and type the chapter.

- **Q:** Is a nonrestrictive element needed to identify the noun?
  **A:** No — it adds extra information and can be removed without changing the core meaning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share and check
*Student:* Read your chapter aloud and take the unit test on informational reading skills.

*How to teach:* The child reads aloud and takes the unit test.

- **Q:** Primary or secondary source: Keller's autobiography?
  **A:** Primary source.

**Assessment:** Memoir chapter rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Memoir chapter rubric — 4: clear turning point, vivid narration, adult-like reflection on meaning, at least three correctly punctuated nonrestrictive elements, few errors; 3: reflection thin or punctuation inconsistent; 2: list of events; 1: incomplete. Unit test sample: central idea, objective summary, text structure, point of view/purpose, memoir vs. biography, primary vs. secondary source, nonrestrictive elements.

## Mathematics 6: Positive and negative numbers

**Prepare:** Gather: outdoor thermometer, number line strip. Check today's temperature on an outdoor thermometer; make a −10 to 10 number-line strip.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking −5 is greater than −2 because 5 > 2. On the number line, −5 is farther left, so it is less.
- Thinking zero always means 'nothing'. In elevation, 0 is sea level; in temperature, 0° is just a point on the scale.

### Day 1 (Monday): Temperature and elevation
*Student:* Record temperatures in different places (e.g., Death Valley highs, Lake Tahoe winter lows) and elevations above and below sea level, and explain the meaning of 0.

*How to teach:* Discuss California extremes: Badwater Basin in Death Valley is 282 ft below sea level (−282), Mount Whitney is about 14,505 ft above. Ask what 0 means for elevation and temperature.

- **Q:** What does an elevation of −282 feet mean?
  **A:** 282 feet below sea level (0 = sea level).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Opposites
*Student:* Mark 12 numbers and their opposites on a number line, including -2.5 and 3/4, and explain why -(-4) = 4.

*How to teach:* Mark pairs like 3 and −3 at equal distances from 0. Explain −(−4) as 'the opposite of the opposite of 4'.

- **Q:** What is the opposite of −2.5? Of ¾?
  **A:** 2.5 and −¾.
- **Q:** Why does −(−4) = 4?
  **A:** −4 is the opposite of 4; the opposite of that is back to 4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Money and signed numbers
*Student:* Represent deposits, withdrawals and debt with positive and negative numbers in 8 situations.

*How to teach:* Model a deposit as +, a withdrawal or debt as −. The child writes signed numbers for 8 situations.

- **Q:** Write signed numbers: deposit $50; withdraw $20; owe $15.
  **A:** +50, −20, −15.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rational numbers on the line
*Student:* Place fractions and decimals between -3 and 3 on a carefully scaled number line.

*How to teach:* Draw −3 to 3 with tick marks every ½ or ¼. The child places −2.5, −¾, 1.25, 2⅓ etc.

- **Q:** Between which two integers is −¾? Is it closer to 0 or −1?
  **A:** Between −1 and 0, closer to −1.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on signed numbers in context and number-line placement.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Which is colder: −5°F or −12°F?
  **A:** −12°F.

**Assessment:** Signed-number quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 20 ft below sea level → −20 ft 2) a gain of 7 yards → +7 3) opposite of −8 → 8 4) −(−6) = 6 5) a bank balance of 0 means you have no money and owe nothing 6) −1.5 halfway between −2 and −1; ¾ between 0 and 1; −2⅓ just left of −2 7) −12°F is colder than −5°F.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Living things are made of cells

**Prepare:** Gather: dry yeast, sugar, warm water, balloon, bottle, pond water (optional), cotton swab. Use warm (not hot) water for yeast. Use a new cotton swab for cheek cells.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking things that move are alive and things that don't aren't. Use all characteristics of life.
- Thinking single-celled organisms are not 'real' living things.

### Day 1 (Monday): Is it alive?
*Student:* Sort 15 items as living, once living or nonliving using characteristics of life.

*How to teach:* Review characteristics: cells, growth, reproduction, use of energy, response, need for water.

- **Q:** Is a fallen log living, once-living or nonliving?
  **A:** Once-living.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yeast are alive
*Student:* Mix yeast, sugar and warm water in a bottle with a balloon on top, and record balloon size every 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Mix yeast, sugar and warm water; balloon on top; measure every 10 minutes.

- **Q:** What gas inflates the balloon?
  **A:** Carbon dioxide produced by the yeast.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pond water
*Student:* Observe pond water or a hay infusion under the microscope and sketch single-celled organisms.

*How to teach:* Observe pond water; sketch organisms.

- **Q:** How do we know microorganisms are alive?
  **A:** They move, take in food, grow and reproduce.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cheek cells
*Student:* Gently swab your inner cheek, stain with food coloring and observe human cells; compare with onion cells.

*How to teach:* Swab cheek gently; stain; compare with onion.

- **Q:** Name one difference between cheek and onion cells.
  **A:** Cheek cells are irregular and lack a cell wall; onion cells are rectangular with a cell wall.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER that yeast is a living, single-celled organism using balloon data.

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER.

- **Q:** What would happen without sugar?
  **A:** Little or no gas — the yeast would have no food for energy.

**Assessment:** Yeast CER.

**Answer key:** CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: 'Yeast is alive: the balloon grew from 0 cm to 25 cm around in 30 minutes because yeast used sugar for energy and released carbon dioxide gas — living things use energy and produce wastes.'

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The Maurya Empire and Indian achievements

**Prepare:** Gather: Rock Edicts of Ashoka excerpts, images of the Lion Capital of Ashoka. Print three Rock Edicts of Ashoka and an image of the Lion Capital.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Ashoka was always peaceful. He turned to Buddhism after the bloody Kalinga war.
- Thinking 'Hindu-Arabic numerals' came from Arabia. They developed in India and spread through Arab scholars.

### Day 1 (Monday): Maurya Empire
*Student:* Map the Maurya Empire under Chandragupta and describe how it was governed.

*How to teach:* Map the Maurya Empire.

- **Q:** Who founded the Maurya Empire?
  **A:** Chandragupta Maurya.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ashoka's edicts
*Student:* Read three Rock Edicts of Ashoka and identify the values he promoted after the Kalinga war.

*How to teach:* Read three edicts.

- **Q:** What values did Ashoka promote?
  **A:** Nonviolence, religious tolerance, respect for elders, kindness to animals, public welfare.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lion Capital
*Student:* Analyze the Lion Capital and explain why it appears on India's flag and currency today.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Lion Capital.

- **Q:** Where does Ashoka's legacy appear in modern India?
  **A:** The Lion Capital is India's national emblem (on currency); the Ashoka Chakra wheel is on the flag.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Indian achievements
*Student:* Research Indian contributions (zero and Hindu-Arabic numerals, metallurgy, medicine, the Bhagavad Gita) and make an illustrated chart.

*How to teach:* Research and chart achievements.

- **Q:** Name an important Indian math contribution.
  **A:** Zero as a number and the place-value decimal numerals we use today.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on ancient India.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** What war changed Ashoka?
  **A:** The Kalinga war.

**Assessment:** Edict analysis and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Edict analysis — 4: identifies values (nonviolence, tolerance of all religions, respect for parents, kindness to animals, care for people's welfare) with quotes and links to remorse after Kalinga. Unit 6 test sample: geography and monsoons; Harappan cities; Vedas; varnas; Hindu beliefs; Buddha; Maurya Empire founded by Chandragupta Maurya (~321 BCE); Ashoka's edicts; the Ashoka Chakra on India's flag and the Lion Capital as the national emblem; zero and decimal numerals.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Adapting a story for the stage

**Prepare:** Choose an Aesop fable or Greek myth (e.g., 'The Tortoise and the Hare' or Perseus/Pegasus). Have a notebook; look at a sample script page showing character names, dialogue and stage directions in italics/parentheses.

**Watch for:**
- Including every detail from the story. A script keeps only essential events and shows them through dialogue and action.
- Writing narration instead of dialogue. Plays show story through what characters say and do.

### Day 1 (Monday): Essential events
*Student:* Choose a fable or myth and identify the essential events that make up its dramatic structure.

*How to teach:* Identify the essential events (beginning, conflict, climax, resolution). Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is the climax of 'The Tortoise and the Hare'?
  **A:** The moment the tortoise passes the sleeping hare and crosses the finish line.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Scripting
*Student:* Write a 2-3 page script with dialogue and stage directions.

*How to teach:* Write a 2–3 page script with dialogue and stage directions. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What are stage directions?
  **A:** Instructions in a script for actors' movements, actions, and setting, often in italics or parentheses.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Staging problems
*Student:* Solve staging challenges (a flying horse, a storm) with simple props, sound or movement.

*How to teach:* Solve staging challenges with props, sound or movement. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How could you show a storm on stage simply?
  **A:** Shake a sheet of cardboard for thunder, flicker lights, actors sway and use sound effects.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Table read
*Student:* Do a table read with family and revise the script based on what did not work.

*How to teach:* Table read with family; revise. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What is a table read?
  **A:** Actors read the script aloud together, seated, to hear how it works.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Finalize the script and plan props and costumes.

*How to teach:* Finalize script; plan props and costumes. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is an adaptation?
  **A:** A new version of a story changed to fit a different form, like a play.

**Assessment:** Original adapted script.

**Answer key:** Script rubric: 4 = essential events with clear conflict and resolution, formatted dialogue and stage directions, creative staging solutions; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing elements; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Create a fitness dance

**Prepare:** Gather: music, paper for routine notes. Have music and paper for 8-count notes. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a song too long. Keep to 2 minutes.
- Low-intensity moves only. Include large movements to raise heart rate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose music and plan a 2-minute routine using at least six steps learned.

*How to teach:* Choose music; plan steps.

- **Q:** Name six steps to use.
  **A:** Grapevine, step-touch, pivot turn, kick-ball-change, jumping jacks, high knees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the routine in 8-counts and write it down.

*How to teach:* Write the routine in 8-counts.

- **Q:** How many 8-counts are in about 1 minute of music at 120 beats per minute?
  **A:** 15 (120 ÷ 8).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice
*Student:* Practice the routine and check heart rate to confirm moderate-to-vigorous intensity.

*How to teach:* Practice; check heart rate.

- **Q:** How do you know the routine is moderate-to-vigorous?
  **A:** Heart rate rises into the target zone and talking becomes harder.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teach
*Student:* Teach your routine to family members.

*How to teach:* Teach the routine.

- **Q:** What's a good teaching strategy?
  **A:** Demonstrate slowly, break into 8-counts, then add music.

### Day 5 (Friday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the routine together and evaluate with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Perform; use the rubric.

- **Q:** Why repeat sequences in a routine?
  **A:** Easier to remember and follow.

**Assessment:** Fitness dance rubric.

**Answer key:** Fitness dance rubric (4/3/2/1): 2-minute routine; 6+ learned steps; written in 8-counts; heart rate in moderate-to-vigorous range; taught clearly; performed on the beat.

## Health 6: Wildfire, flood and emergency kits

**Prepare:** Print the Ready.gov emergency kit checklist and family communication plan. Get a backpack. Look up your county's evacuation zone system (e.g., Genasys Protect) and AirNow.gov for AQI. AQI colors: green 0–50 good, yellow 51–100 moderate, orange 101–150 unhealthy for sensitive groups, red 151–200 unhealthy, purple 201–300 very unhealthy, maroon 301+ hazardous.

**Watch for:**
- Waiting for an evacuation order to start packing. Have a go-bag ready ahead of time and leave when told.
- Thinking any mask blocks smoke. Cloth masks don't filter fine smoke particles; well-fitted N95 masks do.

### Day 1 (Monday): Wildfire and smoke
*Student:* Learn evacuation zones and how to read the Air Quality Index during wildfire smoke.

*How to teach:* Learn evacuation zones and read the AQI. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** The AQI is 165. What should you do?
  **A:** It's 'unhealthy' (red): stay indoors, close windows, limit outdoor activity, and use an air purifier if available.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Go-bag
*Student:* Pack or list a 72-hour go-bag using the Ready.gov checklist.

*How to teach:* Pack or list a 72-hour go-bag. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** How much water should a go-bag plan for?
  **A:** About one gallon per person per day, for at least three days.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family plan
*Student:* Complete a family communication plan with an out-of-area contact.

*How to teach:* Complete a family communication plan with an out-of-area contact. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why have an out-of-area contact?
  **A:** Local phone lines may be overloaded; family members can all check in with one person far away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Help others
*Student:* Plan how to help a neighbor, pet or elderly relative in an emergency.

*How to teach:* Plan how to help a neighbor, pet or elderly relative. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** How can you prepare to help a pet in an emergency?
  **A:** Pack food, water, leash/carrier, medications and vaccination records in the go-bag.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the family emergency plan and take the Preparedness unit quiz.

*How to teach:* Present the family plan; take the Preparedness quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Name five items for a go-bag.
  **A:** Water, non-perishable food, flashlight, batteries, first aid kit, medications, phone charger, N95 masks, copies of documents, cash.

**Assessment:** Family plan and quiz.

**Answer key:** Family plan: meeting places, out-of-area contact, go-bag list, pet/neighbor plan. Quiz key: AQI levels; go-bag contents; 1 gallon/person/day; N95 for smoke; evacuation zones; Drop, Cover, Hold On; fire escape. Proficient = 80%.
