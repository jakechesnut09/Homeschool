# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 21

## English Language Arts 6: Poetry anthology

**Prepare:** Gather: notebook, art supplies, computer. Gather art supplies and a folder or binder for the anthology.

**Watch for:**
- Haiku must be 5-7-5 and nothing else. Also emphasize nature focus and a fresh image.
- Forcing rhymes that make no sense. Meaning comes first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ode
*Student:* Write an ode to an ordinary object using at least three types of figurative language.

*How to teach:* Read an ode example (e.g., Pablo Neruda's 'Ode to My Socks' or a CommonLit ode). The child writes an ode to an ordinary object.

- **Q:** What is an ode?
  **A:** A poem praising or celebrating a person, object or idea, often with strong feeling.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Haiku and concrete poems
*Student:* Write three haiku about nature observations and one concrete (shape) poem.

*How to teach:* Review haiku form; the child writes three from outdoor observation and one concrete poem.

- **Q:** What is the syllable pattern of a traditional English haiku?
  **A:** 5-7-5 syllables in three lines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Narrative poem
*Student:* Write a short narrative poem with a rhyme scheme, modeled on Longfellow.

*How to teach:* Reread a Longfellow stanza for model; the child writes a narrative poem with a rhyme scheme.

- **Q:** What makes a poem a narrative poem?
  **A:** It tells a story with characters, events and a plot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compile
*Student:* Revise and compile 6-8 poems into an illustrated anthology with a table of contents and author's note.

*How to teach:* The child revises and compiles 6–8 poems.

- **Q:** What does an author's note include?
  **A:** Why the poet wrote the poems, inspirations and choices they made.

### Day 5 (Friday): Poetry cafe
*Student:* Host a family poetry reading performing three poems; take the unit test on poetry terms.

*How to teach:* Host the poetry café; give the unit test.

- **Q:** What is free verse?
  **A:** Poetry without a regular rhyme scheme or meter.

**Assessment:** Anthology rubric, performance and unit test.

**Answer key:** Anthology rubric — 4: 6–8 polished poems (ode, haiku, concrete, narrative, plus choices), varied figurative language, table of contents, author's note, illustrations; 3: complete with less polish; 2: fewer than 6 poems or missing parts; 1: incomplete. Unit test sample: metaphor, simile, personification, alliteration, rhyme scheme, meter/rhythm, speaker, tone, theme, free verse, ode, haiku, extended metaphor, connotation.

## Mathematics 6: Solving one-step equations

**Prepare:** Gather: balance scale or hanger diagram printouts, paper clips. Make a coat-hanger balance with two cups and paper clips, or print hanger diagrams.

**Watch for:**
- Doing different operations to each side. Whatever you do to one side of the balance, you must do to the other.
- Dividing the wrong way: for 3x = 12 some write x = 3/12. Ask 'what times 3 is 12?'

### Day 1 (Monday): True or false?
*Student:* Test whether given values make 10 equations true using substitution.

*How to teach:* Show that an equation is a statement that can be true or false; substitute values to test. The child tests 10 equations.

- **Q:** Is x = 4 a solution to 3x + 2 = 14?
  **A:** Yes: 3(4) + 2 = 14.
- **Q:** Is x = 5 a solution to x + 7 = 13?
  **A:** No: 5 + 7 = 12, not 13 (the solution is 6).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hanger diagrams
*Student:* Use hanger diagrams (or a homemade coat-hanger balance with paper clips) to model and solve equations like x + 3 = 7 and 3x = 12.

*How to teach:* Hang 3 clips plus a 'mystery bag' on one side and 7 clips on the other; remove 3 from each side. Draw the hanger diagram alongside.

- **Q:** Solve x + 3 = 7 and 3x = 12 using the balance idea.
  **A:** x = 4 (remove 3 from both sides); x = 4 (split both sides into 3 equal groups).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solving addition equations
*Student:* Solve 12 equations of the form x + p = q with fractions and decimals, checking by substitution.

*How to teach:* Model subtracting the same amount from both sides, then substituting to check. The child solves 12 problems.

- **Q:** Solve x + 2.7 = 5.
  **A:** x = 2.3.
- **Q:** Solve x + ¾ = 2½.
  **A:** x = 1¾.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Solving multiplication equations
*Student:* Solve 12 equations of the form px = q, including 2/3 x = 10, and write stories for two of them.

*How to teach:* Model dividing both sides by the coefficient (or multiplying by its reciprocal). The child solves 12 and writes stories for two.

- **Q:** Solve ⅔x = 10.
  **A:** x = 15 (multiply both sides by 3/2).
- **Q:** Solve 4x = 9.2.
  **A:** x = 2.3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on solving one-step equations and writing equations for word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; the child must write the equation before solving each word problem.

- **Q:** 5 notebooks cost $13.75. Write and solve an equation for the price of one.
  **A:** 5n = 13.75; n = $2.75.

**Assessment:** One-step equation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) x + 8 = 15 → x = 7 2) y + 4.5 = 9 → y = 4.5 3) 6x = 42 → x = 7 4) 0.5x = 7 → x = 14 5) x + 1.25 = 4 → x = 2.75 6) ¾x = 12 → x = 16 7) 5 notebooks cost $13.75: 5n = 13.75, n = $2.75 8) Maya is 12, which is 3 years older than Leo: L + 3 = 12, L = 9.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Kinetic energy

**Prepare:** Gather: ramp (board and books), toy car, balls of different masses, paper cup. Build a ramp (board on books) and cut a door in a paper cup to catch the ball.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking speed and mass affect kinetic energy equally. Doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy; doubling mass only doubles it.
- Measuring cup distance from different starting spots. Always start the cup at the same place.

### Day 1 (Monday): Energy of motion
*Student:* Roll a ball down a ramp into a paper cup and measure how far the cup slides as evidence of kinetic energy.

*How to teach:* Roll a ball into the cup; measure slide distance.

- **Q:** Why is cup distance evidence of kinetic energy?
  **A:** The moving ball transfers energy to the cup; more energy pushes the cup farther.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mass investigation
*Student:* Use balls of different mass from the same ramp height and record cup distance in three trials each.

*How to teach:* Test different-mass balls from the same height, 3 trials each.

- **Q:** What do you predict for a heavier ball?
  **A:** The cup slides farther — more mass, more kinetic energy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speed investigation
*Student:* Release the same ball from three ramp heights (different speeds) and record cup distance.

*How to teach:* Release the same ball from three heights.

- **Q:** Why does a higher release give more speed?
  **A:** More potential energy converts to kinetic energy at the bottom.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graph it
*Student:* Graph cup distance vs. mass and vs. speed and describe which relationship is steeper.

*How to teach:* Graph both relationships; compare steepness.

- **Q:** If speed doubles, how does kinetic energy change?
  **A:** It becomes four times as large (KE = ½mv²).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER explaining why a speeding car is much more dangerous than a slow one (speed squared relationship).

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER.

- **Q:** A car's speed goes from 30 to 60 mph. How many times more kinetic energy?
  **A:** Four times.

**Assessment:** Graphs and CER.

**Answer key:** Graphs: cup distance increases with mass (roughly a straight line) and increases more steeply with speed (ramp height). CER rubric — 4: a claim that answers the question, specific data as evidence (numbers from the investigation), and reasoning that connects the evidence to the science idea; 3: claim and evidence correct but reasoning thin; 2: claim with vague evidence; 1: claim only or incorrect. Expected: a speeding car is far more dangerous because kinetic energy depends on speed squared — doubling speed gives four times the energy that must be absorbed in a crash.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Philosophers, scientists and Alexander

**Prepare:** Gather: Plato's Apology excerpt, map of Alexander's empire, compass and straightedge. Print an excerpt of Plato's Apology and Alexander's empire map; get a compass and straightedge.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Socrates wrote books. Plato recorded his ideas.
- Thinking Alexander only conquered. He also spread Greek culture (Hellenistic Age).

### Day 1 (Monday): Socrates, Plato, Aristotle
*Student:* Read about the three philosophers and practice the Socratic method by questioning a family member about 'What is fairness?'.

*How to teach:* Read about the three philosophers; question a family member on fairness.

- **Q:** What is the Socratic method?
  **A:** Asking a series of questions to examine ideas and find truth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Scientists
*Student:* Research Euclid, Archimedes, Hippocrates and Hypatia and make trading cards for each.

*How to teach:* Research four scientists; make trading cards.

- **Q:** What is Hippocrates known for?
  **A:** Treating illness as having natural causes; the Hippocratic Oath.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Alexander the Great
*Student:* Map Alexander's conquests and explain how he spread Greek culture into Egypt and Asia.

*How to teach:* Map Alexander's conquests.

- **Q:** How far did Alexander's empire reach?
  **A:** From Greece and Egypt across Persia to the Indus River in India.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Alexandria
*Student:* Read about the Library and Lighthouse of Alexandria and do one of Euclid's compass-and-straightedge constructions.

*How to teach:* Read about Alexandria; do a construction (e.g., bisect a line segment).

- **Q:** What was the Library of Alexandria?
  **A:** A great center of learning with many scrolls in Egypt's city of Alexandria.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test on Ancient Greece.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** Who was Aristotle's famous student?
  **A:** Alexander the Great.

**Assessment:** Trading cards and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Trading cards: Euclid — geometry (Elements); Archimedes — levers, buoyancy, 'Eureka'; Hippocrates — medicine, Hippocratic Oath; Hypatia — mathematician and teacher in Alexandria. Unit 5 test sample: geography; polis; forms of government; Athens vs. Sparta; Persian Wars; Golden Age; Peloponnesian War; Socrates, Plato, Aristotle; Alexander; columns.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Composing an ABA piece

**Prepare:** Open Chrome Music Lab Song Maker; have staff paper and an instrument. Criteria: clear motif, A expresses a mood, B contrasts, A returns, ends with a cadence on the tonic.

**Watch for:**
- Making A and B too similar. Change rhythm, register (high/low) or dynamics for contrast.
- Ending on a note that sounds unfinished. End on the tonic (home note) for a final cadence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Motifs
*Student:* Improvise and record five short motifs on your instrument or Song Maker.

*How to teach:* Improvise and record five short motifs. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is a motif?
  **A:** A short musical idea that can be repeated and developed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Section A
*Student:* Compose an 8-measure A section that expresses a chosen mood.

*How to teach:* Compose an 8-measure A section expressing a mood. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How can you make music sound happy?
  **A:** Major key, faster tempo, bright high notes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Section B
*Student:* Compose a contrasting B section that changes rhythm, register or dynamics.

*How to teach:* Compose a contrasting B section. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Name two ways to contrast B with A.
  **A:** Change register, rhythm, dynamics, tempo or major/minor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Notate
*Student:* Write the composition in standard notation or save it in Song Maker.

*How to teach:* Notate or save the composition. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why notate?
  **A:** So it can be performed again accurately.

### Day 5 (Friday): Peer feedback
*Student:* Play your draft for a listener and record their feedback using criteria.

*How to teach:* Play for a listener; record feedback using criteria. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is a cadence?
  **A:** A musical ending of a phrase, like punctuation.

**Assessment:** Documented draft composition.

**Answer key:** Draft: 4 = clear motif, 8-measure A, contrasting B, A returns, notated/saved, feedback recorded; 3 = one missing; 2 = two missing; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 6: Kickball and base running

**Prepare:** Gather: playground ball, bases. Set bases about 15–18 m apart. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Stopping on first base. Run through it.
- Throwing to the wrong base. Think about force outs before the play.

### Day 1 (Monday): Base running
*Student:* Practice running through first base and rounding bases.

*How to teach:* Run through first; round bases.

- **Q:** Why may you run through first base?
  **A:** The rules allow overrunning first, so you keep full speed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Outs
*Student:* Practice force outs and tag outs in a fielding drill.

*How to teach:* Force outs vs. tag outs.

- **Q:** What is a force out?
  **A:** When a runner must advance, the fielder only needs to touch the base with the ball before the runner arrives.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kickball game
*Student:* Play kickball applying fielding positions.

*How to teach:* Play kickball.

- **Q:** Where do fielders stand for a strong kicker?
  **A:** Deeper in the outfield.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relay throws
*Student:* Practice relay throws from the outfield through a cutoff.

*How to teach:* Relay throws through a cutoff.

- **Q:** Why use a cutoff throw?
  **A:** Two shorter throws are faster and more accurate than one long throw.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Explain where fielders should throw with a runner on first and why.

*How to teach:* Explain the runner-on-first decision.

- **Q:** Runner on first, ground ball to shortstop. Where to throw?
  **A:** Second base for the force out (and possibly first for a double play).

**Assessment:** Fielding decision quiz.

**Answer key:** Fielding decision quiz: 1) with a runner on first and a ground ball, throw to second for a force out (or first for the sure out) 2) force out = touch the base before the runner because they must advance 3) tag out = tag a runner not on a base 4) relay throws go to a cutoff fielder 5) you may overrun first base.

## Health 6: First aid basics I

**Prepare:** Open the home first-aid kit; gather disposable gloves, gauze, bandages and a cold pack. Learn Red Cross Check–Call–Care: Check the scene and the person, Call 911 if needed, Care for the person until help arrives.

**Watch for:**
- Putting ice, butter or toothpaste on a burn. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for about 10 minutes, then cover loosely.
- Tilting the head back for a nosebleed. Lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose.

### Day 1 (Monday): Check-Call-Care
*Student:* Learn the Check-Call-Care steps and practice scene safety checks.

*How to teach:* Learn Check–Call–Care; practice scene safety checks. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why check the scene first?
  **A:** So you don't become a second victim (traffic, fire, electricity).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bleeding
*Student:* Practice direct pressure and bandaging on a family member with gloves.

*How to teach:* Practice direct pressure and bandaging with gloves. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** How do you stop bleeding?
  **A:** Wear gloves, press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth/gauze, and keep pressure; add more gauze on top if blood soaks through.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Burns
*Student:* Learn first aid for minor burns (cool water, cover) and when to get help.

*How to teach:* Learn first aid for minor burns and when to get help. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** When does a burn need medical help?
  **A:** If it's large, deep, blistered over a big area, on the face, hands, feet or genitals, or caused by chemicals or electricity.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nosebleeds and sprains
*Student:* Practice nosebleed care and RICE for sprains.

*How to teach:* Practice nosebleed care and RICE for sprains. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What does RICE stand for?
  **A:** Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Inventory and restock the home first aid kit and complete a skills check.

*How to teach:* Inventory and restock the first aid kit; skills check. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why wear gloves when giving first aid?
  **A:** To protect you and the injured person from germs in blood or body fluids (universal precautions).

**Assessment:** First aid skills check.

**Answer key:** Skills check (✓ each): demonstrates Check–Call–Care, direct pressure and bandage, cool-water burn care, nosebleed (lean forward, pinch), RICE, gloves. Proficient = 5 of 6.
