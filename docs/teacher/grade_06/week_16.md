# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 16

## English Language Arts 6: Drafting and revising the argument

**Prepare:** Gather: outline, computer. Have the outline and a computer ready.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 'I think' or 'In my opinion'. State the claim directly.
- Dropping evidence in without explaining how it supports the reason.

### Day 1 (Monday): Introduction
*Student:* Draft an introduction with a hook, background and a precise claim.

*How to teach:* Model a hook, two background sentences and a precise claim.

- **Q:** Make this claim more precise: 'Phones are bad.'
  **A:** Example: 'Middle schools should require students to keep phones in lockers during class because phones distract from learning.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body paragraphs
*Student:* Draft body paragraphs that explain how each piece of evidence supports your reason.

*How to teach:* The child drafts body paragraphs; check each has evidence + explanation.

- **Q:** What sentence frame helps explain evidence?
  **A:** 'This shows that… because…'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Counterclaim
*Student:* Draft a counterclaim-and-rebuttal paragraph and a conclusion that follows from the argument.

*How to teach:* The child drafts counterclaim/rebuttal and conclusion.

- **Q:** Give a frame for a counterclaim.
  **A:** 'Some people argue that… However, …'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for formal style (no slang, no 'I think') and add transitions like 'furthermore' and 'however'.

*How to teach:* Revise for formal tone and transitions.

- **Q:** Rewrite formally: 'Homework is super dumb.'
  **A:** Example: 'Excessive homework provides little benefit for students.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit for pronoun use, commas and spelling and type the final essay; self-score with the argument rubric.

*How to teach:* Edit, type, self-score.

- **Q:** Name two transitions that show contrast.
  **A:** However; on the other hand; although.

**Assessment:** Argument essay scored on rubric.

**Answer key:** Argument essay rubric — 4: precise claim; 2–3 reasons, each with relevant evidence and explanation; counterclaim fairly stated and rebutted; formal style; transitions; conclusion follows from the argument; few errors. 3: all parts, some explanation thin. 2: missing counterclaim or weak evidence. 1: incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Volume with fractional edge lengths

**Prepare:** Gather: small box, 1/2-inch cubes or sugar cubes, ruler. Choose a small box whose edges are whole or half inches. Sugar cubes are roughly ½ inch, so counts will be approximate.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a ½-inch cube has volume ½ cubic inch. It's ½ × ½ × ½ = ⅛ cubic inch; 8 fit in a 1-inch cube.
- Confusing B (area of the base) with b (a base length).

### Day 1 (Monday): Packing cubes
*Student:* Pack a small box with 1/2-inch cubes, count them, and show that the count x 1/8 cubic inch equals l x w x h.

*How to teach:* Pack the box with ½-inch cubes, counting rows × columns × layers. Show the count × ⅛ = l × w × h.

- **Q:** A box is 2 in × 1½ in × 1 in. How many ½-inch cubes fill it, and what is its volume?
  **A:** 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubes; 24 × ⅛ = 3 cubic inches = 2 × 1½ × 1.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Volume formulas
*Student:* Compute volumes of 10 prisms with fractional edges using V = lwh and V = Bh.

*How to teach:* Model V = lwh and V = Bh where B is the base area. The child computes the 10 volumes.

- **Q:** Find the volume of a prism 2½ × 3 × 1½.
  **A:** 11¼ cubic units.
- **Q:** A prism's base has area 10½ sq in and height 2 in. Volume?
  **A:** 21 cubic inches.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Missing dimensions
*Student:* Use fraction division to find a missing edge when volume and two edges are known (6 problems).

*How to teach:* Write V = l × w × h with a blank; multiply the known edges and divide.

- **Q:** Volume 30 cu in, length 4 in, width 2½ in. Height?
  **A:** 3 in (30 ÷ 10).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of fraction division and volume, including one aquarium or garden-bed design problem.

*How to teach:* Assign the mixed review plus a garden-bed design: the child chooses dimensions and computes soil needed.

- **Q:** A garden bed is 6 ft × 2½ ft × ¾ ft. How much soil fills it?
  **A:** 11¼ cubic feet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 assessment and reflect on the meaning of division.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test, then a short reflection on the two meanings of division.

- **Q:** How many ½-inch cubes fill a 1-inch cube?
  **A:** 8.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 end-of-unit assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test sample key: 1) ¾ ÷ ⅜ = 2 2) 5 ÷ ⅚ = 6 3) 2⅓ ÷ ⅔ = 3½ 4) prism 2½ × 3 × 1½ → 11¼ cubic units 5) volume 30, l = 4, w = 2½ → h = 3 6) how many ½-in cubes fill a 2 × 1 × 1 in box? 16 7) 3½ yards cut into ½-yard pieces → 7 8) garden bed 6 × 2½ × ¾ ft → 11¼ cu ft. Reflection names 'how many groups' or 'how much in one group'.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Earthquakes, volcanoes and landslides

**Prepare:** Gather: USGS earthquake map, graph paper, tray of sand and water. Open earthquake.usgs.gov 'Latest Earthquakes'. Do the sand tray outside or in a sink.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking scientists can predict exact earthquake times. They forecast probabilities.
- Thinking earthquakes happen randomly. Most occur along faults and plate boundaries.

### Day 1 (Monday): Earthquake data
*Student:* Use the USGS 'Latest Earthquakes' map to record 20 California earthquakes from the past month and plot them on a map.

*How to teach:* Record and plot 20 quakes.

- **Q:** Where are most California earthquakes?
  **A:** Along faults, especially the San Andreas system and nearby faults.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Patterns along faults
*Student:* Compare your map with a map of the San Andreas fault system and describe the pattern.

*How to teach:* Compare with the fault map.

- **Q:** What is the San Andreas fault?
  **A:** A boundary where the Pacific Plate and North American Plate slide past each other.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Landslide tray
*Student:* Build a sand slope in a tray and add water gradually to observe when a landslide happens; relate to wildfire burn scars and storms.

*How to teach:* Add water to the sand slope.

- **Q:** Why do landslides follow heavy rain on burn scars?
  **A:** Fire removes plants whose roots hold soil; water-saturated soil becomes heavy and slides.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Volcano monitoring
*Student:* Read how scientists monitor Mount Shasta and Lassen with seismometers and gas sensors.

*How to teach:* Read about volcano monitoring.

- **Q:** What signs suggest a volcano might erupt?
  **A:** More small earthquakes, ground swelling and changes in gas emissions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on hazard patterns and forecasting.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Can scientists predict the exact day of an earthquake?
  **A:** No — they estimate probabilities.

**Assessment:** Earthquake map analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Map analysis rubric: 20 quakes plotted; pattern described (clustered along the San Andreas system and other faults); 4/3/2/1. Quiz: 1) San Andreas is where the Pacific and North American plates slide past each other 2) most California earthquakes cluster along faults 3) landslides are more likely on steep, water-soaked slopes and burn scars 4) Mount Shasta and Lassen Peak are monitored with seismometers, GPS and gas sensors 5) a forecast gives a probability, not a date.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Geography and the Greek city-state

**Prepare:** Gather: relief map of Greece, clay or salt dough. Make salt dough (2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, ~¾ cup water) and print a relief map of Greece.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Greece was one unified country. It was many independent city-states.
- Thinking the Minoans and Mycenaeans were the same.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mountains and seas
*Student:* Make a salt-dough relief map of Greece showing mountains and coastlines.

*How to teach:* Make the relief map.

- **Q:** What percent of Greece is mountainous?
  **A:** About three-quarters (roughly 75–80%).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The polis
*Student:* Explain how mountains led to independent city-states and diagram a polis with acropolis and agora.

*How to teach:* Diagram a polis.

- **Q:** What were the acropolis and agora?
  **A:** Acropolis — the high, fortified hill with temples; agora — the marketplace and meeting place.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Minoans and Mycenaeans
*Student:* Read about Knossos and Mycenae and compare the two earlier Aegean cultures.

*How to teach:* Compare Minoans and Mycenaeans.

- **Q:** How were the Minoans and Mycenaeans different?
  **A:** Minoans (Crete, palace of Knossos) were sea traders; Mycenaeans (mainland) were warriors with fortified citadels.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trade and colonies
*Student:* Map Greek colonies around the Mediterranean and Black Sea and list traded goods (olive oil, wine, pottery).

*How to teach:* Map Greek colonies.

- **Q:** Why did Greeks start colonies?
  **A:** Limited farmland and growing population; to trade.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER-style paragraph: How did geography shape Greek life?

*How to teach:* The child writes the paragraph.

- **Q:** How did mountains shape Greek government?
  **A:** They separated communities, so each developed as an independent city-state.

**Assessment:** Relief map and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Map: mountains, peninsula, islands, Aegean and Ionian Seas. Paragraph rubric — 4: clear topic sentence answering the question, at least two specific accurate pieces of evidence (named source, artifact or fact), explanation linking each to the claim, concluding sentence; 3: accurate but only one piece of evidence explained; 2: general statements without evidence; 1: inaccurate or off-topic. Expected: mountains separated communities → independent poleis; little farmland → trade and colonies; the sea → skilled sailors.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Scales and melodies

**Prepare:** Instrument, Chrome Music Lab (Piano Roll/Song Maker can switch to minor), and 'Ode to Joy' sheet music. Major scale pattern: W W H W W W H.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a half step skips a key. A half step is the very next key (white or black).
- Practicing a whole piece at full speed with mistakes. Isolate trouble spots and slow down.

### Day 1 (Monday): Whole and half steps
*Student:* Find whole and half steps on a keyboard diagram and build the C major scale.

*How to teach:* Find whole and half steps on a keyboard; build C major. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Where are the half steps in C major?
  **A:** Between E–F and B–C.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Major vs. minor
*Student:* Listen to major and minor versions of the same tune in Chrome Music Lab and describe the mood.

*How to teach:* Listen to major and minor versions of a tune; describe moods. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What changes when a major tune becomes minor?
  **A:** The third (and often sixth and seventh) notes are lowered, giving a darker sound.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice with criteria
*Student:* Practice 'Ode to Joy' using criteria (steady tempo, correct notes, good tone) and mark trouble spots.

*How to teach:* Practice 'Ode to Joy' using criteria; mark trouble spots. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Name three performance criteria.
  **A:** Steady tempo, correct notes, good tone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Refine
*Student:* Slow-practice the trouble spots with a metronome and gradually increase tempo.

*How to teach:* Slow-practice trouble spots with a metronome, gradually increasing. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why increase tempo gradually?
  **A:** Accuracy first, then speed, so mistakes aren't practiced.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record a performance of the scale and melody and evaluate it with the criteria.

*How to teach:* Record the scale and melody; evaluate with criteria. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a scale?
  **A:** A series of notes in order going up or down.

**Assessment:** Recorded scale and melody.

**Answer key:** Recorded scale and melody: 4 = correct notes, steady tempo, good tone; 3 = minor errors; 2 = several; 1 = incomplete. Evaluation matches recording.

## Physical Education 6: Volleyball: serving and game play

**Prepare:** Gather: volleyball, net or rope. Mark 6 m and 9 m serving lines. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Tossing too high on the underhand serve. Hold or drop the ball slightly.
- Everyone chasing the ball. Call 'mine' and cover zones.

### Day 1 (Monday): Underhand serve
*Student:* Practice underhand serves over the net from 6 and then 9 meters.

*How to teach:* Underhand serves at 6 then 9 m.

- **Q:** Cues for an underhand serve?
  **A:** Opposite foot forward, hold ball at waist, swing arm back and forward, hit with heel of the hand, follow through to target.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Overhand serve
*Student:* Learn the overhand serve toss and contact.

*How to teach:* Overhand serve toss and contact.

- **Q:** Where should the overhand toss go?
  **A:** Just in front of the hitting shoulder, about an arm's length high.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cooperative rally
*Student:* Play a cooperative rally game aiming for 3 contacts per side.

*How to teach:* Cooperative rally, 3 contacts per side.

- **Q:** How many contacts is a team allowed?
  **A:** Three.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game play
*Student:* Play modified volleyball with rotation and placement strategy.

*How to teach:* Modified game with rotation.

- **Q:** Which way do players rotate?
  **A:** Clockwise, when your team wins the serve.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about where to place the ball to win points and why.

*How to teach:* Write placement reflection.

- **Q:** Where should you aim to win points?
  **A:** Open spaces, deep corners, or between two players.

**Assessment:** Serve accuracy and game observation.

**Answer key:** Serve accuracy: successes out of 10 over the net and in bounds. Game observation: calls the ball, uses three contacts, rotates correctly, places the ball into open space or deep corners. Reflection: placing the ball where defenders are not (open spaces, deep corners, between players) wins points.

## Health 6: Mental health conditions

**Prepare:** Read KidsHealth teen articles on depression and anxiety. Draw a continuum line (thriving – surviving – struggling – in crisis). PARENT PREVIEW: read the materials first; if your child shares feelings that worry you (hopelessness, self-harm, not wanting to live), stay calm, listen, and contact your pediatrician or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking mental illness is a weakness or choice. It's a health condition, like asthma, that can be treated.
- Thinking feeling sad sometimes means depression. Depression is lasting (two weeks or more) and affects daily life.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mental health continuum
*Student:* Place examples on a mental health continuum (thriving to struggling).

*How to teach:* Place examples on the continuum. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Can someone move along the mental health continuum?
  **A:** Yes — mental health changes over time, and support and treatment help people move toward thriving.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Depression and anxiety
*Student:* Read KidsHealth articles and list warning signs of each.

*How to teach:* Read articles; list warning signs of depression and anxiety. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name three warning signs of depression.
  **A:** Lasting sadness or irritability, losing interest in activities, sleep or appetite changes, low energy, trouble concentrating, feeling hopeless.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stigma
*Student:* Discuss myths about mental illness and rewrite them as facts.

*How to teach:* Discuss myths and rewrite them as facts. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Rewrite the myth: 'People with mental illness can just snap out of it.'
  **A:** Fact: Mental illness is a health condition; people need support and often treatment, not willpower alone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Treatment works
*Student:* Learn about counseling and treatment and why getting help early matters.

*How to teach:* Learn about counseling and treatment; why early help matters. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why does getting help early matter?
  **A:** Treatment works better early and prevents problems from getting worse.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a signs-and-symptoms matching check.

*How to teach:* Signs-and-symptoms matching check. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name two signs of an anxiety disorder.
  **A:** Constant worry, racing heart, trouble sleeping, avoiding situations, stomachaches.

**Assessment:** Matching check.

**Answer key:** Matching check: depression — lasting sadness, loss of interest, hopelessness, sleep/appetite change; anxiety — excessive worry, restlessness, racing heart, avoidance; stigma = negative attitudes; treatment = counseling, therapy, sometimes medicine. Proficient = 80%.
