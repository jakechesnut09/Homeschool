# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 8

## English Language Arts 6: Hatchet chapters 14-19 and epilogue

**Prepare:** Gather: Hatchet, library audiobook of Hatchet (optional). Optional audiobook from the library app. Preview: Brian sees the pilot's remains when he returns to the plane (brief).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the epilogue is unimportant. It reveals how long he survived and how the experience changed him.
- Assuming the narrator's voice must match their own mental voice; compare thoughtfully.

### Day 1 (Monday): The moose and the tornado
*Student:* Read chapters 14-15 and explain how these setbacks test the 'new Brian'.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 14–15. Ask how Brian responds to setbacks now compared with earlier.

- **Q:** How does the 'new Brian' respond after the tornado destroys his camp?
  **A:** He doesn't give up; he starts rebuilding and decides to go after the survival pack in the plane.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Back to the plane
*Student:* Read chapters 16-17; list the items in the survival pack and predict how they will change his life.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 16–17; the child lists survival-pack items and predicts.

- **Q:** Why does Brian want to reach the plane?
  **A:** To get the survival pack — it might contain food and supplies to help him last.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rescue
*Student:* Read chapters 18-19 and the epilogue; discuss how the epilogue changes our understanding of the story's ending.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 18–19 and the epilogue; discuss the transmitter.

- **Q:** How is Brian rescued?
  **A:** He unknowingly switches on the emergency transmitter from the survival pack, and a pilot hears the signal and lands on the lake.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Listen and compare
*Student:* Listen to a chapter of the audiobook while following along and note what the narrator's voice adds or changes.

*How to teach:* Listen to one chapter while following along; note the narrator's choices.

- **Q:** What can a narrator's voice add to a book?
  **A:** Mood and pacing through tone, pauses and emphasis; it can make tense scenes more intense or change how a character sounds to us.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the final Hatchet comprehension test with short constructed responses.

*How to teach:* Give the test independently.

- **Q:** How long was Brian in the wilderness?
  **A:** 54 days.

**Assessment:** Hatchet novel test.

**Answer key:** Hatchet test key: 1) a moose attacks him in the lake 2) a tornado wrecks his camp and lifts the plane's tail 3) he retrieves the survival pack from the plane 4) the pack contains an emergency transmitter, which he turns on without realizing it 5) a pilot answering the signal rescues him 6) Brian survived 54 days. Short responses (4 = claim + two text details + explanation): how Brian changes; why the epilogue matters (shows lasting changes, e.g., he is more observant and thinks before acting, and notes that if not rescued he would have faced winter).

## Mathematics 6: Part-part-whole ratios and tape diagrams

**Prepare:** Gather: paper strips, trail-mix ingredients. Substitute seeds or cereal for nuts if there are allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Dividing the total by one of the ratio numbers instead of the total number of parts. For 2:3 with 30 students, there are 5 equal parts, so each part is 6.
- Treating the ratio numbers as the actual amounts. 3:2 for 2 cups doesn't mean 3 cups nuts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tape diagrams
*Student:* Use tape diagrams to solve 'ratio of boys to girls is 2:3 and there are 30 students' style problems (8 problems).

*How to teach:* Model: boys to girls 2:3, 30 students. Draw a tape with 2 boy boxes and 3 girl boxes; 5 boxes = 30, so each box is 6. The child solves the 8 problems with tapes.

- **Q:** Boys to girls is 2:3 and there are 30 students. How many of each?
  **A:** 12 boys and 18 girls.
- **Q:** Red to white paint is 4:1 and the mix is 40 liters. How much red?
  **A:** 32 liters (5 parts of 8 L; red = 4 parts).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trail mix lab
*Student:* Make trail mix in a 3:2 nuts-to-raisins ratio for a total of 2 cups, using a tape diagram to plan amounts before measuring.

*How to teach:* Before measuring, the child draws a 5-part tape for 2 cups total: 3 nuts parts, 2 raisin parts. Then measure and mix.

- **Q:** Trail mix is 3:2 nuts to raisins, 2 cups total. How much of each?
  **A:** Each part = 0.4 cup: 1.2 cups nuts, 0.8 cup raisins.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Choosing representations
*Student:* Solve the same 4 problems with a table, a double number line and a tape diagram, and write which was most efficient and why.

*How to teach:* Solve one problem three ways together, then the child does the other 3 and writes which method was fastest and why.

- **Q:** Which representation is often fastest when you know the total of both parts?
  **A:** The tape diagram, because it shows the total split into equal parts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed ratio review set covering language, equivalence, tables, graphs and tape diagrams.

*How to teach:* Assign the mixed review set; spot-check one problem from each type and have the child explain a tricky one.

- **Q:** There are 4 cats for every 3 dogs at a shelter and 12 more cats than dogs. How many cats?
  **A:** 48 cats (the difference of 1 part = 12; cats are 4 parts = 48, dogs 36).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 end-of-unit assessment and write a reflection on the strategy you rely on most.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test independently; afterward the child writes a 3–4 sentence reflection on their favorite strategy.

- **Q:** Blue:yellow paint is 2:3 and the batch is 15 cups. How much blue?
  **A:** 6 cups (5 parts of 3 cups; blue = 2 parts).

**Assessment:** Unit 2 end-of-unit assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test sample key: 1) 12 apples, 8 oranges: apples to oranges 12:8 = 3:2; oranges to fruit 8:20 = 2:5 2) equivalent to 4:9 — 8:18, 12:27 3) 5 lb for $8 → 15 lb for $24 4) table 3:4 → 6:8, 9:12, 15:20 5) points (3,4), (6,8) are on a line through origin 6) boys:girls 4:5, 36 students → 16 boys, 20 girls 7) blue:yellow 2:3, 15 cups total → 6 blue, 9 yellow 8) Recipe A 2 cups juice : 3 cups water vs B 3 : 5 — A is stronger (0.67 vs 0.6 juice per cup water). Reflection: names a strategy and why.

## Science 6 (Integrated): The water cycle

**Prepare:** Gather: large bowl, plastic wrap, small cup, ice, plastic bag, a houseplant or leafy branch. Use a large bowl and a small heavy cup; set in sun.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking evaporated seawater is salty. Salt stays behind.
- Leaving out gravity. Gravity pulls precipitation down and makes water flow downhill.

### Day 1 (Monday): Water cycle in a bowl
*Student:* Build a mini water cycle (bowl with warm salty water, cup in middle, plastic wrap and ice on top) and observe after an hour.

*How to teach:* Build the bowl model; observe after an hour.

- **Q:** What do you predict will collect in the cup?
  **A:** Fresh water droplets that condensed on the wrap.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transpiration
*Student:* Tie a plastic bag around a leafy branch and record water collected after 24 hours; explain transpiration.

*How to teach:* Bag a leafy branch; check after 24 hours.

- **Q:** What is transpiration?
  **A:** Plants releasing water vapor through their leaves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Energy in the cycle
*Student:* Draw a water cycle model labeling where energy from the sun and the force of gravity drive each process.

*How to teach:* Draw a model with sun and gravity arrows.

- **Q:** What provides energy for evaporation?
  **A:** The sun.

### Day 4 (Thursday): California's water
*Student:* Trace how Sierra Nevada snowmelt reaches California homes using a map; discuss droughts and the snowpack.

*How to teach:* Trace Sierra snowmelt to homes (reservoirs, aqueducts).

- **Q:** Why does California's snowpack matter?
  **A:** Melting snow supplies a large share (roughly a third) of the state's water in spring and summer; a small snowpack means drought risk.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why the water in the bowl's center cup was not salty (CER) and quiz on water cycle processes.

*How to teach:* The child writes the CER and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** Why wasn't the cup water salty?
  **A:** Salt doesn't evaporate; only water vapor rose and condensed.

**Assessment:** Water cycle model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) evaporation 2) condensation 3) precipitation 4) transpiration — water vapor released by plants 5) runoff/collection 6) the sun drives evaporation; gravity drives precipitation and runoff. CER: the cup water isn't salty because only water evaporates; the salt is left behind, and the vapor condenses on the wrap and drips into the cup. Model rubric (4/3/2/1): all processes labeled with energy and gravity arrows.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Empires and Hammurabi's Code

**Prepare:** Gather: Code of Hammurabi excerpts (Avalon Project), image of the Hammurabi stele. Print 20 laws from Hammurabi's Code (Avalon Project) and an image of the stele (Louvre).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'eye for an eye' applied equally to all. Punishments depended on social class.
- Thinking Hammurabi wrote the first laws. Earlier codes existed (e.g., Ur-Nammu); his is the best-known complete one.

### Day 1 (Monday): First empires
*Student:* Read about Sargon of Akkad and Hammurabi of Babylon and map their empires.

*How to teach:* Read about Sargon and Hammurabi; map their empires.

- **Q:** Who created the first empire in Mesopotamia?
  **A:** Sargon of Akkad.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading the Code
*Student:* Read 20 laws from Hammurabi's Code and sort them into categories (property, family, trade, injury).

*How to teach:* Sort 20 laws into categories.

- **Q:** Give a category and an example law.
  **A:** Injury: if a man puts out the eye of a free man, his own eye shall be put out (law 196).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fair or not?
*Student:* Analyze how punishments differed by social class and debate whether the code was 'just' by Babylonian and by modern standards.

*How to teach:* Compare punishments by class; debate.

- **Q:** Was Hammurabi's Code equal for all? Explain.
  **A:** No — punishments were harsher or fines smaller depending on whether the victim was a noble, commoner or enslaved person.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Stele analysis
*Student:* Examine the stele image and explain why carving laws in public mattered.

*How to teach:* Examine the stele image.

- **Q:** Why carve laws in public?
  **A:** So everyone knew the rules; it showed the king's power and that laws came from the gods (Shamash is shown giving authority).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test with a document-based short essay on Hammurabi's Code.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test.

- **Q:** What is an empire?
  **A:** Many lands and peoples ruled by one ruler or government.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test with document-based question.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test sample: Fertile Crescent geography; city-states; ziggurats; cuneiform; Sargon of Akkad created the first empire (~2300s BCE); Hammurabi of Babylon (ruled ~1792–1750 BCE); 282 laws carved on a stele. DBQ short essay — 4: thesis on whether the code was just, uses at least three laws as evidence (e.g., law 196: destroying a free man's eye means losing your own eye; law 198: a commoner's eye — a fine of one mina of silver; law 199: a slave's eye — half the slave's value), considers Babylonian vs. modern standards; 3: two documents; 2: one document; 1: no evidence.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Art of India and China

**Prepare:** Gather a compass, protractor, colored markers, black ink or watercolor, a round brush, and long paper for a scroll. Find images of a Gandharan Buddha (Pakistan/Afghanistan region) and a Chinese Buddha (e.g., Longmen Grottoes) and a Chinese ink landscape (e.g., Fan Kuan's 'Travelers among Mountains and Streams').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking radial symmetry is the same as mirror symmetry. Radial symmetry repeats around a center point like spokes.
- Filling all space in Chinese landscapes. Empty space suggests mist, distance and calm.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mandalas
*Student:* Study mandala designs and create a radially symmetric mandala using a compass and protractor.

*How to teach:* Draw concentric circles with a compass and divide them with a protractor (e.g., every 30°); create a radially symmetric mandala.

- **Q:** What is radial symmetry?
  **A:** Design elements repeat evenly around a center point.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Buddhist sculpture
*Student:* Analyze images of Gandharan and Chinese Buddha sculptures and note how style changed as Buddhism spread.

*How to teach:* Compare Gandharan and Chinese Buddhas. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How do Gandharan Buddhas show Greek and Roman influence?
  **A:** Wavy hair, realistic faces and toga-like robes with deep folds, like Greco-Roman sculpture.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Brush strokes
*Student:* Practice basic Chinese brush strokes and paint bamboo stalks and leaves.

*How to teach:* Practice Chinese brush strokes; paint bamboo stalks and leaves. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** How should you hold the brush for Chinese painting?
  **A:** Upright, moving from the arm, pressing and lifting to vary stroke width.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ink landscape
*Student:* Paint a vertical scroll landscape with mountains and mist using ink wash values.

*How to teach:* Paint a vertical scroll landscape with ink washes: dark near, pale far, empty space for mist.

- **Q:** How do you show distance with ink wash?
  **A:** Paint far mountains with pale, watery ink and less detail; near ones darker.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Compare your mandala and scroll and write how each reflects its culture's values.

*How to teach:* Compare mandala and scroll and how each reflects cultural values. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What does a mandala often represent?
  **A:** The universe or a spiritual journey toward the center in Hindu and Buddhist traditions.

**Assessment:** Mandala and ink landscape.

**Answer key:** Mandala: accurate radial symmetry with compass/protractor. Ink landscape: at least three values, mist/empty space, vertical format. Comparison: connects each to culture's values (spiritual wholeness; harmony with nature). Proficient = all.

## Physical Education 6: Soccer: shooting and team play

**Prepare:** Gather: soccer ball, goal or cones. Use a goal or two cones 3 m apart. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Leaning back on shots, sending the ball high. Keep head and knee over the ball.
- Diving in on defense. Stay goal-side and jockey.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shooting
*Student:* Practice instep shots at a target from 10 meters, aiming for the corners.

*How to teach:* Shoot instep at corners from 10 m.

- **Q:** What part of the foot is used for a power shot?
  **A:** The instep (laces).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Offensive spacing
*Student:* Learn to create passing triangles and move to open space in a 3v1 drill.

*How to teach:* 3v1 passing triangles.

- **Q:** Why form triangles on offense?
  **A:** They create two passing options for the player with the ball.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Defense
*Student:* Practice defensive stance, jockeying and staying goal-side.

*How to teach:* Defensive stance and jockeying.

- **Q:** What does 'goal-side' mean?
  **A:** Staying between your opponent and your own goal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play small-sided soccer, rotating positions and practicing fair play.

*How to teach:* Small-sided game, rotating positions.

- **Q:** What is fair play?
  **A:** Following rules, respecting opponents and officials, helping someone who falls.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about one offensive and one defensive strategy you used.

*How to teach:* Write the reflection.

- **Q:** Name an offensive strategy.
  **A:** Spreading out, passing triangles, moving to open space.

**Assessment:** Game-play observation checklist.

**Answer key:** Game-play observation checklist (yes/no each): moves to open space; passes to open teammates; shoots with instep; stays goal-side on defense; jockeys rather than lunging; plays fair and encourages. Reflection names one offensive and one defensive strategy with an example.

## Health 6: Healthy friendships

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards for friendship behaviors and boundary role-plays (a friend copies your homework, shares your secret, pressures you to skip practice). Draw a spectrum chart from healthy to unhealthy.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a 'real friend' never disagrees. Healthy friends can disagree respectfully.
- Thinking setting boundaries is mean. Boundaries protect both people and keep friendships healthy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Qualities
*Student:* Rank 12 friendship qualities and explain your top three.

*How to teach:* Your child ranks 12 friendship qualities (trust, loyalty, humor, honesty, kindness...) and explains the top three.

- **Q:** Why is trust important in a friendship?
  **A:** It lets you share feelings and rely on each other without fear of being hurt or betrayed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Healthy vs. unhealthy
*Student:* Sort friendship behaviors (support, gossip, exclusion, honesty, control) into a spectrum chart.

*How to teach:* Sort behaviors (support, gossip, exclusion, honesty, control, jealousy, encouragement) on the spectrum chart.

- **Q:** Where does 'telling you who you can't hang out with' belong?
  **A:** Unhealthy — it's controlling behavior.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Boundaries
*Student:* Practice setting a boundary with a friend in 3 role-plays.

*How to teach:* Role-play setting a boundary in 3 scenarios with a calm, clear statement. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Give an example of setting a boundary.
  **A:** 'I'm not comfortable sharing my homework. I can help you understand the problem instead.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Making friends
*Student:* Plan ways to make new friends through clubs, sports or co-ops.

*How to teach:* Plan ways to make new friends (clubs, sports, co-ops, volunteering) and conversation starters.

- **Q:** Name two ways to meet new friends.
  **A:** Join a club or team, volunteer, attend co-op classes, invite someone to do an activity.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a letter describing what kind of friend you want to be; complete a check.

*How to teach:* Write a letter about the friend you want to be; complete the check. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is manipulation in a friendship?
  **A:** Trying to control someone through guilt, pressure, lies or threats.

**Assessment:** Friendship letter and check.

**Answer key:** Check key: healthy friendship qualities (trust, respect, honesty, support); unhealthy signs (control, gossip, exclusion, manipulation); boundary definition. Letter rubric: 4 = names 3+ qualities with examples of actions; 3 = qualities with some examples; 2 = list only; 1 = missing.
