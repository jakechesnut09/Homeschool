# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 17

## English Language Arts 6: Speaking: debate

**Prepare:** Gather: essay, timer, a recorded student speech or TED-Ed talk. Choose a short persuasive speech (a TED-Ed or student speech; preview it). Arrange a debate partner.

**Watch for:**
- Reading the speech word-for-word. Use note cards with key words only.
- Ignoring the opponent's points in rebuttal. Listen and respond to specific claims.

### Day 1 (Monday): Evaluate a speech
*Student:* Watch a short persuasive speech and note the speaker's claims and whether each is supported.

*How to teach:* Watch the speech; the child notes claims and support.

- **Q:** How can you tell if a speaker's claim is supported?
  **A:** They give evidence such as facts, data, examples or expert statements that relate directly to the claim.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prepare a speech
*Student:* Turn your essay into a 2-minute opening statement with note cards.

*How to teach:* The child turns the essay into a 2-minute statement on cards.

- **Q:** What goes on a note card?
  **A:** Key words and phrases, not full sentences.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice delivery (eye contact, volume, pace) while recording yourself and critique the video.

*How to teach:* Record practice; the child critiques eye contact, volume and pace.

- **Q:** Name two delivery skills to check on the video.
  **A:** Eye contact, volume, pace, gestures, clear pronunciation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Debate
*Student:* Hold a structured debate with a family member or co-op partner: opening statements, rebuttals and closings.

*How to teach:* Hold the debate: openings (2 min), rebuttals (1 min), closings (1 min).

- **Q:** What makes a good rebuttal?
  **A:** It restates the opponent's point fairly and answers it with reasoning or evidence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a reflection on the debate and take the unit test on argument terms and analysis.

*How to teach:* The child reflects and takes the unit test.

- **Q:** Define counterclaim.
  **A:** An opposing position to your claim.

**Assessment:** Debate performance rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Debate rubric (4/3/2/1): claim clarity; evidence; rebuttal responds directly to opponent's points; delivery (eye contact, volume, pace); respectful conduct. Unit test sample: claim = position; reason; evidence; counterclaim; rebuttal; relevant vs. sufficient evidence; loaded words; formal style.

## Mathematics 6: Decimals: adding, subtracting and multiplying

**Prepare:** Gather: base-ten blocks or grid paper, receipts. Write a 4–5 item shopping list with prices for Day 1.

**Watch for:**
- Lining up the last digits instead of the decimal points when adding. Write zeros as placeholders (4.5 → 4.50).
- Placing the decimal in a product by lining it up. Count total decimal places in the factors (0.3 × 0.4 has 2 → 0.12).

### Day 1 (Monday): Decimals in money
*Student:* Add and subtract prices from a shopping list, lining up place values, and find change from $50.

*How to teach:* Write a shopping list with prices; stack decimals vertically on graph paper, then find change from $50.

- **Q:** Items cost $12.49, $3.75 and $8.06. Total and change from $50?
  **A:** Total $24.30; change $25.70.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adding and subtracting decimals
*Student:* Solve 20 decimal sums and differences with up to thousandths, including regrouping across zeros.

*How to teach:* Model 10.05 − 3.7 by writing 3.70 and regrouping. The child solves 20 problems.

- **Q:** Compute 6 − 2.348.
  **A:** 3.652 (write 6.000).
- **Q:** Compute 10.05 − 3.7.
  **A:** 6.35.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Multiplying decimals
*Student:* Use area diagrams and fractions (0.3 x 0.4 = 3/10 x 4/10) to explain decimal placement, then compute 12 products.

*How to teach:* Shade a 10 × 10 grid: 3 tenths one way, 4 tenths the other; overlap = 12 hundredths. Then teach counting decimal places.

- **Q:** Compute 0.3 × 0.4 and explain the decimal placement.
  **A:** 0.12 — 3/10 × 4/10 = 12/100.
- **Q:** Compute 0.07 × 0.5.
  **A:** 0.035.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decimal applications
*Student:* Calculate total cost of buying fabric at $4.75 per yard for 2.5 yards and 5 similar problems.

*How to teach:* Work the fabric problem; the child solves 5 similar ones and rounds money to the nearest cent.

- **Q:** Fabric is $4.75 per yard. Cost of 2.5 yards?
  **A:** $11.875 → $11.88.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on decimal addition, subtraction and multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the decimal quiz.

- **Q:** Compute 2.5 × 1.6.
  **A:** 4 (4.00).

**Assessment:** Decimal operations quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 3.6 + 4.85 = 8.45 2) 12.4 − 5.67 = 6.73 3) 7 − 0.125 = 6.875 4) 0.6 × 0.8 = 0.48 5) 3.2 × 1.5 = 4.8 6) 0.04 × 0.3 = 0.012 7) 2.25 × 4 = 9 8) 1.75 × 12.6 = 22.05.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Mitigating hazards

**Prepare:** Gather: sugar cubes or blocks, cardboard, rubber bands, pan of gelatin or tray. Build a shake table from cardboard and rubber bands; have sugar cubes.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stronger always means stiffer. Flexible designs and base isolation can absorb shaking.
- Thinking early warning predicts quakes. It detects one that has begun and warns before strong shaking arrives.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shake table
*Student:* Build a simple shake table from cardboard and rubber bands and test a sugar-cube tower.

*How to teach:* Build and test a tower on the shake table.

- **Q:** Where do towers usually fail?
  **A:** At weak joints or the base.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Earthquake-resistant design
*Student:* Redesign the tower with cross-bracing or base isolation and test again, recording results.

*How to teach:* Add cross-bracing or base isolation; retest.

- **Q:** How does cross-bracing help?
  **A:** Diagonal supports form triangles that resist sideways swaying.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Early warning
*Student:* Learn how California's MyShake/ShakeAlert early warning system uses data and write how seconds of warning help.

*How to teach:* Learn ShakeAlert/MyShake.

- **Q:** How can a few seconds' warning help?
  **A:** People can Drop, Cover and Hold On; trains slow; surgeons pause; gas lines shut.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family hazard plan
*Student:* Create a family emergency plan and kit list for an earthquake or wildfire.

*How to teach:* Create a family plan and kit list.

- **Q:** What should an emergency kit include?
  **A:** Water (1 gallon per person per day), food, flashlight, first aid, medications, radio, batteries.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present your shake-table results and family plan; take the Unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Present; take the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** What do you do when shaking starts?
  **A:** Drop, Cover and Hold On.

**Assessment:** Shake-table design report and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Shake-table report rubric: initial and redesigned tower results, explanation (cross-bracing resists swaying; base isolation reduces shaking transferred). Unit 5 test sample: hazard patterns; ShakeAlert detects fast P-waves and sends warnings before slower, stronger S-waves arrive; Drop, Cover and Hold On; family plan elements (meeting place, contacts, kit with water — 1 gallon per person per day — food, flashlight, first aid, medications).

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: From tyranny to democracy

**Prepare:** Gather: pottery shards or paper ostraka, excerpt of Pericles' Funeral Oration (Thucydides). Cut paper ostraka; print an excerpt of Pericles' Funeral Oration.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Athenians could vote. Only free adult male citizens; women, enslaved people and foreigners couldn't.
- Thinking the U.S. is a direct democracy. It's a representative democracy (republic).

### Day 1 (Monday): Forms of government
*Student:* Create a chart defining monarchy, aristocracy, oligarchy, tyranny and democracy with Greek examples.

*How to teach:* Chart forms of government.

- **Q:** What is an oligarchy? Give a Greek example.
  **A:** Rule by a few powerful people; Sparta.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Athenian democracy
*Student:* Read about Solon and Cleisthenes and explain who could and could not participate as citizens.

*How to teach:* Read about Solon and Cleisthenes.

- **Q:** Who could participate in Athenian democracy?
  **A:** Free adult men born to Athenian citizens; not women, enslaved people or foreigners.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ostracism
*Student:* Hold a mock ostracism vote with family using paper 'ostraka' and discuss its purpose.

*How to teach:* Hold the mock ostracism.

- **Q:** What was ostracism?
  **A:** A vote to exile a citizen thought dangerous to democracy for ten years.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pericles' Funeral Oration
*Student:* Read an excerpt and identify three values Pericles praised about Athenian democracy.

*How to teach:* Read the Funeral Oration excerpt.

- **Q:** Name a value Pericles praised.
  **A:** Equality before the law, rule by the many, advancement by merit, citizen participation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a comparison of Athenian direct democracy and U.S. representative democracy; quiz on government forms.

*How to teach:* The child writes the comparison; quiz.

- **Q:** What is direct democracy?
  **A:** Citizens vote on laws themselves.

**Assessment:** Comparison essay and quiz.

**Answer key:** Comparison essay — 4: explains Athenian direct democracy (citizens voted on laws in the Assembly) vs. U.S. representative democracy (elected representatives), who participated in each, with examples; 3: accurate but thin; 2: one side; 1: inaccurate. Quiz: monarchy (one ruler, inherited), aristocracy (nobles), oligarchy (a few rich/powerful — Sparta), tyranny (one ruler who seized power), democracy (rule by citizens — Athens).

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Dynamics, tempo and expression

**Prepare:** Find Ravel's 'Bolero' and five excerpts at different tempos. Terms: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff; crescendo; largo, adagio, andante, moderato, allegro, presto; legato (smooth), staccato (short).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'piano' means the instrument. In dynamics, piano means soft.
- Thinking a crescendo means faster. Crescendo means gradually louder.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dynamics
*Student:* Listen to Ravel's 'Bolero' and chart how dynamics grow; learn p, mp, mf, f symbols.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Bolero' and chart its dynamics; learn p, mp, mf, f. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What happens to dynamics in Ravel's 'Bolero'?
  **A:** It grows in one long crescendo from very soft to very loud while the tempo stays steady.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tempo
*Student:* Learn Italian tempo terms and match them to five recorded excerpts.

*How to teach:* Learn tempo terms; match to five excerpts. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Put in order slow to fast: allegro, adagio, presto, andante.
  **A:** Adagio, andante, allegro, presto.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Articulation
*Student:* Play a melody legato and staccato and explain how it changes character.

*How to teach:* Play a melody legato and staccato. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does staccato mean?
  **A:** Short, detached notes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Expressive performance
*Student:* Add dynamics and articulation to your melody and practice following markings.

*How to teach:* Add dynamics and articulation to your melody and practice. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What does mf mean?
  **A:** Mezzo forte — medium loud.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on musical terms and perform your melody with expression.

*How to teach:* Terms quiz; perform with expression. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What does legato mean?
  **A:** Smooth and connected.

**Assessment:** Terms quiz and expressive performance.

**Answer key:** Terms quiz: dynamics (pp–ff), crescendo/decrescendo, tempo terms in order, legato/staccato. Proficient = 80%. Performance: follows marked dynamics and articulation.

## Physical Education 6: Badminton

**Prepare:** Gather: badminton rackets and shuttlecocks, net or rope. Badminton set and rope net. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Gripping like a frying pan. Use a handshake grip.
- Hitting every shot hard. Mix clears and drops.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grip and serve
*Student:* Learn the handshake grip and practice short and long serves.

*How to teach:* Handshake grip; short and long serves.

- **Q:** Where must the shuttle be hit on a serve?
  **A:** Below the waist, underhand.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forehand and backhand
*Student:* Rally with forehand and backhand strokes with a partner.

*How to teach:* Forehand and backhand rallies.

- **Q:** How does a backhand grip change?
  **A:** The thumb presses flat on the back of the handle.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Clear and drop
*Student:* Practice clears to the back and drop shots near the net.

*How to teach:* Clears and drops.

- **Q:** Why use a clear?
  **A:** To push the opponent to the back of the court and gain time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Singles game
*Student:* Play singles badminton games using placement strategy.

*How to teach:* Singles with placement.

- **Q:** What placement strategy wins points?
  **A:** Alternate deep clears and short drops to move your opponent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Count longest rally and number of successful clears out of 10.

*How to teach:* Rally and clear check.

- **Q:** What is a drop shot?
  **A:** A soft shot that falls just over the net.

**Assessment:** Rally and clear check.

**Answer key:** Check: longest rally count and clears out of 10 that land in the back third. Cues: handshake grip; serve underhand, contact below the waist; clear = high and deep; drop = soft, just over the net.

## Health 6: Getting help and supporting others

**Prepare:** Make a resource card template. Know local resources: pediatrician, school or co-op counselor, county mental health line; 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988); Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741); 911 for immediate danger. PARENT PREVIEW: read the materials first; if your child shares feelings that worry you (hopelessness, self-harm, not wanting to live), stay calm, listen, and contact your pediatrician or the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988).

**Watch for:**
- Promising to keep a friend's dangerous secret. If someone might hurt themselves or others, you must tell a trusted adult.
- Thinking asking about suicide puts the idea in someone's head. Asking directly and caring is safe and helps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Resources
*Student:* List school, community and crisis resources including the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

*How to teach:* List school, community and crisis resources including 988. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What is 988?
  **A:** The Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call or text 988 any time for free, confidential support.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Helping a friend
*Student:* Learn the steps: notice, ask, listen, tell a trusted adult, and practice with a scenario.

*How to teach:* Learn: notice, ask, listen, tell a trusted adult. Practice with a scenario. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** A friend says 'Nothing matters anymore.' What do you do?
  **A:** Take it seriously, ask how they're feeling, listen without judging, and tell a trusted adult right away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): When to tell
*Student:* Discuss why some secrets must be shared with an adult to keep someone safe.

*How to teach:* Discuss why some secrets must be shared. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** When should you break a promise to keep a secret?
  **A:** When someone could be hurt—if they talk about hurting themselves or others, or are being hurt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Self-care plan
*Student:* Write a personal self-care plan for hard days.

*How to teach:* Write a personal self-care plan for hard days. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What belongs in a self-care plan?
  **A:** Coping strategies, people to talk to, healthy activities, and crisis numbers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a resource card and take the Mental Health unit quiz.

*How to teach:* Create a resource card and take the Mental Health unit quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Who can be a trusted adult?
  **A:** A parent, relative, counselor, teacher, coach, doctor or faith leader.

**Assessment:** Resource card and quiz.

**Answer key:** Resource card: 988, 911, a trusted adult's number, a counselor/doctor. Unit quiz key: continuum, warning signs, stigma, treatment works, notice-ask-listen-tell, safety secrets must be shared, 988. Proficient = 80%.
