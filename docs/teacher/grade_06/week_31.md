# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 31

## English Language Arts 6: The Secret Garden, chapters 16-27

**Prepare:** Gather: The Secret Garden. Keep the novel; note chapter titles to help the child track events.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'Magic' is supernatural spells. The children use it for the power of growth, belief and positive thinking.
- Ignoring Mr. Craven's change. His healing completes the theme.

### Day 1 (Monday): Colin's tantrum
*Student:* Read chapters 16-19 and explain how Mary's response to Colin shows her growth.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 16–19; analyze Mary's response to the tantrum.

- **Q:** How does Mary's handling of Colin's tantrum show growth?
  **A:** She's still fierce, but she uses it to help Colin — checking his back and showing him he isn't becoming a hunchback; she cares about someone else.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Into the garden
*Student:* Read chapters 20-22 and identify the garden as a symbol of healing.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 20–22; discuss the garden as a symbol.

- **Q:** What does the garden symbolize?
  **A:** Healing and new life — as it comes back to life, so do the children (and later Mr. Craven).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Magic
*Student:* Read chapters 23-25 and discuss what the children mean by 'Magic'.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 23–25; discuss 'Magic'.

- **Q:** What do the children mean by 'Magic'?
  **A:** The power of nature, growth and positive thinking that makes things (and people) grow strong.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Homecoming
*Student:* Read chapters 26-27 and compare the ending with the opening chapters.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 26–27; compare ending and opening.

- **Q:** How is the ending different from the opening?
  **A:** The book opens with death, neglect and a sour, lonely girl; it ends with healthy children, a reunited father and son, and a living garden.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write two theme statements for the novel and take the final novel test.

*How to teach:* The child writes theme statements and takes the test.

- **Q:** Who returns to the manor at the end?
  **A:** Archibald Craven, Colin's father.

**Assessment:** Novel test and theme statements.

**Answer key:** Novel test: 1) Mary stops Colin's tantrum by scolding him and checking his back — there's no lump 2) Colin goes to the garden in his wheelchair 3) Ben Weatherstaff sees them; Colin stands up 4) 'Magic' = the healing force of nature and positive thinking 5) Mr. Craven dreams of his wife calling him to the garden and returns 6) Colin runs out of the garden into his father's arms. Theme statements (4 = two full-sentence themes with evidence, e.g., 'Nature and friendship can heal both body and spirit'; 'Thoughts and attitudes shape health').

## Mathematics 6: Histograms

**Prepare:** Gather: stopwatch, graph paper. Practice the ruler-drop test: one person holds a 30 cm ruler at 0; the child catches it and you read the cm mark.

**Watch for:**
- Unequal bin widths or gaps between bars. Bins must be equal width, and bars touch.
- Reading a histogram bar as one data value. Each bar shows how many values fall in a range.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reaction time data
*Student:* Measure reaction time 25 times with the ruler-drop test and record the catch distance in centimeters.

*How to teach:* Do 25 ruler drops and record catch distances in cm (smaller = faster reaction).

- **Q:** If the catch distance is smaller, is the reaction faster or slower?
  **A:** Faster — the ruler fell a shorter distance before being caught.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Making histograms
*Student:* Group your reaction data into equal bins and draw a histogram with labeled axes.

*How to teach:* Choose a bin width (e.g., 3 cm), tally the data in bins and draw touching bars.

- **Q:** Why do histogram bars touch?
  **A:** The bins are continuous ranges of numbers with no gaps between them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dot plot or histogram?
*Student:* Compare a dot plot and histogram of the same data and write when each display is more useful.

*How to teach:* Draw a dot plot and histogram of the same data; discuss what each shows.

- **Q:** When is a histogram better than a dot plot?
  **A:** With many data values or a wide range, where grouping shows the shape more clearly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpreting histograms
*Student:* Answer questions about histograms of California city temperatures and dog weights, describing shape and spread.

*How to teach:* Use the dog-weight histogram in the key (and a temperature one). The child answers questions and describes shape.

- **Q:** Using the dog histogram (4, 7, 5, 2, 1 dogs in 20-lb bins from 0), how many dogs weigh 40 lb or more?
  **A:** 8 (5 + 2 + 1).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on building and reading histograms.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the reaction-time display.

- **Q:** Can you find the exact heaviest dog's weight from the histogram?
  **A:** No — only that it is between 80 and 100 lb.

**Assessment:** Histogram quiz and reaction-time display.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (dog-weight histogram, bins in lb: 0–20: 4, 20–40: 7, 40–60: 5, 60–80: 2, 80–100: 1): 1) total dogs = 19 2) most common bin 20–40 lb 3) dogs 40 lb or more = 8 4) shape skewed right 5) you cannot tell the exact heaviest weight, only that it is 80–100 lb. Display rubric: 4 = equal bins, labeled axes and title, accurate heights; 3 = one labeling error; 2 = unequal bins; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Digestive and excretory systems

**Prepare:** Gather: zip bag, crackers, water, vinegar, old pantyhose or sieve, string 9 m long. Cut a 9 m string; have a zip bag, crackers and an old stocking.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking digestion happens only in the stomach. Most nutrient absorption happens in the small intestine.
- Thinking kidneys are part of digestion. They filter the blood (excretory system).

### Day 1 (Monday): Digestion in a bag
*Student:* Model chemical and mechanical digestion by mashing crackers with water and vinegar in a bag, then squeezing through a 'small intestine' stocking.

*How to teach:* Mash crackers with water and vinegar in a bag; squeeze through the stocking.

- **Q:** What does the vinegar represent, and the mashing?
  **A:** Vinegar = stomach acid (chemical digestion); mashing = churning/chewing (mechanical).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Length of the gut
*Student:* Measure out 9 meters of string to represent the digestive tract and label each organ's section.

*How to teach:* Lay out 9 m of string and label sections.

- **Q:** Which section is longest?
  **A:** The small intestine (about 6–7 m).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Saliva enzyme
*Student:* Chew a plain cracker for 1 minute and note the change in taste; explain how enzymes break starch into sugar.

*How to teach:* Chew a cracker for a minute.

- **Q:** Why does the cracker start tasting sweet?
  **A:** Saliva enzymes break starch into sugar.

### Day 4 (Thursday): System connections
*Student:* Map how nutrients travel from the small intestine through blood to cells and how kidneys remove waste.

*How to teach:* Map nutrients from intestine to cells and wastes to kidneys.

- **Q:** How do nutrients reach your cells?
  **A:** They are absorbed through the small intestine walls into the blood, which carries them to cells.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the digestive and excretory systems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What does the large intestine absorb?
  **A:** Water.

**Assessment:** Digestion model write-up and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) mechanical digestion: chewing, stomach churning; chemical: enzymes and acid 2) path: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine 3) most nutrients absorbed in the small intestine 4) large intestine absorbs water 5) saliva enzyme (amylase) breaks starch into sugar 6) kidneys filter wastes from blood to make urine. Model write-up: matches each step to a body part.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The Roman Republic

**Prepare:** Gather: Twelve Tables excerpts (Avalon or Fordham), U.S. Constitution summary. Print Twelve Tables excerpts and a U.S. Constitution summary.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Roman Republic was a democracy for all. Wealthy patricians dominated, especially early.
- Thinking the U.S. copied Rome exactly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Patricians and plebeians
*Student:* Read about the Conflict of the Orders and explain how plebeians gained rights.

*How to teach:* Read about the Conflict of the Orders.

- **Q:** How did plebeians gain rights?
  **A:** By refusing to work or fight (leaving the city), they won tribunes and written laws.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Three branches
*Student:* Diagram consuls, Senate and assemblies and show checks and balances among them.

*How to teach:* Diagram the government.

- **Q:** How did two consuls check each other?
  **A:** Each could veto the other's decisions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Twelve Tables
*Student:* Read selected laws of the Twelve Tables and explain why written law mattered (compare with Hammurabi).

*How to teach:* Read Twelve Tables laws.

- **Q:** Why did written laws matter?
  **A:** Everyone could know the law, protecting plebeians from unfair judges.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rome and the U.S.
*Student:* Compare the Roman Republic with the U.S. government in a chart (veto, Senate, written law).

*How to teach:* Chart Rome vs. the U.S.

- **Q:** Name two U.S. features from Rome.
  **A:** The Senate, the veto, checks and balances, written laws.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an essay paragraph on the Roman Republic's influence on American government; quiz on vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Write the paragraph; quiz.

- **Q:** What does 'veto' mean in Latin?
  **A:** 'I forbid.'

**Assessment:** Comparison chart and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Chart: consuls (two, one-year terms, could veto each other) ↔ president; Senate ↔ U.S. Senate; assemblies/tribunes ↔ House; written law (Twelve Tables) ↔ Constitution; veto in both. Paragraph rubric — 4: clear topic sentence answering the question, at least two specific accurate pieces of evidence (named source, artifact or fact), explanation linking each to the claim, concluding sentence; 3: accurate but only one piece of evidence explained; 2: general statements without evidence; 1: inaccurate or off-topic. Vocabulary quiz: republic, patrician, plebeian, consul, Senate, tribune, veto ('I forbid'), Twelve Tables.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Elements of dance and cultural dance

**Prepare:** Clear an open space; find video tutorials for Kalamatianos (Greek line dance in 7/8, counted slow-quick-quick), ballet folklórico zapateado, and a Bharatanatyam excerpt with mudras.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking folk dances are just steps. They express community history, celebrations and identity.
- Stomping hard for zapateado. Zapateado is controlled, rhythmic footwork with heel and toe strikes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Elements of dance
*Student:* Explore levels, pathways and energy by moving across the room in 10 different ways.

*How to teach:* Explore levels (low, middle, high), pathways (straight, curved, zigzag) and energy (sharp, smooth) moving across the room 10 ways.

- **Q:** Name the three levels in dance.
  **A:** Low, middle and high.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Greek folk dance
*Student:* Learn a basic Greek line dance (e.g., Kalamatianos steps) from a video.

*How to teach:* Learn basic Kalamatianos steps from a video. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What meter is the Kalamatianos danced in?
  **A:** 7/8 (slow-quick-quick).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mexican folk dance
*Student:* Learn the basic zapateado step from ballet folklorico and discuss its regional origin.

*How to teach:* Learn the basic zapateado; discuss regional origin. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is ballet folklórico?
  **A:** Mexican folk dance that shows the regional dances and costumes of Mexico.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Indian dance
*Student:* Watch a Bharatanatyam excerpt and try three hand gestures (mudras) that tell a story.

*How to teach:* Watch Bharatanatyam; try three mudras. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What are mudras?
  **A:** Hand gestures that communicate meaning or tell a story.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Write how one dance communicates its community's history or purpose.

*How to teach:* Write how one dance communicates its community's history or purpose. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Where does Bharatanatyam come from?
  **A:** Tamil Nadu in South India.

**Assessment:** Movement participation and written interpretation.

**Answer key:** Participation: tries all dances with effort and attention to steps. Written interpretation rubric: 4 = explains purpose and history of a dance with specific details; 3 = general explanation; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## Physical Education 6: FITT principle

**Prepare:** Gather: fitness log, stopwatch. Fitness log and stopwatch. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking more is always better. Overload gradually, with rest.
- Confusing type and intensity.

### Day 1 (Monday): FITT
*Student:* Learn the FITT principle and identify FITT in three sample workouts.

*How to teach:* Teach FITT; identify in samples.

- **Q:** What does FITT stand for?
  **A:** Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cardio workout
*Student:* Complete a cardio workout and write its FITT.

*How to teach:* Cardio workout; write FITT.

- **Q:** Write the FITT for a 20-minute brisk bike ride, 3 times a week.
  **A:** F 3/week; I moderate (brisk); T 20 minutes; T biking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Strength workout
*Student:* Complete a body-weight strength circuit (squats, lunges, planks, push-ups) and write its FITT.

*How to teach:* Strength circuit; write FITT.

- **Q:** Give a plank cue.
  **A:** Straight line from head to heels, tight core, elbows under shoulders.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flexibility workout
*Student:* Complete a yoga-based flexibility session and write its FITT.

*How to teach:* Yoga flexibility session; FITT.

- **Q:** How long should stretches be held?
  **A:** About 15–30 seconds, without bouncing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on FITT and the principle of overload.

*How to teach:* Quiz on FITT and overload.

- **Q:** What is the overload principle?
  **A:** To improve, gradually make the body work harder than usual.

**Assessment:** FITT quiz.

**Answer key:** FITT quiz: F = frequency (how often), I = intensity (how hard), T = time (how long), T = type (what activity); overload = gradually doing more than usual to improve; progression = increasing over time; e.g., 'jog 3 days a week, moderate pace, 20 minutes' → F 3/week, I moderate, T 20 min, T jogging.

## Health 6: Noncommunicable diseases and prevention

**Prepare:** Print a simple family health history form (CDC 'My Family Health Portrait' has one). Find kid-friendly sources on asthma, diabetes, heart disease and cancer (KidsHealth, CDC). PARENT PREVIEW: decide what family health information you're comfortable sharing.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking chronic diseases only affect old people. Asthma and type 1 diabetes often start in childhood, and habits now affect later risk.
- Thinking you can 'catch' diabetes or asthma. Noncommunicable diseases aren't spread from person to person.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chronic diseases
*Student:* Research one chronic disease (asthma, diabetes, heart disease, cancer) and make a fact card.

*How to teach:* Your child researches one chronic disease and makes a fact card (what it is, symptoms, risk factors, prevention/treatment).

- **Q:** What makes a disease 'chronic' and 'noncommunicable'?
  **A:** It lasts a long time (or for life) and can't be spread from person to person.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Asthma
*Student:* Learn asthma triggers and how an inhaler helps; discuss how to help a friend having an attack.

*How to teach:* Learn asthma triggers (pollen, dust, smoke, pets, cold air, exercise) and how a quick-relief inhaler opens airways; discuss helping a friend (help them sit up, use their inhaler, get an adult, call 911 if severe).

- **Q:** What does a quick-relief (rescue) inhaler do?
  **A:** It relaxes the muscles around the airways so they open and breathing is easier.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Risk factors
*Student:* Sort controllable and uncontrollable risk factors for chronic disease.

*How to teach:* Sort controllable (diet, activity, smoking, sleep) and uncontrollable (age, genetics, family history) risk factors.

- **Q:** Is family history controllable or uncontrollable?
  **A:** Uncontrollable — but knowing it helps you focus on controllable habits.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family history
*Student:* Complete a simple family health history with a parent.

*How to teach:* Complete a simple family health history with a parent. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why is a family health history useful?
  **A:** It shows which diseases run in the family so doctors can check for them and you can lower your risk.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on habits that lower chronic disease risk; complete a check.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph on habits that lower chronic disease risk; complete the check. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Name three habits that lower risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes.
  **A:** Regular physical activity, eating plenty of fruits/vegetables/whole grains, limiting sugary drinks, not smoking or vaping, getting enough sleep.

**Assessment:** Paragraph and check.

**Answer key:** Check key: chronic vs. communicable; type 1 diabetes = body makes little/no insulin; type 2 = body doesn't use insulin well; asthma triggers and inhaler; controllable vs uncontrollable risk factors. Paragraph rubric: 4 = three or more specific habits linked to specific diseases; 3 = habits without links; 2 = one habit; 1 = missing.
