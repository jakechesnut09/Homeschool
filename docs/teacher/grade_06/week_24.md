# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 24

## English Language Arts 6: Memoir vs. biography vs. drama

**Prepare:** Gather: a children's biography of Helen Keller (library), film clip of the water-pump scene from 'The Miracle Worker' (1962). Borrow a children's biography of Helen Keller; find the water-pump scene from 'The Miracle Worker' (1962; preview, it's intense but appropriate).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all versions are equally accurate. A memoir is a first-hand account; a biography is second-hand; a film dramatizes.
- Listing differences without explaining why authors made those choices.

### Day 1 (Monday): Biography account
*Student:* Read a biography's account of the water-pump scene and annotate differences from Keller's own account.

*How to teach:* Read the biography's account; annotate differences from Keller's.

- **Q:** What can a biography include that a memoir might not?
  **A:** Outside perspectives, historical context, facts the subject didn't know or chose to leave out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare authors
*Student:* Complete a comparison chart: what each author includes, omits and emphasizes, and why.

*How to teach:* Complete a chart: included, omitted, emphasized and why.

- **Q:** Why does Keller's own account focus on feelings?
  **A:** As the person who lived it, she can describe her inner experience — her mind 'awakening'.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Film scene
*Student:* Watch the water-pump scene from 'The Miracle Worker' and note how actors, sound and camera change the experience.

*How to teach:* Watch the scene; note acting, sound and camera.

- **Q:** How does the film change the experience of the water scene?
  **A:** Actors, close-ups, sound of water and music make it dramatic and emotional to watch; we see it from outside rather than inside Helen's mind.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Primary sources
*Student:* Look at Library of Congress photographs and letters related to Helen Keller and explain what they add.

*How to teach:* Browse Library of Congress photos/letters of Keller.

- **Q:** What does a primary source add?
  **A:** Direct evidence from the time — her real handwriting or photos — that makes her life more concrete.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 3-paragraph comparison of memoir vs. biography vs. film versions of the same event.

*How to teach:* The child writes the comparison.

- **Q:** Define memoir.
  **A:** A true account written by a person about their own experiences.

**Assessment:** Comparison essay.

**Answer key:** 3-paragraph comparison rubric — 4: compares Keller's first-person memoir (inner feelings, 'awakened my soul'), a biography (outside view, often adds context and others' observations), and the film (actors, sound, emotion, dramatic struggle at the pump); explains reasons for differences with evidence; 3: compares two well; 2: lists without explanation; 1: incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Relationships between quantities

**Prepare:** Gather: graph paper, Desmos. Have a kitchen scale and a cup for the Day 3 water experiment.

**Watch for:**
- Swapping independent and dependent variables. Ask: 'Which one do I choose or control? That's independent (x-axis).'
- Thinking every relationship's graph starts at (0,0). y = x + 3 starts at (0,3).

### Day 1 (Monday): Two changing quantities
*Student:* For situations like 'earning $9 per hour', name the independent and dependent variables and write an equation d = 9h.

*How to teach:* Ask: 'If you earn $9 per hour, what decides how much you earn?' Name hours as independent, dollars as dependent, and write d = 9h.

- **Q:** For $9 per hour, write an equation and find earnings for 6 hours.
  **A:** d = 9h; $54.
- **Q:** Which variable is independent?
  **A:** Hours worked (h); pay depends on it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tables to graphs
*Student:* Make tables and first-quadrant graphs for three equations (y = 4x, y = x + 3, y = 2.5x) and compare their shapes.

*How to teach:* Make tables for x = 0–5 for each equation and plot all three on one grid. Compare: which go through (0,0), which is steepest?

- **Q:** At x = 2, find y for y = 4x, y = x + 3 and y = 2.5x.
  **A:** 8, 5 and 5.
- **Q:** Which graph does not pass through the origin?
  **A:** y = x + 3 (it starts at 3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Real data
*Student:* Fill a cup with water a few tablespoons at a time and weigh it, then make a table, graph and equation for the relationship.

*How to teach:* Weigh the empty cup, then add 2 tablespoons at a time and weigh. Make a table and graph; help write an equation like w = (cup weight) + 15t (1 tbsp of water ≈ 15 g).

- **Q:** If the cup weighs 150 g and each tablespoon of water adds about 15 g, what is the weight after 6 tablespoons?
  **A:** About 240 g (150 + 90).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of expressions, equations, exponents and variable relationships.

*How to teach:* Assign the mixed review; check equations and graphs for labels.

- **Q:** Solve x + 4.5 = 10 and ⅗x = 9.
  **A:** 5.5 and 15.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 end-of-unit assessment and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test; the child corrects errors.

- **Q:** Evaluate 2³ + 4 × 3.
  **A:** 20.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 end-of-unit assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test sample key: 1) 'x decreased by 7' → x − 7 2) 4a + 3 when a = 1.5 → 9 3) x + 4.5 = 10 → x = 5.5 4) (3/5)x = 9 → x = 15 5) 3(2x + 5) = 6x + 15 6) factor 8x + 12 = 4(2x + 3) 7) 2³ + 4 × 3 = 20 8) A train goes 60 mph: d = 60t; at t = 3.5, d = 210 miles 9) In d = 60t, t is independent and d dependent 10) 6x + 2 + x = 7x + 2.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Discovering cells and using a microscope

**Prepare:** Gather: microscope (or smartphone clip-on microscope), onion, cork, slides or clear tape. A smartphone clip-on microscope works if you lack a microscope. Iodine stains clothes — use gloves.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cells were seen with the naked eye. Microscopes were needed.
- Moving the slide the wrong way. The image is flipped, so movement appears reversed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cell theory history
*Student:* Read about Hooke, Leeuwenhoek and the cell theory and make a timeline of discoveries.

*How to teach:* Read about Hooke, Leeuwenhoek, Schleiden, Schwann and Virchow; build a timeline.

- **Q:** What are the three parts of cell theory?
  **A:** All living things are made of cells; cells are the basic unit of structure and function; all cells come from existing cells.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Microscope skills
*Student:* Learn microscope parts and practice focusing on a newspaper letter 'e', noting how the image flips.

*How to teach:* Label microscope parts; focus on a letter 'e'.

- **Q:** How does the 'e' look under the microscope?
  **A:** Upside down and backwards.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cork cells
*Student:* Observe a thin slice of cork as Hooke did and sketch what you see with magnification noted.

*How to teach:* Observe thin cork; sketch.

- **Q:** What did Hooke think cork cells looked like?
  **A:** Small empty rooms or boxes — so he called them 'cells'.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Onion cells
*Student:* Prepare an onion skin wet mount with iodine or food coloring and draw the cells.

*How to teach:* Prepare an onion wet mount; stain and draw.

- **Q:** What shape are onion cells, and which structures show?
  **A:** Brick-like rectangles; the cell wall and nucleus are visible.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on cell theory and microscope parts and use.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Eyepiece 10×, objective 40×. Total magnification?
  **A:** 400×.

**Assessment:** Observation drawings and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Robert Hooke observed cork (1665) and named 'cells' 2) Antonie van Leeuwenhoek first saw living microorganisms 3) cell theory: all living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; all cells come from existing cells 4) eyepiece, objective, stage, coarse and fine focus, light source 5) total magnification = eyepiece × objective (10× × 40× = 400×) 6) the letter 'e' appears upside down and reversed. Drawings: circular field, labeled magnification, title.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: The Buddha and the spread of Buddhism

**Prepare:** Gather: Dhammapada excerpts (Fordham Sourcebook), map of Asia. Print Dhammapada verses (Fordham).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Buddha is worshipped as a god. He is honored as an enlightened teacher.
- Thinking the Middle Way means doing nothing. It avoids extremes of luxury and harsh self-denial.

### Day 1 (Monday): Siddhartha's life
*Student:* Read the story of Siddhartha Gautama and create a storyboard of key events leading to enlightenment.

*How to teach:* Storyboard Siddhartha's life.

- **Q:** What were the Four Sights?
  **A:** An old man, a sick man, a dead body and a holy man.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Teachings
*Student:* Explain the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path in your own words.

*How to teach:* Explain the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path.

- **Q:** State the Four Noble Truths simply.
  **A:** Life involves suffering; suffering comes from desire/craving; ending desire ends suffering; the Eightfold Path leads there.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dhammapada
*Student:* Read selected verses of the Dhammapada and connect them to Buddhist teachings on the Middle Way.

*How to teach:* Read Dhammapada verses.

- **Q:** Explain 'Hatred does not cease by hatred, but only by love.'
  **A:** Revenge keeps hatred going; only kindness ends it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Spread of Buddhism
*Student:* Map the spread of Buddhism to Sri Lanka (Ceylon), Central Asia and beyond.

*How to teach:* Map the spread of Buddhism.

- **Q:** How did Buddhism reach Sri Lanka?
  **A:** Missionaries sent by Emperor Ashoka (including his son Mahinda).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare Hinduism and Buddhism in a Venn diagram; quiz on Buddhism.

*How to teach:* Make the Venn; quiz.

- **Q:** What is the Middle Way?
  **A:** A balanced life avoiding extremes of luxury and self-denial.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram and quiz.

**Answer key:** Venn: both from India, belief in karma and rebirth; Hinduism — many deities/Brahman, varna system; Buddhism — founded by Siddhartha, Four Noble Truths, rejects caste ranking. Quiz: 1) Four Sights: old man, sick man, dead body, holy man 2) enlightenment under the Bodhi tree 3) Four Noble Truths 4) Eightfold Path 5) Middle Way 6) Ashoka sent missionaries to Sri Lanka.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: Voice and movement for character

**Prepare:** Find short monologues suitable for middle schoolers (e.g., from Aesop adaptations). Prepare a character profile template.

**Watch for:**
- Speaking quietly on stage. Projection uses breath support, not shouting.
- Moving randomly. Blocking and gestures should have purpose.

### Day 1 (Monday): Vocal choices
*Student:* Read one line in 6 different emotions and change pitch, pace and volume.

*How to teach:* Read one line in 6 emotions, changing pitch, pace and volume. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is inflection?
  **A:** The rise and fall of pitch in the voice.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Physical choices
*Student:* Walk as different characters (king, child, old sailor) leading with different body parts.

*How to teach:* Walk as different characters leading with different body parts. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** How might a king walk?
  **A:** Upright, chest leading, slow and confident.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Character profile
*Student:* Choose a monologue and write a character profile (age, wants, fears, inner thoughts).

*How to teach:* Choose a monologue; write a character profile. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why write a character profile?
  **A:** To understand the character's wants, fears and background so choices are believable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Memorize and rehearse the monologue with blocking.

*How to teach:* Memorize and rehearse with blocking. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is blocking?
  **A:** Planned movement and positions on stage.

### Day 5 (Friday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the monologue and record personal reactions to your choices.

*How to teach:* Perform and record reactions. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is projection?
  **A:** Speaking loudly and clearly enough for the whole audience to hear.

**Assessment:** Monologue performance.

**Answer key:** Monologue rubric (1–4 each): memorized, projection and articulation, vocal variety, purposeful movement, character believability. Proficient = 3+ each.

## Physical Education 6: Folk dances from around the world

**Prepare:** Gather: music for Mayim Mayim, Virginia Reel, Tinikling. Find music for Mayim Mayim, Virginia Reel and Tinikling; lay two jump ropes on the ground. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing Tinikling steps. Learn slowly, then speed up.
- Ignoring cultural meaning.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mayim Mayim
*Student:* Learn the Israeli circle dance Mayim Mayim.

*How to teach:* Learn Mayim Mayim.

- **Q:** What does Mayim Mayim celebrate?
  **A:** Finding water ('mayim' means water in Hebrew).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Virginia Reel
*Student:* Learn the Virginia Reel with partners.

*How to teach:* Learn the Virginia Reel.

- **Q:** What formation does the Virginia Reel use?
  **A:** Two facing lines of partners (longways set).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tinikling
*Student:* Learn Tinikling (Philippines) steps using jump ropes on the ground in place of poles.

*How to teach:* Learn Tinikling with ropes.

- **Q:** What does Tinikling imitate?
  **A:** The tikling bird hopping between bamboo or rice stalks/traps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice
*Student:* Practice all three dances and discuss where each comes from.

*How to teach:* Practice all three; discuss origins.

- **Q:** Where is Tinikling from?
  **A:** The Philippines.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform one folk dance for an audience.

*How to teach:* Perform one dance.

- **Q:** Why learn folk dances?
  **A:** They show culture and history and are fun group exercise.

**Assessment:** Folk dance performance.

**Answer key:** Performance: 4 = correct steps and formation, on the beat, explains the dance's origin; 3 = minor errors; 2 = many cues; 1 = can't complete. Origins: Mayim Mayim — Israel (celebrating finding water); Virginia Reel — American (from English country dance), a longways partner dance; Tinikling — Philippines, imitating the tikling bird stepping between bamboo.

## Health 6: Earthquake and fire preparedness

**Prepare:** Graph paper and a flashlight. Read the Great ShakeOut 'Drop, Cover, and Hold On' guidance and NFPA home fire escape planning tips. Check that smoke alarms work.

**Watch for:**
- Running outside during an earthquake. Drop, cover and hold on where you are; falling objects near buildings are dangerous.
- Standing in a doorway during an earthquake. Modern doorways aren't stronger; get under a sturdy table instead.

### Day 1 (Monday): Earthquake science
*Student:* Review why California has earthquakes and what to do indoors, outdoors and in a car.

*How to teach:* Review why California has earthquakes (Pacific and North American plates sliding past along faults like the San Andreas) and what to do indoors, outdoors and in a car.

- **Q:** What should you do if an earthquake hits while you're in a car?
  **A:** Pull over away from buildings, trees, overpasses and power lines, set the brake and stay inside until shaking stops.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drill
*Student:* Practice Drop, Cover and Hold On in 3 rooms and identify safe spots.

*How to teach:* Practice Drop, Cover and Hold On in 3 rooms; identify safe spots. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What are the steps of Drop, Cover and Hold On?
  **A:** Drop to hands and knees, cover your head and neck under a sturdy table, hold on until shaking stops.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fire escape plan
*Student:* Draw a home floor plan with two exits from each room and a meeting place.

*How to teach:* Draw a home floor plan with two exits from each room and a meeting place. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why have two exits from each room?
  **A:** If one exit is blocked by fire or smoke, you can use the other.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fire drill
*Student:* Run a family fire drill and time it; practice stop, drop and roll and crawling low.

*How to teach:* Run a family fire drill and time it; practice stop, drop and roll and crawling low. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why crawl low in a fire?
  **A:** Smoke and heat rise; cleaner air is near the floor.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer a quiz on earthquake and fire safety.

*How to teach:* Quiz on earthquake and fire safety. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** What is an aftershock?
  **A:** A smaller earthquake that follows the main one.

**Assessment:** Escape plan and quiz.

**Answer key:** Escape plan: floor plan with two exits per room, outside meeting place, alarms marked. Quiz key: Drop, Cover, Hold On; car response; aftershocks; crawl low; feel doors with back of hand; stop, drop and roll; test smoke alarms monthly. Proficient = 80%.
