# Teacher's Guide — 6th Grade, Week 10

## English Language Arts 6: Greek myths and Greek/Latin roots

**Prepare:** Gather: 'A Wonder-Book' and 'Tanglewood Tales' by Nathaniel Hawthorne (Project Gutenberg), D'Aulaires' Book of Greek Myths (library, optional). Download A Wonder-Book (Pandora: 'The Paradise of Children'; Midas: 'The Golden Touch'). Prometheus is in D'Aulaires' or any myth collection.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking myths are just 'made-up stories'. Myths explained nature and taught values to ancient people.
- Guessing meaning from one root without checking. Verify with a dictionary.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why myths?
*Student:* Read the myth of Prometheus and list what it explains (fire, human suffering); discuss why cultures created myths.

*How to teach:* Read the Prometheus myth. The child lists what it explains and discusses why cultures made myths.

- **Q:** What does the Prometheus myth explain?
  **A:** How humans got fire (Prometheus stole it from the gods); in many versions it also explains human suffering because Zeus punishes humans and Prometheus.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pandora's box
*Student:* Read Hawthorne's 'The Paradise of Children' (Pandora) and write the theme in one sentence with supporting evidence.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Paradise of Children'. The child writes the theme with evidence.

- **Q:** What remains in the box after the Troubles escape, and why does it matter?
  **A:** Hope; it suggests that hope helps people bear life's troubles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Midas and hubris
*Student:* Read 'The Golden Touch' (King Midas) and analyze how Midas's choices drive the plot to its resolution.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Golden Touch'. Trace Midas's choices from wish to loss to resolution.

- **Q:** How does Midas's golden touch become a curse?
  **A:** His food turns to gold so he can't eat, and he turns his beloved daughter Marygold into a gold statue.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Words from myths
*Student:* Study roots and words from myths (narcissist, echo, panic, tantalize, chronology, psyche) and build a 20-word root map.

*How to teach:* Build a root map from the myth words.

- **Q:** Where does 'panic' come from?
  **A:** From Pan, the god said to cause sudden fear in people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on myth themes and roots; retell a myth orally to a family member in two minutes.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; the child retells a myth orally.

- **Q:** What is a theme of the Midas story?
  **A:** Greed can destroy what truly matters; love is worth more than wealth.

**Assessment:** Roots quiz and oral retelling.

**Answer key:** Roots quiz sample: 1) chron = time (chronology) 2) psych = mind/soul 3) narcissist = person too absorbed in himself (Narcissus) 4) echo (the nymph Echo) 5) panic (Pan) 6) tantalize = tease with something out of reach (Tantalus) 7) graph = write 8) tele = far 9) bio = life 10) phon = sound. Oral retelling rubric: 4 = correct sequence, main characters, the explanation/lesson, clear voice under 2 minutes; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = confused order; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 6: Speed and unit price

**Prepare:** Gather: store flyer or online grocery prices, stopwatch, tape measure. Find prices for 6 pairs of different-size items; mark a 20-meter course outside before Day 2.

**Watch for:**
- Dividing in the wrong order for unit price (ounces ÷ dollars instead of dollars ÷ ounces). Label units: $ per oz means $ ÷ oz.
- Assuming the bigger package is always the better buy. Compute the unit price first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Constant speed
*Student:* Solve 8 problems using distance = rate x time reasoning with tables (e.g., a cyclist at 12 miles per hour).

*How to teach:* Model a table: hours 1, 2, 3 vs. miles 12, 24, 36. The child solves the 8 problems, labeling every column.

- **Q:** A cyclist rides 12 mph. How far in 2.5 hours? How long to ride 42 miles?
  **A:** 30 miles; 3.5 hours.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Speed lab
*Student:* Measure a 20-meter course, time yourself walking, jogging and hopping, and compute meters per second for each.

*How to teach:* Time the child walking, jogging and hopping 20 m. They compute m/s = 20 ÷ seconds and rank speeds.

- **Q:** If walking 20 m takes 16 seconds, what is the speed?
  **A:** 1.25 meters per second.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Unit price
*Student:* Compare 6 pairs of grocery items in different sizes using unit price, and decide the better buy for each.

*How to teach:* For each pair, divide price by size to get a unit price; round to the nearest cent and decide.

- **Q:** 18 oz cereal for $4.50 or 12 oz for $3.24 — which is the better buy?
  **A:** 18 oz: $0.25/oz vs. $0.27/oz.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rate problems
*Student:* Solve mixed rate problems (pages per minute, heartbeats per minute, cost per ounce) and justify one answer to a family member.

*How to teach:* Let the child solve the mixed problems, then explain one to a family member using units ('pages per minute').

- **Q:** A heart beats 18 times in 15 seconds. How many beats per minute?
  **A:** 72 beats per minute (18 × 4).
- **Q:** 48 pages in 60 minutes is how many pages per minute?
  **A:** 0.8 page per minute.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on constant speed and unit price problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the lab table.

- **Q:** A car goes 195 miles in 3 hours. Speed?
  **A:** 65 miles per hour.

**Assessment:** Quiz plus speed lab data table.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 12 mph for 2.5 hours → 30 miles 2) 42 miles at 12 mph → 3.5 hours 3) 18 oz for $4.50 ($0.25/oz) vs 12 oz for $3.24 ($0.27/oz) → 18 oz is the better buy 4) 300 m in 60 s → 5 m/s 5) 3 lb for $5.97 → $1.99/lb. Lab table: each trial lists distance (20 m), time and m/s = 20 ÷ time.

## Science 6 (Integrated): Humidity, clouds and precipitation

**Prepare:** Gather: clear jar, warm water, ice, match (with adult) or aerosol, cloud chart. Adult lights the match. Use a metal can with outside thermometer for dew point.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking clouds are water vapor. Clouds are tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals; vapor is invisible.
- Confusing sleet and hail. Sleet is frozen raindrops falling through cold air; hail grows in thunderstorm updrafts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cloud in a jar
*Student:* With adult help, make a cloud in a jar using warm water, ice and a little smoke, and explain the role of cooling and particles.

*How to teach:* Make the cloud in a jar; ask why smoke helps.

- **Q:** What two things does a cloud need to form?
  **A:** Air cooled to its dew point and tiny particles for water to condense on.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dew point
*Student:* Add ice slowly to water in a metal can, stirring, and record the temperature when dew forms.

*How to teach:* Stir ice water in a metal can; record the dew temperature.

- **Q:** What is the dew point?
  **A:** The temperature at which water vapor condenses into liquid.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cloud types
*Student:* Observe and photograph clouds daily, identify them with a cloud chart and relate them to weather that followed.

*How to teach:* Photograph and identify clouds daily.

- **Q:** What weather do cumulonimbus clouds bring?
  **A:** Thunderstorms, heavy rain, sometimes hail.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Precipitation
*Student:* Read about how droplets grow into rain, snow, sleet and hail, and make a diagram of each.

*How to teach:* Read about precipitation; diagram each.

- **Q:** How does hail form?
  **A:** Updrafts carry raindrops high into freezing air repeatedly, adding layers of ice until they fall.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on humidity, clouds and precipitation types.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Are clouds made of water vapor?
  **A:** No — tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals.

**Assessment:** Cloud log and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) humidity = amount of water vapor in air 2) dew point = temperature at which air becomes saturated and condenses 3) clouds need cooling and particles (condensation nuclei) 4) cumulus = puffy, fair weather; stratus = gray layers; cirrus = wispy, high, ice crystals; cumulonimbus = thunderstorms 5) snow, sleet, freezing rain, hail described correctly. Cloud log: 5+ days with type and weather that followed.

## World History and Geography: Ancient Civilizations: Pharaohs, religion and the afterlife

**Prepare:** Gather: apple slices, salt and baking soda, images of Giza and tomb paintings. Prepare a 1:1 salt and baking-soda mix; weigh apple slices.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking slaves built the pyramids. Evidence (workers' villages, tombs) shows paid laborers and skilled workers, plus seasonal farmers.
- Thinking mummification was only for pharaohs. Wealthier Egyptians were mummified too.

### Day 1 (Monday): God-king
*Student:* Read about the pharaoh as a divine ruler and draw Egypt's social pyramid.

*How to teach:* Read about the god-king; draw the pyramid.

- **Q:** Why did Egyptians obey the pharaoh?
  **A:** They believed he was a god on earth who kept order (ma'at).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mummify an apple
*Student:* Begin an apple mummification experiment with salt and baking soda, weighing the slices first.

*How to teach:* Bury one slice in salt/baking soda and leave a control.

- **Q:** Why does salt preserve the apple?
  **A:** It draws water out; without moisture, decay slows.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Book of the Dead
*Student:* Read the 'Weighing of the Heart' scene and explain what it shows about Egyptian ethics.

*How to teach:* Read the Weighing of the Heart.

- **Q:** What happens in the Weighing of the Heart?
  **A:** The dead person's heart is weighed against the feather of Ma'at (truth); a light heart means a good life and entry to the afterlife.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pyramids
*Student:* Research how the Great Pyramid was built and who built it, then write a short explanation using two sources.

*How to teach:* Research the Great Pyramid using two sources.

- **Q:** Who built the Great Pyramid?
  **A:** Thousands of organized laborers and skilled workers, many paid and housed in nearby villages — not mainly slaves.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Weigh the apple slices, explain the results, and take a quiz on Egyptian religion and government.

*How to teach:* Weigh slices; give the quiz.

- **Q:** What does the Weighing of the Heart show about Egyptian ethics?
  **A:** Living honestly and doing right mattered for the afterlife.

**Assessment:** Mummification lab notes and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab notes: starting and ending masses, observation that the salted slice lost more mass and resisted rot because salt/baking soda draw out water (as natron did). Quiz: 1) pharaoh was seen as a god-king 2) social pyramid: pharaoh, priests/nobles, scribes, artisans/merchants, farmers, enslaved people 3) Weighing of the Heart — heart weighed against the feather of Ma'at 4) Great Pyramid built for Khufu around 2560 BCE 5) built by organized, paid workers.

## Visual and Performing Arts 6: One-point perspective

**Prepare:** Ruler, pencil, photos of hallways and roads (printed). Orthogonals = lines that go back to the vanishing point.

**Watch for:**
- Placing the vanishing point off the horizon. In one-point perspective it's always on the horizon line.
- Slanting vertical lines. Verticals stay vertical; horizontals parallel to the picture stay horizontal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finding vanishing points
*Student:* Trace orthogonal lines on photos of roads and hallways to locate vanishing points.

*How to teach:* Trace orthogonals on photos to find vanishing points. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Where do orthogonal lines meet?
  **A:** At the vanishing point on the horizon line.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Floating boxes
*Student:* Draw 8 boxes above, below and on the horizon line using one vanishing point.

*How to teach:* Draw 8 boxes above, below and on the horizon. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** For a box above the horizon line, which side do you see?
  **A:** The bottom.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hallway
*Student:* Draw a hallway with doors and windows in one-point perspective.

*How to teach:* Draw a hallway with doors and windows. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why do doors farther down the hall look smaller?
  **A:** Objects appear smaller as they get farther away.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Your room
*Student:* Draw your bedroom in one-point perspective with furniture.

*How to teach:* Draw your bedroom with furniture in one-point perspective. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Where should the horizon line be?
  **A:** At your eye level as you look into the room.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & reflect
*Student:* Self-check orthogonals with a ruler and fix any errors.

*How to teach:* Self-check orthogonals with a ruler; fix errors. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is foreshortening?
  **A:** Objects appear shorter when they extend toward or away from the viewer.

**Assessment:** One-point perspective room drawing.

**Answer key:** Room drawing: 4 = horizon and vanishing point marked, all orthogonals converge, verticals vertical, furniture in perspective; 3 = 1–2 errors; 2 = several; 1 = none.

## Physical Education 6: Ultimate: game play and Spirit of the Game

**Prepare:** Gather: flying disc, cones for end zones. Mark two end zones. Check the space for hazards, have water ready, and start every session with the dynamic warm-up.

**Watch for:**
- Running with the disc. The thrower must stop and pivot.
- Arguing calls. Spirit of the Game means players resolve calls respectfully.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules
*Student:* Learn ultimate rules and practice a stall count while marking.

*How to teach:* Teach rules; practice the stall count.

- **Q:** What is the stall count?
  **A:** The marker counts to 10; the thrower must release before 10 or it's a turnover.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cutting
*Student:* Practice cuts to get open and the 'give and go'.

*How to teach:* Practice cuts and give-and-go.

- **Q:** What is a cut?
  **A:** A quick change of direction to get open.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Game
*Student:* Play ultimate, resolving calls through Spirit of the Game discussion.

*How to teach:* Play; resolve calls with Spirit of the Game.

- **Q:** What is Spirit of the Game?
  **A:** Players referee themselves, follow rules and treat opponents with respect.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare strategies
*Student:* Compare soccer and ultimate strategies on a chart and play a modified game.

*How to teach:* Compare soccer and ultimate on a chart.

- **Q:** Name one strategy shared by soccer and ultimate.
  **A:** Moving to open space to receive a pass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Take a quiz on invasion strategies and self-assess sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What happens when a pass is dropped?
  **A:** Turnover — the other team gets the disc.

**Assessment:** Strategy quiz and sportsmanship self-assessment.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) a point is scored by catching the disc in the end zone 2) the thrower can't run with the disc 3) the marker counts the stall to 10 4) a dropped or intercepted disc is a turnover 5) Spirit of the Game = self-officiating with respect 6) shared strategies with soccer: moving to open space, passing, defending space. Sportsmanship self-assessment 1–4.

## Health 6: Conflict resolution

**Prepare:** Print a conflict-resolution steps chart: 1) Cool down 2) Take turns telling your side 3) Listen and paraphrase 4) Brainstorm solutions 5) Agree on a win-win 6) Follow up. Write a scripted peer conflict for mediation.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking conflict is always bad. Conflict is normal and can be solved in ways that strengthen relationships.
- Thinking someone must win. Win-win solutions meet both people's needs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Conflict causes
*Student:* List common causes of conflict among middle schoolers and family members.

*How to teach:* List common causes of conflict (misunderstandings, jealousy, sharing, rumors, different opinions).

- **Q:** Name three common causes of conflict.
  **A:** Misunderstandings, rumors/gossip, competing for the same thing, different values, jealousy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Resolution steps
*Student:* Learn steps: cool down, take turns telling, listen, brainstorm solutions, agree, follow up.

*How to teach:* Teach the six steps. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why is cooling down the first step?
  **A:** When emotions are high, people say hurtful things; calm minds solve problems better.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sibling scenario
*Student:* Use the steps to resolve a realistic sibling or friend conflict in a role-play.

*How to teach:* Role-play a sibling or friend conflict using the steps. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What is a compromise?
  **A:** Each person gives up something to reach an agreement.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mediation
*Student:* Act as a mediator for a scripted peer conflict.

*How to teach:* Your child mediates a scripted conflict. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does a mediator do?
  **A:** A neutral person who helps others talk and find a solution without taking sides.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a solution for a new conflict scenario using every step.

*How to teach:* Write a solution for a new scenario using every step. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a win-win solution?
  **A:** A solution where both people get their needs met.

**Assessment:** Written conflict solution.

**Answer key:** Written solution rubric: 4 = all six steps applied specifically, with a realistic win-win; 3 = five steps; 2 = three or four; 1 = fewer.
