# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 16

## English Language Arts 8: Douglass's Narrative: preface and chapters 1–4

**Prepare:** Download the Narrative (Project Gutenberg). Parental preview: whippings, a murder, separation of families, and racial slurs quoted from slaveholders; Douglass describes these to expose slavery's cruelty. Decide how you'll handle the slur when reading aloud.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the preface was written by Douglass. William Lloyd Garrison wrote it to vouch for the book.
- Mixing up Mr. Severe (chapter 2) with Mr. Gore (chapter 4).

### Day 1 (Monday): Background & preface
*Student:* Read a short biography of Douglass and William Lloyd Garrison's preface; note why the book needed a white abolitionist's endorsement in 1845.

*How to teach:* Read a biography and Garrison's preface. To check, ask: "Why did the book need Garrison's preface in 1845?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Why did the book need Garrison's preface in 1845?
  **A:** Many white readers doubted a formerly enslaved man could write so well; Garrison, a famous abolitionist, vouched for its truth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 1–2
*Student:* Read chapters 1–2 and annotate how Douglass describes not knowing his birthday and being separated from his mother.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2; annotate birthday and mother. Finish by asking: "How does Douglass describe not knowing his birthday?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** How does Douglass describe not knowing his birthday?
  **A:** He says most slaves 'know as little of their ages as horses know of theirs' — slavery denies basic identity.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapters 3–4
*Student:* Read chapters 3–4 and chart how overseers Mr. Severe and Mr. Gore are described and what Douglass wants readers to conclude.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 3–4; chart overseers (Mr. Severe appears in chapter 2).

- **Q:** What happened to Demby?
  **A:** Mr. Gore shot him when he refused to come out of a creek; Gore was not punished.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Author's purpose
*Student:* Write a paragraph identifying Douglass's purpose and intended audience with two pieces of supporting evidence.

*How to teach:* Write a purpose paragraph. Check understanding with: "Who is Douglass's intended audience?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Who is Douglass's intended audience?
  **A:** Northern white readers, to win support for abolition.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Reading quiz on chapters 1–4 and vocabulary from the text.

*How to teach:* Reading and vocabulary quiz. To check, ask: "Define 'dehumanize.'" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Define 'dehumanize.'
  **A:** To treat someone as less than human.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and purpose paragraph.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz: 1) Douglass doesn't know his age/birthday — slaves knew as little of their ages 'as horses know of theirs.' 2) He was separated from his mother as an infant; she visited only a few times at night and died when he was about seven. 3) Aunt Hester's whipping was 'the blood-stained gate, the entrance to the hell of slavery.' 4) Mr. Severe was 'rightly named: he was a cruel man.' 5) Mr. Gore shot Demby and was never punished. Purpose paragraph rubric: purpose (expose slavery's cruelty and support abolition), audience (Northern white readers), two pieces of evidence.

## Mathematics 8: Solving systems algebraically

**Prepare:** Notebook; Desmos for checking. Prepare the Unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Substituting and then forgetting to find the second variable. A solution needs both x and y.
- Comparing intercepts but not slopes in inspection. Different slopes always mean exactly one solution.

### Day 1 (Monday): Substitution with y =
*Student:* Solve systems where one equation is solved for y (y = 2x + 1 and y = -x + 7) by setting expressions equal.

*How to teach:* Model: when both are y = ..., set the expressions equal. To check, ask: "Solve y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 7." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Solve y = 2x + 1 and y = −x + 7.
  **A:** 2x + 1 = −x + 7 → 3x = 6 → x = 2, y = 5. Solution (2, 5).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Substitution in general
*Student:* Solve 6 systems by substitution where you must rearrange one equation first; check with Desmos.

*How to teach:* Model solving one equation for a variable and substituting. Child solves 6 and checks with Desmos.

- **Q:** Solve 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2.
  **A:** x = y + 2; 2(y + 2) + y = 7 → 3y = 3 → y = 1, x = 3. Solution (3, 1).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inspection
*Student:* Decide without solving whether 6 systems have zero, one or infinitely many solutions by comparing slopes and intercepts.

*How to teach:* Rewrite each equation in y = mx + b and compare: different slopes → one solution; same slope, different intercept → none; same line → infinitely many. Before moving on, ask: "Without solving: y = −x + 1 and 2x + 2y = 2." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Without solving: y = −x + 1 and 2x + 2y = 2.
  **A:** The second is y = −x + 1, the same line: infinitely many solutions.
- **Q:** y = 2x and y = 3x?
  **A:** Different slopes: exactly one solution (0, 0).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Real-world systems
*Student:* Solve 4 word problems, such as a coin jar with 20 coins worth $3.20 in dimes and quarters.

*How to teach:* Child defines variables, writes two equations and solves 4 word problems. Check understanding with: "A jar has 20 coins (dimes and quarters) worth $3.20. How many of each?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** A jar has 20 coins (dimes and quarters) worth $3.20. How many of each?
  **A:** d + q = 20 and 0.10d + 0.25q = 3.20 → q = 8 quarters, d = 12 dimes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Unit test on linear equations and systems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test. To check, ask: "Solve y = x − 4 and y = 3x + 2." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Solve y = x − 4 and y = 3x + 2.
  **A:** x − 4 = 3x + 2 → x = −3, y = −7.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test suggested items: 1) 3x + 5 = x − 7 → x = −6. 2) (1/2)(x + 6) = x − 1 → x = 8. 3) 2(x + 4) = 2x + 8 → infinitely many. 4) y = 2x + 1, y = −x + 7 → (2, 5). 5) 2x + y = 7, x − y = 2 → (3, 1). 6) Inspection: y = 4x − 3 and y = 4x + 2 → no solution. 7) 20 dimes and quarters worth $3.20 → 12 dimes, 8 quarters. 8) Explain what the solution of a system means. 1–2 points each.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Sound waves

**Prepare:** Paper cups, string, rubber bands, a tissue box, 4–5 identical glasses, water, a spoon, towels, foam, cardboard; install a free decibel-meter app.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking sound travels best through air. Sound travels faster and often more clearly through solids and liquids, and not at all through a vacuum.
- Confusing pitch with loudness. Pitch depends on frequency; loudness on amplitude.

### Day 1 (Monday): Vibrations
*Student:* Stretch rubber bands over a tissue box and pluck; change tension and length to change pitch and record observations.

*How to teach:* Pluck rubber bands over a tissue box; change tension and length and record pitch. To check, ask: "How does tightening a rubber band change pitch?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** How does tightening a rubber band change pitch?
  **A:** Higher pitch — it vibrates at a higher frequency.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sound through materials
*Student:* Make a cup-and-string telephone and compare how well sound travels through string, air and a table.

*How to teach:* Make a cup-and-string telephone; compare transmission through string, air and a table. Finish by asking: "Why does the string phone work only when the string is tight?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why does the string phone work only when the string is tight?
  **A:** The vibrations travel through the taut string; a slack string doesn't transmit them well.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water glass xylophone
*Student:* Tap glasses filled to different levels and explain pitch differences using frequency.

*How to teach:* Tap glasses filled to different levels; explain pitch differences. Before moving on, ask: "Which glass makes the lowest pitch when tapped?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Which glass makes the lowest pitch when tapped?
  **A:** The fullest — more water makes the glass vibrate more slowly (lower frequency).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sound absorption
*Student:* Test how towels, foam and cardboard reduce sound from a phone speaker using a free decibel meter app.

*How to teach:* Play the same sound through each material and measure decibels with the app. Check understanding with: "Why keep phone volume and distance the same?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why keep phone volume and distance the same?
  **A:** To control variables so only the material changes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on sound properties; write up the absorption test.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the absorption write-up. To check, ask: "Can sound travel through space?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Can sound travel through space?
  **A:** No — there's no medium.

**Assessment:** Sound absorption investigation report.

**Answer key:** Investigation report rubric (4 levels): question; hypothesis; controlled variables (same phone, volume, distance, sound); data table of decibel readings for each material with 3 trials; graph; conclusion (soft, porous materials like towels and foam usually absorb the most sound); sources of error. Quiz: pitch ↔ frequency; loudness ↔ amplitude; sound needs a medium; tighter or shorter band → higher pitch.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Jacksonian democracy and Indian Removal

**Prepare:** Voter turnout data (1824: about 27% of eligible voters; 1828: about 58%); Jackson's 1832 Bank veto message; the Cherokee Memorial (1830); Jackson's Second Annual Message (1830); removal routes map; Private John Burnett's account (written 1890); Worcester v. Georgia summary.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Jacksonian democracy meant everyone could vote. It expanded voting to most white men; women, most free Black men and Native Americans were excluded.
- Believing the Supreme Court approved removal. In Worcester v. Georgia (1832) the Court ruled Georgia had no authority over Cherokee land; Jackson did not enforce it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Jackson's rise
*Student:* Compare voter-turnout data from 1824 and 1828 and explain how the end of property requirements expanded white male suffrage.

*How to teach:* Compare 1824 and 1828 turnout; explain expanded white male suffrage.

- **Q:** Why did turnout jump in 1828?
  **A:** Most states dropped property requirements for white men, and more states let voters (not legislatures) choose electors; parties organized rallies.
- **Q:** What was the 'spoils system'?
  **A:** Rewarding political supporters with government jobs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Bank War
*Student:* Read an excerpt from Jackson's 1832 veto message and summarize his argument against the Second Bank.

*How to teach:* Read Jackson's 1832 veto; summarize his argument.

- **Q:** Why did Jackson veto the Second Bank's recharter?
  **A:** He argued it was unconstitutional, a monopoly that served the rich and foreign investors at the expense of ordinary people.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Indian Removal debate
*Student:* Compare the Cherokee Memorial to Congress with Jackson's 1830 message on removal using a sourcing chart.

*How to teach:* Compare the Cherokee Memorial with Jackson's 1830 message using a sourcing chart.

- **Q:** What did the Cherokee argue in their memorial?
  **A:** They had treaty rights to their homeland, had adopted a constitution, schools and farming, and wished to stay on the land of their ancestors.
- **Q:** How did Jackson justify removal?
  **A:** He claimed it would benefit both whites and Native people, open land for settlement and protect Native nations from destruction by moving them west.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trail of Tears
*Student:* Map the removal routes of the Cherokee, Choctaw, Creek, Chickasaw and Seminole and read a soldier's eyewitness account (Private John Burnett).

*How to teach:* Map removal routes; read Burnett's account.

- **Q:** Which five nations were removed from the Southeast?
  **A:** Cherokee, Choctaw, Creek (Muscogee), Chickasaw and Seminole.
- **Q:** About how many Cherokee died on the Trail of Tears (1838–39)?
  **A:** About 4,000 (roughly a quarter of the nation).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a document-based paragraph: 'Was Indian Removal consistent with the Constitution?' citing Worcester v. Georgia.

*How to teach:* Write the document-based paragraph.

- **Q:** What did Worcester v. Georgia (1832) decide?
  **A:** Georgia had no authority over the Cherokee Nation; only the federal government could deal with it under treaties.

**Assessment:** Document-based paragraph.

**Answer key:** Document-based paragraph rubric (1–4): claim (e.g., removal was inconsistent with the Constitution as interpreted by the Supreme Court); evidence — Worcester v. Georgia (1832) held that the Cherokee Nation was a distinct community where Georgia law had no force and only the federal government could deal with it; federal treaties (supreme law under Article VI) guaranteed Cherokee land; Jackson's message argued removal was benevolent and would protect Native people; the Treaty of New Echota (1835) was signed by a minority faction, not the elected Cherokee government; Burnett's account of suffering (about 4,000 of roughly 16,000 Cherokee died); sourcing of each document; counterclaim addressed.

## Spanish 1B: Spelling changes: -car, -gar, -zar in the preterite

**Prepare:** StudySpanish.com preterite exercises.

**Watch for:**
- Applying the spelling change to all persons. It occurs only in the yo form: busqué but buscaste, buscó.
- Forgetting why: c→qu and g→gu keep the hard sound before é; z→c because Spanish writes ce, not ze.

### Day 1 (Monday): Spelling rules
*Student:* Learn why buscar→busqué, pagar→pagué, empezar→empecé and practice 15 yo forms.

*How to teach:* Teach the spelling rules; practice 15 yo forms.

- **Q:** Give the yo preterite of buscar, pagar and almorzar.
  **A:** busqué, pagué, almorcé.
- **Q:** Why does 'pagar' become 'pagué'?
  **A:** To keep the hard g sound before e (ge would sound like h).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): StudySpanish practice
*Student:* Complete the StudySpanish.com preterite regular-verb exercises and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Complete StudySpanish exercises; correct errors.

- **Q:** Give the tú preterite of tocar.
  **A:** tocaste (no spelling change).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diary
*Student:* Write a 10-sentence diary entry about a shopping trip using at least five spelling-change verbs.

*How to teach:* Write a 10-sentence shopping diary with five spelling-change verbs.

- **Q:** Write: 'I arrived at the store and I looked for a gift.'
  **A:** Llegué a la tienda y busqué un regalo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview
*Student:* Ask a family member ten questions about what they did yesterday and record their answers in the preterite.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about yesterday; record answers.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What time did you arrive home?'
  **A:** ¿A qué hora llegaste a casa?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a spelling-change preterite quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the spelling-change quiz.

- **Q:** Give the yo preterite of practicar.
  **A:** practiqué.

**Assessment:** Preterite spelling-change quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: -car → -qué (buscar → busqué, tocar → toqué, practicar → practiqué, sacar → saqué); -gar → -gué (pagar → pagué, llegar → llegué, jugar → jugué); -zar → -cé (empezar → empecé, almorzar → almorcé, organizar → organicé). Samples: Yo (llegar) tarde → llegué; Él (pagar) → pagó (no change); Yo (empezar) la tarea → empecé; Tú (jugar) → jugaste. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Jazz and ragtime

**Prepare:** Recordings: Scott Joplin 'Maple Leaf Rag' (1899), Louis Armstrong 'West End Blues' (1928), Duke Ellington 'Take the A Train' (composed by Billy Strayhorn), Ella Fitzgerald; PBS LearningMedia jazz videos; C blues backing track. Listening quiz of 6 clips.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking jazz improvisation is random. Correct: it follows the chord progression, scales and style.
- Confusing ragtime and jazz. Correct: ragtime is composed, written-out piano music; jazz adds improvisation and swing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ragtime
*Student:* Listen to Scott Joplin's 'Maple Leaf Rag' and identify syncopation.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Maple Leaf Rag'; identify syncopation.

- **Q:** What instrument is ragtime usually played on?

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Louis Armstrong
*Student:* Listen to 'West End Blues' and analyze the trumpet improvisation.

*How to teach:* Analyze Armstrong's trumpet improvisation in 'West End Blues.'

- **Q:** What is famous about the opening of 'West End Blues'?
  **A:** Armstrong's dazzling unaccompanied trumpet cadenza, a landmark of jazz improvisation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Big band
*Student:* Compare Duke Ellington's 'Take the A Train' with a modern big band.

*How to teach:* Compare Ellington's big band with a modern big band.

- **Q:** What sections make up a big band?
  **A:** Saxophones, trumpets, trombones and a rhythm section (piano, bass, drums, guitar).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improvisation
*Student:* Improvise on the C blues scale over a backing track.

*How to teach:* Improvise on the C blues scale over a backing track.

- **Q:** Spell the C blues scale.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Listening quiz identifying genres and instruments.

*How to teach:* Give the listening quiz.

- **Q:** What is scat singing, and who was famous for it?
  **A:** Improvised singing with nonsense syllables; Ella Fitzgerald (and Louis Armstrong).

**Assessment:** Listening quiz.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz key (match to your clips): ragtime — Joplin, piano, syncopated, composed; New Orleans/early jazz — Armstrong, trumpet improvisation; big band swing — Ellington, sax/brass sections; vocal jazz — Fitzgerald, scat; blues — 12-bar, AAB. 5/6 proficient.

## Physical Education 8: Major muscles and safe resistance training

**Prepare:** Resistance bands, light dumbbells or water jugs, mat, muscle chart. Technique checklist for squat, lunge, glute bridge, push-up, band row, overhead press, plank.

**Watch for:**
- Knees caving in during squats. Correct: push knees out over toes, chest up, weight in heels/mid-foot.
- Arching the back during planks or push-ups. Correct: brace core, straight line from head to heels.

### Day 1 (Monday): Muscle map
*Student:* Label a muscle chart with 10 major muscles and one exercise for each.

*How to teach:* Label 10 muscles with one exercise each (pectorals – push-up; latissimus dorsi – row; deltoids – overhead press; biceps – curl; triceps – dips; abdominals – plank; quadriceps – squat; hamstrings – glute bridge; gluteus maximus – lunge; gastrocnemius – calf raise).

- **Q:** Which muscle does a squat mainly work?
  **A:** Quadriceps and gluteus maximus (also hamstrings).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lower body
*Student:* Learn proper squat, lunge and glute bridge form; do 3 sets of 10-12 reps.

*How to teach:* Teach squat, lunge and glute bridge form; 3×10–12.

- **Q:** Give two squat cues.
  **A:** Chest up, hips back, knees track over toes, weight through heels, thighs toward parallel.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Upper body
*Student:* Learn push-up, band row and overhead press technique; do 3 sets of 10-12 reps.

*How to teach:* Push-up, band row and overhead press; 3×10–12.

- **Q:** Which muscles does a band row work?
  **A:** Upper back (latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, trapezius) and biceps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Core
*Student:* Learn plank, side plank, bird dog and dead bug; hold or repeat for 3 rounds.

*How to teach:* Plank, side plank, bird dog, dead bug; 3 rounds.

- **Q:** Why train the core?
  **A:** It stabilizes the spine, transfers force between upper and lower body and helps prevent back injury.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Demonstrate six exercises with correct form while a parent uses a technique checklist.

*How to teach:* Parent checks six exercises.

- **Q:** Why breathe out during the effort phase?
  **A:** Exhaling on exertion helps brace and avoids holding breath, which spikes blood pressure.

**Assessment:** Technique checklist for six exercises.

**Answer key:** Technique checklist: each exercise scored 0–2 (0 = unsafe form, 1 = minor errors, 2 = correct: neutral spine, controlled tempo, full range, correct joint alignment, breathing). 10+/12 proficient.

## Health Education 8: Healthy and unhealthy dating relationships

**Prepare:** Print the loveisrespect.org relationship spectrum (healthy, unhealthy, abusive). Prepare 12 behavior cards. Choose a short story or scenario with a teen relationship (or write one) for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking jealousy and constant texting show love. Correct: monitoring, jealousy and control are red flags, not signs of caring.
- Believing silence or not saying no equals consent. Correct: consent is a clear, freely given yes, can be withdrawn at any time, and cannot be given if someone is pressured, asleep, or intoxicated.

### Day 1 (Monday): Spectrum
*Student:* Sort relationship behaviors onto a healthy-unhealthy-abusive spectrum.

*How to teach:* Sort behavior cards onto the spectrum (e.g., respects boundaries = healthy; guilt-trips = unhealthy; hits, threatens or controls money = abusive).

- **Q:** Place 'checks your phone without asking' on the spectrum.
  **A:** Unhealthy to abusive — it is controlling and violates privacy, especially if it is repeated or demanded.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Red flags
*Student:* Identify digital red flags such as checking phones, demanding passwords and constant texting.

*How to teach:* List digital red flags and discuss what to do (talk to a trusted adult, set boundaries, block, save evidence).

- **Q:** Name three digital red flags.
  **A:** Demanding passwords, checking location, constant texting and anger if you don't reply, pressuring for photos, posting private information, controlling who you follow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Affirmative consent
*Student:* Learn that consent must be clear, ongoing and can be withdrawn; analyze scenarios (non-sexual and sexual examples, age-appropriate).

*How to teach:* Teach consent as clear, freely given, ongoing and reversible using everyday examples (borrowing a phone, a hug) and age-appropriate relationship scenarios.

- **Q:** If someone said yes earlier but now says stop, what must happen?
  **A:** Stop immediately — consent can be withdrawn at any time.
- **Q:** Can a person who is pressured or drunk give consent?
  **A:** No. Consent must be freely given by someone able to make the choice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Communication
*Student:* Practice refusal and negotiation phrases with a parent.

*How to teach:* Practice refusal phrases ('No, I'm not comfortable with that') and negotiation ('I don't want to __, but we could __'). Use firm voice and body language.

- **Q:** Give a refusal line and a negotiation line.
  **A:** Refusal: 'No. I'm not ready, and I need you to respect that.' Negotiation: 'I don't want to go to that party, but we could see a movie with friends.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Analyze a short story's relationship and identify healthy and unhealthy behaviors.

*How to teach:* Read the story; the child lists healthy and unhealthy behaviors with evidence and suggests what the character could do.

- **Q:** What makes a relationship healthy?
  **A:** Respect, trust, honesty, good communication, equality, and each person keeps their own friends and interests.

**Assessment:** Relationship analysis.

**Answer key:** Relationship analysis: 4 = at least three healthy and three unhealthy/abusive behaviors with evidence from the story, correctly placed on the spectrum, and a realistic action step (talk to trusted adult, loveisrespect.org); 3 = two of each plus action; 2 = lists without evidence; 1 = incomplete.
