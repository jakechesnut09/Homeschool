# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 13

## English Language Arts 8: Women's rights voices: Sojourner Truth and Elizabeth Cady Stanton

**Prepare:** Print both versions of Sojourner Truth's 1851 Akron speech (Marius Robinson's report in the Anti-Slavery Bugle, 1851; Frances Dana Gage's version, 1863) and the Declaration of Sentiments (Seneca Falls, 1848). Prepare a short new argument passage for Friday (e.g., a letter to the editor on school start times).

**Watch for:**
- Assuming the famous 'Ain't I a Woman?' text is a word-for-word record. Gage published it 12 years later, added the refrain and a Southern dialect (Truth grew up in New York speaking Dutch); Robinson's 1851 report, written by someone present, is considered more reliable.
- Confusing counterclaim with rebuttal. Counterclaim = the opposing view; rebuttal = the answer that weakens it.
- Shifting voice or mood mid-sentence ('First read the speech, and then you should annotate it'). Keep one mood: 'Read the speech, and then annotate it.'

### Day 1 (Monday): 'Ain't I a Woman?'
*Student:* Read the speech and trace how each repetition of the refrain answers a specific claim made by male opponents.

*How to teach:* Read Gage's version aloud. For each 'Ain't I a woman?', the child writes the opponent's claim it answers (women are delicate; women have less intellect; Christ was a man; Eve brought sin).

- **Q:** What claim does the first refrain answer?
  **A:** That women must be helped into carriages and over ditches; she says nobody ever helped her, and she has worked, plowed and borne the lash as well as any man.
- **Q:** How does she answer 'Christ wasn't a woman'?
  **A:** 'Where did your Christ come from? From God and a woman! Man had nothing to do with Him.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Two versions
*Student:* Compare the 1851 Robinson report with the 1863 Gage version and discuss why the texts differ and which is more reliable.

*How to teach:* Read Robinson's report and compare line by line. Discuss which is more reliable and why texts differ.

- **Q:** Does Robinson's 1851 version contain the refrain 'Ain't I a woman?'
  **A:** No — it is in standard English and lacks the refrain.
- **Q:** Why is Robinson's version considered more reliable?
  **A:** He was a friend who was present and published it within weeks; Gage wrote it 12 years later and added dialect and details.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Declaration of Sentiments
*Student:* Read the Declaration of Sentiments and chart how Stanton borrows the structure of the Declaration of Independence.

*How to teach:* Read the Declaration of Sentiments; chart its parallel to the Declaration of Independence (preamble, rights, grievances against 'man,' resolutions). Check understanding with: "How does Stanton revise Jefferson's famous sentence?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** How does Stanton revise Jefferson's famous sentence?
  **A:** 'We hold these truths to be self-evident: that all men and women are created equal.'
- **Q:** Name one grievance.
  **A:** Women are denied the right to vote (the 'elective franchise'); married women lose property rights; women are barred from most colleges and professions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: shifts
*Student:* Correct 10 sentences with inappropriate shifts in voice or mood and write a paragraph with consistent active, indicative verbs.

*How to teach:* Teach voice and mood shifts; child corrects 10 sentences and writes a paragraph in consistent active, indicative verbs. To check, ask: "Fix the shift: 'First read the speech, and then you should annotate it.'" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Fix the shift: 'First read the speech, and then you should annotate it.'
  **A:** 'Read the speech, and then annotate it.' (both imperative)

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz analyzing a short new passage for claim, counterclaim and rebuttal.

*How to teach:* Give the new-passage quiz (key). Finish by asking: "Define rebuttal." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Define rebuttal.
  **A:** A response that answers and weakens a counterclaim.

**Assessment:** Argument analysis quiz.

**Answer key:** Argument-analysis quiz rubric (on the new passage, 4 points): names the author's claim (1); identifies the counterclaim the author acknowledges (1); identifies the rebuttal (1); judges whether the rebuttal is convincing with a reason (1). Shifts items: 'If I was mayor, I will fix the roads' → 'If I were mayor, I would fix the roads'; 'Stanton wrote the declaration, and it was signed by one hundred people' → 'Stanton wrote the declaration, and one hundred people signed it.'

## Mathematics 8: Equations with variables on both sides and rational coefficients

**Prepare:** Notebook; calculator for checking only.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying only the fraction terms by the LCD. Every term on both sides must be multiplied.
- Misplacing decimals when distributing, e.g., 0.4(x + 5) = 0.4x + 5. Remind: 0.4 × 5 = 2.

### Day 1 (Monday): Variables on both sides
*Student:* Solve 10 equations such as 7x - 4 = 3x + 12 and explain the strategy of collecting variable terms.

*How to teach:* Model collecting variable terms on the side with the larger coefficient to avoid negatives. Child solves 10.

- **Q:** Solve 7x − 4 = 3x + 12.
  **A:** 4x = 16, x = 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fractions in equations
*Student:* Clear denominators by multiplying by the LCD to solve 6 equations such as (2/3)x + 5 = (1/2)x + 7.

*How to teach:* Model multiplying every term by the LCD to clear fractions. Child solves 6.

- **Q:** Solve (2/3)x + 5 = (1/2)x + 7.
  **A:** Multiply by 6: 4x + 30 = 3x + 42, so x = 12. Check: 8 + 5 = 13 and 6 + 7 = 13.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decimals in equations
*Student:* Solve decimal equations like 0.4(x + 5) = 1.2x - 3 and check with a calculator.

*How to teach:* Child solves decimal equations (optionally multiply by 10 to clear decimals) and checks with a calculator. Check understanding with: "Solve 0.4(x + 5) = 1.2x − 3." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Solve 0.4(x + 5) = 1.2x − 3.
  **A:** 0.4x + 2 = 1.2x − 3 → 5 = 0.8x → x = 6.25.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing equations from stories
*Student:* Write and solve equations for 5 situations, e.g., two phone plans that cost the same at some number of minutes.

*How to teach:* Model defining the variable, writing each side as an expression, and setting them equal. Child writes and solves 5.

- **Q:** Plan A: $20 + $0.10/min. Plan B: $5 + $0.25/min. When do they cost the same?
  **A:** 20 + 0.1m = 5 + 0.25m → 15 = 0.15m → m = 100 minutes (both $30).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: 6 equations with rational coefficients and 2 word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Solve x/4 + 2 = x/3.
  **A:** Multiply by 12: 3x + 24 = 4x, so x = 24.

**Assessment:** Quiz on equations with rational coefficients.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 5x + 3 = 2x + 18 → x = 5. 2) x/4 + 2 = x/3 → x = 24. 3) 0.5x − 1.5 = 0.2x + 3 → x = 15. 4) (3/4)(x − 8) = x/2 → x = 24. 5) 1.5(2x + 4) = 4x − 1 → x = 7. 6) (1/5)x − 2 = (1/10)x + 1 → x = 30. 7) Taxi A: $3 + $2/mile; Taxi B: $5 + $1.50/mile → equal at 4 miles ($11). 8) Sam has $45 and saves $6/week; Ana has $81 and spends $3/week → equal after 4 weeks ($69).

## Science 8 (Integrated): Gravity, orbits and galaxies

**Prepare:** String, a soft ball or eraser, open outdoor space; PhET 'Gravity and Orbits'; NASA galaxy images (nasa.gov / hubblesite.org).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking there's no gravity in space. Astronauts in orbit are in free fall; gravity there is still about 90% of surface gravity.
- Thinking orbiting objects need a push to keep moving. Inertia keeps them moving; gravity curves the path.

### Day 1 (Monday): Orbit model
*Student:* Swing a ball on a string in a circle (outdoors) and release it to see inertia; relate the string to gravity.

*How to teach:* Swing the ball on a string in a circle and release it (outdoors, safely). Note it flies off in a straight line.

- **Q:** What does the string represent?
  **A:** Gravity — the inward pull that keeps the object curving.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gravity and Orbits sim
*Student:* Use PhET 'Gravity and Orbits' to turn gravity off and change masses; record effects on orbits.

*How to teach:* In the sim, turn gravity off and change masses; record effects. Before moving on, ask: "What happens when gravity is turned off?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What happens when gravity is turned off?
  **A:** The planet moves off in a straight line.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solar system formation
*Student:* Read about the solar nebula hypothesis and draw a 4-panel model of gravity forming the Sun and planets.

*How to teach:* Read about the solar nebula; draw a 4-panel model. Check understanding with: "How did the Sun form?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** How did the Sun form?
  **A:** Gravity pulled the center of a spinning gas cloud together until it was hot enough for fusion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Galaxies
*Student:* Explore NASA images of galaxies; explain how gravity holds billions of stars in the Milky Way.

*How to teach:* Explore galaxy images; explain gravity's role. To check, ask: "About how many stars are in the Milky Way?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** About how many stars are in the Milky Way?
  **A:** Roughly 100–400 billion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on orbits and gravity's role in the universe.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Finish by asking: "Why doesn't the Moon fly away?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why doesn't the Moon fly away?
  **A:** Earth's gravity pulls it inward.

**Assessment:** Formation model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Formation model rubric: 4 panels — a rotating cloud of gas and dust (solar nebula); gravity pulls it inward and it spins faster and flattens into a disk; the Sun forms in the hot center; planets form by accretion of particles. Quiz: an orbit results from inertia (forward motion) plus gravity (pull toward center); without gravity an orbiting object moves off in a straight line; the Milky Way is a spiral galaxy with hundreds of billions of stars held by gravity.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Reform movements

**Prepare:** Readings on Richard Allen and the AME Church, Horace Mann's arguments (12th Annual Report, 1848), the Declaration of Sentiments (1848), Thoreau's Walden and Emerson's 'Self-Reliance' excerpts. Write the unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Seneca Falls immediately won women the vote. It began the organized movement; national women's suffrage came with the 19th Amendment (1920).
- Assuming Northern free Black people had equal rights. They faced segregation, voting limits and discrimination, and built their own institutions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Free Black communities
*Student:* Read about Richard Allen and the AME Church and free Black schools in the North.

*How to teach:* Read about Richard Allen and free Black institutions in the North.

- **Q:** Why did Richard Allen found the AME Church?
  **A:** Black worshippers were forced into segregated seating and pulled from prayer at St. George's Methodist Church in Philadelphia, so Allen created an independent Black church (Bethel, 1794; AME denomination 1816).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Education reform
*Student:* Read Horace Mann's arguments for public schools.

*How to teach:* Read Horace Mann's arguments.

- **Q:** What did Horace Mann argue public schools would do?
  **A:** Create informed citizens and equal opportunity — education as 'the great equalizer' — funded by taxes and open to all children.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Women's rights
*Student:* Compare the Declaration of Sentiments (1848) with the Declaration of Independence.

*How to teach:* Compare the Declaration of Sentiments with the Declaration of Independence.

- **Q:** How did the Declaration of Sentiments change Jefferson's words?
  **A:** 'We hold these truths to be self-evident: that all men and women are created equal.'
- **Q:** Name one grievance in the Declaration of Sentiments.
  **A:** Women were denied the right to vote; married women lost property rights; women were barred from most colleges and professions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Transcendentalism
*Student:* Read excerpts from Thoreau's Walden and Emerson's 'Self-Reliance'; discuss individualism.

*How to teach:* Read Walden and 'Self-Reliance' excerpts; discuss individualism.

- **Q:** What did Thoreau seek at Walden Pond?
  **A:** To 'live deliberately,' simply and close to nature, to discover what truly matters.
- **Q:** What is Emerson's main message in 'Self-Reliance'?
  **A:** Trust yourself and your own judgment rather than conforming to society.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Unit test on foreign policy and the Northeast.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 4 test.

- **Q:** Who organized the Seneca Falls Convention?
  **A:** Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test (suggested): War of 1812 causes and outcome; Monroe Doctrine; Lowell mills; Erie Canal; American System; inventions; Irish/German immigration and nativism; Richard Allen founded the African Methodist Episcopal (AME) Church (independent denomination 1816) after Black worshippers were mistreated at St. George's Methodist Church; Horace Mann — Massachusetts education leader who argued for free, tax-supported common schools to create good citizens and equal opportunity ('the great equalizer'); Seneca Falls Convention (July 1848), Elizabeth Cady Stanton and Lucretia Mott, Declaration of Sentiments: 'all men and women are created equal' and demanded rights including voting; transcendentalism — truth found through nature, intuition and individual conscience (Emerson's 'Trust thyself'; Thoreau lived at Walden Pond 1845–47).

## Spanish 1B: De compras: shopping role-plays and culture

**Prepare:** Play money, clothing items. Read about the Chichicastenango market (Guatemala) and El Rastro (Madrid). A short video on Guatemalan Maya weaving (huipiles).

**Watch for:**
- Bargaining everywhere. Bargaining (regatear) is expected at many open-air markets and street stalls, not in department stores or fixed-price shops.
- Saying 'Me queda bueno.' Use 'Me queda bien' (it fits well); 'Me queda grande/pequeño' for size.

### Day 1 (Monday): Store phrases
*Student:* Learn ¿En qué puedo servirle?, ¿Qué talla usa?, Me queda grande/bien, and practice a fitting-room dialogue.

*How to teach:* Learn store phrases; practice a fitting-room dialogue.

- **Q:** A clerk asks '¿Qué talla usa?' What is she asking?
  **A:** What size do you wear?
- **Q:** Say: 'These pants are too big on me.'
  **A:** Estos pantalones me quedan grandes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Markets
*Student:* Read about markets such as Chichicastenango (Guatemala) and El Rastro (Madrid) and when bargaining is appropriate.

*How to teach:* Read about markets and when bargaining is appropriate.

- **Q:** Where is bargaining usually acceptable?
  **A:** At open-air markets and street stalls, not in department stores or chain shops.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Textiles
*Student:* Watch a short video on Guatemalan Maya weaving (huipiles) and write five sentences about the product and its meaning.

*How to teach:* Watch the weaving video; write five sentences about huipiles.

- **Q:** What is a huipil?
  **A:** A traditional handwoven tunic/blouse worn by Maya women; its patterns can show the weaver's village and beliefs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Role-play
*Student:* Role-play buying an outfit in a store and then bargaining for a souvenir in a market with play money.

*How to teach:* Role-play a store purchase and market bargaining with play money.

- **Q:** Bargain: 'It's very expensive. Can you give me a discount?'
  **A:** Es muy caro. ¿Me puede dar un descuento?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a shopping-dialogue listening quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the shopping listening quiz.

- **Q:** What does 'barato' mean?
  **A:** Cheap/inexpensive.

**Assessment:** Shopping role-play rubric and listening quiz.

**Answer key:** Shopping role-play rubric (1–4): clerk/customer phrases (¿En qué puedo servirle? ¿Qué talla usa? Me queda bien/grande), asks and states prices, uses demonstratives, bargains politely (¿Me puede dar un descuento? Es muy caro), closing. Listening quiz: identifies item, size, color, price and whether it fits from a dialogue. Culture: Chichicastenango — large Maya market in the Guatemalan highlands (Thursdays and Sundays) with textiles, masks, produce; El Rastro — Madrid's Sunday open-air flea market; huipil — handwoven blouse whose designs and colors often identify the weaver's community and carry symbolic meaning.

## Physical Education 8: Advanced volleyball: spike, block and rotation

**Prepare:** Volleyball (light trainer for beginners), net or rope at about 7'4" (or lower), cones.

**Watch for:**
- Hitting the ball with fists or open palms in bumps. Correct: form a flat platform with forearms, angle toward target.
- Running past the ball on the approach. Correct: take off behind the ball so contact is in front of the hitting shoulder.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review
*Student:* Review grade 7 forearm pass and set with 50 partner contacts each.

*How to teach:* 50 forearm passes and 50 sets with a partner.

- **Q:** Where does the ball contact the arms on a forearm pass?
  **A:** On the flat part of the forearms above the wrists, with arms straight and thumbs together.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Approach
*Student:* Learn the 3-step spike approach (left-right-left) and arm swing using a tossed ball.

*How to teach:* 3-step approach (right-hander: left-right-left), both arms swing back then up, high elbow, contact at the top.

- **Q:** Why swing both arms back on the approach?
  **A:** The arm swing adds momentum and height to the jump.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Block
*Student:* Practice the two-hand block footwork and timing at the net.

*How to teach:* Block: shuffle step, jump with hands up and over the net, press hands forward, land on both feet.

- **Q:** What should the blocker's hands do?
  **A:** Reach up and over the net, fingers spread, penetrating forward to deflect the ball down.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rotation
*Student:* Learn 6-player rotation and practice pass-set-hit sequences in small groups.

*How to teach:* Teach rotation (clockwise when gaining serve) and run pass-set-hit.

- **Q:** When does a team rotate?
  **A:** When it wins back the serve (side-out), players rotate one position clockwise.

### Day 5 (Friday): Game
*Student:* Play a modified game requiring three contacts before returning the ball.

*How to teach:* Modified game requiring three contacts.

- **Q:** Why require three contacts?
  **A:** It builds the pass-set-attack pattern used in real volleyball.

**Assessment:** Spike approach checklist.

**Answer key:** Spike approach checklist: correct 3-step footwork (L-R-L for right-handers), arms swing back on the last two steps, two-foot takeoff, high elbow 'bow and arrow' arm, contact at highest reach in front of hitting shoulder, wrist snap, two-foot landing. 5+/7 proficient.

## Health Education 8: Abstinence and preventing pregnancy

**Prepare:** Parent preview the CDC 'Contraception' effectiveness chart (typical-use failure rates). No demonstrations are included. Review a decision-making model (e.g., DECIDE: Define, Explore options, Consider consequences, Identify values, Decide, Evaluate). Opt-out alternative: decision-making on a non-sexual scenario.

**Watch for:**
- Believing any method is 100% effective. Correct: only abstinence is 100% effective at preventing pregnancy and STIs; other methods reduce risk by different amounts.
- Thinking hormonal birth control protects against STIs. Correct: only condoms (external/internal) reduce STI risk; hormonal methods and IUDs do not.

### Day 1 (Monday): Abstinence
*Student:* Discuss why abstinence is the only certain way to avoid pregnancy and STIs and reasons teens choose it. (Sexual health lesson: parents may opt out under CA Ed. Code 51938; parent previews materials.)

*How to teach:* Discuss what abstinence means and reasons teens choose it (values, health, goals, readiness). Share your family's view.

- **Q:** Why is abstinence the only certain way to prevent pregnancy and STIs?
  **A:** If there is no sexual contact, there is no exposure to sperm or to infections passed through sexual contact.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Methods overview
*Student:* Review a chart comparing the effectiveness of FDA-approved methods (no demonstrations).

*How to teach:* Review the chart and discuss typical-use effectiveness (it includes human error). Emphasize the difference between pregnancy and STI protection.

- **Q:** Which methods are most effective at preventing pregnancy with typical use?
  **A:** Implant and IUDs (over 99%); sterilization also over 99%.
- **Q:** Which method also reduces STI risk?
  **A:** Condoms (external or internal); typical-use pregnancy prevention for external condoms is about 87%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decision model
*Student:* Apply a decision-making model to a scenario about waiting to have sex.

*How to teach:* Walk through the decision model on a scenario where a teen is pressured to have sex and wants to wait.

- **Q:** Apply one step: what values and consequences should the teen consider?
  **A:** Personal and family values, goals, emotional readiness, risk of pregnancy and STIs, and the right to say no; a healthy partner respects the decision.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family talk
*Student:* Discuss family values about relationships and decisions with a parent.

*How to teach:* Have a private conversation about your family's values on relationships and decisions; the child may write a short reflection.

- **Q:** What is one value your family holds about relationships?
  **A:** Answers vary; check the child can state it and connect it to decisions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Answer short-response questions on abstinence and method effectiveness.

*How to teach:* Give short-response questions (key below).

- **Q:** Does a hormonal method protect against HIV?
  **A:** No. Only condoms reduce the risk of HIV and other STIs during sex; abstinence eliminates it.

**Assessment:** Short-response check.

**Answer key:** Short-response key: 1) abstinence is 100% effective at preventing pregnancy and STIs 2) reasons to choose abstinence: values, goals, not ready, avoid risks 3) IUD/implant are most effective reversible methods (>99%) 4) condoms are the only method that also reduce STI risk 5) typical use is lower than perfect use due to human error 6) a partner who pressures you is not respecting you; you can always say no. Proficient = 5 of 6 correct with explanations.
