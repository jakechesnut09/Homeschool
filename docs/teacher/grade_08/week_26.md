# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 26

## English Language Arts 8: The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 2, Scenes 4–5 and film comparison

**Prepare:** Read Act 2, Scenes 4–5. Arrange to watch selected scenes of the 1959 film 'The Diary of Anne Frank' (directed by George Stevens). Parental preview: the arrest scene and the epilogue revealing the deaths.

**Watch for:**
- Reading Anne's 'good at heart' line as naive. Discuss it as a choice to hold on to hope despite evidence.
- Judging the film only by faithfulness. Ask what each director's choice adds or loses.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scene 4
*Student:* Read the discovery scene and analyze Anne's line 'In spite of everything, I still believe that people are really good at heart.'

*How to teach:* Read Act 2, Scene 4 aloud, including the tense ringing phone and the arrival of the police. The child writes what Anne's 'good at heart' line reveals and why the playwrights placed it just before the arrest.

- **Q:** What does 'In spite of everything, I still believe that people are really good at heart' reveal?
  **A:** Anne's hope and faith in humanity, even while persecuted — the play's central theme.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Scene 5
*Student:* Read the epilogue scene and discuss how it frames the play.

*How to teach:* Read the final scene. Discuss how returning to November 1945 frames the whole play as Mr. Frank's memory and why he says 'She puts me to shame.'

- **Q:** How does Scene 5 connect to Act 1, Scene 1?
  **A:** Both are set in November 1945 with Mr. Frank; the play is framed as his memory of reading the diary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Film comparison
*Student:* Watch selected film scenes and chart differences from the script, explaining the effect of each choice.

*How to teach:* Watch 2–3 selected scenes of the 1959 film with the script open. The child charts what the director kept, changed or added and the effect of each choice.

- **Q:** Who directed the 1959 film?
  **A:** George Stevens.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme essay plan
*Student:* Plan an essay on the play's theme of hope amid persecution.

*How to teach:* Model a theme thesis. The child plans an essay with thesis, three supporting points and two quotations for each.

- **Q:** Example thesis?
  **A:** 'Through Anne's choices, the play shows that hope can survive even the cruelest persecution.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the play and film comparison chart.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on the play and collect the film comparison chart. Review any missed questions with the script.

- **Q:** Where did Anne die?
  **A:** Bergen-Belsen concentration camp, early 1945.

**Assessment:** Film comparison chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Film comparison chart rubric: ≥4 differences between script and film, each with the effect on the audience (1 point each). Quiz: Scene 4 — the phone rings repeatedly below; Anne tells Peter she still believes people are good at heart; the Green Police/Gestapo arrive; Mr. Frank says they can now live in hope rather than fear. Scene 5 — November 1945, Mr. Frank tells Miep and Kraler that all the others died, Anne at Bergen-Belsen; he reads Anne's line and says 'She puts me to shame.'

## Mathematics 8: Zero, negative exponents and different bases

**Prepare:** Notebook; make a 20-card exponent sort (10 pairs of equivalent expressions).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a negative exponent makes a number negative. 10⁻² = 1/100, a small positive number.
- Thinking a⁰ = 0. Use the pattern (each step down divides by a) to show a⁰ = 1.

### Day 1 (Monday): Zero exponents
*Student:* Use a pattern table (10^3, 10^2, 10^1, ...) to justify 10^0 = 1; extend to other bases.

*How to teach:* Table: 10³ = 1000, 10² = 100, 10¹ = 10; each step divides by 10, so 10⁰ = 1. Repeat with base 2.

- **Q:** Why is 10⁰ = 1?
  **A:** Each time the exponent drops by 1 we divide by 10; 10¹ ÷ 10 = 1.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Negative exponents
*Student:* Continue the pattern to 10^(-1), 10^(-2); rewrite 10 expressions with positive exponents.

*How to teach:* Continue the pattern below 0. Child rewrites 10 expressions with positive exponents.

- **Q:** Evaluate 10⁻² and 2⁻³.
  **A:** 1/100 and 1/8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Different bases
*Student:* Use (ab)^n = a^n b^n to simplify expressions like 2^3 x 5^3 = 10^3; evaluate 8 expressions.

*How to teach:* Show 2³ × 5³ = (2·5)(2·5)(2·5) = 10³. Child evaluates 8 expressions.

- **Q:** Evaluate 2³ × 5³ and 4² × 25².
  **A:** 10³ = 1,000 and 100² = 10,000.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mixed practice
*Student:* Complete a 20-problem mixed exponent card sort, matching equivalent expressions.

*How to teach:* Child completes the 20-card sort matching equivalents. Finish by asking: "Simplify 3⁻² × 3⁵." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Simplify 3⁻² × 3⁵.
  **A:** 3³ = 27.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: evaluate and simplify 12 expressions with zero and negative exponents.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Simplify 6⁴ ÷ 6⁶.
  **A:** 6⁻² = 1/36.

**Assessment:** Quiz on integer exponents.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 7⁰ = 1. 2) 10⁻² = 1/100. 3) 2⁻³ = 1/8. 4) 5⁻¹ = 1/5. 5) 3⁻² × 3⁵ = 27. 6) 2³ × 5³ = 1,000. 7) 4⁻² = 1/16. 8) (10⁻¹)² = 10⁻² = 1/100. 9) 6⁴ ÷ 6⁶ = 6⁻² = 1/36. 10) (−3)⁰ = 1. 11) 2⁻² × 2⁻¹ = 1/8. 12) (3 × 4)² = 144 = 3² × 4².

## Science 8 (Integrated): Genes, chromosomes and Punnett squares

**Prepare:** Coins (2 per child), paper, colored pencils. A summary of Mendel's pea data (e.g., 705 purple : 224 white flowers in F2).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking dominant means most common. Dominant means it shows when present; some dominant traits are rare.
- Mixing up genotype and phenotype. Genotype = alleles (Bb); phenotype = observable trait (brown).

### Day 1 (Monday): Mendel's peas
*Student:* Read about Gregor Mendel's pea experiments and analyze his F1 and F2 data.

*How to teach:* Read about Mendel; analyze F1 and F2 data. Before moving on, ask: "Mendel crossed purple × white; F1 all purple; F2 705 purple : 224 white. Ratio?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Mendel crossed purple × white; F1 all purple; F2 705 purple : 224 white. Ratio?
  **A:** About 3:1.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Alleles
*Student:* Model alleles with two coins (heads = dominant); flip 50 times to predict offspring ratios.

*How to teach:* Flip two coins 50 times to model offspring. Check understanding with: "What fraction of flips should be two tails (recessive)?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What fraction of flips should be two tails (recessive)?
  **A:** About 1/4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Punnett squares
*Student:* Complete 10 Punnett squares and give phenotype and genotype ratios.

*How to teach:* Complete 10 Punnett squares. To check, ask: "Cross Bb × bb." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Cross Bb × bb.
  **A:** 1/2 Bb, 1/2 bb.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family traits survey
*Student:* Survey family members for traits like attached earlobes and tongue rolling; consider limits of single-gene models.

*How to teach:* Survey family traits; discuss limits. Finish by asking: "Why might single-gene models not fit?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why might single-gene models not fit?
  **A:** Many traits involve several genes and environment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Punnett squares and genetics vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Before moving on, ask: "Genotype vs phenotype?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Genotype vs phenotype?
  **A:** Genotype: alleles; phenotype: observable trait.

**Assessment:** Punnett square quiz.

**Answer key:** Punnett square quiz: 1) Bb × Bb → 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb; 3 dominant : 1 recessive phenotype. 2) BB × bb → all Bb; all dominant. 3) Bb × bb → 1 Bb : 1 bb; 1:1. 4) Define allele, genotype, phenotype, homozygous, heterozygous. 5) Probability of bb from Bb × Bb = 25%.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Turning points and home fronts

**Prepare:** Maps of Gettysburg and Vicksburg (1863); Gettysburg Address text (November 19, 1863); readings on Clara Barton and the New York draft riots (July 1863); Brady and Gardner photographs.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking photographs were taken during combat. Long exposure times meant photos showed camps, portraits and aftermath; some (like Gardner's 'Home of a Rebel Sharpshooter') were staged.
- Thinking the Gettysburg Address was a long speech. It was about 270 words and took about two minutes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gettysburg and Vicksburg
*Student:* Map both 1863 campaigns and explain how they changed the war in the East and the West.

*How to teach:* Map both 1863 campaigns; explain their effect.

- **Q:** Why were Gettysburg and Vicksburg turning points?
  **A:** Gettysburg ended Lee's last major invasion of the North; Vicksburg gave the Union control of the Mississippi River, splitting the Confederacy in two.
- **Q:** When did Vicksburg surrender?
  **A:** July 4, 1863.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gettysburg Address
*Student:* Close-read the Gettysburg Address and connect 'a new birth of freedom' to the Declaration of Independence.

*How to teach:* Close-read the Gettysburg Address; connect to the Declaration.

- **Q:** What year does 'four score and seven years ago' refer to?
  **A:** 1776 (87 years before 1863) — the Declaration of Independence.
- **Q:** What did Lincoln mean by 'a new birth of freedom'?
  **A:** The war would renew the nation's commitment to the Declaration's ideal of equality, now including ending slavery.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Home fronts
*Student:* Read about Clara Barton and the New York draft riots and compare civilian hardships North and South.

*How to teach:* Read about Clara Barton and the draft riots; compare civilian hardships.

- **Q:** Why did the New York draft riots break out?
  **A:** Anger at the draft, especially the $300 fee that let wealthier men avoid service, mixed with racism; rioters attacked Black New Yorkers.
- **Q:** What hardship did Southern civilians face?
  **A:** Food shortages, high inflation, the Union blockade and bread riots.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Technology and photography
*Student:* Analyze Brady and Gardner battlefield photographs and list new technologies (rifled musket, ironclads, telegraph).

*How to teach:* Analyze Brady and Gardner photographs; list new technologies.

- **Q:** How did battlefield photographs change public views of war?
  **A:** They showed the dead on battlefields to civilians for the first time, making the war's cost real.
- **Q:** Name two new military technologies.
  **A:** Rifled muskets with Minié balls, ironclad warships, telegraph, railroads, observation balloons.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on turning points and the home front.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Who was Clara Barton?
  **A:** A Civil War nurse who later founded the American Red Cross.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Gettysburg (July 1–3, 1863) — Lee's invasion of the North defeated (Pickett's Charge failed); Vicksburg surrendered to Grant on July 4, 1863, giving the Union control of the Mississippi and splitting the Confederacy; Gettysburg Address — 'Four score and seven years ago' (87 years earlier = 1776, linking to the Declaration), 'a new birth of freedom', 'government of the people, by the people, for the people'; home fronts — Northern draft (1863 Enrollment Act; $300 commutation fee) sparked New York draft riots in which mobs attacked Black residents and burned the Colored Orphan Asylum; Southern shortages, inflation and bread riots (Richmond, 1863); Clara Barton nursed soldiers ('Angel of the Battlefield') and later founded the American Red Cross (1881); new technology — rifled muskets and Minié balls, ironclads (Monitor vs Virginia, March 1862), telegraph, railroads, photography.

## Spanish 1B: Irregular preterite: hacer and decir

**Prepare:** StudySpanish.com irregular preterite pages. Read a bilingual short biography of César Chávez and Dolores Huerta.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 'hico.' The él form is hizo (z keeps the 'th/s' sound).
- Adding -ieron to decir: 'dijieron.' It's dijeron.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hacer
*Student:* Learn hice, hiciste, hizo (z spelling), hicimos, hicieron and answer ¿Qué hiciste ayer? in 8 ways.

*How to teach:* Teach hacer preterite; answer ¿Qué hiciste ayer? 8 ways.

- **Q:** Conjugate hacer for él.
  **A:** hizo.
- **Q:** Answer: ¿Qué hiciste ayer? (I made a cake)
  **A:** Hice un pastel.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decir
*Student:* Learn dije, dijiste, dijo, dijimos, dijeron and report what five people said using IOPs: Me dijo que...

*How to teach:* Teach decir; report what five people said with IOPs.

- **Q:** Write: 'My friend told me that he went to the beach.'
  **A:** Mi amigo me dijo que fue a la playa.
- **Q:** Conjugate decir for ellos.
  **A:** dijeron.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice
*Student:* Complete StudySpanish.com irregular preterite practice for hacer and decir.

*How to teach:* Complete StudySpanish practice.

- **Q:** Conjugate hacer for nosotros.
  **A:** hicimos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Culture: service
*Student:* Read about César Chávez and Dolores Huerta's community organizing in California and write five preterite sentences about what they did.

*How to teach:* Read about Chávez and Huerta; write five preterite sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'César Chávez helped the farm workers.'
  **A:** César Chávez ayudó a los trabajadores del campo.
- **Q:** What slogan did Dolores Huerta create?
  **A:** '¡Sí, se puede!' (Yes, we can!)

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an irregular preterite quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the irregular preterite quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'What did they say?'
  **A:** ¿Qué dijeron?

**Assessment:** Hacer/decir preterite quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: hacer — hice, hiciste, hizo, hicimos, hicisteis, hicieron; decir — dije, dijiste, dijo, dijimos, dijisteis, dijeron (no accents). Samples: ¿Qué (hacer) tú ayer? → hiciste; Ella me (decir) la verdad → dijo; Nosotros (hacer) la tarea → hicimos; Ellos nos (decir) 'gracias' → dijeron. Culture: César Chávez and Dolores Huerta co-founded the National Farm Workers Association (1962, later United Farm Workers), led the Delano grape strike and boycott (1965–1970) using nonviolence; Huerta coined '¡Sí, se puede!' 80% proficient.

*How to teach:* Map four 8-count sections with fitness moves.

## Health Education 8: Heat, water and wildfire smoke emergencies

**Prepare:** Bookmark AirNow.gov and a first-aid guide (Red Cross app). Learn rip-current escape. Write a scenario quiz (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heat stroke victims always sweat. Correct: in heat stroke, skin may be hot and dry or still sweaty; confusion and body temperature of 103°F+ are key — call 911.
- Swimming straight back to shore against a rip current. Correct: swim parallel to shore until out of the current, or float and signal for help.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heat illness
*Student:* Compare heat exhaustion and heat stroke and first aid for each.

*How to teach:* Compare heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, cool clammy skin, nausea, dizziness) and heat stroke (high body temp, confusion, hot skin, possible fainting). First aid: move to cool place, loosen clothing, cool with water; heat stroke = call 911 and cool rapidly.

- **Q:** What's the main difference and what do you do for heat stroke?
  **A:** Heat stroke involves very high body temperature and confusion/altered mental status; it is a medical emergency — call 911 and cool the person rapidly (cold water, ice packs).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water rescue
*Student:* Learn reach-throw-don't go and rip current escape.

*How to teach:* Teach reach-throw-don't go: reach with a pole or towel, throw a flotation device, don't swim out. Show how a rip current looks and how to escape.

- **Q:** How do you escape a rip current?
  **A:** Stay calm, don't fight it; swim parallel to the shore until out of the current, then swim back at an angle, or float/tread water and wave for help.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Air quality
*Student:* Check the AQI on AirNow and learn when to limit outdoor activity.

*How to teach:* Check today's AQI on AirNow and learn the color scale (0–50 good, 51–100 moderate, 101–150 unhealthy for sensitive groups, 151+ unhealthy).

- **Q:** At what AQI should sensitive groups limit outdoor activity?
  **A:** Above 100 (orange, unhealthy for sensitive groups); above 150 everyone should reduce outdoor exertion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan
*Student:* Write a family plan for a smoke day or heat wave.

*How to teach:* The child writes a family plan: cooling spots, water, check on elderly neighbors, close windows, HEPA/air purifier, N95 masks, indoor activities.

- **Q:** Name two steps for a wildfire smoke day.
  **A:** Stay indoors with windows closed, run a HEPA air purifier or AC on recirculate, wear a well-fitted N95 outdoors, avoid exercise outside.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a scenario quiz on heat, water and smoke emergencies.

*How to teach:* Give the scenario quiz.

- **Q:** A teammate is confused and has hot skin after practice in the heat. What do you do?
  **A:** Suspect heat stroke: call 911, move them to shade, cool them rapidly with water/ice, and stay with them.

**Assessment:** Scenario quiz.

**Answer key:** Scenario quiz key: 1) heavy sweating, nausea, dizziness = heat exhaustion: move to cool place, fluids, rest 2) confusion and very high temp = heat stroke: 911 + rapid cooling 3) friend struggling in a pool: reach or throw, don't go 4) caught in rip current: swim parallel to shore 5) AQI 160: everyone limit outdoor activity 6) smoke day: stay inside, HEPA, N95 outdoors. 5/6 proficient.
