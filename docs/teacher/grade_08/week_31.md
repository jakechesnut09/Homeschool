# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 31

## English Language Arts 8: Act 5: the play within a play and comparison essay

**Prepare:** Read Act 5 and reread Ovid's 'Pyramus and Thisbe.' Gather simple props (a lantern for Moonshine, a sign for Wall, a toy sword).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking parody means mockery of the original story's value. Shakespeare parodies bad acting and over-the-top tragedy while honoring the source.
- Listing differences without explaining effects. Always add 'which makes the audience...'

### Day 1 (Monday): Act 5
*Student:* Read the mechanicals' performance of 'Pyramus and Thisbe' and list ways it parodies Ovid's tragic tone (the Wall, Moonshine, the Lion).

*How to teach:* Read Act 5's 'Pyramus and Thisbe' aloud with Ovid's version beside it. The child lists ways it parodies Ovid's tragic tone (Wall, Moonshine, the Lion, Pyramus's overlong death).

- **Q:** How do the Wall and Moonshine parody Ovid?
  **A:** Actors literally play the wall (Snout holding up fingers for the chink) and moonlight (Starveling with a lantern, thorn bush and dog), turning tragic setting into silliness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparison chart
*Student:* Chart what Shakespeare keeps from Ovid and what he changes and explain how the material is 'rendered new.'

*How to teach:* The child charts what Shakespeare keeps from Ovid and what he changes, adding the effect of each change on the audience. To check, ask: "What is kept from Ovid?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What is kept from Ovid?
  **A:** The wall, the meeting at Ninus's tomb, the lion, the bloody mantle, the double suicide.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Essay draft
*Student:* Draft a 4-paragraph comparison essay on how Shakespeare transforms Ovid's myth, with quotations from both texts.

*How to teach:* Model a thesis about transformation. The child drafts a 4-paragraph essay with quotations from both texts.

- **Q:** What does 'rendered new' mean?
  **A:** Turning old material into a new form with new purpose.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Performance
*Student:* Rehearse and perform a 2-minute scene (Bottom as Pyramus or Puck's epilogue) with props and clear diction.

*How to teach:* The child rehearses Bottom as Pyramus or Puck's epilogue with simple props, then performs for family with clear diction and gestures. Before moving on, ask: "Puck's epilogue begins?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Puck's epilogue begins?
  **A:** 'If we shadows have offended, / Think but this, and all is mended.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the unit test on myth and Shakespeare and submit the revised essay.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test on myth and Shakespeare and collect the revised essay. Check understanding with: "Define play within a play." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Define play within a play.
  **A:** A performance staged inside a play.

**Assessment:** Comparison essay (rubric), performance and Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Comparison essay rubric (4 levels): thesis on how Shakespeare transforms Ovid's tragedy into comedy; evidence from both texts (e.g., Ovid's crack in the wall vs Snout playing Wall and holding up his fingers as the 'chink'; Ovid's terrifying lioness vs Snug reassuring the ladies he's just a joiner; Pyramus's death 'Now die, die, die, die, die'); commentary; conventions. Performance rubric: memorized or fluent, clear diction, gestures, props. Unit 7 test: myth terms, play events, iambic pentameter, dramatic irony, parody.

## Mathematics 8: Applying the Pythagorean theorem

**Prepare:** A rectangular box (shoe box), tape measure, straw or string, graph paper. Measure a TV's width, height and diagonal.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to square-root at the end. a² + b² gives c², not c.
- Using the face diagonal instead of the space diagonal in 3-D. Do two steps: face diagonal first, then use it with the height.

### Day 1 (Monday): Real-world right triangles
*Student:* Solve problems with ladders, TV screen diagonals and ramps; measure a TV to check its advertised diagonal.

*How to teach:* Model the ladder problem and measure a TV. Check understanding with: "A 13-ft ladder's base is 5 ft from the wall. How high does it reach?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** A 13-ft ladder's base is 5 ft from the wall. How high does it reach?
  **A:** √(169 − 25) = 12 ft.
- **Q:** A TV screen is 48 × 27 in. Diagonal?
  **A:** √(2304 + 729) = √3033 ≈ 55.1 in (a '55-inch' TV).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): 3-D diagonals
*Student:* Find the longest straight object that fits in a shoe box by computing the space diagonal; verify with a measured straw or string.

*How to teach:* Compute the shoe box's space diagonal in two steps and check with a straw. To check, ask: "Box 30 × 18 × 12 cm. Space diagonal?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Box 30 × 18 × 12 cm. Space diagonal?
  **A:** √(30² + 18² + 12²) = √1368 ≈ 37.0 cm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distance on the coordinate plane
*Student:* Draw the right triangle under segments between points and find 8 distances.

*How to teach:* Draw the right triangle under the segment; legs are differences of coordinates. Finish by asking: "Distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6)?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Distance from (1, 2) to (4, 6)?
  **A:** Legs 3 and 4, distance 5.
- **Q:** Distance from (−2, 1) to (3, −3)?
  **A:** √(25 + 16) = √41 ≈ 6.40.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mixed applications
*Student:* Solve 6 multi-step problems such as walking diagonally across a park vs around it.

*How to teach:* Child solves 6 multi-step problems. Before moving on, ask: "A park is 300 m by 400 m. How much shorter is walking the diagonal than walking around two sides?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** A park is 300 m by 400 m. How much shorter is walking the diagonal than walking around two sides?
  **A:** Diagonal 500 m vs 700 m: 200 m shorter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: 3 real-world, 2 three-dimensional and 3 coordinate distance problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Space diagonal of a 10-cm cube?
  **A:** √300 ≈ 17.3 cm.

**Assessment:** Quiz on Pythagorean applications.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 13-ft ladder, base 5 ft from wall → 12 ft high. 2) TV 48 × 27 in → √3033 ≈ 55.1 in. 3) Ramp 12 ft long, rises 2 ft → horizontal √140 ≈ 11.8 ft. 4) Box 30 × 18 × 12 cm → √1368 ≈ 37.0 cm. 5) Cube 10 cm → √300 ≈ 17.3 cm. 6) (1, 2) to (4, 6) → 5. 7) (−2, 1) to (3, −3) → √41 ≈ 6.40. 8) (0, 0) to (−6, 8) → 10.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Natural selection in numbers

**Prepare:** PhET 'Natural Selection' sim, graph paper, calculator; HHMI BioInteractive film 'The Making of the Fittest: Natural Selection and Adaptation' (rock pocket mouse, free online).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a mutation appears because the environment needs it. Mutations are random; selection acts on them.
- Graphing raw counts when populations change size. Use percentages.

### Day 1 (Monday): PhET Natural Selection
*Student:* Add a mutation (brown fur) and a selection factor (wolves) and record population data for 10 generations.

*How to teach:* Run PhET with brown fur and wolves for 10 generations. Check understanding with: "In the equator environment with wolves, which fur increases?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** In the equator environment with wolves, which fur increases?
  **A:** Brown.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Graphing trait frequency
*Student:* Convert counts to percentages and graph trait frequency over time.

*How to teach:* Convert counts to percentages; graph. To check, ask: "12 brown of 40 total — percent?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** 12 brown of 40 total — percent?
  **A:** 30%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rock pocket mice
*Student:* Watch the HHMI 'Making of the Fittest: Rock Pocket Mouse' film and analyze its data.

*How to teach:* Watch the rock pocket mouse film. Finish by asking: "Why are dark mice common on lava?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why are dark mice common on lava?
  **A:** Camouflage from owls.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Predicting change
*Student:* Calculate how a 10% survival advantage changes trait frequency over 5 generations.

*How to teach:* Calculate a 10% survival advantage over 5 generations. Before moving on, ask: "Start 10%; trait carriers survive 1.1× as well. After 5 generations?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Start 10%; trait carriers survive 1.1× as well. After 5 generations?
  **A:** About 15% (10.9%, 11.9%, 12.9%, 14.0%, 15.2%).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz with graph interpretation questions.

*How to teach:* Give the graph quiz. Check understanding with: "What is selective pressure?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What is selective pressure?
  **A:** An environmental factor that affects survival.

**Assessment:** Graph analysis quiz.

**Answer key:** Graph analysis quiz: read trait percentages; identify selective pressure; in PhET with wolves, brown fur increases in the brown (equator) environment and white fur in the arctic; rock pocket mice: dark mice increase on dark lava rock because owls see light mice; 10% advantage from 10% → about 15% after 5 generations.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Railroads and the West

**Prepare:** Railroad and land-grant maps; readings on Chinese workers (Central Pacific) and Irish workers (Union Pacific); Homestead Act (1862) text; Chief Joseph's surrender speech (1877); accounts of Little Bighorn (1876) and Wounded Knee (1890) from Lakota and Army perspectives; Dawes Act summary; Carlisle Indian School before-and-after photos (e.g., Tom Torlino). Parent preview: violence and cultural loss.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Little Bighorn was a 'massacre' of soldiers. It was a battle in which Lakota, Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors defeated Custer's 7th Cavalry; Wounded Knee (1890) was the massacre of mostly unarmed Lakota, including women and children.
- Believing the Dawes Act helped Native Americans by giving them land. It broke up communal reservation land; 'surplus' land was sold to settlers, and Native landholdings fell from about 138 million acres (1887) to about 48 million (1934).

### Day 1 (Monday): Transcontinental railroad
*Student:* Read about Chinese workers on Leland Stanford's Central Pacific and Irish workers on the Union Pacific; map the 1869 route.

*How to teach:* Read about railroad workers; map the 1869 route.

- **Q:** Where did the two railroads meet, and when?
  **A:** Promontory Summit, Utah, on May 10, 1869.
- **Q:** What role did Chinese workers play?
  **A:** They made up most of the Central Pacific's workforce, blasting tunnels through the Sierra Nevada in dangerous conditions for less pay than white workers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Homestead Act
*Student:* Read the Homestead Act (1862) and analyze settlement maps of the Great Plains.

*How to teach:* Read the Homestead Act; analyze settlement maps.

- **Q:** What did the Homestead Act offer?
  **A:** 160 acres of public land to settlers who lived on and farmed it for five years (paying a small filing fee).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plains wars
*Student:* Read Chief Joseph's surrender speech and accounts of Little Bighorn and Wounded Knee from multiple perspectives.

*How to teach:* Read Chief Joseph's speech and multiple-perspective accounts of Little Bighorn and Wounded Knee.

- **Q:** Quote Chief Joseph's famous line.
  **A:** 'From where the sun now stands I will fight no more forever.'
- **Q:** Why is Wounded Knee called a massacre?
  **A:** U.S. soldiers killed about 250–300 Lakota, many unarmed women and children, while disarming them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dawes Act and boarding schools
*Student:* Explain the Dawes Act's allotment policy and analyze before-and-after photographs from Carlisle Indian School.

*How to teach:* Explain allotment; analyze Carlisle photographs.

- **Q:** What was the goal of the Dawes Act?
  **A:** To assimilate Native Americans by breaking up tribal land into individual farms; it led to massive loss of Native land.
- **Q:** What do before-and-after Carlisle photos show?
  **A:** Students' traditional clothing and long hair replaced with uniforms and short hair — the schools' goal of erasing Native culture.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the West, railroads and Indian policy.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What was the transcontinental railroad's effect on Plains nations?
  **A:** It brought settlers and hunters who destroyed the bison herds, splitting and undermining Plains nations' way of life.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Pacific Railway Act (1862); Central Pacific built east from Sacramento with thousands of Chinese workers (most of its workforce), who did dangerous Sierra Nevada tunneling for lower pay; Union Pacific built west from Omaha with many Irish workers and Civil War veterans; joined with the golden spike at Promontory Summit, Utah, May 10, 1869; Homestead Act — 160 acres free to citizens or intending citizens (21+ or heads of household, including women and later freedpeople) who lived on and improved it for 5 years; Little Bighorn (June 1876) — Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse's warriors defeated Custer; Nez Perce — Chief Joseph surrendered October 1877 ('I will fight no more forever'); Wounded Knee (December 29, 1890) — U.S. troops killed about 250–300 Lakota; Dawes Act (1887) — allotted 160 acres to family heads, sold surplus land, aimed at assimilation; boarding schools (Carlisle 1879, Richard Pratt: 'Kill the Indian… save the man') forbade Native languages and cultures.

## Spanish 1B: La tecnología y la computadora

**Prepare:** Computer and phone for labeling; Conjuguemos e→i practice. Vocabulary: la computadora portátil, el teléfono celular, la pantalla, el teclado, el ratón, la impresora, el cargador, los audífonos, la red, navegar en la red, el correo electrónico, el sitio web, la contraseña, bajar/descargar, subir, la aplicación, el mensaje de texto, la cámara.

**Watch for:**
- Treating pedir as e→ie: 'piedo.' Pedir and servir are e→i: pido, sirve.
- Forgetting preterite stem change in third person for -ir stem-changers: pidió, pidieron; sirvió, sirvieron.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tech vocabulary
*Student:* Learn 18 technology words and label the parts of a computer and phone.

*How to teach:* Learn 18 tech words; label a computer and phone.

- **Q:** How do you say 'password' and 'to download'?
  **A:** la contraseña; bajar (descargar).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pedir & servir
*Student:* Learn the e→i boot pattern (pido, sirve) and complete a Conjuguemos drill, including preterite pidió/sirvió.

*How to teach:* Teach e→i boot; Conjuguemos drill including pidió/sirvió.

- **Q:** Conjugate pedir (present) for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** pido; pedimos.
- **Q:** Conjugate pedir (preterite) for él.
  **A:** pidió.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): ¿Para qué sirve?
*Student:* Explain what 8 devices or apps are for: La computadora sirve para escribir.

*How to teach:* Explain what 8 devices are for with servir para.

- **Q:** Write: 'The phone is used to send messages.'
  **A:** El teléfono sirve para mandar mensajes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Survey
*Student:* Survey family about daily screen use and write a report with numbers.

*How to teach:* Survey family about daily screen use; write a report with numbers.

- **Q:** Write: 'My mom uses the computer three hours a day.'
  **A:** Mi mamá usa la computadora tres horas al día.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a technology vocabulary and e→i verb quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the technology and e→i quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'The waiter served the food.'
  **A:** El camarero sirvió la comida.

**Assessment:** Technology and e→i quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: pedir present — pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden; preterite — pedí, pediste, pidió, pedimos, pedisteis, pidieron. Servir present — sirvo, sirves, sirve, servimos, servís, sirven; preterite third person sirvió, sirvieron. Samples: ¿Para qué sirve la impresora? → Sirve para imprimir; Mi hermano pidió un celular nuevo; Las computadoras sirven para escribir. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Stop-motion animation: production

**Prepare:** Stop Motion Studio, figures, set, steady lighting (close curtains to avoid flicker), free music/sound effects (YouTube Audio Library or recorded).

**Watch for:**
- Flickering from changing daylight. Correct: use lamps and block window light.
- Using copyrighted music for a shared video. Correct: use royalty-free or original sound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shooting 1
*Student:* Shoot the first half of the animation.

*How to teach:* Shoot first half.

- **Q:** Why use onion-skinning?
  **A:** It shows the previous frame faintly so you can make small, consistent moves.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shooting 2
*Student:* Shoot the second half.

*How to teach:* Shoot second half.

- **Q:** How many frames should you have by the end?
  **A:** About 360 for 30 seconds at 12 fps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Editing & sound
*Student:* Edit the frames and add music or sound effects.

*How to teach:* Edit frames and add sound.

- **Q:** Why might you duplicate frames?
  **A:** To hold a pose longer (a pause) without reshooting.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Titles & credits
*Student:* Add titles and credits.

*How to teach:* Add titles and credits.

- **Q:** What should credits include?
  **A:** Title, creator, helpers and music sources.

### Day 5 (Friday): Premiere
*Student:* Screen the animation for family and collect feedback.

*How to teach:* Screen and collect feedback.

- **Q:** What feedback would help most?
  **A:** Was the story clear? Was the motion smooth? What was most memorable?

**Assessment:** Completed animation.

**Answer key:** Completed animation: 4 = ~30 seconds, clear story following storyboard, smooth motion, steady lighting, sound and titles/credits; 3 = minor jumps or flicker; 2 = short or unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Track meet and pacing strategy

**Prepare:** Stopwatch, cones, measuring wheel or a local track, event cards, grade 7 marks.

**Watch for:**
- Starting the 800 m too fast. Correct: run even splits (roughly equal 200 m times).
- Not warming up before sprints. Correct: dynamic warm-up and strides prevent hamstring pulls.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pacing
*Student:* Run 4 x 200 m at goal 800 m pace with rest, checking split times.

*How to teach:* Run 4×200 at goal 800 pace with rest, checking splits.

- **Q:** If your 800 m goal is 3:20, what is the target 200 m split?
  **A:** 3:20 = 200 s ÷ 4 = 50 seconds per 200 m.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Meet plan
*Student:* Choose three events and set a goal for each.

*How to teach:* Choose three events and set goals.

- **Q:** Why set goals based on past marks?
  **A:** They are realistic and measurable.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Meet day 1
*Student:* Compete in sprint and relay events.

*How to teach:* Sprints and relay.

- **Q:** What starting position is used in sprints?
  **A:** Crouch/block start or standing start with weight forward; drive out low.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Meet day 2
*Student:* Compete in the 800 m, triple jump and put; record results.

*How to teach:* 800 m, triple jump, put; record.

- **Q:** Why run even pace in the 800 m?
  **A:** It avoids early fatigue from lactate buildup and usually produces the fastest time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Compare results to goals and grade 7 marks; write a personal-record reflection.

*How to teach:* Reflect vs. goals and grade 7.

- **Q:** What is a personal record (PR)?
  **A:** Your best-ever performance in an event.

**Assessment:** Track meet results and reflection.

**Answer key:** Track meet results and reflection: 4 = all results recorded with units, compared to goals and grade 7, explains reasons (pacing, technique, training) and sets next goal; 3 = comparison without much reasoning; 2 = results only; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Chronic disease risk factors

**Prepare:** Print a family health history form (CDC 'My Family Health Portrait') and CDC fact sheets on heart disease, type 2 diabetes, cancer and asthma. Be ready to share family history sensitively.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking chronic diseases only affect old people. Correct: habits started in youth (diet, activity, tobacco) shape lifetime risk; type 2 diabetes and asthma occur in teens.
- Believing family history means you're destined to get a disease. Correct: it raises risk, but controllable behaviors can lower it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grade 7 link
*Student:* Review noncommunicable diseases from grade 7 and list examples.

*How to teach:* Review communicable vs. noncommunicable diseases and list chronic examples.

- **Q:** Name four chronic noncommunicable diseases.
  **A:** Heart disease, type 2 diabetes, cancer, asthma (also stroke, COPD, arthritis).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Risk factors
*Student:* Sort risk factors into controllable and uncontrollable.

*How to teach:* Sort risk factors: controllable (diet, activity, tobacco, sleep, sun exposure) vs. uncontrollable (age, genetics, family history, sex).

- **Q:** Is family history controllable?
  **A:** No — but knowing it helps you control other risk factors and get screenings.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family history
*Student:* Complete a family health history with a parent.

*How to teach:* Fill out the family health history together for parents, grandparents, siblings.

- **Q:** Why share family health history with a doctor?
  **A:** It helps the doctor assess risk and recommend earlier screening or prevention.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prevention
*Student:* Research one chronic disease and create a prevention plan.

*How to teach:* The child researches one disease and writes a prevention plan with 3 behaviors.

- **Q:** Name two ways to lower type 2 diabetes risk.
  **A:** Regular physical activity (60 min/day for teens), balanced diet with fewer sugary drinks, healthy weight, enough sleep.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present your chronic disease research to a family member.

*How to teach:* The child presents to a family member.

- **Q:** What does asthma do and how is it managed?
  **A:** It inflames and narrows airways; managed by avoiding triggers and using controller and quick-relief (rescue) inhalers as prescribed.

**Assessment:** Chronic disease research presentation.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = defines the disease, explains causes and at least three risk factors (controllable vs. not), gives at least three prevention behaviors and cites 2 valid sources; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = facts without prevention plan; 1 = inaccurate.
