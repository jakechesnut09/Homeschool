# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 18

## English Language Arts 8: Douglass's Narrative: chapters 9–11; semester review

**Prepare:** Read chapters 9–11 and the Appendix of the Narrative. Prepare a semester review packet and exam: one literary and one informational cold-read passage with questions, 10 grammar items, and a timed essay prompt. Parental preview: the fight with Covey and descriptions of beatings.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Douglass rejects religion. In the Appendix he separates slaveholding religion from what he calls 'the Christianity of Christ,' which he embraces.
- Calling any repetition chiasmus. Chiasmus reverses the order of key words in two parallel phrases (A–B, B–A).

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 9–10
*Student:* Read the Covey chapters and analyze the chiasmus 'You have seen how a man was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man.'

*How to teach:* Read chapters 9–10 (Thomas Auld, then Edward Covey). Write the chiasmus on the board, draw arrows showing the reversed word order, and ask the child how the sentence's shape mirrors what happens to Douglass.

- **Q:** Explain 'You have seen how a man was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man.'
  **A:** The reversed order mirrors the reversal in his life: Covey's brutality nearly broke him, but fighting back restored his sense of manhood and self-worth — the turning point of the book.
- **Q:** What happened after Douglass fought Covey?
  **A:** Covey never tried to whip him again; Douglass said he was a changed man, determined to be free.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 11 & Appendix
*Student:* Read the escape chapter and appendix; discuss why Douglass withholds the details of how he escaped.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 11 and the Appendix. Discuss why a book about escaping would leave out how the escape happened.

- **Q:** Why does Douglass withhold the details of his escape?
  **A:** Publishing them could endanger the people who helped him and would alert slaveholders to the method, closing that route for others.
- **Q:** Where does he settle and what name does he take?
  **A:** New Bedford, Massachusetts; he takes the name Douglass (suggested by Nathan Johnson) and marries Anna Murray.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Semester review: reading
*Student:* Review literary and informational reading skills with a practice cold read and answer key.

*How to teach:* Child takes a practice cold read independently, then checks with the answer key and writes why each missed answer was wrong. To check, ask: "What makes a summary 'objective'?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What makes a summary 'objective'?
  **A:** It reports the main ideas in order without opinions, judgments or 'I.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review: grammar & writing
*Student:* Review verbals, voice, mood and punctuation and plan a timed essay response.

*How to teach:* Quick-fire review of verbals, voice, mood and punctuation (10 items), then plan — but don't write — a timed essay: claim, three reasons, evidence list. Finish by asking: "Identify the verbal: 'Waiting for news, Della counted her money.'" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Identify the verbal: 'Waiting for news, Della counted her money.'
  **A:** 'Waiting for news' is a participial phrase modifying Della.
- **Q:** Which mood is 'I insist that he be on time'?
  **A:** Subjunctive.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam: cold reads, grammar items and a timed analytical essay.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam: cold reads, grammar items and a timed analytical essay (about 40 minutes). Before moving on, ask: "Define chiasmus and give the example from Douglass." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Define chiasmus and give the example from Douglass.
  **A:** A reversal of word order in parallel phrases: 'how a man was made a slave ... how a slave was made a man.'

**Assessment:** Semester 1 exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam scoring: cold reads — score with your key (main idea, inference, word meaning, author's purpose, text evidence). Grammar items with answers to use: 1) 'Swimming is good exercise' → gerund (subject). 2) 'The broken paw lay on the floor' → past participle (adjective). 3) 'Douglass wanted to read' → infinitive (noun/object). 4) Rewrite 'The speech was delivered by Henry' → 'Henry delivered the speech.' 5) 'If I were free, I would write' → subjunctive + conditional. 6) Fix 'Running to the door, the knocking stopped' → 'Running to the door, Mrs. White heard the knocking stop.' Timed essay rubric: arguable claim (4), evidence (4), commentary (4), organization (4), conventions (4).

## Mathematics 8: Representing and comparing functions; semester review

**Prepare:** Graph paper, Desmos. Prepare the semester exam (Units 1–5 so far) and a blank sheet for the formula/strategy page.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any curve that goes up is linear. Check a table: equal steps in x must give equal changes in y.
- Comparing functions by intercept only. 'Grows faster' is about the rate of change (slope).

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparing functions
*Student:* Compare a function given by a table with one given by an equation (e.g., two car rental companies) and decide which grows faster.

*How to teach:* Present Company A as a table (0 mi $40, 100 mi $65, 200 mi $90) and Company B as C = 0.30m + 30. Child finds each rate and decides which grows faster and when they cost the same.

- **Q:** Which grows faster, and when do they cost the same?
  **A:** A's rate is $0.25/mile, B's is $0.30/mile, so B grows faster. 40 + 0.25m = 30 + 0.30m → m = 200 miles ($90).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Linear vs nonlinear
*Student:* Graph y = 2x + 1, y = x^2 and A = pi r^2 from tables and explain why only the first is linear.

*How to teach:* Child makes tables and graphs of y = 2x + 1, y = x² and A = πr² and checks first differences. Check understanding with: "Why is y = x² not linear?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why is y = x² not linear?
  **A:** For x = 0, 1, 2, 3, y = 0, 1, 4, 9; the changes 1, 3, 5 are not constant, and the graph curves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Semester review A
*Student:* Mixed review of transformations, similarity and slope (Units 1–3) with worked corrections.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of Units 1–3; child corrects each miss with a written reason. To check, ask: "Find the slope through (−1, 2) and (3, 10)." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Find the slope through (−1, 2) and (3, 10).
  **A:** 8/4 = 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review B
*Student:* Mixed review of equations and systems (Unit 4) and functions; make a one-page formula and strategy sheet.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of equations, systems and functions; child builds a one-page formula and strategy sheet. Finish by asking: "Solve y = x + 2 and y = −2x + 8." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Solve y = x + 2 and y = −2x + 8.
  **A:** 3x = 6 → x = 2, y = 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester 1 exam
*Student:* Cumulative semester exam covering Units 1–5 so far.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam; allow the formula sheet only if you choose to. Before moving on, ask: "Solve 3(x − 4) = x + 6." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Solve 3(x − 4) = x + 6.
  **A:** 3x − 12 = x + 6 → 2x = 18 → x = 9.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 cumulative exam.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 exam: grade with the item answers you write from Weeks 1–17; suggested items and answers: 1) Rotate (3, −1) 180° → (−3, 1). 2) Dilate (2, 6) by 1/2 from origin → (1, 3). 3) Triangle angles 52° and 71° → 57°. 4) Slope through (−1, 2) and (3, 10) → 2. 5) y-intercept of y = −4x + 9 → 9. 6) 3(x − 4) = x + 6 → x = 9. 7) 2x + 3 = 2x − 1 → no solution. 8) y = x + 2 and y = −2x + 8 → (2, 4). 9) Is {(2, 3), (3, 3), (4, 5)} a function? Yes. 10) Which grows faster: table rate $0.25/mi or y = 0.30x + 30? The second. Score by percent; 80% = proficient.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Light and color; semester review

**Prepare:** A prism or glass of water, sunny window, white paper, red and blue cellophane, flashlight, colored objects. Prepare the semester exam (Units 1–4).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking objects 'have' color. An object reflects certain wavelengths and absorbs the rest; color depends on the light.
- Mixing up light and paint (pigment) color rules.

### Day 1 (Monday): Making a spectrum
*Student:* Use a glass of water in sunlight or a prism to make a rainbow; explain how white light separates.

*How to teach:* Make a spectrum with a prism or water glass in sunlight. Before moving on, ask: "Why does white light separate?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Why does white light separate?
  **A:** Different wavelengths refract by different amounts; violet bends most, red least.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Colored filters
*Student:* Look at colored objects through red and blue cellophane and explain the results with absorption and reflection.

*How to teach:* View colored objects through red and blue cellophane. Check understanding with: "A blue object through red cellophane looks how?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** A blue object through red cellophane looks how?
  **A:** Dark/black — the red filter blocks the blue light it reflects.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Semester review: forces & energy
*Student:* Review Units 1–2 with a practice test and corrections.

*How to teach:* Review forces and energy with a practice test. To check, ask: "Net force: 15 N right, 5 N left?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Net force: 15 N right, 5 N left?
  **A:** 10 N right.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review: space & waves
*Student:* Review Units 3–4 with a concept map.

*How to teach:* Review space and waves with a concept map. Finish by asking: "What causes a lunar eclipse?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What causes a lunar eclipse?
  **A:** Earth blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester 1 exam
*Student:* Cumulative semester exam.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam. Before moving on, ask: "What is the order of colors in the spectrum?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What is the order of colors in the spectrum?
  **A:** Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam sample answers: speed = d/t; net force; F = ma; Newton's three laws; KE = ½mv²; GPE = mgh; energy conservation; gravity and mass; phases, eclipses, seasons; wave parts and v = fλ; reflection and refraction; red object under blue light looks dark. Score by percent; 80% proficient.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Mexican California and Texas independence; semester review

**Prepare:** Calisphere images of ranchos, Californios and diseños; accounts of the Alamo including José Enrique de la Peña's diary; review materials for units 1–6. Write the semester exam.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Texas Revolution was only about liberty. Causes included Mexico's centralization under Santa Anna, cultural differences, and Anglo settlers' insistence on slavery, which Mexico had abolished (1829).
- Treating the Alamo as the end of the revolution. Texas won independence at San Jacinto (April 21, 1836).

### Day 1 (Monday): Mexican California
*Student:* Use Calisphere photographs and diseños (land-grant maps) to describe rancho life, the economy of hides and tallow and Mexico's 1829 abolition of slavery.

*How to teach:* Use Calisphere photos and diseños to describe rancho life.

- **Q:** What was the hide-and-tallow trade?
  **A:** Californio ranchos raised cattle and traded hides (for leather) and tallow (fat for candles and soap) with ships, mostly from New England; hides were called 'California banknotes.'
- **Q:** What was a diseño?
  **A:** A hand-drawn map showing the boundaries of a Mexican land grant, using landmarks like rivers, hills and trees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Texas Revolution
*Student:* Compare a Texan account and a Mexican account (José Enrique de la Peña) of the Alamo and identify each perspective.

*How to teach:* Compare Texan and Mexican accounts of the Alamo.

- **Q:** How did de la Peña's account differ from the Texan legend?
  **A:** He described defenders, possibly including Davy Crockett, being captured and executed after the battle on Santa Anna's orders, rather than all dying fighting; he wrote from the Mexican army's point of view and criticized Santa Anna.
- **Q:** Where did Texas win its independence?
  **A:** At San Jacinto (April 21, 1836), where Sam Houston defeated and captured Santa Anna.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Semester review A
*Student:* Review Units 1–3 with a timeline sort and practice document questions.

*How to teach:* Review units 1–3 with a timeline sort and document questions.

- **Q:** Put in order: Constitution signed, Declaration, Treaty of Paris, Articles ratified.
  **A:** Declaration (1776), Articles ratified (1781), Treaty of Paris (1783), Constitution signed (1787).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review B
*Student:* Review Units 4–6 with a vocabulary matching game and a practice DBQ outline.

*How to teach:* Review units 4–6 with vocabulary matching and a DBQ outline.

- **Q:** Match: Seneca Falls, Erie Canal, Worcester v. Georgia.
  **A:** Seneca Falls — women's rights convention (1848); Erie Canal — Hudson to Lake Erie (1825); Worcester v. Georgia — Cherokee sovereignty ruling (1832).

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester 1 exam
*Student:* Cumulative semester exam with multiple-choice and document-based items.

*How to teach:* Give the semester 1 exam.

- **Q:** Why did Mexico's 1829 abolition of slavery matter in Texas?
  **A:** Many Anglo settlers held enslaved people and resisted Mexican antislavery laws, one cause of the Texas Revolution.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 exam.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 exam (suggested cumulative): English/Enlightenment roots; causes of the Revolution; Declaration; Articles and their weaknesses; Constitutional compromises; Federalists vs Anti-Federalists; Bill of Rights; Washington through Monroe; Marbury, McCulloch, Gibbons; War of 1812; Monroe Doctrine; industrialization; reform; Southern economy and slavery; Jackson and Indian Removal; Manifest Destiny; Mexican California (ranchos, hide-and-tallow trade, mission secularization 1833–34, Mexico abolished slavery 1829); Texas (Alamo Feb–March 1836, San Jacinto April 1836, Republic of Texas 1836–1845, annexed 1845). DBQ items scored for claim, evidence, sourcing.

## Physical Education 8: Fueling performance: nutrition, hydration and sleep

**Prepare:** Food labels, water bottle, bathroom scale, sleep log, poster paper.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping carbohydrates before exercise. Correct: carbohydrates are the main fuel for moderate-to-intense exercise.
- Waiting until thirsty to drink. Correct: drink regularly before, during and after; thirst lags behind fluid needs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pre-exercise fuel
*Student:* Plan three pre-workout snacks and explain why carbohydrates are the main fuel; do a 30-minute activity.

*How to teach:* Plan three pre-workout snacks (banana, toast with jam, granola bar) 1–3 hours before; explain carbs fuel muscles as glycogen.

- **Q:** Why are carbohydrates the main fuel for exercise?
  **A:** They break down to glucose and are stored as glycogen in muscles and liver, providing quick energy for moderate-to-intense activity.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hydration
*Student:* Weigh yourself before and after a 30-minute workout to estimate sweat loss and calculate fluid needs.

*How to teach:* Weigh before and after a 30-minute workout (no drinking or bathroom between if possible). Replace about 16–24 oz of fluid per pound lost.

- **Q:** You lost 1.5 lb during practice. How much should you drink?
  **A:** About 24–36 oz (16–24 oz per pound).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recovery
*Student:* Plan a post-workout snack with protein and carbohydrates and explain muscle repair.

*How to teach:* Plan a post-workout snack with protein and carbs within about an hour (chocolate milk, yogurt with fruit, turkey sandwich).

- **Q:** Why include protein after a workout?
  **A:** Protein provides amino acids to repair and rebuild muscle; carbs refill glycogen.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sleep
*Student:* Log sleep for the week and compare to the 8-10 hours recommended for teens; do a yoga cool-down.

*How to teach:* Log sleep; compare to 8–10 hours. Yoga cool-down.

- **Q:** How does sleep affect performance?
  **A:** Sleep restores energy, supports muscle repair (growth hormone release), reaction time, mood and injury prevention.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a one-day 'athlete's fuel plan' poster.

*How to teach:* Create a one-day fuel plan poster.

- **Q:** What should a fuel plan include?
  **A:** Balanced meals, pre- and post-exercise snacks, hydration schedule and sleep target.

**Assessment:** Athlete's fuel plan poster.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric: 4 = three balanced meals, pre-workout carbohydrate snack with timing, post-workout carb + protein snack, hydration schedule with amounts, sleep target 8–10 h, avoids energy drinks; 3 = one element missing; 2 = partial; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Sexual health review and decision making

**Prepare:** Prepare three relationship scenario cards, vocabulary flashcards from weeks 11–17 and the unit test (key below). Opted-out students: alternative test on puberty, hygiene and healthy friendships.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking decisions now don't affect the future. Correct: choices about relationships and risk can affect health, education and goals.
- Forgetting that refusing pressure is a skill that takes practice. Correct: rehearse words and plans in advance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scenarios
*Student:* Apply the decision-making model to three relationship scenarios.

*How to teach:* The child applies the decision model to three scenarios, writing options, consequences and the healthy choice.

- **Q:** A partner says 'If you loved me you would.' What is happening and what is a healthy response?
  **A:** Pressure/manipulation. Healthy response: a clear refusal ('No. Loving me means respecting my answer') and talking to a trusted adult if pressure continues.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Future goals
*Student:* Write how decisions now affect future goals (school, sports, career).

*How to teach:* The child writes how relationship decisions now could affect school, sports and career goals.

- **Q:** Give one way an early pregnancy or STI could affect future goals.
  **A:** It could interrupt school or sports, add financial and caregiving responsibilities, or create long-term health effects.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Communication
*Student:* Write a script for talking to a parent about a relationship question.

*How to teach:* The child writes and rehearses a script to raise a relationship question with you.

- **Q:** What's a good opening line?
  **A:** Example: 'Can I ask you something kind of awkward? I want your advice about a friend situation.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review vocabulary and concepts from the unit with flashcards.

*How to teach:* Quiz vocabulary with flashcards: consent, abstinence, STI, HIV, implantation, trimester, grooming, sextortion, gender identity.

- **Q:** Define affirmative consent.
  **A:** A clear, voluntary, ongoing agreement to engage in an activity, which can be withdrawn at any time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit test
*Student:* Take the unit test (alternative assignment on puberty, hygiene and relationships for opted-out students).

*How to teach:* Give the unit test closed-book and review answers.

- **Q:** What is the only 100% effective way to avoid pregnancy and STIs?
  **A:** Abstinence.

**Assessment:** Unit test or alternative assignment.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: 1) fertilization in fallopian tube 2) pregnancy ~40 weeks, three trimesters 3) no safe amount of alcohol in pregnancy 4) abstinence 100% effective 5) condoms reduce STI risk; hormonal methods don't 6) many STIs have no symptoms 7) HIV treatable with ART, not curable 8) CA minors 12+ can consent to STI testing 9) definitions of gender identity, expression, orientation 10) consent is clear, ongoing, can be withdrawn 11) digital red flags: password demands, tracking 12) sextortion steps: stop, don't pay, save evidence, tell, report 13) trafficking hotline 1-888-373-7888. Alternative test: puberty changes, daily hygiene, qualities of healthy friendships.
