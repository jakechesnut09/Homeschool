# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 6

## English Language Arts 8: The Outsiders, chapters 1–3: characters and class

**Prepare:** Get a library copy of The Outsiders; read chapters 1–3. Parental preview: gang violence, smoking, drinking, a teen is beaten; later a stabbing and deaths.

**Watch for:**
- Treating Greasers as 'good' and Socs as 'bad.' Track how Hinton shows both groups suffering.
- Thinking passive voice is always wrong. It's useful when the doer is unknown or unimportant.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pre-reading
*Student:* Read a short article about Tulsa in the 1960s and S. E. Hinton writing the book at 16; predict conflicts from the cover and title.

*How to teach:* Read about 1960s Tulsa and Hinton writing the novel as a teen; predict conflicts. Before moving on, ask: "How old was S. E. Hinton when she began The Outsiders?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** How old was S. E. Hinton when she began The Outsiders?
  **A:** About 15–16; it was published in 1967.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 1–2
*Student:* Read chapters 1–2 and start a character chart (Ponyboy, Johnny, Dally, Darry, Sodapop, Cherry) with direct and indirect characterization quotes.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2; start the character chart. Check understanding with: "What happens to Ponyboy in chapter 1?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What happens to Ponyboy in chapter 1?
  **A:** Socs jump him walking home from the movies; his brothers and friends rescue him.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 3
*Student:* Read chapter 3 and write a journal entry analyzing Cherry's line 'Things are rough all over' and what it reveals about class.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 3; journal entry on 'Things are rough all over.' To check, ask: "What does Cherry mean by 'Things are rough all over'?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What does Cherry mean by 'Things are rough all over'?
  **A:** Socs have problems too — emotional ones — even though they have money.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: active and passive voice
*Student:* Identify active and passive sentences; rewrite 10 sentences from passive to active and explain when passive is useful.

*How to teach:* Teach active vs passive voice; rewrite 10 sentences. Finish by asking: "Rewrite in active: 'Ponyboy was jumped by the Socs.'" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Rewrite in active: 'Ponyboy was jumped by the Socs.'
  **A:** 'The Socs jumped Ponyboy.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on chapters 1–3; participate in a discussion on whether the Greasers and Socs are more alike or different.

*How to teach:* Quiz; discuss whether the groups are more alike or different. Before moving on, ask: "Name one thing Ponyboy and Cherry share." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Name one thing Ponyboy and Cherry share.
  **A:** They both like watching sunsets.

**Assessment:** Chapter quiz and discussion participation.

**Answer key:** Chapter 1–3 quiz: 1) Narrator: Ponyboy Curtis, 14. 2) Brothers: Darry (oldest, works two jobs, acts as guardian) and Sodapop. 3) Johnny was badly beaten by Socs before the story. 4) At the drive-in Johnny tells Dally to leave Cherry and Marcia alone. 5) Cherry says 'Things are rough all over.' 6) Ponyboy runs away after Darry slaps him. Discussion rubric: cites text, responds to others.

## Mathematics 8: Similarity

**Prepare:** Gather ruler, protractor, graph paper, a small mirror and a tape measure. Choose a tree or doorway for Thursday's mirror lab and check the floor/ground is safe and flat.

**Watch for:**
- Believing all rectangles are similar. Compare a 2×4 and a 2×3 rectangle: ratios 2/2 and 4/3 differ, so they are not similar.
- Setting up proportions with mismatched sides. Have the child label corresponding sides in the same color before writing the ratio.

### Day 1 (Monday): Similarity sequences
*Student:* Describe a sequence of a dilation plus rigid motions that maps one polygon to a similar one; do 4 grid problems.

*How to teach:* Explain: two figures are similar if a sequence of rigid motions and dilations maps one onto the other. Model one grid problem (dilate by 2 from the origin, then translate), and the child does 4.

- **Q:** Triangle (0,0),(2,0),(0,1) maps to (3,0),(7,0),(3,2). Describe a sequence.
  **A:** Dilate by scale factor 2 with center at the origin to get (0,0),(4,0),(0,2), then translate 3 units right.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Similar polygons
*Student:* Test pairs of rectangles and triangles for similarity by comparing side ratios and angles; explain why all squares are similar but not all rectangles.

*How to teach:* Child tests pairs of rectangles and triangles by comparing side ratios and angles, then explains why all squares are similar but not all rectangles. Check understanding with: "Are a 4×6 rectangle and a 6×9 rectangle similar?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Are a 4×6 rectangle and a 6×9 rectangle similar?
  **A:** Yes: 4/6 = 6/9 = 2/3, and all angles are 90°.
- **Q:** Why are all squares similar?
  **A:** Every square has four 90° angles and four equal sides, so one square can be dilated by (its side ÷ the other's side) to match any other.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): AA similarity
*Student:* Draw two triangles with the same two angle measures but different sizes; measure sides to confirm proportional lengths and state the AA criterion.

*How to teach:* Child draws two triangles with angles 50° and 60° at different sizes, measures sides and computes ratios. State the AA criterion: if two angles of one triangle equal two angles of another, the triangles are similar.

- **Q:** Triangle 1 has angles 40° and 60°; triangle 2 has angles 40° and 80°. Similar?
  **A:** Yes. The third angles are 80° and 60°, so both have 40°, 60°, 80° — similar by AA.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mirror method investigation
*Student:* Use a small mirror on the floor and similar triangles to estimate the height of a tree or doorway; show the proportion and compare to a tape-measure value.

*How to teach:* Place the mirror on the floor; the child backs up until they see the top of the object in it. Measure eye height, child-to-mirror distance and mirror-to-object distance; the two triangles are similar because the angles of reflection are equal and both have right angles.

- **Q:** Eye height 1.5 m, you stand 2 m from the mirror, mirror is 6 m from the tree. How tall is the tree?
  **A:** 1.5/2 = h/6, so h = 4.5 m.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 6 missing-side problems using similar triangles and write one AA justification.

*How to teach:* Child solves 6 missing-side problems and writes one AA justification. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Similar triangles have sides 3, 4, 5 and 9, x, 15. Find x.
  **A:** Scale factor 3, so x = 12.

**Assessment:** Mirror-height lab write-up plus similarity problem set.

**Answer key:** Lab write-up rubric (4 levels): 4 = labeled diagram showing two similar right triangles, correct proportion (eye height / distance from person to mirror = object height / distance from mirror to object), computed height, comparison to tape-measure value with percent error and sources of error; 3 = all parts with a minor error; 2 = proportion set up but missing comparison or diagram; 1 = incomplete. Problem set sample answers: triangles with sides 3, 4, 5 and 9, x, 15 → x = 12; a 4×6 rectangle and a 6×9 rectangle are similar (ratio 2/3); triangles with angles 40°, 60° and 40°, 80° are similar by AA (third angles 80° and 60°).

## Science 8 (Integrated): Kinetic energy

**Prepare:** A ramp (board + books), balls of different mass but similar size (golf ball, ping-pong ball, rubber ball), a paper cup with a 'door' cut in the rim, a ruler, masking tape.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kinetic energy doubles when speed doubles. KE depends on v², so doubling speed quadruples KE.
- Thinking heavier objects always have more KE. A fast light object can have more KE than a slow heavy one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Energy of motion
*Student:* Brainstorm examples of moving objects and rank them by kinetic energy; read an introduction to KE = 1/2 m v^2.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm moving objects (a jogger, a bullet, a truck) and rank by kinetic energy. Introduce KE = ½mv² and units (joules).

- **Q:** Rank: a slow-rolling bowling ball or a fast-thrown baseball?
  **A:** It depends on actual values; a fast baseball can have more KE because speed is squared.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mass and kinetic energy
*Student:* Roll balls of different mass from the same ramp height into a cup; measure how far the cup slides as evidence of energy.

*How to teach:* Roll balls of different mass from the same height into the cup; measure how far the cup slides. Check understanding with: "Why use the same ramp height for each ball?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why use the same ramp height for each ball?
  **A:** So the speed at the bottom is about the same; only mass changes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speed and kinetic energy
*Student:* Roll the same ball from increasing ramp heights (faster speeds) and measure cup distance.

*How to teach:* Roll one ball from increasing heights; measure cup distance. To check, ask: "What happens to the cup distance as height increases?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What happens to the cup distance as height increases?
  **A:** It increases — more speed means more kinetic energy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graphing KE
*Student:* Graph both data sets and use KE calculations to explain why doubling speed has a bigger effect than doubling mass.

*How to teach:* The child graphs both data sets and calculates KE to explain why doubling speed matters more. Finish by asking: "A 2-kg ball goes 3 m/s, then 6 m/s. Compare KE." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** A 2-kg ball goes 3 m/s, then 6 m/s. Compare KE.
  **A:** 9 J vs 36 J — four times as much.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on interpreting KE graphs and 5 KE calculations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (key). Before moving on, ask: "1000-kg car at 10 m/s: KE?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** 1000-kg car at 10 m/s: KE?
  **A:** ½ × 1000 × 100 = 50,000 J.

**Assessment:** Kinetic energy graphs and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) KE = ½mv². 2) 2-kg ball at 3 m/s → 9 J. 3) 1000-kg car at 10 m/s → 50,000 J. 4) Same car at 20 m/s → 200,000 J (4 times). 5) 4-kg ball at 2 m/s → 8 J. Graph interpretation: mass vs KE is a straight line (proportional); speed vs KE curves upward (nonlinear). Graphs: labeled axes, two data sets, best-fit line or curve.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Ratification and the Bill of Rights

**Prepare:** Excerpts of Federalist No. 10, No. 51, Brutus No. 1, Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom; Bill of Rights transcript. Write 10 modern scenarios for the quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Bill of Rights was part of the original Constitution. It was added in 1791 after Anti-Federalists demanded it during ratification.
- Believing the First Amendment protects you from private companies or parents. It limits government action.

### Day 1 (Monday): Federalists vs Anti-Federalists
*Student:* Read excerpts of Federalist No. 10 and an Anti-Federalist essay (Brutus No. 1); chart main arguments.

*How to teach:* Read Federalist No. 10 and Brutus No. 1; chart arguments.

- **Q:** What was Madison's solution to faction in Federalist No. 10?
  **A:** A large republic with many competing interests makes it hard for any single faction to dominate.
- **Q:** What did Brutus fear?
  **A:** That a large republic could not represent the people, and that the necessary-and-proper and supremacy clauses would let the national government swallow the states and threaten liberty; he also wanted a bill of rights.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Federalist No. 51
*Student:* Analyze 'If men were angels' and explain checks and balances.

*How to teach:* Analyze Federalist No. 51.

- **Q:** Explain 'If men were angels, no government would be necessary.'
  **A:** People aren't perfect, so we need government — but government is run by people too, so it must be checked.
- **Q:** What does 'ambition must be made to counteract ambition' mean?
  **A:** Each branch should have the power and motive to check the others.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Religious freedom
*Student:* Read Jefferson's Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom and connect it to the First Amendment.

*How to teach:* Read Jefferson's Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom; connect to the First Amendment.

- **Q:** What did the Virginia Statute (1786) establish?
  **A:** No one could be forced to attend or support any church or be punished for religious beliefs — the basis for the First Amendment's religion clauses.
- **Q:** What are the two religion clauses of the First Amendment?
  **A:** The establishment clause (no official national religion) and the free exercise clause.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bill of Rights
*Student:* Illustrate each of the first 10 amendments with a modern scenario.

*How to teach:* Illustrate each amendment with a modern scenario.

- **Q:** Which amendment protects you from being tried twice for the same crime?
  **A:** The 5th (double jeopardy).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Bill of Rights scenario quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the scenario quiz.

- **Q:** What does the 10th Amendment say?
  **A:** Powers not delegated to the federal government nor prohibited to the states are reserved to the states or the people.

**Assessment:** Scenario quiz on the Bill of Rights.

**Answer key:** Scenario quiz key (Bill of Rights): 1st — religion, speech, press, assembly, petition; 2nd — keep and bear arms; 3rd — no quartering of soldiers in peacetime without consent; 4th — no unreasonable searches and seizures, warrants need probable cause; 5th — grand jury, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination, due process, just compensation for takings; 6th — speedy public trial, impartial jury, lawyer, confront witnesses; 7th — jury in civil cases; 8th — no excessive bail or cruel and unusual punishment; 9th — rights not listed are still retained by the people; 10th — powers not delegated to the U.S. are reserved to the states or the people. Example scenarios: police search a locker without a warrant (4th); a newspaper is shut down for criticizing the mayor (1st); a suspect refuses to testify against himself (5th).

## Spanish 1B: Comparisons and superlatives

**Prepare:** Photos of rooms or Spanish real-estate ads (e.g., from Idealista or Inmuebles24).

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'más bueno' and 'más malo.' Use mejor and peor.
- Using 'que' with tan. Equal comparisons use tan… como: tan grande como.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparatives
*Student:* Learn más/menos...que and tan...como and compare 10 pairs of objects in your house.

*How to teach:* Teach más/menos…que, tan…como; compare 10 pairs.

- **Q:** Compare: the sofa (big) vs the chair.
  **A:** El sofá es más grande que la silla.
- **Q:** Write: 'The lamp is as tall as the shelf.'
  **A:** La lámpara es tan alta como el estante.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Irregulars
*Student:* Learn mejor, peor, mayor, menor and compare family members' ages and skills.

*How to teach:* Teach mejor, peor, mayor, menor; compare family.

- **Q:** Write: 'My sister is younger than me.'
  **A:** Mi hermana es menor que yo.
- **Q:** Write: 'My dad cooks better than I do.'
  **A:** Mi papá cocina mejor que yo.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Superlatives
*Student:* Learn el/la más... de and write superlatives about your family, house, and town.

*How to teach:* Teach el/la más… de; write superlatives.

- **Q:** Write: 'My room is the smallest in the house.'
  **A:** Mi cuarto es el más pequeño de la casa.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Real-estate ads
*Student:* Read three Spanish apartment ads and compare them in eight sentences, choosing the best for your family.

*How to teach:* Read three apartment ads; compare in 8 sentences.

- **Q:** What does 'tres dormitorios, dos baños' mean?
  **A:** Three bedrooms, two bathrooms.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a comparisons quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the comparisons quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'She is the best student in the class.'
  **A:** Ella es la mejor estudiante de la clase.

**Assessment:** Comparisons quiz.

**Answer key:** Comparisons quiz samples: The kitchen is bigger than the bathroom → La cocina es más grande que el baño; My room is as small as yours → Mi cuarto es tan pequeño como el tuyo; My brother is older than I → Mi hermano es mayor que yo; This apartment is the best in the city → Este apartamento es el mejor de la ciudad; The sofa is less comfortable than the armchair → El sofá es menos cómodo que el sillón. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Facial proportions

**Prepare:** Mirror, pencils, paper, ruler. Draw a sample proportion guide.

**Watch for:**
- Placing eyes high on the head. Correct: eyes are about halfway between the top of the head and the chin.
- Drawing eyes as almond outlines only. Correct: include the eyelid crease, iris partly covered by the upper lid, and shadows.

### Day 1 (Monday): Proportion guide
*Student:* Draw an oval head with guidelines: eyes halfway down, nose at one-third, mouth at one-third of the remaining distance; label each.

*How to teach:* Draw an oval head with a vertical center line; eye line halfway down; bottom of the nose about halfway between the eye line and the chin; mouth about one-third of the way from nose to chin. Label.

- **Q:** Where are the eyes located on an adult head?
  **A:** About halfway between the top of the head and the chin.
- **Q:** How far apart are the eyes?
  **A:** About one eye-width apart.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eyes and noses
*Student:* Fill a sketchbook page with 4 eyes and 4 noses drawn from a mirror, shading the eyelid and nostril shadows.

*How to teach:* Four eyes and four noses from the mirror, shading eyelids and nostrils.

- **Q:** Why is the top of the iris often hidden?
  **A:** The upper eyelid covers part of it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mouths and ears
*Student:* Draw 4 mouths (neutral, smiling, open, profile) and 2 ears, noting where ears line up with eyes and nose.

*How to teach:* Four mouths and two ears; note ear placement.

- **Q:** Where do the ears line up?
  **A:** Roughly between the eyebrow/eye line and the bottom of the nose.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Measuring real faces
*Student:* Photograph or observe three family members and record how their proportions differ from the guideline face.

*How to teach:* Observe three family members and record differences.

- **Q:** Why do real faces differ from the guideline?
  **A:** Proportions vary person to person; the guideline is an average starting point.

### Day 5 (Friday): Self-portrait sketch
*Student:* Draw a full self-portrait from a mirror using the guidelines, then compare it to a photo and note one correction.

*How to teach:* Draw a self-portrait from a mirror; compare to a photo; note one correction.

- **Q:** Where do the corners of the mouth usually line up?
  **A:** Roughly below the centers of the eyes (pupils).

**Assessment:** Self-portrait sketch checked for proportion.

**Answer key:** Self-portrait proportion check: 4 = eyes at halfway line, one eye-width apart, nose and mouth placed by guidelines, ears aligned eye-to-nose, value shading, one correction noted; 3 = one placement error; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Flag football: throwing, catching and routes

**Prepare:** Football (junior size for smaller hands), flag belts or bandanas, cones every 5 and 10 yards. Phone for video on Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing with only the arm. Correct: step toward the target with the opposite foot and rotate hips and shoulders.
- Catching against the body. Correct: catch with hands in a diamond or basket shape, then tuck.

### Day 1 (Monday): Spiral grip
*Student:* Practice the laces grip, step and follow-through; complete 50 partner throws at 10 yards.

*How to teach:* Teach grip (fingers on laces, gap between ball and palm), side stance, step with opposite foot, elbow at/above shoulder, release with wrist flick and follow-through to opposite hip. 50 throws at 10 yards.

- **Q:** Which foot steps when a right-handed player throws?
  **A:** The left foot steps toward the target.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Catching
*Student:* Practice the diamond (above chest) and basket (below chest) catch positions on 40 partner throws.

*How to teach:* Diamond catch (thumbs together) for balls above chest; basket (pinkies together) below chest. 40 throws.

- **Q:** Which hand shape for a ball at the knees?
  **A:** Basket — pinkies together, palms up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Routes
*Student:* Run slant, out, post and curl routes against air; partner throws on time.

*How to teach:* Run slant, out, post and curl against air; QB throws on time.

- **Q:** Describe a slant and a curl.
  **A:** Slant: a few steps upfield then a sharp diagonal cut toward the middle. Curl: run upfield then turn back toward the quarterback.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flag pulling
*Student:* Practice flag-pulling defense and evasive moves (juke, spin) in a 1-v-1 grid.

*How to teach:* 1-v-1 grid: defender pulls flag; attacker uses juke or spin. Emphasize no contact, no flag guarding.

- **Q:** What is flag guarding?
  **A:** Using hands, arms or the ball to block a defender from pulling your flag — it's a penalty.

### Day 5 (Friday): Analyze
*Student:* Video a partner's throw and identify one biomechanical fix (step foot, elbow height, release point).

*How to teach:* Video a partner's throw and identify one biomechanical fix.

- **Q:** Why does a spiral fly farther and more accurately?
  **A:** Spin stabilizes the ball (gyroscopic stability) and reduces air resistance.

**Assessment:** Throw analysis with one correction.

**Answer key:** Throw analysis: proficient answer identifies one specific fault from video (e.g., stepping with the same-side foot, low elbow, early release, no follow-through), explains its effect on accuracy or distance, and gives a cue to fix it (e.g., 'step to target with opposite foot').

## Health Education 8: Mental health advocacy and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Gather poster paper or set up slide/video software. Write a 12–15 question unit test on weeks 1–5 (use the key below). Have the child's SMART tech goal from week 2.

**Watch for:**
- Making a PSA that only lists facts. Correct: an effective PSA has one clear message, a target audience and a call to action (e.g., 'Text 988').
- Thinking resources are only for emergencies. Correct: counselors and doctors help with everyday struggles too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a short PSA promoting help-seeking or reducing stigma for teens.

*How to teach:* The child chooses a message (help-seeking or anti-stigma), an audience (teens) and a call to action, and sketches the plan.

- **Q:** What three things does a PSA plan need?
  **A:** A clear message, a target audience and a specific call to action (plus accurate facts).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Create
*Student:* Create the PSA as a poster, 60-second video or slide.

*How to teach:* The child creates the PSA. Check facts and that wording is person-first.

- **Q:** What call to action would fit a help-seeking PSA?
  **A:** Examples: 'Talk to a trusted adult,' 'Call or text 988,' 'Visit your school counselor.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Share
*Student:* Share the PSA with family and gather feedback.

*How to teach:* The child shows the PSA to family members and asks two feedback questions (Was the message clear? Would it change what you do?).

- **Q:** Why gather audience feedback?
  **A:** To see whether the message is clear and persuasive and to improve it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Goal check
*Student:* Review progress on your tech-balance goal and adjust.

*How to teach:* Compare the tech log to the SMART goal. The child writes whether they met it and one adjustment.

- **Q:** If you missed your goal, what should you do?
  **A:** Analyze what got in the way and adjust the plan (smaller step, new strategy, accountability) rather than giving up.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit test
*Student:* Take a unit test on resilience, mental illness, warning signs and resources.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test closed-book; review missed items together.

- **Q:** Name two protective factors.
  **A:** Examples: a supportive family, a trusted adult, friends, a team or club, coping skills, a sense of purpose.

**Assessment:** PSA and unit test.

**Answer key:** PSA: 4 = accurate, one clear message, audience-appropriate, person-first, includes call to action and resource. Unit test key (sample items): 1) resilience = ability to adapt and recover from adversity 2) protective factor example: caring adult 3) fixed vs growth mindset: belief abilities can't vs can grow with effort 4) feeds use algorithms to maximize engagement 5) mental illness is common and treatable 6) psychiatrist can prescribe medication 7) person-first: 'person with depression' 8) asking about suicide does NOT plant the idea 9) warning signs: talking about death, giving away possessions, withdrawing 10) Ask-Listen-Tell 11) 988 call/text 24/7; Crisis Text Line text HOME to 741741 12) grief is not linear 13) helpful response: listen, offer specific help. 80%+ = proficient.
