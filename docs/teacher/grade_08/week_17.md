# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 17

## English Language Arts 8: Douglass's Narrative: chapters 5–8, literacy and freedom

**Prepare:** Read chapters 5–8 of the Narrative. Make a punctuation sheet on dashes, commas and ellipses. Parental preview: chapter 8 describes Douglass's grandmother turned out to die alone.

**Watch for:**
- Using an ellipsis to change a quotation's meaning. Omissions must keep the author's meaning.
- Using a hyphen (-) where a dash (—) is needed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 5–6
*Student:* Read chapters 5–6 and analyze Mr. Auld's warning that learning would 'spoil' a slave and Douglass's response: 'the pathway from slavery to freedom.'

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5–6. Discuss Mr. Auld's warning that learning would 'forever unfit him to be a slave' and Douglass's realization.

- **Q:** What did Douglass realize after Mr. Auld forbade his lessons?
  **A:** 'I now understood ... the pathway from slavery to freedom' — literacy.
- **Q:** How does Sophia Auld change?
  **A:** She begins kind, but slavery's power hardens her.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 7
*Student:* Read chapter 7 and list every strategy Douglass used to learn to read and write, including trading bread for lessons.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 7; list every learning strategy. Before moving on, ask: "Name three strategies." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Name three strategies.
  **A:** Trading bread with poor white boys for lessons; reading The Columbian Orator; copying letters marked on ship timbers; copying from Master Thomas's copybook.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 8
*Student:* Read chapter 8 and analyze the word choice and tone of the passage about his grandmother.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 8; analyze the grandmother passage's word choice and tone. Check understanding with: "What happened to his grandmother?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What happened to his grandmother?
  **A:** After serving the family her whole life, she was sent to a hut in the woods to die alone.
- **Q:** What is the tone?
  **A:** Bitter, ironic outrage — e.g., she was 'made welcome to the privilege of supporting herself.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: commas, ellipses, dashes
*Student:* Practice using dashes and commas for breaks and ellipses for omissions by quoting Douglass accurately in 8 sentences.

*How to teach:* Practice dashes, commas and ellipses quoting Douglass in 8 sentences. To check, ask: "When do you use an ellipsis in a quotation?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** When do you use an ellipsis in a quotation?
  **A:** To show words were omitted.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an objective summary of chapters 5–8 and take a punctuation quiz.

*How to teach:* Objective summary of chapters 5–8; punctuation quiz. Finish by asking: "What book did Douglass buy at about age 12?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What book did Douglass buy at about age 12?
  **A:** The Columbian Orator.

**Assessment:** Summary and punctuation quiz.

**Answer key:** Summary rubric: objective, in order, covers Baltimore move, Auld's warning, learning to read/write, the valuation and grandmother. Punctuation quiz example answers: 'Douglass realized that learning was "the pathway from slavery to freedom."' / 'He traded bread—his only payment—for lessons.' / ellipsis used correctly with three spaced dots at an omission.

## Mathematics 8: Introduction to functions

**Prepare:** Index cards for 'guess my rule', graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a function cannot repeat outputs. Repeated outputs are fine; repeated inputs with different outputs are not.
- Reading f(3) as f times 3. Explain f(3) means 'the output when the input is 3.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Input-output machines
*Student:* Play 'guess my rule' with a family member using input/output cards, then decide which rules are functions.

*How to teach:* Play 'guess my rule': you think of a rule, the child gives inputs and you give outputs until they guess. Then swap roles. Ask which rules always give one output per input.

- **Q:** Rule: double and add 1. What is the output for input 4?
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** Is 'give a number whose square is the input' a function?
  **A:** No — input 9 could give 3 or −3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Is it a function?
*Student:* Classify 10 relations (tables, mapping diagrams, graphs, statements like 'a person's age -> height') as functions or not, using the vertical line test.

*How to teach:* Teach: each input has exactly one output. Child classifies 10 relations and uses the vertical line test on graphs.

- **Q:** Is {(1, 2), (1, 3), (2, 4)} a function?
  **A:** No — input 1 has two different outputs.
- **Q:** Is 'each student → their birthday' a function? What about 'birthday → student'?
  **A:** Student → birthday is a function (one birthday each); birthday → student is not (several students can share a birthday).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Function notation & language
*Student:* Write statements like 'distance is a function of time' and evaluate functions from equations and graphs.

*How to teach:* Model statements like 'distance is a function of time' (time is the input). Evaluate functions from equations and graphs.

- **Q:** If f(x) = 3x − 2, find f(4).
  **A:** f(4) = 12 − 2 = 10.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Equations for functions
*Student:* Write equations for functions such as the perimeter of a square or the cost of n tickets, naming the independent and dependent variables.

*How to teach:* Child writes equations for perimeter of a square, cost of n tickets, etc., naming independent and dependent variables. To check, ask: "Write the perimeter of a square as a function of its side length. Name the variables." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Write the perimeter of a square as a function of its side length. Name the variables.
  **A:** P = 4s; s (side length) is independent, P (perimeter) is dependent.
- **Q:** Tickets cost $12 each. Write the cost as a function of n.
  **A:** C = 12n.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: identify functions and evaluate them from 4 representations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Does a vertical line graph represent a function?
  **A:** No — one x-value has infinitely many y-values.

**Assessment:** Quiz on identifying functions.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) {(1, 2), (2, 4), (1, 5)} → not a function (input 1 has two outputs). 2) {(1, 3), (2, 3), (3, 3)} → function. 3) A graph failing the vertical line test (a circle) → not a function. 4) f(x) = 3x − 2: f(4) = 10. 5) From a graph through (2, 5): output at input 2 is 5. 6) Table: input 0, 1, 2 → output 7, 9, 11; output at 2 is 11. 1 point each.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Light: reflection, refraction, absorption, transmission

**Prepare:** Flashlight, mirror, protractor, glass of water, pencil, coin, opaque cup, wax paper, aluminum foil, clear plastic, cardboard. Bookmark PhET 'Bending Light.'

**Watch for:**
- Measuring reflection angles from the mirror's surface. Angles are measured from the normal (a line perpendicular to the surface).
- Thinking refraction happens because light 'bends inside' water. It changes direction at the boundary because its speed changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Light behavior sort
*Student:* Shine a flashlight through clear plastic, wax paper, foil and cardboard; classify as transparent, translucent or opaque.

*How to teach:* Shine the flashlight through each material; classify. Finish by asking: "Wax paper lets some light through but blurs it. Category?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Wax paper lets some light through but blurs it. Category?
  **A:** Translucent.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reflection
*Student:* Use a mirror and flashlight to test the law of reflection; measure angles with a protractor.

*How to teach:* Test the law of reflection with mirror, flashlight and protractor. Before moving on, ask: "Light hits a mirror at 40° from the normal. Reflection angle?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Light hits a mirror at 40° from the normal. Reflection angle?
  **A:** 40°.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Refraction
*Student:* Observe a pencil in water and a coin that 'appears' in a cup when water is added; draw ray diagrams.

*How to teach:* Observe a pencil in water and the 'appearing coin' trick; draw ray diagrams. Check understanding with: "Why does the coin appear when water is added?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why does the coin appear when water is added?
  **A:** Light from the coin refracts at the water's surface and bends toward your eye.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bending Light sim
*Student:* Use PhET 'Bending Light' to explore how materials change light's path.

*How to teach:* Use Bending Light to change materials and record ray paths. To check, ask: "When light enters a slower material at an angle, which way does it bend?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** When light enters a slower material at an angle, which way does it bend?
  **A:** Toward the normal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz with ray diagrams for reflection and refraction.

*How to teach:* Give the ray-diagram quiz. Finish by asking: "State the law of reflection." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** State the law of reflection.
  **A:** Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

**Assessment:** Ray diagram quiz.

**Answer key:** Ray diagram quiz: 1) Reflection: draw incident ray, normal, reflected ray; angle of incidence = angle of reflection (e.g., 30° in → 30° out). 2) Refraction from air into water: ray bends toward the normal. 3) From water into air: bends away from the normal. 4) Classify: clear plastic transparent, wax paper translucent, foil and cardboard opaque.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Manifest Destiny and the trails west

**Prepare:** Image of John Gast's 'American Progress' (1872); O'Sullivan's 1845 excerpt; trail map with distances (Oregon Trail about 2,000 miles from Independence, Missouri; Santa Fe Trail about 900 miles; Mormon Trail about 1,300 miles from Nauvoo, Illinois); Oregon Trail women's diaries; Annie Bidwell biography.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the West was empty. Native nations, Mexicans and others already lived there.
- Believing most pioneers died from Native attacks. Disease (especially cholera), accidents and drownings caused far more deaths.

### Day 1 (Monday): Manifest Destiny
*Student:* Analyze John Gast's 'American Progress' painting and John O'Sullivan's 1845 essay; list who is shown and who is missing.

*How to teach:* Analyze Gast's painting and O'Sullivan's essay; list who is shown and missing.

- **Q:** What does the floating woman (Columbia) carry, and what does it symbolize?
  **A:** A schoolbook and telegraph wire — 'civilization,' education and technology spreading west.
- **Q:** How are Native Americans shown?
  **A:** Fleeing west into darkness with the bison, suggesting they must give way to 'progress' — a one-sided view.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trails west
*Student:* Map the Oregon, California, Mormon and Santa Fe trails and calculate travel times at 15 miles per day.

*How to teach:* Map four trails; calculate travel times at 15 miles per day.

- **Q:** At 15 miles per day, how long did the 2,000-mile Oregon Trail take?
  **A:** About 133 days (roughly 4–5 months).
- **Q:** At 15 miles/day, how long for the ~900-mile Santa Fe Trail?
  **A:** About 60 days.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pioneer women
*Student:* Read diary excerpts from women on the Oregon Trail and a short biography of Annie Bidwell; explain why Wyoming granted women suffrage in 1869.

*How to teach:* Read women's diaries and about Annie Bidwell; explain Wyoming suffrage (1869).

- **Q:** Why did Wyoming grant women the vote in 1869?
  **A:** To attract more women settlers to a mostly male territory, gain publicity, and recognize pioneer women's contributions.
- **Q:** Who was Annie Bidwell?
  **A:** A Chico, California, reformer (wife of John Bidwell) who supported women's suffrage, temperance and education and worked with the Mechoopda Maidu community.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rivers and water
*Student:* Examine why the Mississippi, Missouri and Colorado rivers mattered and read about early Western water-rights conflicts.

*How to teach:* Examine the rivers and early water-rights conflicts.

- **Q:** What is 'first in time, first in right'?
  **A:** The prior-appropriation rule: whoever first used water for a beneficial purpose has the senior right to it — common in the dry West.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on westward expansion plus a one-paragraph analysis of Gast's painting.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and painting analysis.

- **Q:** What was the leading cause of death on the Oregon Trail?
  **A:** Disease, especially cholera.

**Assessment:** Quiz and painting analysis.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Manifest Destiny — belief (John O'Sullivan, 1845) that the U.S. was destined by God to spread across the continent; trails — Oregon (Independence, MO to Oregon City, about 2,000 miles), California (branch to Sacramento Valley), Mormon (Nauvoo, IL to Salt Lake Valley, 1846–47, led by Brigham Young), Santa Fe (trade route, Missouri to Santa Fe); dangers — cholera, river crossings, accidents, weather; Wyoming Territory granted women suffrage in 1869 (to attract women settlers, reward pioneer women's contributions, gain publicity, and as a political gamble); Western water — rivers like the Mississippi, Missouri and Colorado carried trade and irrigation; 'first in time, first in right' (prior appropriation) water-rights conflicts. Painting analysis rubric: describes Columbia carrying a schoolbook and stringing telegraph wire westward, settlers, farmers and railroads following, Native Americans and bison fleeing into darkness; interprets purpose (glorify expansion as progress); notes who's missing or shown negatively.

## Spanish 1B: Direct object pronouns lo, la, los, las

**Prepare:** A shopping bag with items (a shirt, shoes, a book, socks).

**Watch for:**
- Placing the pronoun after a conjugated verb: 'Compré la.' It goes before: La compré.
- Choosing gender by the person, not the noun. The pronoun matches the object: los zapatos → los.

### Day 1 (Monday): DOPs
*Student:* Learn lo/la/los/las and replace the object in 15 sentences: Compré la camisa → La compré.

*How to teach:* Teach lo/la/los/las; replace objects in 15 sentences.

- **Q:** Replace: Compré los zapatos.
  **A:** Los compré.
- **Q:** Replace: Busco la chaqueta.
  **A:** La busco.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Placement
*Student:* Practice pronoun placement before conjugated verbs and attached to infinitives and progressives.

*How to teach:* Teach placement: before conjugated verb, or attached to infinitive/gerund (add accent on gerund).

- **Q:** Rewrite two ways: Voy a comprar la falda.
  **A:** La voy a comprar. / Voy a comprarla.
- **Q:** Rewrite: Estoy leyendo el libro.
  **A:** Lo estoy leyendo. / Estoy leyéndolo.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): ¿Lo compraste?
*Student:* Answer a parent's questions about purchases using pronouns: ¿Compraste los zapatos? Sí, los compré.

*How to teach:* Answer your questions with pronouns.

- **Q:** ¿Compraste los calcetines?
  **A:** Sí, los compré. (or No, no los compré.)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gift story
*Student:* Write a short story about buying gifts for three relatives using preterite and direct object pronouns.

*How to teach:* Write a gift story with preterite and DOPs.

- **Q:** Write: 'I bought a scarf for my mom. I bought it at the market.'
  **A:** Compré una bufanda para mi mamá. La compré en el mercado.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a direct object pronoun quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the DOP quiz.

- **Q:** Replace: ¿Dónde compraste el vestido?
  **A:** ¿Dónde lo compraste?

**Assessment:** Direct object pronoun quiz.

**Answer key:** DOP quiz samples: Compré la camisa → La compré; ¿Tienes los libros? → Sí, los tengo; Voy a comprar el regalo → Lo voy a comprar / Voy a comprarlo; Estoy lavando los platos → Los estoy lavando / Estoy lavándolos; ¿Viste a María? → Sí, la vi. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Composing a melody

**Prepare:** Chrome Music Lab Song Maker; staff paper.

**Watch for:**
- Making phrases that wander. Correct: use a motif and repeat or vary it.
- Ending on a random note. Correct: answer phrases usually end on the tonic (home note).

### Day 1 (Monday): Motifs
*Student:* Create 4 two-measure motifs in Song Maker and choose the strongest.

*How to teach:* Create 4 two-measure motifs; choose the strongest.

- **Q:** What is a motif?
  **A:** A short musical idea that is repeated and developed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Phrases
*Student:* Develop the motif into an 8-measure phrase with a question-answer structure.

*How to teach:* Develop into an 8-measure question-answer phrase.

- **Q:** How does an 'answer' phrase sound different from a 'question' phrase?
  **A:** The question ends unresolved (often on a note other than the tonic); the answer ends on the tonic, sounding finished.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Form
*Student:* Organize phrases into ABA form.

*How to teach:* Organize into ABA form.

- **Q:** What is ABA form?
  **A:** Section A, a contrasting section B, then a return of A.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Harmony
*Student:* Add chords under your melody.

*How to teach:* Add chords.

- **Q:** Which chord usually ends a piece in C major?
  **A:** C major (I).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share a draft with family and record feedback.

*How to teach:* Share with family; record feedback.

- **Q:** Why get feedback on a draft?
  **A:** Listeners notice what's memorable or confusing.

**Assessment:** Composition draft.

**Answer key:** Composition draft: 4 = clear motif, 8-measure question-answer phrase ending on tonic, ABA form with contrasting B, chords that fit, feedback recorded; 3 = one element weak; 2 = no clear form; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Designing a strength workout

**Prepare:** Resistance bands, workout log. Rep-range chart: endurance 15–20 reps, 30–60 s rest; strength/hypertrophy for teens 8–12 reps with good form, 60–90 s rest.

**Watch for:**
- Training only 'mirror muscles.' Correct: balance opposing muscle groups to prevent injury and posture problems.
- Adding weight before mastering form. Correct: progress gradually (about 10% or a few reps at a time) only with good form.

### Day 1 (Monday): Endurance vs. strength
*Student:* Compare rep ranges and rest periods for endurance and strength; do an endurance circuit (15-20 reps).

*How to teach:* Compare rep ranges and rest; do an endurance circuit (15–20 reps).

- **Q:** Which builds muscular endurance: 3×20 light or 3×8 heavier?
  **A:** 3×20 with light resistance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Balance
*Student:* Plan a full-body workout that pairs opposing muscles (chest/back, quads/hamstrings).

*How to teach:* Plan a full-body workout pairing opposing muscles.

- **Q:** Name the opposing muscle for chest and for quadriceps.
  **A:** Chest (pectorals) ↔ upper back (lats/rhomboids); quadriceps ↔ hamstrings.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Try it
*Student:* Complete your designed workout and record sets, reps and perceived exertion.

*How to teach:* Do the workout; record sets, reps and RPE (1–10).

- **Q:** What does an RPE of 7 mean?
  **A:** Hard effort, but could do a few more reps with good form.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Adjust exercises that felt too easy or hard using the progression principle.

*How to teach:* Adjust exercises using progression.

- **Q:** What is the progression principle?
  **A:** Gradually increase the load (reps, resistance, sets) as the body adapts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Submit your workout plan with rationale for each exercise.

*How to teach:* Submit plan with rationale.

- **Q:** Why include a warm-up before strength work?
  **A:** Raises muscle temperature and prepares joints, reducing injury risk.

**Assessment:** Written strength workout plan.

**Answer key:** Workout plan rubric: 4 = warm-up, 6–8 exercises covering all major muscle groups with opposing pairs, sets/reps/rest matched to goal, progression plan, cool-down, rationale for each; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = unbalanced or missing parameters; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Human trafficking, sextortion and online exploitation

**Prepare:** Parental preview: read CA DOJ human-trafficking pages and NCMEC 'Take It Down' (takeitdown.ncmec.org). Note the National Human Trafficking Hotline 1-888-373-7888 (text 233733). Reassure the child they will never be in trouble for telling you.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking trafficking always involves strangers kidnapping someone. Correct: most victims are recruited by someone they know or meet online, using manipulation, not force.
- Believing a sextortion victim should pay or stay silent. Correct: it is never the victim's fault; stop responding, don't pay, save evidence, tell an adult and report.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is trafficking
*Student:* Learn what human trafficking is and that it happens in California, often by someone the victim knows.

*How to teach:* Define trafficking as using force, fraud or coercion to exploit someone for labor or commercial sex (any commercial sex involving a minor is trafficking). Note it happens in California, often by someone known.

- **Q:** What is human trafficking?
  **A:** Exploiting a person for labor or commercial sex through force, fraud or coercion; any minor involved in commercial sex is a victim of trafficking regardless of force.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Grooming
*Student:* Identify grooming and recruitment tactics online and in person (gifts, secrecy, isolation).

*How to teach:* List grooming steps: targeting, gaining trust, filling a need (gifts, attention), isolating, secrecy, control.

- **Q:** Name three grooming red flags.
  **A:** Excessive gifts or attention, asking to keep things secret, trying to isolate you from family/friends, moving the chat to private apps, asking for photos, promising jobs or modeling deals.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sextortion
*Student:* Learn what sextortion is, that it is never the victim's fault, and steps: stop, don't pay, save evidence, tell an adult, use NCMEC Take It Down.

*How to teach:* Explain sextortion (threats to share images unless you send money or more images). Teach: stop responding, don't pay, save evidence (screenshots, usernames), tell an adult, report to NCMEC (CyberTipline) and use Take It Down.

- **Q:** What are the steps if someone threatens to share an image of you?
  **A:** Stop responding, don't pay or send more, save evidence, block after saving, tell a trusted adult, report to the platform and NCMEC CyberTipline, and use Take It Down to help remove images.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Help
*Student:* Role-play telling a trusted adult and list the National Human Trafficking Hotline.

*How to teach:* Role-play telling you about an uncomfortable online contact. Write the National Human Trafficking Hotline number.

- **Q:** What is the National Human Trafficking Hotline?
  **A:** 1-888-373-7888, or text 233733; call 911 in immediate danger.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a safety tip card for younger teens.

*How to teach:* The child makes a tip card for younger teens with red flags, steps and contacts.

- **Q:** Whose fault is sextortion?
  **A:** Never the victim's — the person making threats is committing a crime.

**Assessment:** Safety tip card.

**Answer key:** Safety tip card: 4 = accurate definition, at least three grooming red flags, sextortion steps (stop, don't pay, save evidence, tell, report/Take It Down), hotline number and trusted adult, age-appropriate wording; 3 = one element missing; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
