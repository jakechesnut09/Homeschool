# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 30

## English Language Arts 8: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 3–4

**Prepare:** Read Acts 3–4 (Folger). Prepare a three-column chart: Athenian court / lovers / mechanicals & fairies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the comedy is random. The confusion follows from specific mistakes (Puck's potion) and is resolved by Oberon.
- Confusing subplot with main plot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bottom transformed
*Student:* Read Act 3, Scene 1 and analyze how Bottom's oblivious dialogue with Titania creates humor through dramatic irony.

*How to teach:* Read Act 3, Scene 1 aloud; let the child read Bottom. Ask what the audience knows that Bottom does not and why that makes the scene funny.

- **Q:** Why is the scene funny?
  **A:** Bottom has an ass's head but doesn't know it, while the queen of fairies adores him — dramatic irony.
- **Q:** What wise line does Bottom say?
  **A:** 'Reason and love keep little company together nowadays.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The lovers' quarrel
*Student:* Read Act 3, Scene 2 and track how Puck's mistake reverses the lovers' feelings; act out the Hermia–Helena insult exchange.

*How to teach:* Read Act 3, Scene 2, tracking how Puck's mistake and Oberon's fix make both men love Helena. Act out the Hermia–Helena insult exchange with exaggerated expression.

- **Q:** Why does Helena think everyone mocks her?
  **A:** Now both men claim to love her, which she thinks is a cruel joke.
- **Q:** Give an insult.
  **A:** Helena calls Hermia 'puppet'; Hermia calls her a 'painted maypole.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Resolution
*Student:* Read Act 4 and explain how Oberon restores order and how Bottom describes his 'most rare vision.'

*How to teach:* Read Act 4. The child explains step by step how Oberon restores order and paraphrases Bottom's speech about his 'most rare vision.'

- **Q:** How does order return?
  **A:** Titania gives up the boy, Oberon frees her, Puck removes Bottom's head, Demetrius stays in love with Helena, and Theseus overrules Egeus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Structure chart
*Student:* Complete a three-column chart (Athenian court, lovers, mechanicals/fairies) showing how the plots cross and resolve.

*How to teach:* The child completes the three-column chart showing each plotline's conflict, where it crosses the others in the woods, and how it resolves. Finish by asking: "Where do the plots cross?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Where do the plots cross?
  **A:** In the woods.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Acts 3–4 with a short response on dramatic irony.

*How to teach:* Give the Acts 3–4 quiz with a short response explaining one example of dramatic irony. Before moving on, ask: "What is Bottom's vision?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What is Bottom's vision?
  **A:** He thinks he had a strange dream.

**Assessment:** Plot structure chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Plot structure chart: each column shows the conflict (Theseus's wedding & Egeus's demand; the four lovers' tangle; Oberon–Titania quarrel & mechanicals' play), how they cross in the woods, and the resolution (Oberon frees Titania, lovers paired correctly, Theseus overrules Egeus, triple wedding, mechanicals' play chosen). Quiz: Puck gives Bottom an ass's head; Titania loves Bottom; Oberon anoints Demetrius, who loves Helena; Hermia and Helena quarrel; Puck fixes Lysander; Bottom's 'most rare vision.' Short response: dramatic irony — we know Bottom has an ass's head while he doesn't.

## Mathematics 8: The Pythagorean theorem and its converse

**Prepare:** Graph paper, scissors, ruler, a long string with 12 equally spaced knots (for a 3-4-5 triangle).

**Watch for:**
- Adding legs then squaring: (a + b)² instead of a² + b². Square each first.
- Using a² + b² = c² where c isn't the longest side. c is always the hypotenuse, opposite the right angle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finding a pattern
*Student:* Draw right triangles on a grid, build squares on each side and record areas to discover a^2 + b^2 = c^2.

*How to teach:* Draw right triangles on grid, build squares on each side, record areas. Before moving on, ask: "For a 3-4-5 right triangle, what are the areas of the squares?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** For a 3-4-5 right triangle, what are the areas of the squares?
  **A:** 9, 16 and 25; 9 + 16 = 25.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Visual proof
*Student:* Cut and rearrange four congruent right triangles inside a square (the 'two squares' proof) and explain in writing why it proves the theorem.

*How to teach:* Cut four congruent right triangles and arrange them in an (a + b) square two ways. Check understanding with: "Explain the 'two squares' proof." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Explain the 'two squares' proof.
  **A:** Both big squares have side a + b and contain the same four triangles. The uncovered area is c² in one and a² + b² in the other, so a² + b² = c².

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finding unknown sides
*Student:* Solve 10 problems finding a missing leg or hypotenuse, leaving irrational answers as square roots and as decimals.

*How to teach:* Child solves 10 missing-side problems, leaving answers as square roots and decimals. To check, ask: "Legs 6 and 8: hypotenuse?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Legs 6 and 8: hypotenuse?
  **A:** 10.
- **Q:** Hypotenuse 13, leg 5: other leg?
  **A:** √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12.
- **Q:** Legs 5 and 7?
  **A:** √74 ≈ 8.60.

### Day 4 (Thursday): The converse
*Student:* Test side lengths (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-7-9) with the converse; use a 3-4-5 knotted string to check a corner of a room for square.

*How to teach:* Test triples with the converse; check a room corner with the 3-4-5 knotted string. Finish by asking: "Is 6-7-9 a right triangle?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Is 6-7-9 a right triangle?
  **A:** No: 36 + 49 = 85, not 81.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: explain the proof in your own words and solve 6 problems including converse questions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz plus written proof explanation. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Is 5-12-13 a right triangle?
  **A:** Yes: 25 + 144 = 169 = 13².

**Assessment:** Written proof explanation and quiz.

**Answer key:** Proof explanation rubric (4 levels): 4 = describes a square of side a + b containing four congruent right triangles; the leftover area is c² in one arrangement and a² + b² in the other, so a² + b² = c²; 3 = correct idea with a gap; 2 = describes the picture without the area reasoning; 1 = restates the formula only. Quiz: 1) legs 6, 8 → 10. 2) legs 5, 7 → √74 ≈ 8.60. 3) hyp 13, leg 5 → 12. 4) legs 2, 3 → √13 ≈ 3.61. 5) Is 5-12-13 right? Yes (25 + 144 = 169). 6) Is 6-7-9 right? No (36 + 49 = 85 ≠ 81).

## Science 8 (Integrated): Variation and survival

**Prepare:** Tweezers, spoons, clothespins, beads/beans/paper dots of several colors, patterned fabric, timer, cups.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking individuals evolve. Populations change over generations; individuals don't change their genes.
- Thinking organisms 'try' to adapt. Variation exists first; the environment selects.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bird beak lab
*Student:* Use tweezers, spoons and clothespins as 'beaks' to collect different 'foods'; record which beaks survive.

*How to teach:* Use tools as beaks to collect foods. Before moving on, ask: "Which beak works best for small seeds?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Which beak works best for small seeds?
  **A:** Tweezers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Camouflage simulation
*Student:* Scatter colored paper dots on patterned fabric; 'hunt' for 30 seconds and record which colors survive.

*How to teach:* Scatter dots on fabric; hunt 30 s. Check understanding with: "Which colors survive?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Which colors survive?
  **A:** Those matching the background.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Next generation
*Student:* Let survivors 'reproduce' and repeat the hunt for 3 generations; track color frequencies.

*How to teach:* Survivors reproduce; repeat 3 generations. To check, ask: "What happens to camouflaged colors?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What happens to camouflaged colors?
  **A:** They increase.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Explaining the data
*Student:* Write a CER explaining how variation led to change in the population.

*How to teach:* Write a CER on population change. Finish by asking: "What is natural selection?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What is natural selection?
  **A:** Individuals with helpful traits survive and reproduce more.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on natural selection vocabulary and scenarios.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Before moving on, ask: "Define adaptation." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Define adaptation.
  **A:** An inherited trait that improves survival and reproduction.

**Assessment:** Simulation data and CER.

**Answer key:** Simulation data: color frequencies tracked for 3 generations; colors matching the fabric increase. CER rubric (4 levels): 4 = claim answers the question; evidence cites specific measured data (numbers with units) from the lab; reasoning links evidence to the science idea; 3 = all parts, reasoning thin; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only. Claim example: 'The green dots increased from 25% to 60% because they were camouflaged and survived to reproduce.'

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: The end of Reconstruction and Jim Crow

**Prepare:** Parent preview: congressional testimony on Klan violence is disturbing. Thomas Nast cartoons; 1871 testimony excerpts; information on the 1876 election; examples of Jim Crow laws; voter registration data (e.g., Louisiana registered Black voters fell from about 130,000 in 1896 to about 1,300 in 1904). Prepare at least 4 DBQ documents. Write the unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Reconstruction achieved nothing. It produced the 13th–15th Amendments, Black officeholders, public schools and churches, even though many gains were rolled back.
- Thinking Jim Crow laws existed only in the South. Segregation and discrimination existed nationwide, though Southern laws were most systematic.

### Day 1 (Monday): Violence and the KKK
*Student:* Read 1871 congressional testimony from survivors of Klan violence and explain the purpose of the Enforcement Acts.

*How to teach:* Read 1871 Klan testimony; explain the Enforcement Acts.

- **Q:** What was the purpose of the KKK during Reconstruction?
  **A:** To restore white supremacy by terrorizing Black people and white Republicans, especially to stop Black voting.
- **Q:** What did the Enforcement Acts do?
  **A:** Made it a federal crime to interfere with voting and allowed the president to use troops and suspend habeas corpus against the Klan.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compromise of 1877
*Student:* Explain how the disputed 1876 election ended Reconstruction and federal troop presence.

*How to teach:* Explain the 1876 election and Compromise of 1877.

- **Q:** What did Democrats get in the Compromise of 1877?
  **A:** The withdrawal of federal troops from the South, ending Reconstruction (Republicans got Hayes as president).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Jim Crow
*Student:* Analyze examples of Jim Crow laws, poll taxes and literacy tests and their effect on voter registration data.

*How to teach:* Analyze Jim Crow laws, poll taxes and literacy tests with registration data.

- **Q:** How did poll taxes and literacy tests limit Black voting without naming race?
  **A:** Many Black citizens were poor or denied schooling, and white officials applied tests unfairly; grandfather clauses exempted most whites.
- **Q:** What did Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) decide?
  **A:** Segregation was constitutional if facilities were 'separate but equal.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): DBQ essay
*Student:* Write a DBQ essay: 'Was Reconstruction a success or a failure?' using at least 4 documents.

*How to teach:* Write the DBQ essay: Was Reconstruction a success or failure?

- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence for 'success.'
  **A:** The 14th and 15th Amendments, Black officeholders like Revels, public schools and new Black colleges.
- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence for 'failure.'
  **A:** Klan violence, Black Codes, sharecropping debt, withdrawal of troops in 1877 and Jim Crow disenfranchisement.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Unit test on Reconstruction.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 9 test.

- **Q:** What ended Reconstruction?
  **A:** The Compromise of 1877 and the withdrawal of federal troops from the South.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test and DBQ essay.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test (suggested): Reconstruction plans; 13th–15th Amendments; Black Codes; Freedmen's Bureau; Revels and Smalls; KKK founded 1865–66, used terror to stop Black voting and Republican government; Enforcement Acts (1870–71, including the Ku Klux Klan Act) let the federal government prosecute Klan violence; Compromise of 1877 — disputed 1876 election (Tilden won the popular vote; 20 electoral votes disputed in Florida, Louisiana, South Carolina and Oregon) resolved by giving Hayes the presidency in exchange for withdrawing federal troops from the South, ending Reconstruction; Jim Crow — segregation laws, poll taxes, literacy tests, grandfather clauses; Plessy v. Ferguson (1896) 'separate but equal.' DBQ essay rubric (1–4): thesis taking a position (success, failure, or mixed), uses 4+ documents accurately, outside knowledge, sourcing, counterargument, organization.

*How to teach:* Watch the film with Spanish audio/subtitles; take character notes.

*How to teach:* Write a 10-sentence preterite plot summary.

Unit 7 test: media vocabulary, acabar de, gustar-like verbs.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Stop-motion animation: planning

**Prepare:** Phone with Stop Motion Studio (free), clay or LEGO figures, tripod or stack of books, steady lighting, a short stop-motion example (e.g., an Aardman clip).

**Watch for:**
- Moving figures too far between frames. Correct: tiny moves create smooth motion.
- Bumping the camera. Correct: fix the camera with a tripod or tape.

### Day 1 (Monday): Animation basics
*Student:* Watch a short stop-motion example and calculate frames needed for 30 seconds at 12 fps.

*How to teach:* Watch an example; calculate frames for 30 s at 12 fps.

- **Q:** How many frames for 30 seconds at 12 frames per second?
  **A:** 30 × 12 = 360 frames.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test animation
*Student:* Make a 3-second test animation of a bouncing ball.

*How to teach:* Make a 3-second bouncing-ball test.

- **Q:** How many frames is 3 seconds at 12 fps?
  **A:** 36 frames.
- **Q:** How can you show the ball speeding up as it falls?
  **A:** Space the ball's positions farther apart near the bottom (ease in/out) and squash it on impact.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story
*Student:* Write a short story idea for a 30-second animation.

*How to teach:* Write a 30-second story idea.

- **Q:** What does a 30-second story need?
  **A:** One character, one simple goal or problem, and a quick resolution.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Storyboard
*Student:* Draw a 12-panel storyboard.

*How to teach:* Draw a 12-panel storyboard.

- **Q:** What is a storyboard?
  **A:** A sequence of sketches showing each shot or key moment, with notes on action and sound.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Build characters and set.

*How to teach:* Build characters and set.

- **Q:** Why build sturdy characters?
  **A:** They must hold poses for many frames without falling.

**Assessment:** Storyboard and test animation.

**Answer key:** Storyboard and test animation: 4 = 12 panels showing beginning-middle-end with shot notes; test animation 3 seconds, smooth motion with spacing changes, steady camera; 3 = minor jumps; 2 = rough; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Jumps and throws: triple jump and shot technique

**Prepare:** Sand area or soft grass, tape measure, softball or 4-lb medicine ball, protractor (optional) for angle testing.

**Watch for:**
- Hop and step on the wrong feet. Correct: hop takes off and lands on the same foot, step lands on the other, jump lands on both.
- Throwing the shot like a baseball. Correct: the put is a push from the neck with the elbow high.

### Day 1 (Monday): Triple jump phases
*Student:* Practice hop-step-jump from a standing start, then with a short approach.

*How to teach:* Hop (same foot), step (opposite foot), jump (both feet landing) standing, then with a short approach.

- **Q:** If you hop off your right foot, which foot do you land the hop on?
  **A:** The right foot; then step onto the left; then jump and land on both feet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Triple jump measure
*Student:* Take three measured jumps and record the best.

*How to teach:* Three measured jumps; record best.

- **Q:** Where is the triple jump measured from?
  **A:** From the takeoff board/line to the nearest mark in the landing area.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Standing put
*Student:* Practice the standing put with a softball or light medicine ball, pushing not throwing.

*How to teach:* Standing put: ball at neck under jaw, elbow high, push up and out from the legs through the hips.

- **Q:** Why push instead of throw?
  **A:** Rules require it, and pushing with the whole body prevents elbow and shoulder injury.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Optimal angle
*Student:* Test release angles of about 30, 40 and 50 degrees and record distances.

*How to teach:* Test release angles ~30°, 40°, 50°.

- **Q:** Which angle gave the longest distance and why isn't it exactly 45°?
  **A:** Usually around 35–42°: the ball is released above the ground and people can push faster at lower angles, so the optimal angle is below 45°.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Explain how momentum transfers through each phase of the triple jump.

*How to teach:* Explain momentum transfer in the triple jump.

- **Q:** How is momentum transferred?
  **A:** Horizontal speed from the approach is carried through each phase; each takeoff must keep speed while redirecting some upward, with arms and free leg driving forward.

**Assessment:** Triple jump and put distance log with explanation.

**Answer key:** Distance log and explanation: all trials recorded with best marks; explanation proficient if it covers hop-step-jump order with correct feet, the role of approach speed, minimizing braking at each contact, and the push technique and release angle for the put.

## Health Education 8: Safety unit review and assessment

**Prepare:** Gather a first-aid kit and the Red Cross checklist, a CPR pillow, flashcards and the unit test (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a family plan isn't needed because 911 exists. Correct: phones and roads can fail; you need meeting places and out-of-area contacts.
- Forgetting that supplies expire. Correct: check dates and restock regularly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kit
*Student:* Inventory and restock a home first-aid kit.

*How to teach:* Inventory the kit against a Red Cross list (bandages, gauze, tape, gloves, antiseptic wipes, scissors, tweezers, thermometer, cold pack, emergency blanket) and list items to buy.

- **Q:** Why include disposable gloves?
  **A:** To protect against contact with blood and body fluids.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Action plan
*Student:* Create a family emergency action plan with contacts and meeting places.

*How to teach:* Create a plan: emergency contacts, out-of-area contact, meeting place near home and outside neighborhood, evacuation routes, go-bag.

- **Q:** Why choose an out-of-area contact?
  **A:** Long-distance calls/texts may get through when local lines are overloaded; everyone can check in with one person.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Skills
*Student:* Demonstrate CPR and choking response again.

*How to teach:* The child demonstrates hands-only CPR and choking response; check with the week 25 checklist.

- **Q:** What is the compression rate?
  **A:** 100–120 per minute.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review unit concepts with flashcards.

*How to teach:* Review flashcards for weeks 25–29.

- **Q:** Reach, throw, don't ___?
  **A:** Go — don't swim out to rescue someone unless trained.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit test
*Student:* Take the safety unit test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test.

- **Q:** What do the 5 D's stand for?
  **A:** Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay, Document.

**Assessment:** Skills demo and unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: 1) Check-Call-Care 2) CPR 100–120/min, ~2 in 3) AED: turn on first 4) choking sign = hands at throat 5) heat stroke = 911 6) rip current: swim parallel 7) AQI >150 unhealthy for all 8) helmet required under 18 9) 5 D's 10) found gun: don't touch, leave, tell 11) threats: report. Skills demo: proficient on week 25 checklist.
