# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 29

## English Language Arts 8: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 1–2

**Prepare:** Use the free Folger Shakespeare edition of 'A Midsummer Night's Dream' (folger.edu) and a Folger video on the Globe Theatre. Read Acts 1–2 yourself; a plot summary (Folger synopsis) helps.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'wherefore' or 'thou' make the language impossible. Paraphrase line by line with Folger's notes.
- Losing track of the lovers. Keep a character map with arrows for who loves whom and update it after each potion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Introducing Shakespeare
*Student:* Watch a short Folger video on the Globe Theatre, then read Act 1, Scene 1 and paraphrase Egeus's complaint and Hermia's choice.

*How to teach:* Watch the Globe video, then read Act 1, Scene 1; child paraphrases Egeus's complaint and Hermia's choice. Finish by asking: "What is Hermia's choice?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What is Hermia's choice?
  **A:** Marry Demetrius as her father demands, be put to death, or become a nun — 'to abjure forever the society of men.'
- **Q:** What does 'The course of true love never did run smooth' mean?
  **A:** True love always faces obstacles — said by Lysander.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The mechanicals
*Student:* Read Act 1, Scene 2 and list each mechanical's assigned role in 'Pyramus and Thisbe' and what Bottom's eagerness reveals.

*How to teach:* Read Act 1, Scene 2 aloud with funny voices for the mechanicals. The child lists each mechanical's assigned role and what Bottom's eagerness shows about him.

- **Q:** Which roles are assigned?
  **A:** Bottom: Pyramus; Flute: Thisbe; Snug: the Lion; Starveling: Thisbe's mother; Snout: Pyramus's father; Quince: Thisbe's father.
- **Q:** What does Bottom's eagerness reveal?
  **A:** He's overconfident and comic — he wants to play every part, including Thisbe and the Lion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Into the woods
*Student:* Read Act 2, Scenes 1–2 and draw a character map showing who loves whom before and after Puck's love potion.

*How to teach:* Read Act 2 (summarize long fairy speeches if needed). The child draws a love map with arrows before and after Puck's mistake.

- **Q:** Who loves whom after Puck's mistake?
  **A:** Before: Lysander and Demetrius both love Hermia; Hermia loves Lysander; Helena loves Demetrius. After Puck anoints Lysander: Lysander loves Helena, Demetrius still loves Hermia, Hermia loves Lysander, Helena loves Demetrius — no one's love is returned.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Language play
*Student:* Clap out iambic pentameter in 4 lines and explain 5 puns or ironic lines (e.g., Lysander's 'The course of true love never did run smooth').

*How to teach:* Clap da-DUM five times while reading 'The course of true love never did run smooth.' The child marks stresses in 4 lines and explains 5 puns or ironic lines in plain English. To check, ask: "What is iambic pentameter?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What is iambic pentameter?
  **A:** Ten syllables per line in five da-DUM beats.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Acts 1–2 plus a paraphrase of a 10-line passage into modern English.

*How to teach:* Give the Acts 1–2 quiz, then the 10-line paraphrase task. Check that the paraphrase keeps every idea.

- **Q:** Who is Puck?
  **A:** Robin Goodfellow, Oberon's mischievous servant.

**Assessment:** Paraphrase task and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz Acts 1–2: 1) Egeus wants Hermia to marry Demetrius; Athenian law says she must obey, die, or become a nun. 2) Hermia loves Lysander; they plan to flee through the woods. 3) Helena loves Demetrius and tells him of the plan. 4) The mechanicals: Bottom = Pyramus, Flute = Thisbe, Snug = Lion. 5) Oberon and Titania quarrel over a changeling boy. 6) Puck puts the potion on Lysander by mistake; he wakes loving Helena. Paraphrase task: score for accuracy of meaning (2), modern English (1), no omitted ideas (1).

## Mathematics 8: Square roots and side lengths of squares

**Prepare:** Graph paper, ruler. Make a perfect-squares chart 1² to 15².

**Watch for:**
- Thinking √50 ≈ 25. Square root asks 'what times itself', not 'half of'.
- Forgetting x² = p has two solutions. x² = 81 → x = 9 or −9.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tilted squares
*Student:* Draw squares on a grid with tilted sides; find their areas by decomposing or surrounding with a larger square.

*How to teach:* Child draws a tilted square with vertices (0,0), (3,1), (2,4), (−1,3) and finds the area by surrounding it with a 4×4 square and subtracting four triangles. Finish by asking: "Area of that tilted square?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Area of that tilted square?
  **A:** 16 − 4(1.5) = 10 square units.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Side lengths as square roots
*Student:* Write side lengths of those squares as square roots (e.g., sqrt 10) and estimate each between two whole numbers.

*How to teach:* Side length is √(area). Child writes side lengths as square roots and estimates.

- **Q:** Between which two whole numbers is √10?
  **A:** 3 and 4 (closer to 3, since 9 < 10 < 16).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Perfect squares
*Student:* Memorize perfect squares 1–225; solve x^2 = p for 10 values of p including non-perfect squares.

*How to teach:* Drill perfect squares 1–225. Solve x² = p for 10 values.

- **Q:** Solve x² = 81 and x² = 20.
  **A:** x = ±9; x = ±√20 ≈ ±4.47.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Estimating roots
*Student:* Estimate sqrt 50, sqrt 72 and sqrt 130 to the tenth by guess-and-check squaring; check with a calculator.

*How to teach:* Estimate by squaring guesses to the tenth. To check, ask: "Estimate √50, √72 and √130 to the tenth." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Estimate √50, √72 and √130 to the tenth.
  **A:** √50 ≈ 7.1, √72 ≈ 8.5, √130 ≈ 11.4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: tilted-square areas, solving x^2 = p and placing square roots on a number line.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Place √30 on a number line.
  **A:** Between 5 and 6, about 5.5 (5.48).

**Assessment:** Quiz on square roots.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Tilted square with side from (0,0) to (3,1): area 10, side √10. 2) Tilted square side from (0,0) to (2,2): area 8, side √8. 3) x² = 81 → ±9. 4) x² = 20 → ±√20 ≈ ±4.47. 5) x² = 144 → ±12. 6) Place √10 (≈3.16), √30 (≈5.48), √2 (≈1.41) on a number line. 7) √50 between 7 and 8 (≈7.07).

## Science 8 (Integrated): Technology and inheritance

**Prepare:** Images of teosinte and modern corn, wolves and dog breeds; short readings on insulin-producing bacteria, Golden Rice and CRISPR. Prepare the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing artificial selection with genetic engineering. Selective breeding chooses parents; genetic engineering directly changes DNA.
- Thinking GMOs are all the same. Each modification must be evaluated.

### Day 1 (Monday): Artificial selection
*Student:* Compare wild teosinte to modern corn and dog breeds to wolves; list traits humans selected for.

*How to teach:* Compare teosinte to corn and wolves to dogs. Finish by asking: "Trait humans selected in corn?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Trait humans selected in corn?
  **A:** Larger ears with many soft kernels.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Genetic engineering
*Student:* Read about insulin-producing bacteria and Golden Rice; summarize the steps.

*How to teach:* Read about insulin and Golden Rice. Before moving on, ask: "How do bacteria make human insulin?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** How do bacteria make human insulin?
  **A:** The human insulin gene is inserted into bacterial DNA (a plasmid); bacteria then produce insulin.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): CRISPR & ethics
*Student:* Read about CRISPR and write a balanced pro/con chart about editing crop and human genes.

*How to teach:* Read about CRISPR; pro/con chart. Check understanding with: "One pro and one con of editing human embryos?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** One pro and one con of editing human embryos?
  **A:** Pro: prevent genetic diseases; con: unknown effects passed to future generations, ethical concerns.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review heredity concepts with a practice test.

*How to teach:* Practice test. To check, ask: "BB × bb offspring?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** BB × bb offspring?
  **A:** All Bb.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Unit test on heredity and genetic technologies.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test. Finish by asking: "What does Golden Rice produce?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What does Golden Rice produce?
  **A:** Beta-carotene (vitamin A precursor).

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test sample answers: asexual vs sexual; Punnett squares; DNA bases; gene→protein→trait; mutation types; artificial selection; insulin gene inserted into bacteria; Golden Rice makes beta-carotene; CRISPR edits DNA at targeted sites.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Freedom and new rights

**Prepare:** Freedmen's Bureau letters (National Archives 'Freedmen's Bureau records'); 14th and 15th Amendment texts; biographies of Hiram Revels and Robert Smalls; readings on the Exodusters and Buffalo Soldiers.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the 15th Amendment gave everyone the vote. It barred denial based on race, color or previous servitude, but not sex; women were excluded, and states later used poll taxes and literacy tests.
- Believing freedpeople received land. 'Forty acres and a mule' (Sherman's Field Order No. 15) was largely reversed by Johnson; most became sharecroppers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Freedmen's Bureau
*Student:* Read letters from freedpeople to the Freedmen's Bureau seeking family members, schools and fair wages.

*How to teach:* Read letters to the Freedmen's Bureau.

- **Q:** What did freedpeople most often seek from the Bureau?
  **A:** Reuniting with family members sold away, schools for their children, and fair wages and contracts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): 14th and 15th Amendments
*Student:* Paraphrase the citizenship, equal protection and voting clauses and explain whom they protected and whom they left out.

*How to teach:* Paraphrase the 14th and 15th Amendments; who was protected and left out.

- **Q:** Paraphrase the 14th Amendment's equal protection clause.
  **A:** States must treat all people equally under the law.
- **Q:** Who was left out of the 15th Amendment?
  **A:** Women of all races; also most Native Americans and Asian immigrants could not vote because they were not citizens.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Black political leaders
*Student:* Research Hiram Revels and Robert Smalls and write a short biography card for each.

*How to teach:* Research Revels and Smalls; write biography cards.

- **Q:** What 'first' did Hiram Revels achieve?
  **A:** First African American U.S. senator (1870, Mississippi).
- **Q:** How did Robert Smalls gain freedom?
  **A:** In 1862 he piloted the Confederate ship Planter out of Charleston Harbor and delivered it to the Union navy with his family and others aboard.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Moving north and west
*Student:* Read about the Exodusters to Kansas and the Buffalo Soldiers and list push and pull factors.

*How to teach:* Read about the Exodusters and Buffalo Soldiers; list push and pull factors.

- **Q:** Give one push and one pull factor for the Exodusters.
  **A:** Push: violence and debt in the South after Reconstruction; pull: cheap land and freedom in Kansas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the Reconstruction amendments and the Freedmen's Bureau.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What was sharecropping?
  **A:** A system where families farmed a landowner's land in exchange for a share of the crop, often leaving them in debt.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Freedmen's Bureau (1865) — provided food, medical care, schools (helped found Howard, Fisk and others), negotiated labor contracts, helped reunite families; 14th Amendment (1868) — all persons born or naturalized in the U.S. are citizens; no state may deny due process or equal protection; 15th Amendment (1870) — right to vote can't be denied because of race, color or previous condition of servitude; left out women, most Native Americans (not citizens until 1924), Chinese immigrants (barred from naturalization); sharecropping — farming land for a share of the crop, often trapped in debt; Hiram Revels — first Black U.S. senator (Mississippi, 1870); Robert Smalls — escaped slavery by commandeering the Confederate ship Planter (1862), later congressman from South Carolina; Exodusters — about 6,000 or more Black Southerners who moved to Kansas in 1879 (push: violence, sharecropping debt, end of Reconstruction; pull: land, freedom); Buffalo Soldiers — Black regiments (9th and 10th Cavalry, 24th and 25th Infantry) formed 1866 serving in the West.

## Spanish 1B: Acabar de + infinitive and verbs like gustar

**Prepare:** A free Señor Wooly video. Verbs: aburrir, interesar, encantar, importar, faltar, doler.

**Watch for:**
- Using a singular verb with plural things: 'Me aburre las películas.' Use aburren with plurals.
- Conjugating acabar de with the second verb: 'Acabo de vi.' Use the infinitive: Acabo de ver.

### Day 1 (Monday): Acabar de
*Student:* Learn acabar de + infinitive and write 8 sentences about things you just did.

*How to teach:* Teach acabar de + infinitive; 8 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'We just ate.'
  **A:** Acabamos de comer.
- **Q:** Write: 'She just watched a comedy.'
  **A:** Ella acaba de ver una comedia.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gustar-like verbs
*Student:* Learn me aburre(n), me interesa(n), me encanta(n), me importa(n), me falta(n) and write opinions about movies.

*How to teach:* Teach gustar-like verbs; write movie opinions.

- **Q:** Write: 'Dramas bore me.'
  **A:** Me aburren los dramas.
- **Q:** Write: 'Science fiction interests my brother.'
  **A:** A mi hermano le interesa la ciencia ficción.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Señor Wooly
*Student:* Watch a free Señor Wooly video and describe what the characters like, bore, or interest them.

*How to teach:* Watch a Señor Wooly video; describe characters' likes/boredom/interests.

- **Q:** Use 'interesar' about a character you saw.
  **A:** Example: Al chico le interesa ___.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversation
*Student:* Discuss favorite movies and shows with a parent using five gustar-like verbs.

*How to teach:* Discuss favorite movies with five gustar-like verbs.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What kind of movies interest you?'
  **A:** ¿Qué tipo de películas te interesan?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a gustar-like verbs quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the gustar-like verbs quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My feet hurt.'
  **A:** Me duelen los pies.

**Assessment:** Gustar-like verbs quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key samples: Horror movies bore me → Me aburren las películas de horror; Documentaries interest us → Nos interesan los documentales; My head hurts → Me duele la cabeza; I just watched a movie → Acabo de ver una película; He is missing a ticket → Le falta un boleto; The environment matters to them → Les importa el medio ambiente. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Photo essay and media influence

**Prepare:** Phone/camera, free photo editor (built-in Photos app, Snapseed). Examples of one event shown in news, ads and social media. Consent/permission form or verbal script.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking photos are always objective. Correct: framing, cropping and editing shape the message.
- Posting photos of people without permission. Correct: ask consent, especially for minors and private moments.

### Day 1 (Monday): Media influence
*Student:* Compare how the same event is shown in news photos, ads and social media and how each influences viewers.

*How to teach:* Compare how one event is shown across media.

- **Q:** How can cropping change a photo's message?
  **A:** It can remove context, changing what viewers believe happened.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planning a photo essay
*Student:* Plan a 10-photo essay on 'A day in my community.'

*How to teach:* Plan a 10-photo essay on 'A day in my community.'

- **Q:** What makes a photo essay a story?
  **A:** A sequence with a beginning, middle and end, variety of shots (wide, medium, close-up) and a theme.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shooting
*Student:* Shoot photos following the plan.

*How to teach:* Shoot following the plan.

- **Q:** Why vary wide, medium and close-up shots?
  **A:** Wide shots set the scene, medium show action, close-ups show detail and emotion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Editing
*Student:* Edit photos (crop, exposure) and sequence them.

*How to teach:* Edit (crop, exposure) and sequence.

- **Q:** What does adjusting exposure do?
  **A:** Makes the image lighter or darker.

### Day 5 (Friday): Ethical sharing
*Student:* Write captions, get consent from people pictured, and present the photo essay.

*How to teach:* Write captions, get consent, present.

- **Q:** Why get consent before sharing someone's photo?
  **A:** It respects privacy and their right to control their image; some situations can harm the person if shared.

**Assessment:** Photo essay.

**Answer key:** Photo essay: 4 = 10 photos in a logical sequence with variety of shots, thoughtful edits, informative captions, consent obtained, ethical choices explained; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = weak sequence or missing captions; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Relays and hurdles

**Prepare:** Baton or paper towel tube; pool-noodle hurdles on cones (low!); an exchange zone marked (30 m under current World Athletics rules; many school meets use 20 m); stopwatch; phone for video.

**Watch for:**
- Incoming runner slowing down at the handoff. Correct: incoming runner sprints through; outgoing runner starts early from a go-mark.
- Jumping over hurdles. Correct: run over them with a lead leg extended and trail leg pulled to the side.

### Day 1 (Monday): Baton exchange
*Student:* Practice the upsweep and downsweep handoff while jogging, then at speed.

*How to teach:* Practice upsweep and downsweep handoffs jogging, then at speed.

- **Q:** In the downsweep, how does the receiver hold the hand?
  **A:** Arm extended back, palm up, thumb in, fingers together; the passer presses the baton down into the palm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Relay
*Student:* Run a 4 x 100 m relay (or shuttle relay) with a team, staying in the exchange zone.

*How to teach:* Run a 4×100 (or shuttle) relay staying inside the marked exchange zone.

- **Q:** What happens if the baton is passed outside the zone?
  **A:** The team is disqualified.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hurdle drills
*Student:* Practice lead-leg and trail-leg drills walking beside low pool-noodle hurdles.

*How to teach:* Lead- and trail-leg drills walking beside hurdles.

- **Q:** What does the trail leg do?
  **A:** It folds to the side with the knee high and toe up, then pulls through to the front.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hurdle runs
*Student:* Run a 3-step rhythm over three low hurdles and time it.

*How to teach:* 3-step rhythm over three low hurdles; time it.

- **Q:** Why use a consistent step pattern between hurdles?
  **A:** It allows the same lead leg each time and maintains speed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Analyze
*Student:* Video a hurdle clearance and identify one technique fix.

*How to teach:* Video a clearance; identify a fix.

- **Q:** Name one common hurdle fault.
  **A:** Jumping too high, leaning back, trail leg dragging low, or stuttering steps.

**Assessment:** Hurdle technique analysis.

**Answer key:** Hurdle technique analysis: proficient identifies one fault on video (e.g., floating too high, trail knee low), explains its effect (loses time, hits hurdle) and gives a correcting cue/drill (e.g., attack the hurdle, lean forward; trail-leg wall drill).

## Health Education 8: Weapons safety and threat reporting

**Prepare:** Parental preview: weapons and threats. Review Sandy Hook Promise 'Say Something' resources. Know whether firearms are in your home or relatives' homes and how they're stored. Prepare scenario cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a found gun is safe to pick up if it looks like a toy or unloaded. Correct: never touch it — leave the area and tell an adult.
- Believing threats online are 'just jokes.' Correct: always report threats; adults will assess them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Safe storage
*Student:* Learn why safe storage matters and what to do if you find a gun: don't touch, leave, tell an adult.

*How to teach:* Teach: Stop, don't touch, leave the area, tell an adult. Explain safe storage (locked, unloaded, ammunition separate).

- **Q:** You find a gun at a friend's house. What do you do?
  **A:** Don't touch it, leave the room/area immediately, and tell an adult right away.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Warning signs
*Student:* Identify warning signs that someone may hurt themselves or others.

*How to teach:* List warning signs someone may hurt themselves or others: threats, fascination with weapons/violence, withdrawal, posts about violence, severe anger.

- **Q:** Name two warning signs of potential violence.
  **A:** Making threats (in person or online), talking about bringing a weapon, obsession with weapons or past attacks, sudden withdrawal and anger, social posts about hurting others.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reporting
*Student:* Practice reporting a threat seen online to a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Practice showing a parent a screenshot of a threat and explaining it. Learn anonymous options (Say Something app, school tip line, 911 if imminent).

- **Q:** If you see a threat online, what should you do?
  **A:** Don't share it; screenshot it, tell a trusted adult immediately or use an anonymous tip line; call 911 if there's immediate danger.

### Day 4 (Thursday): De-escalation
*Student:* Practice calm-voice, space and exit strategies in conflict scenarios.

*How to teach:* Practice de-escalation: calm low voice, give space, don't block exits, acknowledge feelings, walk away and get help.

- **Q:** Name two de-escalation strategies.
  **A:** Speak calmly, keep a safe distance, listen and acknowledge feelings, avoid insults or threats, leave and get an adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a scenario response worksheet.

*How to teach:* The child completes a scenario worksheet.

- **Q:** Why report even if you aren't sure it's serious?
  **A:** Adults trained to assess threats can decide; reporting may prevent harm and get help for the person.

**Assessment:** Scenario worksheet.

**Answer key:** Scenario worksheet key: 1) found gun – don't touch, leave, tell adult 2) classmate posts a threat – screenshot, tell adult/tip line 3) friend talks about hurting themselves – Ask-Listen-Tell, 988 4) heated argument – calm voice, space, leave 5) imminent danger – 911. Proficient: all five correct.
