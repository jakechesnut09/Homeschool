# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 28

## English Language Arts 8: Ovid's 'Pyramus and Thisbe' and patterns from myth

**Prepare:** Print Ovid's 'Pyramus and Thisbe' (Metamorphoses Book IV, Project Gutenberg translation).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Shakespeare invented the star-crossed lovers pattern. It goes back to ancient myth.
- Confusing archetype with stereotype. An archetype is a recurring pattern across literature.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading Ovid
*Student:* Read 'Pyramus and Thisbe' and summarize the plot in 8 key events.

*How to teach:* Read the myth aloud together. The child lists the 8 key events in order.

- **Q:** Give the 8 key events.
  **A:** Neighbors Pyramus and Thisbe fall in love in Babylon; their parents forbid it; they whisper through a crack in the wall; they plan to meet at Ninus's tomb under a white mulberry tree; Thisbe arrives first and flees a lioness, dropping her veil; the lioness bloodies the veil; Pyramus finds it, thinks she's dead and kills himself with his sword; Thisbe returns, finds him and kills herself — the berries turn dark red forever.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Archetypes
*Student:* Identify the star-crossed lovers, forbidden love and tragic misunderstanding patterns.

*How to teach:* Define archetype as a pattern that recurs across many stories and cultures. The child lists the star-crossed lovers, forbidden love and tragic misunderstanding patterns in Ovid and names another story that uses each.

- **Q:** Name the archetypal patterns.
  **A:** Star-crossed lovers, forbidden love, tragic misunderstanding.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mythological allusions
*Student:* Explore Greek and Latin roots from mythology (narcissism, echo, tantalize) and use them in sentences.

*How to teach:* Tell the short myths of Narcissus, Echo and Tantalus. The child defines narcissism, echo and tantalize, explains each word's origin, and writes one original sentence for each.

- **Q:** What does 'tantalize' mean and where does it come from?
  **A:** To tease with something out of reach; from Tantalus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Echoes in modern works
*Student:* Compare Ovid's pattern with 'Romeo and Juliet' and a modern story or film.

*How to teach:* Summarize Romeo and Juliet briefly if the child doesn't know it. The child makes a three-column chart (Ovid / Romeo and Juliet / a modern story or film) for obstacle, secret meeting, misunderstanding and ending.

- **Q:** What does Romeo and Juliet share with Ovid?
  **A:** Feuding/forbidding families, secret love, a misunderstanding about death, double suicide.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on myth vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Give the myth-vocabulary quiz (key). Have the child correct misses by writing the myth behind each word.

- **Q:** What is a metamorphosis in the myth?
  **A:** The mulberries change from white to dark red.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Myth vocabulary quiz: narcissism (excessive self-love; from Narcissus, who fell in love with his reflection); echo (from the nymph Echo who could only repeat others' words); tantalize (from Tantalus, punished with food and water always just out of reach); metamorphosis (transformation); archetype; allusion. 1 point each plus a sentence using each correctly.

## Mathematics 8: Operations with scientific notation

**Prepare:** Calculator, NASA planetary distance data. Prepare the self-checking review puzzle and the Unit 7 test.

**Watch for:**
- Adding exponents when adding numbers in scientific notation. To add, first rewrite with the same power of 10, then add coefficients.
- Leaving answers like 30 × 10⁷. Adjust to 3 × 10⁸.

### Day 1 (Monday): Multiplying & dividing
*Student:* Compute products and quotients like (3 x 10^8)(2 x 10^3) and adjust answers to proper scientific notation.

*How to teach:* Multiply coefficients, add exponents, then fix the coefficient if needed. To check, ask: "(5 × 10⁴)(6 × 10³)?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** (5 × 10⁴)(6 × 10³)?
  **A:** 30 × 10⁷ = 3 × 10⁸.
- **Q:** (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (4 × 10⁹)?
  **A:** 2 × 10⁻³.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adding & subtracting
*Student:* Rewrite with a common power of 10 before adding/subtracting; complete 8 problems.

*How to teach:* Rewrite with a common power before adding/subtracting. Child completes 8.

- **Q:** 3.2 × 10⁵ + 4.1 × 10⁴?
  **A:** 3.2 × 10⁵ + 0.41 × 10⁵ = 3.61 × 10⁵.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Space math
*Student:* Calculate how long light takes to travel from the Sun to each planet using distance / (3 x 10^8 m/s).

*How to teach:* Child computes light travel time = distance ÷ (3 × 10⁸ m/s) for each planet. Before moving on, ask: "Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun. How long does sunlight take?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun. How long does sunlight take?
  **A:** 5 × 10² s = 500 s ≈ 8.3 minutes.
- **Q:** Jupiter is about 7.8 × 10¹¹ m away. Light time?
  **A:** 2.6 × 10³ s ≈ 43 minutes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 7 review
*Student:* Review exponent rules and scientific notation with a self-checking puzzle.

*How to teach:* Child works the self-checking puzzle. Check understanding with: "Simplify (10³)⁻²." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Simplify (10³)⁻².
  **A:** 10⁻⁶.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Unit test on exponents and scientific notation.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test. To check, ask: "(3 × 10⁸)(2 × 10³)?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** (3 × 10⁸)(2 × 10³)?
  **A:** 6 × 10¹¹.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test suggested items: 1) (3 × 10⁸)(2 × 10³) = 6 × 10¹¹. 2) (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (4 × 10⁹) = 2 × 10⁻³. 3) (5 × 10⁴)(6 × 10³) = 3 × 10⁸. 4) 3.2 × 10⁵ + 4.1 × 10⁴ = 3.61 × 10⁵. 5) 2⁴ × 2⁻⁶ = 1/4. 6) (10³)⁻² = 10⁻⁶. 7) Light from Sun to Earth: 1.5 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3 × 10⁸ = 500 s. 8) 0.00072 → 7.2 × 10⁻⁴. 1–2 points each.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Mutations

**Prepare:** Codon chart, paper strips.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all mutations are harmful. Many are neutral; some are helpful in certain environments.
- Thinking a substitution always changes the protein. Some codons code the same amino acid.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mutation model
*Student:* Change one letter in a sentence ('THE CAT ATE THE RAT') to model substitution, insertion and deletion.

*How to teach:* Change letters in 'THE CAT ATE THE RAT' to model mutations. To check, ask: "Delete the first 'E' and regroup in threes." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Delete the first 'E' and regroup in threes.
  **A:** THC ATA TET HER AT — a frameshift garbles every word after it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Codon mutations
*Student:* Apply mutations to a DNA sequence and translate; see which change the protein.

*How to teach:* Apply mutations to a DNA sequence and translate. Finish by asking: "Which mutation type usually has the biggest effect?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Which mutation type usually has the biggest effect?
  **A:** Insertion or deletion (frameshift).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Case studies
*Student:* Research sickle cell trait and malaria resistance, and lactase persistence; classify effects.

*How to teach:* Research sickle cell and lactase persistence. Before moving on, ask: "Why is the sickle cell allele common in malaria regions?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Why is the sickle cell allele common in malaria regions?
  **A:** Carriers resist malaria.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modeling effects
*Student:* Draw a model connecting gene change -> protein change -> trait change for one case.

*How to teach:* Draw a gene→protein→trait model. Check understanding with: "Sickle cell model?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Sickle cell model?
  **A:** Base substitution → altered hemoglobin → sickle-shaped cells.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mutation types and effects.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. To check, ask: "Can a mutation be neutral?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Can a mutation be neutral?
  **A:** Yes.

**Assessment:** Mutation model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: substitution, insertion, deletion; insertions/deletions cause frameshifts; sickle cell from a single base substitution in the hemoglobin gene, carriers have some malaria resistance; lactase persistence lets adults digest milk. Model rubric: gene change → protein change → trait.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Plans for Reconstruction

**Prepare:** 13th Amendment text; Mississippi Black Codes (1865) excerpt; summaries of the Ten Percent Plan, Johnson's plan, the Radical Republican plan, the Civil Rights Act of 1866 and Johnson's impeachment.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the 13th Amendment ended all forced labor. Its exception 'as a punishment for crime' allowed convict leasing that re-enslaved many Black Southerners.
- Believing Johnson was removed from office. The House impeached him (1868), but the Senate acquitted him by one vote.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plans compared
*Student:* Compare the Ten Percent Plan, Johnson's plan and the Radical Republicans' plan in a three-column chart.

*How to teach:* Chart the three plans.

- **Q:** What did the Ten Percent Plan require?
  **A:** 10% of a state's 1860 voters to take a loyalty oath and accept the end of slavery before forming a new government.
- **Q:** What did Radical Republicans want?
  **A:** Congressional control, punishment of Confederate leaders, and protection of freedpeople's civil and voting rights.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): 13th Amendment
*Student:* Read the 13th Amendment and discuss the 'except as a punishment for crime' clause and its later effects.

*How to teach:* Read the 13th Amendment; discuss the exception clause.

- **Q:** What does the 13th Amendment's exception allow?
  **A:** Involuntary servitude 'as a punishment for crime' — used to justify convict leasing and forced prison labor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Black codes
*Student:* Read excerpts of the Mississippi Black Codes and explain how they restricted freedpeople.

*How to teach:* Read the Mississippi Black Codes; explain restrictions.

- **Q:** Give two ways Black Codes restricted freedpeople.
  **A:** Required yearly labor contracts, arrested unemployed people as 'vagrants' and leased them out, barred renting land in some areas, forced apprenticeships of children, limited gun ownership.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Johnson vs Congress
*Student:* Explain the Civil Rights Act of 1866, Johnson's vetoes and his impeachment trial.

*How to teach:* Explain the Civil Rights Act of 1866, Johnson's vetoes and impeachment.

- **Q:** What did the Civil Rights Act of 1866 do?
  **A:** Declared all persons born in the U.S. citizens (except untaxed Native Americans) with equal rights to make contracts, own property and sue; Congress passed it over Johnson's veto.
- **Q:** Why was Johnson impeached?
  **A:** Officially for violating the Tenure of Office Act by firing Secretary of War Stanton; really over his clash with Congress on Reconstruction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on early Reconstruction.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What was the result of Johnson's Senate trial?
  **A:** Acquitted by one vote (35–19, one short of two-thirds).

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Ten Percent Plan (Lincoln, 1863) — a state could rejoin when 10% of 1860 voters swore loyalty and accepted emancipation; Johnson's plan — pardons for most Confederates who swore loyalty, states had to ratify the 13th Amendment and repudiate secession and Confederate debts; allowed former Confederates back into power; Radical Republicans (Thaddeus Stevens, Charles Sumner) — Congress should control Reconstruction, protect freedpeople's rights, Reconstruction Acts (1867) divided the South into five military districts and required states to ratify the 14th Amendment and allow Black male suffrage; 13th Amendment (ratified December 1865) abolished slavery 'except as a punishment for crime'; Mississippi Black Codes — required annual labor contracts, punished 'vagrancy', limited land rental, allowed apprenticing Black children; Civil Rights Act of 1866 — declared citizenship and equal legal rights, passed over Johnson's veto; impeachment — for violating the Tenure of Office Act (firing Stanton); acquitted 35–19, one vote short.

## Spanish 1B: El cine y la televisión

**Prepare:** A Spanish-language TV guide (e.g., Univision or Telemundo listings). Genre words: la película de horror/terror, la comedia, el drama, la película de acción, la película de ciencia ficción, la película romántica, los dibujos animados, la telenovela, el programa de concursos, el programa deportivo, las noticias, el documental.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'Me parece que es emocionante' is correct; avoid 'Yo parece…' — parecer works like gustar: me parece.
- Thinking telenovelas are just like U.S. soap operas. Telenovelas usually have a set ending after several months, unlike long-running soaps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Genres
*Student:* Learn 12 genre words and classify 15 well-known movies and shows.

*How to teach:* Learn 12 genre words; classify 15 movies and shows.

- **Q:** What genre is 'Toy Story'?
  **A:** Dibujos animados (película animada / infantil).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Opinions
*Student:* Give opinions with me parece, creo que, and adjectives like emocionante, violento, tonto.

*How to teach:* Give opinions with me parece, creo que and adjectives.

- **Q:** Write: 'Action movies seem exciting to me.'
  **A:** Las películas de acción me parecen emocionantes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): TV listings
*Student:* Read a Spanish-language TV guide and answer questions about times, channels, and genres.

*How to teach:* Read a TV guide; answer questions on times, channels, genres.

- **Q:** A listing says '20:00 Noticias.' What and when?
  **A:** The news at 8:00 p.m.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Telenovelas
*Student:* Read about the telenovela tradition in Mexico and Colombia and compare it with U.S. TV dramas.

*How to teach:* Read about telenovelas; compare with U.S. dramas.

- **Q:** Name two countries famous for telenovelas.
  **A:** Mexico and Colombia (also Venezuela, Brazil in Portuguese).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a genre vocabulary and opinions quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the genre and opinions quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I think that cartoons are childish.'
  **A:** Creo que los dibujos animados son infantiles.

**Assessment:** Genre and opinions quiz.

**Answer key:** Genre and opinions quiz: classify movies/shows (e.g., Coco = dibujos animados/película animada; a news show = las noticias); opinions with me parece, creo que + adjectives emocionante, violento, tonto, infantil, divertido, aburrido, realista. Samples: I think horror movies are violent → Creo que las películas de horror son violentas; This program seems silly to me → Este programa me parece tonto. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Photography: composition

**Prepare:** Phone or camera with grid lines turned on. Images: Dorothea Lange's 'Migrant Mother' (1936, Nipomo, California) and Ansel Adams's Yosemite photographs (e.g., 'Monolith, the Face of Half Dome,' 'Clearing Winter Storm').

**Watch for:**
- Always centering the subject. Correct: placing it on rule-of-thirds lines often makes a stronger composition.
- Shooting everything from eye level. Correct: change point of view for interest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analyzing photos
*Student:* Analyze Dorothea Lange's 'Migrant Mother' and Ansel Adams's Yosemite images for composition and intent.

*How to teach:* Analyze 'Migrant Mother' and Adams's images.

- **Q:** What was Lange's purpose in 'Migrant Mother'?
  **A:** To document the suffering of migrant farm workers during the Great Depression for the Farm Security Administration and build support for aid.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rule of thirds
*Student:* Take 10 photos using a rule-of-thirds grid.

*How to teach:* Take 10 rule-of-thirds photos.

- **Q:** What is the rule of thirds?
  **A:** Divide the frame into a 3×3 grid and place key subjects along the lines or at intersections.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Leading lines & framing
*Student:* Take 10 photos using leading lines and natural frames around the house or neighborhood.

*How to teach:* 10 photos with leading lines and natural frames.

- **Q:** What are leading lines?
  **A:** Lines (roads, fences, rails) that guide the viewer's eye toward the subject.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Point of view
*Student:* Photograph one object from 5 viewpoints (bird's eye, worm's eye) and compare.

*How to teach:* Photograph one object from 5 viewpoints.

- **Q:** How does a worm's-eye view change a subject?
  **A:** Shooting from below makes the subject look larger, powerful or imposing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Select your 5 best photos and caption each with the technique used.

*How to teach:* Select and caption 5 best photos.

- **Q:** What is framing?
  **A:** Using elements like doorways or branches to surround and highlight the subject.

**Assessment:** Photo portfolio of 5 images with captions.

**Answer key:** Photo portfolio: 4 = 5 strong photos each clearly demonstrating a named technique (rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, point of view), in focus, captions explain the technique and effect; 3 = 4 strong; 2 = techniques unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Exercise and stress management

**Prepare:** Journal, timer, a beginner tai chi video (e.g., '10-minute tai chi for beginners'), safe walking route.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking exercise only helps the body. Correct: it improves mood, sleep and stress by releasing endorphins and lowering stress hormones over time.
- Breathing from the chest. Correct: diaphragmatic breathing expands the belly and calms the nervous system.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stress and exercise
*Student:* Learn how endorphins and reduced cortisol help mood; rate your mood before and after a 20-minute run.

*How to teach:* Rate mood 1–10 before and after a 20-minute run.

- **Q:** Why might mood improve after exercise?
  **A:** Endorphins and other brain chemicals (e.g., dopamine, serotonin) rise, and regular exercise lowers cortisol over time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Breathing
*Student:* Practice box breathing and diaphragmatic breathing, then a 10-minute walk.

*How to teach:* Box breathing and diaphragmatic breathing, then a 10-minute walk.

- **Q:** How do you know you're breathing diaphragmatically?
  **A:** The hand on your belly rises more than the hand on your chest.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tai chi
*Student:* Follow a beginner tai chi sequence for 15 minutes.

*How to teach:* Tai chi 15 minutes.

- **Q:** What are benefits of tai chi?
  **A:** Balance, flexibility, relaxation and reduced stress through slow, controlled movement and breathing.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mindful walk
*Student:* Take a 30-minute mindful nature walk, noting five things you see, hear and feel.

*How to teach:* 30-minute mindful walk; note five things seen, heard, felt.

- **Q:** What is mindfulness?
  **A:** Paying attention to the present moment without judgment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a personal stress-relief activity plan with three activities.

*How to teach:* Write a stress-relief plan with three activities.

- **Q:** Name three physical activities that relieve stress.
  **A:** Running, yoga, walking in nature, dancing, swimming, tai chi.

**Assessment:** Stress-relief activity plan.

**Answer key:** Stress-relief plan: 4 = three activities with when/how often, why each works (physiological explanation), mood data from the week; 3 = activities and timing; 2 = list only; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Dating violence, harassment and assault prevention

**Prepare:** Prepare scenario cards (in person and online harassment). Read loveisrespect.org and the 5 D's (Right To Be/Hollaback). Know how your school or co-op reports harassment.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking harassment only counts if it's physical. Correct: unwelcome comments, jokes, gestures, images or messages of a sexual nature are harassment.
- Believing bystanders can only confront directly. Correct: the 5 D's give safer options.

### Day 1 (Monday): Harassment
*Student:* Define sexual harassment and identify examples in person and online.

*How to teach:* Define sexual harassment and list examples in person and online.

- **Q:** Give two examples of online sexual harassment.
  **A:** Sending unwanted sexual messages or images, spreading sexual rumors, sharing someone's photo without consent, sexual comments on posts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bystander
*Student:* Learn the 5 D's of bystander intervention (direct, distract, delegate, delay, document).

*How to teach:* Teach the 5 D's: Direct, Distract, Delegate, Delay, Document.

- **Q:** Explain 'Delegate' and 'Delay.'
  **A:** Delegate: get help from someone with authority (teacher, coach, adult). Delay: check in with the person afterward to support them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice
*Student:* Role-play bystander responses to scenarios.

*How to teach:* Role-play three scenarios using different D's.

- **Q:** Someone is being teased in a group chat. What could you do?
  **A:** Distract by changing the subject, message the target privately (Delay), screenshot (Document), and tell an adult (Delegate).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reporting
*Student:* Identify how and where to report harassment or assault.

*How to teach:* List reporting options: parent, school counselor/Title IX coordinator, platform reports, police for assault, RAINN hotline 1-800-656-4673.

- **Q:** Where can someone report sexual assault?
  **A:** Tell a trusted adult, call 911 or police, contact RAINN 1-800-656-HOPE (4673), or a school Title IX coordinator.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a reflection on how you would support a friend.

*How to teach:* The child writes how they would support a friend who was harassed.

- **Q:** What should you say first to a friend who discloses harassment or assault?
  **A:** 'I believe you. It's not your fault. I'm here for you,' then help them get adult support.

**Assessment:** Bystander role-play.

**Answer key:** Bystander role-play rubric: 4 = uses at least two D's appropriately, prioritizes safety, supports the target and involves an adult; 3 = one D used well; 2 = hesitant/unsafe; 1 = no action. Reflection: believes, supports, connects to help.
