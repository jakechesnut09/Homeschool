# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 32

## English Language Arts 8: Research: questions and sources

**Prepare:** Library card for online databases; Purdue OWL; research notebook. Suggest topics: printing press, vaccines, transcontinental railroad.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing too broad a topic. Narrow to a question that can be answered in 4–6 pages.
- Trusting the first search result. Evaluate every source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a topic
*Student:* Brainstorm topics on 'An innovation that changed the world' (e.g., the printing press, vaccines, the transcontinental railroad) and choose one.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm 8–10 innovations together and talk through which interest the child most. The child picks one and lists what he or she already knows and wonders.

- **Q:** What makes a good research topic?
  **A:** Interesting, narrow enough, with available sources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research questions
*Student:* Write a focused research question and 5 related sub-questions; get feedback and narrow the scope.

*How to teach:* Model narrowing a broad topic to a question. The child writes a focused research question and 5 sub-questions; give feedback on scope.

- **Q:** Narrow 'vaccines.'
  **A:** 'How did Edward Jenner's smallpox vaccine lead to smallpox eradication?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finding sources
*Student:* Use the public library's online databases and search engines with advanced operators to find 6 possible sources.

*How to teach:* Show the library's databases (e.g., Gale, Britannica School) and search operators (quotation marks, site:, minus sign). The child finds 6 possible sources and logs them.

- **Q:** What does putting quotation marks around words in a search do?
  **A:** Searches for that exact phrase.
- **Q:** What does site:.gov do?
  **A:** Limits results to government websites.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluating sources
*Student:* Apply the CRAAP test (currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose) and keep 4 credible sources.

*How to teach:* Model the CRAAP test on one source. The child scores each source and keeps the 4 most credible, noting why.

- **Q:** What does CRAAP stand for?
  **A:** Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit a research proposal with question, sources and a working bibliography.

*How to teach:* The child submits a one-page proposal: question, sub-questions, 4 sources and a working MLA bibliography. To check, ask: "Primary vs secondary source?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Primary vs secondary source?
  **A:** Primary is firsthand from the time; secondary analyzes it later.

**Assessment:** Research proposal and source evaluation.

**Answer key:** Research proposal rubric: focused question; 5 sub-questions; 4 credible sources (mix of primary and secondary) with CRAAP notes; working bibliography in MLA (1 point per element). Example question: 'How did the printing press change the spread of ideas in 16th-century Europe?'

## Mathematics 8: Cube roots and rational vs irrational numbers

**Prepare:** Sugar cubes or snap cubes (at least 64), calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing cube root with dividing by 3. ∛27 = 3 because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27, not 27 ÷ 3.
- Thinking every decimal that goes on forever is irrational. Repeating decimals are rational.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cube roots
*Student:* Build cubes of side 1–5 with sugar or snap cubes; solve x^3 = p for perfect and non-perfect cubes and estimate.

*How to teach:* Build cubes of side 1–5 and count small cubes. Solve x³ = p.

- **Q:** Solve x³ = 64.
  **A:** x = 4.
- **Q:** Estimate ∛30.
  **A:** Between 3 (27) and 4 (64), about 3.1.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decimal expansions
*Student:* Use long division to write fractions such as 5/11 and 7/12 as decimals; identify repeating patterns.

*How to teach:* Long division of 5/11 and 7/12; mark repeating parts. Finish by asking: "Write 5/11 and 7/12 as decimals." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Write 5/11 and 7/12 as decimals.
  **A:** 0.454545... (45 repeats) and 0.58333... (3 repeats).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Repeating decimals to fractions
*Student:* Use the 'multiply by 10^n and subtract' method to convert 0.7777..., 0.272727... and 1.16666... to fractions.

*How to teach:* Model: x = 0.777..., 10x = 7.777..., subtract: 9x = 7. Before moving on, ask: "Convert 0.2727... to a fraction." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Convert 0.2727... to a fraction.
  **A:** 100x − x = 27, so x = 27/99 = 3/11.
- **Q:** Convert 1.1666...
  **A:** 100x − 10x = 116.666... − 11.666... = 105, so x = 105/90 = 7/6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rational or irrational?
*Student:* Sort 16 numbers (sqrt 16, pi, 0.121212..., sqrt 2, 22/7) and justify each placement.

*How to teach:* Child sorts 16 numbers into rational/irrational with justification. Check understanding with: "Classify √16, π, 0.1212..., √2, 22/7." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Classify √16, π, 0.1212..., √2, 22/7.
  **A:** √16 = 4 rational; π irrational; 0.1212... = 4/33 rational; √2 irrational; 22/7 rational (it only approximates π).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: cube roots, conversions and classification of real numbers.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Convert 0.777... to a fraction.
  **A:** 7/9.

**Assessment:** Quiz on cube roots and the real number system.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) ∛64 = 4. 2) ∛125 = 5. 3) x³ = 30 → between 3 and 4 (≈3.1). 4) 5/11 = 0.454545... 5) 7/12 = 0.58333... 6) 0.777... = 7/9. 7) 0.2727... = 3/11. 8) 1.1666... = 7/6. 9) Classify: √16 rational, π irrational, 0.1212... rational (4/33), √2 irrational, 22/7 rational, √7 irrational.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Case studies: finches, moths and bacteria

**Prepare:** Grant finch beak-depth data (HHMI BioInteractive), peppered moth data, beans or candies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking antibiotics make bacteria resistant. Resistant bacteria already exist; antibiotics kill the others.
- Thinking finch beaks grew during the drought. Birds with deeper beaks survived.

### Day 1 (Monday): Darwin's finches
*Student:* Analyze Grant beak-depth data before and after the 1977 drought.

*How to teach:* Analyze finch data before/after 1977 drought. To check, ask: "What happened to average beak depth?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What happened to average beak depth?
  **A:** Increased — deeper beaks could crack hard seeds.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Peppered moths
*Student:* Graph moth color frequencies before and after the Industrial Revolution; explain the change.

*How to teach:* Graph peppered moth frequencies. Finish by asking: "Why did dark moths increase?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why did dark moths increase?
  **A:** Soot darkened trees, so dark moths were camouflaged from birds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Antibiotic resistance
*Student:* Model resistance with beans or candies; explain why finishing prescribed antibiotics matters.

*How to teach:* Model antibiotic resistance with beans. Before moving on, ask: "Why finish antibiotics?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Why finish antibiotics?
  **A:** To kill remaining bacteria and avoid selecting for resistant ones.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing a case study
*Student:* Choose one case and write a 4-paragraph explanation of the selection process.

*How to teach:* Write a 4-paragraph case study. Check understanding with: "Steps of natural selection?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Steps of natural selection?
  **A:** Variation, inheritance, selection, time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Peer/parent review of case study with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Review with rubric. To check, ask: "What happened to dark moths after clean air laws?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What happened to dark moths after clean air laws?
  **A:** They declined.

**Assessment:** Case study essay.

**Answer key:** Case study rubric: describes variation, selective pressure, differential survival/reproduction, inheritance, change over generations, with data.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Big business and inventors

**Prepare:** Carnegie's 'The Gospel of Wealth' (1889) excerpt; political cartoons of Rockefeller (e.g., Standard Oil as an octopus, 1904); readings on Edison, Bell, Lewis Latimer and the Wright brothers.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up vertical and horizontal integration. Vertical = owning every stage of production (Carnegie: mines, railroads, mills); horizontal = buying up competitors in one stage (Rockefeller: refineries).
- Thinking Edison invented everything alone. He ran a research lab at Menlo Park with teams; Latimer, for example, improved the carbon filament.

### Day 1 (Monday): Carnegie and Rockefeller
*Student:* Diagram vertical integration (Carnegie Steel) and horizontal integration (Standard Oil).

*How to teach:* Diagram vertical and horizontal integration.

- **Q:** Define vertical integration with Carnegie's example.
  **A:** Controlling all stages of production — Carnegie owned iron ore mines, coal mines, railroads/ships and steel mills.
- **Q:** Define horizontal integration with Rockefeller's example.
  **A:** Buying or merging with competitors in the same business — Standard Oil absorbed rival refineries.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Robber barons or captains of industry?
*Student:* Read the 'Gospel of Wealth' and two political cartoons; write an argument paragraph taking a side.

*How to teach:* Read the Gospel of Wealth and two cartoons; write an argument paragraph.

- **Q:** What did Carnegie believe the wealthy should do with their money?
  **A:** Give it away during their lives for the public good (libraries, universities); 'The man who dies thus rich dies disgraced.'
- **Q:** What does a cartoon showing Standard Oil as an octopus suggest?
  **A:** That the company's tentacles strangled competitors, government and the public — a monopoly abusing power.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inventors
*Student:* Research Edison, Bell, Lewis Latimer and the Wright brothers and make a chart of how each invention changed daily life.

*How to teach:* Chart inventors and how each changed daily life.

- **Q:** How did Lewis Latimer contribute to electric lighting?
  **A:** He patented an improved process for making long-lasting carbon filaments, making bulbs cheaper and more practical.
- **Q:** When and where did the Wright brothers first fly?
  **A:** December 17, 1903, at Kitty Hawk (Kill Devil Hills), North Carolina.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Government and business
*Student:* Explain how tariffs, railroad land grants and subsidies helped business expand.

*How to teach:* Explain tariffs, land grants and subsidies.

- **Q:** How did protective tariffs help American industry?
  **A:** They taxed imported goods, making foreign products more expensive and protecting U.S. manufacturers from competition.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on industrialization.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What is philanthropy?
  **A:** Giving money or effort to promote the welfare of others, such as funding libraries or schools.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: monopoly — one company controls an industry; trust — combination of companies controlled by one board (Standard Oil Trust, 1882; refined about 90% of U.S. oil by 1880); Carnegie Steel — vertical integration, Bessemer process; Gospel of Wealth — the rich have a duty to give their fortunes away for public good during their lifetime ('The man who dies thus rich dies disgraced'); Carnegie funded about 2,500 libraries; 'robber baron' (exploited workers and crushed competitors) vs 'captain of industry' (built the economy, philanthropy); inventors — Bell telephone (1876), Edison phonograph (1877) and practical light bulb (1879), Latimer improved the carbon filament (1881) and drafted Bell's patent drawings, Wright brothers first powered flight at Kitty Hawk (December 17, 1903); government aid — protective tariffs, railroad land grants, subsidies, laissez-faire regulation. Argument paragraph rubric: clear claim, two pieces of evidence, counterclaim.

## Spanish 1B: Saber vs. conocer

**Prepare:** 20 sorting cards. Saber and conocer charts.

**Watch for:**
- Using conocer for facts or skills: 'Conozco nadar.' Use saber + infinitive: Sé nadar.
- Forgetting the personal a before people: Conozco a tu hermana.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules
*Student:* Learn saber (facts, how to) vs. conocer (people, places) and the personal a, then sort 20 sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach saber vs conocer and the personal a; sort 20 sentences.

- **Q:** Choose: Yo (sé/conozco) a la profesora.
  **A:** conozco — a person (with personal a).
- **Q:** Choose: ¿(Sabes/Conoces) dónde está el museo?
  **A:** Sabes — information.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sé + infinitive
*Student:* Write 10 sentences about tech skills you know how to do: Sé crear una página web.

*How to teach:* Write 10 'Sé + infinitive' tech skills.

- **Q:** Write: 'I know how to download apps.'
  **A:** Sé bajar (descargar) aplicaciones.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conozco
*Student:* Write about people and places you know, including the personal a.

*How to teach:* Write about people and places you know with the personal a.

- **Q:** Write: 'I know your parents.'
  **A:** Conozco a tus padres.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a parent about their tech skills and places they know, using both verbs.

*How to teach:* Interview a parent with both verbs.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Do you know how to use this app?'
  **A:** ¿Sabes usar esta aplicación?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a saber vs. conocer quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the saber/conocer quiz.

- **Q:** Give the yo form of conocer and saber.
  **A:** conozco; sé.

**Assessment:** Saber/conocer quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: saber — sé, sabes, sabe, sabemos, sabéis, saben (facts, information, how to + infinitive); conocer — conozco, conoces, conoce, conocemos, conocéis, conocen (people, places, familiarity) with personal a before people. Samples: I know how to make a web page → Sé crear una página web; Do you know Madrid? → ¿Conoces Madrid?; We know your cousin → Conocemos a tu primo; She knows the password → Ella sabe la contraseña. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Elements of dance

**Prepare:** Open space, music; video of Alvin Ailey's 'Revelations' (1960) excerpt (e.g., 'Wade in the Water' or 'Rocka My Soul') and a folk dance. Elements of dance: Body, Energy, Space, Time (BEST).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking dance is only steps. Correct: dance uses body, energy, space and time to express meaning.
- Improvising the same movement to every song. Correct: respond to changes in tempo, mood and dynamics.

### Day 1 (Monday): Elements
*Student:* Learn the elements of dance through movement exploration.

*How to teach:* Explore BEST through movement.

- **Q:** Name the four elements of dance.
  **A:** Body, Energy, Space, Time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dance styles
*Student:* Watch Alvin Ailey's 'Revelations' excerpt and a folk dance; compare.

*How to teach:* Compare 'Revelations' and a folk dance.

- **Q:** What music is 'Revelations' set to and what does it express?
  **A:** African American spirituals, gospel and blues; it expresses African American experience, grief, faith and joy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Improvising
*Student:* Improvise movement to 3 contrasting pieces of music.

*How to teach:* Improvise to three contrasting pieces.

- **Q:** How might energy change between music pieces?
  **A:** Sharp, strong movement for driving music; smooth, light movement for gentle music.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Movement phrases
*Student:* Create a 16-count phrase.

*How to teach:* Create a 16-count phrase.

- **Q:** How many 8-counts is 16 counts?
  **A:** Two.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform and describe the phrase.

*How to teach:* Perform and describe using BEST.

- **Q:** Describe a movement using 'Space.'
  **A:** Example: low level, curved pathway, moving backward.

**Assessment:** Phrase performance.

**Answer key:** Phrase performance: 4 = 16 counts performed on the beat, uses at least three elements intentionally (e.g., level changes, sharp vs smooth energy, tempo change), described accurately with BEST vocabulary; 3 = minor timing; 2 = few elements; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Lifetime activities: cycling, hiking and swimming safety

**Prepare:** Bicycle and helmet; daypack with water, snacks, first aid; map app; local parks/recreation guide; pool access if available.

**Watch for:**
- Helmet worn tilted back. Correct: level, two fingers above eyebrows, side straps form a V under ears, one finger under chin strap.
- Riding against traffic. Correct: ride with traffic on the right.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bike safety
*Student:* Do a helmet fit and ABC bike check and practice hand signals on a quiet street.

*How to teach:* Helmet fit; ABC check (Air, Brakes, Chain); hand signals (left arm straight = left; left arm bent up or right arm straight = right; arm down = stop).

- **Q:** What does the ABC bike check stand for?
  **A:** Air (tires), Brakes, Chain (and cranks).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ride
*Student:* Ride a planned 30- to 60-minute route with a parent.

*How to teach:* Ride a planned 30–60 minute route with a parent.

- **Q:** Which side of the road do cyclists ride on?
  **A:** The right side, with traffic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water safety
*Student:* Learn water safety rules and, if available, swim 10 minutes at a local pool.

*How to teach:* Water safety rules; swim 10 minutes if possible.

- **Q:** Name two water safety rules.
  **A:** Swim with a buddy and near a lifeguard, enter feet first in unknown water, wear a life jacket in boats, reach or throw — don't go.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hike
*Student:* Plan and complete a hike with a map, water and Leave No Trace principles.

*How to teach:* Plan a hike with map, water, Leave No Trace.

- **Q:** Name two Leave No Trace principles.
  **A:** Plan ahead, stay on trails, pack out trash, leave what you find, respect wildlife, minimize campfire impact, be considerate of others.

### Day 5 (Friday): Community
*Student:* List five free or low-cost recreation programs in your community.

*How to teach:* List five community recreation programs.

- **Q:** Why do community programs matter for lifetime fitness?
  **A:** They provide low-cost, social, ongoing opportunities to stay active after school sports.

**Assessment:** Outing plan and community recreation list.

**Answer key:** Outing plan: 4 = route/map, time, gear list (water, helmet, first aid), safety rules, emergency contact and Leave No Trace; community list: 5 programs with cost and location. 3 = minor gaps; 2 = partial; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Health consumer skills

**Prepare:** Find three health product ads (acne cream, weight loss, sleep aid). Print a CRAAP checklist (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose). Have a brand-name and generic label (e.g., ibuprofen).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking testimonials prove a product works. Correct: testimonials are individual stories, often paid; look for scientific evidence.
- Believing generic medicine is weaker. Correct: FDA requires generics to have the same active ingredient, strength and effect as the brand.

### Day 1 (Monday): Claims
*Student:* Analyze three health product ads for testimonials and unsupported claims.

*How to teach:* Analyze ads for testimonials, bandwagon, 'clinically proven' without citation, before/after photos.

- **Q:** What's wrong with 'Doctors recommend!' with no details?
  **A:** It doesn't say which doctors or what evidence — an unsupported claim.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluate sources
*Student:* Use a source checklist on three health websites.

*How to teach:* Apply CRAAP to three health websites.

- **Q:** What does 'Purpose' ask?
  **A:** Why the information exists — to inform, sell, persuade or entertain; sites selling products may be biased.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Quackery
*Student:* Learn signs of health fraud and the FTC's role.

*How to teach:* Explain quackery signs (miracle cure, secret formula, works for many diseases, money-back pressure) and that the FTC fights deceptive advertising (FDA regulates labels/safety).

- **Q:** What does the FTC do?
  **A:** The Federal Trade Commission protects consumers from false or deceptive advertising and fraud.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare a brand-name and generic medicine or product label.

*How to teach:* Compare labels: same active ingredient (e.g., ibuprofen 200 mg), different price and inactive ingredients.

- **Q:** A brand costs $9.99 for 100 tablets and generic $4.99 for 100. Same active ingredient — which is better value?
  **A:** The generic — same medicine at half the price ($0.05 vs. $0.10 per tablet).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a consumer guide for one product category.

*How to teach:* The child writes a consumer guide for one category (sunscreen, acne, protein bars): what to look for, red flags, trusted sources.

- **Q:** What should you look for in sunscreen?
  **A:** Broad spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, water-resistant; reapply every 2 hours.

**Assessment:** Consumer guide.

**Answer key:** Consumer guide: 4 = clear criteria for choosing products, at least three red flags, compares cost/unit price, cites valid sources (FDA, AAD, NIH), practical tips; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = general opinions; 1 = incomplete.
