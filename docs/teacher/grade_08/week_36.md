# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 36

## English Language Arts 8: Portfolio and final assessment

**Prepare:** The year's writing portfolio. Prepare the final exam: two unfamiliar passages (one literary, one informational) with 8–10 questions each, a timed argument prompt and 15 grammar/vocabulary items drawn from the year (verbals, voice, mood, punctuation, Greek/Latin roots).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing portfolio pieces by length or neatness rather than growth. Each cover note must name a specific skill the piece shows.
- Rushing the timed essay without planning. Spend 5 minutes outlining claim, reasons and evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Portfolio selection
*Student:* Select your best narrative, argument, analysis and research pieces and write a cover note for each.

*How to teach:* Child selects best narrative, argument, analysis and research pieces and writes a cover note for each explaining the skill it shows. To check, ask: "What should a cover note say?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What should a cover note say?
  **A:** What the assignment was, what skill it shows (e.g., embedding quotations), and what the writer would still improve.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final exam: reading
*Student:* Take the final reading assessment on unfamiliar literary and informational passages.

*How to teach:* Give the reading assessment on unfamiliar passages; no notes. Finish by asking: "A good strategy for a cold read?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** A good strategy for a cold read?
  **A:** Preview the questions, read once for the gist, annotate key lines, then return to the text for each answer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam: writing & language
*Student:* Write a timed argument essay and complete grammar and vocabulary items.

*How to teach:* Give the timed argument essay (about 45 minutes) and the grammar/vocabulary items. Before moving on, ask: "Label the mood: 'If I were you, I would revise the ending.'" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Label the mood: 'If I were you, I would revise the ending.'
  **A:** Subjunctive ('If I were') with a conditional ('would revise').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reading reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on your independent reading this year and set summer reading goals.

*How to teach:* Child writes a reflection on this year's independent reading and sets summer reading goals. Check understanding with: "What makes a strong reading goal?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What makes a strong reading goal?
  **A:** Specific and measurable — e.g., 'Read four books this summer, including one nonfiction and one classic.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebration
*Student:* Share a favorite piece aloud and discuss your growth with family.

*How to teach:* Child reads a favorite piece aloud; discuss growth from September to now using early and late work samples. To check, ask: "Name one way to show growth." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Name one way to show growth.
  **A:** Compare an early paragraph with a later one — e.g., from summarizing quotations to analyzing them.

**Assessment:** Final exam and portfolio.

**Answer key:** Final exam: reading — score with your key (main idea, inference, word meaning, structure, author's purpose; answers must cite the passage). Argument essay rubric: claim, evidence, reasoning, counterclaim/rebuttal, organization, conventions (4 levels each). Grammar/vocabulary sample items: identify gerund/participle/infinitive; rewrite passive to active; label indicative/imperative/interrogative/conditional/subjunctive; fix a dangling modifier; punctuate a quotation with an ellipsis; define roots acu- (sharp), cred- (believe), fid- (faith). Portfolio: four pieces (narrative, argument, analysis, research), each with a cover note naming the skill shown.

## Mathematics 8: Final review and cumulative exam

**Prepare:** Notebook, formula sheet, calculator; prepare a two-part cumulative final.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing on multistep problems. Encourage writing each step and checking.
- Leaving irrational answers unrounded or unlabeled. State whether the answer is exact or approximate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: geometry
*Student:* Review transformations, similarity, angles, Pythagorean theorem and volume with 15 problems.

*How to teach:* 15 geometry review problems. To check, ask: "Reflect (2, −3) over the x-axis." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Reflect (2, −3) over the x-axis.
  **A:** (2, 3).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: algebra
*Student:* Review linear equations, systems, functions, exponents and scientific notation with 15 problems.

*How to teach:* 15 algebra review problems. Finish by asking: "Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Solve 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4.
  **A:** 5x − 10 = 3x + 4 → x = 7.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Take part 1 of the cumulative final (geometry, number system, data).

*How to teach:* Final exam part 1. Before moving on, ask: "Legs 7 and 24: hypotenuse?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Legs 7 and 24: hypotenuse?
  **A:** 25.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Take part 2 of the cumulative final (expressions, equations, functions).

*How to teach:* Final exam part 2. Check understanding with: "Solve y = 2x − 3 and y = −x + 6." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Solve y = 2x − 3 and y = −x + 6.
  **A:** (3, 3).

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection & portfolio
*Student:* Correct final exam errors and write a one-page reflection on your three most improved skills; organize the math portfolio for Algebra I.

*How to teach:* Correct exam errors; write reflection; organize portfolio for Algebra I. To check, ask: "What does 3⁻² equal?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What does 3⁻² equal?
  **A:** 1/9.

**Assessment:** Cumulative final exam and portfolio reflection.

**Answer key:** Final exam suggested items: Part 1: 1) Reflect (2, −3) over the x-axis → (2, 3). 2) Similar triangles 4, 6 and 10, x → x = 15. 3) Triangle angles 90° and 38° → 52°. 4) Legs 7 and 24 → 25. 5) Cylinder r = 3, h = 4 → 36π ≈ 113.1. 6) 0.6... = 2/3. 7) Scatter plot description. Part 2: 8) 5(x − 2) = 3x + 4 → x = 7. 9) y = 2x − 3 and y = −x + 6 → (3, 3). 10) Function? {(1, 4), (2, 4)} yes. 11) (2 × 10⁵)(4 × 10⁻²) = 8 × 10³. 12) 3⁻² = 1/9. Portfolio reflection rubric: names 3 skills with evidence (before/after work samples).

## Science 8 (Integrated): Capstone presentation and final exam

**Prepare:** Poster board or slides. Prepare the final exam: part 1 physical and Earth science; part 2 life science.

**Watch for:**
- Presenting only the final design. Show the process, data and iteration.

### Day 1 (Monday): Presentation prep
*Student:* Create slides or a poster showing problem, designs, data and final solution.

*How to teach:* The child creates slides or a poster showing problem, designs, data and the final solution. To check, ask: "What should a data slide include?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What should a data slide include?
  **A:** A labeled graph or table comparing versions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Presentation
*Student:* Present the capstone to family and answer questions.

*How to teach:* The child presents and answers questions. Finish by asking: "How do you answer a question about a failure?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** How do you answer a question about a failure?
  **A:** Explain what the data showed and what you'd change.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Physical science (forces, energy, waves) and Earth science.

*How to teach:* Final exam part 1. Before moving on, ask: "v = fλ: f = 10 Hz, λ = 3 m?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** v = fλ: f = 10 Hz, λ = 3 m?
  **A:** 30 m/s.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Life science (heredity, evolution).

*How to teach:* Final exam part 2. Check understanding with: "Bb × Bb: chance of bb?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Bb × Bb: chance of bb?
  **A:** 25%.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on growth as a scientist and set goals for high school Biology.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection on growth and goals for high school Biology. To check, ask: "What should the reflection include?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What should the reflection include?
  **A:** Skills gained, favorite investigation, a goal.

**Assessment:** Capstone presentation rubric and final exam.

**Answer key:** Capstone presentation rubric (4 levels): problem and criteria; science explanation; data from both versions; iteration reasoning; delivery and Q&A. Final exam sample answers: speed, net force, F = ma, Newton's laws, KE/PE, energy conservation, gravity, phases, seasons, waves (v = fλ), light, analog/digital, superposition, geologic time, fossils, homologous structures, Punnett squares, DNA, mutations, natural selection.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Year in review: the expanding promise of the Declaration

**Prepare:** Notebook timeline; all course documents. Write the final exam in two parts (Revolution–antebellum; Civil War–Industrial America).

**Watch for:**
- Describing the Declaration's promise as fulfilled by 1900. Many groups gained rights, but women, Black Americans, Native Americans and Asian immigrants still faced major barriers.
- Listing events without explaining significance. Each turning point should explain why it changed the course of history.

### Day 1 (Monday): Timeline review
*Student:* Complete the 1750–1900 timeline and mark 10 turning points with one-sentence explanations.

*How to teach:* Complete the timeline; mark 10 turning points with explanations.

- **Q:** Why is 1863 a turning point?
  **A:** The Emancipation Proclamation made ending slavery a war aim, and Gettysburg and Vicksburg turned the war in the Union's favor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Essential question essay
*Student:* Write an essay: 'How did Americans struggle to fulfill the promise of the Declaration from 1776 to 1900?'

*How to teach:* Write the essential question essay.

- **Q:** Give one example of progress and one setback in fulfilling the Declaration.
  **A:** Progress: the 13th–15th Amendments; setback: Jim Crow laws and disenfranchisement after 1877 (or Indian Removal, Chinese Exclusion).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Take part 1 of the final covering the Revolution through the antebellum era.

*How to teach:* Give final exam part 1.

- **Q:** What compromise created the House and Senate structure?
  **A:** The Great (Connecticut) Compromise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Take part 2 covering the Civil War through Industrial America.

*How to teach:* Give final exam part 2.

- **Q:** What did the 14th Amendment guarantee?
  **A:** Citizenship for all born or naturalized in the U.S., due process and equal protection of the laws.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on the most important person and event studied this year and why.

*How to teach:* Write the reflection on the most important person and event.

- **Q:** What makes a person or event 'most important'?
  **A:** Its scale of impact, how long its effects lasted and how many people it affected — answer should justify with evidence.

**Assessment:** Final exam and essential question essay.

**Answer key:** Essential question essay rubric (1–4): thesis on how Americans struggled to fulfill 'all men are created equal' and unalienable rights (e.g., expanding but incomplete); evidence across the period — Revolution and Constitution's compromises with slavery, Bill of Rights, abolitionists (Douglass, Garrison, Tubman), Seneca Falls, Indian Removal and Worcester, Civil War and Emancipation, 13th–15th Amendments, Reconstruction's rollback and Jim Crow, Chinese Exclusion, labor and Populist movements; analysis of progress and setbacks; organization and conventions. Final exam: part 1 and part 2 drawn from unit tests; 80%+ proficient. Suggested 10 turning points: 1776 Declaration, 1787 Constitution, 1791 Bill of Rights, 1803 Louisiana Purchase/Marbury, 1830 Indian Removal Act, 1848 Mexican Cession/Seneca Falls, 1857 Dred Scott, 1861 Fort Sumter, 1863 Emancipation/Gettysburg, 1865–1870 Reconstruction Amendments, 1869 transcontinental railroad, 1877 end of Reconstruction, 1882 Chinese Exclusion.

## Spanish 1B: Spanish I final integrated performance assessment

**Prepare:** Final exam packet: an authentic text and a native-speaker clip with comprehension and inference questions you write ahead; Spanish I can-do checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Answering inference questions with only literal facts. Explain what the text suggests using evidence.
- Using only present tense in the narrative. A memorable day requires the preterite for events.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interpretive
*Student:* Read an authentic text and listen to a native-speaker clip, answering comprehension and inference questions.

*How to teach:* Read an authentic text and listen to a clip; answer comprehension and inference questions.

- **Q:** How do you answer an inference question?
  **A:** State what the text suggests and support it with a detail from the text or audio.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interpersonal
*Student:* Hold a 5-minute unscripted conversation about home, shopping, a past trip, and technology.

*How to teach:* 5-minute unscripted conversation.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Cómo es tu casa?
  **A:** Mi casa es ___ y tiene ___ dormitorios; mi cuarto es más ___ que ___.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentational writing
*Student:* Write a 15–20 sentence narrative about a memorable day using present and preterite.

*How to teach:* Write a 15–20 sentence narrative of a memorable day.

- **Q:** Write an opening in the preterite.
  **A:** Example: El verano pasado fui a la playa con mi familia.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Presentational speaking
*Student:* Present a 2-minute cultural comparison from the year.

*How to teach:* Present a 2-minute cultural comparison.

- **Q:** Give one comparison sentence.
  **A:** Example: En España la cena es más tarde que en mi casa.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Complete the Spanish I can-do self-assessment and set goals for Spanish II.

*How to teach:* Complete the Spanish I can-do self-assessment; set Spanish II goals.

- **Q:** Write a can-do statement.
  **A:** Example: 'I can describe a past trip using the preterite.'

**Assessment:** Integrated performance assessment rubric; completion of Spanish I.

**Answer key:** Integrated performance assessment rubric (1–4 each): Interpretive — main idea, key details and one supported inference; Interpersonal — 5-minute unscripted conversation on home, shopping, a past trip and technology with questions and follow-ups (Novice High: strings of sentences, some past tense); Presentational writing — 15–20 sentences narrating a memorable day using present for background and preterite for events, sequence words, varied verbs, accurate agreement; Presentational speaking — 2-minute cultural comparison with specific details. Proficient = 3s across; completes Spanish I.

**Watch for:**

- Ignoring feedback. Correct: use it to set goals.

*How to teach:* Set up the exhibit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse performance pieces.

*How to teach:* Rehearse performance pieces.

- **Q:** Why rehearse in the actual space?
  **A:** To adjust to space, sound and sightlines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Showcase
*Student:* Host the showcase.

*How to teach:* Host the showcase.

- **Q:** How should an artist talk to guests?
  **A:** Welcome them, explain intent and process briefly and listen to their responses.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Collect guest feedback.

*How to teach:* Collect guest feedback.

- **Q:** What makes feedback useful?
  **A:** Specific comments about what was effective and what was memorable.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection.

## Physical Education 8: Plan evaluation and transition to high school PE

**Prepare:** Fitness journal; search your local high school's PE requirements (California requires 2 years of high school PE; students may be exempted after passing FitnessGram in grade 10 per district policy). Plan field day stations.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking PE ends after school requirements. Correct: lifelong activity is the goal.
- Evaluating a plan only by results. Correct: also evaluate adherence and enjoyment.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the scheduled workout and log it.

*How to teach:* Complete and log the workout.

- **Q:** Why log even routine workouts?
  **A:** Logs show patterns and progress.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the final scheduled workout of the two weeks.

*How to teach:* Final scheduled workout.

- **Q:** How did the final workout compare to day 1?
  **A:** Answers vary; compare HR, RPE or reps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Evaluate
*Student:* Write an evaluation of your plan: adherence, enjoyment and results, plus one change.

*How to teach:* Write evaluation: adherence, enjoyment, results, one change.

- **Q:** What makes a fitness plan successful?
  **A:** It's followed consistently, is enjoyable, safe and leads toward goals.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Looking ahead
*Student:* Research high school PE course requirements and write two goals for grade 9.

*How to teach:* Research high school PE and write two goals.

- **Q:** How many years of PE does California require for high school graduation?
  **A:** Two years (20 credits), with possible exemption options set by state law and district policy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Field day
*Student:* Organize and lead a family or co-op field day with stations from this year's units.

*How to teach:* Organize and lead a field day.

- **Q:** What makes a good field day station?
  **A:** Clear instructions, safe setup, everyone active, a fun challenge from a unit this year.

**Assessment:** Plan evaluation and field day leadership.

**Answer key:** Plan evaluation: 4 = adherence %, enjoyment, results with data, one specific change; field day leadership: 4 = 4+ stations planned, clear instructions, safety, everyone included; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = disorganized; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: High school wellness plan and year review

**Prepare:** Find the grade 7 wellness wheel (or draw one: physical, mental/emotional, social, safety, environmental, intellectual). Prepare a cumulative final (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Making vague goals. Correct: use SMART goals with tracking.
- Forgetting resources. Correct: list specific names and numbers (counselor, doctor, 988).

### Day 1 (Monday): Wellness wheel
*Student:* Re-rate your wellness wheel from grade 7 and compare.

*How to teach:* Re-rate each wedge 1–10 and compare with grade 7.

- **Q:** Which area improved most?
  **A:** Answers vary; the child should explain why.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Write a high school wellness plan with goals for physical, mental, social and safety health.

*How to teach:* Write a high school wellness plan with SMART goals for physical, mental, social and safety health.

- **Q:** Give one SMART physical goal.
  **A:** Example: 'I will be active 60 minutes a day at least 5 days a week for the fall semester, tracked in a log.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Supports
*Student:* List supports and resources you will use in high school.

*How to teach:* List supports: counselor, doctor, family, coaches, 988.

- **Q:** Name two high school resources.
  **A:** School counselor, school nurse, teachers/coaches, clubs, wellness center.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review key concepts from all six units.

*How to teach:* Review key concepts from all units.

- **Q:** What does Ask-Listen-Tell mean?
  **A:** Ask directly about suicide, listen without judgment, tell a trusted adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final
*Student:* Take a cumulative final assessment and share your plan with a parent.

*How to teach:* Give the final and share the plan.

- **Q:** What reverses an opioid overdose?
  **A:** Naloxone.

**Assessment:** Wellness plan and final assessment.

**Answer key:** Final key (sample): resilience definition; 988; protective factors; calcium 1,300 mg; supplements not FDA-approved before sale; abstinence 100%; consent clear/ongoing; minors 12+ STI consent; 0.01% under-21 BAC; naloxone; addiction = treatable disease; CPR 100–120/min; reach-throw-don't go; 5 D's; FTC false ads; CDC/CDPH roles. Wellness plan: 4 = SMART goals in four areas, supports, tracking plan.
