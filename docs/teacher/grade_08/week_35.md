# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 35

## English Language Arts 8: Multimedia presentation

**Prepare:** Slide software (Google Slides, PowerPoint or Keynote), image sources with licenses (e.g., Wikimedia Commons, Library of Congress), a timer and a phone to record rehearsal. Invite an audience for Thursday.

**Watch for:**
- Putting paragraphs on slides and reading them. Slides should show images, a chart or key words; the speaker explains.
- Using any image from a search without credit. Credit the creator and source on the slide or a credits slide.

### Day 1 (Monday): Planning
*Student:* Plan a 5-minute presentation of the research with key points and evidence.

*How to teach:* Child plans a 5-minute talk: hook, thesis, 3 key points with one piece of evidence each, conclusion. Check understanding with: "About how many words fit in a 5-minute talk?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** About how many words fit in a 5-minute talk?
  **A:** Roughly 600–700 words at a natural speaking pace.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slide design
*Student:* Create slides with images, a chart or timeline and minimal text, crediting all images.

*How to teach:* Child builds slides with images, one chart or timeline and minimal text, crediting each image. To check, ask: "A good guideline for text per slide?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** A good guideline for text per slide?
  **A:** A title plus a few key words — about 6 words per line and no more than 3–4 lines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearsal
*Student:* Rehearse with a timer and record yourself; evaluate eye contact, volume and pacing.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with a timer and record. Watch together and note one strength and one fix for eye contact, volume and pacing.

- **Q:** What are filler words and how do you reduce them?
  **A:** 'Um,' 'like,' 'so' — pause silently instead and practice transitions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Presentation
*Student:* Deliver the presentation to family or a homeschool group and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Child presents to family or a homeschool group and answers questions. Before moving on, ask: "How should you answer a question you can't answer?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** How should you answer a question you can't answer?
  **A:** Say you don't know, explain what you do know, and offer to find out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on the research process and what you would improve.

*How to teach:* Child writes a one-page reflection on the research process and improvements. Check understanding with: "Give an example of a specific improvement." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Give an example of a specific improvement.
  **A:** 'Start evaluating sources earlier so I don't lose time on unreliable websites.'

**Assessment:** Presentation rubric and reflection.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric (4 levels per trait): Content (thesis and 3 key points with evidence); Visuals (minimal text, image/chart/timeline, all credited); Delivery (eye contact, volume, pacing, few filler words); Time (4.5–5.5 minutes); Q&A (answers accurately or honestly says 'I'd need to check'). Reflection rubric: describes the research process, two strengths, two specific improvements.

## Mathematics 8: Modeling project: planning a small business

**Prepare:** Desmos or a spreadsheet, graph paper; prepare a 30-problem cumulative review set.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing profit with revenue. Profit = revenue − cost.
- Thinking break-even means zero sales. It's where revenue equals cost.

### Day 1 (Monday): Business idea and costs
*Student:* Choose a small business (lemonade stand, dog walking, baking) and write a cost function with fixed and per-item costs.

*How to teach:* Child chooses a business and writes a cost function. Check understanding with: "Lemonade: $20 fixed (pitcher, sign) + $0.25 per cup. Cost function?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Lemonade: $20 fixed (pitcher, sign) + $0.25 per cup. Cost function?
  **A:** C = 20 + 0.25x.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revenue and break-even
*Student:* Write a revenue function and solve the system to find the break-even point; graph both in Desmos.

*How to teach:* Write revenue; solve the system. To check, ask: "Selling for $1 per cup, find break-even." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Selling for $1 per cup, find break-even.
  **A:** x = 20 + 0.25x → 0.75x = 20 → x ≈ 26.7, so 27 cups.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Using data
*Student:* Use a short table of sales vs price (given or surveyed) to fit a line and predict sales at a new price.

*How to teach:* Fit a line to price vs sales data and predict. Finish by asking: "Sales are 50 at $1.00 and 30 at $1.50. Predict sales at $1.25 with a line." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Sales are 50 at $1.00 and 30 at $1.50. Predict sales at $1.25 with a line.
  **A:** Slope −20/0.5 = −40 per dollar; at $1.25: 50 − 40(0.25) = 40 cups.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final review
*Student:* Cumulative review of all units using a 30-problem mixed set; mark items to revisit.

*How to teach:* 30-problem cumulative review; mark items to revisit. Before moving on, ask: "Solve 4x − 7 = 2x + 9." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Solve 4x − 7 = 2x + 9.
  **A:** x = 8.

### Day 5 (Friday): Project presentation
*Student:* Present the business plan with graphs, break-even point and recommendations.

*How to teach:* Child presents the plan. Check understanding with: "What is profit at 50 cups for the lemonade stand?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What is profit at 50 cups for the lemonade stand?
  **A:** R − C = 50 − (20 + 12.50) = $17.50.

**Assessment:** Business modeling project rubric.

**Answer key:** Project rubric (4 levels): 4 = cost function with fixed and per-item cost, revenue function, correct break-even point from solving the system and the graph, data-based line of fit with prediction, clear recommendation; 3 = one error; 2 = functions but no break-even or no recommendation; 1 = incomplete. Example check: cost C = 20 + 0.25x, revenue R = 1x → break-even at about 27 cups.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Capstone: build, test, iterate

**Prepare:** Project materials and measuring tools (thermometer, decibel app, scale, ruler).

**Watch for:**
- Changing several variables between versions. Change one main feature so the test shows what caused improvement.
- Deciding by feel instead of data.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build
*Student:* Build prototype version 1.

*How to teach:* The child builds prototype version 1 following the plan, noting any changes made during building. Check understanding with: "Why document changes during the build?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why document changes during the build?
  **A:** So results can be explained and the design reproduced.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test 1
*Student:* Run 3 tests and record data in a table.

*How to teach:* Run 3 tests; record data in a table with units. To check, ask: "Version 1 readings: 18, 22, 20 dB reduction. Average?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Version 1 readings: 18, 22, 20 dB reduction. Average?
  **A:** 20 dB.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Analyze & redesign
*Student:* Analyze data and identify the weakest part; redesign.

*How to teach:* The child analyzes data, identifies the weakest part, and redesigns one feature. Finish by asking: "What's an example of using data to redesign?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What's an example of using data to redesign?
  **A:** If heat escapes through the lid, add insulation there.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test 2
*Student:* Build and test version 2; compare results with version 1.

*How to teach:* Build and test version 2; compare averages. Before moving on, ask: "V1 average 20 dB, V2 average 27 dB. Improvement?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** V1 average 20 dB, V2 average 27 dB. Improvement?
  **A:** 7 dB more reduction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final review
*Student:* Review the year's key science ideas with a cumulative study guide.

*How to teach:* Cumulative review with study guide. Check understanding with: "F = ma: 3 kg at 2 m/s²?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** F = ma: 3 kg at 2 m/s²?
  **A:** 6 N.

**Assessment:** Testing data and iteration log.

**Answer key:** Testing data and iteration log rubric: data tables for version 1 and version 2 (3 trials each, averages, units); weakest part identified with evidence; one targeted change; comparison of averages; conclusion about whether criteria were met.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Industrial America museum project

**Prepare:** Library books; Library of Congress and Calisphere sources; poster board or slides; citation format (author/creator, title, date, source/URL). Write the unit 10 test.

**Watch for:**
- Using mainly secondary sources. The exhibit should center on at least 3 primary sources with captions that source them.
- Writing a topic, not a thesis. A thesis makes an arguable claim about impact.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a topic
*Student:* Select an inventor, immigrant group, labor leader or industry and locate 3 primary sources.

*How to teach:* Choose a topic; locate 3 primary sources.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a primary and a secondary source?
  **A:** A primary source was created at the time by someone involved (letter, photo, law); a secondary source analyzes events later (textbook, article).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Take notes with citations and write a thesis about the topic's impact on American life.

*How to teach:* Take cited notes; write a thesis.

- **Q:** Turn 'Jacob Riis' into a thesis.
  **A:** Example: 'Jacob Riis's photographs exposed tenement conditions and pushed New York to pass housing reforms, showing the power of photography to drive change.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Build a museum exhibit poster or slideshow with captions explaining each source.

*How to teach:* Build the exhibit with captions.

- **Q:** What should a caption include?
  **A:** Creator, date, what the source shows and why it matters to the thesis.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the exhibit to family and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present to family; answer questions.

- **Q:** How does your topic connect to the Industrial Age?
  **A:** Open answer — must link to industrialization, immigration, labor or business with evidence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 test
*Student:* Unit test on Industrial America.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 10 test.

- **Q:** What was the significance of the Chinese Exclusion Act?
  **A:** It was the first U.S. law to ban immigration based on nationality.

**Assessment:** Exhibit rubric and Unit 10 test.

**Answer key:** Exhibit rubric (1–4 each): thesis (arguable claim about the topic's impact on American life), 3+ primary sources correctly identified and cited, captions that source and explain each, historical accuracy and context, presentation and answers to questions. Unit 10 test (suggested): transcontinental railroad, Homestead Act, Plains wars, Dawes Act, boarding schools, vertical/horizontal integration, Gospel of Wealth, inventors, new immigration, Ellis/Angel Island, Chinese Exclusion Act, tenements and Riis, conservation, child labor, AFL, Haymarket, Pullman, Grange, Populists, Cross of Gold.

## Spanish 1B: Spanish I review: past tense and culture

**Prepare:** Unit tests, travel blog, self-made culture quiz, two authentic texts (a blog post and an ad).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing preterite forms of ir/ser with other verbs. Practice in stories.
- Forgetting accents on regular preterite yo and él forms (compré, compró).

### Day 1 (Monday): Regular preterite
*Student:* Review regular and spelling-change preterite verbs with a narrative writing task.

*How to teach:* Narrative writing reviewing regular and spelling-change preterite.

- **Q:** Write: 'I arrived, looked for my friends and played soccer.'
  **A:** Llegué, busqué a mis amigos y jugué al fútbol.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Irregular preterite
*Student:* Review ir/ser, hacer, decir, and dar in the preterite with a story-retelling activity.

*How to teach:* Story retelling with ir/ser, hacer, decir, dar.

- **Q:** Write: 'She went to the store, made a cake and gave it to me.'
  **A:** Ella fue a la tienda, hizo un pastel y me lo dio (or y me dio el pastel).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Culture review
*Student:* Review the year's culture topics (tapas, homes, markets, destinations, telenovelas) with a self-made quiz.

*How to teach:* Culture review with a self-made quiz.

- **Q:** What is the Rastro?
  **A:** Madrid's Sunday open-air flea market.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reading practice
*Student:* Read two authentic texts (a blog post and an ad) and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Read a blog post and an ad; answer questions.

- **Q:** On an ad, what does 'oferta' mean?
  **A:** Sale/special offer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Speaking practice
*Student:* Practice a mock 5-minute conversation covering all year's topics.

*How to teach:* Mock 5-minute conversation on all topics.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana pasado?
  **A:** El fin de semana pasado ___ (preterite), e.g., fui al cine y comí con mi familia.

**Assessment:** Self-scored practice tasks.

**Answer key:** Self-scored practice tasks: narrative uses regular and spelling-change preterite correctly; story retelling uses fui/fue, hice/hizo, dije/dijo, di/dio; culture quiz answers (tapas = small shared plates in Spain; huipiles = Maya woven blouses; azotea = rooftop terrace; Machu Picchu = Inca, Peru; telenovelas = serial dramas with a set ending); reading questions answered with evidence; mock 5-minute conversation covers present and past.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Curating the portfolio

**Prepare:** All year's artwork (drawings, paintings, photos, scripts, recordings, animation, dance video), mats or cardstock, glue stick, label cards.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing only 'favorite' pieces. Correct: show range and growth across art forms.
- Labels without information. Correct: include title, artist, medium, date and size.

### Day 1 (Monday): Selection
*Student:* Review the year's work and select 8–10 pieces.

*How to teach:* Select 8–10 pieces.

- **Q:** What criteria should guide selection?
  **A:** Quality, variety of media/art forms, growth over time and personal meaning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mounting
*Student:* Mount or mat 2-D works.

*How to teach:* Mount or mat 2-D works.

- **Q:** Why mat artwork?
  **A:** It frames the piece, protects it and gives a finished, professional look.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Labels
*Student:* Write labels.

*How to teach:* Write labels.

- **Q:** What goes on a museum label?
  **A:** Title, artist name, date, medium and size (plus a short description if desired).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Statement
*Student:* Write an artist statement.

*How to teach:* Write an artist statement.

- **Q:** What does an artist statement do?
  **A:** Explains the artist's themes, process and intent for viewers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Layout
*Student:* Plan the exhibit layout.

*How to teach:* Plan the exhibit layout.

- **Q:** How should you arrange an exhibit?
  **A:** Group by theme or medium, hang at eye level (center about 57–60 inches), leave space between works, plan a viewing path.

**Assessment:** Portfolio selection and statements.

**Answer key:** Portfolio selection and statements: 4 = 8–10 works across at least three art forms showing growth, neatly mounted, complete labels, thoughtful artist statement (themes, growth, intent); 3 = minor gaps; 2 = limited range; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Two-week personal fitness plan: implement

**Prepare:** Fitness journal, watch or heart-rate monitor.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping logging. Correct: log immediately after each session.
- Pushing through pain. Correct: distinguish muscle effort from sharp joint pain; stop and rest for pain.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the scheduled workout and log heart rate and perceived exertion.

*How to teach:* Complete and log workout with HR and RPE.

- **Q:** What RPE fits a moderate workout?
  **A:** About 5–6 out of 10.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the scheduled strength and flexibility workout.

*How to teach:* Strength and flexibility workout.

- **Q:** Why stretch after strength training?
  **A:** Muscles are warm, so static stretching can maintain flexibility and aid relaxation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the scheduled cardio workout, recording time and intensity.

*How to teach:* Cardio workout; record time and intensity.

- **Q:** How can you check intensity without a monitor?
  **A:** Talk test or RPE.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan day
*Student:* Complete the scheduled activity or active rest day.

*How to teach:* Activity or active rest day.

- **Q:** What is active rest?
  **A:** Light activity (walking, easy stretching) that promotes recovery.

### Day 5 (Friday): Mid-plan check
*Student:* Calculate adherence so far and adjust next week's plan if needed.

*How to teach:* Calculate adherence and adjust.

- **Q:** You completed 6 of 7 planned sessions. What's your adherence?
  **A:** 6 ÷ 7 ≈ 86%.

**Assessment:** Activity log for plan week 1.

**Answer key:** Activity log week 1: proficient = 5+ entries with date, activity, duration, HR/RPE and notes; adherence calculated correctly; one adjustment written.

## Health Education 8: Health advocacy campaign: carry out

**Prepare:** Gather art supplies or video tools. Prepare a short audience survey (before/after questions).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking sharing once means success. Correct: measure impact with feedback or a survey.
- Ignoring feedback. Correct: revise based on audience reactions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Create
*Student:* Create campaign materials.

*How to teach:* The child creates materials using the plan.

- **Q:** Does your material include a clear message and call to action?
  **A:** Yes — check that both are visible and accurate.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise materials based on feedback.

*How to teach:* Revise with feedback.

- **Q:** What feedback did you get and what did you change?
  **A:** Answers vary; a good answer names specific changes (clearer wording, bigger visual).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Share
*Student:* Share the campaign with family, co-op or community.

*How to teach:* Share with audience.

- **Q:** Who saw your campaign?
  **A:** Answers vary; should match the target audience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Measure
*Student:* Survey your audience to measure impact.

*How to teach:* Survey the audience on knowledge and intent.

- **Q:** How can a survey measure impact?
  **A:** Ask what they learned or whether they plan to change a behavior; compare before and after answers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on the campaign's results.

*How to teach:* Write reflection.

- **Q:** What would you do differently?
  **A:** Answers vary; a good answer uses survey evidence.

**Assessment:** Campaign and impact reflection.

**Answer key:** Campaign and reflection: 4 = polished, accurate materials, shared with target audience, survey data reported, thoughtful reflection on impact and improvements; 3 = shared with some data; 2 = materials only; 1 = incomplete.
