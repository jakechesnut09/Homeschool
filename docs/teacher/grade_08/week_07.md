# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 7

## English Language Arts 8: The Outsiders, chapters 4–6: turning point and allusion

**Prepare:** Read chapters 4–6 and 'Nothing Gold Can Stay' (8 lines). Parental preview: Johnny kills Bob; a church fire injures Johnny.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'gold' means money. In Frost's poem gold is the early, fleeting beauty of nature — and of youth/innocence.
- Confusing allusion with illusion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 4
*Student:* Read chapter 4 and analyze the turning point in the park; list the consequences Ponyboy and Johnny face.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 4; analyze the park. Check understanding with: "What happens in the park?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What happens in the park?
  **A:** Socs try to drown Ponyboy in the fountain and Johnny stabs and kills Bob.
- **Q:** Who helps them flee?
  **A:** Dally gives money and a gun and sends them to an abandoned church in Windrixville.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 5 & Frost
*Student:* Read chapter 5 and the poem 'Nothing Gold Can Stay'; annotate the poem's imagery and connect 'gold' to Ponyboy and Johnny.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 5 and the poem; annotate imagery. To check, ask: "What does 'Nature's first green is gold' mean?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What does 'Nature's first green is gold' mean?
  **A:** New leaves first look golden; the poem says beauty and innocence don't last.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 6
*Student:* Read chapter 6 and analyze the church fire as a moment that changes how characters see themselves.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 6 together or independently. Ask the child to find where Ponyboy, Johnny and Dally act differently than their 'hood' reputations suggest, and write how the fire changes how they see themselves.

- **Q:** What happens at the church in chapter 6?
  **A:** The church catches fire (likely from their cigarette) while schoolchildren are inside; Ponyboy and Johnny go in and save them; a falling timber badly injures Johnny, and Dally pulls them out.
- **Q:** How does the fire change how the boys see themselves?
  **A:** They act as heroes rather than 'hoods'; Ponyboy realizes Dally and Johnny are capable of gallantry, and the newspapers call them heroes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Allusion study
*Student:* Explain the allusions to 'Gone with the Wind' and Frost and how each deepens the theme.

*How to teach:* Explain allusion (a reference to a well-known work). Child explains how Gone with the Wind and the Frost poem deepen the theme.

- **Q:** Why does Johnny connect the Southern gentlemen in Gone with the Wind to Dally?
  **A:** They rode into sure death gallantly; Johnny sees Dally's loyalty and courage that way — which foreshadows Johnny's own sacrifice.
- **Q:** How does the Frost allusion deepen the theme?
  **A:** It frames youth and innocence as 'gold' that fades, which becomes Johnny's message to Ponyboy to 'stay gold.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a theme paragraph about 'staying gold' with two pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* Model a theme statement as a complete sentence about life, not one word. Child writes a theme paragraph about 'staying gold' with two pieces of evidence and commentary.

- **Q:** Who recites 'Nothing Gold Can Stay,' and when?
  **A:** Ponyboy, while he and Johnny watch the sunrise from the church.
- **Q:** Turn the topic 'innocence' into a theme statement.
  **A:** Example: 'Innocence fades as people face hardship, but holding on to wonder and kindness lets a person stay "gold."'

**Assessment:** Theme paragraph.

**Answer key:** Theme paragraph rubric: 4 = theme stated as a universal idea (e.g., innocence is precious and fleeting but can be kept by holding on to goodness), two pieces of evidence (Frost's 'Nothing gold can stay,' Johnny's 'Stay gold, Ponyboy'), explanation.

## Mathematics 8: Slope from similar triangles

**Prepare:** Graph paper, colored pencils, Desmos (desmos.com/calculator). Prepare the Unit 2 review set and test.

**Watch for:**
- Computing run/rise instead of rise/run. Always say 'rise over run' — vertical change on top.
- Thinking slope depends on which two points you choose. Similar slope triangles show it is the same everywhere on a line.

### Day 1 (Monday): Slope triangles
*Student:* Draw three different slope triangles on the same line and show they are similar, so rise/run is equal for each.

*How to teach:* Child draws a line and three different-size slope triangles on it. Ask: why are these triangles similar? (They each have a right angle and the same angle where the line meets the horizontal — AA.) So rise/run is the same for all.

- **Q:** Why is rise/run the same for any slope triangle on one line?
  **A:** All slope triangles on the same line are similar (AA: a right angle and a shared angle with the line), so the ratio of their vertical to horizontal sides is constant.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finding slope
*Student:* Calculate the slope of 10 lines on grids using any two points; check with Desmos.

*How to teach:* Child computes slopes of 10 lines from two grid points, then types the points in Desmos to check. To check, ask: "Find the slope through (1, 2) and (4, 8)." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Find the slope through (1, 2) and (4, 8).
  **A:** (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 6/3 = 2.
- **Q:** Find the slope through (−2, 5) and (2, −3).
  **A:** (−3 − 5)/(2 − (−2)) = −8/4 = −2.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Writing equations from slope
*Student:* Use a point (x, y) on a line through (0, 0) or (0, b) and similar triangles to derive y = mx and y = mx + b.

*How to teach:* Model: for a line through (0, b) with slope m, a general point (x, y) gives the slope triangle (y − b)/x = m, so y = mx + b. The child derives y = mx for a line through the origin.

- **Q:** Write the equation of the line through (0, 3) with slope 2.
  **A:** y = 2x + 3.
- **Q:** A line through the origin contains (4, 10). What is its equation?
  **A:** y = 2.5x (slope 10/4).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 2 review
*Student:* Mixed review of dilations, similarity and slope with a partner-check answer key.

*How to teach:* Partner-check review (you are the partner): child works the mixed set, then checks with the key and reworks errors. Before moving on, ask: "Dilate (−1, 2) by scale factor 4 with center at the origin." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Dilate (−1, 2) by scale factor 4 with center at the origin.
  **A:** (−4, 8).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Unit test on dilations, similarity, AA criterion and slope.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test (key below). Check understanding with: "Two triangles each have a 90° angle and a 30° angle. Must they be similar?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Two triangles each have a 90° angle and a 30° angle. Must they be similar?
  **A:** Yes, by AA (the third angles are both 60°).

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test suggested items: 1) Dilate (−1, 2) by 4 from origin → (−4, 8). 2) Scale factor from 10 to 25 → 2.5. 3) Are 3×5 and 6×10 rectangles similar? Yes, ratio 1/2. 4) Triangles with angles 35°, 75° and 35°, 70° → similar by AA (the third angles are 70° and 75°, so both triangles have 35°, 70°, 75°). 5) Missing side: similar triangles 5, 12, 13 and 10, 24, x → x = 26. 6) Slope through (1, 2) and (4, 8) → 2. 7) Slope through (−2, 5) and (2, −3) → −2. 8) Equation of line through (0, 3) with slope 1/2 → y = (1/2)x + 3. 9) Explain why slope triangles on one line are similar (AA: right angle + equal angle with the line). 1 point each; 2 for item 9.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Potential energy and fields

**Prepare:** Rubber bands, a ruler, a tray of flour or sand, a ball, magnets, paper clips, books.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking potential energy only means height. Energy can be stored by stretching (elastic), position in a magnetic or electric field, or chemical bonds.
- Forgetting that PE depends on the system (e.g., ball and Earth together).

### Day 1 (Monday): Gravitational PE
*Student:* Drop a ball from 3 heights onto a tray of flour or sand and measure crater size; model energy stored by height.

*How to teach:* Drop a ball from 3 heights into flour; measure crater diameter. Check understanding with: "What does a bigger crater show?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What does a bigger crater show?
  **A:** More energy at impact — from more gravitational PE stored at greater height.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Elastic PE
*Student:* Launch rubber bands stretched 5, 10 and 15 cm and measure distance traveled; relate stretch to stored energy.

*How to teach:* Launch rubber bands stretched 5, 10 and 15 cm (point away from people); measure distance. To check, ask: "Why does a 15-cm stretch fly farther?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Why does a 15-cm stretch fly farther?
  **A:** More stretch stores more elastic PE, which becomes more KE.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Magnetic PE
*Student:* Measure the distance at which a paper clip jumps to a magnet; model how arrangement changes stored energy with diagrams.

*How to teach:* Bring a paper clip slowly toward a magnet and measure the jump distance; draw how arrangement changes stored energy. Finish by asking: "When is magnetic PE greatest between a magnet and paper clip?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** When is magnetic PE greatest between a magnet and paper clip?
  **A:** When they are held apart (separated), since the attraction can do work as they come together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modeling systems
*Student:* Draw energy models (before/after diagrams) for a roller coaster, bow and arrow and two magnets.

*How to teach:* The child draws before/after energy models for a roller coaster, a bow and arrow, and two magnets. Before moving on, ask: "Describe energy in a bow and arrow." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Describe energy in a bow and arrow.
  **A:** Elastic PE in the drawn bow becomes KE of the arrow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on potential energy types and models.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (key). Check understanding with: "Calculate GPE of a 2-kg book on a 1.5-m shelf." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Calculate GPE of a 2-kg book on a 1.5-m shelf.
  **A:** 29.4 J.

**Assessment:** Potential energy model diagrams and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) GPE = mgh; 2-kg book on a 1.5-m shelf → 2 × 9.8 × 1.5 = 29.4 J. 2) More stretch = more elastic PE. 3) Pulling two attracting magnets apart increases PE; letting them snap together converts it to KE. 4) Roller coaster: most GPE at the top, most KE at the bottom. Model diagrams rubric: before/after pictures with energy bars or labels for each energy type and a statement that total energy is conserved (some becomes thermal).

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: How the Constitution works

**Prepare:** Constitution transcript; congress.gov; two news articles on one current bill from different outlets. Write the unit 2 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the president makes laws. Congress makes laws; the president signs or vetoes and enforces them.
- Believing judicial review is written in the Constitution. It was established by Marbury v. Madison (1803).

### Day 1 (Monday): Structure of the Constitution
*Student:* Scavenger hunt through Articles I–VII to locate powers of each branch.

*How to teach:* Scavenger hunt through Articles I–VII.

- **Q:** Which article creates Congress? Which the presidency? Which the courts?
  **A:** Article I; Article II; Article III.
- **Q:** How many states were needed to ratify the Constitution?
  **A:** Nine (Article VII).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Checks & balances
*Student:* Create a diagram of checks between branches with real examples (veto, judicial review).

*How to teach:* Diagram checks and balances with examples.

- **Q:** How can Congress check the president?
  **A:** Override vetoes (2/3), reject appointments and treaties (Senate), control funding, impeach and remove.
- **Q:** How can courts check Congress?
  **A:** By declaring laws unconstitutional (judicial review).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Federalism
*Student:* Sort powers into federal, state and shared; discuss enumerated vs implied powers.

*How to teach:* Sort powers into federal, state and shared; discuss enumerated vs implied powers.

- **Q:** Is printing money a federal or state power?
  **A:** Federal.
- **Q:** What is an implied power?
  **A:** A power not listed but reasonably needed to carry out enumerated powers, justified by the necessary and proper (elastic) clause — e.g., creating a national bank.

### Day 4 (Thursday): How a bill becomes law & the press
*Student:* Trace a current bill on congress.gov; compare two news articles on it to evaluate the role of a free press.

*How to teach:* Trace a current bill on congress.gov; compare two news articles.

- **Q:** List the main steps of how a bill becomes law.
  **A:** Introduced → committee → passes House and Senate in identical form (conference if needed) → president signs (or vetoes; Congress can override with 2/3).
- **Q:** Why is a free press important to democracy?
  **A:** It informs citizens and holds officials accountable; comparing outlets helps detect bias.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Unit test on the Constitution.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test.

- **Q:** What does the supremacy clause say?
  **A:** The Constitution, federal laws and treaties are the supreme law of the land, above state laws.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test (suggested): Article I — legislative (Congress); II — executive (president); III — judicial (Supreme Court); IV — states' relations; V — amendment process (2/3 of Congress proposes, 3/4 of states ratify); VI — supremacy clause; VII — ratification by 9 states. Checks: president vetoes bills; Congress overrides with 2/3 of both houses; Senate confirms appointments and ratifies treaties (2/3); courts declare laws unconstitutional; Congress can impeach (House) and remove (Senate 2/3). Federalism: federal — coin money, declare war, foreign treaties, regulate interstate commerce; state — schools, local governments, driver's licenses, elections; shared (concurrent) — tax, borrow, make courts, enforce laws. Enumerated powers are listed; implied powers come from the necessary and proper clause. Federalist/Anti-Federalist arguments; Bill of Rights.

## Spanish 1B: Stem-changing verbs o→ue and u→ue

**Prepare:** Boot-chart template (draw a boot around yo, tú, él, ellos forms), Conjuguemos stem-changer practice.

**Watch for:**
- Changing the stem in nosotros: 'puedemos.' Nosotros and vosotros keep the original stem: podemos.
- Changing the ending vowel instead of the stem: 'dormo.' The change is in the stem: duermo.

### Day 1 (Monday): The boot
*Student:* Learn the stem-change 'boot' pattern and conjugate poder and dormir, noting nosotros/vosotros don't change.

*How to teach:* Teach the boot; conjugate poder and dormir.

- **Q:** Conjugate dormir for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** duermo; dormimos.
- **Q:** Which forms don't change in a boot verb?
  **A:** Nosotros and vosotros.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): More verbs
*Student:* Add costar, volver, encontrar, almorzar, and jugar (u→ue) and complete a Conjuguemos stem-changer drill.

*How to teach:* Add costar, volver, encontrar, almorzar, jugar (u→ue); Conjuguemos drill.

- **Q:** Conjugate jugar for tú.
  **A:** juegas.
- **Q:** Complete: ¿Cuánto ___ la lámpara? (costar)
  **A:** cuesta.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sleep survey
*Student:* Ask family ¿Cuántas horas duermes? and ¿A qué hora vuelves a casa? and graph the results.

*How to teach:* Survey family: ¿Cuántas horas duermes? ¿A qué hora vuelves a casa? Graph.

- **Q:** Report: 'My dad sleeps seven hours.'
  **A:** Mi papá duerme siete horas.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Puedo...
*Student:* Write 10 sentences about what you can and cannot do in your house using poder + infinitive.

*How to teach:* Write 10 puedo/no puedo sentences about the house.

- **Q:** Write: 'I can't play ball in the living room.'
  **A:** No puedo jugar a la pelota en la sala.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an o→ue stem-changer quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the o→ue quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'We can eat lunch at noon.'
  **A:** Podemos almorzar a mediodía.

**Assessment:** Stem-changer quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** Stem-changer quiz: poder — puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, podéis, pueden; dormir — duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormís, duermen; jugar — juego, juegas, juega, jugamos, jugáis, juegan. Samples: ¿Cuánto (costar) los zapatos? → cuestan; Nosotros (volver) a las cinco → volvemos; Yo (almorzar) a la una → almuerzo; Ella no (encontrar) sus llaves → encuentra. 80% passing.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Color theory and mood

**Prepare:** Acrylic or tempera in red, yellow, blue, white, black; brushes; palette; heavy paper; water cup. Write a 10-question color vocabulary quiz. Pull up 'The Old Guitarist' (1903–04) and 'The Dessert: Harmony in Red' (1908).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking adding black makes a darker version of a color. Correct: it makes a shade but can muddy color; complements can also darken.
- Confusing tints and tones. Correct: tint = + white; shade = + black; tone = + gray.

### Day 1 (Monday): Color wheel
*Student:* Mix and paint a 12-part color wheel of primary, secondary and tertiary colors.

*How to teach:* Mix and paint a 12-part wheel.

- **Q:** Name the secondary colors and how to mix them.
  **A:** Orange (red + yellow), green (yellow + blue), violet (blue + red).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tints, shades, tones
*Student:* Paint a 7-step tint-to-shade strip for one hue and a tone strip adding gray.

*How to teach:* Paint a tint-to-shade strip and a tone strip.

- **Q:** What is a tint?
  **A:** A color plus white.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Color schemes
*Student:* Paint the same simple landscape thumbnail 4 times in complementary, analogous, monochromatic and warm/cool schemes.

*How to teach:* Paint four thumbnails in different schemes.

- **Q:** What are complementary colors? Give a pair.
  **A:** Colors opposite each other on the wheel, e.g., blue and orange.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Color and mood in art
*Student:* Compare Picasso's 'The Old Guitarist' (Blue Period) and Matisse's 'The Dessert: Harmony in Red' and explain how color shapes mood.

*How to teach:* Compare the two paintings and how color shapes mood.

- **Q:** How does color affect mood in 'The Old Guitarist'?
  **A:** Dominant blues create a cold, sad, lonely mood matching the frail, poor man.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on color vocabulary and write which of your 4 thumbnails best conveys a chosen mood and why.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; child writes which thumbnail best conveys a mood.

- **Q:** What is an analogous scheme?
  **A:** Colors next to each other on the wheel, e.g., yellow, yellow-orange, orange.

**Assessment:** Color wheel, scheme studies and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) primary: red, yellow, blue 2) secondary: orange, green, violet 3) tertiary: primary + neighboring secondary (e.g., red-orange) 4) tint = + white 5) shade = + black 6) tone = + gray 7) complementary = opposites 8) analogous = neighbors 9) monochromatic = one hue with tints/shades 10) warm (red/orange/yellow) vs cool (blue/green/violet). Written choice: names scheme, mood and specific color evidence.

## Physical Education 8: Flag football: plays and game play

**Prepare:** Football, flag belts, cones, paper/whiteboard for play cards. Write a rules quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Running in a pack around the ball. Correct: spread out to create passing lanes.
- Rushing the quarterback immediately. Correct: rushers must start from the rush line (often 7 yards back) or wait for a count.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draw plays
*Student:* Draw three plays (one run, two pass) on a play card with routes for each player.

*How to teach:* Draw three plays with routes and a QB.

- **Q:** Why give each receiver a different route?
  **A:** To spread the defense and create open passing options at different depths.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Run plays
*Student:* Walk through, then run the plays at full speed in 3-v-3 or 2-v-2.

*How to teach:* Walk through, then run plays at speed.

- **Q:** Why walk through first?
  **A:** So everyone learns routes and timing before adding speed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Defense
*Student:* Practice person-to-person and zone coverage and compare which stops short passes better.

*How to teach:* Practice person-to-person and zone; compare.

- **Q:** Which defense often stops short passes better and why?
  **A:** Person-to-person often stops short passes because each receiver is closely covered; zone can leave gaps underneath.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game
*Student:* Play a 5-on-5 or small-sided flag football game with rotating quarterbacks.

*How to teach:* Play a small-sided game with rotating QBs.

- **Q:** What is a down?
  **A:** One play; a team gets a set number of downs (often 4) to reach midfield or score.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Self-assess play execution and sportsmanship; take a rules quiz (downs, rushing, scoring).

*How to teach:* Self-assess; give the rules quiz.

- **Q:** How many points is a touchdown?
  **A:** 6 points.

**Assessment:** Play cards and rules quiz.

**Answer key:** Rules quiz key (typical recreational flag football): 1) touchdown = 6 2) extra point: 1 point from 5 yards, 2 points from 10–12 yards (league-dependent) 3) safety = 2 4) 4 downs to cross midfield, 4 more to score 5) no contact, no tackling 6) flag guarding is a penalty 7) rusher must start 7 yards from the line of scrimmage 8) ball is dead where the flag is pulled. Play cards: 3 plays with routes drawn and labeled.

## Health Education 8: Energy balance and teen nutrient needs

**Prepare:** Open myplate.gov 'MyPlate Plan' and preview the calculator with the child's age, sex, height, weight and activity. Collect 3–4 nutrition labels. Write a 10-question quiz from the key.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking teens should eat as little as possible to stay healthy. Correct: adolescents need more energy and nutrients (especially calcium and iron) to fuel growth.
- Believing all calories are the same nutritionally. Correct: energy balance is about calories, but nutrient density determines what the body gets for those calories.

### Day 1 (Monday): Energy balance
*Student:* Learn how calories in and out relate to growth; calculate a sample MyPlate Plan for your age and activity.

*How to teach:* Explain energy in (food) vs. energy out (growth, basal metabolism, activity). Enter the child's data in the MyPlate Plan tool and record daily food-group targets.

- **Q:** What is energy balance?
  **A:** When calories eaten equal calories used; during adolescence a positive balance is needed to support growth.
- **Q:** Roughly how many calories does an active 13–14-year-old need?
  **A:** About 2,000–2,600 for girls and 2,200–3,200 for boys depending on activity (follow the MyPlate Plan result).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Key nutrients
*Student:* Research calcium, iron and vitamin D needs during adolescence and list three food sources each.

*How to teach:* The child researches calcium, iron and vitamin D and makes a chart of why each matters and three sources.

- **Q:** How much calcium do 9–18-year-olds need, and why?
  **A:** 1,300 mg per day; teens build most of their peak bone mass now. Sources: milk, yogurt, cheese, fortified soy milk, tofu made with calcium, kale.
- **Q:** Give one source each of iron and vitamin D.
  **A:** Iron: lean red meat, beans, lentils, spinach, fortified cereal. Vitamin D: sunlight, fortified milk, salmon, eggs.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Food log
*Student:* Log one day of meals and compare with your MyPlate Plan.

*How to teach:* The child logs everything eaten for one day and compares food-group amounts with the MyPlate Plan targets.

- **Q:** Which food groups did you fall short or go over?
  **A:** Answers vary; a good answer compares cup/ounce amounts to the targets for all five groups.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fix it
*Student:* Identify one nutrient gap and plan three snacks to fill it.

*How to teach:* Choose the biggest gap and plan three snacks to fill it; check that each snack actually contains the nutrient.

- **Q:** Plan a snack to raise calcium.
  **A:** Examples: yogurt with fruit, cheese and whole-grain crackers, a glass of milk or fortified soy milk with a banana.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a quiz on energy balance and teen nutrient needs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; discuss any misses.

- **Q:** What happens if energy intake is consistently too low for an active teen?
  **A:** Fatigue, poor performance, slowed growth, weaker bones, possible missed periods and higher injury risk.

**Assessment:** Food log analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Food log (4 pts): full day recorded, compared to all 5 groups, gap identified, 3 snacks that address it. Quiz key: 1) energy balance = calories in vs out 2) teens need more energy for growth 3) calcium 1,300 mg/day 4) calcium sources: milk, yogurt, cheese 5) iron carries oxygen in hemoglobin 6) iron sources: meat, beans, spinach, fortified cereal 7) vitamin D helps absorb calcium 8) MyPlate groups: fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy 9) half of grains should be whole 10) too little energy: fatigue, slowed growth.
