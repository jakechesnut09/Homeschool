# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 11

## English Language Arts 8: Rhetoric: Patrick Henry's 'Give Me Liberty'

**Prepare:** Print Patrick Henry's 'Speech to the Second Virginia Convention' (March 23, 1775). Collect 6 ads (print or online).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any emotional word is pathos and any number is logos. Ask what the appeal makes the audience feel, think or trust.
- Confusing mood with tone. Grammatical mood is the verb form (statement, command, question, condition, wish).

### Day 1 (Monday): Rhetorical appeals
*Student:* Learn ethos, pathos and logos and label the dominant appeal in 6 modern advertisements with a sentence of explanation each.

*How to teach:* Teach ethos, pathos, logos. Child labels 6 ads with an explanation each.

- **Q:** A dentist in a white coat recommends a toothpaste. Which appeal?
  **A:** Ethos — trust in the expert.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read the speech
*Student:* Read Henry's speech aloud and annotate rhetorical questions, repetition and each appeal in the margins.

*How to teach:* Read the speech aloud; annotate rhetorical questions, repetition and appeals. To check, ask: "Give a rhetorical question from the speech." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Give a rhetorical question from the speech.
  **A:** 'Is life so dear, or peace so sweet, as to be purchased at the price of chains and slavery?'
- **Q:** How does Henry use ethos in the opening?
  **A:** He respects the patriotism and abilities of those who disagree, which makes him seem fair and trustworthy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Evaluating the argument
*Student:* Delineate Henry's central claim, supporting claims and evidence in a chart and judge whether the reasoning is sound and sufficient.

*How to teach:* Chart claim, supporting claims, evidence; judge soundness. Finish by asking: "What is Henry's central claim?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What is Henry's central claim?
  **A:** The colonies must fight Britain now — 'give me liberty or give me death.'
- **Q:** What evidence does he use?
  **A:** British fleets and armies arriving, ten years of failed petitions, and 'the lamp of experience.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: verb moods
*Student:* Learn the indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional and subjunctive moods; label 10 sentences from the speech by mood.

*How to teach:* Teach the five moods; child labels 10 sentences. Before moving on, ask: "Mood of 'Give me liberty, or give me death!'?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Mood of 'Give me liberty, or give me death!'?
  **A:** Imperative.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on rhetorical appeals and moods; deliver the speech's final paragraph aloud with deliberate emphasis.

*How to teach:* Quiz; child delivers the last paragraph aloud. Check understanding with: "Which appeal dominates the final line?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Which appeal dominates the final line?
  **A:** Pathos.

**Assessment:** Appeals and moods quiz; oral delivery.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) ethos = credibility; pathos = emotion; logos = logic/evidence. 2) 'Is life so dear, or peace so sweet, as to be purchased at the price of chains and slavery?' → rhetorical question, pathos. 3) 'I have but one lamp by which my feet are guided, and that is the lamp of experience' → logos (reasoning from past experience). 4) Label moods: 'Give me liberty' imperative; 'The war is actually begun' indicative; 'Shall we gather strength by irresolution and inaction?' interrogative; 'If I were a delegate, I would vote for war' subjunctive + conditional. Oral delivery rubric: volume, pacing, emphasis on key words, eye contact.

## Mathematics 8: Solutions to linear equations in two variables

**Prepare:** Graph paper, Desmos. Prepare the Unit 3 review stations and test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only one point satisfies an equation like 2x + 3y = 12. Every point on its line is a solution.
- Dividing only some terms when solving for y. Divide every term by the coefficient of y.

### Day 1 (Monday): Points on a line
*Student:* Test whether 8 ordered pairs are solutions of a given equation and explain the connection to points on its graph.

*How to teach:* Give a line equation; child substitutes 8 ordered pairs and checks if each makes it true, then plots them to see true ones fall on the line. Check understanding with: "Is (3, 1) a solution of 2x + 3y = 9? Is (0, 4)?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Is (3, 1) a solution of 2x + 3y = 9? Is (0, 4)?
  **A:** (3, 1): 6 + 3 = 9, yes. (0, 4): 0 + 12 = 12 ≠ 9, no.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Coordinates and constraints
*Student:* Model a budget like $2 apples and $3 pears with $12 by writing 2x + 3y = 12; graph using intercepts.

*How to teach:* Set up the budget story: apples $2, pears $3, $12 total → 2x + 3y = 12. Find intercepts by setting x = 0 then y = 0; graph.

- **Q:** Find the intercepts of 2x + 3y = 12 and explain them.
  **A:** (6, 0): 6 apples, no pears; (0, 4): 4 pears, no apples.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rearranging equations
*Student:* Rewrite 6 equations into slope-intercept form and interpret the slope in context.

*How to teach:* Model solving for y step by step, then the child rewrites 6 equations and interprets slope. Finish by asking: "Rewrite 2x + 3y = 12 in slope-intercept form." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Rewrite 2x + 3y = 12 in slope-intercept form.
  **A:** 3y = −2x + 12, so y = −(2/3)x + 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 3 review
*Student:* Complete a review stations set: proportional vs nonproportional, slope, intercepts, equations from stories.

*How to teach:* Set up stations (proportional vs nonproportional, slope, intercepts, equations from stories); child rotates and you check each. Before moving on, ask: "Rewrite 4x − 2y = 10 in slope-intercept form." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Rewrite 4x − 2y = 10 in slope-intercept form.
  **A:** −2y = −4x + 10, so y = 2x − 5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Unit test on linear relationships.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test. Check understanding with: "What does a point on the graph of 2x + 3y = 12 represent?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What does a point on the graph of 2x + 3y = 12 represent?
  **A:** A combination of apples and pears that costs exactly $12.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test suggested items: 1) Is (3, 1) a solution of 2x + 3y = 9? Yes. 2) Intercepts of 2x + 3y = 12 → (6, 0) and (0, 4). 3) Rewrite 4x − 2y = 10 in slope-intercept form → y = 2x − 5. 4) Proportional or not: y = 7x (yes), y = 7x + 1 (no). 5) Slope through (0, −1) and (2, 5) → 3. 6) Story: $3 delivery fee + $2 per item → y = 2x + 3. 7) Interpret slope −15 in y = −15x + 300 (gallons, minutes) → drains 15 gallons per minute. 1–2 points each.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Moon phases and eclipses

**Prepare:** A lamp without a shade, a foam ball on a pencil, a dark room, a moon observation chart. Check tonight's moon phase (e.g., timeanddate.com).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Earth's shadow causes moon phases. Phases come from how much of the sunlit half we see; Earth's shadow only matters in lunar eclipses.
- Thinking eclipses should happen every month. The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5°, so the Sun, Earth and Moon line up only at certain times.

### Day 1 (Monday): Moon journal
*Student:* Begin a 14-day moon observation log (sketch shape and time nightly); research tonight's phase.

*How to teach:* Start the 14-day moon log tonight; research tonight's phase. Check understanding with: "Where should you record each observation?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Where should you record each observation?
  **A:** Date, time, shape sketch, and position in the sky.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Modeling phases
*Student:* In a dark room, hold a ball on a pencil at arm's length around a lamp to model all 8 phases; sketch each.

*How to teach:* In a dark room, hold the ball at arm's length and turn in a circle around the lamp; sketch the lit part at 8 positions. To check, ask: "When the ball is between you and the lamp, what phase do you see?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** When the ball is between you and the lamp, what phase do you see?
  **A:** New moon — the lit side faces away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Phase sequence
*Student:* Order phase photos and explain why we always see the same side of the Moon.

*How to teach:* Order phase photos; explain why we always see the same side. Finish by asking: "Why do we always see the same side of the Moon?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why do we always see the same side of the Moon?
  **A:** It rotates once for each orbit (about 27 days), so the same face points toward Earth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Eclipses
*Student:* Use the lamp model to show solar and lunar eclipses; explain why eclipses do not happen every month (tilted orbit).

*How to teach:* Use the lamp model to show solar and lunar eclipses. Before moving on, ask: "Why don't eclipses happen every month?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Why don't eclipses happen every month?
  **A:** The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5° to Earth's orbit, so it usually passes above or below the Sun-Earth line.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz with diagrams of phases and eclipses; update moon log.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; update the moon log. Check understanding with: "During which phase can a solar eclipse occur?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** During which phase can a solar eclipse occur?
  **A:** New moon.

**Assessment:** Phase model sketches and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: phases in order: new, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full, waning gibbous, third (last) quarter, waning crescent; cycle ≈ 29.5 days; solar eclipse: Moon between Sun and Earth at new moon; lunar eclipse: Earth between Sun and Moon at full moon; umbra = darkest part of a shadow. Sketch rubric: 8 phases correctly shaded and labeled.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: The War of 1812 and the Monroe Doctrine

**Prepare:** Text of 'The Star-Spangled Banner' (all four stanzas); War of 1812 map; Monroe Doctrine key passage (1823).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Battle of New Orleans decided the war. It was fought on January 8, 1815, after the Treaty of Ghent was signed (December 24, 1814), though it boosted nationalism and Jackson's fame.
- Thinking the Monroe Doctrine was enforced by U.S. power in 1823. It was a statement of policy; Britain's navy effectively backed it at first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Causes
*Student:* Read about impressment and Tecumseh's confederacy; chart causes of the war.

*How to teach:* Read about impressment and Tecumseh; chart causes.

- **Q:** What was impressment?
  **A:** The British practice of seizing sailors from American ships and forcing them into the Royal Navy.
- **Q:** What was Tecumseh's goal?
  **A:** To unite Native nations in a confederacy to resist American expansion and land cessions; he allied with Britain.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Major battles
*Student:* Map major battles (Washington burned, Fort McHenry, New Orleans).

*How to teach:* Map major battles.

- **Q:** What happened to Washington, D.C., in August 1814?
  **A:** British troops burned the Capitol and the White House.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Star-Spangled Banner
*Student:* Read Francis Scott Key's full poem and explain its context.

*How to teach:* Read Key's full poem; explain its context.

- **Q:** What event inspired 'The Star-Spangled Banner'?
  **A:** The British bombardment of Fort McHenry in Baltimore Harbor in September 1814; Key saw the flag still flying at dawn.
- **Q:** When did it become the national anthem?
  **A:** 1931.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Monroe Doctrine
*Student:* Read the key passage of the Monroe Doctrine and explain its message to Europe.

*How to teach:* Read the Monroe Doctrine's key passage.

- **Q:** What was the Monroe Doctrine's message to Europe?
  **A:** The Americas are closed to new European colonization; European interference would be seen as hostile; the U.S. would not interfere in Europe.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the War of 1812 and foreign policy.

*How to teach:* Give the foreign policy quiz.

- **Q:** What did the Treaty of Ghent do?
  **A:** Ended the War of 1812 and restored prewar boundaries (status quo).

**Assessment:** Foreign policy quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: causes — British impressment of American sailors, trade restrictions and seizure of ships, British support for Native resistance (Tecumseh's confederacy), War Hawks (Henry Clay, John C. Calhoun) wanting expansion and national honor; battles — British burned Washington, D.C. (August 1814); Fort McHenry withstood bombardment in Baltimore (September 1814), inspiring Francis Scott Key; Battle of New Orleans (January 1815) won by Andrew Jackson; Treaty of Ghent restored prewar borders; results — nationalism, end of Native confederacy resistance in the Old Northwest (Tecumseh killed 1813 at the Thames), Era of Good Feelings. Monroe Doctrine: Western Hemisphere closed to new European colonization and interference; U.S. would stay out of European affairs.

## Spanish 1B: Stem-changing e→ie verbs: pensar, querer, preferir

**Prepare:** Conjuguemos e→ie practice.

**Watch for:**
- Changing nosotros: 'queremos' (correct), not 'quieremos.'
- Confusing pensar + infinitive (plan to) with pensar en (think about).

### Day 1 (Monday): e→ie
*Student:* Learn pensar, querer, preferir, empezar, entender, cerrar in the boot pattern and complete a Conjuguemos drill.

*How to teach:* Teach e→ie verbs in the boot; Conjuguemos drill.

- **Q:** Conjugate querer for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** quiero; queremos.
- **Q:** Conjugate empezar for la clase.
  **A:** empieza.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Preferences
*Student:* Write 10 sentences comparing clothing choices: Prefiero la camisa roja, pero mi hermano quiere la azul.

*How to teach:* Write 10 preference sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'I prefer the red shirt, but my brother wants the blue one.'
  **A:** Prefiero la camisa roja, pero mi hermano quiere la azul.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pensar + inf
*Student:* Use pensar + infinitive to describe three upcoming plans and compare with ir a + infinitive.

*How to teach:* Use pensar + infinitive for three plans; compare with ir a.

- **Q:** Write: 'I plan to buy new shoes.'
  **A:** Pienso comprar zapatos nuevos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversation
*Student:* Discuss with a parent what clothes you want, prefer, and plan to buy for the next season.

*How to teach:* Discuss clothes you want, prefer and plan to buy.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What do you prefer, the jacket or the sweater?'
  **A:** ¿Qué prefieres, la chaqueta o el suéter?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an e→ie quiz with mixed o→ue items.

*How to teach:* Give the e→ie quiz with mixed o→ue items.

- **Q:** Translate: 'They don't understand the question.'
  **A:** Ellos no entienden la pregunta.

**Assessment:** Stem-changer quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: pensar — pienso, piensas, piensa, pensamos, pensáis, piensan; querer — quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, queréis, quieren; preferir — prefiero, prefieres, prefiere, preferimos, preferís, prefieren; empezar — empiezo…; entender — entiendo…; cerrar — cierro… Mixed o→ue: puedo, duermes, vuelven. Samples: La tienda (cerrar) a las nueve → cierra; Nosotros (preferir) la camisa azul → preferimos; ¿Tú (entender)? → entiendes. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Pitch reading on the staff

**Prepare:** musictheory.net note-identification exercise; keyboard app or piano. Write a quiz: 10 notes (5 treble, 5 bass) and 5 intervals.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up treble and bass clef names. Correct: treble lines E-G-B-D-F, spaces F-A-C-E; bass lines G-B-D-F-A, spaces A-C-E-G.
- Counting intervals from 0. Correct: count the starting note as 1 (C to E = third).

### Day 1 (Monday): Treble clef
*Student:* Learn line and space names in treble clef and reach 20 correct in a row on the musictheory.net note trainer.

*How to teach:* Learn treble names; 20 correct in a row.

- **Q:** Name the treble clef lines and spaces.
  **A:** Lines E-G-B-D-F ('Every Good Boy Does Fine'); spaces F-A-C-E.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bass clef
*Student:* Learn bass clef names and repeat the note-trainer goal.

*How to teach:* Learn bass clef names.

- **Q:** Name the bass clef lines and spaces.
  **A:** Lines G-B-D-F-A ('Good Boys Do Fine Always'); spaces A-C-E-G ('All Cows Eat Grass').

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Accidentals
*Student:* Learn sharps, flats and naturals and find them on a keyboard diagram.

*How to teach:* Sharps, flats, naturals on a keyboard.

- **Q:** What does a sharp do?
  **A:** Raises a note a half step (to the next key to the right).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Intervals
*Student:* Identify seconds through octaves by sight and by ear using 'Here Comes the Bride' (4th) and 'Twinkle' (5th) references.

*How to teach:* Identify intervals by sight and ear.

- **Q:** What interval opens 'Twinkle, Twinkle' (first to third note)?
  **A:** A perfect fifth (C up to G).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Note-naming and interval quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** C up to F is what interval?
  **A:** A fourth (C-D-E-F = 4).

**Assessment:** Note-naming and interval quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: score against your chosen notes using EGBDF/FACE (treble) and GBDFA/ACEG (bass); interval items counted inclusively (e.g., C–E 3rd, C–G 5th, C–C octave, D–F 3rd, E–A 4th). 12/15 proficient.

## Physical Education 8: Tennis: forehand, backhand and serve

**Prepare:** Tennis racket (junior 25–26 inch if needed), 6+ balls, a wall or court, chalk. Phone for Friday video.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging only with the arm. Correct: turn shoulders early, step in, and rotate through the ball.
- Hitting late beside or behind the body. Correct: contact slightly in front of the front hip.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grip and ready
*Student:* Learn the eastern forehand grip and ready position; rally against a wall for 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Eastern forehand grip ('shake hands'); ready position. Rally against a wall 10 minutes.

- **Q:** Describe the ready position.
  **A:** Feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on balls of feet, racket in front with both hands.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forehand
*Student:* Practice drop-and-hit forehands, focusing on low-to-high swing for topspin.

*How to teach:* Drop-and-hit forehands with low-to-high swing.

- **Q:** Why swing low to high?
  **A:** It brushes up the back of the ball, creating topspin that brings it down into the court.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Backhand
*Student:* Practice the two-hand backhand from a partner toss.

*How to teach:* Two-hand backhand from tosses.

- **Q:** Name one advantage of a two-handed backhand.
  **A:** More stability and power for players with less wrist strength.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Serve
*Student:* Practice the overhand serve with the 'trophy' position into a chalked target box.

*How to teach:* Overhand serve with trophy position into a target box.

- **Q:** What is the trophy position?
  **A:** Tossing arm up, racket arm bent behind with elbow up, knees bent — like a trophy figure, ready to swing up.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Self-video a forehand and compare it to a skill checklist (turn, contact point, follow-through).

*How to teach:* Self-video a forehand and compare to the checklist.

- **Q:** Where should the contact point be?
  **A:** In front of the body, about waist height, beside the front hip.

**Assessment:** Forehand checklist from video.

**Answer key:** Forehand checklist (1 pt each): ready position; early unit turn; eastern grip; step with front foot; contact in front; low-to-high swing; follow-through over opposite shoulder; recovery to ready. 6+/8 proficient.

## Health Education 8: Introduction: sexual health, values and trusted adults

**Prepare:** Parental preview: read the unit plan for weeks 11–18 and KidsHealth for Teens sexual health pages. Decide whether to opt out under Ed. Code 51938; if so, use the alternative (puberty, hygiene, healthy friendships). Have a labeled reproductive anatomy diagram from grade 7.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking media portrayals of relationships are realistic. Correct: TV and music often skip consequences, consent and communication.
- Believing any website that looks professional is accurate. Correct: check who runs it, evidence, date and purpose (.gov, .edu, medical organizations are more reliable).

### Day 1 (Monday): Family preview
*Student:* Parent and student preview the unit, discuss family values and decide on any opt-out. (Sexual health lesson: parents may opt out under CA Ed. Code 51938; parent previews materials.)

*How to teach:* Sit together and preview topics. Share your family's values and invite questions; record the opt-out decision.

- **Q:** Who can you ask when you have questions about growing up and relationships?
  **A:** Parents/guardians, a doctor or nurse, the school counselor, or another trusted adult.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Grade 7 recap
*Student:* Review puberty, hormones and reproductive anatomy from grade 7 with a labeling warm-up.

*How to teach:* Label a male and female reproductive-system diagram and review the role of hormones in puberty.

- **Q:** Which hormones drive puberty?
  **A:** Testosterone (mainly in males) and estrogen and progesterone (mainly in females), triggered by signals from the pituitary gland (LH and FSH).
- **Q:** Where are eggs produced and where is sperm produced?
  **A:** Eggs mature in the ovaries; sperm are produced in the testes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Influences
*Student:* Analyze how TV, music and social media portray relationships and sexuality versus reality.

*How to teach:* Pick a TV scene, song or post and compare what it shows with reality (consent, communication, consequences, respect).

- **Q:** What do media portrayals of relationships often leave out?
  **A:** Communication, consent, respect, emotional and physical consequences (pregnancy, STIs, heartbreak) and responsibilities.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Valid sources
*Student:* Compare one reliable (e.g., KidsHealth) and one unreliable website on a sexual health question.

*How to teach:* Compare one reliable site (KidsHealth, CDC) with one unreliable site (anonymous forum) on one question using who/what/when/why.

- **Q:** Give two signs a health website is reliable.
  **A:** Run by a medical, government or educational organization; cites evidence; recently updated; authors have credentials; not selling products.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* List three trusted adults and two valid resources; write a question box question for a parent.

*How to teach:* The child lists three trusted adults and two valid resources, then writes a question for the parent question box.

- **Q:** Why is it helpful to have more than one trusted adult?
  **A:** If one is unavailable or the topic feels awkward, another can help.

**Assessment:** Trusted-adult/resource list.

**Answer key:** Trusted-adult/resource list: 4 = three named trusted adults with roles/contact info, two valid resources (e.g., KidsHealth, CDC, doctor's office) with why they're reliable, and one question submitted; 3 = one missing element; 2 = vague names, unreliable sources; 1 = incomplete. Opted-out students: complete a list of trusted adults and resources for general health questions.
