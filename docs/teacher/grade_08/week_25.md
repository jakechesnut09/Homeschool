# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 25

## English Language Arts 8: The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 2, Scenes 1–3: dramatic irony

**Prepare:** Read Act 2, Scenes 1–3. Parental preview: hunger and quarrels intensify; references to arrests and deportations.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing dramatic irony with suspense. Dramatic irony is the gap between what the audience knows and what characters know; it creates suspense or dread.
- Seeing Mr. Van Daan's theft as only greed. Discuss how fear and starvation test everyone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Act 2, Scene 1
*Student:* Read Scene 1 and discuss Anne's growth and her relationship with Peter.

*How to teach:* Read Act 2, Scene 1 with assigned parts. Ask how Anne's voice in her diary reading has changed since Act 1 and how her relationship with Peter is shifting.

- **Q:** How has Anne changed since Act 1?
  **A:** She is more thoughtful and mature, reflecting on growing up and her feelings, and becoming closer to Peter.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Scene 2
*Student:* Read Scene 2 and analyze dramatic irony—what the audience knows about the Annex's fate that characters do not.

*How to teach:* Read Scene 2. Remind the child that Act 1, Scene 1 told us Mr. Frank returned alone; list moments where characters' hopes are painful because the audience knows the outcome.

- **Q:** What does the audience know that characters don't?
  **A:** That they will be discovered and only Mr. Frank will survive — so every plan for 'after the war' feels heartbreaking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scene 3
*Student:* Read Scene 3 (the stolen bread) and analyze how scarcity tests the characters.

*How to teach:* Read Scene 3 (the stolen bread). Discuss how hunger and fear push the characters to conflict and how the invasion news changes the mood.

- **Q:** What does Mr. Van Daan do and how does Mrs. Frank react?
  **A:** He steals bread at night; she demands that he leave the Annex.
- **Q:** What news changes the mood?
  **A:** Miep brings news of the Allied invasion of Normandy (D-Day).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Irony paragraph
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how dramatic irony makes the audience feel suspense or dread, with two examples.

*How to teach:* Review the claim-evidence-commentary pattern. The child writes the irony paragraph with two examples and explains the dread each creates.

- **Q:** Example of dramatic irony?
  **A:** Characters celebrate the invasion news believing liberation is near.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Act 2, Scenes 1–3.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on Act 2, Scenes 1–3 and collect the irony paragraph. Finish by asking: "What does Kraler report about a worker?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What does Kraler report about a worker?
  **A:** A worker is suspicious and asking for more money — a threat of blackmail.

**Assessment:** Irony paragraph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Irony paragraph rubric (4 levels): explains dramatic irony (the audience knows from Act 1, Scene 1 that only Mr. Frank survives), gives two examples (e.g., hope at news of the invasion; Anne's plans for the future; Kraler's report of a suspicious worker), and explains the feeling it creates (dread). Quiz: Scene 1 — New Year's 1944, Miep and Mr. Kraler visit with a cake, Kraler says a worker (Carl) is asking suspicious questions and wants more money; Scene 2 — Anne and Peter grow close; Scene 3 — Mr. Van Daan is caught stealing bread, Mrs. Frank demands he leave, then news of the D-Day invasion brings joy.

## Mathematics 8: Exponent rules for products, quotients and powers

**Prepare:** Several sheets of paper for folding; notebook.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying the bases: 2³ × 2⁴ = 4⁷. The base stays the same; only exponents add.
- Adding exponents for a power of a power: (5²)³ = 5⁵. Expand to see it is 5⁶.

### Day 1 (Monday): Paper folding
*Student:* Fold a sheet of paper in half repeatedly, record layers as powers of 2, and predict layers after 10 folds.

*How to teach:* Fold a sheet in half repeatedly, recording layers: 2, 4, 8 ... as powers of 2. Finish by asking: "How many layers after 10 folds?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** How many layers after 10 folds?
  **A:** 2¹⁰ = 1,024.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiplying powers
*Student:* Expand expressions like 10^3 x 10^4 to discover a^m x a^n = a^(m+n); practice 12 problems.

*How to teach:* Expand 10³ × 10⁴ into factors and count. Child states the rule and practices 12.

- **Q:** Simplify 2⁵ × 2³ and evaluate.
  **A:** 2⁸ = 256.
- **Q:** Why don't you multiply the exponents in a^m × a^n?
  **A:** Writing out the factors shows m factors of a followed by n more, for m + n factors in total.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Powers of powers
*Student:* Expand (5^2)^3 to discover (a^m)^n = a^(mn); mix with products.

*How to teach:* Expand (5²)³ as 5² · 5² · 5² to see 5⁶. Check understanding with: "Simplify (5²)³." If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Simplify (5²)³.
  **A:** 5⁶ = 15,625.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dividing powers
*Student:* Expand 7^6 / 7^2 to discover a^m / a^n = a^(m-n); solve 12 mixed problems.

*How to teach:* Expand 7⁶ / 7² and cancel to see 7⁴. Child solves 12 mixed problems.

- **Q:** Simplify 7⁶ ÷ 7².
  **A:** 7⁴ = 2,401.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: simplify 12 expressions using exponent rules and explain one rule with expanded form.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Simplify (4²)³ ÷ 4⁵.
  **A:** 4⁶ ÷ 4⁵ = 4.

**Assessment:** Quiz on exponent rules.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 10³ × 10⁴ = 10⁷. 2) 2⁵ × 2³ = 2⁸ = 256. 3) (5²)³ = 5⁶. 4) 7⁶ ÷ 7² = 7⁴. 5) x³ · x⁵ = x⁸. 6) (x⁴)² = x⁸. 7) 3⁷ ÷ 3⁴ = 3³ = 27. 8) (2³)² = 2⁶ = 64. 9) a⁹ ÷ a = a⁸. 10) 10² × 10⁵ ÷ 10³ = 10⁴. 11) (4²)³ ÷ 4⁵ = 4. 12) 6³ × 6 = 6⁴. Explanation: e.g., 10³ × 10⁴ = (10·10·10)(10·10·10·10) = 7 tens multiplied.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Asexual and sexual reproduction

**Prepare:** A potato with eyes or an onion, a pothos (or other houseplant) cutting, a glass of water, index cards for the trait model.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking asexual offspring are always identical forever. They are clones of the parent except for rare mutations.
- Thinking sexual reproduction is always 'better.' Each strategy has advantages depending on the environment.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plant cuttings
*Student:* Start a pothos cutting in water and a sprouting potato eye; predict traits of offspring.

*How to teach:* Start the pothos cutting and potato eye; predict traits of the offspring. Finish by asking: "Will the new pothos look like the parent?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Will the new pothos look like the parent?
  **A:** Yes — it is a clone (same genes).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparing reproduction
*Student:* Compare budding, binary fission and fragmentation with sexual reproduction using a Venn diagram.

*How to teach:* Venn diagram: budding, binary fission, fragmentation vs sexual reproduction. Before moving on, ask: "Example of binary fission?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Example of binary fission?
  **A:** Bacteria splitting in two.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Variation modeling
*Student:* Model gametes by randomly drawing one of two cards per trait from each 'parent'; compare 5 offspring.

*How to teach:* Model gametes by drawing one card per trait from each parent. Check understanding with: "Why are offspring different?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Why are offspring different?
  **A:** Each gets a random mix of genes from two parents.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pros and cons
*Student:* Write a short argument on the advantages of each reproduction type in changing environments.

*How to teach:* Write an argument on advantages of each in changing environments. To check, ask: "Advantage of sexual reproduction?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Advantage of sexual reproduction?
  **A:** Variation that can help some offspring survive change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on reproduction and variation.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Finish by asking: "Define gamete." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Define gamete.
  **A:** A sex cell (egg or sperm) with half the chromosomes.

**Assessment:** Variation model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Variation model: offspring receive one card per trait from each parent at random; 5 offspring show different combinations. Quiz: asexual = one parent, offspring genetically identical (budding, binary fission, fragmentation, cuttings); sexual = two parents, gametes combine, offspring vary; variation helps a population survive environmental change; asexual is fast and needs no mate.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Emancipation and Black soldiers

**Prepare:** Emancipation Proclamation transcript; USCT photographs (Library of Congress); Corporal James Henry Gooding's letter to Lincoln (September 1863); Douglass's 'Men of Color, To Arms!' (March 1863); biographies of Grant, Lee and Davis; Union and Confederate soldier letters.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Emancipation Proclamation freed all enslaved people. It applied only to areas in rebellion; it did not cover the loyal border states or Union-held areas; the 13th Amendment (1865) ended slavery nationwide.
- Thinking Black soldiers only appeared late and in small numbers. About 180,000 served in the U.S. Colored Troops — roughly 10% of the Union Army — plus thousands in the Navy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Emancipation Proclamation
*Student:* Read the full Proclamation and determine exactly which enslaved people it freed and why Lincoln framed it as a war measure.

*How to teach:* Read the Proclamation; determine exactly who it freed and why it was a war measure.

- **Q:** Which enslaved people did the Emancipation Proclamation free?
  **A:** Those in areas still in rebellion against the United States on January 1, 1863 — not those in the loyal border states or Union-controlled areas.
- **Q:** Why did Lincoln frame it as a war measure?
  **A:** As commander in chief he could seize enemy resources during war; this gave it a constitutional basis and kept border states loyal.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Black soldiers
*Student:* Read Corporal James Henry Gooding's letter to Lincoln on equal pay and Frederick Douglass's 'Men of Color, To Arms!'

*How to teach:* Read Gooding's letter and Douglass's 'Men of Color, To Arms!'

- **Q:** What did Corporal Gooding ask Lincoln?
  **A:** For equal pay — Black soldiers were paid $10 (minus $3 for clothing) while white privates received $13.
- **Q:** Why did Douglass urge Black men to enlist?
  **A:** Fighting would help end slavery and earn a claim to citizenship and equal rights.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Leaders
*Student:* Compare short biographies of Grant, Lee and Jefferson Davis on a character chart.

*How to teach:* Compare Grant, Lee and Davis on a character chart.

- **Q:** Give one strength of Grant as a general.
  **A:** Persistence and aggressiveness — he kept attacking and coordinated a war of attrition using Union resources.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Soldier life
*Student:* Read letters and diaries from a Union and a Confederate soldier and compare their reasons for fighting.

*How to teach:* Compare a Union and a Confederate soldier's reasons for fighting.

- **Q:** Name a common reason Union soldiers gave for fighting.
  **A:** To preserve the Union (and, for many, to end slavery).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on emancipation and soldiers' experiences.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** About how many Black soldiers served in the U.S. Colored Troops?
  **A:** About 180,000.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Emancipation Proclamation (issued January 1, 1863) — freed enslaved people in Confederate-held areas, as a military measure under the president's war powers; exempted border states and Union-occupied areas such as Tennessee and parts of Louisiana and Virginia; allowed Black men to enlist; changed war aims to include ending slavery and discouraged Britain and France from aiding the Confederacy; 'contraband' — Union term for people who escaped to Union lines; 54th Massachusetts — famous Black regiment, assault on Fort Wagner (July 1863); Gooding protested being paid $10 a month (minus $3 for clothing) versus $13 for white privates — Congress equalized pay in 1864; Grant — persistent Union general who won at Vicksburg; Lee — Confederate commander of the Army of Northern Virginia; Davis — Confederate president. Soldier comparison: Union soldiers often cited preserving the Union (and increasingly ending slavery); Confederates often cited defending home, independence and slavery.

## Spanish 1B: Indirect object pronouns and the verb dar

**Prepare:** Conjuguemos indirect object pronouns and dar.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving out 'le' when the person is named. Spanish usually includes both: Le di el libro a Juan.
- Confusing direct and indirect. Indirect answers 'to/for whom' (le, les); direct answers 'what' (lo, la).

### Day 1 (Monday): IOPs
*Student:* Learn indirect object pronouns and identify the indirect object in 15 sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach IOPs; identify the indirect object in 15 sentences.

- **Q:** In 'Mi mamá me compra una camisa,' what is the indirect object?
  **A:** me (for me).
- **Q:** Which IOP replaces 'to my friends'?
  **A:** les.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dar
*Student:* Learn present dar (doy, das...) and preterite dar (di, diste, dio) and complete a drill.

*How to teach:* Teach dar present and preterite; drill.

- **Q:** Conjugate dar (present) for yo.
  **A:** doy.
- **Q:** Conjugate dar (preterite) for ella and ellos.
  **A:** dio; dieron.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Le/les clarification
*Student:* Practice clarifying le/les with a + person: Le di la ropa a mi abuela.

*How to teach:* Clarify le/les with a + person.

- **Q:** Write: 'I gave the clothes to my grandmother.'
  **A:** Le di la ropa a mi abuela.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gifts and help
*Student:* Write 10 sentences about what you gave or did for others using IOPs.

*How to teach:* Write 10 sentences about what you gave or did for others.

- **Q:** Write: 'We gave food to the homeless people.'
  **A:** Les dimos comida a la gente sin hogar.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an indirect object pronoun and dar quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the IOP and dar quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My teacher gave me a book.'
  **A:** Mi profesor(a) me dio un libro.

**Assessment:** IOP and dar quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: IOPs me, te, le, nos, os, les. Dar present: doy, das, da, damos, dais, dan; preterite: di, diste, dio, dimos, disteis, dieron (no accents). Samples: I gave the clothes to my grandma → Le di la ropa a mi abuela; She gave us food → Nos dio comida; They give toys to the children → Les dan juguetes a los niños; Did you give him the book? → ¿Le diste el libro? 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Writing the script

**Prepare:** Script template and word processor. Example script page showing character names, dialogue and italic stage directions in parentheses.

**Watch for:**
- Writing narration in stage directions. Correct: stage directions describe only what can be seen or heard.
- Dialogue that states feelings directly. Correct: let subtext and action reveal feelings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Script format
*Student:* Study script format and write the opening page.

*How to teach:* Study script format; write page one.

- **Q:** How are stage directions formatted?
  **A:** In italics and/or parentheses, in present tense, describing action, setting and sound.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft scene 1
*Student:* Draft the first half with dialogue revealing character.

*How to teach:* Draft the first half.

- **Q:** How does dialogue reveal character?
  **A:** Word choice, rhythm, what characters avoid saying and how they respond show personality and relationships.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft scene 2
*Student:* Draft the climax and resolution.

*How to teach:* Draft climax and resolution.

- **Q:** Does your climax resolve the central conflict?
  **A:** It should show the conflict peaking and lead to a resolution.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Staging problems
*Student:* Identify two staging challenges (e.g., a storm, a crowd) and write creative solutions in stage directions.

*How to teach:* Solve two staging challenges in stage directions.

- **Q:** How might you stage a storm simply?
  **A:** Sound effects, flickering lights, actors swaying, fabric waving.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete the first draft and read it aloud alone, marking lines that sound unnatural.

*How to teach:* Finish draft; read aloud and mark unnatural lines.

- **Q:** Why read your script aloud?
  **A:** To hear lines that sound stiff, too long or unnatural.

**Assessment:** Complete first draft.

**Answer key:** First draft: 4 = correct script format, 5–8 pages, complete arc (exposition to resolution), distinct character voices, creative staging solutions; 3 = minor format issues; 2 = incomplete arc; 1 = unfinished.

## Physical Education 8: Social and partner dance

**Prepare:** Music in 4/4 (swing and salsa songs), heart-rate monitor or watch.

**Watch for:**
- Not counting the beat. Correct: listen for the downbeat and count 1–8.
- Leading by pushing/pulling. Correct: clear, gentle connection through the frame.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rhythm
*Student:* Count beats in different songs and clap the tempo; march to 8-counts.

*How to teach:* Count beats and clap tempo; march 8-counts.

- **Q:** How many beats are in an 8-count phrase?
  **A:** Eight beats (two measures of 4/4).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Swing
*Student:* Learn the East Coast swing basic and an underarm turn.

*How to teach:* East Coast swing basic (triple step, triple step, rock step) and underarm turn.

- **Q:** What are the counts of the East Coast swing basic?
  **A:** 1&2, 3&4, 5-6 (triple, triple, rock step) — or the single-time version: step, step, rock step.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Salsa
*Student:* Learn the salsa basic and cross-body lead.

*How to teach:* Salsa basic (forward on 1-2-3, pause 4, back on 5-6-7, pause 8) and cross-body lead.

- **Q:** On which counts does salsa pause?
  **A:** On 4 and 8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Intensity
*Student:* Dance 15 minutes continuously and measure heart rate to check if it is MVPA.

*How to teach:* Dance 15 minutes; check HR against moderate zone (about 64–76% of max; max ≈ 220 − age).

- **Q:** For a 14-year-old, what HR range is moderate intensity?
  **A:** Max ≈ 206; 64–76% ≈ 132–157 bpm.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a 1-minute partner routine combining steps.

*How to teach:* Perform a 1-minute partner routine.

- **Q:** What makes a partner routine look smooth?
  **A:** Staying on the beat, clear leads and follows, connected transitions.

**Assessment:** Partner routine performance.

**Answer key:** Partner routine: 4 = at least 3 steps/turns combined, on the beat throughout, smooth transitions, respectful partnering, ~1 minute; 3 = minor timing errors; 2 = 1–2 steps only; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Hands-only CPR, AED and choking

**Prepare:** Watch the AHA Hands-Only CPR video (cpr.heart.org) together. Use a firm pillow or manikin on the floor. Pick a song at 100–120 bpm ('Stayin' Alive'). Find an AED demonstration video. Print a CPR skills checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Compressing too gently or too slowly. Correct: push hard (at least 2 inches for adults) and fast (100–120/min), letting the chest recoil fully.
- Fearing you'll hurt someone or get sued. Correct: without CPR the person will likely die; California's Good Samaritan laws protect people giving emergency care in good faith.

### Day 1 (Monday): Check-call-care
*Student:* Review emergency steps and practice a 911 call script.

*How to teach:* Review Check (scene safe, tap and shout), Call (911, get AED), Care. Practice a 911 script: location, what happened, breathing status.

- **Q:** What should you tell the 911 dispatcher?
  **A:** Exact location, what happened, number of victims, whether the person is conscious and breathing; stay on the line and follow instructions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hands-only CPR
*Student:* Practice compressions at 100-120 per minute on a pillow to a song beat.

*How to teach:* Practice hand position (heel of hand on center of chest, other hand on top, arms straight) and compressions to the beat for 2 minutes.

- **Q:** What are the two steps of hands-only CPR?
  **A:** Call 911, then push hard and fast in the center of the chest at 100–120 compressions per minute, at least 2 inches deep for adults.
- **Q:** When can you stop?
  **A:** When EMS or someone trained takes over, an AED is ready, the person starts breathing, the scene becomes unsafe or you're too exhausted to continue.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): AED
*Student:* Watch an AED demonstration and sequence the steps.

*How to teach:* Watch the AED video. The child sequences: turn on, follow voice prompts, bare chest, attach pads, stand clear for analysis, press shock if advised, resume CPR immediately.

- **Q:** What is the first step when an AED arrives?
  **A:** Turn it on and follow the voice prompts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Choking
*Student:* Practice the universal choking sign and abdominal thrust positioning (no real thrusts).

*How to teach:* Teach the universal choking sign (hands at throat). Ask 'Are you choking?' Show hand placement for abdominal thrusts (fist above navel, below ribs) without real thrusts.

- **Q:** If someone can cough forcefully, what should you do?
  **A:** Encourage them to keep coughing; give abdominal thrusts only if they can't cough, speak or breathe.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a CPR scenario walk-through while a parent checks steps.

*How to teach:* Run a scenario: someone collapses. The parent checks each step on the checklist.

- **Q:** Why start compressions right away?
  **A:** Brain damage begins within minutes without oxygen; compressions circulate oxygenated blood until help arrives.

**Assessment:** CPR skills checklist.

**Answer key:** CPR skills checklist (1 pt each): checks scene safety; taps and shouts; calls 911 and sends for AED; correct hand position; rate 100–120/min; depth ~2 inches with full recoil; minimal interruptions; uses AED steps correctly; states choking sign and thrust placement. 8–9 = proficient.
