# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 27

## English Language Arts 8: Remembering: Holocaust nonfiction and research

**Prepare:** Print Elie Wiesel's 1986 Nobel Peace Prize acceptance speech; select two USHMM survivor testimonies. Find two accounts of the Annex's discovery (e.g., Anne Frank House website and a USHMM article). Parental preview: survivor testimony.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking neutrality is fair. Wiesel argues neutrality helps the oppressor.
- Assuming conflicting accounts mean someone lied. Memory, perspective and available evidence differ.

### Day 1 (Monday): Wiesel's Nobel speech
*Student:* Read Elie Wiesel's Nobel speech and analyze his claim about neutrality.

*How to teach:* Give brief background: Wiesel survived Auschwitz and Buchenwald as a teenager and wrote 'Night.' Read the speech together; the child underlines his central claim and explains in writing why he says neutrality helps the oppressor. Check understanding with: "What is Wiesel's claim?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What is Wiesel's claim?
  **A:** 'We must always take sides. Neutrality helps the oppressor, never the victim. Silence encourages the tormentor, never the tormented.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Conflicting accounts
*Student:* Compare two accounts of the Annex's discovery and identify discrepancies.

*How to teach:* The child reads two accounts of the August 4, 1944 arrest and lists details that agree and differ. Discuss why accounts differ (memory, sources, date written).

- **Q:** What remains unknown?
  **A:** Who betrayed the people in the Annex — it has never been proven.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mini research
*Student:* Research one upstander (Miep Gies, Irena Sendler, Chiune Sugihara) using 3 sources.

*How to teach:* The child picks Miep Gies, Irena Sendler or Chiune Sugihara and takes notes from three credible sources (USHMM, Yad Vashem, a library book), recording source and page for each fact. Finish by asking: "What did Miep Gies do?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What did Miep Gies do?
  **A:** Helped hide the Franks and saved Anne's diary, giving it to Otto after the war.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Informative write-up
*Student:* Write a 1-page informative profile with citations.

*How to teach:* The child writes a one-page informative profile: introduction, what the person did, the risks and results, and a conclusion, with in-text citations and a works-cited list. Before moving on, ask: "What is an upstander?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What is an upstander?
  **A:** Someone who acts to help others facing injustice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Unit test with drama and nonfiction items.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test on drama terms, the play and the nonfiction texts. Afterward the child corrects misses using the play text.

- **Q:** Define foil.
  **A:** A character who contrasts with another to highlight traits.

**Assessment:** Research profile and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Research profile rubric (4 levels): accurate facts from 3 cited sources about one upstander — e.g., Miep Gies helped hide the Franks and saved Anne's diary; Irena Sendler smuggled about 2,500 Jewish children out of the Warsaw Ghetto; Chiune Sugihara, Japanese diplomat in Lithuania, issued transit visas in 1940 that saved thousands; organized, in own words, with MLA citations. Unit 6 test: drama terms (stage direction, dramatic irony, foil, epilogue), play events, Wiesel's claim.

## Mathematics 8: Powers of 10 and scientific notation

**Prepare:** Print the NASA planetary fact sheet (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet); calculator; number lines labeled 10⁻⁴ to 10⁸.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 35 × 10⁴ as scientific notation. The coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10: 3.5 × 10⁵.
- Moving the decimal the wrong way for small numbers. Small numbers (less than 1) have negative exponents.

### Day 1 (Monday): Powers of 10 on a number line
*Student:* Place numbers like 3 x 10^5 and 7 x 10^(-3) on number lines labeled with powers of 10.

*How to teach:* Place 3 × 10⁵ and 7 × 10⁻³ on number lines marked with powers of 10. Check understanding with: "Between which powers of 10 is 3 × 10⁵?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Between which powers of 10 is 3 × 10⁵?
  **A:** Between 10⁵ and 10⁶.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Writing scientific notation
*Student:* Convert 12 quantities (distance to the Sun, mass of a dust grain, U.S. population) to and from scientific notation.

*How to teach:* Model moving the decimal and counting places; child converts 12 quantities. To check, ask: "Write 93,000,000 miles (Earth–Sun distance) in scientific notation." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Write 93,000,000 miles (Earth–Sun distance) in scientific notation.
  **A:** 9.3 × 10⁷ miles.
- **Q:** Write 0.00045 in scientific notation.
  **A:** 4.5 × 10⁻⁴.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing magnitudes
*Student:* Use powers of 10 to estimate how many times larger one quantity is (Earth vs Moon mass, a blue whale vs an ant).

*How to teach:* Use powers of 10 to estimate ratios. Finish by asking: "Earth's mass ≈ 6 × 10²⁴ kg, the Moon's ≈ 7 × 10²² kg. About how many times more massive is Earth?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Earth's mass ≈ 6 × 10²⁴ kg, the Moon's ≈ 7 × 10²² kg. About how many times more massive is Earth?
  **A:** 6/7 × 10² ≈ 86, so roughly 80–90 times (actual ≈ 81).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Using a calculator
*Student:* Read calculator 'E' notation and convert planetary data from NASA into scientific notation.

*How to teach:* Show calculator 'E' notation (1.496E11 = 1.496 × 10¹¹). Child converts NASA data.

- **Q:** What does 1.496E11 mean?
  **A:** 1.496 × 10¹¹ — Earth's average distance from the Sun in meters.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: convert, compare and order 10 numbers in scientific notation.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** Order: 3 × 10⁻², 5 × 10⁻⁴, 2 × 10⁻³.
  **A:** 5 × 10⁻⁴ < 2 × 10⁻³ < 3 × 10⁻².

**Assessment:** Quiz on scientific notation.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴. 2) 0.00045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴. 3) 93,000,000 = 9.3 × 10⁷. 4) 6.02 × 10³ = 6,020. 5) 7 × 10⁻³ = 0.007. 6) Larger: 3 × 10⁵ or 9 × 10⁴? 3 × 10⁵. 7) Order: 2 × 10⁻³, 5 × 10⁻⁴, 1 × 10⁻², 3 × 10⁻² → 5 × 10⁻⁴ < 2 × 10⁻³ < 1 × 10⁻² < 3 × 10⁻². 8) How many times larger is 6 × 10⁸ than 2 × 10⁵? 3,000. 9) 1.2E7 = 1.2 × 10⁷. 10) 0.000000052 = 5.2 × 10⁻⁸.

## Science 8 (Integrated): DNA, genes and proteins

**Prepare:** Candy or beads (4 colors), toothpicks; strawberries, zip bag, dish soap, salt, cold rubbing alcohol, coffee filter, clear cup; codon chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking genes are traits. Genes are DNA instructions for proteins; proteins produce traits.
- Pairing A with C. Bases pair A–T and C–G.

### Day 1 (Monday): DNA structure
*Student:* Build a DNA ladder model using candy or beads for bases (A-T, C-G).

*How to teach:* Build a DNA ladder with candy bases. Check understanding with: "Complementary strand of A-T-G-C-C-A?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Complementary strand of A-T-G-C-C-A?
  **A:** T-A-C-G-G-T.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strawberry DNA extraction
*Student:* Mash strawberries with soap and salt, filter, and add cold alcohol to see DNA strands; record observations.

*How to teach:* Extract strawberry DNA. To check, ask: "What does the soap do?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** What does the soap do?
  **A:** Breaks down cell and nuclear membranes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Genes to proteins
*Student:* Use a codon chart to 'translate' a short DNA sequence into amino acids.

*How to teach:* Translate an mRNA sequence with a codon chart. Finish by asking: "Translate AUG-GCA-UUU-UAA." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Translate AUG-GCA-UUU-UAA.
  **A:** Methionine – Alanine – Phenylalanine – Stop.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Proteins and traits
*Student:* Read about how proteins such as melanin and hemoglobin affect traits; write a summary.

*How to teach:* Read about proteins and traits; summarize. Before moving on, ask: "What does hemoglobin do?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What does hemoglobin do?
  **A:** Carries oxygen in red blood cells.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on DNA structure and the gene-protein-trait connection.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check understanding with: "Gene → ? → trait" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Gene → ? → trait
  **A:** Protein.

**Assessment:** Lab report and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: procedure, observations (white stringy DNA at the alcohol layer), explanation (soap breaks membranes, salt clumps DNA, DNA is not soluble in alcohol). Quiz: DNA is a double helix of nucleotides; A–T, C–G; complementary strand of ATGC is TACG; a gene codes for a protein; codon AUG = methionine (start); proteins such as melanin-producing enzymes and hemoglobin affect traits.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: The end of the war

**Prepare:** Map of Sherman's March; Lincoln's Second Inaugural Address (March 4, 1865); accounts of Appomattox (April 9, 1865) including Grant's terms; casualty statistics (traditional estimate about 620,000 deaths; recent research about 750,000). Write the unit 8 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the war ended the moment Lee surrendered. Other Confederate forces surrendered over the following weeks.
- Believing Lincoln planned harsh punishment for the South. His Second Inaugural called for 'malice toward none' and binding up the nation's wounds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sherman's March
*Student:* Map Sherman's march from Atlanta to Savannah and debate whether total war was justified.

*How to teach:* Map Sherman's march; debate total war.

- **Q:** What is total war?
  **A:** Warfare aimed at destroying the enemy's ability and will to fight, including civilian resources like farms, railroads and supplies.
- **Q:** What did Sherman's March accomplish?
  **A:** It destroyed Confederate resources in Georgia, captured Savannah (December 1864) and undermined Southern morale.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Second Inaugural
*Student:* Read Lincoln's Second Inaugural and analyze 'with malice toward none, with charity for all.'

*How to teach:* Read the Second Inaugural; analyze 'with malice toward none.'

- **Q:** What did Lincoln mean by 'with malice toward none, with charity for all'?
  **A:** The nation should reunite without revenge, healing and caring for all who suffered.
- **Q:** What did Lincoln say was the cause of the war?
  **A:** Slavery — enslaved people were 'somehow the cause of the war.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Appomattox
*Student:* Read accounts of Lee's surrender, including the terms Grant offered.

*How to teach:* Read accounts of Appomattox and Grant's terms.

- **Q:** What terms did Grant offer Lee's army?
  **A:** Soldiers could go home on parole and not be prosecuted, officers kept sidearms, men kept their horses, and the Union gave rations to the hungry Confederates.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Costs and consequences
*Student:* Graph casualty statistics and chart political, economic and environmental consequences of the war.

*How to teach:* Graph casualties; chart consequences.

- **Q:** Name one political and one economic consequence.
  **A:** Political: Union preserved, slavery abolished, stronger federal government. Economic: Southern economy devastated; Northern industry expanded.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Unit test on the Civil War.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 8 test.

- **Q:** Who assassinated Lincoln and when?
  **A:** John Wilkes Booth, on April 14, 1865, at Ford's Theatre (Lincoln died April 15).

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test (suggested): Fort Sumter; border states; Anaconda Plan; Bull Run; Antietam; Emancipation Proclamation; USCT and 54th Massachusetts; Gettysburg and Vicksburg; Gettysburg Address; draft riots; Sherman's March to the Sea (November–December 1864, Atlanta to Savannah, total war destroying railroads, farms and supplies); Second Inaugural ('With malice toward none, with charity for all'); Appomattox Court House — Lee surrendered to Grant April 9, 1865; terms let Confederate soldiers go home on parole, officers keep sidearms, men keep horses, and Grant provided rations; Lincoln assassinated by John Wilkes Booth April 14, 1865 (died April 15); consequences — slavery ended (13th Amendment), Union preserved, federal power grew, South's economy devastated, about 620,000–750,000 dead.

## Spanish 1B: Unit 6 project: A service report

**Prepare:** Materials for a small service project (food drive bags, cards for seniors, gloves and trash bags for a park cleanup). Camera.

**Watch for:**
- Writing the report in present tense. It's a completed event: use the preterite.
- Forgetting le/les with dar and decir: Les dimos…, Nos dijeron…

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a small service activity (food drive, letters to seniors, park cleanup) and write the plan in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Plan the service activity; write the plan in Spanish (Vamos a…).

- **Q:** Write: 'We are going to collect food for the food bank.'
  **A:** Vamos a recoger comida para el banco de comida.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Do it
*Student:* Carry out the service activity with family and take photos.

*How to teach:* Carry out the activity; take photos.

- **Q:** What does 'juntar' mean?
  **A:** To gather/collect.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Write
*Student:* Write a 12-sentence report in the preterite on what you did, what people said, and what you gave.

*How to teach:* Write the 12-sentence preterite report.

- **Q:** Write: 'We gave the cards to the elderly and they said thank you.'
  **A:** Les dimos las tarjetas a los ancianos y nos dijeron 'gracias'.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the report with photos to a family audience and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present with photos; answer questions.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Qué hicieron ustedes?
  **A:** Hicimos ___, e.g., Recogimos basura en el parque.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the unit 6 test on IOPs, dar, hacer, decir, and community vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 6 test.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I did a service project.'
  **A:** Hice un proyecto de servicio.

**Assessment:** Service report rubric and unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Service report rubric (1–4): 12 sentences in the preterite describing plan, actions, what people said (decir) and what was given (dar + IOPs), uses hacer, community vocabulary, sequence words, photos; presentation clear. Unit 6 test: IOPs, dar, hacer, decir, community vocabulary.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Staging a reading

**Prepare:** Scripts, simple props and costumes, sound effects (phone), an audience, recording device.

**Watch for:**
- Staged readings without movement. Correct: simple blocking and eye contact bring a reading to life.
- Skipping a full run-through. Correct: run the whole show at least once with props.

### Day 1 (Monday): Casting & planning
*Student:* Cast family or friends and plan simple props and set.

*How to teach:* Cast and plan props and set.

- **Q:** What is a staged reading?
  **A:** A performance with scripts in hand, minimal set, props and blocking.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearsal 1
*Student:* Rehearse with blocking.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with blocking.

- **Q:** Why give each actor clear blocking?
  **A:** So movement is purposeful and the audience can see everyone.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearsal 2
*Student:* Run-through with props and sound effects.

*How to teach:* Run-through with props and sound.

- **Q:** What is a cue?
  **A:** A signal (line or action) for an actor, sound or light change.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Performance
*Student:* Perform the staged reading for an audience and record it.

*How to teach:* Perform and record.

- **Q:** What should actors do if they make a mistake?
  **A:** Stay in character and keep going.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on what worked and what you would change.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection.

- **Q:** What should a reflection include?
  **A:** What worked, what didn't, audience response and what you'd change.

**Assessment:** Staged reading and reflection.

**Answer key:** Staged reading and reflection: 4 = organized performance with blocking, props/sound, clear voices; reflection uses audience response and video to evaluate specific choices and changes; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = disorganized; 1 = incomplete.

## Physical Education 8: Yoga and flexibility

**Prepare:** Yoga mat or towel, strap or towel, sit-and-reach box. Find a beginner sun salutation video.

**Watch for:**
- Forcing stretches. Correct: stretch to mild tension, never pain; breathe steadily.
- Holding breath in balance poses. Correct: slow, steady breathing improves balance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sun salutation
*Student:* Learn and perform a sun salutation sequence five times.

*How to teach:* Teach sun salutation A (mountain, upward salute, forward fold, half lift, plank/low push-up, upward dog/cobra, downward dog, forward fold, mountain); repeat 5 times.

- **Q:** Name four poses in a sun salutation.
  **A:** Mountain, forward fold, plank, cobra/upward dog, downward dog.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Balance poses
*Student:* Practice tree, warrior III and chair pose; hold each 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* Tree, warrior III, chair; 30 seconds each.

- **Q:** Give one cue for tree pose.
  **A:** Press foot into inner thigh or calf (not the knee), fix eyes on a point, hips level.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): PNF stretching
*Student:* Learn contract-relax PNF hamstring stretching with a strap and measure sit-and-reach before and after.

*How to teach:* PNF contract-relax: stretch hamstring with strap ~10 s, contract against the strap 5–6 s, relax and stretch deeper ~10–30 s; measure before and after.

- **Q:** Why does PNF increase range of motion?
  **A:** Contracting then relaxing the muscle reduces its reflex resistance, allowing a deeper stretch.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flow
*Student:* Create a 15-minute yoga flow of 10 poses.

*How to teach:* Create a 10-pose, 15-minute flow.

- **Q:** Why sequence poses carefully?
  **A:** Warm up first, balance forward and backward bends, end with calming poses.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Lead your flow for a family member and record flexibility progress.

*How to teach:* Lead the flow; record flexibility.

- **Q:** How often should you stretch to improve flexibility?
  **A:** At least 2–3 days a week, holding static stretches 10–30 seconds.

**Assessment:** Yoga flow sequence and flexibility measurement.

**Answer key:** Yoga flow sequence: 4 = 10 poses in logical order, breath cues, safety modifications, led smoothly; flexibility measurements before/after PNF recorded. 3 = minor gaps; 2 = fewer poses or no measurements; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Distracted walking, biking and safe rides

**Prepare:** Find teen pedestrian/cyclist data (NHTSA or CA Office of Traffic Safety). Set up a cone (or shoe) path for the distraction test. Check bike and helmet fit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pedestrians and cyclists are always seen by drivers. Correct: drivers have blind spots and may be distracted; make eye contact and wear bright/reflective gear.
- Believing you can text and walk safely. Correct: distraction reduces awareness and slows reaction time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Data
*Student:* Analyze teen pedestrian and cyclist injury data.

*How to teach:* Read the data together and identify patterns (time of day, distraction, ages, helmet use).

- **Q:** When are pedestrian deaths most common?
  **A:** After dark (most occur at night) and in urban areas away from intersections.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Distraction test
*Student:* Try texting while walking a cone path and record errors.

*How to teach:* The child walks the cone path once normally and once while typing a message; record errors and time.

- **Q:** What did the distraction test show?
  **A:** More errors (hitting cones, drifting) and/or slower time while texting — attention can't fully focus on two tasks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Blind spots
*Student:* Learn vehicle blind spots and how to be visible.

*How to teach:* Sit in the driver's seat of a parked car with the child walking around it to find blind spots (behind, sides, in front of large vehicles).

- **Q:** How can a cyclist be more visible?
  **A:** Wear bright/reflective clothing, use front white light and rear red reflector/light at night, ride predictably with traffic, make eye contact with drivers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safe rides
*Student:* Discuss refusing rides with distracted or impaired drivers and a family code word.

*How to teach:* Agree on a family code word and no-questions-asked pickup. Practice refusing a ride from a distracted or impaired driver.

- **Q:** What might you say to refuse a ride?
  **A:** 'No thanks, my mom is picking me up,' or text the code word to a parent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a safety checklist for walking and biking.

*How to teach:* The child creates a walking/biking safety checklist.

- **Q:** Does California require helmets for cyclists under 18?
  **A:** Yes — riders under 18 must wear a helmet on a bicycle, scooter, skateboard or skates.

**Assessment:** Safety checklist.

**Answer key:** Safety checklist: 4 = at least 8 accurate items covering helmet (required under 18 in CA), visibility, phone away, crosswalks and eye contact, riding with traffic, lights at night, safe-ride code word; 3 = 6–7 items; 2 = 4–5; 1 = fewer.
