# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 10

## English Language Arts 8: Literary analysis essay and film comparison

**Prepare:** Arrange to watch the 1983 film The Outsiders (directed by Francis Ford Coppola; rated PG) — preview the church-fire and rumble scenes. Have the essay outline ready.

**Watch for:**
- Dropping quotations without introducing or explaining them. Use claim–quotation–commentary every time.
- Judging a film only by whether it's 'like the book.' Ask what each change accomplishes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft body paragraphs
*Student:* Draft three body paragraphs using the claim-quotation-commentary pattern with embedded, cited quotations.

*How to teach:* Model one body paragraph: topic sentence, introduced quotation with page number, 2–3 sentences of commentary. Child drafts three.

- **Q:** Embed this correctly: Cherry says things are rough all over.
  **A:** As Cherry tells Ponyboy, "Things are rough all over" (Hinton 35). [page number varies by edition]

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Introduction, conclusion & revision
*Student:* Write the introduction and conclusion, then revise for transitions and verb voice using a checklist.

*How to teach:* Child writes introduction and conclusion; revises for transitions and active voice. Check understanding with: "What three parts belong in an introduction?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** What three parts belong in an introduction?
  **A:** A hook, brief context (title, author, situation), and the thesis.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Film comparison
*Student:* Watch the church-fire and rumble scenes from the 1983 film and chart what was kept, changed or cut and why.

*How to teach:* Watch the two scenes; chart kept / changed / cut. To check, ask: "Who directed the 1983 film?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Who directed the 1983 film?
  **A:** Francis Ford Coppola.
- **Q:** Why might a film cut scenes?
  **A:** Running time, visual pacing, or focusing the story on action.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Socratic seminar
*Student:* Prepare 3 open questions and discuss 'Did the film capture the novel's theme?' citing both the text and film.

*How to teach:* Child prepares 3 open questions; hold a seminar on whether the film captured the theme. Finish by asking: "Give an open (not yes/no) question." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Give an open (not yes/no) question.
  **A:** 'How does the film use the sunset to show what Ponyboy and Cherry share?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the novel test (vocabulary, character, theme, allusion) and submit the final essay.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test and collect the final essay. Before moving on, ask: "What does 'Stay gold' mean?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What does 'Stay gold' mean?
  **A:** Keep your innocence and ability to see beauty.

**Assessment:** Literary analysis essay (rubric) and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Literary analysis essay rubric (4 levels per trait): Thesis (arguable, answers the prompt); Evidence (≥6 embedded quotations from across the novel, cited by page); Commentary (explains how each quotation supports the claim); Organization (intro, 3 bodies, conclusion, transitions); Conventions (voice, verbals, punctuation). Unit 2 test answers: Greasers vs Socs; Ponyboy narrates; Darry is the oldest brother/guardian; 'Stay gold' alludes to Frost's 'Nothing Gold Can Stay'; Johnny kills Bob to save Ponyboy; theme example: violence and labels hurt everyone.

## Mathematics 8: Slope signs, special lines, writing equations

**Prepare:** Graph paper and Desmos. Prepare 10 point pairs for Wednesday and 8 graphs/tables for Thursday.

**Watch for:**
- Sign errors in the slope formula, e.g., subtracting in different orders on top and bottom. Keep the order: (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1).
- Thinking a horizontal line has no slope and a vertical line has zero slope. Horizontal: slope 0; vertical: slope undefined.

### Day 1 (Monday): Negative slope
*Student:* Model a draining tank or spending a gift card balance; graph and interpret the negative rate of change.

*How to teach:* Model spending a $50 gift card at $5 per week: make a table, graph it, and ask what the negative slope means. Before moving on, ask: "Write the equation for a $50 gift card spent at $5 per week. When is it empty?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Write the equation for a $50 gift card spent at $5 per week. When is it empty?
  **A:** y = 50 − 5x; empty at x = 10 weeks.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Horizontal and vertical lines
*Student:* Graph y = 4 and x = -2, find their slopes, and explain why x = -2 is not a function of x.

*How to teach:* Graph y = 4 and x = −2. Compute slope using two points on each.

- **Q:** What are the slopes of y = 4 and x = −2?
  **A:** y = 4 has slope 0; x = −2 has undefined slope (run is 0).
- **Q:** Why is x = −2 not a function of x?
  **A:** The single input x = −2 is paired with infinitely many y-values.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Slope from two points
*Student:* Use the formula (y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1) on 10 point pairs, then write the full equation of each line.

*How to teach:* Teach (y2 − y1)/(x2 − x1), then substitute one point into y = mx + b to find b. Child completes 10 pairs.

- **Q:** Write the equation of the line through (2, 3) and (6, 11).
  **A:** m = 8/4 = 2; 3 = 2(2) + b gives b = −1; y = 2x − 1.
- **Q:** Through (−1, 4) and (3, −4)?
  **A:** m = −8/4 = −2; 4 = −2(−1) + b gives b = 2; y = −2x + 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Equations from graphs and tables
*Student:* Write equations for 8 lines given as graphs or tables and check with Desmos.

*How to teach:* Child writes equations for 8 lines from graphs and tables and checks each in Desmos. Finish by asking: "x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 5, 8, 11, 14. Write the equation." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** x: 0, 1, 2, 3 and y: 5, 8, 11, 14. Write the equation.
  **A:** y = 3x + 5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: find slopes and write equations from two points, a table, a graph and a story.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Review & check day: have the child redo any missed problem in the notebook and explain the error in one sentence.

- **Q:** A line has slope −1/2 and passes through (4, 1). Find the equation.
  **A:** 1 = −2 + b, so b = 3; y = −(1/2)x + 3.

**Assessment:** Quiz on writing linear equations.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Slope through (2, 3) and (6, 11) → 2; equation y = 2x − 1. 2) Through (−1, 4) and (3, −4) → slope −2; y = −2x + 2. 3) Table x: 0,1,2,3; y: 5,8,11,14 → y = 3x + 5. 4) Graph crossing (0, −2) and (3, 4) → y = 2x − 2. 5) Story: $50 gift card, spend $5 per week → y = −5x + 50. 6) Equation of horizontal line through (1, 4) → y = 4; vertical line through (−2, 3) → x = −2. 1 point each.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Gravity is attractive and depends on mass

**Prepare:** Bathroom scale, NASA planet fact sheet, a few balls, a stretchy sheet or fabric, marbles. Bookmark PhET 'Gravity Force Lab.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster. Without air resistance all objects fall with the same acceleration.
- Confusing mass and weight. Mass (kg) is amount of matter and doesn't change; weight (N) is the gravitational force and depends on location.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is gravity?
*Student:* Drop objects of different mass and observe they land together; discuss mass vs weight.

*How to teach:* Drop two objects of different mass (e.g., a book and a closed water bottle) together; they land at the same time. Discuss mass vs weight.

- **Q:** Why does a feather fall slower than a hammer on Earth but not on the Moon?
  **A:** Air resistance slows the feather on Earth; the Moon has no air, so both fall together (shown by Apollo 15).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gravity well model
*Student:* Stretch a sheet or fabric and place heavy and light balls on it; roll marbles to model gravitational attraction.

*How to teach:* Stretch a sheet; place a heavy ball in the middle and roll marbles. Discuss what the model shows and its limits.

- **Q:** Limit of the sheet model?
  **A:** It relies on Earth's gravity pulling down on the sheet and shows 2-D only; it is just an analogy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data on gravity
*Student:* Use NASA data to compute your weight on each planet and graph planet mass vs surface gravity.

*How to teach:* Use NASA data to calculate weight on each planet; graph mass vs surface gravity. To check, ask: "If you weigh 100 lb on Earth, about how much on Mars?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** If you weigh 100 lb on Earth, about how much on Mars?
  **A:** About 38 lb (Mars gravity ≈ 0.38 × Earth's).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Arguing from evidence
*Student:* Write an argument that gravitational interaction depends on mass, using planet data and the PhET 'Gravity Force Lab' sim.

*How to teach:* Use PhET Gravity Force Lab to change masses and distance; write an argument with evidence. Finish by asking: "What happens to gravitational force if one mass doubles?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** What happens to gravitational force if one mass doubles?
  **A:** The force doubles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on gravity, mass and weight.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (key). Before moving on, ask: "Does your mass change on the Moon?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** Does your mass change on the Moon?
  **A:** No — only your weight changes.

**Assessment:** Gravity argument and quiz.

**Answer key:** Argument rubric: claim that gravitational force depends on the masses (and distance); evidence from planet data (more massive planets generally have stronger surface gravity, e.g., Jupiter 24.8 m/s² vs Earth 9.8 vs Mars 3.7) and the PhET sim (doubling a mass doubles the force); reasoning. Quiz: mass stays the same on the Moon; 100 lb on Earth ≈ 38 lb on Mars and ≈ 17 lb on the Moon; gravity is always attractive; force decreases with distance.

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Life, economy and culture in the early Republic

**Prepare:** Summaries of McCulloch v. Maryland (1819) and Gibbons v. Ogden (1824); Treaty of Greenville (1795) summary and map; 'Rip Van Winkle' (Project Gutenberg); images of Gilbert Stuart's Washington portraits and Charles Willson Peale portraits; recordings of 'Yankee Doodle' and 'Hail, Columbia.' Write the unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking treaties with Native nations were equal agreements freely made. Many, like Greenville, followed military defeat and pressured nations to cede land.
- Reading 'Rip Van Winkle' as just a funny tale. It comments on how much changed with the Revolution — and how much stayed the same.

### Day 1 (Monday): Early capitalism
*Student:* Examine McCulloch v. Maryland and Gibbons v. Ogden and how they strengthened national markets.

*How to teach:* Examine McCulloch and Gibbons and how they strengthened national markets.

- **Q:** What did McCulloch v. Maryland decide?
  **A:** Congress had implied power to create a national bank, and Maryland could not tax it — federal law is supreme over state law.
- **Q:** What did Gibbons v. Ogden decide?
  **A:** Congress, not states, regulates interstate commerce, so New York's steamboat monopoly was invalid — opening national trade.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Treaties with American Indian nations
*Student:* Analyze the Treaty of Greenville (1795) and compare outcomes for Native nations.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Treaty of Greenville and its outcomes for Native nations.

- **Q:** What led to the Treaty of Greenville?
  **A:** The U.S. victory under Anthony Wayne at Fallen Timbers (1794) over a Native confederacy; in 1795 nations including the Shawnee, Miami and Delaware ceded most of Ohio.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): American literature
*Student:* Read 'Rip Van Winkle' and discuss what it says about the new nation.

*How to teach:* Read 'Rip Van Winkle'; discuss what it says about the new nation.

- **Q:** What changes does Rip notice when he returns?
  **A:** The inn's sign now shows George Washington instead of King George III, people argue about elections and 'rights of citizens', and he is suspected of being a Tory when he says he's a loyal subject of the king.
- **Q:** What does the story suggest about the Revolution?
  **A:** Big political changes happened, but daily village life and human nature stayed much the same.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Art and music
*Student:* Analyze Gilbert Stuart and Charles Willson Peale portraits and early American songs.

*How to teach:* Analyze portraits and early songs.

- **Q:** How did Gilbert Stuart's portraits shape the image of Washington?
  **A:** His dignified portraits (one appears on the $1 bill) presented Washington as a calm, republican leader and became the standard national image.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Unit test on the early Republic.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 3 test.

- **Q:** What is the 'necessary and proper' clause used for in McCulloch?
  **A:** To justify Congress creating a bank as a means to carry out its enumerated powers (taxing, borrowing, regulating currency).

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test (suggested): Washington's precedents; Hamilton vs Jefferson; Whiskey Rebellion; Farewell Address warnings; Alien and Sedition Acts; Marbury v. Madison (judicial review); Louisiana Purchase 1803; Lewis and Clark; McCulloch v. Maryland — Congress could create a national bank (implied powers) and states could not tax it ('the power to tax involves the power to destroy'); Gibbons v. Ogden — only Congress regulates interstate commerce (struck down New York's steamboat monopoly); Treaty of Greenville — after the Battle of Fallen Timbers (1794), Native nations ceded most of present-day Ohio; Rip Van Winkle sleeps 20 years through the Revolution.

## Spanish 1B: La ropa

**Prepare:** Closet items, catalog photos, a short teen fashion blog in Spanish. Vocabulary: la camisa, la camiseta, los pantalones, los jeans/vaqueros, la falda, el vestido, los zapatos, los tenis, las botas, la chaqueta, el abrigo, el suéter, la gorra, el sombrero, los calcetines, la sudadera, los pantalones cortos, el traje de baño, la bufanda, los guantes.

**Watch for:**
- Using 'usar' and 'llevar' interchangeably is fine, but 'llevar' is the standard for wearing; don't use 'tener' for wearing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clothing
*Student:* Learn 20 clothing words and label clothes from your closet.

*How to teach:* Learn 20 clothing words; label closet items.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Llevar

*How to teach:* Describe what five family members wear with llevar + colors.

  **A:** Mi papá lleva unos jeans azules y una camisa blanca.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weather & clothes
*Student:* Connect weather and seasons to clothing: Cuando hace frío, llevo un abrigo; write 8 sentences.

*How to teach:* Connect weather to clothes; write 8 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'When it's cold, I wear a coat.'
  **A:** Cuando hace frío, llevo un abrigo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fashion blog
*Student:* Read a short teen fashion blog post in Spanish and list the items and opinions mentioned.

*How to teach:* Read a teen fashion blog; list items and opinions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a clothing vocabulary quiz with a listening description.

*How to teach:* Give the clothing quiz with listening.

**Assessment:** Clothing vocabulary quiz.

**Answer key:** Clothing quiz: 20 items with articles; listening — child identifies what a person is wearing. Samples: She is wearing a red dress → Ella lleva un vestido rojo; I wear boots when it's cold → Llevo botas cuando hace frío; a black jacket → una chaqueta negra. 80% proficient.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Rhythm reading

**Prepare:** musictheory.net lessons, metronome app, staff paper, pots or drum pad. Write a 4-measure dictation in 4/4 (e.g., quarter-quarter-half | eighths×4-half | dotted half-quarter | whole) and a sight-reading rhythm.

**Watch for:**
- Counting a dotted half note as 2 beats. Correct: the dot adds half the note's value, so a dotted half = 3 beats in 4/4.
- Thinking 6/8 has six beats felt evenly. Correct: 6/8 is usually felt in 2 big beats, each a dotted quarter.

### Day 1 (Monday): Note values
*Student:* Complete the musictheory.net lessons on note durations and rests; clap and count 8 one-measure patterns.

*How to teach:* Lessons on note durations; clap and count 8 patterns.

- **Q:** In 4/4, how many beats do whole, half, quarter and eighth notes get?
  **A:** 4, 2, 1 and ½.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Time signatures
*Student:* Count and tap rhythms in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8 with a metronome at 80 bpm.

*How to teach:* Count and tap in 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 at 80 bpm.

- **Q:** What does the top number of a time signature mean?
  **A:** Number of beats (or pulses) per measure; the bottom shows which note gets the beat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Syncopation
*Student:* Clap syncopated patterns and identify syncopation in a song like Scott Joplin's 'The Entertainer.'

*How to teach:* Clap syncopated patterns; find syncopation in 'The Entertainer.'

- **Q:** What is syncopation?
  **A:** Accents on weak beats or off-beats, giving a 'pushed' feel.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Body percussion
*Student:* Compose and perform a 4-measure body-percussion rhythm (stomp, clap, snap, pat).

*How to teach:* Compose and perform a 4-measure body-percussion rhythm.

- **Q:** Does each 4/4 measure add up to 4 beats?
  **A:** Check every measure totals 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a rhythm dictation of 4 measures and perform a sight-reading rhythm at 80 bpm.

*How to teach:* Give the dictation and sight-reading.

- **Q:** How many eighth notes fit in one measure of 4/4?
  **A:** Eight.

**Assessment:** Rhythm dictation and performance check.

**Answer key:** Rhythm dictation: 1 pt per correct measure (4) — compare to your written answer. Performance check: steady tempo at 80 bpm, correct durations, rests observed (3/3 proficient).

*How to teach:* Round-robin; child officiates one game.

*How to teach:* Second round; team plans strategy.

## Health Education 8: Budget meals and food security

**Prepare:** Get a grocery ad or visit a store/website with unit prices. Set a dinner budget (e.g., $20 for four). Choose a recipe and buy ingredients. Write a nutrition unit test (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking healthy food is always expensive. Correct: beans, rice, oats, eggs, frozen vegetables and seasonal produce are cheap and nutritious.
- Comparing package price instead of unit price. Correct: divide price by amount to compare value.

### Day 1 (Monday): Unit price
*Student:* Compare unit prices of three staple foods in a grocery ad to find the best value.

*How to teach:* Teach unit price = price ÷ units. Compare three sizes or brands of a staple (rice, oats, milk).

- **Q:** A 2-lb bag of rice costs $3.00 and a 5-lb bag costs $6.50. Which is the better value?
  **A:** 2 lb: $1.50/lb; 5 lb: $1.30/lb. The 5-lb bag is cheaper per pound.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a balanced dinner for four under a set budget.

*How to teach:* The child plans a balanced dinner for four (all or most MyPlate groups) and prices each ingredient to stay under budget.

- **Q:** Does your meal include at least four food groups and stay under budget?
  **A:** Answers vary; check the list has protein, grain, vegetable, fruit and/or dairy and that the total is at or under the budget.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cook
*Student:* Cook the meal with a parent using food-safety practices from grade 7.

*How to teach:* Cook together. Review clean, separate, cook, chill: wash hands, separate raw meat, use a thermometer (poultry 165°F, ground beef 160°F), refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

- **Q:** What is the safe internal temperature for chicken?
  **A:** 165°F.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Food security
*Student:* Learn about food insecurity in California and local food banks and CalFresh.

*How to teach:* Read about food insecurity in California and locate the nearest food bank; explain CalFresh (California's SNAP program).

- **Q:** What is food insecurity?
  **A:** Lacking consistent access to enough food for an active, healthy life.
- **Q:** What is CalFresh?
  **A:** California's name for SNAP, a program that gives eligible households money on an EBT card to buy groceries.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Take the nutrition unit test and reflect on the meal you made.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test; the child also writes a short reflection on the meal (cost, nutrition, what they'd change).

- **Q:** Name two ways to save money on healthy food.
  **A:** Compare unit prices, buy in season or frozen, cook at home, plan meals, buy store brands, use beans/eggs as protein.

**Assessment:** Meal plan, cooking and unit test.

**Answer key:** Meal plan (4): balanced, priced, under budget, safe-cooking steps noted. Unit test key: 1) energy balance definition 2) calcium 1,300 mg 3) iron and vitamin D roles 4) edited media images are unrealistic 5) anorexia/bulimia/binge eating definitions 6) tell a trusted adult if worried about a friend 7) supplements are not FDA-approved before sale 8) teens: ≤100 mg caffeine, avoid energy drinks 9) unit price calculation 10) chicken 165°F 11) CalFresh = CA SNAP. 80%+ proficient.
