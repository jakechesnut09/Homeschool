# Teacher's Guide — 8th Grade, Week 9

## English Language Arts 8: The Outsiders, chapters 10–12: resolution and theme

**Prepare:** Read chapters 10–12. Print an essay planning sheet; highlighters. Parental preview: Dally is shot by police.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a thesis that is a fact ('The Outsiders is about two gangs'). A thesis must be arguable and answer 'what does Hinton suggest?'
- Choosing quotations only from one part of the book. Require evidence from beginning, middle and end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 10
*Student:* Read chapter 10 and write a journal entry analyzing Dally's death and why Ponyboy refuses to accept Johnny is gone.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 10; journal entry on Dally's death and Ponyboy's denial. Finish by asking: "Why does Dally get himself killed?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Why does Dally get himself killed?
  **A:** Johnny was the one person Dally loved; without him Dally breaks and wants to die, raising an unloaded gun so police shoot.
- **Q:** How does Ponyboy react?
  **A:** He insists Johnny isn't dead, then collapses with a concussion and fever.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 11–12
*Student:* Finish the novel; annotate Johnny's letter and explain how the last line's return to the first line creates a circular structure.

*How to teach:* Finish the novel; annotate Johnny's letter and the last line. Before moving on, ask: "What does Johnny's letter say?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What does Johnny's letter say?
  **A:** Staying gold means staying young and seeing beauty; there's 'still lots of good in the world'; tell Dally.
- **Q:** What is the circular structure?
  **A:** The final line returns to the first line, showing Ponyboy is writing the book as his theme.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Thesis workshop
*Student:* Draft three thesis statements answering 'What does Hinton suggest about growing up?' and choose the most arguable one.

*How to teach:* Model one weak thesis and one arguable one. Child drafts three and chooses one.

- **Q:** Example arguable thesis about growing up?
  **A:** 'Hinton suggests that growing up means seeing past labels like Greaser and Soc to recognize everyone's shared pain.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evidence gathering
*Student:* Select 6 quotations from across the novel and write two sentences of analysis for each in an evidence chart.

*How to teach:* Child selects 6 quotations across the novel with two sentences of analysis each. To check, ask: "Give a quotation about seeing beyond labels and explain it." and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** Give a quotation about seeing beyond labels and explain it.
  **A:** 'Things are rough all over' (Cherry) — shows Socs suffer too, challenging Ponyboy's assumptions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Build a full outline (introduction, three body paragraphs, conclusion) and take a quiz on chapters 10–12.

*How to teach:* Build the outline; give the quiz. Finish by asking: "Who is cleared at the hearing?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Who is cleared at the hearing?
  **A:** Ponyboy.

**Assessment:** Essay outline and chapter quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) After Johnny dies, Dally robs a grocery store and is shot by police under a streetlight, raising an unloaded gun. 2) Ponyboy is sick with a concussion for days and insists he killed Bob. 3) At the hearing Ponyboy is cleared. 4) Johnny's letter (in Gone with the Wind) says there's still lots of good in the world, tells Pony to stay gold, and to tell Dally. 5) Ponyboy decides to write his English theme — the book itself. 6) The last line repeats the first: 'When I stepped out into the bright sunlight from the darkness of the movie house...' Outline rubric: arguable thesis; three body topics with two quotations each; conclusion idea.

## Mathematics 8: Representing linear relationships

**Prepare:** 8 paper or plastic cups, ruler, a clear cup or jar, 10–15 marbles or coins, water, graph paper, Desmos.

**Watch for:**
- Treating every linear relationship as proportional. Point to the y-intercept: when it is not 0, the relationship is linear but not proportional.
- Swapping slope and intercept in context. Ask: what is the value at the start (x = 0)? That's b. What changes per one unit? That's m.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stacking cups
*Student:* Measure the height of a stack of 1 to 8 paper cups, graph the data, and find the slope (height per cup) and intercept (base height).

*How to teach:* Measure stacks of 1 to 8 cups and graph height vs number of cups. Find the slope (height added per cup) and the intercept (the extra base height).

- **Q:** If 1 cup is 9 cm tall and each added cup adds 1.5 cm, write the equation and find the height of 8 cups.
  **A:** h = 1.5n + 7.5; 8 cups: 1.5(8) + 7.5 = 19.5 cm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slope and intercept in context
*Student:* For 6 stories (a gym membership with a sign-up fee, a filling pool), identify the rate of change and initial value and write y = mx + b.

*How to teach:* For each story, ask 'what is the starting amount?' and 'what changes per unit?' The child writes y = mx + b for 6 stories. Before moving on, ask: "A gym charges $25 to sign up plus $15 per month. Write the equation." and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** A gym charges $25 to sign up plus $15 per month. Write the equation.
  **A:** y = 15x + 25 (x = months).
- **Q:** A pool has 200 gallons and fills at 50 gallons per minute. Write the equation.
  **A:** y = 50x + 200.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): More linear relationships
*Student:* Match 8 equations to graphs and tables; explain what the slope and intercept mean in each context.

*How to teach:* Child matches 8 equations to graphs and tables, explaining slope and intercept in each context. Check understanding with: "Which graph matches y = −3x + 12?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** Which graph matches y = −3x + 12?
  **A:** The line crossing the y-axis at 12 and falling 3 units for every 1 unit right (x-intercept 4).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Translating to y = mx + b
*Student:* Show that the graph of y = mx + b is a translation of y = mx by b units; graph 4 pairs in Desmos.

*How to teach:* In Desmos graph y = 2x and y = 2x + 3 together and ask what changed. The child graphs 4 pairs and states that y = mx + b is y = mx translated b units up (down if b < 0).

- **Q:** How is y = 2x + 3 related to y = 2x?
  **A:** It is the same line translated 3 units up; same slope, y-intercept 3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Water-displacement mini lab: drop marbles in a cup of water, record water level, write the linear equation; quiz on slope/intercept meaning.

*How to teach:* Mini lab: drop marbles one at a time into a cup of water and record the water level; write the linear equation. Then give the quiz.

- **Q:** Water starts at 8 cm and rises 0.4 cm per marble. What is the level after 10 marbles?
  **A:** y = 0.4(10) + 8 = 12 cm.

**Assessment:** Quiz on interpreting slope and intercept; lab equation.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Gym: $25 sign-up + $15/month → y = 15x + 25; slope = cost per month, intercept = sign-up fee. 2) Pool starts at 200 gal, fills 50 gal/min → y = 50x + 200. 3) Line through (0, 12) and (4, 0) → y = −3x + 12. 4) Interpret y = 1.5n + 7.5 for cups → each added cup adds 1.5 cm; 7.5 cm is the part of a cup that does not nest (the first cup's 9 cm minus one 1.5-cm lip). Lab equation: example y = 0.4x + 8 (cm) — accept any equation matching the child's data with slope = rise per marble and intercept = starting water level.

## Science 8 (Integrated): Collisions and energy review

**Prepare:** Tennis, rubber (bouncy) and clay (or play-dough) balls; meter stick; phone with slow-motion video. Prepare the Unit 2 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a rebound shows energy was 'lost.' It was transformed into heat, sound and deformation.
- Confusing elastic and inelastic collisions. Elastic: little energy transformed (objects bounce); inelastic: much transformed (objects deform or stick).

### Day 1 (Monday): Bounce test
*Student:* Drop tennis, rubber and clay balls from 1 m; measure rebound heights and calculate the percent energy retained.

*How to teach:* Drop each ball from 1 m; measure rebound height; calculate percent energy retained = rebound ÷ drop × 100. Finish by asking: "A tennis ball rebounds to 55 cm from 100 cm. Percent energy retained?" — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** A tennis ball rebounds to 55 cm from 100 cm. Percent energy retained?
  **A:** About 55% (GPE is proportional to height).
- **Q:** Where did the other 45% go?
  **A:** Thermal energy, sound and deformation of the ball and floor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slow-motion analysis
*Student:* Film collisions in slow motion and identify where energy goes (sound, heat, deformation).

*How to teach:* Film collisions in slow motion; identify where energy goes. Before moving on, ask: "What does the clay ball's flattening show?" and have the child point to evidence or show work.

- **Q:** What does the clay ball's flattening show?
  **A:** Energy went into permanent deformation — an inelastic collision.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Unit review
*Student:* Create a concept map connecting force, motion, kinetic energy, potential energy and energy transfer.

*How to teach:* The child creates a concept map connecting force, motion, KE, PE and energy transfer. Check understanding with: "How are force and energy connected?" If the answer misses the key idea, revisit today's model together.

- **Q:** How are force and energy connected?
  **A:** A force acting over a distance transfers energy (work).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice assessment
*Student:* Answer 3 data-analysis questions using kinetic energy graphs.

*How to teach:* Practice with 3 KE graph questions. To check, ask: "A KE vs speed graph curves upward. Why?" and compare the answer with the model below.

- **Q:** A KE vs speed graph curves upward. Why?
  **A:** KE depends on speed squared.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Unit test on energy.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test. Finish by asking: "Calculate GPE of a 5-kg object 2 m high." — look for the specific points in the model answer.

- **Q:** Calculate GPE of a 5-kg object 2 m high.
  **A:** 5 × 9.8 × 2 = 98 J.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test sample answers: KE = ½mv² (3-kg at 4 m/s → 24 J); doubling speed quadruples KE; GPE = mgh (5-kg at 2 m → 98 J); energy is conserved, transforming to thermal energy; elastic vs inelastic; data question: steeper KE vs speed curve explained by v².

## U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict: Adams, Jefferson and the Louisiana Purchase

**Prepare:** Sedition Act text (1798); Jefferson's First Inaugural excerpt (1801); Marbury v. Madison summary; U.S. map; Lewis and Clark journal excerpts.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Marbury got his commission. The Court ruled he deserved it but that the law giving the Court power to issue the writ was unconstitutional — so he didn't get it, and the Court gained judicial review.
- Thinking the Louisiana Purchase was just the state of Louisiana. It covered about 828,000 square miles from the Mississippi to the Rocky Mountains, doubling the nation's size.

### Day 1 (Monday): Adams and the Alien and Sedition Acts
*Student:* Read the Sedition Act and argue whether it violated the First Amendment.

*How to teach:* Read the Sedition Act; argue whether it violated the First Amendment.

- **Q:** What did the Sedition Act (1798) make a crime?
  **A:** Writing or publishing 'false, scandalous and malicious' statements against the government, Congress or the president.
- **Q:** How did Jefferson and Madison respond?
  **A:** With the Kentucky and Virginia Resolutions, arguing states could judge the acts unconstitutional.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jefferson's Inaugural
*Student:* Read Jefferson's 1801 Inaugural Address excerpt: 'We are all Republicans, we are all Federalists.'

*How to teach:* Read Jefferson's 1801 Inaugural excerpt.

- **Q:** What did Jefferson mean by 'We are all Republicans, we are all Federalists'?
  **A:** Despite party differences, Americans share basic principles; he called for unity after the bitter 1800 election and the first peaceful transfer of power between parties.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marbury v. Madison
*Student:* Summarize the case and explain judicial review.

*How to teach:* Summarize Marbury v. Madison (1803).

- **Q:** What is judicial review?
  **A:** The power of courts to declare laws or government actions unconstitutional.
- **Q:** Who wrote the Marbury opinion?
  **A:** Chief Justice John Marshall.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Louisiana Purchase & Lewis and Clark
*Student:* Map the purchase and route; read journal entries and Sacagawea's role.

*How to teach:* Map the purchase and route; read journal entries.

- **Q:** Why did Jefferson hesitate to buy Louisiana?
  **A:** The Constitution didn't explicitly allow buying territory, conflicting with his strict construction view; he went ahead anyway.
- **Q:** Why did Napoleon sell Louisiana?
  **A:** He needed money for war in Europe and had lost his army in Haiti (Saint-Domingue), ending plans for an American empire.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Map quiz and short answer.

*How to teach:* Give the map quiz and short answer.

- **Q:** What were Lewis and Clark's goals?
  **A:** Explore the new territory, find a water route to the Pacific, map the land, record plants and animals, and establish relations with Native nations.

**Assessment:** Map quiz on 1803 territories and expedition route.

**Answer key:** Map quiz key: Louisiana Purchase (1803, from France/Napoleon, about $15 million) between the Mississippi River and the Rockies; New Orleans; Lewis and Clark route (1804–1806) from near St. Louis up the Missouri River, across the Rockies (Lemhi Pass), down the Snake and Columbia Rivers to the Pacific (Fort Clatsop) and back. Short answer: Sacagawea, a Lemhi Shoshone woman, served as interpreter and guide, helped obtain horses from her brother Cameahwait's band, and her presence signaled the group's peaceful intent.

## Spanish 1B: Present progressive and unit 2 assessment

**Prepare:** Phone for the home tour. A busy-house illustration (search 'Spanish present progressive picture'). Read about homes in Spain and Latin America.

**Watch for:**
- Using -ando with -er/-ir verbs: 'comando.' -ar → -ando; -er/-ir → -iendo (comiendo, escribiendo).
- Missing the irregulars: leer → leyendo, dormir → durmiendo.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present progressive
*Student:* Learn estar + -ando/-iendo, including leyendo and durmiendo, and describe what each family member is doing right now.

*How to teach:* Teach estar + -ando/-iendo; describe what family members are doing now.

- **Q:** Say: 'My mom is reading.'
  **A:** Mi mamá está leyendo.
- **Q:** Say: 'We are cleaning the kitchen.'
  **A:** Estamos limpiando la cocina.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Picture description
*Student:* Describe a busy house illustration in 10 progressive sentences.

*How to teach:* Describe a busy house picture in 10 sentences.

- **Q:** Say: 'The baby is sleeping.'
  **A:** El bebé está durmiendo.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Culture: casas
*Student:* Read about homes in Spain and Latin America (interior patios, rooftop azoteas, apartment living) and write five comparisons.

*How to teach:* Read about homes; write five comparisons.

- **Q:** What is an 'azotea'?
  **A:** A flat rooftop terrace used for laundry, plants or gathering.
- **Q:** Write: 'In Spain many families live in apartments.'
  **A:** En España muchas familias viven en pisos (apartamentos).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Home tour
*Student:* Record a 2-minute Spanish video tour of your home with comparisons and chores done in each room.

*How to teach:* Record a 2-minute home tour.

- **Q:** Say: 'This is the living room. It is bigger than the kitchen.'
  **A:** Esta es la sala. Es más grande que la cocina.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the unit test on house vocabulary, comparisons, stem-changers, commands, and the progressive.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My brother is washing the dishes.'
  **A:** Mi hermano está lavando los platos.

**Assessment:** Home tour video rubric and unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Home tour rubric (1–4): 2 minutes, names rooms and furniture, uses comparisons (más/menos…que), mentions chores with present tense or commands, uses estar + prepositions, pronunciation. Unit 2 test: house vocabulary, comparisons, o→ue verbs, tú commands, present progressive (estoy limpiando, está leyendo, están durmiendo). Culture: interior patios (Spain/colonial Latin America), rooftop azoteas, many families live in apartments (pisos) in cities.

## Visual and Performing Arts 8: Painting the identity portrait

**Prepare:** Paints, canvas board or heavy paper, brushes (large flat, medium round, small detail), palette.

**Watch for:**
- Painting details first. Correct: work from big shapes to small details, dark/thin layers first.
- Using straight black for shadows on skin. Correct: mix shadows from complements or darker skin mixes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Underpainting
*Student:* Transfer the sketch and block in large areas with thin underpainting in your chosen scheme.

*How to teach:* Transfer sketch; thin underpainting.

- **Q:** What is an underpainting?
  **A:** A thin first layer blocking in big shapes and values to guide later layers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Building layers
*Student:* Add mid-tones and shadows to the face and background, mixing skin tones from primaries.

*How to teach:* Add mid-tones and shadows; mix skin tones from primaries plus white.

- **Q:** How do you mix a basic skin tone from primaries?
  **A:** Mix red, yellow and a little blue to make a brown, then adjust lightness with white and warmth with red/yellow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Symbols and details
*Student:* Paint symbolic objects and fine details with small brushes; add highlights last.

*How to teach:* Paint symbols and details; highlights last.

- **Q:** Why add highlights last?
  **A:** Light colors sit on top and stay clean; they'd be muddied by later layers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finishing
*Student:* Refine edges, check values by squinting, and sign the finished portrait.

*How to teach:* Refine edges, squint to check values, sign.

- **Q:** Why squint at a painting?
  **A:** It blurs details so you can see value and big shapes clearly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Artist statement
*Student:* Write a 150-word artist statement explaining symbols and color choices and evaluate the portrait using a 4-criterion rubric.

*How to teach:* Write a 150-word statement and self-evaluate.

- **Q:** What belongs in an artist statement?
  **A:** What the work is, what the symbols and color choices mean, process, and what you want viewers to feel or understand.

**Assessment:** Finished portrait and artist statement.

**Answer key:** Portrait rubric (4 criteria × 4 levels): Composition (planned symbols and setting), Color (intentional scheme, mixed tones), Technique (layering, edges, highlights), Meaning (symbols clearly convey identity). Artist statement: ~150 words explaining symbols and colors.

## Physical Education 8: Team handball: transition and game play

**Prepare:** Handball, cones, pinnies. Recruit at least 3 players for 2-v-1 and 3-v-2 drills.

**Watch for:**
- Standing still after passing. Correct: pass and move (cut) into open space.
- Slow transition. Correct: after a save, look long immediately for a fast break.

### Day 1 (Monday): Give-and-go
*Student:* Practice the give-and-go and the cut to open space in 2-v-1 drills.

*How to teach:* Give-and-go: pass, cut past the defender, receive the return pass.

- **Q:** Describe a give-and-go.
  **A:** Pass to a teammate, immediately cut toward the goal, and receive a return pass in space.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fast break
*Student:* Practice transitioning from defense to a 3-on-2 fast break after a goalkeeper save.

*How to teach:* After a goalkeeper save, three attackers sprint to attack two defenders.

- **Q:** Why is a 3-on-2 fast break effective?
  **A:** Attackers outnumber defenders, so one attacker is always open.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Screens
*Student:* Learn how to set and use a screen to free a shooter.

*How to teach:* Set a screen: stand still beside the defender; shooter uses it to get free.

- **Q:** What makes a legal screen?
  **A:** The screener is stationary, with arms close to the body, and allows the defender room to see it; moving screens are fouls.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game
*Student:* Play a small-sided handball game, rotating positions every 5 minutes.

*How to teach:* Small-sided game, rotating positions every 5 minutes.

- **Q:** What should defenders do when the other team scores?
  **A:** Sprint back and set up between attackers and the goal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Compare
*Student:* Write a comparison of strategies in handball, soccer and basketball (all invasion games).

*How to teach:* Write a comparison of handball, soccer and basketball strategies.

- **Q:** Name two strategies common to all invasion games.
  **A:** Creating space by spreading out, passing and moving, fast breaks, man-to-man or zone defense, keeping between opponent and goal.

**Assessment:** Invasion-games strategy comparison.

**Answer key:** Comparison rubric: 4 = names at least three shared strategies (create space, give-and-go, fast break, defensive positioning) and one difference per sport (e.g., handball 3-step rule, soccer uses feet and offside, basketball shot clock/dribble), with examples; 3 = two shared and differences; 2 = lists rules only; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 8: Supplements, energy drinks and diet claims

**Prepare:** Collect labels (or photos online) of 3 energy drinks and 2 supplements. Print a claim-checking checklist (Who sells it? Evidence? 'Too good to be true'? FDA disclaimer?).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking supplements are FDA-approved for safety and effectiveness before sale. Correct: supplements do not need FDA approval before marketing; the manufacturer is responsible for safety.
- Believing energy drinks are a good sports drink. Correct: they contain caffeine and sugar, not appropriate hydration; the American Academy of Pediatrics says children and teens should not have energy drinks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Label study
*Student:* Compare caffeine and sugar in three energy drinks with the recommended limit for teens.

*How to teach:* Read labels and record caffeine (mg) and sugar (g) per can. Compare: health experts advise teens limit caffeine to no more than 100 mg/day; the AHA recommends under 25 g added sugar a day for kids.

- **Q:** A 16-oz energy drink has 160 mg caffeine and 54 g sugar. How does it compare with teen guidance?
  **A:** Caffeine is about 60 mg over the 100 mg/day guideline, and sugar is more than twice the 25 g limit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Supplement claims
*Student:* Evaluate two protein or 'fat-burner' supplement ads with a claim-checking checklist.

*How to teach:* Use the checklist on two supplement ads. Look for words like 'miracle,' 'shred fat fast,' before/after photos and paid testimonials.

- **Q:** Name two red flags in a supplement ad.
  **A:** Examples: promises of quick or dramatic results, 'secret' or 'miracle' formula, testimonials instead of studies, celebrity endorsements, 'all natural so it's safe.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Regulation
*Student:* Learn how FDA regulation differs for supplements and medicines.

*How to teach:* Explain medicines must be proven safe and effective before FDA approval; supplements do not, and the FDA acts only after problems arise. Read the 'This statement has not been evaluated by the FDA' disclaimer.

- **Q:** What does the FDA disclaimer on supplements mean?
  **A:** The FDA has not reviewed the claim; the product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent disease.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sports nutrition
*Student:* Compare marketing claims with the evidence-based fuel plan from PE.

*How to teach:* Compare a supplement's claim to the PE fuel plan: carbohydrates for energy, protein from food (meat, eggs, dairy, beans), water, sleep.

- **Q:** Do most teen athletes need protein powder?
  **A:** No — most get enough protein from regular meals; food sources provide protein plus other nutrients.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a consumer review debunking or supporting one product claim with sources.

*How to teach:* The child writes a consumer review of one product claim citing at least two reliable sources (NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, FDA, AAP, KidsHealth).

- **Q:** Which is a more reliable source: the product's website or NIH Office of Dietary Supplements? Why?
  **A:** NIH ODS — it is a government health agency with no product to sell and relies on research.

**Assessment:** Consumer review.

**Answer key:** Consumer review: 4 = states the claim, evaluates it with at least two reliable sources, explains FDA regulation difference, gives a clear verdict and safer alternative; 3 = verdict with one source; 2 = opinion without evidence; 1 = incomplete. Label study: correct caffeine and sugar comparison to 100 mg and 25 g.
