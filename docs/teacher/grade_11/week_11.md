# Teacher's Guide — 11th Grade, Week 11

## English 11 – American Literature: Poetry of a new nation: Whitman and Dickinson

**Prepare:** Whitman's 'Song of Myself' sections 1, 6, 52 and 'I Hear America Singing'; Dickinson's 'Because I could not stop for Death,' 'Hope is the thing with feathers,' 'Tell all the truth but tell it slant,' and one more (Poetry Foundation).

**Watch for:**
- Assuming Dickinson's dashes are mistakes. They are deliberate pauses that create hesitation and multiple readings.
- Calling free verse 'random.' Whitman uses rhythm through anaphora, catalogs, and parallelism instead of meter.

### Day 1 (Monday): Whitman
*Student:* Read 'Song of Myself' sections aloud; identify catalogs and anaphora and their effect.

*How to teach:* Read Whitman aloud. The student marks catalogs and anaphora and explains their effect. Today's task from the plan: Read 'Song of Myself' sections aloud; identify catalogs and anaphora and their effect. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What does the catalog in 'I Hear America Singing' suggest?
  **A:** By listing mechanics, carpenters, masons, boatmen, shoemakers, and mothers, each singing 'what belongs to him or her,' Whitman celebrates democracy as many individuals together.
- **Q:** How does Whitman answer death at the end of section 52?
  **A:** He bequeaths himself 'to the dirt to grow from the grass I love' and says 'look for me under your boot-soles' — death is a continuation into nature.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dickinson
*Student:* Read four Dickinson poems; scan meter, mark dashes and slant rhymes, and paraphrase each.

*How to teach:* Read four Dickinson poems. Model scanning common meter (alternating 8 and 6 syllables). The student paraphrases each. Today's task from the plan: Read four Dickinson poems; scan meter, mark dashes and slant rhymes, and paraphrase each. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** In 'Because I could not stop for Death,' how is Death portrayed?
  **A:** As a polite gentleman caller who 'kindly stopped' and drives the speaker in a carriage, with Immortality, toward eternity.
- **Q:** Paraphrase 'Tell all the truth but tell it slant.'
  **A:** Truth is too bright to take all at once, so tell it indirectly ('Success in Circuit lies'); 'The Truth must dazzle gradually / Or every man be blind.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparison
*Student:* Plan a comparison of how Whitman and Dickinson treat death or the self; collect six quotations.

*How to teach:* The student chooses death or the self and collects six quotations (three per poet). Today's task from the plan: Plan a comparison of how Whitman and Dickinson treat death or the self; collect six quotations. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give a contrast between Whitman and Dickinson on the self.
  **A:** Whitman's self is expansive and public ('I celebrate myself'); Dickinson's is private and compressed, exploring inner experience in short stanzas.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write
*Student:* Draft a 600-word poetry comparison essay, then memorize and recite one poem (12+ lines).

*How to teach:* The student drafts 600 words and practices recitation (12+ lines) for voice and pacing. Today's task from the plan: Draft a 600-word poetry comparison essay, then memorize and recite one poem (12+ lines). Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What makes a strong recitation?
  **A:** Accurate memorization, clear pacing that follows punctuation (not line ends), and tone that matches meaning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Submit revised essay and take the Unit 3 test on Romantic and Transcendentalist texts.

*How to teach:* Collect the essay and give the Unit 3 test. Today's task from the plan: Submit revised essay and take the Unit 3 test on Romantic and Transcendentalist texts. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Define slant rhyme with an example.
  **A:** Near rhyme with similar but not identical sounds, e.g., 'soul / all' in 'Hope is the thing with feathers.'

**Assessment:** Poetry comparison essay, recitation, Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Poetry comparison essay rubric (4 = exceeds, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning) on four rows: Claim/Thesis (arguable, specific, answers the prompt); Evidence (well-chosen quotations, introduced and cited); Analysis/Reasoning (explains HOW evidence supports the claim, not just what it says); Conventions & organization (logical paragraphs, transitions, few errors). A proficient (3) paper makes a comparative thesis about death or the self, uses three quotations from each poet, and analyzes form (Whitman's catalogs and free verse vs. Dickinson's dashes and common meter) as well as content. Recitation: 4 = accurate and expressive; 3 = minor slips; 2 = several prompts; 1 = read from page. Unit 3 test key: Romanticism, Transcendentalism, Dark Romanticism, unreliable narrator, symbol, free verse, slant rhyme, anaphora, catalog.

## Algebra II: Graphing polynomial functions

**Prepare:** Desmos and graph paper. Prepare a few printed polynomial graphs for Day 4 and the quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a zero with multiplicity 2 crosses the axis. Even multiplicity touches and turns (bounces); odd crosses (multiplicity 3 flattens as it crosses).
- Forgetting to use the y-intercept to find the leading coefficient when writing an equation from a graph.

### Day 1 (Monday): End behavior
*Student:* Investigate how degree and leading coefficient determine end behavior using Desmos.

*How to teach:* In Desmos, graph x², x³, −x⁴, −x⁵ and record end behavior in a 2×2 table (even/odd degree × positive/negative lead). Today's task from the plan: Investigate how degree and leading coefficient determine end behavior using Desmos. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Describe the end behavior of f(x) = −2x⁵ + 3x² − 1.
  **A:** Odd degree, negative leading coefficient: as x → ∞, f(x) → −∞; as x → −∞, f(x) → +∞.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiplicity
*Student:* Explore how even vs. odd multiplicity affects the graph at a zero.

*How to teach:* Graph y = (x − 1), (x − 1)², (x − 1)³ and compare behavior at x = 1. Today's task from the plan: Explore how even vs. odd multiplicity affects the graph at a zero. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How does y = (x + 2)²(x − 3) behave at each zero?
  **A:** At x = −2 (multiplicity 2) it touches and turns; at x = 3 (multiplicity 1) it crosses.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sketching
*Student:* Sketch y = (x+2)(x−1)²(x−4) by hand from features; check with Desmos.

*How to teach:* Model the sketch checklist: degree & end behavior, zeros & multiplicity, y-intercept, then a smooth curve. Student sketches y = (x + 2)(x − 1)²(x − 4) and two more.

- **Q:** List the features of y = (x + 2)(x − 1)²(x − 4).
  **A:** Degree 4, leading coefficient positive: both ends up. Crosses at −2 and 4, bounces at 1. y-intercept (2)(1)(−4) = −8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare functions
*Student:* Compare a polynomial given by graph to one given by equation (max, zeros, end behavior).

*How to teach:* Give one polynomial as a graph and one as an equation; student compares zeros, end behavior, and approximate maximum. Today's task from the plan: Compare a polynomial given by graph to one given by equation (max, zeros, end behavior). Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Which has more real zeros: f(x) = x³ − x, or a graph that crosses the x-axis only once?
  **A:** f(x) = x(x − 1)(x + 1) has three real zeros (−1, 0, 1), so f has more.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: sketch three polynomials and write equations from two graphs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 35 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz: sketch three polynomials and write equations from two graphs. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Write a cubic with zeros −3 (cross) and 2 (bounce) passing through (0, 12).
  **A:** y = a(x + 3)(x − 2)²; at x = 0: a·3·4 = 12, so a = 1: y = (x + 3)(x − 2)².

**Assessment:** Graphing quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: sketches graded on end behavior (1), zeros with correct cross/bounce (2), y-intercept (1). Sketch 1: y=x(x−3)² — starts low, crosses at 0, bounces at 3, ends high. Sketch 2: y=−(x+1)(x−2)(x−4) — up on left, down on right, crosses −1, 2, 4, y-int −8. Sketch 3: y=(x−1)³ — flattens and crosses at 1, y-int −1. Equations: graph with zeros −2, 1, 3 (all crossing) and y-int 6 → y=(x+2)(x−1)(x−3); quartic bouncing at ±2 with y-int 16 opening up → y=(x−2)²(x+2)².

## Physics of the Universe: Thermal energy and heat transfer

**Prepare:** Insulated cups (foam), thermometer, hot and cold water, food coloring, kitchen scale. Adult supervision with hot water.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing heat and temperature. Temperature measures average kinetic energy of particles; heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference.
- Thinking cold 'flows' into objects. Energy flows from hot to cold.

### Day 1 (Monday): Temperature & particles
*Student:* Compare food coloring diffusion in hot vs. cold water; model particle motion.

*How to teach:* Drop food coloring in hot and cold water simultaneously. Today's task from the plan: Compare food coloring diffusion in hot vs. cold water; model particle motion. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does coloring spread faster in hot water?
  **A:** Particles in hot water move faster (higher average kinetic energy).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Heat transfer
*Student:* Demonstrate conduction, convection, and radiation with household setups.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate conduction (metal spoon in hot water), convection (coloring in heated water), radiation (hand near a lamp). Today's task from the plan: Demonstrate conduction, convection, and radiation with household setups. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Which heat transfer method doesn't require matter?
  **A:** Radiation (electromagnetic waves).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Calorimetry lab
*Student:* Mix hot and cold water in insulated cups; graph temperatures over time to equilibrium.

*How to teach:* Mix hot and cold water in insulated cups; record temperature every 30 s until equilibrium. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** Mix 200 g at 80 °C with 100 g at 20 °C. Predict the final temperature.
  **A:** T = (200 × 80 + 100 × 20)/300 = 60 °C.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Specific heat
*Student:* Calculate specific heat using Q = mcΔT and compare sand vs. water heating.

*How to teach:* Model Q = mcΔT. Today's task from the plan: Calculate specific heat using Q = mcΔT and compare sand vs. water heating. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How much energy heats 0.50 kg of water from 20 °C to 80 °C?
  **A:** Q = 0.50 × 4,186 × 60 ≈ 126,000 J.
- **Q:** Why does sand heat faster than water in the sun?
  **A:** Sand's specific heat (≈ 830 J/kg°C) is about 1/5 that of water (4,186 J/kg°C).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Lab report showing evidence of energy uniformity.

*How to teach:* Lab report on energy uniformity. Today's task from the plan: Lab report showing evidence of energy uniformity. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What does reaching equilibrium show?
  **A:** Energy flowed until the temperatures were uniform.

**Assessment:** Calorimetry lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: data table and graph, final temperature compared to prediction, explanation of energy transfer, error sources (heat loss to cup/air).

## Spanish IV: Ética y tecnología: la inteligencia artificial

**Prepare:** Find a BBC Mundo (or similar) article on artificial intelligence in schools. Chart the imperfect subjunctive: ellos preterite minus -ron + -ra, -ras, -ra, -'ramos, -rais, -ran (hablaron → hablara; tuvieron → tuviera; fueron → fuera; pidieron → pidiera). Chart sequence of tenses: present/future/command main verb → present subjunctive; preterite/imperfect/conditional main verb → imperfect subjunctive.

**Watch for:**
- Building the imperfect subjunctive from the infinitive (*tenera, *hacera): it comes from the preterite ellos stem — tuviera, hiciera.
- Keeping the present subjunctive after a past trigger (*Quería que vengas): Quería que vinieras.
- Forgetting the accent on nosotros forms: habláramos, tuviéramos.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lectura
*Student:* Read a BBC Mundo article on artificial intelligence in schools; list three benefits and three risks in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Read the article; your child lists three benefits and three risks of AI in schools in Spanish.

- **Q:** Give one benefit and one risk of AI in schools.
  **A:** Example: Beneficio: puede ofrecer tutoría personalizada. Riesgo: los estudiantes pueden copiar sin aprender, o la IA puede dar información falsa.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Imperfecto del subjuntivo
*Student:* Form the imperfect subjunctive from the 3rd-person plural preterite (hablaran, tuvieran) and conjugate 20 verbs.

*How to teach:* Teach formation from the ellos preterite; your child conjugates 20 verbs.

- **Q:** Give the imperfect subjunctive yo form of ir, hacer, and estar.
  **A:** fuera, hiciera, estuviera
- **Q:** Give the nosotros form of poder.
  **A:** pudiéramos

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Secuencia de tiempos
*Student:* Transform 15 present-tense sentences (Quiero que...) into the past (Quería que...) and explain the rule in English.

*How to teach:* Your child transforms 15 sentences from present to past and explains the rule in English.

- **Q:** Transform: 'Mis padres quieren que yo use la IA con cuidado.'
  **A:** Mis padres querían que yo usara la IA con cuidado.
- **Q:** State the rule in English.
  **A:** When the main verb is in a past tense (or conditional), the subordinate subjunctive verb shifts to the imperfect subjunctive.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversación
*Student:* Hold a 10-minute discussion: ¿Debería la IA tomar decisiones médicas? using Era necesario que... and Me preocupaba que...

*How to teach:* Hold a 10-minute discussion: ¿Debería la IA tomar decisiones médicas? Your child must use Era necesario que... and Me preocupaba que...

- **Q:** Translate: 'I was worried that doctors would trust the machine too much.'
  **A:** Me preocupaba que los médicos confiaran demasiado en la máquina.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Quiz on imperfect subjunctive forms and sequence of tenses.

*How to teach:* Give the imperfect subjunctive quiz.

- **Q:** Correct: 'El profesor nos pidió que no usemos la IA en el examen.'
  **A:** El profesor nos pidió que no usáramos la IA en el examen.

**Assessment:** Imperfect subjunctive quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz sample key: 1) hablar/yo — hablara 2) comer/tú — comieras 3) tener/él — tuviera 4) ser/nosotros — fuéramos 5) decir/ellos — dijeran 6) poder/ustedes — pudieran 7) pedir/yo — pidiera 8) Quiero que estudies → Quería que estudiaras 9) Es necesario que la IA sea segura → Era necesario que la IA fuera segura 10) Me preocupa que reemplace a los maestros → Me preocupaba que reemplazara a los maestros.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Monologue performance and critique

**Prepare:** Set up a performance space and camera. Find a professional monologue video (e.g., a filmed stage production) to critique; preview. Invite an audience for Day 3.

**Watch for:**
- Slating in character. Slate as yourself: warm, confident, 'Hi, I'm ___, and I'll be performing ___ from ___ by ___.'
- Critique as only opinion. Use criteria (objective, voice, movement, truthfulness) and evidence.
- Apologizing after mistakes. Keep going; recovery is part of performance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Slating
*Student:* Learn to slate (name and piece) and practice audition etiquette.

*How to teach:* Teach slating and audition etiquette: walk in confidently, slate, take a moment, perform, hold a beat, say thank you.

- **Q:** What information goes in a slate?
  **A:** Your name, the monologue's title/character, the play, and the playwright.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Run the monologue twice with notes.

*How to teach:* Run the monologue twice; give and apply notes.

- **Q:** What should you do if you forget a line during performance?
  **A:** Stay in character, keep the objective, paraphrase or move to the next thought — don't apologize.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Performance
*Student:* Perform the monologue for an audience and record it.

*How to teach:* Perform for the audience; record.

- **Q:** Why take a moment before starting?
  **A:** To focus, step into the given circumstances and 'moment before,' and make the transition from self to character.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique
*Student:* Watch a professional monologue and write a critique using criteria.

*How to teach:* Watch a professional monologue and write a critique using criteria.

- **Q:** Name four criteria for evaluating a monologue.
  **A:** Clarity of objective and tactics, vocal expression and projection, purposeful movement/focus, emotional truthfulness/believability.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Self-assess performance video and set goals.

*How to teach:* Self-assess with the rubric; set two goals.

- **Q:** What is the difference between critique and criticism?
  **A:** Critique evaluates against criteria with evidence and balanced strengths/weaknesses; criticism is often just negative opinion.

**Assessment:** Monologue performance rubric.

**Answer key:** Monologue performance rubric (each 1–4): Slate/etiquette; Objective & tactics (clear want, at least 3 tactic shifts); Voice (projected, articulate, varied); Body (purposeful blocking, fixed focal point); Truthfulness/commitment. Proficient (3) = clear objective, audible and varied voice, motivated movement, off-book, confident slate.

## Lifetime Fitness: Designing a strength program

**Prepare:** Print a 4-week program template (exercise, sets, reps, load, rest, weekly progression). Gather dumbbells/jugs/bands. Review RPE and reps in reserve (RIR).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a split routine with too few days to hit each muscle group. With 3 days/week, full-body sessions are usually best for beginners.
- Picking loads that are far too easy or to failure every set. Aim for 2–3 reps in reserve.
- Changing exercises every session. Consistency is needed to measure progression.

### Day 1 (Monday): Program types
*Student:* Compare full-body vs. split routines and choose one; complete a 25-minute bodyweight circuit.

*How to teach:* Compare full-body (all major groups each session, 2–3 days/week) vs. split (e.g., upper/lower on different days, 4+ days). Student picks one and explains why. Do a 25-minute bodyweight circuit.

- **Q:** Why is a full-body routine good for someone training 3 days a week?
  **A:** Each muscle group is trained 3 times weekly with rest days between, giving good frequency and recovery.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Estimating load
*Student:* Learn reps in reserve (RIR); test loads for goblet squat, row, and press to find a weight leaving 2–3 reps in reserve.

*How to teach:* Teach RIR: after a set, how many more good reps could you have done? Test goblet squat, row, and press loads to find a weight leaving 2–3 RIR at 10 reps.

- **Q:** You do 10 reps and could have done 2 more. What is your RIR and approximate RPE?
  **A:** RIR 2 ≈ RPE 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design
*Student:* Write a 4-week, 3-day-per-week full-body program with sets, reps, rest, and weekly progression.

*How to teach:* Student writes a 4-week, 3-day full-body program: Workout A (squat, push, hinge, core) and B (lunge, pull, press, carry), alternating, with sets × reps, rest, and a progression rule (e.g., add 1–2 reps per week then increase load).

- **Q:** Give a simple progression rule for a 4-week program.
  **A:** Example (double progression): stay at the same load and add reps from 8 to 12; when all sets reach 12, increase the load and drop back to 8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Workout A
*Student:* Complete Workout A (squat, push, hinge, core) and log loads and RPE.

*How to teach:* Complete Workout A and log loads and RPE.

- **Q:** Why log RPE in addition to load?
  **A:** RPE shows how hard the load felt, helping decide when to progress or back off.

### Day 5 (Friday): Workout B & submit
*Student:* Complete Workout B (lunge, pull, press, carry) and submit the written program.

*How to teach:* Complete Workout B (including a farmer's carry with jugs) and submit the program.

- **Q:** What does a farmer's carry train?
  **A:** Grip strength, core stability, posture, and overall work capacity.

**Assessment:** Strength program design.

**Answer key:** Strength program rubric. 4 – 4 weeks, 3 days/week, alternates A/B covering squat, hinge, push, pull, lunge, press, core, carry; specifies sets, reps, rest, starting loads (2–3 RIR), clear weekly progression, warm-up and cool-down, safety notes. 3 (proficient) – All movement patterns, sets/reps/rest, and a progression. 2 – Missing patterns or progression. 1 – Incomplete.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Introduction to CAD

**Prepare:** Create free Tinkercad and Onshape Education accounts (parent may need to verify the education account). Work through the first Onshape Learning Center 'Introduction to Onshape' module yourself so you can help.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving sketches under-defined. In Onshape, blue lines are under-defined; black means fully constrained. Add dimensions and relations until all lines are black.
- Dimensioning everything instead of using geometric constraints. Use constraints (horizontal, vertical, equal, coincident, tangent) for relationships and dimensions for sizes.
- Building everything in one complicated sketch. Simpler sketches with several features are easier to edit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tinkercad warm-up
*Student:* Build a keychain in Tinkercad using primitive shapes and holes.

*How to teach:* In Tinkercad, drag a box and text, make a cylinder a 'hole' and group to cut the keyring hole. Discuss how this differs from professional CAD.

- **Q:** In Tinkercad, how do you cut a hole through a shape?
  **A:** Set a shape as a 'Hole', position it overlapping the solid, select both and Group.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Onshape setup
*Student:* Create an Onshape education account and complete the interface tutorial.

*How to teach:* Set up Onshape: create a document, find the Part Studio, feature list, sketch tools and view cube. Complete the interface tutorial.

- **Q:** What is parametric CAD?
  **A:** CAD where features are driven by dimensions and constraints, so changing a value automatically updates the model.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sketch & extrude
*Student:* Create constrained 2D sketches and extrude them into a block with holes.

*How to teach:* Sketch a rectangle with circles on the Top plane, dimension it, and extrude. Show that changing a dimension updates the 3D part.

- **Q:** What does 'extrude' do?
  **A:** Pushes a 2D sketch profile into a 3D solid (or removes material) to a given depth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Constraints
*Student:* Practice fully constraining sketches with dimensions and geometric relations.

*How to teach:* Practice fully constraining sketches: add horizontal/vertical, equal, coincident and dimension constraints until the sketch turns black.

- **Q:** What color shows an under-defined sketch entity in Onshape, and a fully defined one?
  **A:** Blue = under-defined; black = fully defined.
- **Q:** Why fully constrain a sketch?
  **A:** So geometry can't accidentally move, and changes to dimensions update the model predictably.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Model a dimensioned block part from Unit 2 in Onshape.

*How to teach:* Model a dimensioned block part from Unit 2 in Onshape and check it with the Measure tool against the drawing.

- **Q:** How can you verify your model matches the drawing?
  **A:** Use the Measure tool (or inspect dimensions) to check key distances and hole sizes against the drawing.

**Assessment:** CAD part check.

**Answer key:** CAD part check (score each 0–2; 8–10 = mastery): 1) sketch on the correct plane; 2) sketch fully constrained (all black); 3) dimensions match the Unit 2 drawing; 4) extrude depth correct; 5) holes correctly sized and located (use 'Measure' to verify). Tinkercad keychain: complete if it has a hole for the ring and is a single connected object.
