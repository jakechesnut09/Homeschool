# Teacher's Guide — 11th Grade, Week 14

## English 11 – American Literature: Oratory of the Civil War era

**Prepare:** Douglass' 'What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?'; Sojourner Truth's 1851 speech (Robinson version) and 1863 Gage version; Lincoln's Gettysburg Address and Second Inaugural. Phone for video.

**Watch for:**
- Quoting 'Ain't I a Woman?' as Truth's exact words. The most-quoted version was published by Frances Gage 12 years later with added Southern dialect; the 1851 report lacks that refrain.
- Reading the Second Inaugural as a victory speech. Lincoln emphasizes shared guilt, humility, and reconciliation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fourth of July speech
*Student:* Read the central section; chart how Douglass turns the audience's celebration into an indictment.

*How to teach:* Read the central section of Douglass's speech. The student charts how he moves from praising the founders to indicting the nation. Today's task from the plan: Read the central section; chart how Douglass turns the audience's celebration into an indictment. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What answer does Douglass give to 'What, to the American slave, is your 4th of July?'
  **A:** 'A day that reveals to him... the gross injustice and cruelty to which he is the constant victim'; the celebration is a 'sham.'
- **Q:** Why does Douglass say 'This Fourth of July is yours, not mine'?
  **A:** Freedom celebrated by white Americans excludes enslaved people; he forces the audience to see the hypocrisy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sojourner Truth
*Student:* Compare the 1851 Robinson transcription with the 1863 Gage version; discuss how editing changed her voice.

*How to teach:* Read both Truth versions side by side. The student lists differences and discusses how editing shapes voice. Today's task from the plan: Compare the 1851 Robinson transcription with the 1863 Gage version; discuss how editing changed her voice. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Name two ways Gage's 1863 version differs from the 1851 report.
  **A:** Gage added the repeated refrain 'Ain't I a woman?' and a heavy Southern dialect, though Truth was from New York and spoke Dutch first.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lincoln
*Student:* Read the Gettysburg Address and Second Inaugural; mark antithesis and biblical allusion and explain the shift in purpose.

*How to teach:* Read both Lincoln texts. The student marks antithesis and biblical allusion and explains the shift in purpose. Today's task from the plan: Read the Gettysburg Address and Second Inaugural; mark antithesis and biblical allusion and explain the shift in purpose. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find an antithesis in the Second Inaugural.
  **A:** 'Both read the same Bible and pray to the same God; and each invokes His aid against the other.'
- **Q:** How do the purposes differ?
  **A:** Gettysburg honors the dead and asks the living to finish the work so government 'of the people, by the people, for the people' survives; the Second Inaugural frames the war as judgment for slavery and calls for reconciliation 'with malice toward none.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Deliver a speech
*Student:* Memorize and deliver the Gettysburg Address or a 2-minute excerpt with deliberate pauses; self-evaluate from video.

*How to teach:* The student memorizes and delivers the Gettysburg Address or a 2-minute excerpt with deliberate pauses; review the video. Today's task from the plan: Memorize and deliver the Gettysburg Address or a 2-minute excerpt with deliberate pauses; self-evaluate from video. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Where should the speaker pause in 'we can not dedicate—we can not consecrate—we can not hallow—this ground'?
  **A:** At each dash, so the three parallel verbs build to 'this ground.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Timed rhetorical analysis (40 min) of the Second Inaugural.

*How to teach:* Timed 40-minute rhetorical analysis of the Second Inaugural. Today's task from the plan: Timed rhetorical analysis (40 min) of the Second Inaugural. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What is the purpose of 'With malice toward none, with charity for all'?
  **A:** To call the nation to reconcile and 'bind up the nation's wounds' rather than punish the South.

**Assessment:** Speech delivery rubric and timed rhetorical analysis.

**Answer key:** Delivery rubric (4-point): memorization, pacing and pauses, volume/clarity, eye contact. Timed analysis rubric: proficient essay explains Lincoln's purpose (reconciliation and moral reckoning) and analyzes antithesis, biblical allusion ('judge not, that we be not judged'), and the long sentence on 'every drop of blood drawn with the lash' with effect on a war-weary audience.

## Algebra II: Rational functions and graphs

**Prepare:** Desmos. Look up Boyle's law (pressure × volume constant at fixed temperature).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a graph can never cross a horizontal asymptote. Horizontal asymptotes describe end behavior only; graph y = x/(x² + 1) to see it cross y = 0.
- Calling every zero of the denominator a vertical asymptote. If the factor cancels, it is a hole.

### Day 1 (Monday): Inverse variation
*Student:* Model Boyle's law-type inverse variation and graph y = k/x.

*How to teach:* Introduce y = k/x. Model finding k from one data point. Graph and discuss why y never reaches 0. Today's task from the plan: Model Boyle's law-type inverse variation and graph y = k/x. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** y varies inversely with x and y = 6 when x = 4. Find y when x = 8.
  **A:** k = 24, y = 24/8 = 3.
- **Q:** A gas at 2 atm occupies 6 L. At constant temperature, what is its volume at 3 atm?
  **A:** P₁V₁ = P₂V₂: 12 = 3V, V = 4 L.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transformations of 1/x
*Student:* Graph y = a/(x−h)+k and identify asymptotes.

*How to teach:* Treat y = a/(x − h) + k like any transformation: asymptotes x = h and y = k. Today's task from the plan: Graph y = a/(x−h)+k and identify asymptotes. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give the asymptotes of y = 3/(x + 2) − 1.
  **A:** Vertical x = −2; horizontal y = −1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): General rational functions
*Student:* Find vertical asymptotes, holes, and horizontal asymptotes from factored forms.

*How to teach:* Model factoring, finding holes (cancelled factors), vertical asymptotes (remaining denominator zeros), and horizontal asymptote by comparing degrees. Today's task from the plan: Find vertical asymptotes, holes, and horizontal asymptotes from factored forms. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find holes and asymptotes of f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x² − x − 2).
  **A:** (x − 1)(x + 1)/((x − 2)(x + 1)): hole at x = −1 (point (−1, 2/3)), vertical asymptote x = 2, horizontal asymptote y = 1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rewriting
*Student:* Use long division to rewrite (2x+3)/(x−1) as 2 + 5/(x−1) and interpret the graph.

*How to teach:* Model dividing (2x + 3) by (x − 1) to get 2 + 5/(x − 1), and read the graph as 5/x shifted right 1, up 2. Today's task from the plan: Use long division to rewrite (2x+3)/(x−1) as 2 + 5/(x−1) and interpret the graph. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Rewrite (2x + 3)/(x − 1) and name its asymptotes.
  **A:** 2 + 5/(x − 1) (since 2(x − 1) + 5 = 2x + 3). Vertical x = 1, horizontal y = 2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: graph three rational functions with all features labeled.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz: graph three rational functions with all features labeled (about 35 minutes). Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What is the horizontal asymptote of y = 3x/(x² + 4)?
  **A:** Degree of denominator is greater, so y = 0.

**Assessment:** Rational graphs quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: 1) y=1/(x−3)+2: VA x=3, HA y=2, y-int 5/3, x-int 5/2. 2) y=(x+1)/(x−2): VA x=2, HA y=1, x-int −1, y-int −1/2. 3) y=(x²−4)/(x−2): hole at (2, 4), otherwise the line y = x+2, no asymptotes. Each graph: 1 pt each for VA, HA/hole, intercepts, correct branches.

## Physics of the Universe: Electric circuits

**Prepare:** AA batteries with holders, small bulbs (or LEDs with resistors), alligator-clip wires; PhET Circuit Construction Kit: DC. Photos of labels from three home appliances (watts). A recent electric bill for the rate per kWh.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking current is 'used up' by a bulb. Current is the same everywhere in a series loop; what the bulb uses is energy.
- Thinking adding branches in parallel increases resistance. Each new path lowers total resistance, so total current increases.

### Day 1 (Monday): Circuits
*Student:* Build simple circuits with batteries and bulbs; identify parts.

*How to teach:* Build a one-bulb circuit and name parts (source, conductor, load, switch). Try an open circuit and a short circuit (briefly) to define each. Today's task from the plan: Build simple circuits with batteries and bulbs; identify parts. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What does a circuit need for current to flow?
  **A:** A complete, closed conducting loop and a source of potential difference (voltage).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ohm's law
*Student:* Use PhET Circuit Construction Kit to measure V, I, R and derive Ohm's law.

*How to teach:* In PhET, hold resistance constant and vary voltage, recording current; graph V vs I. Today's task from the plan: Use PhET Circuit Construction Kit to measure V, I, R and derive Ohm's law. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A 9.0 V battery is connected across a 3.0 Ω resistor. What current flows?
  **A:** I = V/R = 9.0/3.0 = 3.0 A.
- **Q:** What does the slope of the V vs I graph equal?
  **A:** The resistance R.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Series & parallel
*Student:* Build series and parallel circuits; compare bulb brightness.

*How to teach:* Build two bulbs in series, then in parallel; compare brightness and what happens when one bulb is removed. Today's task from the plan: Build series and parallel circuits; compare bulb brightness. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Which arrangement makes two identical bulbs brighter, and why?
  **A:** Parallel: each bulb gets the full battery voltage; in series they share it.
- **Q:** Find the total resistance of 6 Ω and 3 Ω in parallel.
  **A:** 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2, R = 2 Ω.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Power
*Student:* Calculate electrical power and energy cost of home appliances from labels.

*How to teach:* Model P = VI and energy = P × t in kWh. The student finds monthly costs for three appliances. Today's task from the plan: Calculate electrical power and energy cost of home appliances from labels. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A 1,500 W hair dryer runs 30 minutes a day for 30 days. At $0.30/kWh, what does it cost?
  **A:** 1.5 kW × 15 h = 22.5 kWh; 22.5 × $0.30 = $6.75.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on circuit analysis.

*How to teach:* Circuits quiz (30 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on circuit analysis. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** A 4 Ω and a 6 Ω resistor are in series with 12 V. Find the current and the voltage across the 6 Ω resistor.
  **A:** R = 10 Ω, I = 1.2 A; V = 1.2 × 6 = 7.2 V.

**Assessment:** Circuits quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (sample): 1) 12 V across 4 Ω → 3 A. 2) series 2 Ω + 8 Ω = 10 Ω. 3) parallel 4 Ω and 4 Ω = 2 Ω. 4) power of 120 V, 0.5 A lamp = 60 W. 5) 100 W bulb for 10 h = 1 kWh. 6) in series, current is the same; in parallel, voltage is the same. 7) why home outlets are wired in parallel (each device gets full voltage, independent switching).

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: Human toll: Dust Bowl and migration to California

**Prepare:** Print Dorothea Lange photographs (Migrant Mother, 1936, Nipomo, California; others from Calisphere/LOC), Woody Guthrie's 'Dust Bowl Blues' lyrics, excerpts of Steinbeck's The Grapes of Wrath (1939), and a reading on Mexican repatriation (including California's 2005 Apology Act).

**Watch for:**
- Students think the Dust Bowl was purely natural. Drought combined with plowing up native grasslands (wheat boom of the 1910s–20s) left soil exposed to wind.
- Students think 'Okies' were welcomed in California. Many faced hostility, low wages, migrant camps and a 1936 Los Angeles 'bum blockade' at the state border.
- Students think repatriation was voluntary emigration. Many Mexicans and Mexican Americans, including U.S. citizens, were deported or pressured to leave.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dust Bowl
*Student:* Study causes of the Dust Bowl and farming practices.

*How to teach:* Study causes of the Dust Bowl and farming practices.

- **Q:** How did farming practices contribute to the Dust Bowl?
  **A:** Farmers plowed up deep-rooted prairie grasses to plant wheat; when drought came, the loose topsoil blew away in massive dust storms.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Migrant Mother
*Student:* Analyze Dorothea Lange's photographs of migrants in California.

*How to teach:* Analyze Lange's photographs of migrants in California.

- **Q:** What does Migrant Mother show and why is it powerful?
  **A:** Florence Owens Thompson, a pea picker in Nipomo, California, with her children; her worried face became a symbol of Depression suffering.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Music
*Student:* Analyze Woody Guthrie's 'Dust Bowl Blues' lyrics.

*How to teach:* Analyze Woody Guthrie's 'Dust Bowl Blues' lyrics.

- **Q:** What does Guthrie's music reveal about Dust Bowl migrants?
  **A:** Their losses, hardship on the road and in California, and resilience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Repatriation
*Student:* Learn about Mexican repatriation in the 1930s.

*How to teach:* Learn about Mexican repatriation in the 1930s.

- **Q:** Why were Mexicans and Mexican Americans targeted?
  **A:** Officials blamed them for taking jobs and using relief during high unemployment; raids and pressure drove hundreds of thousands to Mexico.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short essay on the human toll in California.

*How to teach:* The student writes a short essay on the human toll in California.

- **Q:** What did California's 2005 Apology Act do?
  **A:** It formally apologized for the state's role in the unconstitutional removal of Mexicans and Mexican Americans during the 1930s.

**Assessment:** Essay.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric: 4 = thesis on the human toll in California; evidence from at least three sources (Lange's photos of migrant families; Guthrie's lyrics; Steinbeck; data — about 300,000–400,000 Dust Bowl-era migrants to California; estimates of 400,000 to over a million people repatriated to Mexico nationally, many of them U.S. citizens); analysis of discrimination and labor conditions; conclusion on significance. 3 = two sources. 2 = general. 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish IV: Arte latinoamericano: los muralistas y Frida Kahlo

**Prepare:** Gather images of Diego Rivera murals (Detroit Industry, 1932–33, Detroit Institute of Arts; Pan American Unity, 1940, City College of San Francisco), works by Orozco and Siqueiros, Frida Kahlo's Las dos Fridas (1939), and California murals (Mission District's Balmy Alley and the Women's Building; Judy Baca's Great Wall of Los Angeles, which is in the San Fernando Valley, and East LA murals such as those at Estrada Courts). Chart relative pronouns: que, quien(es) (people, after prepositions or commas), lo que (idea/what), el cual/la cual/los cuales/las cuales (after longer prepositions or for clarity).

**Watch for:**
- Using 'quien' as a general 'who' without a preposition or comma (*El pintor quien...): El pintor que pintó el mural...; El pintor con quien hablé...
- Using 'que' for 'what' at the start of a statement (*Que me gusta es...): Lo que me gusta es...
- Assuming muralism was only decoration: post-Revolution murals were political and educational, celebrating Indigenous history and workers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Muralismo
*Student:* Study Diego Rivera, Orozco, and Siqueiros and the purpose of Mexican muralism after the Revolution.

*How to teach:* Study los tres grandes (Rivera, Orozco, Siqueiros) and the purpose of muralism after the Mexican Revolution.

- **Q:** Why did the Mexican government sponsor murals in the 1920s?
  **A:** After the Revolution (1910–1920), the government (led in education by José Vasconcelos) used public murals to teach history, build national identity, and honor Indigenous people and workers, reaching people who could not read.
- **Q:** Name los tres grandes.
  **A:** Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco, David Alfaro Siqueiros.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pronombres relativos
*Student:* Practice que, quien, lo que, and el cual in 20 sentences describing murals.

*How to teach:* Teach relative pronouns; your child writes 20 sentences describing murals.

- **Q:** Fill in: 'El muralista con ___ hablamos vive en Oakland.'
  **A:** quien
- **Q:** Fill in: '___ más me impresiona es el color.'
  **A:** Lo que

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Frida Kahlo
*Student:* Analyze Las dos Fridas and discuss how Kahlo represents identity.

*How to teach:* Analyze Las dos Fridas; discuss how Kahlo represents identity.

- **Q:** Describe Las dos Fridas.
  **A:** Two Fridas sit holding hands: one in a white European-style dress, one in a Tehuana (Mexican) dress; their exposed hearts are connected by a vein; the European Frida holds surgical scissors with blood dripping, and the Mexican Frida holds a small portrait of Diego.
- **Q:** What might the painting express?
  **A:** Her dual European and Mexican heritage and the pain of her divorce from Diego Rivera (1939).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Murales en California
*Student:* Explore murals in San Francisco's Mission District, East LA, or Judy Baca's Great Wall of Los Angeles in the San Fernando Valley online and describe one.

*How to teach:* Explore California murals online; your child describes one with relative pronouns.

- **Q:** What is the Great Wall of Los Angeles?
  **A:** A half-mile-long mural directed by Judy Baca, painted with youth from 1976 onward in the Tujunga Wash, showing California history from the perspective of people of color and women.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Design and describe your own mural (sketch plus 150-word explanation in Spanish).

*How to teach:* Your child designs a mural (sketch) and writes a 150-word explanation.

- **Q:** Translate: 'The mural, which shows my neighborhood, is what I am proudest of.'
  **A:** El mural, el cual (que) muestra mi barrio, es lo que más me enorgullece.

**Assessment:** Mural design and explanation.

**Answer key:** Mural design rubric (sketch + 150 words): 4 = clear theme tied to community or identity, explains symbols and colors, 4+ correct relative pronouns, purpose stated, accurate grammar; 3 = minor errors; 2 = vague explanation; 1 = incomplete.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Shakespeare: language and verse

**Prepare:** Get the Folger Shakespeare text of A Midsummer Night's Dream (free at folger.edu). Choose a short speech (e.g., Puck's 'If we shadows have offended' or Helena's 'How happy some o'er other some can be'). Find images or a virtual tour of the Globe.

**Watch for:**
- Pausing at the end of every line. Follow punctuation and thought; enjambed lines run on.
- Thinking Shakespeare's language is 'Old English.' It is Early Modern English.
- Stressing every syllable evenly. Iambic pentameter is da-DUM ×5; stress falls on the important words.

### Day 1 (Monday): Globe
*Student:* Explore the Globe Theatre and Elizabethan staging conventions.

*How to teach:* Explore the Globe (built 1599): open-air, thrust stage, groundlings standing in the yard, daylight performances, minimal sets, boys playing women.

- **Q:** Who were the groundlings?
  **A:** Audience members who paid a penny to stand in the yard around the stage.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Verse
*Student:* Scan lines of iambic pentameter and speak them aloud.

*How to teach:* Teach iambic pentameter: five iambs (unstressed-stressed). Scan 'The course of true love never did run smooth' (1.1). Speak lines aloud, tapping stress.

- **Q:** Scan 'The course of true love never did run smooth.'
  **A:** the COURSE / of TRUE / love NEV / er DID / run SMOOTH — five iambs, 10 syllables.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Text work
*Student:* Paraphrase a Shakespeare speech and identify operative words.

*How to teach:* Student paraphrases the chosen speech line by line, then circles operative words.

- **Q:** Paraphrase Puck's 'If we shadows have offended, / Think but this, and all is mended, / That you have but slumber'd here / While these visions did appear.'
  **A:** If we actors displeased you, just imagine you were asleep and dreamed all this, and everything will be fine.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Insults & sonnets
*Student:* Perform a sonnet with attention to rhythm.

*How to teach:* Perform a sonnet (e.g., Sonnet 18) focusing on rhythm and the turn. Optionally play with Shakespearean insults for verbal energy.

- **Q:** What is the structure of a Shakespearean sonnet?
  **A:** 14 lines of iambic pentameter: three quatrains and a final couplet, rhymed ABAB CDCD EFEF GG.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a short Shakespeare speech.

*How to teach:* Student performs the speech from memory.

- **Q:** Why do some characters speak in prose and others in verse?
  **A:** Verse is often used by nobles and for heightened emotion; prose by lower-class or comic characters (e.g., the mechanicals mostly speak prose).

**Assessment:** Shakespeare speech.

**Answer key:** Shakespeare speech rubric. 4 – Memorized, meaning clear (paraphrase evident), rhythm felt without sing-song, operative words stressed, breath at punctuation, physical and vocal commitment. 3 (proficient) – Memorized, clear meaning, audible, mostly natural rhythm. 2 – Meaning unclear or several prompts. 1 – Not ready.

## Lifetime Fitness: Yoga foundations

**Prepare:** Gather a mat and choose a free beginner yoga video (e.g., Yoga with Adriene's beginner sessions); preview it. Prepare a stress rating (1–10) sheet for before/after.

**Watch for:**
- Forcing poses to look like a photo. Use props and modifications; alignment and breathing matter more than depth.
- Holding the breath in difficult poses. Breath should stay smooth; if it can't, ease out.
- Locking (hyperextending) knees in standing poses. Keep a micro-bend.

### Day 1 (Monday): Breathing
*Student:* Practice diaphragmatic and box breathing; journal stress level before and after.

*How to teach:* Teach diaphragmatic breathing (hand on belly rises on inhale) and box breathing (inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4). Practice 5 minutes; rate stress before and after in the journal.

- **Q:** Describe box breathing.
  **A:** Inhale for 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4; repeat.
- **Q:** How does slow breathing reduce stress?
  **A:** Slow exhalation activates the parasympathetic ('rest and digest') nervous system, lowering heart rate and arousal.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Standing poses
*Student:* Learn mountain, warrior I and II, triangle, and tree pose with alignment cues.

*How to teach:* Teach mountain, warrior I, warrior II, triangle, and tree with cues: front knee over ankle in warriors, hips square in warrior I and open in warrior II, foot on calf or thigh (never on the knee) in tree.

- **Q:** Where should the lifted foot go in tree pose?
  **A:** On the inner calf or inner thigh — never pressing on the side of the knee.
- **Q:** What is a key difference between warrior I and warrior II?
  **A:** Warrior I hips face forward with arms overhead; warrior II hips open to the side with arms extended parallel to the floor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sun salutation
*Student:* Learn sun salutation A and complete five rounds.

*How to teach:* Teach sun salutation A step by step (mountain, upward salute, forward fold, half lift, plank/chaturanga, upward dog or cobra, downward dog, step forward, fold, rise), linking breath; do five rounds.

- **Q:** What does 'vinyasa' mean in yoga practice?
  **A:** Linking breath with movement in a flowing sequence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Full practice
*Student:* Complete a 40-minute guided yoga practice.

*How to teach:* Follow a 40-minute guided practice. Afterwards discuss which poses were hardest and why.

- **Q:** Name a benefit of regular yoga.
  **A:** Improved flexibility, balance, and strength, plus reduced stress and better breath control.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Demonstrate a 10-pose sequence with correct alignment and reflect on stress reduction.

*How to teach:* Student performs a 10-pose sequence while you check alignment, then writes a stress reflection.

- **Q:** What is an alignment cue for downward dog?
  **A:** Hands shoulder-width, push the floor away, hips lift up and back, long spine; heels reach toward the floor (bent knees are fine).

**Assessment:** Pose sequence demonstration.

**Answer key:** Pose sequence demonstration rubric. 4 – 10 poses flow smoothly with breath, correct alignment cues in each (e.g., knee over ankle, foot off knee in tree), modifications used appropriately, thoughtful reflection on stress change using before/after ratings. 3 (proficient) – 10 poses with mostly correct alignment and steady breath; reflection included. 2 – Several alignment errors or missing poses. 1 – Incomplete.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Assemblies

**Prepare:** Preview the Onshape Assembly tutorial (Fastened, Revolute and Slider mates). Have simple parts ready (two hinge leaves and a pin; car body, two axles, wheels).

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to fix (ground) a base part, so the whole assembly floats.
- Choosing the wrong mate type. A hinge needs a revolute mate (1 rotational DOF), not fastened.
- Ignoring interference. Overlapping solids mean the real parts wouldn't fit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Assembly basics
*Student:* Insert parts into an Onshape assembly and fix the base part.

*How to teach:* Insert parts into an assembly and fix the base part.

- **Q:** Why fix one part in an assembly?
  **A:** It gives the assembly a stationary reference; other parts are mated relative to it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mates
*Student:* Apply fastened, revolute, and slider mates to model a simple hinge.

*How to teach:* Model a hinge: fasten one leaf, use a revolute mate for the pin, and a revolute mate for the moving leaf.

- **Q:** Which mate allows rotation about one axis only?
  **A:** Revolute.
- **Q:** Which mate allows sliding along one direction only?
  **A:** Slider.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Degrees of freedom
*Student:* Analyze remaining degrees of freedom and animate the mechanism.

*How to teach:* Explain degrees of freedom: an unconstrained part has 6 (3 translations, 3 rotations). Animate the hinge to show its 1 rotational DOF.

- **Q:** How many degrees of freedom does a free rigid body have?
  **A:** Six: translation along x, y, z and rotation about x, y, z.
- **Q:** How many DOF does a door on a hinge have?
  **A:** One (rotation about the hinge axis).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interference
*Student:* Check for interference and fix problems.

*How to teach:* Run an interference check; fix any overlaps by adjusting dimensions or clearances.

- **Q:** Why add clearance between a pin and its hole?
  **A:** So the parts can actually assemble and move; real parts vary in size (tolerance).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Build a 4-part assembly (e.g., a toy car with axles and wheels).

*How to teach:* Build the 4-part toy car assembly; score with the checklist.

- **Q:** Which mate would you use for a wheel spinning on an axle?
  **A:** Revolute (or fasten the wheel to an axle that has a revolute mate to the body).

**Assessment:** Assembly check.

**Answer key:** Assembly check (4-part toy car): 4 = base part fixed; axles mated to the body with revolute or cylindrical mates; wheels fastened or revolute to axles; no interference; mechanism animates (wheels spin) without parts drifting; 3 = works with one unnecessary DOF; 2 = interference or missing mates; 1 = parts not mated. Written: A free body has 6 DOF (3 translations, 3 rotations); revolute mate leaves 1 DOF; slider leaves 1 DOF; fastened leaves 0.
