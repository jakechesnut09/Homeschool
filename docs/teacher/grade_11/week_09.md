## English 11 – American Literature: Transcendentalism: Emerson and Thoreau

**Prepare:** Emerson's 'Self-Reliance' and 'Nature' (ch. 1) and Thoreau's Walden ch. 2 and 'Civil Disobedience' excerpts (Gutenberg). Plan a safe place for a solo 20-minute nature walk.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Transcendentalism means rejecting all society. Emerson and Thoreau valued intuition and nature but also engaged in public debate (lectures, abolition).
- Assuming civil disobedience means any lawbreaking. Thoreau's idea is open, nonviolent refusal to obey an unjust law and accepting the penalty.

### Day 1 (Monday): Emerson
*Student:* Read excerpts of 'Self-Reliance'; select five aphorisms and explain each in plain language.

*How to teach:* Read 'Self-Reliance' excerpts. The student chooses five aphorisms and explains each in plain language. Today's task from the plan: Read excerpts of 'Self-Reliance'; select five aphorisms and explain each in plain language. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Explain 'A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds.'
  **A:** Refusing to change your views just to look consistent is a small-minded fear; a thinking person should follow what they now believe even if it contradicts what they said before.
- **Q:** Explain 'Whoso would be a man must be a nonconformist.'
  **A:** To be a full person you must think for yourself instead of following society's expectations.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Nature

Today's task from the plan: Read the 'transparent eyeball' passage from 'Nature'; take a 20-minute solo nature walk and write a journal entry in imitation. - **Q:** What does Emerson mean by 'I become a transparent eye-ball; I am nothing; I see all'?
  **A:** In nature his ego disappears; he becomes pure perception and feels united with the divine ('part or particle of God').

## Algebra II: Dividing polynomials and the Remainder Theorem

**Prepare:** Practice set of division problems. Remind the student of numerical long division (divide, multiply, subtract, bring down).

**Watch for:**
- Leaving out missing terms (e.g., dividing x³ − 7x + 6 without a 0x² placeholder). Always write every power with zeros as placeholders.
- Using +3 instead of −3 in synthetic division for the divisor x + 3. The number in the box is the zero of the divisor: x + 3 = 0 → −3.

### Day 1 (Monday): Long division
*Student:* Divide polynomials by binomials using long division, connecting to numerical long division.

*How to teach:* Model (x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6) ÷ (x − 2) side by side with 1,452 ÷ 12. Student does five long divisions. Today's task from the plan: Divide polynomials by binomials using long division, connecting to numerical long division. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Divide (2x² + 5x − 1) ÷ (x + 3).
  **A:** 2x − 1 with remainder 2: (2x² + 5x − 1) = (x + 3)(2x − 1) + 2.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Synthetic division
*Student:* Use synthetic division for divisors of the form x−a; compare speed.

*How to teach:* Model synthetic division for the same problem as Day 1, comparing time. Student does eight synthetic divisions. Today's task from the plan: Use synthetic division for divisors of the form x−a; compare speed. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Divide (x³ − 7x + 6) ÷ (x + 3) synthetically.
  **A:** Use −3 with coefficients 1, 0, −7, 6: bottom row 1, −3, 2, 0 → x² − 3x + 2, remainder 0.
- **Q:** Divide (x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6) ÷ (x − 2).
  **A:** Bottom row 1, 4, 3, 0 → x² + 4x + 3, remainder 0.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Remainder Theorem
*Student:* Verify that p(a) equals the remainder; use it to evaluate p(3) quickly.

*How to teach:* Have the student compute p(3) directly and by synthetic division with 3; they match. State the Remainder Theorem. Today's task from the plan: Verify that p(a) equals the remainder; use it to evaluate p(3) quickly. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** For p(x) = x³ − 4x² + x + 5, find p(3) and the remainder of p(x) ÷ (x − 3).
  **A:** p(3) = 27 − 36 + 3 + 5 = −1, and the remainder is also −1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Factor Theorem
*Student:* Determine whether x−a is a factor and fully factor cubics given one root.

*How to teach:* Remainder 0 means x − a is a factor. Model factoring x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 given root 1. Student factors four cubics. Today's task from the plan: Determine whether x−a is a factor and fully factor cubics given one root. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Factor x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 given that x = 1 is a zero.
  **A:** Synthetic with 1 gives x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3), so (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3).
- **Q:** Is x + 1 a factor of x³ + 1?
  **A:** Yes: p(−1) = −1 + 1 = 0.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on division methods and theorems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 30 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on division methods and theorems. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Find the remainder when x⁴ − 3x + 2 is divided by x − 2.
  **A:** p(2) = 16 − 6 + 2 = 12.

**Assessment:** Division quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: 1) (x²+7x+10)÷(x+2) = x+5. 2) (3x³−2x²+4)÷(x−1) = 3x²+x+1 R5. 3) (x³−8)÷(x−2) = x²+2x+4. 4) remainder of x³+2x−1 ÷ (x+2) = −13. 5) Is x−4 a factor of x³−3x²−6x+8? p(4)=64−48−24+8=0, yes; = (x−4)(x²+x−2) = (x−4)(x+2)(x−1). 6) Factor 2x³+x²−13x+6 given x=2: (x−2)(2x²+5x−3) = (x−2)(2x−1)(x+3).

## Physics of the Universe: Work, energy, and power

**Prepare:** Stairs (measure height of each step), stopwatch, bathroom scale. Remind the student to run safely.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking carrying a box across a room at constant height does work against gravity. Work requires force in the direction of displacement.
- Confusing power with energy. Power is the rate (energy per time).

### Day 1 (Monday): Work
*Student:* Calculate work done lifting and pushing objects.

*How to teach:* Model W = Fd cos θ. The student calculates work lifting and pushing. Today's task from the plan: Calculate work done lifting and pushing objects. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How much work to lift a 10 kg box 2.0 m?
  **A:** W = mgh = 10 × 9.8 × 2.0 = 196 J.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Kinetic & potential energy
*Student:* Calculate KE and PE for a roller coaster; relate to particle motion and fields.

*How to teach:* Model KE and PE for a coaster. Today's task from the plan: Calculate KE and PE for a roller coaster; relate to particle motion and fields. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A 1,000 kg coaster moves at 20 m/s. Find its KE.
  **A:** KE = ½(1,000)(20²) = 200,000 J.
- **Q:** What is the PE of a 500 kg car at the top of a 30 m hill?
  **A:** PE = 500 × 9.8 × 30 = 147,000 J.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stair power lab
*Student:* Run up stairs timed; calculate personal power output in watts and horsepower.

*How to teach:* Measure stair height, weigh the student, time running up. P = mgh/t. Today's task from the plan: Run up stairs timed; calculate personal power output in watts and horsepower. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A 60 kg student climbs 3.0 m in 2.0 s. Power?
  **A:** P = 60 × 9.8 × 3.0/2.0 = 882 W ≈ 1.2 hp.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Energy bar charts
*Student:* Draw energy bar charts for skier, pendulum, and spring scenarios.

*How to teach:* Draw energy bar charts for skier, pendulum, spring. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** At the lowest point of a pendulum's swing, which energy is maximum?
  **A:** Kinetic energy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on energy calculations.

*How to teach:* Energy quiz. Today's task from the plan: Quiz on energy calculations. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** A 2 kg ball is dropped from 5 m. What is its speed just before hitting the ground (ignore air resistance)?
  **A:** v = √(2gh) = √98 ≈ 9.9 m/s.

**Assessment:** Stair power lab and quiz.

**Answer key:** Stair lab rubric: measurements with units, correct calculation, comparison to 746 W/hp, error sources. Quiz key (sample): work 50 N × 4 m = 200 J; KE of 0.5 kg at 6 m/s = 9 J; PE of 3 kg at 10 m = 294 J; power 1,200 J in 4 s = 300 W.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Choosing and analyzing a monologue

**Prepare:** Borrow monologue books from the library (contemporary and classical) and check Drama Notebook lists. Pre-screen choices for age-appropriate content. The student must be able to get the full play the monologue comes from.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a monologue for length or 'drama' without reading the full play. Context determines the objective.
- Memorizing with a flat line reading. Score first, then memorize with intention.
- Treating a monologue as talking to the audience. Most monologues speak to a specific person (imaginary partner).

### Day 1 (Monday): Selection
*Student:* Read 10 age-appropriate monologues and choose a contemporary or classical piece.

*How to teach:* Read 10 monologues; shortlist 3; choose one (1–2 minutes) suited to age and type. Explain the choice.

- **Q:** What makes a good audition monologue choice?
  **A:** Age-appropriate, close to the actor's type, 1–2 minutes, a clear objective and change, from a published play the actor has read.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read the play
*Student:* Read the full play the monologue comes from and write the given circumstances.

*How to teach:* Read the full play; write the given circumstances and the moment in the story.

- **Q:** Why read the whole play?
  **A:** To understand the character's relationships, history, and objective so the choices are accurate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scoring
*Student:* Score the monologue with beats, tactics, operative words, and pauses.

*How to teach:* Score: mark beats, tactics (active verbs), operative words (key words to stress), and pauses.

- **Q:** What is an operative word?
  **A:** The most important word in a line, which carries meaning and is emphasized.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Memorization
*Student:* Memorize using chunking and walking rehearsal.

*How to teach:* Memorize by chunking (one thought at a time) and walking rehearsal (moving while repeating).

- **Q:** Name two memorization strategies.
  **A:** Chunking by thoughts/beats, writing out lines, recording and listening, walking/moving while speaking, first-letter cue sheets.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform off-book for family and receive feedback.

*How to teach:* Student performs off-book for family; feedback uses 'one strength, one note.'

- **Q:** What is subtext?
  **A:** The unspoken thoughts and meaning beneath the lines.

**Assessment:** Monologue progress check.

**Answer key:** Monologue progress check: proficient = fully off-book (≤2 prompts), clear objective, scored script with beats/tactics/operative words, given circumstances written. 4 adds varied tactics and a clear change; 2 = several prompts or unscored; 1 = not memorized.

## Lifetime Fitness: Lower body strength

**Prepare:** Gather light dumbbells or filled water jugs (a gallon of water ≈ 8.3 lb), resistance bands, a dowel or broomstick, and a sturdy step or stair. Set up video recording.

**Watch for:**
- Rounding the back in a hinge. The dowel should touch head, upper back, and tailbone throughout.
- Squatting a deadlift. A hinge moves mainly at the hips with a slight knee bend; a squat bends knees and hips more equally.
- Letting the front knee collapse inward in lunges or step-ups. Keep the knee in line with the second toe.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hip hinge
*Student:* Learn the hip hinge with a dowel and perform Romanian deadlifts with light weight.

*How to teach:* Hold a dowel along the spine (three points of contact). Push hips back with soft knees until hamstrings stretch, then drive hips forward. Progress to Romanian deadlifts with light jugs, 3×10.

- **Q:** What muscles does the Romanian deadlift target?
  **A:** Hamstrings and glutes, with spinal erectors stabilizing.
- **Q:** How does the dowel help in learning the hinge?
  **A:** If it loses contact with the head, upper back, or tailbone, the spine is rounding or arching — it gives feedback for a neutral spine.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lunges
*Student:* Learn forward, reverse, and lateral lunges.

*How to teach:* Teach forward, reverse, and lateral lunges: long enough stride, torso tall, back knee toward floor, front knee over ankle. 2×8 each leg each variation.

- **Q:** Which lunge is usually easier on the knees for beginners, forward or reverse?
  **A:** Reverse lunge — the front shin stays more vertical and braking forces are lower.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Step-ups
*Student:* Perform step-ups and calf raises.

*How to teach:* Step-ups onto a sturdy step (whole foot on step, drive through heel, control the descent), 3×10 each leg; calf raises 3×15 with a pause at the top.

- **Q:** Which muscles do calf raises strengthen?
  **A:** Gastrocnemius and soleus (calves).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lower body workout
*Student:* Complete a full lower-body workout (3 sets of 10–12).

*How to teach:* Full lower-body workout: goblet squat, RDL, reverse lunge, step-up, calf raise — 3 sets of 10–12, rest 60–90 s. Log loads and RPE.

- **Q:** Why rest 60–90 seconds between sets?
  **A:** To partially recover so the next set can be done with good form and similar effort, appropriate for muscular endurance/hypertrophy work.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Log workout and self-assess form via video.

*How to teach:* Student records a set of each exercise and self-assesses with the checklist; log the workout.

- **Q:** In your video, how would you know if your hinge is correct?
  **A:** Back stays flat, hips move back (not down), shins nearly vertical, feel stretch in hamstrings.

**Assessment:** Video form check.

**Answer key:** Video form check (proficient = correct on at least 4 of 5 exercises). RDL: neutral spine, hips back, slight knee bend. Squat: chest up, knees track toes, depth. Lunge: torso tall, front knee over ankle, controlled descent. Step-up: whole foot on step, drive through heel, controlled lowering. Calf raise: full range, pause at top. Self-assessment must name one strength and one correction.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Statistics for quality

**Prepare:** Gather 50 'identical' items (pencils, nails, crackers or craft sticks), calipers and a spreadsheet. Review standard deviation (Khan Academy). Decide a tolerance for the Day 5 report (e.g. nominal length ± 1.0 mm).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'identical' manufactured items are exactly the same. All manufacturing has variation; statistics measure how much.
- Confusing sample and population standard deviation. For a sample of items from a larger batch, use the sample formula (divide by n − 1), which is STDEV.S in a spreadsheet.
- Using only the mean to judge quality. A batch can have the right mean but too much spread; check standard deviation and whether all items fall within tolerance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Data collection
*Student:* Measure the length of 50 'identical' items and record in a spreadsheet.

*How to teach:* Measure the length of 50 items with calipers and record in a spreadsheet column with units in the header.

- **Q:** Why measure 50 items rather than 3?
  **A:** A larger sample gives a more reliable picture of the batch's typical value and variation.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Central tendency
*Student:* Calculate mean, median, mode, and range by hand and with spreadsheet functions.

*How to teach:* Calculate mean, median, mode and range by hand for the first 10 values, then check with =AVERAGE, =MEDIAN, =MODE, =MAX−MIN.

- **Q:** Find the mean, median, mode and range of 10, 12, 11, 12, 15.
  **A:** Mean = 60/5 = 12; median = 12; mode = 12; range = 15 − 10 = 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Standard deviation
*Student:* Calculate standard deviation and explain what it reveals about manufacturing consistency.

*How to teach:* Work an example: 10, 12, 11, 13, 14 → mean 12; squared deviations 4, 0, 1, 1, 4 sum to 10; sample variance 10/4 = 2.5; SD ≈ 1.58. Then use =STDEV.S on the full data.

- **Q:** What does a small standard deviation say about a manufacturing process?
  **A:** The parts are very consistent — values are close to the mean.
- **Q:** Compute the sample standard deviation of 4, 6, 8.
  **A:** Mean 6; squared deviations 4, 0, 4 = 8; 8 ÷ 2 = 4; SD = 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Histograms
*Student:* Create a histogram and assess whether the data is approximately normal.

*How to teach:* Make a histogram (Insert → Chart → Histogram). Discuss whether it's roughly bell-shaped and the 68–95–99.7 rule.

- **Q:** In a normal distribution, about what percent of values fall within 2 standard deviations of the mean?
  **A:** About 95% (68% within 1 SD, 99.7% within 3 SD).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a quality-control report recommending whether the batch meets a given tolerance.

*How to teach:* The student writes the quality-control report recommending accept or reject; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Mean = 100.2 mm, SD = 0.3 mm, tolerance 100 ± 1 mm. Would you accept the batch?
  **A:** Yes — mean ± 3 SD is 99.3 to 101.1 mm; nearly all parts are within tolerance, though values near the 101.1 end slightly exceed 101.0, so check the actual count outside the limit (likely only a few, about 0.4%).

**Assessment:** Quality-control report.

**Answer key:** Quality-control report rubric (4 levels): 4 = states the tolerance; reports n, mean, median, mode, range and standard deviation (with units); includes a labeled histogram; identifies how many items fall outside tolerance and whether mean ± 3 SD falls within the tolerance limits; makes a clear accept/reject recommendation with reasons; 3 = all statistics, weak recommendation; 2 = missing statistics or histogram; 1 = incomplete. Check numbers by rerunning AVERAGE, MEDIAN, MODE, MAX−MIN and STDEV.S on the student's data.
