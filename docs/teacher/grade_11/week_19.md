## Algebra II: Exponential growth and decay

**Prepare:** 100 coins (or M&Ms) and a cup. Desmos. Make a table template: round, number remaining.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing growth factor and rate: a 6% increase is b = 1.06, not 0.06 or 6. A 15% decrease is b = 0.85.
- Thinking an exponential function eventually reaches its asymptote. y = 100(0.5)^x gets closer to 0 but never equals 0.

### Day 1 (Monday): Linear vs. exponential
*Student:* Compare constant difference vs. constant ratio in tables.

*How to teach:* Show two tables (constant difference vs constant ratio). Student classifies six tables and writes each rule. Today's task from the plan: Compare constant difference vs. constant ratio in tables. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Classify 3, 6, 12, 24 and 5, 8, 11, 14 (x = 0, 1, 2, 3).
  **A:** First: ratio 2, exponential, y = 3·2^x. Second: difference 3, linear, y = 3x + 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Graphs
*Student:* Graph y = ab^x and transformations; identify asymptote and intercept.

*How to teach:* Graph y = ab^x and transformations; identify y-intercept and horizontal asymptote. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give the asymptote and y-intercept of y = 3(2)^x − 4.
  **A:** Asymptote y = −4; y-intercept 3 − 4 = −1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decay simulation
*Student:* Shake 100 coins, remove heads each round, record counts, and fit an exponential model.

*How to teach:* Shake coins, remove heads, record remaining each round until few left. Fit an exponential regression in Desmos. Today's task from the plan: Shake 100 coins, remove heads each round, record counts, and fit an exponential model. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** Starting with 100 coins, what model do you expect, and about how many remain after 4 rounds?
  **A:** N ≈ 100(0.5)^n; after 4 rounds about 100/16 ≈ 6 coins.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing models
*Student:* Write models from two points and from percent rate statements (population, depreciation).

*How to teach:* Model writing y = ab^x from (0, 5) and (2, 45), and from percent statements. Today's task from the plan: Write models from two points and from percent rate statements (population, depreciation). Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Write the exponential through (0, 5) and (2, 45).
  **A:** a = 5, 5b² = 45, b = 3: y = 5·3^x.
- **Q:** A $24,000 car loses 15% per year. Write a model and find its value after 3 years.
  **A:** V = 24000(0.85)^t; V(3) = $14,739.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on graphs and writing exponential models.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 30 minutes) and collect the decay lab table and graph. Today's task from the plan: Quiz on graphs and writing exponential models. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Is y = 200(1.03)^t growth or decay, and by what percent?
  **A:** Growth, 3% per time period.

**Assessment:** Decay lab and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab rubric: data table (1), graph with regression (1), b-value compared to 0.5 (1), explanation of why the ratio is about one half (1). Quiz sample key: 1) y=2(3)^x → y-int 2, HA y=0, growth. 2) through (1, 6) and (3, 54): b=3, a=2, y=2·3^x. 3) population 50,000 growing 2%/yr: P=50000(1.02)^t. 4) 0.7^x decay by 30%. 5) table 80, 40, 20 → y = 80(0.5)^x.

## Physics of the Universe: Wave properties

**Prepare:** A Slinky (metal preferred), a 3–5 m rope, stopwatch, meter stick; PhET Wave on a String.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking waves carry matter along. Waves carry energy; the medium oscillates in place.
- Thinking increasing frequency increases wave speed. Speed depends on the medium; changing frequency changes wavelength (v = fλ).

### Day 1 (Monday): Wave types
*Student:* Make transverse and longitudinal waves with a Slinky.

*How to teach:* Stretch the Slinky on the floor. Make transverse waves (side to side) and longitudinal waves (push-pull); tie a ribbon on one coil to watch its motion.

- **Q:** How does the ribbon move in each type of wave?
  **A:** Transverse: perpendicular to the wave direction (side to side). Longitudinal: back and forth parallel to the wave direction.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wave equation
*Student:* Measure wavelength and frequency on a rope; calculate v = fλ.

*How to teach:* Shake the rope steadily; measure wavelength with the meter stick and count oscillations per second for frequency; compute v = fλ. Today's task from the plan: Measure wavelength and frequency on a rope; calculate v = fλ. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A rope wave has frequency 2.0 Hz and wavelength 1.5 m. Wave speed?
  **A:** v = 2.0 × 1.5 = 3.0 m/s.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Simulation
*Student:* Use PhET Wave on a String to explore tension and wave speed.

*How to teach:* In PhET, vary tension and frequency separately and record wave speed. Today's task from the plan: Use PhET Wave on a String to explore tension and wave speed. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** What happens to wave speed when tension increases?
  **A:** It increases; the wavelength gets longer at the same frequency.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Media
*Student:* Compare sound speed in air, water, and steel; solve problems.

*How to teach:* Give speeds of sound: air ≈ 343 m/s, water ≈ 1,480 m/s, steel ≈ 5,900 m/s. Solve problems. Today's task from the plan: Compare sound speed in air, water, and steel; solve problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How long does sound take to travel 1,000 m in air and in water?
  **A:** Air: 1,000/343 ≈ 2.9 s. Water: 1,000/1,480 ≈ 0.68 s.
- **Q:** Why does sound travel faster in steel than in air?
  **A:** Particles in solids are tightly bonded, so vibrations are passed along much faster.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on wave calculations.

*How to teach:* Wave quiz (30 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on wave calculations. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** A 440 Hz sound in air (343 m/s) has what wavelength?
  **A:** λ = 343/440 ≈ 0.78 m.

**Assessment:** Wave quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (sample): 1) label crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength on a diagram. 2) v = fλ: 5 Hz, 0.4 m → 2 m/s. 3) λ for 680 Hz in air = 0.50 m. 4) period of 4 Hz wave = 0.25 s. 5) sound is longitudinal; light is transverse. 6) speed depends on medium, not frequency. 7) thunder heard 3 s after lightning → ≈ 1 km away.

## Spanish IV: El sistema educativo

**Prepare:** Find a Spanish article comparing education in Spain and Mexico, the UC or CSU Spanish-language admissions page, and an article on free public universities in Argentina (e.g., the Universidad de Buenos Aires). Prepare a three-column comparison chart. Chart real si clauses: si + present → future, present, or command.

**Watch for:**
- Using the future after si (*Si estudiaré, aprobaré): Si estudio, aprobaré.
- False friends: 'el colegio' usually means a K–12 school, not college; 'la facultad' = a university school/department; 'la carrera' = degree program/major; 'la matrícula' = enrollment or tuition.
- Equating 'bachillerato' with a bachelor's degree: in Spain and Mexico it is the final stage of secondary school (like 11th–12th grade); a bachelor's is 'la licenciatura' or 'el grado'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sistemas
*Student:* Compare the educational systems of Spain, Mexico, and the U.S. in a chart.

*How to teach:* Your child compares Spain, Mexico, and the U.S. in a chart: stages, ages, exit exams, university access, cost.

- **Q:** What is the 'bachillerato' in Spain?
  **A:** The two-year, non-compulsory upper-secondary stage (ages 16–18) after the ESO, which prepares students for the university entrance exam (often called la Selectividad, now PAU/EBAU).
- **Q:** What are 'primaria', 'secundaria', and 'preparatoria' in Mexico?
  **A:** Elementary school (6 years), lower-secondary (3 years), and upper-secondary/high school (3 years, also called bachillerato).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cláusulas con si
*Student:* Practice si + present → future (Si estudio, aprobaré) with 15 sentences about your plans.

*How to teach:* Teach si + present → future; your child writes 15 sentences about plans.

- **Q:** Translate: 'If I get a scholarship, I will study engineering.'
  **A:** Si consigo (recibo) una beca, estudiaré ingeniería.
- **Q:** Translate: 'If you have questions, call the university.'
  **A:** Si tienes preguntas, llama a la universidad.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lectura
*Student:* Read an article on free public university in Argentina and summarize it.

*How to teach:* Read the article on Argentina's free public universities; your child summarizes it in 6 sentences.

- **Q:** What is distinctive about public universities in Argentina?
  **A:** Undergraduate study at national public universities is tuition-free (since 1949), with open or easy admission, which gives wide access but large classes and limited funding.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversación
*Student:* Discuss college plans and goals with a partner.

*How to teach:* Discuss college and career plans with you or a tutor for 10 minutes.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What would you like to study and why?'
  **A:** ¿Qué te gustaría estudiar y por qué?

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Write a 200-word paragraph about your future education plans.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the 200-word paragraph on future education plans.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Si yo voy a la universidad, estudiaré medicina y seré médico.' (Is it correct?)
  **A:** Yes — si + present (voy) with the future (estudiaré, seré) is correct.

**Assessment:** Paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric (200 words, future education plans): 4 = specific plans (school, major, how to pay), 4+ si clauses and future verbs, comparison with another country's system, accurate; 3 = minor errors; 2 = vague or many errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Realism and Trifles

**Prepare:** Find Susan Glaspell's Trifles (1916, public domain) and an excerpt of Ibsen's A Doll's House (e.g., the final scene). Parental note: Trifles involves a murder investigation (a husband strangled with a rope); it is not graphic.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking nothing happens in realism. Action is internal and revealed through subtext and small details.
- Reading the women's lines literally. Their talk about quilting and preserves carries hidden meaning about Minnie's life and motive.
- Assuming the men find the evidence. The men search and dismiss 'kitchen things'; the women discover the motive.

### Day 1 (Monday): Realism
*Student:* Read about Ibsen, Chekhov, and the rise of realism and the fourth wall; read a scene from A Doll's House.

*How to teach:* Read about realism: Ibsen and Chekhov in the late 1800s brought everyday life, real social problems, and the 'fourth wall' (invisible wall between stage and audience). Read the final scene of A Doll's House.

- **Q:** What is the 'fourth wall'?
  **A:** The imaginary wall at the front of a realistic set through which the audience watches; actors behave as if it's a real, closed room.
- **Q:** Why was the ending of A Doll's House shocking in 1879?
  **A:** Nora leaves her husband and children to find her own identity — slamming the door on conventional marriage and gender roles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trifles reading
*Student:* Read Trifles aloud by roles and list the clues the women find.

*How to teach:* Read Trifles aloud by roles; list clues the women find.

- **Q:** List the clues Mrs. Hale and Mrs. Peters find.
  **A:** The half-cleaned kitchen and unfinished work, broken jars of frozen preserves, erratic quilt stitching, the birdcage with a broken door hinge, and the dead canary with its neck wrung in a box in the sewing basket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Subtext
*Student:* Analyze subtext in the women's dialogue and write what each line really means.

*How to teach:* Analyze subtext: for 5 lines, write what each line literally says and really means.

- **Q:** What is the subtext when Mrs. Hale says she wishes she had visited Minnie?
  **A:** She feels guilty — she sees that Minnie's isolation and loneliness with a harsh husband contributed to the crime, and she shares responsibility for neglecting her.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scene work
*Student:* Rehearse the final scene of Trifles with attention to silence and small gestures.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the final scene emphasizing silence and small gestures (looking at the box, hiding it).

- **Q:** How does Trifles end?
  **A:** The women hide the box with the dead bird, concealing the motive; Mrs. Hale answers the county attorney that Minnie was going to 'knot it.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform the scene and write a paragraph on how Glaspell uses objects to tell the story.

*How to teach:* Perform; student writes a paragraph on how Glaspell uses objects.

- **Q:** What does the canary symbolize?
  **A:** Minnie herself — her lost voice and joy (she sang as Minnie Foster) and her life caged and silenced by her husband, who killed the bird as he killed her spirit.

**Assessment:** Scene performance and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric. 4 – Thesis on objects as storytelling; analyzes at least three objects (preserves, quilt, birdcage/canary) with quotations; explains how they reveal motive and the women's solidarity. 3 (proficient) – Two or three objects explained with evidence. 2 – Summary rather than analysis. 1 – Inaccurate. Scene performance: proficient = honors silences, small purposeful gestures, clear shift as the women decide to hide the evidence.

## Lifetime Fitness: Tennis and racquet tournament

**Prepare:** Locate a public tennis court and wall; gather a racket and balls. Read tennis scoring (love, 15, 30, 40, deuce, advantage) and basic etiquette. Choose a racquet sport for the Day 4 round-robin and invite players.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging only with the arm. Turn the shoulders and step into the ball.
- Calling the score with the receiver's score first. The server's score is announced first.
- Thinking a let serve is a fault. A serve that clips the net and lands in the correct box is replayed (let).

### Day 1 (Monday): Groundstrokes
*Student:* Learn forehand and backhand groundstrokes and rally against a wall.

*How to teach:* Teach the forehand (turn, racket back, low-to-high swing, contact in front, follow through over the shoulder) and two-handed backhand. Rally against a wall.

- **Q:** How does a low-to-high swing create topspin?
  **A:** Brushing up the back of the ball makes it spin forward, so it dips down into the court and bounces high.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Serve
*Student:* Learn the flat serve with a trophy position.

*How to teach:* Teach the flat serve: toss slightly in front, 'trophy position' (racket back, tossing arm up), reach up and swing through. Serve into the diagonal service box.

- **Q:** Into which box must the first serve of a game go?
  **A:** The diagonally opposite service box — from the right (deuce) side into the opponent's right service box crosswise.
- **Q:** What is the trophy position?
  **A:** Mid-serve position with the tossing arm pointing up, racket arm bent behind the head, knees bent — resembling a trophy figure.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scoring & etiquette
*Student:* Learn tennis scoring and court etiquette.

*How to teach:* Teach scoring: 0 (love), 15, 30, 40, game; 40-40 is deuce; next point is advantage; win by two points. Games to 6 win by 2 for a set. Etiquette: call your own lines fairly, wait for points to finish before crossing courts, return stray balls.

- **Q:** The score is 40-30 and the receiver wins the next point. What is the score?
  **A:** Deuce (40-40).
- **Q:** What is a 'let' on a serve?
  **A:** The serve touches the net and lands in the correct box; it is replayed with no penalty.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tournament
*Student:* Play a round-robin of your choice of racquet sport with family or friends.

*How to teach:* Hold a round-robin with family/friends in the chosen racquet sport. Student keeps a results table and practices sportsmanship and self-officiating.

- **Q:** In a 4-player round-robin, how many matches are played?
  **A:** 6 (each player plays the other 3; 4×3÷2 = 6).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Peer/self evaluation of skills and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Student completes self and peer evaluations on skill and sportsmanship.

- **Q:** Give one example of good sportsmanship in racquet sports.
  **A:** Making honest line calls, shaking hands or tapping paddles after play, and complimenting opponents' good shots.

**Assessment:** Tournament evaluation.

**Answer key:** Tournament evaluation rubric (skills and sportsmanship). 4 – Consistent legal serves, controlled rallies, correct scoring called aloud, fair line calls, encourages others, accurate self/peer reflection. 3 (proficient) – Mostly consistent strokes, correct scoring, good etiquette, completed reflection. 2 – Frequent errors or scoring confusion; some etiquette lapses. 1 – Unsafe or unsportsmanlike behavior.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Materials and their properties

**Prepare:** Gather material samples: thin wood strips (paint stirrers or craft sticks), plastic strips (e.g. rulers), metal strips (aluminum flashing or a thin metal ruler), aluminum foil, cardboard; string, yarn and fishing line; a bucket or bag with weights (water bottles, coins) and a ruler for deflection. Find an Ashby chart image (strength or stiffness vs. density) online.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing strength and stiffness. Stiffness resists bending/stretching (how much it deflects); strength is the load at which it permanently deforms or breaks. A material can be stiff but brittle (glass).
- Thinking 'hard' means 'tough'. Hardness is resistance to scratching/indentation; toughness is ability to absorb energy before fracturing.
- Comparing materials with different sample sizes. Keep length, width and thickness the same so only the material changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Material families
*Student:* Classify common household items by material family (metals, polymers, ceramics, composites).

*How to teach:* Sort 15 household items into metals, polymers, ceramics and composites; note one property clue for each (e.g. metals conduct heat, ceramics are brittle).

- **Q:** Classify: glass jar, nylon rope, plywood, steel spoon.
  **A:** Glass jar — ceramic; nylon rope — polymer; plywood — composite (wood layers and glue); steel spoon — metal.
- **Q:** What is a composite?
  **A:** A material made of two or more different materials combined to get better properties (e.g. fiberglass, carbon fiber, concrete, plywood).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Properties
*Student:* Learn mechanical properties and test bending stiffness of wood, plastic, and metal strips with weights.

*How to teach:* Clamp equal-size strips of wood, plastic and metal over a table edge with equal overhang; hang the same weight from the end and measure deflection. Discuss stiffness.

- **Q:** Which strip deflected least, and what property does that show?
  **A:** Usually the metal strip — it has the highest stiffness (elastic modulus).
- **Q:** Why must all strips be the same size?
  **A:** So the only variable is the material; size and shape also affect bending.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tensile test
*Student:* Test tensile strength of string, yarn, and fishing line by adding weights until failure.

*How to teach:* Hang each fiber from a support with a cup; add weights until failure. Record failure load for three trials each.

- **Q:** What property does this test measure?
  **A:** Tensile strength (the maximum pulling load before failure).
- **Q:** Why do three trials and average?
  **A:** Results vary from sample to sample; averaging gives a more reliable value.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Selection
*Student:* Use an Ashby-style chart to choose a material for a bike frame and a water bottle.

*How to teach:* Show an Ashby chart (strength or stiffness vs. density). Choose a material region for a bike frame (strong and stiff for low weight) and a water bottle (light, cheap, tough, food-safe).

- **Q:** What two properties would you plot to choose a lightweight, stiff material?
  **A:** Stiffness (Young's modulus) versus density.
- **Q:** Why are many bike frames made of aluminum or carbon fiber?
  **A:** They have high stiffness and strength for their weight.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Material selection quiz and lab report.

*How to teach:* Give the material selection quiz in the key and collect the lab report.

- **Q:** Define ductility and give a ductile material.
  **A:** The ability to stretch or bend permanently without breaking; copper or aluminum.

**Assessment:** Materials lab report.

**Answer key:** Material selection quiz: 1) Metals, polymers, ceramics, composites — classify: aluminum can (metal), water bottle (polymer, PET), coffee mug (ceramic), carbon-fiber bike frame or fiberglass (composite). 2) Ductility = ability to deform plastically (stretch) without breaking. 3) Elasticity = ability to return to original shape after load is removed. 4) Tensile strength = maximum stress a material withstands while being pulled. 5) Bike frame: high stiffness and strength-to-weight — aluminum, steel, titanium or carbon-fiber composite. 6) Water bottle: low cost, light, food-safe, tough — PET or polypropylene plastic (or stainless steel/aluminum for reusable). Lab report rubric (4 levels): 4 = controlled variables stated; data tables with units for bending deflection and tensile failure load (multiple trials, averages); comparison graph; conclusions linking results to properties; error sources; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing data or analysis; 1 = incomplete.
