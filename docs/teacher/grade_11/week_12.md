## English 11 – American Literature: Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, Ch. 1–6

**Prepare:** Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (Gutenberg). Parent note: depicts the brutal whipping of Douglass' Aunt Hester (Ch. 1) and other violence; read ahead and discuss.

**Watch for:**
- Reading the Narrative as only a personal story. Douglass makes an argument against slavery; each episode is evidence.
- Overlooking that slavery corrupts enslavers too. Track Sophia Auld's transformation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Context & preface
*Student:* Read Garrison's preface and Douglass' Ch. 1; note how prefaces were used to authenticate narratives.

*How to teach:* Read Garrison's preface and Ch. 1. Explain why prefaces by white abolitionists were used. Today's task from the plan: Read Garrison's preface and Douglass' Ch. 1; note how prefaces were used to authenticate narratives. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why did the Narrative open with Garrison's preface?
  **A:** White abolitionists' endorsements helped convince white readers that a formerly enslaved man really wrote it and that it was true.
- **Q:** What does Douglass not know about himself, and why does it matter?
  **A:** His birthday and his father's identity; enslavers kept enslaved people ignorant to deny them identity.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ch. 2–4
*Student:* Read and record examples of slavery's effect on enslavers as well as the enslaved.

*How to teach:* Read Ch. 2–4. The student records effects of slavery on the enslaved and the enslavers. Today's task from the plan: Read and record examples of slavery's effect on enslavers as well as the enslaved. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** How do the slave songs reveal suffering?
  **A:** Douglass says the songs expressed deep sorrow, not happiness, which rebuts the claim that singing showed contentment.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ch. 5–6
*Student:* Read about Baltimore and learning letters; explain why Douglass calls literacy 'the pathway from slavery to freedom'.

*How to teach:* Read Ch. 5–6. Discuss Hugh Auld's prohibition and Douglass' realization. Today's task from the plan: Read about Baltimore and learning letters; explain why Douglass calls literacy 'the pathway from slavery to freedom'. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does Douglass call literacy 'the pathway from slavery to freedom'?
  **A:** When Mr. Auld forbids reading because it would 'unfit him to be a slave,' Douglass realizes literacy gives power and freedom of mind.
- **Q:** How does Sophia Auld change?
  **A:** Kind at first, she becomes cruel under slavery's power: her 'cheerful eye... soon became red with rage.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Close reading

*How to teach:* Model analysis of a balanced sentence or antithesis. The student writes a 300-word close reading.   **A:** Contrasting ideas in parallel structure; it makes contradictions of slavery and 'Christian' enslavers stark.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz key: 1) Douglass born in Maryland. 2) Separated from his mother as an infant. 3) Aunt Hester's whipping = 'the blood-stained gate.' 4) Songs reveal sorrow. 5) Moved to Baltimore with the Aulds. 6) Sophia begins teaching him letters. 7) Hugh forbids it. 8) Douglass learns reading is the pathway to freedom. ## Algebra II: Polynomial modeling and the Binomial Theorem

**Prepare:** Cardstock (letter size 8.5 × 11 in), scissors, ruler, tape. Draw Pascal's triangle to row 6 yourself.

**Watch for:**
- In the binomial expansion, forgetting to raise the coefficient (e.g., the 2 in 2x) to the power. Put each term in parentheses: (2x)³ = 8x³.
- In the box problem, allowing x values that make a side negative. The domain is 0 < x < (shorter side)/2.

### Day 1 (Monday): Open-box lab
*Student:* Cut squares from cardstock corners to make boxes; model V(x) = x(L−2x)(W−2x) and find max volume.

*How to teach:* Each corner square is x in; student builds boxes with x = 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 in, measures volume, then models V(x) = x(11 − 2x)(8.5 − 2x) and finds the max in Desmos.

- **Q:** What cut size maximizes volume for an 8.5 × 11 sheet, and what is the domain?
  **A:** Domain 0 < x < 4.25. Maximum at x ≈ 1.59 in, V ≈ 66.1 in³.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pascal's Triangle
*Student:* Build Pascal's triangle and connect rows to (a+b)^n coefficients.

*How to teach:* Build Pascal's triangle (each entry = sum of two above). Expand (a + b)² and (a + b)³ and match coefficients. Today's task from the plan: Build Pascal's triangle and connect rows to (a+b)^n coefficients. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What is row 4 of Pascal's triangle and what does it give?
  **A:** 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 — the coefficients of (a + b)⁴ = a⁴ + 4a³b + 6a²b² + 4ab³ + b⁴.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Binomial Theorem
*Student:* Expand (2x−3)⁴ and find a specific term using combinations.

*How to teach:* Model (2x − 3)⁴ using C(4, k)(2x)^(4−k)(−3)^k. Then find one term of (x + 2)⁷ without expanding. Today's task from the plan: Expand (2x−3)⁴ and find a specific term using combinations. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Expand (2x − 3)⁴.
  **A:** 16x⁴ − 96x³ + 216x² − 216x + 81.
- **Q:** Find the x³ term of (x + 2)⁷.
  **A:** C(7, 4)·x³·2⁴ = 35·16x³ = 560x³.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Combining functions
*Student:* Add and multiply functions (e.g., revenue minus cost = profit) and interpret.

*How to teach:* Model profit = revenue − cost with function notation. Student adds/multiplies two functions and interprets. Today's task from the plan: Add and multiply functions (e.g., revenue minus cost = profit) and interpret. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** R(x) = 15x and C(x) = 5x + 2000. Write P(x) and find the break-even point.
  **A:** P(x) = 10x − 2000; break-even when P = 0: x = 200 units.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 3 test on polynomial operations, zeros, graphs, and modeling.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test (about 50 minutes) and collect the box lab write-up. Today's task from the plan: Unit 3 test on polynomial operations, zeros, graphs, and modeling. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Find the remainder of x³ + 2x² − x + 4 divided by x + 1.
  **A:** p(−1) = −1 + 2 + 1 + 4 = 6.

**Assessment:** Box lab and Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Box lab rubric: measured volumes table (1), correct V(x) and domain (1), max found (x≈1.59 in, V≈66.1 in³ for letter paper) (1), comparison of physical boxes to the model (1). Unit 3 test sample answers: (x²−3x+2)(x+4) = x³+x²−10x+8; zeros of x³−7x−6 = −1, −2, 3; (x+1)⁵ coefficients 1,5,10,10,5,1; end behavior of −x⁴: both down; x²+4x+5 zeros −2±i.

## Physics of the Universe: Engineering: energy conversion devices

**Prepare:** Cardboard box (pizza box works), aluminum foil, black construction paper, plastic wrap, tape, oven thermometer, a sunny day near midday. Adult supervision; the oven can get hot enough to burn.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking an efficient device can output more energy than it takes in. Efficiency = useful output ÷ input and is always below 100%; the rest becomes thermal energy.
- Thinking black paper reflects heat. Dark surfaces absorb most visible light and convert it to thermal energy; foil reflects light into the box.

### Day 1 (Monday): Efficiency
*Student:* Calculate efficiency of common devices (light bulbs, car engines).

*How to teach:* Model efficiency = useful energy out ÷ energy in × 100%. The student calculates efficiency for an incandescent bulb, LED, and car engine from given numbers.

- **Q:** A 60 W incandescent bulb produces about 6 W of visible light. What is its efficiency, and where does the rest go?
  **A:** 6/60 = 10%; the other 54 W becomes thermal energy (heat).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Design
*Student:* Design a solar oven with constraints (size, materials).

*How to teach:* List criteria (highest temperature rise in 30 minutes) and constraints (box size, listed materials). The student sketches a design labeling where light is reflected, absorbed, and trapped.

- **Q:** What job does the plastic wrap do?
  **A:** It lets sunlight in but traps warm air, reducing heat loss by convection (a greenhouse effect).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build the solar oven.

*How to teach:* Build the oven following the sketch: foil-lined reflector flap, black-lined bottom, sealed plastic window. Today's task from the plan: Build the solar oven. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why line the inside bottom with black paper rather than foil?
  **A:** Black absorbs light and converts it to thermal energy; foil on the bottom would reflect light back out.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test & refine
*Student:* Test the oven, measure temperature rise, and refine design.

*How to teach:* Test in full sun, recording temperature every 5 minutes for 30 minutes; change ONE feature (reflector angle or insulation) and retest. Today's task from the plan: Test the oven, measure temperature rise, and refine design. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Why change only one variable between tests?
  **A:** So any temperature difference can be attributed to that single change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present design results and efficiency estimate.

*How to teach:* The student presents results and an efficiency estimate. Today's task from the plan: Present design results and efficiency estimate. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** How can you estimate the oven's efficiency?
  **A:** Heat a known mass of water: energy absorbed = mcΔT; divide by solar input ≈ 1,000 W/m² × window area × time. E.g., 0.2 kg warmed 15 °C absorbs 12,600 J; 0.1 m² × 1,000 W/m² × 1,800 s = 180,000 J; efficiency ≈ 7%.

**Assessment:** Solar oven design report.

**Answer key:** Design report rubric (4-point per row): design meets constraints with labeled sketch; temperature-time data for at least two tests; one evidence-based refinement; efficiency estimate with calculation and explanation of energy losses (reflection, convection, conduction through walls). Proficient = complete data and a reasonable efficiency (typically 5–20%) with correct method.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Greek theatre

**Prepare:** Find Sophocles' Antigone on Project Gutenberg (public-domain translation) and choose key scenes: Antigone and Ismene (opening), Creon and Antigone, Creon and Haemon, Tiresias. Gather paper plates, paint, string. Read about the Theatre of Dionysus layout.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Greek plays had violence on stage. Deaths happen offstage and are reported by messengers.
- Assuming women acted. All roles were played by men wearing masks.
- Viewing Creon as purely a villain. He is a tragic figure whose pride (hubris) leads to his downfall.

### Day 1 (Monday): Origins
*Student:* Read about the Festival of Dionysus and the structure of a Greek theatre.

*How to teach:* Read about the City Dionysia festival in Athens and the theatre: theatron (seating), orchestra (circular dancing place for the chorus), skene (scene building), parodos (entrances). Thespis is traditionally the first actor.

- **Q:** What were the orchestra and skene in a Greek theatre?
  **A:** The orchestra was the circular performance area for the chorus; the skene was the building behind it used as backdrop and changing area.
- **Q:** For which god were Athenian dramatic festivals held?
  **A:** Dionysus.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Antigone
*Student:* Read key scenes of Antigone and discuss conflict between law and conscience.

*How to teach:* Read key scenes. Discuss: Creon forbids burial of Polyneices; Antigone buries him, obeying divine law and family duty over the king's law.

- **Q:** What is the central conflict in Antigone?
  **A:** Antigone's duty to bury her brother Polyneices (divine law, family loyalty, conscience) versus Creon's decree forbidding it (state law).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Masks
*Student:* Make a Greek tragedy mask and explore how masks change movement.

*How to teach:* Make a tragedy mask with an exaggerated expression; explore how a mask requires larger, clearer movement and turning the whole head.

- **Q:** Why did Greek actors wear masks?
  **A:** To show character/emotion to a large outdoor audience, allow few actors to play many roles (including women), and amplify the voice somewhat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chorus
*Student:* Stage a chorus ode with unison movement.

*How to teach:* Stage a short chorus ode with unison movement (strophe moving one way, antistrophe the other).

- **Q:** What was the role of the chorus?
  **A:** To comment on the action, represent the community's view, provide background, and link episodes through song and dance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on Greek theatre and a perform-the-chorus showing.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and watch the chorus performance.

- **Q:** What is catharsis?
  **A:** The emotional release or purging of pity and fear the audience experiences through tragedy (Aristotle).

**Assessment:** Quiz and chorus performance.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Festival = City Dionysia, honoring Dionysus. 2) Theatron = seating; orchestra = chorus area; skene = stage building. 3) Thespis = first actor. 4) Hubris = excessive pride. 5) Catharsis = release of pity and fear. 6) Antigone buries Polyneices against Creon's decree. 7) Consequences: Antigone dies (hangs herself in the tomb), Haemon kills himself, and Eurydice (Creon's wife) kills herself. 8) Three actors played all roles, all male, masked. Chorus performance (proficient): unified movement, clear choral speaking, masks used with enlarged gestures.

## Lifetime Fitness: Strength reassessment

**Prepare:** Gather the fitness journal with week 1 push-up and curl-up results and the strength logs; graph paper or a spreadsheet. Prepare the unit quiz (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Expecting linear progress forever. Plateaus are normal; change variables (reps, rest, exercise) or deload.
- Testing on a tired day. Retests should follow a rest day and the same protocol as baseline.
- Attributing all change to one factor. Consider training, sleep, nutrition, and technique.

### Day 1 (Monday): Workout A+
*Student:* Complete Workout A with a progression (added rep or load) and log it.

*How to teach:* Repeat Workout A adding one rep or a small load increase per exercise; log.

- **Q:** What is progressive overload?
  **A:** Gradually increasing training demands (load, reps, sets, or difficulty) so the body keeps adapting.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Retest
*Student:* Retest 90° push-ups, curl-ups, and plank hold; compare with week 1.

*How to teach:* Retest 90° push-ups and curl-ups with cadence, plus a max plank hold with good form. Compare to week 1.

- **Q:** Why must the retest use the same protocol as baseline?
  **A:** So the comparison is valid — changes reflect fitness, not different test conditions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Workout B+
*Student:* Complete Workout B with progression.

*How to teach:* Repeat Workout B with progression; log.

- **Q:** What is a plateau, and one way to break it?
  **A:** A period with no improvement; change a variable (rep range, exercise, volume), deload for a week, or improve sleep/nutrition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analysis
*Student:* Graph baseline vs. retest results and explain what changed and why.

*How to teach:* Student graphs baseline vs. retest (bar graph) and writes an explanation of changes.

- **Q:** Push-ups went from 12 to 18. What is the percent increase?
  **A:** (18 − 12) ÷ 12 = 0.5 = 50% increase.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Quiz on resistance-training principles, muscle groups, and safety.

*How to teach:* Give the unit quiz.

- **Q:** Name one muscle group for each: squat, row, overhead press.
  **A:** Squat: quadriceps/glutes; row: latissimus dorsi/rhomboids; press: deltoids/triceps.

**Assessment:** Retest results and quiz.

**Answer key:** Unit quiz key: 1) Concentric = muscle shortens under load; eccentric = lengthens under control. 2) Progressive overload = gradually increasing demand. 3) Specificity = adaptations match the training. 4) Squat works quads, glutes, hamstrings. 5) Rows work lats, rhomboids, biceps. 6) Safety: warm up, use correct form, appropriate load, controlled tempo, clear space, never hold breath for long. 7) RIR 2 = could do 2 more reps. 8) Beginners' early gains are mostly neural. Retest rubric: proficient = all retests done with correct protocol, graphed against baseline, and changes explained with reasons.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): CAD features

**Prepare:** Preview Onshape tutorials for Revolve, Fillet, Chamfer, Shell and Pattern (Learning Center). Have a simple working drawing of a three-feature part ready for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Revolving a sketch that crosses the axis. The profile must lie entirely on one side of the axis of revolution.
- Adding fillets too early in the feature tree. Add cosmetic fillets and chamfers near the end so earlier edits don't break them.
- Patterning the wrong thing. Pattern a feature (like a cut) — not the whole part — when you want repeated holes or vents.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revolve
*Student:* Model a bottle or knob using a revolve feature.

*How to teach:* Sketch half a bottle profile beside a center line and revolve 360°.

- **Q:** What shape do you get by revolving a rectangle around one of its sides?
  **A:** A cylinder.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fillets & chamfers
*Student:* Add fillets and chamfers and explain why designers soften edges.

*How to teach:* Add fillets (rounded edges) and chamfers (angled edges). Discuss safety, comfort, strength (fillets reduce stress concentrations) and ease of manufacturing.

- **Q:** Why do designers add fillets to inside corners of parts?
  **A:** Sharp inside corners concentrate stress and can crack; fillets spread the stress and also help molding and machining.
- **Q:** What's the difference between a fillet and a chamfer?
  **A:** A fillet is a rounded edge; a chamfer is an angled, flat cut edge.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shell
*Student:* Shell a box to create a hollow enclosure with uniform wall thickness.

*How to teach:* Model a box and shell it, removing the top face with uniform wall thickness (e.g. 2 mm).

- **Q:** Why keep wall thickness uniform in a plastic enclosure?
  **A:** Uniform walls cool evenly in molding, preventing warping and sink marks, and use material efficiently.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Patterns
*Student:* Use linear and circular patterns to model a phone stand with vents.

*How to teach:* Model a phone stand and use a linear pattern for vent slots and a circular pattern for holes.

- **Q:** What's the advantage of a pattern over drawing each slot?
  **A:** One feature is repeated automatically; changing the original or count updates all instances.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Model a three-feature part from a working drawing.

*How to teach:* Model a three-feature part from a working drawing; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Which feature would you use for a doorknob?
  **A:** Revolve.

**Assessment:** Feature model.

**Answer key:** Feature model rubric: 4 = part matches the working drawing's dimensions, uses at least three different feature types correctly, sketches fully constrained, logical feature tree with descriptive names; 3 = matches with one dimension off or one under-defined sketch; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.
