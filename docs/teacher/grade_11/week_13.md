## English 11 – American Literature: Douglass Ch. 7–11 and Harriet Jacobs

**Prepare:** Narrative Ch. 7–11 and Appendix; Harriet Jacobs, Incidents ch. 21 'The Loophole of Retreat' (Gutenberg). Parent note: Jacobs discusses sexual harassment by her enslaver.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking learning to read immediately made Douglass happy. He writes learning had been 'a curse rather than a blessing' because it made him aware of his condition.
- Assuming the narratives make identical appeals. Jacobs targets Northern white women's sense of domestic virtue and motherhood.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ch. 7–9
*Student:* Read; identify how learning to read changes Douglass' self-understanding.

*How to teach:* Read Ch. 7–9. The student tracks how Douglass learns from boys in the street and The Columbian Orator. Today's task from the plan: Read; identify how learning to read changes Douglass' self-understanding. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How did Douglass learn to read and write after his lessons stopped?
  **A:** He traded bread for lessons from poor white boys, studied The Columbian Orator, and copied letters in the shipyard and from young Master Thomas's old copy-books.
- **Q:** Why did reading also cause anguish?
  **A:** It let him understand his enslavement but offered no escape; he envied animals for not thinking.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ch. 10
*Student:* Read the Covey chapter; analyze the chiasmus 'You have seen how a man was made a slave...' and its placement.

*How to teach:* Read Ch. 10. Analyze the chiasmus and why it marks the book's turning point. Today's task from the plan: Read the Covey chapter; analyze the chiasmus 'You have seen how a man was made a slave...' and its placement. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Explain 'You have seen how a man was made a slave; you shall see how a slave was made a man.'
  **A:** The reversed structure mirrors the reversal in his life: after fighting Covey, he regains his sense of manhood and resolves never to be a slave in spirit.
- **Q:** What changed after the fight with Covey?
  **A:** Covey never whipped him again; Douglass says that though he remained a slave 'in form,' he would never be one 'in fact.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ch. 11 & Appendix
*Student:* Finish the Narrative; explain why Douglass withholds escape details and how the appendix addresses religion.

*How to teach:* Finish the Narrative. Discuss why he hides escape details and what the Appendix argues. Today's task from the plan: Finish the Narrative; explain why Douglass withholds escape details and how the appendix addresses religion. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does Douglass withhold how he escaped?
  **A:** Revealing methods would endanger those who helped him and alert enslavers, closing routes for others.
- **Q:** What does the Appendix clarify about religion?
  **A:** He opposes 'the slaveholding religion of this land,' not 'the pure, peaceable, and impartial Christianity of Christ.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Harriet Jacobs
*Student:* Read the 'loophole of retreat' excerpt; compare Jacobs' appeals to women readers with Douglass' appeals.

*How to teach:* Read the Jacobs excerpt. The student compares her appeals with Douglass'. Today's task from the plan: Read the 'loophole of retreat' excerpt; compare Jacobs' appeals to women readers with Douglass' appeals. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What was the 'loophole of retreat'?
  **A:** A tiny crawl space in her grandmother's attic where Jacobs hid for about seven years, watching her children through a small hole.

## Algebra II: Rational expressions

**Prepare:** Practice set. Remind the student how to simplify numerical fractions and find LCDs.

**Watch for:**
- Cancelling terms instead of factors, e.g., (x + 3)/(x + 5) → 3/5. Only common FACTORS cancel; factor first.
- Forgetting excluded values from the original denominator after a factor cancels.

### Day 1 (Monday): Simplifying
*Student:* Factor and simplify rational expressions, stating excluded values.

*How to teach:* Model factoring numerator and denominator, cancelling common factors, and listing excluded values from the ORIGINAL denominator. Today's task from the plan: Factor and simplify rational expressions, stating excluded values. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + 5x + 6) and state excluded values.
  **A:** (x − 3)(x + 3)/((x + 2)(x + 3)) = (x − 3)/(x + 2), x ≠ −2, −3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiply & divide
*Student:* Multiply and divide rational expressions with factoring.

*How to teach:* Model multiply = factor everything then cancel; divide = multiply by the reciprocal. Today's task from the plan: Multiply and divide rational expressions with factoring. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify (x² − 4)/(x + 1) · (x + 1)/(x − 2).
  **A:** (x − 2)(x + 2)(x + 1)/((x + 1)(x − 2)) = x + 2, x ≠ −1, 2.
- **Q:** Simplify (x/3) ÷ (x²/6).
  **A:** (x/3)(6/x²) = 2/x, x ≠ 0.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add & subtract
*Student:* Find LCDs and add/subtract rational expressions.

*How to teach:* Model finding the LCD from factored denominators and rewriting each fraction. Today's task from the plan: Find LCDs and add/subtract rational expressions. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify 3/x + 2/(x + 1).
  **A:** (3(x + 1) + 2x)/(x(x + 1)) = (5x + 3)/(x(x + 1)).
- **Q:** Simplify 1/(x − 2) − 4/(x² − 4).
  **A:** (x + 2 − 4)/((x − 2)(x + 2)) = 1/(x + 2), x ≠ ±2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Complex fractions
*Student:* Simplify complex fractions by two methods and compare.

*How to teach:* Method 1: simplify top and bottom then divide. Method 2: multiply top and bottom by the LCD of all small fractions. Compare. Today's task from the plan: Simplify complex fractions by two methods and compare. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify (1 + 1/x)/(1 − 1/x²).
  **A:** Multiply by x²: (x² + x)/(x² − 1) = x(x + 1)/((x − 1)(x + 1)) = x/(x − 1).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on rational expression operations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 30 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on rational expression operations. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Simplify (2x + 6)/(x² − 9).
  **A:** 2(x + 3)/((x − 3)(x + 3)) = 2/(x − 3), x ≠ ±3.

**Assessment:** Rational expressions quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: 1) (x²+x)/x = x+1, x≠0. 2) (x²−1)/(x²−2x+1) = (x+1)/(x−1). 3) (3x/(x−2))·((x−2)/(6x²)) = 1/(2x). 4) 2/x − 1/(x+2) = (x+4)/(x(x+2)). 5) x/(x−1) + 1/(1−x) = 1, x≠1. 6) (1/x − 1/2)/(x−2) = −1/(2x). Excluded values required for full credit.

## Physics of the Universe: Energy unit synthesis

**Prepare:** Review packet of mixed problems from Weeks 9–12; old quizzes; poster paper for a concept map.

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing equations without knowing when to use them. Have the student state the principle (conservation, Q = mcΔT, P = E/t) before choosing an equation.
- Leaving out thermal energy in conservation problems. When friction acts, the 'missing' mechanical energy is thermal energy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: mechanical energy
*Student:* Mixed problems on work, KE, PE, and power.

*How to teach:* Mixed problems on work, KE, PE, and power; the student explains which principle each uses. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A 0.50 kg ball is thrown straight up at 10 m/s. How high does it rise (no air resistance)?
  **A:** ½mv² = mgh → h = v²/(2g) = 100/19.6 ≈ 5.1 m.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: thermal
*Student:* Mixed problems on heat transfer and specific heat.

*How to teach:* Mixed problems on heat transfer and specific heat. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How much energy raises 2.0 kg of water by 10 °C?
  **A:** Q = 2.0 × 4,186 × 10 ≈ 83,700 J.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Concept map
*Student:* Build a concept map connecting energy forms and transfers.

*How to teach:* The student builds a concept map linking energy forms (kinetic, gravitational, elastic, thermal, chemical, electrical, radiant) with labeled transfer arrows (work, heating, radiation).

- **Q:** Trace energy from gasoline to a moving car.
  **A:** Chemical energy → thermal energy in combustion → mechanical work on pistons → kinetic energy of the car, with much lost as thermal energy to the surroundings.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice test
*Student:* Take a practice test and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Practice test under time; the student corrects each error with a one-line explanation. Today's task from the plan: Take a practice test and correct errors. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** A 1,000 kg car at 20 m/s brakes to rest. How much thermal energy is produced in the brakes?
  **A:** All of its KE: ½(1,000)(20²) = 200,000 J.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 3 test on energy.

*How to teach:* Unit 3 test (50 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Unit 3 test on energy. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Define power and give its unit.
  **A:** Power is the rate of energy transfer or work done, P = E/t, measured in watts (1 W = 1 J/s).

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key (sample): 1) work pushing 50 N for 6 m = 300 J. 2) KE of 4 kg at 3 m/s = 18 J. 3) PE of 3 kg at 10 m = 294 J. 4) power: 1,200 J in 4 s = 300 W. 5) speed at the bottom of a 5 m frictionless drop = 9.9 m/s. 6) Q to heat 0.3 kg water 20 °C → 100 °C = 100,464 J. 7) efficiency: 400 J useful of 1,600 J = 25%. 8) mixing 1 kg at 90 °C with 1 kg at 30 °C → 60 °C. 9) conduction/convection/radiation examples. 10) explain why a skater on a real ramp never quite returns to the starting height (friction → thermal).

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: Causes of the Great Depression

**Prepare:** Print 1920s–30s stock market data (Dow Jones: peak about 381 in Sept. 1929, low about 41 in July 1932), a margin-buying example, unemployment data (about 25% in 1933), and Hoover speech excerpts (e.g., 1928 'rugged individualism'; 1931–32 statements).

**Watch for:**
- Students think the 1929 crash alone caused the Depression. The crash was a trigger; deeper causes included overproduction, unequal income, farm debt, bank failures, Federal Reserve policy and falling world trade.
- Students think Hoover did nothing. He pushed voluntarism and later created the Reconstruction Finance Corporation (1932), but opposed direct federal relief to individuals.
- Students think buying on margin meant buying cheap stock. It meant borrowing most of the price from a broker; when prices fell, margin calls forced selling.

### Day 1 (Monday): Federal Reserve
*Student:* Learn the origins of the Federal Reserve and the monetary debates.

*How to teach:* Learn the origins of the Federal Reserve and monetary debates.

- **Q:** Why was the Federal Reserve created?
  **A:** To prevent banking panics like 1907 by providing a central bank that could lend to banks and manage the money supply.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Crash
*Student:* Analyze 1929 stock market data and buying on margin.

*How to teach:* Analyze 1929 market data and buying on margin with a worked example.

- **Q:** You buy $1,000 of stock with $100 down on margin. The price falls 20%. What happens?
  **A:** The stock is worth $800, but you still owe $900; your $100 is wiped out and you owe $100 more — the broker issues a margin call.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Causes
*Student:* Rank the causes of the Depression with evidence.

*How to teach:* The student ranks the causes with evidence.

- **Q:** How did bank failures worsen the Depression?
  **A:** Depositors lost savings (no deposit insurance), credit dried up, and the money supply shrank, cutting spending and investment.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hoover
*Student:* Read Hoover's speeches and evaluate his response.

*How to teach:* Read Hoover's speeches and evaluate his response.

- **Q:** What was Hoover's philosophy of relief?
  **A:** Voluntarism and 'rugged individualism' — local and private charity, not direct federal aid to individuals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on causes.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on causes.

- **Q:** What was the Smoot-Hawley Tariff's effect?
  **A:** Other countries retaliated with their own tariffs and world trade collapsed, deepening the Depression.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Federal Reserve (1913) — 12 regional banks after the Panic of 1907. 2) Buying on margin — paying as little as 10% and borrowing the rest. 3) Black Thursday (Oct. 24) and Black Tuesday (Oct. 29, 1929). 4) Dow fell about 89% from 1929 peak to 1932 low. 5) Bank runs and failures — about 9,000 banks failed 1930–33. 6) Money supply fell about one-third; the Fed did not act as lender of last resort. 7) Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930) — raised tariffs, other nations retaliated, trade collapsed. 8) Overproduction/underconsumption and unequal income. 9) Hoover's RFC (1932). 10) Bonus Army (1932) — WWI veterans demanding early bonus payment were driven out by the Army.

## Spanish IV: El arte visual: Velázquez, Goya y Picasso

**Prepare:** Open the Museo del Prado online collection (Las Meninas, 1656; El 3 de mayo de 1808, painted 1814) and the Museo Reina Sofía site (Guernica, 1937). Chart the passive voice: ser + participle (agreeing) + por (El cuadro fue pintado por Velázquez) and passive se (Se pintó en 1656; Se pintaron muchos retratos). Preview: Goya's and Picasso's paintings show executions and wartime death.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting participle agreement in the passive with ser: the participle agrees with the subject — La obra fue pintada por Goya; Los murales fueron pintados por Rivera.
- Making passive se singular with a plural subject (*Se vende libros): Se venden libros.
- Thinking Guernica shows a battle between soldiers: it depicts the 1937 bombing of civilians in a Basque town.

### Day 1 (Monday): Las Meninas
*Student:* Explore Las Meninas in the Prado's online collection and describe it using hay, está, and se ve.

*How to teach:* Explore Las Meninas; your child describes it in 8 sentences with hay, está/están, and se ve/se ven.

- **Q:** Who appears in Las Meninas?
  **A:** The Infanta Margarita Teresa with her maids of honor (meninas), a dwarf and a dog, Velázquez himself at a canvas, and King Philip IV and Queen Mariana reflected in the mirror at the back.
- **Q:** Translate: 'In the mirror one sees the king and queen.'
  **A:** En el espejo se ven los reyes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Voz pasiva
*Student:* Learn the passive voice (fue pintado por) and passive se (se pintó); rewrite 15 active sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach the passive with ser and the passive se; your child rewrites 15 active sentences.

- **Q:** Rewrite in the passive: 'Los romanos construyeron el acueducto de Segovia.'
  **A:** El acueducto de Segovia fue construido por los romanos.
- **Q:** Rewrite with passive se: 'Venden entradas en línea.'
  **A:** Se venden entradas en línea.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Goya
*Student:* Analyze El 3 de mayo de 1808 and write a paragraph on how the painting conveys violence.

*How to teach:* Analyze El 3 de mayo de 1808; your child writes a paragraph on how Goya conveys violence.

- **Q:** What historical event does El 3 de mayo de 1808 show?
  **A:** French soldiers executing Spanish civilians in Madrid after the uprising of May 2, 1808, against Napoleon's occupation.
- **Q:** How does Goya create sympathy for the victims?
  **A:** The central man in a white shirt is lit by a lantern with arms raised like Christ on the cross, while the soldiers are faceless, lined up like a machine; dead bodies lie in blood in the foreground.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Guernica
*Student:* Read about the bombing of Guernica and interpret Picasso's symbols in a guided Spanish discussion.

*How to teach:* Read about the bombing of Guernica; discuss Picasso's symbols in Spanish (el toro, el caballo herido, la madre con el hijo muerto, la bombilla/el ojo, la espada rota).

- **Q:** When and why was Guernica bombed?
  **A:** April 26, 1937, during the Spanish Civil War, by German (and Italian) aircraft supporting Franco; many civilians died.
- **Q:** Give a possible interpretation of the screaming mother holding her dead child.
  **A:** She represents the suffering of innocent civilians, recalling a Pietà image of grief.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Compare two paintings in a 200-word written description; passive voice quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 200-word comparison of two paintings; give the passive voice quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Guernica was painted in 1937 and is exhibited in Madrid.'
  **A:** Guernica fue pintado en 1937 y se exhibe (está expuesto) en Madrid.

**Assessment:** Comparison paragraph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Comparison paragraph rubric (200 words, two paintings): 4 = describes both with hay/se ve/está, compares subject, color, and message, includes historical context, 3+ correct passive constructions; 3 = minor errors; 2 = describes only one well; 1 = incomplete. Passive quiz sample key: 1) Velázquez pintó el cuadro Las Meninas → El cuadro Las Meninas fue pintado por Velázquez 2) Goya pintó El 3 de mayo en 1814 → El 3 de mayo fue pintado por Goya en 1814 3) Picasso pintó Guernica en 1937 → Guernica fue pintado por Picasso en 1937 4) Se construyó el museo en 1819 (Prado) 5) Se venden carteles en la tienda 6) Se pintaron murales.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Medieval, commedia dell'arte, and world traditions

**Prepare:** Gather commedia stock character images and short Noh and kabuki clips (e.g., from Japan Arts Council or museum channels); preview. Read about mystery and morality plays (York cycle, Everyman).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking commedia was scripted. It used scenarios (plot outlines) and improvised dialogue with rehearsed lazzi.
- Seeing Noh and kabuki as the same. Noh is older (14th c.), slow, spare, masked; kabuki (17th c.) is colorful, dramatic, with elaborate makeup.
- Assuming medieval theatre was all religious services. Mystery plays were performed by town guilds outdoors in the vernacular.

### Day 1 (Monday): Medieval theatre
*Student:* Read about mystery and morality plays and pageant wagons.

*How to teach:* Read about mystery plays (Bible stories performed by guilds on pageant wagons, e.g., York cycle) and morality plays (allegories such as Everyman).

- **Q:** What is a pageant wagon?
  **A:** A movable stage on wheels used in medieval mystery cycles, moving from station to station in town.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Commedia
*Student:* Learn stock characters (Arlecchino, Pantalone) and their physical lazzi.

*How to teach:* Teach stock characters: Arlecchino (acrobatic, hungry servant), Pantalone (old, greedy Venetian merchant), Il Dottore (pompous scholar), Il Capitano (bragging coward), the Innamorati (lovers, unmasked). Practice each character's walk and lazzi.

- **Q:** What are lazzi?
  **A:** Rehearsed comic bits of physical business (e.g., catching a fly and eating it) inserted into commedia performances.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Commedia scene
*Student:* Improvise a commedia scenario with stock characters.

*How to teach:* Improvise a commedia scenario (e.g., Pantalone hides his money; Arlecchino tries to steal food) with masks or half-mask gestures.

- **Q:** Who is Pantalone?
  **A:** An old, miserly Venetian merchant, often a father blocking young lovers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Asian traditions
*Student:* Watch Noh and kabuki clips and compare conventions with Western theatre.

*How to teach:* Watch Noh and kabuki clips; compare with Western theatre (masks, music, stylized movement, hanamichi runway in kabuki, male actors including onnagata).

- **Q:** Name one convention of kabuki.
  **A:** Male actors (onnagata play women), the hanamichi runway through the audience, mie (dramatic frozen poses), kumadori makeup.
- **Q:** Name one convention of Noh.
  **A:** Masked lead (shite), slow stylized movement, a bridgeway (hashigakari), chorus and musicians, minimal set with a painted pine tree.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a comparison of two traditions.

*How to teach:* Student writes a comparison of two traditions (e.g., commedia vs. kabuki): origins, actors, staging, audience.

- **Q:** How are commedia and kabuki similar?
  **A:** Both rely on stock characters/types, stylized physical performance, and popular audiences.

**Assessment:** Comparison writing.

**Answer key:** Comparison writing rubric. 4 – Accurately compares two traditions on origin/time, performers, conventions, staging, and audience with specific examples and a thesis. 3 (proficient) – Accurate comparison with at least three points and examples. 2 – Some inaccuracies or list-like. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness: Flexibility and mobility

**Prepare:** Gather a mat, towel or strap, optional foam roller, and the sit-and-reach box. Read about PNF contract-relax so you can partner safely (gentle pressure only).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking flexibility is fixed. Regular stretching improves range of motion within weeks.
- Stretching into pain. Stretch to mild tension; pain signals risk of injury.
- Pushing hard as the partner in PNF. The partner only holds the position; the stretcher controls intensity.

### Day 1 (Monday): Types of stretching
*Student:* Learn static, dynamic, and PNF stretching; retest sit-and-reach.

*How to teach:* Explain static (hold), dynamic (controlled movement through range), and PNF (contract then relax to stretch further). Retest sit-and-reach and compare to week 1.

- **Q:** Which type of stretching is best before a sprint workout, and which after?
  **A:** Dynamic before; static (and PNF) after or in separate flexibility sessions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hip & hamstring mobility
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute hip and hamstring mobility routine (90/90, pigeon, strap hamstring).

*How to teach:* Lead a 20-minute hip and hamstring routine: 90/90 hip switches, pigeon stretch, supine strap hamstring stretch, hip flexor lunge. Hold 30 s; breathe.

- **Q:** Why might tight hip flexors matter for people who sit a lot?
  **A:** Prolonged sitting keeps the hip flexors shortened, which can limit hip extension and contribute to poor posture or low-back discomfort.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoulder & spine
*Student:* Complete thoracic rotations, wall slides, and cat-cow; assess shoulder flexibility.

*How to teach:* Thoracic rotations (open books), wall slides, and cat-cow. Assess shoulder flexibility with the Fitnessgram shoulder stretch (reach one hand over the shoulder and the other up the back; do fingers touch?).

- **Q:** How is the Fitnessgram shoulder stretch scored?
  **A:** Pass ('Y') if fingertips touch behind the back on that side; tested on both sides.

### Day 4 (Thursday): PNF with a partner
*Student:* Practice contract-relax hamstring stretching with a family member.

*How to teach:* Contract-relax: partner holds the student's straight leg at a mild stretch; student pushes the leg against the partner at ~25–50% effort for 6 seconds, relaxes, then gently moves further into the stretch for 20–30 s. Repeat 3 times each leg.

- **Q:** Describe contract-relax PNF in order.
  **A:** Stretch to mild tension, isometrically contract the muscle against resistance ~6 s, relax, then move into a deeper stretch and hold.
- **Q:** What is the partner's safety role?
  **A:** Hold the limb steady, apply only gentle pressure, and stop immediately if the stretcher says to.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Design and perform a 15-minute personal mobility routine.

*How to teach:* Student designs and performs a 15-minute routine targeting their tight areas, with hold times.

- **Q:** Why is foam rolling used?
  **A:** It may reduce perceived tightness and soreness and temporarily improve range of motion.

**Assessment:** Mobility routine.

**Answer key:** Mobility routine rubric. 4 – 15 minutes, targets the student's measured tight areas (cites sit-and-reach/shoulder results), includes dynamic and static elements with correct hold times (15–30 s), safe technique cues, and a frequency plan. 3 (proficient) – Covers hips, hamstrings, shoulders, spine with correct hold times and safe form. 2 – Incomplete or incorrect hold times. 1 – Not performed.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Modeling real parts

**Prepare:** Gather calipers and a few household objects with moderate complexity (bottle opener, spatula handle, cable clip). Read the Onshape Learning Center lesson on design intent.

**Watch for:**
- Modeling the shape without thinking about how it might change. Plan which dimensions are key and reference them so edits behave sensibly (e.g. a hole stays centered).
- Measuring once. Measure critical features twice and average; check for symmetry.
- Sketching on random faces. Choose sketch planes deliberately (often the default Front/Top/Right planes) to keep the feature tree stable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Design intent
*Student:* Learn how to plan a feature tree that captures design intent.

*How to teach:* Explain design intent with an example: a hole that should stay centered — constrain it to the midpoint, not a fixed distance from one edge. The student plans a feature tree for a simple part on paper.

- **Q:** What is design intent?
  **A:** How a model should behave when it is changed — the planned relationships that keep important features (symmetry, centered holes, equal sizes) correct.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure
*Student:* Measure a household object (e.g., a bottle opener) with calipers.

*How to teach:* Measure the household object, sketching it with all dimensions in the notebook.

- **Q:** Why measure critical dimensions more than once?
  **A:** To catch errors and reduce random measurement error by averaging.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Model
*Student:* Model the object in Onshape from your measurements.

*How to teach:* Model the object in Onshape following the feature-tree plan.

- **Q:** What is reference geometry?
  **A:** Planes, axes or points created to help position sketches and features (e.g. an offset plane).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modify
*Student:* Change a key dimension and verify the model updates correctly.

*How to teach:* Change a key dimension (length or thickness) and check the model updates without errors.

- **Q:** You changed the length and a hole ended up off the part. What went wrong?
  **A:** The hole was dimensioned from the wrong reference (e.g. a fixed distance from one end); constrain it to the midpoint or relevant edge to capture intent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Submit the model and a reflection on design intent.

*How to teach:* Submit the model and reflection; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Name one design-intent choice you made.
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g. used symmetric or equal constraints so features stay balanced.

**Assessment:** Modeled part.

**Answer key:** Modeled part rubric: 4 = model within ±0.5 mm of measured key dimensions, captures design intent (symmetric features constrained symmetric, holes located from meaningful references), a key dimension change updates correctly, and reflection explains the feature-tree plan; 3 = accurate but intent partly captured; 2 = model breaks on modification; 1 = incomplete. Reflection should name at least one design-intent decision, e.g. 'I used a midpoint constraint so the hole stays centered if the length changes.'
