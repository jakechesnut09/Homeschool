# Teacher's Guide — 11th Grade, Week 18

## English 11 – American Literature: Semester review and exam

**Prepare:** Assemble the semester reading journal and essays. Make a practice set of two unseen passages (e.g., a Dickinson poem not studied and a Thoreau paragraph) with questions. Plan two exam days.

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing period names without traits or texts. Every period on the timeline needs traits AND an example quotation.
- Writing a synthesis essay that summarizes each text in turn. Organize by idea, using several texts in each paragraph.

### Day 1 (Monday): Timeline review
*Student:* Build a literary-period timeline (Puritan to Realism) with two texts, traits, and a key quotation per period.

*How to teach:* The student builds a timeline: Puritan, Revolutionary/Enlightenment, Romantic, Transcendentalist, Dark Romantic, Realism/Naturalism, with two texts, traits, and one quotation each.

- **Q:** Give the traits and one text for Transcendentalism.
  **A:** Intuition, self-reliance, nature as divine, nonconformity; Emerson's 'Self-Reliance' ('Trust thyself').

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice passages
*Student:* Complete a practice set of unseen prose and poetry passages with multiple choice and short response.

*How to teach:* Complete the practice passages timed; review answers. Today's task from the plan: Complete a practice set of unseen prose and poetry passages with multiple choice and short response. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What strategy helps on unseen poetry?
  **A:** Read twice, paraphrase each stanza, note shifts (words like 'but'), then answer using exact lines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Writing review
*Student:* Revise the weakest essay of the semester using teacher comments; write a reflection on growth.

*How to teach:* The student revises the weakest essay and writes a reflection. Today's task from the plan: Revise the weakest essay of the semester using teacher comments; write a reflection on growth. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What should the growth reflection include?
  **A:** Specific skills improved (e.g., warrants), evidence from essays, and one goal for next semester.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam I
*Student:* Reading section: three unseen passages with multiple-choice and short constructed responses.

*How to teach:* Exam I: three unseen passages with multiple choice and short responses, 60 minutes. Today's task from the plan: Reading section: three unseen passages with multiple-choice and short constructed responses. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What makes a strong short constructed response?
  **A:** A direct answer, one or two short quotations, and a sentence explaining how the evidence supports the answer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam II
*Student:* Timed 50-minute synthesis essay: how American writers defined freedom from Bradford to Chopin.

*How to teach:* Exam II: 50-minute synthesis essay on how American writers defined freedom. Today's task from the plan: Timed 50-minute synthesis essay: how American writers defined freedom from Bradford to Chopin. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Name three texts with different views of freedom.
  **A:** Henry: political liberty; Thoreau: freedom of conscience; Douglass: literacy and self-ownership; Chopin: personal freedom from marriage.

**Assessment:** Two-part semester exam and revised essay portfolio.

**Answer key:** Exam I: score multiple choice by key; short responses on a 2-point scale. Exam II rubric (4-point): thesis about how definitions of freedom changed; at least four texts across periods; organized by idea; accurate quotations. Portfolio: revised essay shows substantive changes.

## Algebra II: Semester 1 review and exam

**Prepare:** Print or assemble a semester review packet (Khan Academy unit tests work well) and a 25-problem non-calculator exam plus a calculator section. Clear two quiet 60-minute blocks for the exam.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing the review by only re-reading notes. Have the student do mixed problems without knowing the topic first, which is what the exam requires.
- Leaving answers unchecked under time pressure. Teach a 5-minute end-of-test check of extraneous solutions and signs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: functions & quadratics
*Student:* Mixed review on transformations, systems, quadratics, and complex numbers.

*How to teach:* Student works a mixed set (transformations, systems, quadratics, complex numbers) for 40 minutes; you mark and they redo misses. Today's task from the plan: Mixed review on transformations, systems, quadratics, and complex numbers. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Solve x² + 4x + 8 = 0.
  **A:** x = (−4 ± √(16 − 32))/2 = −2 ± 2i.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: polynomials & rationals
*Student:* Mixed review on polynomial zeros, graphs, and rational functions.

*How to teach:* Mixed set on polynomial zeros, graphs, and rational functions; correct together. Today's task from the plan: Mixed review on polynomial zeros, graphs, and rational functions. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find all zeros of x³ − 2x² − 3x.
  **A:** x(x − 3)(x + 1): 0, 3, −1.
- **Q:** Name the asymptotes of y = (x + 1)/(x − 4).
  **A:** VA x = 4, HA y = 1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: radicals
*Student:* Mixed review on radicals and inverses; make a one-page formula sheet.

*How to teach:* Mixed set on radicals and inverses; student builds a one-page formula sheet as study (not for use on exam unless you allow it). Today's task from the plan: Mixed review on radicals and inverses; make a one-page formula sheet. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find the inverse of f(x) = (x − 2)³.
  **A:** f⁻¹(x) = ∛x + 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam part 1
*Student:* Non-calculator section (25 problems).

*How to teach:* Exam part 1: 25 non-calculator problems, 60 minutes, no help. Grade with the key that night. Today's task from the plan: Non-calculator section (25 problems). Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Simplify (3 − i)(2 + 5i).
  **A:** 6 + 15i − 2i − 5i² = 11 + 13i.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam part 2
*Student:* Calculator and modeling section with two extended-response tasks.

*How to teach:* Exam part 2: calculator and two extended-response modeling tasks. Afterward, the student corrects errors for half credit back. Today's task from the plan: Calculator and modeling section with two extended-response tasks. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** A ball's height is h = −16t² + 64t + 5. When is it highest and how high?
  **A:** t = 2 s; h = −64 + 128 + 5 = 69 ft.

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Score part 1 by item (1 pt each, 25 pts). Representative answers: (x+3)²−7 vertex (−3,−7); |x−2|<5 → −3<x<7; (2,−1) solves 2x+y=3, x−y=3; i³⁰=−1; x³−8 = (x−2)(x²+2x+4); (x²−x−2)/(x−2) = x+1; 8^(2/3)=4; √(x−1)=x−3 → x=5. Part 2 extended responses (4-point rubric each): 4 = correct model, correct answer with units, interpretation of domain/limits; 3 = minor error; 2 = correct setup, incomplete solution; 1 = some relevant work. Semester grade: 90%+ excellent, 75–89% proficient, below 75% reteach weak units in January.

## Physics of the Universe: Semester 1 review and exam

**Prepare:** Semester review packet (questions from Units 1–4); a practice CER prompt with data; two quiet exam blocks.

**Watch for:**
- Studying by rereading notes only. Use mixed practice problems and explain answers aloud.
- Dropping units. Require units at every step; many exam errors come from unit mistakes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: forces
*Student:* Review Newton's laws, momentum, and gravitation problems.

*How to teach:* Review Newton's laws, momentum, and gravitation problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A 3.0 kg object has a net force of 12 N. Acceleration?
  **A:** 4.0 m/s².
- **Q:** A 0.5 kg ball at 6 m/s hits a wall and bounces back at 4 m/s. Impulse?
  **A:** Δp = 0.5(−4 − 6) = −5 kg·m/s (5 N·s directed away from the wall).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: energy
*Student:* Review energy conservation and thermal transfer.

*How to teach:* Review energy conservation and thermal transfer. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Speed of an object after falling 10 m from rest?
  **A:** v = √(2 × 9.8 × 10) = 14 m/s.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: E&M
*Student:* Review circuits and electromagnetism.

*How to teach:* Review circuits and electromagnetism. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Current through a 10 Ω resistor with 5 V?
  **A:** 0.5 A.
- **Q:** How can you induce current in a coil?
  **A:** Change the magnetic field through it, e.g., by moving a magnet in or out.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam part 1
*Student:* Multiple-choice and short-answer.

*How to teach:* Exam part 1: multiple choice and short answer (50 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Multiple-choice and short-answer. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Define inertia.
  **A:** An object's tendency to resist changes in its motion; it depends on mass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam part 2
*Student:* Data-analysis and CER section.

*How to teach:* Exam part 2: data analysis and CER (50 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Data-analysis and CER section. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What three parts must a CER include?
  **A:** A claim answering the question, evidence (specific data), and reasoning that links the evidence to a scientific principle.

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam key (sample items): F = ma (5 kg, 2 m/s² → 10 N); third-law pair; momentum conservation (2 kg at 5 m/s sticks to 3 kg at rest → 2 m/s); gravitation inverse square; Kepler's third law; KE/PE calculations; power; Q = mcΔT; Ohm's law; series vs parallel; induction. Part 2 CER scored with the 4-point CER rubric. Grade bands: 90%+ excellent, 75–89% proficient, below 75% review weak units in January.

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: Semester 1 review and exam

**Prepare:** Print the semester review packet, a 5-document set on women's suffrage (e.g., Seneca Falls Declaration 1848, an anti-suffrage poster, California 1911 results, the 19th Amendment, a suffrage parade photo 1913), DBQ thesis prompts and rubric, and the semester exam (50 multiple-choice/stimulus questions plus a DBQ document set on the federal government's role 1900–1945).

**Watch for:**
- Students write a thesis that restates the prompt. A thesis must take a position and preview reasons.
- Students summarize documents instead of using them as evidence. Use documents to support a claim and explain how.
- Students skip sourcing. Explain how the author's point of view, purpose, audience or context affects the document.

### Day 1 (Monday): Timeline review
*Student:* Build an illustrated timeline from 1877 to 1945 with 25 events and cause-effect arrows.

*How to teach:* Build an illustrated timeline (1877–1945) with 25 events and cause-effect arrows.

- **Q:** Connect two events with cause and effect.
  **A:** Example: the Zimmermann Telegram (1917) → U.S. entry into WWI.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Document skills
*Student:* Practice sourcing, contextualizing, and corroborating with a 5-document set on women's suffrage.

*How to teach:* Practice sourcing, contextualizing and corroborating with the suffrage document set.

- **Q:** What does 'corroborate' mean?
  **A:** Check whether other sources agree with or contradict a document.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Thesis practice
*Student:* Write three DBQ thesis statements for practice prompts and self-score with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Write three DBQ thesis statements for practice prompts and self-score.

- **Q:** Strengthen: 'The New Deal changed America.'
  **A:** 'The New Deal permanently expanded the federal government's role by regulating banks and stocks, creating Social Security and protecting labor unions, though it did not end the Depression.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam part 1
*Student:* 50 multiple-choice and stimulus-based questions on Units 1–6.

*How to teach:* Give semester exam part 1 (50 questions).

- **Q:** (Exam day) Which unit felt hardest?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam part 2
*Student:* Timed 50-minute DBQ: How did the federal government's role change between 1900 and 1945?

*How to teach:* Give the timed 50-minute DBQ.

- **Q:** Name one way the federal role expanded under the New Deal.
  **A:** Social Security (1935), FDIC deposit insurance, SEC regulation, Wagner Act labor protections.

**Assessment:** Semester exam (multiple choice and DBQ).

**Answer key:** Semester exam part 1: score multiple choice using your answer key built from Units 1–6 (sample items: 14th Amendment's equal protection; Sherman Act 1890; 17th Amendment direct election; Zimmermann Telegram; 1924 quotas; buying on margin; FDIC; EO 9066). DBQ rubric (How did the federal government's role change between 1900 and 1945?): Thesis (1 pt) — e.g., it expanded from limited regulation to managing the economy, providing social welfare and mobilizing society for war; Contextualization (1); Evidence from documents (up to 3: uses 4+ documents to support the argument); Outside evidence (1: e.g., Federal Reserve, FDIC, Social Security, WPA, War Production Board); Sourcing (1); Complexity (1: continuity, such as limits on civil rights enforcement).

## Spanish IV: Repaso del semestre

**Prepare:** Assemble a review packet on subjunctive (present, imperfect, adjective and adverbial clauses), perfect tenses, and passive voice. Choose an authentic reading and a listening passage with multiple-choice questions, a persuasive essay prompt with sources, and a cultural comparison prompt.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing the subjunctive by keyword alone: check meaning (doubt vs. certainty, known vs. unknown antecedent, future vs. habitual).
- Running out of time in simulated conversations: answer each prompt fully and quickly, using fillers rather than silence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Repaso de gramática
*Student:* Complete a mixed grammar review on subjunctive, perfect tenses, and passive voice.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the mixed grammar review; reteach any category below 80%.

### Day 5 (Friday): Examen parte 2

## Lifetime Fitness: Semester fitness reassessment

**Prepare:** Gather stopwatch, measuring tape, sit-and-reach box, the journal with week 1 data and HFZ charts. Use the same mile route as week 1.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing to others instead of to your own baseline. Focus on personal change and HFZ.
- Assuming no improvement means training failed. Consider test-day factors (sleep, weather) and look at multiple measures.
- Writing goals that are already met. Revise goals upward or toward new components.

### Day 1 (Monday): Aerobic retest
*Student:* Retest the 1-mile run/walk and record resting heart rate.

*How to teach:* Take resting HR on waking or after 5 minutes seated. Warm up and run the mile; record time.

- **Q:** Resting HR dropped from 78 to 70. What might that indicate?
  **A:** Improved cardiovascular fitness — a stronger heart pumps more blood per beat, so it needs fewer beats at rest.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Muscular retest
*Student:* Retest push-ups, curl-ups, and trunk lift.

*How to teach:* Retest push-ups and curl-ups with cadence, and trunk lift.

- **Q:** Why use the same cadence as the baseline?
  **A:** So results are comparable and form standards are identical.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility retest
*Student:* Retest sit-and-reach and shoulder stretch.

*How to teach:* Retest sit-and-reach (both sides) and the shoulder stretch.

- **Q:** Which muscles does the sit-and-reach mainly measure?
  **A:** Hamstrings (and to some degree the lower back).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analysis
*Student:* Compare all results to baseline and graph progress.

*How to teach:* Student builds a table and bar graph: baseline vs. midyear for each test, with HFZ lines.

- **Q:** Mile time improved from 10:40 to 9:52. How many seconds faster?
  **A:** 48 seconds faster (640 s − 592 s).

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester reflection
*Student:* Write a semester reflection and revise SMART goals for spring.

*How to teach:* Student writes a semester reflection and revises SMART goals.

- **Q:** Name one revised goal type for spring.
  **A:** Example: maintain HFZ in aerobic capacity while raising push-ups from 18 to 25 by May 15.

**Assessment:** Semester fitness report.

**Answer key:** Semester fitness report rubric. 4 – All tests retested with correct protocol, a labeled comparison table/graph with HFZ, correct calculations of change, analysis linking results to training, and revised SMART goals. 3 (proficient) – Complete data, graph, explanation, revised goals. 2 – Partial data or analysis. 1 – Incomplete.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Semester review and exam

**Prepare:** Compile a review packet from Units 1–5 and design a semester exam: a written part (design process, sketching, measurement and statistics) and a practical (sketch three views of a block from an isometric; model a part in Onshape from a dimensioned sketch).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up which views share which dimension. Front/top share width, front/side share height, top/side share depth.
- Population vs. sample standard deviation. Use sample (n − 1) for samples.
- Rushing the CAD practical without constraining sketches. Under-defined sketches cause errors when editing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: sketching
*Student:* Mixed practice on isometric and multiview sketching.

*How to teach:* Mixed sketching practice: isometric and multiview problems; check alignment and line types.

- **Q:** Draw the front view of an L-shaped block. What lines show a hidden slot?
  **A:** Dashed hidden lines.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: measurement
*Student:* Practice measurement and statistics problems.

*How to teach:* Mixed measurement and statistics problems.

- **Q:** Find mean and range of 12.1, 12.3, 11.9, 12.0, 12.2 mm.
  **A:** Mean = 60.5 ÷ 5 = 12.1 mm; range = 12.3 − 11.9 = 0.4 mm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: CAD
*Student:* Practice CAD modeling of a review part.

*How to teach:* Model a review part in Onshape, practicing extrude, revolve, fillet and pattern.

- **Q:** Which feature hollows a part with uniform walls?
  **A:** Shell.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam part 1
*Student:* Written exam on design process, sketching, and statistics.

*How to teach:* Give the written exam (part 1) in the key.

- **Q:** What does a decision matrix help you do?
  **A:** Compare design ideas objectively against weighted criteria to select the best one.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam part 2
*Student:* CAD and sketching practical.

*How to teach:* Give the practical (part 2): sketching and CAD. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What shows a sketch is fully defined in Onshape?
  **A:** All its lines turn black.

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam part 1 (written) key — representative items: 1) Design process order. 2) Criteria vs. constraints. 3) Isometric axes 30° from horizontal. 4) Cabinet oblique uses half depth. 5) In third-angle projection the top view is above the front view. 6) Hidden lines are dashed. 7) 30 ± 0.2 → 29.8 to 30.2. 8) Mean/median/range of 3, 5, 5, 7, 10 → 6, 5, 7. 9) Sample SD of 2, 4, 6 → 2. 10) Density of 27 g / 10 cm³ → 2.7 g/cm³ (aluminum). 11) Revolve creates axisymmetric parts. 12) Revolute mate leaves one rotational DOF. Part 2 practical rubric: 4 = three correctly aligned views with hidden lines; Onshape part matches the sketch dimensions within tolerance with fully constrained sketches; 3 = minor errors; 2 = major errors; 1 = incomplete.
