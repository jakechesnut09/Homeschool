# Teacher's Guide — 11th Grade, Week 30

## English 11 – American Literature: Personal narrative and the college essay

**Prepare:** Print the eight UC Personal Insight Questions (universityofcalifornia.edu/apply). Amy Tan's 'Mother Tongue' as a model of voice plus one model student personal essay (many universities post examples). Sticky notes or paper for a memory map.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a list of accomplishments (a résumé in paragraphs). Focus on one specific scene and reflect on what it reveals about the writer.
- Using clichés ('hard work pays off,' 'I stepped out of my comfort zone'). Replace each with a concrete detail only this writer could include.

### Day 1 (Monday): Models
*Student:* Read two model personal essays (e.g., Amy Tan's 'Mother Tongue' as a model of voice); note structure and reflection.

*How to teach:* Read 'Mother Tongue' and one model essay. Have the student mark where each writer narrates a scene and where they reflect, and note the ratio. Discuss what makes the voice sound like a real person.

- **Q:** What makes Tan's 'Mother Tongue' a model of personal voice?
  **A:** She builds the essay on specific anecdotes (the stockbroker call, the hospital) and honest reflection on how language shaped her and her mother, in a conversational but precise style.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Read the UC Personal Insight Questions; brainstorm with a memory map and choose two prompts.

*How to teach:* Read all eight UC prompts. The student makes a memory map (people, places, challenges, turning points) and chooses two prompts that fit real stories. Today's task from the plan: Read the UC Personal Insight Questions; brainstorm with a memory map and choose two prompts. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How many UC Personal Insight Questions must applicants answer, and how long can each be?
  **A:** Four of the eight, up to 350 words each.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft a 350-word response focused on a single scene and what it reveals.

*How to teach:* Model opening a draft inside a scene ('The smoke alarm went off just as...'). The student drafts a 350-word response: about one third scene, the rest reflection and what they learned or did next.

- **Q:** What should a strong personal insight response focus on?
  **A:** One specific moment or experience that shows a quality, followed by reflection on what it reveals and how it shaped later actions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for specificity and reflection; cut clichés; read aloud for voice.

*How to teach:* Read the draft aloud together. The student circles vague phrases and clichés and replaces each with specific detail; cut any sentence that doesn't reveal something new.

- **Q:** Revise: 'I learned a lot from volunteering.'
  **A:** Name the specific lesson and moment, e.g., 'Teaching Mr. Ruiz to video-call his granddaughter taught me to slow down and explain one step at a time.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Submit a polished personal insight essay and write a second draft for another prompt.

*How to teach:* Collect the polished essay; the student starts a second prompt using the same process. Today's task from the plan: Submit a polished personal insight essay and write a second draft for another prompt. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What is the difference between telling and showing in a personal essay?
  **A:** Telling states a trait ('I am persistent'); showing gives actions and details that let the reader see it ('I rewrote the code eleven times before the robot turned left').

**Assessment:** Personal narrative rubric.

**Answer key:** Personal narrative rubric (4-point per row): Focus (one clear experience that answers the prompt); Specificity (concrete details, no clichés); Reflection (insight into growth and what it shows about the writer); Voice and conventions (sounds authentic, error-free, within 350 words). Proficient = a focused scene with genuine reflection and only minor wordiness.

## Algebra II: Series and sigma notation

**Prepare:** Spreadsheet. Tell the Gauss 1-to-100 story.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing the number of terms with the last term in Σ. Count terms as (top − bottom + 1).
- Applying the infinite-series formula when |r| ≥ 1. Such series diverge (no finite sum).

### Day 1 (Monday): Sigma notation
*Student:* Write and evaluate sums in sigma notation.

*How to teach:* Model reading and writing Σ; student evaluates six sums by hand. Today's task from the plan: Write and evaluate sums in sigma notation. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Evaluate Σ_{k=1}^{5} (2k + 1).
  **A:** 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 35.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Arithmetic series
*Student:* Derive Gauss's formula for arithmetic series and apply it.

*How to teach:* Write the sum forwards and backwards and add to derive S = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2. Today's task from the plan: Derive Gauss's formula for arithmetic series and apply it. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find 1 + 2 + … + 100.
  **A:** 100(101)/2 = 5,050.
- **Q:** Find Σ_{k=1}^{20} (3k + 2).
  **A:** a₁ = 5, a₂₀ = 62: 20(67)/2 = 670.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geometric series
*Student:* Derive S_n = a(1−r^n)/(1−r) and apply to mortgage/loan payment problems.

*How to teach:* Derive S − rS to get S_n = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r). Apply to a loan: payment = P·i/(1 − (1 + i)^(−n)). Today's task from the plan: Derive S_n = a(1−r^n)/(1−r) and apply to mortgage/loan payment problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Sum 3 + 6 + 12 + … (8 terms).
  **A:** 3(1 − 2⁸)/(1 − 2) = 3·255 = 765.
- **Q:** Monthly payment on $20,000 at 6%/year for 60 months?
  **A:** i = 0.005: 20000(0.005)/(1 − 1.005^(−60)) ≈ $386.66.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Infinite geometric series
*Student:* Explore convergence when |r| < 1 with a spreadsheet.

*How to teach:* Spreadsheet partial sums for r = 1/2, 0.9, 1.1; observe which level off. S = a/(1 − r) when |r| < 1. Today's task from the plan: Explore convergence when |r| < 1 with a spreadsheet. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + ….
  **A:** 8/(1 − 1/2) = 16.
- **Q:** Does Σ 2(1.1)^n converge?
  **A:** No: r = 1.1 > 1, the sums grow without bound.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 8 test on sequences and series.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test (about 50 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Unit 8 test on sequences and series. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Write 2 + 4 + 6 + … + 40 in sigma notation and evaluate.
  **A:** Σ_{k=1}^{20} 2k = 20(2 + 40)/2 = 420.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 sample answers: 1) a₁₂ of 5, 9, 13: 49. 2) a₇ of 81, 27, 9: 1/9. 3) Σ_{k=1}^{4} k² = 30. 4) sum of first 30 odd numbers = 900. 5) 1 + 3 + 9 + … + 3⁶ = 1,093. 6) 12 − 6 + 3 − … infinite = 12/(1.5) = 8. 7) Explain why 1 + 2 + 4 + … has no sum. Rubric: formula (1), substitution (1), answer (1).

## Physics of the Universe: Observing the night sky

**Prepare:** A free star chart app (e.g., Stellarium); binoculars; a clear evening and a dark spot. Check weather and Moon phase (a new Moon is best).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stars are all the same distance away. The 'constellation' stars can be at very different distances.
- Thinking a light-year measures time. It is a distance: how far light travels in one year (about 9.46 trillion km).

### Day 1 (Monday): Parallax
*Student:* Measure parallax with thumb and objects; relate to stellar distances.

*How to teach:* Hold a thumb at arm's length; close one eye, then the other, and watch it shift against the background. Relate to stellar parallax. Today's task from the plan: Measure parallax with thumb and objects; relate to stellar distances. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A star has a parallax of 0.5 arcseconds. How far away is it?
  **A:** d = 1/p = 2 parsecs ≈ 6.5 light-years.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Night observation
*Student:* Observe the night sky and record constellations and planets.

*How to teach:* Observe the night sky for 30 minutes; record constellations, bright stars, planets, and the Moon. Today's task from the plan: Observe the night sky and record constellations and planets. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** How can you tell a planet from a star?
  **A:** Planets usually shine steadily (twinkle less), lie along the ecliptic, and move against the background stars over days.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Light-years
*Student:* Calculate distances in light-years.

*How to teach:* Calculate distances in light-years and light travel times. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How long does sunlight take to reach Earth?
  **A:** 1.5 × 10¹¹ m ÷ 3 × 10⁸ m/s = 500 s ≈ 8.3 minutes.
- **Q:** Proxima Centauri is 4.24 light-years away. What does that mean?
  **A:** Its light took 4.24 years to reach us; we see it as it was then.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Telescopes
*Student:* Research how telescopes detect different wavelengths.

*How to teach:* Research how telescopes detect different wavelengths (radio, infrared/JWST, X-ray/Chandra). Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why are X-ray telescopes placed in space?
  **A:** Earth's atmosphere absorbs X-rays, so they can only be observed above it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 7 test on the Sun and stars.

*How to teach:* Unit 7 test. Today's task from the plan: Unit 7 test on the Sun and stars. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Why do we see distant galaxies as they were in the past?
  **A:** Their light took millions or billions of years to reach us.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test key (sample): Sun's layers and fusion; E = mc²; half-life (1/8 after 3); alpha/beta decay equations; H-R diagram regions; life cycles; parallax (d = 1/p); light-year definition; spectra (emission vs continuous).

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: The Reagan era and the end of the Cold War

**Prepare:** Print Reagan's First Inaugural excerpt (Jan. 20, 1981), data for 1980–1990 (top marginal income tax rate 70% → 50% in 1982 → 28% in 1988; unemployment 7.1% in 1980, peak 10.8% late 1982, 5.3% in 1989; federal debt about $0.9 trillion in 1980 → about $2.9 trillion in 1989), 'Tear Down This Wall' (June 12, 1987), nuclear freeze rally photos, and debate sources.

**Watch for:**
- Students think Reagan's tax cuts balanced the budget. Deficits and debt grew sharply in the 1980s because of tax cuts and increased defense spending.
- Students think the Cold War ended because of a single speech. Many factors combined: Soviet economic stagnation, Gorbachev's reforms, U.S. pressure and diplomacy, and Eastern European movements.
- Students confuse glasnost and perestroika. Glasnost = openness (more free speech); perestroika = restructuring of the economy.

### Day 1 (Monday): New Right
*Student:* Read Reagan's First Inaugural ('government is the problem') and explain the rise of modern conservatism.

*How to teach:* Read Reagan's First Inaugural and explain the rise of modern conservatism.

- **Q:** Which groups formed the New Right coalition?
  **A:** Evangelical Christians, fiscal conservatives, anti-communists and many suburban and Sunbelt voters.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reaganomics
*Student:* Graph tax rates, deficits, and unemployment 1980–1990 and evaluate supply-side economics.

*How to teach:* Graph tax rates, deficits and unemployment 1980–1990; evaluate supply-side economics.

- **Q:** What happened to the federal debt during the 1980s?
  **A:** It roughly tripled, from about $0.9 trillion to about $2.9 trillion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cold War endgame
*Student:* Read the 'Tear Down This Wall' speech and study Gorbachev's reforms and the fall of the Berlin Wall.

*How to teach:* Read 'Tear Down This Wall'; study Gorbachev's reforms and the fall of the Wall.

- **Q:** What did glasnost and perestroika mean?
  **A:** Glasnost: openness and freer speech; perestroika: restructuring the Soviet economy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Debate
*Student:* Hold a structured debate on 'What ended the Cold War: U.S. pressure or Soviet internal collapse?' citing two sources each.

*How to teach:* Hold a structured debate on what ended the Cold War.

- **Q:** Give one argument for each side.
  **A:** U.S. pressure: defense buildup and SDI strained the Soviet economy. Internal collapse: economic stagnation, the Afghanistan war, nationalism and Gorbachev's reforms unraveled the system.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the 1980s with a stimulus-based short answer.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz with a stimulus-based short answer.

- **Q:** When did the Berlin Wall fall?
  **A:** November 9, 1989.

**Assessment:** Quiz and debate rubric.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) New Right — evangelicals, anti-tax and anti-communist conservatives. 2) 'Government is not the solution to our problem; government is the problem.' 3) Reaganomics — tax cuts, deregulation, reduced domestic spending, defense buildup. 4) Supply-side theory — lower taxes boost investment and growth. 5) Deficits/debt rose. 6) Gorbachev (1985) — glasnost and perestroika. 7) INF Treaty (1987). 8) Berlin Wall fell Nov. 9, 1989. 9) USSR dissolved Dec. 1991. 10) Nuclear freeze movement. Debate rubric: clear position, two cited sources each side, rebuttal, respectful delivery.

## Spanish IV: Teatro y cine

**Prepare:** Obtain Federico García Lorca's La casa de Bernarda Alba (1936) from a library; read Act I in advance. Choose a Spanish-language film: 'Coco' (PG) in Spanish, or clips of 'El laberinto del fauno' (rated R for graphic violence — preview and choose clips carefully). Chart reported speech: present → imperfect, preterite → pluperfect, future → conditional, command → imperfect subjunctive.

**Watch for:**
- Not shifting tenses in reported speech: 'Tengo hambre,' dijo → Dijo que tenía hambre.
- Reporting commands with the indicative (*Bernarda dijo que se callan): Bernarda les dijo que se callaran.
- Forgetting to change pronouns and time words: hoy → ese día, mañana → al día siguiente, aquí → allí.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lorca
*Student:* Read Act I of La casa de Bernarda Alba aloud by roles and discuss oppression and silence.

*How to teach:* Read Act I aloud by roles; discuss oppression and silence.

- **Q:** What does Bernarda impose on her daughters after her husband's death?
  **A:** Eight years of mourning, shut inside the house, cut off from men and the outside world.
- **Q:** Who is Pepe el Romano, and why does he matter in Act I?
  **A:** A young man of the village who is to court Angustias, the eldest daughter, who has inherited money; the other daughters, especially Adela, are jealous.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Estilo indirecto
*Student:* Learn reported speech (Bernarda dijo que...) and convert 15 direct quotes.

*How to teach:* Teach reported speech; your child converts 15 direct quotes (from the play or invented).

- **Q:** Convert: Bernarda: '¡Silencio!' (command to her daughters)
  **A:** Bernarda les ordenó a sus hijas que se callaran (que guardaran silencio).
- **Q:** Convert: Adela dijo: 'Me pondré el vestido verde.'
  **A:** Adela dijo que se pondría el vestido verde.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cine
*Student:* Watch a Spanish-language film or extended clips and complete a viewing guide.

*How to teach:* Watch the film or clips with a viewing guide (characters, setting, conflict, themes, memorable scenes).

- **Q:** In 'Coco', what is the role of the ofrenda in the story?
  **A:** Photos on the family ofrenda allow the dead to cross over to visit on Día de Muertos; being remembered keeps a person alive in the Land of the Dead.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reseña
*Student:* Write a 250-word film review using reported speech and opinion phrases.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 250-word film review using reported speech and opinion phrases.

- **Q:** Write a sentence reporting what a character said.
  **A:** Example: Miguel le dijo a su familia que quería ser músico.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Present the review orally in 2 minutes.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the review orally in 2 minutes.

- **Q:** Give a recommendation phrase.
  **A:** Recomiendo esta película a quien le guste... / Les aconsejo que la vean porque...

**Assessment:** Film review and presentation.

**Answer key:** Film review rubric (250 words): 4 = summary without spoilers, analysis of characters/themes/cinematography, opinion phrases, 3+ correct reported-speech sentences, recommendation; 3 = minor errors; 2 = mostly summary; 1 = incomplete. Oral presentation (2 minutes): 4 = clear, fluent, accurate.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: New work festival

**Prepare:** Binders for scripts, music stands or chairs, volunteer actors. Read Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process (4 steps). Invite an audience.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a staged reading needs memorization. Actors hold scripts; focus is on hearing the play.
- Talkbacks becoming opinion free-for-alls. Use the Critical Response Process order.
- Defending the play in a talkback. The playwright listens and asks questions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Casting & rehearsal
*Student:* Cast your ten-minute play with family/friends and rehearse it as a staged reading.

*How to teach:* Cast and rehearse the ten-minute play as a staged reading (one person reads stage directions).

- **Q:** What is a staged reading?
  **A:** A performance where actors read from scripts with minimal staging, to present and develop a new play.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Polish
*Student:* Refine stage-direction reading and transitions; finalize the devised piece.

*How to teach:* Refine stage-direction reading and transitions; finalize devised piece.

- **Q:** Why read only key stage directions?
  **A:** To keep momentum while giving the audience necessary information.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Festival
*Student:* Present the staged reading and devised piece to an audience.

*How to teach:* Present the staged reading and devised piece.

- **Q:** What should the audience understand before a staged reading?
  **A:** That it's a work in progress, read with scripts, to help the playwright develop the play.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Talkback
*Student:* Lead a talkback using Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process and record feedback.

*How to teach:* Lead the talkback with the Critical Response Process: 1) statements of meaning, 2) artist asks questions, 3) responders ask neutral questions, 4) opinion time (with the artist's permission).

- **Q:** List the four steps of Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process.
  **A:** 1) Statements of meaning, 2) Artist as questioner, 3) Neutral questions from responders, 4) Opinion time (permissioned opinions).

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a 400-word reflection on what you learned as a playwright and deviser.

*How to teach:* 400-word reflection on learning as playwright and deviser.

- **Q:** What is a 'neutral question'?
  **A:** A question without an embedded opinion, e.g., 'What were you going for with the ending?' rather than 'Why was the ending so confusing?'

**Assessment:** Festival rubric and reflection.

**Answer key:** Festival rubric. 4 – Staged reading well-rehearsed with clear transitions; devised piece polished; talkback follows all four CRP steps and feedback recorded; 400-word reflection analyzes specific learning and next revisions. 3 (proficient) – Both pieces presented, CRP used, reflection complete. 2 – Talkback disorganized or reflection thin. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness: Nutrition for performance

**Prepare:** Gather 5 food labels and print MyPlate guidelines (myplate.gov). Install a food-log app or use a notebook. Parental note: keep the focus on fueling performance, not weight; watch for signs of disordered eating.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking carbohydrates are bad. Carbohydrates are the main fuel for moderate-to-intense exercise.
- Believing more protein always means more muscle. Beyond needs, extra protein doesn't build more muscle; training does.
- Using sports drinks for all workouts. Water is enough for most sessions under about an hour.

### Day 1 (Monday): Macronutrients
*Student:* Learn roles of carbohydrate, protein, and fat; read five food labels; complete a 30-minute workout.

*How to teach:* Teach macronutrients: carbohydrate (4 kcal/g, main exercise fuel), protein (4 kcal/g, repair/build tissue), fat (9 kcal/g, long-duration fuel, vitamin absorption). Read five labels. Then a 30-minute workout.

- **Q:** A snack has 20 g carbohydrate, 5 g protein, and 8 g fat. How many calories?
  **A:** 20×4 + 5×4 + 8×9 = 80 + 20 + 72 = 172 kcal.
- **Q:** Which macronutrient is the main fuel for high-intensity exercise?
  **A:** Carbohydrate.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Food log
*Student:* Keep a 3-day food log and compare to MyPlate recommendations.

*How to teach:* Keep a 3-day food log; compare with MyPlate food groups (half the plate fruits and vegetables, grains with at least half whole, protein, dairy).

- **Q:** What does MyPlate recommend filling half your plate with?
  **A:** Fruits and vegetables.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timing & hydration
*Student:* Plan pre- and post-workout snacks and calculate daily fluid needs; complete a tempo run.

*How to teach:* Plan pre-workout (1–3 hours before, carb-rich, moderate protein, low fat/fiber) and post-workout (within about 1–2 hours, carbs + protein) snacks. Discuss hydration: drink regularly, monitor urine color (pale yellow); estimate needs. Then a tempo run.

- **Q:** Give an example of a good post-workout snack.
  **A:** Chocolate milk, Greek yogurt with fruit, or a turkey sandwich — carbohydrate plus protein.
- **Q:** What urine color suggests good hydration?
  **A:** Pale yellow (like lemonade).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Meal plan
*Student:* Plan one day of meals for an active teen within a family budget.

*How to teach:* Student plans one day of meals for an active teen within a budget, using MyPlate groups.

- **Q:** Name two budget-friendly protein sources.
  **A:** Beans/lentils, eggs, canned tuna, peanut butter, chicken thighs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Nutrition quiz and food-log analysis.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and review the food-log analysis.

- **Q:** How many calories per gram are in fat vs. carbohydrate?
  **A:** Fat 9 kcal/g; carbohydrate 4 kcal/g.

**Assessment:** Food-log analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Carbs 4, protein 4, fat 9 kcal/g. 2) Carbohydrate = primary fuel for intense exercise. 3) Protein = growth and repair. 4) Pre-workout meal 1–3 h before: carbs, moderate protein, low fat/fiber. 5) Recovery: carbs + protein. 6) Pale yellow urine = hydrated. 7) Half the plate fruits/vegetables. Food-log analysis rubric: proficient = 3 complete days, compared to MyPlate groups with specific gaps/strengths and two realistic changes.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Capstone: ideation and selection

**Prepare:** Have sketch paper, isometric paper, sticky notes and a decision-matrix template. Print the SCAMPER prompts (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify/Magnify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse/Rearrange).

**Watch for:**
- Judging ideas during brainstorming. Defer judgment: quantity first, evaluate later.
- Developing only one concept. Developing three forces comparison and often combines strengths.
- Weighted matrices where weights don't reflect the brief. Weights should come from the most important criteria in the design brief.

### Day 1 (Monday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Generate at least 20 ideas using brainstorming and SCAMPER.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm at least 20 ideas in timed rounds, then use SCAMPER on existing products for more.

- **Q:** Apply SCAMPER 'Combine' to a backpack and a lamp.
  **A:** Example: a backpack with a built-in LED light for walking at night (any reasonable combination).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Concept sketches
*Student:* Develop three concepts into annotated isometric sketches.

*How to teach:* Develop three concepts into annotated isometric sketches with materials and key dimensions.

- **Q:** What should annotations on a concept sketch include?
  **A:** Key dimensions, materials, how parts move or connect, and how it meets the criteria.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feedback
*Student:* Get feedback on the three concepts from your user.

*How to teach:* Show the user the three concepts; record their reactions and preferences.

- **Q:** Why get user feedback before choosing a concept?
  **A:** The user may value different features than the designer expects, preventing wasted work on the wrong concept.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Selection
*Student:* Select a final concept with a weighted decision matrix.

*How to teach:* Select a final concept with a weighted decision matrix.

- **Q:** Concept X scores 4, 3, 5 on criteria weighted 3, 2, 1; Concept Y scores 3, 5, 4. Which wins?
  **A:** X: 12 + 6 + 5 = 23; Y: 9 + 10 + 4 = 23 — a tie; revisit with user feedback or an added criterion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present the selected concept and justification.

*How to teach:* The student presents the selected concept and justification.

- **Q:** What evidence best justifies your choice?
  **A:** Matrix scores tied to the brief's criteria plus specific user feedback.

**Assessment:** Concept selection.

**Answer key:** Concept selection rubric: 4 = at least 20 documented ideas (including SCAMPER variations); three annotated isometric concept sketches with key dimensions and materials; recorded user feedback on each; weighted decision matrix with weights tied to the brief and scores justified; clear presentation of the chosen concept and why; 3 = all parts, thin justification; 2 = fewer than three concepts or no user feedback; 1 = incomplete.
