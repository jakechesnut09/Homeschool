## English 11 – American Literature: Civil rights rhetoric

**Prepare:** King's 'Letter from Birmingham Jail' (full text at the King Institute, Stanford), Malcolm X's 'The Ballot or the Bullet' (excerpt), and JFK's Inaugural Address (text and video at jfklibrary.org).

**Watch for:**
- Seeing King's approach as passive. He defends direct action and calls himself an 'extremist for love.'
- Seeing Malcolm X as only calling for violence. The speech centers on voting rights and self-defense if the ballot fails.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kennedy
*Student:* Read/listen to the Inaugural Address; mark antimetabole and parallel structure.

*How to teach:* Listen to the JFK Inaugural while reading. The student marks antimetabole and parallel structure. Today's task from the plan: Read/listen to the Inaugural Address; mark antimetabole and parallel structure. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give JFK's best-known antimetabole.
  **A:** 'Ask not what your country can do for you—ask what you can do for your country.'
- **Q:** Find another example.
  **A:** 'Let us never negotiate out of fear. But let us never fear to negotiate.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Letter I
*Student:* Read the first half of 'Letter from Birmingham Jail'; outline how King rebuts each criticism from the clergymen.

*How to teach:* Read the first half of the Letter. The student outlines each criticism and King's rebuttal. Today's task from the plan: Read the first half of 'Letter from Birmingham Jail'; outline how King rebuts each criticism from the clergymen. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How does King answer being called an 'outsider'?
  **A:** He was invited and says 'Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere.'
- **Q:** How does King respond to 'wait'?
  **A:** 'Wait' has almost always meant 'Never'; 'justice too long delayed is justice denied.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Letter II
*Student:* Finish the Letter; analyze King's definition of just and unjust laws and his appeal to Founders.

*How to teach:* Finish the Letter. Have the student explain just vs. unjust laws and King's appeal to the Founders.   **A:** A just law 'squares with the moral law or the law of God'; an unjust law 'degrades human personality' and is out of harmony with moral law.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Malcolm X
*Student:* Read 'The Ballot or the Bullet' excerpt; compare purpose and audience with King.

*How to teach:* Read the Malcolm X excerpt. The student compares purpose, audience, and tone with King. ## Algebra II: Arithmetic and geometric sequences

**Prepare:** Stacking cups (or blocks), ruler. A ball and tape measure for Day 4.

**Watch for:**
- Using n instead of n − 1 in a_n = a₁ + (n − 1)d. Test with n = 1: the formula must give a₁.
- Confusing arithmetic (add d) with geometric (multiply by r). Check differences and ratios first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Patterns
*Student:* Build a stacking-cup pattern and write recursive and explicit rules.

*How to teach:* Measure the height of 1, 2, 3, 4 nested cups. Write a recursive rule and an explicit rule. Today's task from the plan: Build a stacking-cup pattern and write recursive and explicit rules. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A cup stack is 12 cm for 1 cup and grows 1.5 cm per added cup. Give recursive and explicit rules.
  **A:** Recursive: h₁ = 12, hₙ = hₙ₋₁ + 1.5. Explicit: hₙ = 12 + 1.5(n − 1).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Arithmetic sequences
*Student:* Find terms and write formulas a_n = a₁ + (n−1)d.

*How to teach:* Model finding d, writing a_n, and finding any term. Today's task from the plan: Find terms and write formulas a_n = a₁ + (n−1)d. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** a₁ = 7, d = 4. Find a₂₀.
  **A:** 7 + 19·4 = 83.
- **Q:** Write a formula for 3, 10, 17, ….
  **A:** aₙ = 3 + 7(n − 1) = 7n − 4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geometric sequences
*Student:* Find terms and write formulas a_n = a₁·r^(n−1); connect to exponential functions.

*How to teach:* Model finding r, a_n = a₁r^(n−1), and the link to y = ab^x. Today's task from the plan: Find terms and write formulas a_n = a₁·r^(n−1); connect to exponential functions. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Find a formula and the 8th term of 2, 6, 18, ….
  **A:** aₙ = 2·3^(n−1); a₈ = 2·3⁷ = 4,374.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modeling
*Student:* Model a savings plan and a bouncing-ball height sequence with real measurement.

*How to teach:* Drop a ball from 2 m and measure rebound heights; compute ratios. Also model a savings plan that adds $50/month. Today's task from the plan: Model a savings plan and a bouncing-ball height sequence with real measurement. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** A ball dropped from 2 m rebounds to 60% of its height each time. How high after the 3rd bounce?
  **A:** 2(0.6)³ = 0.432 m.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on sequence formulas.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 30 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on sequence formulas. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Is 5, 10, 20, 40 arithmetic or geometric? Give the next term.
  **A:** Geometric with r = 2; next term 80.

**Assessment:** Sequences quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: 1) 4, 9, 14, … a₁₅ = 74. 2) explicit for 20, 17, 14: aₙ = 23 − 3n. 3) 3, −6, 12, … aₙ = 3(−2)^(n−1), a₆ = −96. 4) recursive a₁=5, aₙ=2aₙ₋₁ → 5, 10, 20, 40. 5) Which term of 2, 5, 8 … is 62? n = 21. 6) $1,000 earning 5%/yr: aₙ = 1000(1.05)^(n−1) for the start of year n.

## Physics of the Universe: Stars and the elements

**Prepare:** Star data table (name, temperature, luminosity relative to the Sun, color) of about 20 stars; a shoebox, a CD (or piece of one), tape, foil for a slit; light sources: incandescent bulb, LED, fluorescent/CFL, sodium streetlight if available. Never point the spectroscope at the Sun.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking star color is random. Color shows surface temperature: blue stars are hottest, red stars coolest.
- Thinking all elements formed in the Big Bang. Only hydrogen, helium, and a little lithium did; heavier elements were made in stars and stellar explosions.

### Day 1 (Monday): H-R diagram
*Student:* Plot star data on an H-R diagram.

*How to teach:* The student plots the stars on an H-R diagram (temperature decreasing to the right, luminosity up) and labels main sequence, giants, supergiants, white dwarfs.

- **Q:** Where is the Sun on the H-R diagram?
  **A:** On the main sequence, in the middle (a yellow G-type star, about 5,800 K).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stellar life cycles
*Student:* Trace life cycles of low- and high-mass stars.

*How to teach:* Trace life cycles with a flowchart for low-mass vs high-mass stars. Today's task from the plan: Trace life cycles of low- and high-mass stars. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Describe the life cycle of a Sun-like star.
  **A:** Nebula → protostar → main sequence → red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf.
- **Q:** What happens to a star much more massive than the Sun?
  **A:** Main sequence → red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spectroscopy
*Student:* Build a CD spectroscope and view different light sources.

*How to teach:* Build the CD spectroscope and compare spectra of light sources. Today's task from the plan: Build a CD spectroscope and view different light sources. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does a fluorescent bulb show bright lines but an incandescent bulb a continuous rainbow?
  **A:** The fluorescent bulb's excited gas atoms emit only specific wavelengths (emission lines); the hot filament emits all wavelengths (continuous spectrum).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nucleosynthesis
*Student:* Create a periodic table color-coded by element origin.

*How to teach:* The student color-codes a periodic table by element origin (Big Bang, low-mass stars, massive stars/supernovae, neutron-star mergers, cosmic rays). Today's task from the plan: Create a periodic table color-coded by element origin. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does fusion in stars stop producing energy at iron?
  **A:** Fusing iron consumes energy rather than releasing it; heavier elements form in supernovae and neutron-star mergers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present how stars produce elements.

*How to teach:* The student presents (5 minutes) how stars produce elements. Today's task from the plan: Present how stars produce elements. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What does 'we are made of star stuff' mean?
  **A:** The carbon, oxygen, calcium, and iron in our bodies were made by nuclear fusion in earlier stars and scattered by stellar explosions.

**Assessment:** Presentation.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric (4-point per row): accurate life cycles for low- and high-mass stars; explanation of fusion up to iron and heavier elements from supernovae/mergers; use of the H-R diagram or periodic table as evidence; clear visuals and delivery. Proficient = correct sequence of element formation with one example from each source.

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: Nixon, Watergate, and the 1970s

**Prepare:** Print Nixon's resignation speech (Aug. 8, 1974), United States v. Nixon (1974) excerpts, Earth Day 1970 photos, a summary of the 1969 Santa Barbara oil spill, oil price and inflation/unemployment data for the 1970s, and Carter's 'Crisis of Confidence' (July 15, 1979) excerpt.

**Watch for:**
- Students think Nixon was impeached and removed. The House Judiciary Committee approved articles of impeachment, but Nixon resigned before a House vote.
- Students think Nixon was hostile to all environmental laws. The EPA (1970), Clean Air Act (1970) and Endangered Species Act (1973) were created under Nixon.
- Students think stagflation is just inflation. It is high inflation plus high unemployment and slow growth at the same time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nixon
*Student:* Analyze Nixon's domestic and foreign policies including détente and China.

*How to teach:* Analyze Nixon's domestic and foreign policies including détente and China.

*How to teach:* Read U.S. v. Nixon and explain the constitutional crisis.

Nixon (1974, 8–0) — executive privilege is not absolute; Nixon must turn over the tapes. 6) Nixon resigned Aug. 9, 1974; Ford pardoned him. 7) Santa Barbara oil spill (1969) — helped spark Earth Day (April 22, 1970) and environmental laws. 8) EPA (1970), Clean Air Act (1970). 9) 1973 OPEC oil embargo — prices roughly quadrupled. 10) Stagflation. 11) Carter's 'Crisis of Confidence' speech.

## Spanish IV: El realismo mágico: García Márquez

**Prepare:** Obtain Gabriel García Márquez's 'Un señor muy viejo con unas alas enormes' (1968) from a library anthology (under copyright; do not copy). Gather background on García Márquez (born 1927 in Aracataca, Colombia; Nobel Prize 1982; Cien años de soledad, 1967). Write a quiz on the story and past tenses.

**Watch for:**
- Defining magical realism as fantasy: in magical realism, supernatural events appear in an otherwise realistic world and characters treat them as ordinary.
- Assuming the townspeople honor the angel: they treat him as a curiosity and a source of profit, showing human cruelty and indifference.
- Using the pluperfect where the preterite suffices: use había + participle only for an action before another past action.

### Day 1 (Monday): Contexto
*Student:* Read about García Márquez and Colombia and define magical realism.

*How to teach:* Read about García Márquez and Colombia; define magical realism with examples.

- **Q:** What is 'el realismo mágico'?
  **A:** A literary style in which supernatural or impossible events occur in a realistic setting and are described as normal by the narrator and characters.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lectura I
*Student:* Read the first half of 'Un señor muy viejo con unas alas enormes' and annotate supernatural details treated as ordinary.

*How to teach:* Read the first half; your child annotates supernatural details treated as ordinary.

- **Q:** How do Pelayo and Elisenda first react to the winged man?
  **A:** They are puzzled more than amazed, notice that he looks like a ragged old sailor, and a neighbor tells them he is an angel; they shut him in the chicken coop.
- **Q:** Why does Father Gonzaga doubt the man is an angel?
  **A:** He does not understand Latin (the language of God's ministers) and looks too human and miserable; the priest writes to Church authorities for a ruling.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lectura II
*Student:* Finish the story and discuss how the community treats the angel.

*How to teach:* Finish the story; discuss how the community treats the angel and what this reveals.

- **Q:** How do Pelayo and Elisenda profit from the angel?
  **A:** They charge admission to see him and become rich enough to build a two-story mansion.
- **Q:** How does the story end?
  **A:** The angel survives, grows new feathers, and finally flies away; Elisenda watches until he disappears, relieved.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Escritura creativa
*Student:* Write a 300-word magical realist story using preterite, imperfect, and pluperfect.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 300-word magical realist story using preterite, imperfect, and pluperfect.

- **Q:** Write a magical realist opening.
  **A:** Example: El martes amaneció lloviendo flores amarillas, y como siempre, mi abuela salió a barrerlas antes del desayuno.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Quiz on the story and past tenses.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on the story and past tenses.

- **Q:** Fill in: Cuando el ángel se fue volando, Pelayo y Elisenda ya ___ (construir) una casa de dos pisos.
  **A:** habían construido

**Assessment:** Creative story and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz sample key: 1) Pelayo and Elisenda find an old man with huge wings in their courtyard after days of rain 2) a neighbor woman says he is an angel 3) they keep him in a chicken coop and charge admission, and become rich 4) Father Gonzaga doubts he is an angel because he does not understand Latin 5) the crowds leave for a woman who was turned into a spider for disobeying her parents 6) his 'miracles' are strange or useless 7) at the end he grows new feathers and flies away, and Elisenda watches with relief. Grammar: encontraron (preterite), llovía (imperfect), había llovido (pluperfect). Creative story rubric (300 words): 4 = one supernatural element treated as ordinary, realistic setting, preterite/imperfect/pluperfect used correctly, vivid detail; 3 = minor errors; 2 = fantasy without realism or many errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Devised theatre

**Prepare:** Find short documentary clips about Tectonic Theater Project's The Laramie Project (verbatim interviews in Laramie, Wyoming after the 1998 murder of Matthew Shepard) and about Frantic Assembly; preview content. Prepare interview questions on a theme such as 'home.' Get permission to record interviewees.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking devised theatre has no structure. It is built collaboratively from sources, then shaped and edited.
- Editing verbatim text to change meaning. Verbatim theatre must respect speakers' actual words and consent.
- Moving randomly in Viewpoints. Respond to tempo, space, and others with awareness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Devising companies
*Student:* Watch clips about Tectonic Theater Project (The Laramie Project) and Frantic Assembly; list their devising methods.

*How to teach:* Watch clips; list devising methods (interviews, moment work, physical scores, chair duets, building from tasks).

- **Q:** What is verbatim theatre?
  **A:** Theatre made from the exact words of real people, usually gathered through interviews.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Source gathering
*Student:* Interview two community members on a shared theme (e.g., 'home') and collect found text.

*How to teach:* Interview two community members on the theme; collect found text (letters, articles).

- **Q:** What ethical steps should you take with interviews?
  **A:** Get consent to record and use words, represent people accurately, and let them review or remain anonymous if they wish.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Viewpoints
*Student:* Explore Viewpoints (tempo, spatial relationship, repetition) to generate movement material.

*How to teach:* Explore Viewpoints: tempo, spatial relationship, repetition (plus shape, gesture, architecture) to generate movement.

- **Q:** Name three Viewpoints.
  **A:** Tempo, duration, kinesthetic response, repetition, shape, gesture, architecture, spatial relationship, topography (any three).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Composition
*Student:* Combine verbatim lines and movement into three short compositions.

*How to teach:* Combine verbatim lines and movement into three short compositions.

- **Q:** What is a composition in devising?
  **A:** A short piece created by an ensemble combining elements (text, movement, image) around a prompt.

### Day 5 (Friday): Showing
*Student:* Shape the compositions into a 6–8 minute piece and show it for feedback.

*How to teach:* Shape into a 6–8 minute piece; show for feedback.

- **Q:** What did Tectonic Theater Project use as the source for The Laramie Project?
  **A:** Hundreds of interviews with residents of Laramie, Wyoming, plus journals and news reports, after the murder of Matthew Shepard.

**Assessment:** Devised showing.

**Answer key:** Devised showing rubric. 4 – 6–8 minutes; integrates verbatim text accurately with purposeful movement from Viewpoints; clear structure and theme; ethical use of sources; ensemble commitment. 3 (proficient) – Clear theme, verbatim text and movement combined, shaped piece. 2 – Loosely connected material. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness: Officiating and sport leadership

**Prepare:** Download the official rules of one sport (e.g., USA Volleyball or USA Pickleball) and a hand-signal chart. Get a whistle and a bracket template. Arrange a youth or family game to officiate.

**Watch for:**
- Officials who hesitate lose control. Make calls clearly and promptly with correct signals.
- Risk management is only first aid. It begins with prevention: inspecting space, equipment, and planning.
- A bracket for 6 teams needs 6 slots. Use byes to fill to 8 in single elimination.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules study
*Student:* Study the official rules of one sport and learn its hand signals.

*How to teach:* Student studies one sport's rules and practices 6+ hand signals (e.g., volleyball: point/side-out, ball in, ball out, net fault, double contact).

- **Q:** In volleyball, what is the signal for 'ball out'?
  **A:** Both forearms raised vertically, palms facing the body (line judge flags up; referee raises forearms).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Officiating practice
*Student:* Officiate a youth or family game (e.g., volleyball, pickleball) using correct signals.

*How to teach:* Officiate a game using whistle and signals; keep score.

- **Q:** Name two qualities of a good official.
  **A:** Knows the rules, is consistent, fair, decisive, communicates clearly, and remains calm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Risk management
*Student:* Identify safety hazards at a play area and write a risk-management checklist.

*How to teach:* Walk a play area and identify hazards (uneven ground, glass, sprinklers, poles, sun/heat, no water). Write a checklist with prevention steps and an emergency plan (phone, first aid kit, address).

- **Q:** Give three items for a risk-management checklist.
  **A:** Inspect surface and remove hazards; check equipment; have first aid kit, water, and phone; know address/emergency plan; warm-up planned.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan an event
*Student:* Plan a small tournament or fun-run with a bracket, schedule, and safety plan.

*How to teach:* Plan a small tournament or fun run: bracket, schedule, roles, and safety plan.

- **Q:** How many games are needed in a single-elimination bracket of 8 teams?
  **A:** 7 (each game eliminates one team; 8 − 1 = 7).
- **Q:** How many byes for 6 teams in single elimination?
  **A:** 2 byes (to fill an 8-team bracket).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Run the event or simulate it and reflect on leadership challenges.

*How to teach:* Run or simulate the event; student reflects on leadership challenges.

- **Q:** What should an event leader do if lightning or heat danger appears?
  **A:** Pause or cancel the event, move participants to safety, and follow the safety plan.

**Assessment:** Event plan and officiating reflection.

**Answer key:** Event plan and officiating reflection rubric. 4 – Correct bracket (byes if needed), realistic schedule, roles, risk-management checklist with emergency plan, rules summary; reflection discusses specific calls, signals, and leadership problems with solutions. 3 (proficient) – Bracket, schedule, safety plan, and reflection on officiating. 2 – Missing safety plan or reflection lacks detail. 1 – Incomplete.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Capstone: research

**Prepare:** Help the student access library databases (public library card) and Google Patents. Have a benchmarking table template ready.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping existing products. If a cheap product already solves the problem well, the design needs a clear improvement.
- Writing specifications that can't be tested ('easy to use'). Specifications need numbers and test methods ('a first-time user can open it in under 10 seconds').
- Citing unreliable sources. Use manufacturer data, standards, textbooks, patents and reputable articles.

### Day 1 (Monday): Existing products
*Student:* Find and benchmark five existing solutions on price, features, and weaknesses.

*How to teach:* Benchmark five existing solutions in a table: price, features, weaknesses (from reviews).

- **Q:** What is benchmarking?
  **A:** Studying and comparing existing products to learn their strengths and weaknesses and set targets for a new design.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Patent search
*Student:* Search for related patents and summarize two.

*How to teach:* Search Google Patents; summarize two related patents.

- **Q:** What three things should you record from each patent?
  **A:** The patent number and title, what its claims protect, and how your design is different.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Technical research
*Student:* Research the science or engineering principles your solution will depend on.

*How to teach:* Research the scientific or engineering principles the solution will depend on and note sources.

- **Q:** Why research technical principles before designing?
  **A:** So the design is based on how things actually work (e.g. forces, materials, heat) and avoids predictable failures.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Specifications
*Student:* Write measurable design specifications from your criteria.

*How to teach:* Turn criteria into measurable specifications with target values and test methods.

- **Q:** Turn 'the organizer should be sturdy' into a specification.
  **A:** Example: 'Holds 2 kg on each shelf with less than 2 mm sag, tested with weights over 5 trials.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Submit research summary with citations.

*How to teach:* Submit the research summary with citations; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What's the difference between a criterion and a specification?
  **A:** A criterion is a goal (sturdy); a specification is a measurable, testable target (holds 2 kg with < 2 mm sag).

**Assessment:** Research summary.

**Answer key:** Research summary rubric: 4 = benchmarking table of five existing solutions (price, key features, weaknesses) with sources; summaries of two related patents (number, title, what it claims, how the design differs); technical principles explained (e.g. levers, friction, heat transfer, circuits) with sources; at least five measurable specifications each linked to a criterion with a test method; citations in a consistent format; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing patents or specs; 1 = incomplete.
