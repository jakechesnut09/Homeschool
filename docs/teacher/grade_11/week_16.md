## Algebra II: Rational exponents and radicals

**Prepare:** Practice set. A calculator without radical simplification is fine; much of this is mental.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 27^(2/3) = 27·(2/3) = 18. The denominator is the root and the numerator is the power: (∛27)² = 9.
- Adding unlike radicals: √2 + √3 ≠ √5. Only like radicals (same index and radicand) combine.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nth roots
*Student:* Evaluate nth roots and explain why 8^(1/3) = 2 using exponent properties.

*How to teach:* Use the power rule: (8^(1/3))³ = 8¹ = 8, so 8^(1/3) must be ∛8 = 2. Student evaluates nth roots, including odd roots of negatives. Today's task from the plan: Evaluate nth roots and explain why 8^(1/3) = 2 using exponent properties. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Evaluate ⁴√81 and ⁵√(−32).
  **A:** 3 and −2.
- **Q:** Why does 8^(1/3) = 2?
  **A:** (8^(1/3))³ = 8^((1/3)·3) = 8, and 2³ = 8, so 8^(1/3) is the cube root of 8, which is 2.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rational exponents
*Student:* Convert and simplify expressions like 27^(2/3) and x^(3/4)·x^(1/2).

*How to teach:* Model converting x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m and applying exponent rules. Today's task from the plan: Convert and simplify expressions like 27^(2/3) and x^(3/4)·x^(1/2). Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Evaluate 27^(2/3) and 16^(−3/4).
  **A:** 27^(2/3) = 3² = 9. 16^(−3/4) = 1/(2³) = 1/8.
- **Q:** Simplify x^(3/4) · x^(1/2).
  **A:** x^(3/4 + 2/4) = x^(5/4).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Simplifying radicals
*Student:* Simplify and multiply radicals with different indices.

*How to teach:* Model pulling out perfect powers, and multiplying different indices by converting to rational exponents. Today's task from the plan: Simplify and multiply radicals with different indices. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify √72 and ∛54.
  **A:** 6√2 and 3∛2.
- **Q:** Write √2 · ∛2 as a single radical.
  **A:** 2^(1/2 + 1/3) = 2^(5/6) = ⁶√32.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Operations
*Student:* Add, subtract, and rationalize denominators with radicals.

*How to teach:* Model combining like radicals and rationalizing with a conjugate. Today's task from the plan: Add, subtract, and rationalize denominators with radicals. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Simplify 3√12 + √27.
  **A:** 6√3 + 3√3 = 9√3.
- **Q:** Rationalize 4/(3 − √5).
  **A:** 4(3 + √5)/(9 − 5) = 3 + √5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on rational exponents and radical simplification.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (about 30 minutes, no calculator). Today's task from the plan: Quiz on rational exponents and radical simplification. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Simplify (x⁶y³)^(1/3).
  **A:** x²y.

**Assessment:** Radicals quiz.

**Answer key:** Sample key: 1) 32^(3/5) = 8. 2) 49^(−1/2) = 1/7. 3) √50 = 5√2. 4) ∛(16x⁴) = 2x∛(2x). 5) √8 + √18 = 5√2. 6) (2 + √3)(2 − √3) = 1. 7) 6/√2 = 3√2. 8) a^(2/3)·a^(1/3) = a. Mastery 7/8.

## Physics of the Universe: Electromagnetic induction and generators

**Prepare:** A strong magnet, a coil of many turns of thin wire (or wire wrapped around a toilet paper tube), an LED or multimeter, a D battery, a small neodymium disk magnet, bare copper wire for the homopolar motor. PhET Faraday's Electromagnetic Lab. Caution: homopolar motors get hot quickly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a stationary magnet inside a coil makes current. Only a CHANGING magnetic field (motion) induces current.
- Thinking a generator creates energy. It converts mechanical energy into electrical energy; something must do work to turn it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Faraday's lab
*Student:* Use PhET Faraday's Lab to explore induced current.

*How to teach:* In PhET, move the bar magnet through the coil at different speeds and with more loops. Today's task from the plan: Use PhET Faraday's Lab to explore induced current. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** What increases the induced current?
  **A:** Moving the magnet faster, adding more loops, or using a stronger magnet—anything that increases the rate of change of magnetic flux.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Induction
*Student:* Move a magnet through a coil and detect current with an LED or multimeter.

*How to teach:* Push a magnet in and out of the real coil connected to the LED/multimeter. Today's task from the plan: Move a magnet through a coil and detect current with an LED or multimeter. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does the current reverse direction when the magnet is pulled out?
  **A:** The magnetic field through the coil is now decreasing instead of increasing, so the induced current reverses (Lenz's law).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Motors
*Student:* Build a simple homopolar motor with battery, magnet, and wire.

*How to teach:* Build the homopolar motor (magnet under battery, bent wire touching top and magnet). Today's task from the plan: Build a simple homopolar motor with battery, magnet, and wire. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What makes the wire spin?
  **A:** A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force, producing rotation (electrical → mechanical energy).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Generators
*Student:* Explain how power plants generate electricity; compare hydro, wind, and gas.

*How to teach:* Read about hydro, wind, and natural gas plants; compare how each spins its turbine. Today's task from the plan: Explain how power plants generate electricity; compare hydro, wind, and gas. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What do hydro, wind, and natural gas plants have in common?
  **A:** Each spins a turbine (by falling water, wind, or hot gas/steam) that turns a generator, where coils rotate in a magnetic field to induce current.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* CER on how generators and motors are reversible.

*How to teach:* CER: motors and generators are reverse processes. Today's task from the plan: CER on how generators and motors are reversible. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Give the energy conversions of a motor and a generator.
  **A:** Motor: electrical → mechanical. Generator: mechanical → electrical.

**Assessment:** Induction CER.

**Answer key:** CER rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning). Claim: answers the question about why motors and generators are reversible in one sentence. Evidence: specific data from the student's own lab/simulation with numbers and units. Reasoning: connects the evidence to the scientific principle. A proficient response: the homopolar motor (current + magnetic field → motion) and the coil experiment (motion of magnet → current) as evidence, and reasoning that both depend on the interaction between magnetic fields and moving charges, so the same device can convert energy either way.

## Spanish IV: La arquitectura y el patrimonio

**Prepare:** Open UNESCO World Heritage pages (whc.unesco.org/es) for the Alhambra (Granada, Spain), Machu Picchu (Peru), and Chichén Itzá (Mexico). Find a Spanish reading on the California missions (21 missions, 1769–1823). Chart the pluperfect: había, habías, había, habíamos, habíais, habían + participle. Preview: mission history includes forced labor and disease among Indigenous Californians.

**Watch for:**
- Using the preterite for the earlier of two past actions: Cuando llegaron los españoles, los incas ya habían construido Machu Picchu.
- Presenting the missions only as romantic history: they also brought forced labor, harsh punishment, loss of land and culture, and disease that devastated Indigenous populations.
- Calling the Alhambra a church: it is a palace and fortress complex built mainly by the Nasrid dynasty (13th–14th centuries).

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Scene study: partner work

**Prepare:** Borrow a contemporary two-person scene collection (pre-screen for content). Recruit a scene partner for the week. Read about Sanford Meisner's repetition exercise.

**Watch for:**
- Waiting for your cue instead of listening. Real listening changes how you respond.
- Low stakes ('it doesn't really matter'). Raise stakes: what does the character lose if they fail?
- Blocking for convenience. Each cross should come from a tactic change.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose scene
*Student:* Read three two-person scenes, choose one with a partner, and read the full play it comes from.

*How to teach:* Read three scenes; choose one with a partner; read the full play.

- **Q:** Why do actors need to know the whole play for a two-person scene?
  **A:** The relationship history and what happens before/after determine objectives and stakes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Table work
*Student:* Write the given circumstances, each character's objective, and the relationship history.

*How to teach:* Table work: given circumstances, objectives, relationship history.

- **Q:** What are 'stakes'?
  **A:** What the character stands to win or lose — high stakes create urgency.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Repetition exercise
*Student:* Practice the Meisner repetition exercise for 15 minutes to train listening and impulse.

*How to teach:* Repetition exercise: partners face each other; A makes an observation ('You're smiling'); B repeats it from their point of view ('I'm smiling'); continue, letting changes in tone come from impulse. 15 minutes.

- **Q:** What does the Meisner repetition exercise train?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Blocking
*Student:* Block the scene so that every cross is motivated by a tactic change.

*How to teach:* Block so every cross is motivated by a tactic change; note blocking in scripts.

## Lifetime Fitness: Pickleball fundamentals

**Prepare:** Get paddles and a pickleball (perforated plastic) or locate a public court; find a smooth wall. Read the USA Pickleball basics: underhand serve, double-bounce rule, non-volley zone (kitchen, 7 ft from net), doubles scoring.

**Watch for:**
- Volleying while standing in the kitchen. You may not volley (hit before the bounce) with any part of your body in the non-volley zone, including momentum carrying you in.
- Thinking the receiving team can score. Only the serving side scores points.
- Rushing the net immediately after serving. The double-bounce rule means the return must bounce, so the serving team usually stays back for the third shot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grip & groundstrokes
*Student:* Learn continental grip and practice forehand and backhand against a wall (50 each).

*How to teach:* Teach continental grip ('shake hands' with the paddle). Forehand and backhand against a wall: short backswing, contact in front, follow through toward target. 50 each.

- **Q:** Describe the continental grip.
  **A:** Hold the paddle like shaking hands, with the 'V' between thumb and index finger on top of the handle — it works for both forehand and backhand.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Serve & return
*Student:* Practice the underhand serve and deep return; learn double-bounce rule.

*How to teach:* Teach the serve: underhand, contact below the waist, upward arc, cross-court into the diagonal service box, beyond the kitchen line. Practice serves and deep returns. Explain double-bounce: serve must bounce, and the return must bounce before either team may volley.

- **Q:** State the double-bounce rule.
  **A:** After the serve, the ball must bounce once on each side (the return of serve and the third shot) before players may volley.
- **Q:** Where must a serve land?
  **A:** In the diagonal service court, past the non-volley zone line (a serve landing on the kitchen line is a fault).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dinking
*Student:* Practice dinking at the kitchen line and learn non-volley-zone rules.

*How to teach:* At the kitchen line, practice soft dinks that arc over the net and land in the opponent's kitchen. Explain kitchen rules.

- **Q:** Can you step into the kitchen to hit a ball that bounced there?
  **A:** Yes — you may enter the kitchen anytime to play a ball that has bounced; you may not volley while in it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scoring & play
*Student:* Learn doubles scoring and play practice games at a local court.

*How to teach:* Teach doubles scoring: score called as serving team, receiving team, server number (game starts 0-0-2); games to 11, win by 2. Play practice games.

- **Q:** Why does a doubles game start at '0-0-2'?
  **A:** The first serving team gets only one server before the first side-out, so play starts as if it were the second server.
- **Q:** What score wins a standard game?
  **A:** 11 points, winning by at least 2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Skill check (serve accuracy 7/10, 20-dink rally) and rules quiz.

*How to teach:* Skill check: 10 serves (target 7 in), 20-dink cooperative rally; give rules quiz.

- **Q:** What is a 'third-shot drop'?
  **A:** A soft shot by the serving team on the third shot that lands in the opponent's kitchen, allowing the servers to move up to the net.

**Assessment:** Skill check and rules quiz.

**Answer key:** Skill check: proficient = 7/10 legal serves and a 20-dink rally. Rules quiz key: 1) Kitchen = non-volley zone, 7 ft from the net. 2) Serve underhand, contact below waist, cross-court, past the kitchen. 3) Double-bounce rule (each side lets ball bounce once). 4) Only serving team scores. 5) Games to 11, win by 2. 6) Score call = server score, receiver score, server number. 7) A ball landing on a line (except kitchen line on serve) is in.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Reverse engineering: observation and disassembly

**Prepare:** Choose an inexpensive product with screws or snaps (stapler, flashlight, retractable pen, toy). Gather small screwdrivers, safety glasses, small containers/egg carton for parts and a phone camera. Make sure the product has no batteries left in it and no springs under heavy tension.

**Watch for:**
- Taking things apart without documenting. Photograph and label every step so the product can be reassembled.
- Thinking reverse engineering is the same as copying or stealing. It's legitimate for learning, compatibility, repair and improvement, but copying patented designs or circumventing protections can be illegal.
- Prying with force. Look for hidden screws under labels and feet, and snap-fit clips, before prying.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why reverse engineer
*Student:* Learn legitimate uses of reverse engineering and safety for disassembly.

*How to teach:* Discuss legitimate uses (learning how products work, repair, compatibility, competitive analysis, improving a design) and limits (patents, copyright, licensing). Review disassembly safety: glasses, springs under tension, sharp edges, no plugged-in devices.

- **Q:** Give two legitimate reasons engineers reverse engineer products.
  **A:** To understand how a product works, to make compatible parts or repairs, to analyze competitors, or to improve a design.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Observation
*Student:* Document the product's function with a black-box diagram of inputs and outputs.

*How to teach:* Operate the product and draw a black-box diagram: inputs on the left (energy, materials, information), the product as a box, outputs on the right.

- **Q:** For a flashlight, list inputs and outputs.
  **A:** Inputs: chemical energy from batteries, human force on the switch. Outputs: light (and some heat).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Disassembly
*Student:* Disassemble the product, photographing and labeling each step.

*How to teach:* Disassemble step by step, photographing and labeling each part, storing parts in order.

- **Q:** Why photograph each step?
  **A:** So the product can be reassembled correctly and the order of assembly is documented.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Parts list
*Student:* Create a parts list with quantities, materials, and functions.

*How to teach:* Create a parts list: number, name, quantity, material, function.

- **Q:** What is a fastener? Give two examples.
  **A:** A component that joins parts — e.g. screws, rivets, snap fits, pins.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Submit the reverse engineering notebook entries.

*How to teach:* Check the notebook entries against the list in the key.

- **Q:** What subsystems does your product have?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g. a flashlight has a power subsystem (batteries, springs), a switch subsystem, and a light subsystem (bulb/LED, reflector, lens).
