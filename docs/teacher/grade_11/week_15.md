# Teacher's Guide — 11th Grade, Week 15

## English 11 – American Literature: Argument essay: the American promise

**Prepare:** Unit 4 texts. Optional: excerpt from the 1858 Lincoln-Douglas debates (Ottawa or Freeport) for the test.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing evidence with warrant. Evidence is the quotation; the warrant explains why it proves the claim.
- Writing comma splices ('Douglass praised the founders, he condemned slavery'). Fix with a period, semicolon, or conjunction.

### Day 1 (Monday): Prompt & claim
*Student:* Respond to 'Did the founding documents contain the seeds of abolition?' Draft a claim and gather evidence from three Unit 4 texts.

*How to teach:* Discuss the prompt. The student writes a claim and gathers evidence from three Unit 4 texts. Today's task from the plan: Respond to 'Did the founding documents contain the seeds of abolition?' Draft a claim and gather evidence from three Unit 4 texts. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence that the founding documents contained the 'seeds of abolition.'
  **A:** Douglass in the Fourth of July speech calls the Constitution 'a GLORIOUS LIBERTY DOCUMENT' and appeals to the Declaration's principles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Outline
*Student:* Build an outline with claim, reasons, evidence, warrants, and a counterclaim with rebuttal.

*How to teach:* Model one claim-reason-evidence-warrant chain. The student builds a full outline with counterclaim and rebuttal. Today's task from the plan: Build an outline with claim, reasons, evidence, warrants, and a counterclaim with rebuttal. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What is a strong counterclaim?
  **A:** The Constitution protected slavery (the three-fifths clause and the fugitive slave clause), so the founders did not intend abolition.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Write a 900-word argument essay; grammar focus: parallel structure in thesis and topic sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach parallel structure in thesis and topic sentences. The student drafts 900 words. Today's task from the plan: Write a 900-word argument essay; grammar focus: parallel structure in thesis and topic sentences. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Fix the parallelism: 'Douglass used irony, questions that were rhetorical, and he appealed to the founders.'
  **A:** 'Douglass used irony, rhetorical questions, and appeals to the founders.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Peer/parent review with a rubric; revise for warrants and transitions; edit for comma splices.

*How to teach:* Parent reviews with the rubric. The student revises warrants and transitions and fixes comma splices. Today's task from the plan: Peer/parent review with a rubric; revise for warrants and transitions; edit for comma splices. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Fix: 'Lincoln spoke briefly, the speech became famous.'
  **A:** 'Lincoln spoke briefly, yet the speech became famous.' or 'Lincoln spoke briefly; the speech became famous.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Submit the final essay and take the Unit 4 test (unseen passage from Lincoln-Douglas debates).

*How to teach:* Collect the final essay; give the Unit 4 test with an unseen Lincoln-Douglas debate passage. Today's task from the plan: Submit the final essay and take the Unit 4 test (unseen passage from Lincoln-Douglas debates). Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What was Stephen Douglas's 'popular sovereignty'?
  **A:** Letting settlers of each territory vote on whether to allow slavery.

**Assessment:** Argument essay and Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Argument essay rubric (4 = exceeds, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning) on four rows: Claim/Thesis (arguable, specific, answers the prompt); Evidence (well-chosen quotations, introduced and cited); Analysis/Reasoning (explains HOW evidence supports the claim, not just what it says); Conventions & organization (logical paragraphs, transitions, few errors). A proficient (3) paper takes a clear position, uses evidence from three texts (Douglass, Lincoln, the Declaration/Constitution), explains warrants, and rebuts a real counterclaim (e.g., the three-fifths and fugitive slave clauses). Unit 4 test key: Lincoln argues slavery is a moral wrong whose spread must be stopped; Douglas argues popular sovereignty; identify antithesis, appeals, and Douglass's Fourth of July irony.

## Algebra II: Rational equations and applications

**Prepare:** Practice set. Prepare one 'two pipes' and one boat-current problem. Calculator allowed.

**Watch for:**
- Not checking solutions in the original equation. Any value that makes an original denominator zero is extraneous.
- Averaging speeds (e.g., 60 and 40 → 50) for a round trip. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Solving
*Student:* Solve rational equations by multiplying by the LCD; check for extraneous solutions.

*How to teach:* Model multiplying both sides by the LCD, solving, then checking each answer against excluded values. Today's task from the plan: Solve rational equations by multiplying by the LCD; check for extraneous solutions. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Solve 3/x + 1/2 = 2.
  **A:** Multiply by 2x: 6 + x = 4x, x = 2. Check: 1.5 + 0.5 = 2 ✓.
- **Q:** Solve x/(x − 3) = 3/(x − 3) + 2.
  **A:** x = 3 + 2(x − 3) → x = 3, which makes a denominator zero: extraneous, so no solution.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Work problems
*Student:* Solve 'two pipes fill a tank' and shared-job problems.

*How to teach:* Model 'work rate = 1/time' and adding rates. Student solves four job problems. Today's task from the plan: Solve 'two pipes fill a tank' and shared-job problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Pipe A fills a tank in 6 h, pipe B in 3 h. Together?
  **A:** 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 tank per hour, so 2 hours.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rate problems
*Student:* Solve upstream/downstream and average-speed problems.

*How to teach:* Model a d = rt table for upstream and downstream. Student solves three rate problems. Today's task from the plan: Solve upstream/downstream and average-speed problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A boat goes 12 mph in still water against a 3 mph current for 30 miles, then back. Total time?
  **A:** Upstream 30/9 = 3⅓ h, downstream 30/15 = 2 h; total 5⅓ h (5 h 20 min).
- **Q:** Drive 120 miles at 60 mph and return at 40 mph. Average speed?
  **A:** Time 2 + 3 = 5 h for 240 miles: 48 mph.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Formulas
*Student:* Rearrange formulas like 1/f = 1/a + 1/b (lens formula) to isolate variables.

*How to teach:* Model isolating f in the lens formula by multiplying through by the LCD. Today's task from the plan: Rearrange formulas like 1/f = 1/a + 1/b (lens formula) to isolate variables. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Solve 1/f = 1/a + 1/b for f, then find b when a = 30 and f = 10.
  **A:** f = ab/(a + b). b = af/(a − f) = 300/20 = 15.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 4 test on rational expressions, functions, and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test (about 50 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Unit 4 test on rational expressions, functions, and equations. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Solve 2/(x + 1) = 1/(x − 2).
  **A:** 2(x − 2) = x + 1 → x = 5. Check: 2/6 = 1/3 ✓.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 sample key: 1) simplify (x²−x−6)/(x²−9) = (x+2)/(x+3). 2) 1/x + 1/(2x) = 3/(2x). 3) y=(x−1)/(x+4): VA x=−4, HA y=1. 4) 5/x = 10/(x+3) → x=3. 5) x/(x−1) − 1/(x−1) = 1 → 1 = 1 for all x ≠ 1 (an identity; x = 1 is excluded). 6) Two painters, 4 h and 6 h: 2.4 h together. 7) Solve 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ for R₂: R₂ = RR₁/(R₁ − R). Rubric: method 2 pts, answer and check 1 pt each.

## Physics of the Universe: Magnetism and electromagnetism

**Prepare:** Large iron nail, 2 m of thin insulated wire (ends stripped), D battery, paper clips, compass, bar magnet. Caution: the wire and battery heat up; connect only for a few seconds at a time.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all metals are magnetic. Only some (iron, nickel, cobalt) are strongly attracted; aluminum and copper are not.
- Thinking magnetic poles are the same as electric charges. A magnet always has both poles; cutting it makes two smaller magnets, each with N and S.

### Day 1 (Monday): Magnets
*Student:* Map magnetic field lines with a compass around a magnet.

*How to teach:* Place the compass at many points around a bar magnet and draw the needle direction to map field lines. Today's task from the plan: Map magnetic field lines with a compass around a magnet. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Which direction do magnetic field lines point outside a magnet?
  **A:** From the north pole to the south pole.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Oersted
*Student:* Place a compass near a current-carrying wire and observe deflection.

*How to teach:* Lay a wire over a compass (aligned north-south) and briefly connect to the battery; reverse the battery. Today's task from the plan: Place a compass near a current-carrying wire and observe deflection. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** What did Oersted discover with this setup?
  **A:** An electric current produces a magnetic field around the wire; reversing the current reverses the field and the needle's deflection.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Electromagnet lab
*Student:* Wrap a nail with wire; vary coils and count paper clips lifted.

*How to teach:* Wrap 10, 20, 30, 40 coils around the nail; count paper clips lifted each time (3 trials). Today's task from the plan: Wrap a nail with wire; vary coils and count paper clips lifted. Before the student writes conclusions, check that the data table has units and repeated trials, and ask them to explain one result in their own words.

- **Q:** How does the number of coils affect the electromagnet's strength?
  **A:** More coils produce a stronger field, so more clips are lifted (roughly proportional until the core saturates).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Model
*Student:* Draw a field model explaining forces and energy between magnets.

*How to teach:* The student draws a field model of two magnets attracting and repelling, explaining energy stored in the field. Today's task from the plan: Draw a field model explaining forces and energy between magnets. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Why does it take work to push two north poles together?
  **A:** The repulsive field force opposes the motion; the work done is stored as potential energy in the magnetic field and is released if they fly apart.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Lab report on electromagnet strength.

*How to teach:* Lab report on electromagnet strength. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Name three ways to make an electromagnet stronger.
  **A:** More coils, more current (bigger voltage), and an iron core.

**Assessment:** Electromagnet lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric (4-point per row): question and hypothesis; data table with 3 trials per condition and averages; graph of clips vs coils; conclusion explaining that current in coils creates a magnetic field that adds up with each turn and is concentrated by the iron core; sources of error (battery draining, clip size).

## U.S. History & Geography: Continuity and Change in the Twentieth Century: The New Deal

**Prepare:** Get audio of FDR's First Inaugural (March 4, 1933) and the first fireside chat on banking (March 12, 1933); WPA posters; a chart template for relief/recovery/reform; critic excerpts (Huey Long 'Share Our Wealth', Father Coughlin, American Liberty League); the Wagner Act summary. Assemble DBQ documents (unemployment data 1929–1941, programs, critics).

**Watch for:**
- Students think the New Deal ended the Depression. Unemployment fell from about 25% (1933) to about 14% (1937), then rose again in the 1937–38 recession; full recovery came with WWII spending.
- Students think all New Deal programs survived. The Supreme Court struck down the NRA (Schechter, 1935) and the first AAA (Butler, 1936).
- Students think critics were only conservatives. Critics came from the left (Long, Coughlin, Townsend) and the right (Liberty League).

### Day 1 (Monday): FDR
*Student:* Listen to FDR's First Inaugural and a fireside chat.

*How to teach:* Listen to FDR's First Inaugural and a fireside chat.

- **Q:** What is FDR's most famous line from his First Inaugural?
  **A:** 'The only thing we have to fear is fear itself.'
- **Q:** What was the purpose of the first fireside chat?
  **A:** To explain the bank holiday and restore confidence so people would redeposit money.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Programs
*Student:* Chart New Deal programs by relief, recovery, and reform.

*How to teach:* Chart programs by relief, recovery and reform.

- **Q:** Classify: CCC, FDIC, Social Security, NRA.
  **A:** CCC — relief; NRA — recovery; FDIC — reform; Social Security — reform (and relief).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Critics
*Student:* Analyze critiques from Huey Long, Coughlin, and the Liberty League.

*How to teach:* Analyze critiques from Long, Coughlin and the Liberty League.

- **Q:** What did Huey Long's 'Share Our Wealth' propose?
  **A:** Heavy taxes on the rich to guarantee every family a basic income and home.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Labor
*Student:* Study the Wagner Act and CIO.

*How to teach:* Study the Wagner Act and the CIO.

- **Q:** What did the Wagner Act (1935) do?
  **A:** Guaranteed workers' right to organize and bargain collectively and created the National Labor Relations Board.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Unit 5 DBQ: Did the New Deal end the Depression?

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 DBQ.

- **Q:** What was FDR's court-packing plan?
  **A:** A 1937 proposal to add up to six justices to the Supreme Court; Congress rejected it.

**Assessment:** DBQ.

**Answer key:** DBQ rubric (Did the New Deal end the Depression?): 4 = defensible thesis (e.g., it provided relief and lasting reform but didn't fully end the Depression, which ended with WWII mobilization); uses 4+ documents with sourcing (e.g., unemployment data, FDR's speeches, critics); outside evidence (FDIC, SEC, Social Security Act 1935, Wagner Act 1935, 1937 recession, court-packing plan 1937); complexity (distinguishes relief/recovery/reform). 3 = thesis with some evidence. 2 = partial. 1 = no thesis.

## Spanish IV: Música y baile

**Prepare:** Build a playlist: flamenco (Spain), tango (Argentina/Uruguay), cumbia (Colombia), son cubano (Cuba), and salsa (New York with Cuban and Puerto Rican roots). Preview song lyrics: 'Pedro Navaja' (Rubén Blades, 1978) narrates a street crime in which two people are killed — preview for maturity; 'La camisa negra' (Juanes, 2005) is about heartbreak. Chart adverbial conjunctions that always take subjunctive: para que, antes de que, a menos que, con tal de que, sin que, en caso de que.

**Watch for:**
- Using the indicative after para que or antes de que (*para que aprendes): para que aprendas.
- Using que + subjunctive with the same subject: 'Practico para bailar mejor' (same subject) vs. 'Practico para que mi hermana baile mejor'.
- Thinking salsa was invented in Cuba: it developed in New York in the 1960s–70s from Cuban son and Puerto Rican music.

### Day 1 (Monday): Géneros
*Student:* Listen to five genres and map each to its country and cultural roots (African, Indigenous, European).

*How to teach:* Listen to five genres; your child maps each to its country and cultural roots.

- **Q:** Where does flamenco come from and what influences shaped it?
  **A:** Andalusia, southern Spain; Roma (gitano) culture with Moorish, Jewish, and Castilian influences.
- **Q:** What are the roots of son cubano?
  **A:** Spanish guitar traditions (the tres) and song forms combined with African rhythms and percussion (bongó, clave, maracas).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cláusulas adverbiales
*Student:* Practice subjunctive with para que, antes de que, a menos que, and con tal de que.

*How to teach:* Teach the adverbial conjunctions; your child writes 10 sentences about music and dance.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I'll play the song so that you can dance.'
  **A:** Pondré la canción para que puedas bailar.
- **Q:** Translate: 'Let's practice before the party starts.'
  **A:** Practiquemos antes de que empiece la fiesta.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Letra
*Student:* Analyze the lyrics of a song (e.g., Rubén Blades 'Pedro Navaja' or Juanes 'La camisa negra') for theme and figurative language.

*How to teach:* Analyze song lyrics for theme and figurative language.

- **Q:** In 'La camisa negra', what does the black shirt symbolize?
  **A:** Mourning — the singer's heartbreak over a lost love (his soul is 'black' with sorrow).
- **Q:** What story does 'Pedro Navaja' tell?
  **A:** A street hustler with a knife attacks a woman in the barrio; she shoots him as he stabs her, both die, and a drunk passerby takes their things — the refrain says life is full of surprises.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Baile
*Student:* Learn the basic steps of cumbia or salsa from a video and describe the process in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Learn the basic cumbia or salsa step from a video; your child narrates the process with commands or se.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Step back with the right foot and then return.'
  **A:** Da un paso atrás con el pie derecho y luego vuelve.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Record a 2-minute presentation comparing a Hispanic genre with a U.S. genre.

*How to teach:* Your child records a 2-minute presentation comparing a Hispanic genre with a U.S. genre.

- **Q:** Write a comparison sentence.
  **A:** Example: Al igual que el jazz, la salsa nació en comunidades urbanas y mezcla raíces africanas y europeas.

**Assessment:** Recorded presentation.

**Answer key:** Recorded presentation rubric (2 minutes, Hispanic vs. U.S. genre): 4 = compares origins, instruments, rhythm, and cultural role of both genres with examples, uses comparison phrases and at least two adverbial-clause subjunctives, clear pronunciation; 3 = minor errors; 2 = little comparison; 1 = incomplete.

## Theatre & Performing Arts: Shakespeare scene work

**Prepare:** Use the Folger text of A Midsummer Night's Dream. Act 3.1: the mechanicals rehearse 'Pyramus and Thisbe'; Puck gives Bottom an ass's head; Titania wakes and falls in love with him. Act 3.2: Lysander and Demetrius, enchanted, now both love Helena; Hermia and Helena quarrel. Find the 1999 film (dir. Michael Hoffman, Kevin Kline as Bottom) and a Globe recording. Tape out a stage. Prepare the history test.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing comic lines. Comedy needs a clear setup, a pause, and the punchline.
- Ignoring the verse/prose difference: the lovers speak verse, the mechanicals mostly prose.
- Asides spoken to the other characters. An aside is to the audience (or self) and unheard by others on stage.

### Day 1 (Monday): Casting & reading
*Student:* Choose the mechanicals' rehearsal (Act 3.1) or the lovers' quarrel (Act 3.2); read it aloud and paraphrase each line.

*How to teach:* Choose 3.1 or 3.2; read aloud and paraphrase every line.

- **Q:** In 3.1, what does Puck do to Bottom?
  **A:** He transforms Bottom's head into an ass's (donkey's) head, scaring off the other mechanicals.
- **Q:** In 3.2, why are Lysander and Demetrius both pursuing Helena?
  **A:** The love-juice from the flower (applied by Puck and Oberon) makes each fall in love with Helena when he wakes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Blocking
*Student:* Block the scene on a taped-out stage, marking entrances, crosses, and asides in the script.

*How to teach:* Block on the taped stage; mark entrances, crosses, and asides in the script.

- **Q:** What is an aside?
  **A:** A short remark spoken to the audience that other characters on stage are not meant to hear.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comic timing
*Student:* Rehearse for comic timing, practicing pauses before punchlines and physical bits.

*How to teach:* Rehearse comic timing: pause before punchlines, take reactions, physical bits (Bottom's braying; Hermia's 'little' fury).

- **Q:** Hermia is mocked for being short. What line does Helena say about her?
  **A:** 'And though she be but little, she is fierce.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Watch & compare
*Student:* Watch the same scene in two filmed versions (e.g., 1999 film and a Globe stage recording) and chart differing choices.

*How to teach:* Watch the scene in two versions; chart differences in setting, casting, comic business, and pacing.

- **Q:** Where is the 1999 film set?
  **A:** In a fictional Tuscan town (Monte Athena) in Italy around 1900 — updated from ancient Athens.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Perform the scene and take the Unit 4 theatre history test (Greek through Elizabethan).

*How to teach:* Perform the scene; give the Unit 4 history test.

- **Q:** Define soliloquy vs. aside.
  **A:** Soliloquy: a speech alone on stage revealing thoughts. Aside: a brief remark to the audience while others are present.

**Assessment:** Scene performance and history test.

**Answer key:** Scene performance rubric: proficient = memorized, clear meaning, motivated blocking, comic timing with pauses, distinguishes verse/prose. History test key (Greek–Elizabethan): 1) Greek drama honored Dionysus. 2) Skene/orchestra/theatron. 3) Antigone by Sophocles. 4) Hubris, catharsis. 5) Medieval mystery plays on pageant wagons by guilds; morality plays like Everyman. 6) Commedia: improvised from scenarios, stock characters (Arlecchino, Pantalone), lazzi. 7) Noh 14th-c. Japan, masked, slow; kabuki 17th-c., onnagata, hanamichi. 8) Globe 1599, thrust stage, groundlings, boys played women. 9) Iambic pentameter = five iambs. 10) Sonnet = 14 lines ending in a couplet.

## Lifetime Fitness: Mind-body fitness: Pilates and balance

**Prepare:** Gather a mat and a firm pillow or cushion for balance work. Preview the Pilates hundred, roll-up, and single-leg stretch. Prepare the unit quiz and the flexibility log.

**Watch for:**
- Doing the hundred with neck strain. Support the head or keep it down; the work comes from the abdominals.
- Thinking balance only matters for athletes. Balance and proprioception prevent falls and ankle injuries at every age.
- Believing more screen time before bed helps relaxation. Bright screens can delay sleep; build a screen-free wind-down.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pilates basics
*Student:* Learn the hundred, roll-up, and single-leg stretch with neutral spine.

*How to teach:* Teach neutral spine (natural curve, not flattened or arched). Teach the hundred (pump arms, inhale 5 counts, exhale 5, ×10), the roll-up, and single-leg stretch.

- **Q:** What does 'neutral spine' mean?
  **A:** Keeping the spine's natural curves — not pressing the low back flat nor arching excessively.
- **Q:** How many pumps in the Pilates hundred?
  **A:** 100 arm pumps: 10 breath cycles of 5 pumps inhaling and 5 exhaling.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Balance
*Student:* Practice single-leg balance progressions, including eyes closed and on a pillow.

*How to teach:* Progress single-leg balance: eyes open 30 s, eyes closed, on a pillow, eyes closed on a pillow. Record times for each leg.

- **Q:** What is proprioception?
  **A:** The body's sense of its position and movement, from receptors in muscles, tendons, and joints.
- **Q:** Why is balance harder with eyes closed?
  **A:** Vision is one of the main balance inputs; without it, you rely on proprioception and the inner ear.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Combined practice
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute Pilates and balance session.

*How to teach:* Complete a 30-minute session combining Pilates mat moves and balance drills.

- **Q:** Name two muscles groups Pilates emphasizes.
  **A:** Deep abdominals (transverse abdominis) and the muscles of the back/hips that stabilize the trunk.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Stress & sleep
*Student:* Read about exercise, sleep, and stress hormones; plan a nightly wind-down routine.

*How to teach:* Read about exercise, sleep, and cortisol. Student plans a nightly wind-down (consistent bedtime, no screens 30–60 min before, stretching/breathing, cool dark room).

- **Q:** How does regular exercise affect stress hormones and sleep?
  **A:** Regular exercise helps regulate cortisol and stress responses and generally improves sleep quality — though intense exercise right before bed may delay sleep for some people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Quiz on flexibility and mind-body concepts and log sit-and-reach progress.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and update sit-and-reach in the flexibility log.

- **Q:** Which stretching method uses a muscle contraction followed by a stretch?
  **A:** PNF (proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation), e.g., contract-relax.

**Assessment:** Quiz and flexibility log.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Static = hold; dynamic = controlled movement; PNF = contract then stretch. 2) Hold static stretches 15–30 s. 3) Proprioception = body position sense. 4) Neutral spine = natural curves. 5) Diaphragmatic breathing = belly rises on inhale. 6) Teens need 8–10 hours of sleep. 7) Benefits of flexibility: range of motion, posture, possibly fewer injuries. Flexibility log: proficient = sit-and-reach (both sides) recorded weeks 1, 13, 15 with a sentence on change.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drawings

*How to teach:* Create a drawing from a part with front, top, right and isometric views.

*How to teach:* Add dimensions and notes following standards (once each, most descriptive view).

*How to teach:* Create a section view of a hollow part: cutting-plane line with arrows, then the section with hatched cut surfaces.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Assembly drawing

*How to teach:* Create an exploded assembly drawing with balloons and a bill of materials.

- **Q:** What does a bill of materials list?

Written: a bill of materials lists item number, part name, quantity and material/source for each part.
