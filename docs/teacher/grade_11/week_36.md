## Algebra II: Final exam

**Prepare:** Final review packet (Khan Academy course challenge works well). Two quiet 60-minute exam blocks.

**Watch for:**
- Spending review time only on favorite topics. Use the semester exam and quiz errors to target weak units.
- Skipping the reflection. Error analysis is where most learning from an exam happens.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: Units 1–5
*Student:* Mixed review on quadratics, polynomials, rationals, and radicals.

*How to teach:* Mixed review on Units 1–5; student marks unsure items to revisit. Today's task from the plan: Mixed review on quadratics, polynomials, rationals, and radicals. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Solve √(x + 5) = x − 1.
  **A:** x + 5 = x² − 2x + 1 → x² − 3x − 4 = 0 → x = 4 or −1. Check: x = 4 ✓ (3 = 3), x = −1 ✗. x = 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: Units 6–9
*Student:* Mixed review on exponentials, logs, trig, sequences, and statistics.

*How to teach:* Mixed review on Units 6–9. Today's task from the plan: Mixed review on exponentials, logs, trig, sequences, and statistics. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Solve 5e^(2t) = 40.
  **A:** e^(2t) = 8 → t = ln 8/2 ≈ 1.040.
- **Q:** Find the sum of the infinite series 9 + 3 + 1 + ….
  **A:** 9/(1 − 1/3) = 13.5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Non-calculator section.

*How to teach:* Final part 1: non-calculator (60 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Non-calculator section. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** Evaluate log₃81 + sin(π/6).
  **A:** 4 + 1/2 = 4.5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Calculator section with modeling tasks.

*How to teach:* Final part 2: calculator and modeling (60 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Calculator section with modeling tasks. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** $3,000 at 5% compounded monthly; when does it reach $5,000?
  **A:** (1 + 0.05/12)^(12t) = 5/3 → t = ln(5/3)/(12 ln(1.0041667)) ≈ 10.2 years.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Review exam errors and write a reflection on growth and readiness for Precalculus.

*How to teach:* Return the graded exam; student writes a reflection on each missed item and on readiness for Precalculus. Today's task from the plan: Review exam errors and write a reflection on growth and readiness for Precalculus. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What should an error reflection include?
  **A:** The item, what went wrong (concept vs careless), the correct solution, and one action to prevent it.

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam representative answers: (x−3)²+2 vertex (3,2); 2x²+7x+3 = (2x+1)(x+3); x³−6x²+11x−6 zeros 1, 2, 3; (x+1)/(x²−1) = 1/(x−1); 16^(3/4)=8; log₂(x)=5 → 32; cos(5π/3)=1/2; period of 3 sin(2x) is π; a₁₀ of 2, 6, 18 is 39,366; 95% of normal data within 2 SD. Grade bands: 90%+ excellent, 75–89% proficient, 60–74% basic (review before Precalculus), below 60% needs targeted summer work. Reflection rubric: complete error analysis for every missed item = full credit.

## Physics of the Universe: Final review and exam

**Prepare:** Final review packet with mixed problems from all nine units; the student's lab notebook and returned tests; two quiet 60-minute exam blocks.

**Watch for:**
- Studying only favorite or easy topics. Use earlier test errors to choose what to review first.
- Skipping data-analysis practice. Part 2 of the exam uses unfamiliar data; practice reading axes, units, and trends.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: forces & energy
*Student:* Rework missed problems from Units 1–3 tests and complete a mixed problem set on momentum and energy conservation.

*How to teach:* The student reworks every missed problem from Units 1–3 tests, then completes a mixed set on momentum and energy conservation. Today's task from the plan: Rework missed problems from Units 1–3 tests and complete a mixed problem set on momentum and energy conservation. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** A 1,500 kg car at 20 m/s hits a stopped 1,000 kg car and they lock together. What is their speed afterward?
  **A:** Total momentum 30,000 kg·m/s ÷ 2,500 kg = 12 m/s.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: E&M & waves
*Student:* Complete a concept map linking fields, induction, and EM waves, then solve 10 wave/circuit problems.

*How to teach:* Concept map linking charges, fields, induction, and EM waves; then 10 wave/circuit problems. Today's task from the plan: Complete a concept map linking fields, induction, and EM waves, then solve 10 wave/circuit problems. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** How are electromagnetic waves produced?
  **A:** Accelerating (oscillating) charges create changing electric fields that create changing magnetic fields, and vice versa; the linked fields travel as an EM wave at the speed of light.
- **Q:** A radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. Wavelength?
  **A:** λ = 3 × 10⁸ / 1 × 10⁸ = 3 m.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: Earth & space
*Student:* Make a one-page study sheet on plate tectonics, stellar life cycles, and Big Bang evidence.

*How to teach:* The student writes a one-page study sheet on plate tectonics, stellar life cycles, and Big Bang evidence. Today's task from the plan: Make a one-page study sheet on plate tectonics, stellar life cycles, and Big Bang evidence. Use the questions below as a quick oral check; if an answer is shaky, reteach with one more example before moving on.

- **Q:** Give three lines of evidence for plate tectonics.
  **A:** Seafloor age increasing from ridges with symmetric magnetic striping, matching fossils and rocks across oceans, and earthquakes/volcanoes concentrated along plate boundaries.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Multiple-choice and short-answer covering all nine units.

*How to teach:* Final exam part 1: multiple-choice and short answer across all nine units (60 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Multiple-choice and short-answer covering all nine units. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** State Newton's third law and give an example.
  **A:** For every force one object exerts on another, the second exerts an equal and opposite force on the first; e.g., a rocket pushes gas backward and the gas pushes the rocket forward.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Data-analysis section: interpret two unfamiliar data sets and write CER explanations.

*How to teach:* Final exam part 2: interpret two unfamiliar data sets and write CER explanations (60 minutes). Today's task from the plan: Data-analysis section: interpret two unfamiliar data sets and write CER explanations. Grade it the same day with the key below and have the student correct every missed item with a one-line note on what went wrong.

- **Q:** What steps should the student follow with an unfamiliar graph?
  **A:** Read the title, axes, and units; describe the trend with numbers; identify the relevant principle; then write a CER linking the data to that principle.

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam key (representative items): 1) a = Δv/Δt (0→30 m/s in 6 s = 5 m/s²). 2) F = ma (1,200 kg × 2 m/s² = 2,400 N). 3) momentum conservation (12 m/s above). 4) PE→KE speed after 20 m drop ≈ 19.8 m/s. 5) Q = mcΔT. 6) Ohm's law (24 V, 8 Ω → 3 A). 7) induction requires a changing field. 8) v = fλ. 9) photoelectric effect → particle model. 10) S-wave shadow zone → liquid outer core. 11) high-mass star → supernova → neutron star/black hole. 12) redshift + CMB + light-element abundance → Big Bang. Part 2 CERs scored with the 4-point rubric. Grade bands: 90%+ excellent, 75–89% proficient, below 75% revisit weak units.

## Lifetime Fitness: Five-year fitness plan and final reflection

**Prepare:** Gather the fitness journal, final report, and community resource map. Schedule a family time for the 40-minute workout and the 5-minute presentation.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a 5-year plan with no changes over time. Life changes (college, work); plan for adjustments.

- Reflections that just list activities. Reflection explains how and why attitudes changed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Five-year plan

*How to teach:* Student writes a 5-year plan with yearly goals for each component and expected life context.

- **Q:** What adult activity guideline should a 5-year plan meet?
  **A:** At least 150 minutes moderate (or 75 vigorous) aerobic activity per week, plus muscle strengthening 2+ days per week.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Barriers
*Student:* List likely barriers after high school (schedule, cost, location) and a specific strategy and community resource for each.

*How to teach:* List barriers (schedule, cost, location, motivation) with specific strategies and community resources.

  **A:** Bodyweight/band workouts at home, run or walk outdoors, find a community rec center or campus gym.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lead a workout
*Student:* Plan and lead a 40-minute family workout combining cardio, strength, and flexibility.

*How to teach:* Student plans and leads a 40-minute family workout: warm-up, cardio, strength, flexibility, cool-down.

- **Q:** What order should a balanced session follow?
  **A:** Warm-up → main cardio/strength work → cool-down with flexibility.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Presentation
*Student:* Present your five-year plan and year-long fitness data in a 5-minute talk.

*How to teach:* Student presents the 5-year plan with data in a 5-minute talk.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection

*How to teach:* 400-word reflection on how attitudes toward fitness changed.

**Assessment:** Five-year plan and reflection.

**Answer key:** Five-year plan and reflection rubric. 4 – Yearly goals for all components meeting guidelines, specific barriers with strategies and named resources, data-supported presentation, led workout safely; 400-word reflection with specific examples of changed attitudes. 3 (proficient) – Complete plan, barriers/strategies, presentation, reflection of required length. 2 – Missing parts or generic reflection. 1 – Incomplete.

## Introduction to Engineering Design (CTE): Capstone presentation and course reflection

**Prepare:** Invite a panel (family, community members, ideally an engineer). Prepare the room with a screen and table for the prototype. Prepare a written final exam covering the course from the key below. Bookmark California community college engineering pathways (ASSIST.org for transfer) and UC/CSU engineering program pages.

**Watch for:**
- Reading slides aloud. Slides show visuals and data; the presenter explains.
- Defensive answers in Q&A. Treat questions as feedback; it's fine to say 'I didn't test that — here's how I would.'
- Assuming there is one path to engineering. Community college transfer (ASSIST.org, transfer agreements) and 4-year admission are both strong routes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rehearsal

*How to teach:* Build a 10-minute slide deck; rehearse twice with a timer.

*How to teach:* Present to the panel and demonstrate the prototype.

- **Q:** What should you do if the prototype fails during the demo?
  **A:** Stay calm, explain what failed and why, and show test data or a video of it working.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Q&A follow-up

*How to teach:* Write responses to panel questions and list next steps for development.

- **Q:** Name two next steps if the product were developed further.

  **A:** ASSIST.org.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the written final covering the design process, drawing, measurement, CAD, materials, and ethics.

*How to teach:* Give the written final in the key.

- **Q:** What is the purpose of a factor of safety?
  **A:** To make sure a design can carry more than the expected load, covering uncertainty in loads, materials and manufacturing.

**Assessment:** Presentation rubric and final exam.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric (4 levels): 4 = about 10 minutes; covers problem, user, research, concept selection, design, build, test data and next steps; live prototype demo; clear visuals; confident Q&A; 3 = complete but uneven; 2 = missing test data or demo; 1 = unprepared. Final exam representative key: 1) Design process steps. 2) Criteria vs. constraints. 3) Isometric axes at 30°. 4) Third-angle view placement (top above front, right side to right). 5) Tolerance 12 ± 0.05 → 11.95–12.05. 6) Accuracy vs. precision. 7) Mean/SD calculation (e.g. 4, 6, 8 → mean 6, SD 2). 8) Density = mass/volume. 9) Extrude, revolve, shell definitions. 10) Revolute mate leaves 1 DOF. 11) Injection molding for high volume; 3D printing for prototypes. 12) Stiffness vs. strength. 13) 5th–95th percentile design. 14) First NSPE canon. 15) Patent vs. trademark vs. copyright. Mastery 12+/15. Pathway plan: complete if it names at least one community college and one university program, required math/science courses (calculus, physics, chemistry), and transfer or admission steps.
