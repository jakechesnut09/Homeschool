# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 31

## English Language Arts 7: Fiction vs. history: comparative essay

**Prepare:** Library of Congress primary sources: photographs of 1930s segregated Black schools and sharecropper families; a short historical article on sharecropping/segregated schools (e.g., Library of Congress 'Jim Crow' or Smithsonian). Roll of Thunder for evidence.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking historical fiction is 'made up' and therefore not useful. Authors invent characters but often use real conditions to show truth about the period.
- Using evidence from only the novel. The essay must balance both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Historical accounts
*Student:* Read a historical article and two primary sources on segregated schooling and sharecropping in the 1930s.

*How to teach:* Read the historical article and two primary sources together. Your child notes key facts about segregated schooling and sharecropping. Check before moving on: ask "What did the sources show about schools?" Look for: Segregated Black schools received less funding, older books and shorter terms because children were needed in the fields.

- **Q:** What did the sources show about schools?
  **A:** Segregated Black schools received less funding, older books and shorter terms because children were needed in the fields.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare
*Student:* Fill in a comparison chart of what the novel and historical sources each show about the period.

*How to teach:* Your child fills a comparison chart of what the novel and historical sources each show about the period. Check before moving on: ask "Something the novel shows that matches history?" Look for: Hand-me-down books, the bus only for white students, sharecroppers' debt to stores.

- **Q:** Something the novel shows that matches history?
  **A:** Hand-me-down books, the bus only for white students, sharecroppers' debt to stores.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft a 5-paragraph essay explaining how Taylor uses or alters history and why.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts a 5-paragraph essay explaining how Taylor uses or alters history and why. Check before moving on: ask "Why might Taylor alter history?" Look for: To create characters readers care about and focus events through one family.

- **Q:** Why might Taylor alter history?
  **A:** To create characters readers care about and focus events through one family.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise & edit
*Student:* Revise with a checklist focused on balanced evidence from both sources, then edit.

*How to teach:* Your child revises with a checklist focused on balanced evidence from both sources, then edits. Check before moving on: ask "How to check balance?" Look for: Each body paragraph cites both the novel and a historical source.

- **Q:** How to check balance?
  **A:** Each body paragraph cites both the novel and a historical source.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Publish the essay and discuss with a parent how fiction helps us understand history.

*How to teach:* Your child publishes the essay. Discuss with your child how fiction helps us understand history. Check before moving on: ask "Example answer?" Look for: Fiction lets us feel what it was like to live through history.

- **Q:** Example answer?
  **A:** Fiction lets us feel what it was like to live through history.

**Assessment:** Comparative essay (rubric).

**Answer key:** Essay rubric: 4 = thesis on how Taylor uses/alters history and why; each body paragraph cites both the novel and a historical source; explanation; conventions. 3 = imbalanced; 2 = summary; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 7: Cross sections and volume of prisms

**Prepare:** Clay or play dough, dental floss, cereal box, Toblerone-style triangular box, ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Using the side face instead of the base for a triangular prism. The base is the triangle; height is the distance between triangles.
- Forgetting ½ in triangle area.

### Day 1 (Monday): Slicing solids
*Student:* Shape clay into a cube, cylinder and pyramid and slice with floss parallel and perpendicular to the base; sketch each cross section.

*How to teach:* Your child slices clay shapes parallel and perpendicular to the base and sketches cross sections. Check before moving on: ask "Cube sliced parallel to a face?" Look for: A square.

- **Q:** Cube sliced parallel to a face?
  **A:** A square.
- **Q:** Cylinder sliced perpendicular to its base (vertically through the middle)?
  **A:** A rectangle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Volume of prisms
*Student:* Find volume as area of base times height for rectangular and triangular prisms.

*How to teach:* Model V = Bh. Your child finds volumes. Check before moving on: ask "Rectangular prism 3 × 4 × 5?" Look for: 60 units³.

- **Q:** Rectangular prism 3 × 4 × 5?
  **A:** 60 units³.
- **Q:** Triangular prism, triangle base 6 and height 4, length 10?
  **A:** 12 × 10 = 120 units³.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Composite prisms
*Student:* Find the volume of a step-shaped and L-shaped prism by decomposing.

*How to teach:* Your child decomposes L-shaped prisms into two rectangular prisms. Check before moving on: ask "L-shaped base area 22 cm², height 5 cm?" Look for: 110 cm³.

- **Q:** L-shaped base area 22 cm², height 5 cm?
  **A:** 110 cm³.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Measuring real prisms
*Student:* Measure a cereal box and a triangular Toblerone-style box and compute their volumes.

*How to teach:* Measure the boxes. Your child computes volumes with units. Check before moving on: ask "Cereal box 30 × 20 × 7 cm?" Look for: 4,200 cm³.

- **Q:** Cereal box 30 × 20 × 7 cm?
  **A:** 4,200 cm³.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on cross sections and prism volume.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Cross section of a square pyramid parallel to the base?" Look for: A smaller square.

- **Q:** Cross section of a square pyramid parallel to the base?
  **A:** A smaller square.

**Assessment:** Cross-section sketches and volume quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Cylinder cut parallel to base: circle. 2) Prism 2 × 5 × 8: 80. 3) Triangular prism base area 15, height 9: 135. 4) Triangle base 10, h 3, prism length 12: 180. 5) Cube side 4: 64. Sketches: correct shape for each slice.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Disrupting ecosystems

**Prepare:** Before/after images of a California wildfire area (e.g., NASA Earth Observatory); succession reading; data on quagga mussels or Argentine ants (CA Dept. of Fish and Wildlife or UC IPM); graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fire only destroys. Many California ecosystems (chaparral, some pines) are adapted to fire and recover through succession.
- Confusing correlation with cause. Data must link the change to the population effect.

### Day 1 (Monday): Phenomenon: wildfire
*Student:* Compare before-and-after images of a California wildfire area and predict population changes.

*How to teach:* Compare images. Your child predicts which populations decline and which increase after fire. Check before moving on: ask "Which might increase soon after fire?" Look for: Grasses and fire-following wildflowers, and animals that eat them; also woodpeckers feeding on insects in dead trees.

- **Q:** Which might increase soon after fire?
  **A:** Grasses and fire-following wildflowers, and animals that eat them; also woodpeckers feeding on insects in dead trees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Succession
*Student:* Read about ecological succession after fire and sequence the stages.

*How to teach:* Read about succession. Your child sequences stages. Check before moving on: ask "Typical sequence after fire?" Look for: Bare/ash → grasses and wildflowers → shrubs → young trees → mature forest or chaparral.

- **Q:** Typical sequence after fire?
  **A:** Bare/ash → grasses and wildflowers → shrubs → young trees → mature forest or chaparral.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Invasive species
*Student:* Analyze data on quagga mussels or Argentine ants in California and graph native species decline.

*How to teach:* Your child graphs data on an invasive species and native species decline. Check before moving on: ask "How do Argentine ants affect coast horned lizards?" Look for: They displace native ants the lizards eat, so lizards decline.

- **Q:** How do Argentine ants affect coast horned lizards?
  **A:** They displace native ants the lizards eat, so lizards decline.
- **Q:** Quagga mussel impact?
  **A:** Filter out plankton, clog water pipes, outcompete native species.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Argument
*Student:* Write an argument supported by data on how one change affected a population.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a data-based argument on how one change affected a population. Check before moving on: ask "What makes it data-based?" Look for: Specific numbers or graph trends cited as evidence.

- **Q:** What makes it data-based?
  **A:** Specific numbers or graph trends cited as evidence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on ecosystem disruptions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "What is an invasive species?" Look for: A non-native species that spreads and harms the ecosystem.

- **Q:** What is an invasive species?
  **A:** A non-native species that spreads and harms the ecosystem.

**Assessment:** Data-based argument.

**Answer key:** Quiz: succession stages, fire-adapted ecosystems, invasive species definition and example, predicting population changes from disruptions. Argument rubric: claim, data cited, reasoning about cause.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The scientific method and new instruments

**Prepare:** Jar, balloon, rubber band, straw (barometer); magnifier.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking empiricism and rationalism are opposite enemies.
- Thinking science and religion always conflicted.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bacon and Descartes
*Student:* Compare Bacon's empiricism and Descartes's rationalism using short quotes.

*How to teach:* Read short quotes from Bacon and Descartes. Your child compares empiricism and rationalism in a T-chart. Check before moving on: ask "Bacon?" Look for: Knowledge through observation and experiment (empiricism).

- **Q:** Bacon?
  **A:** Knowledge through observation and experiment (empiricism).
- **Q:** Descartes?
  **A:** 'I think, therefore I am'; reason (rationalism).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build a barometer
*Student:* Build a jar barometer and record air-pressure changes for a week.

*How to teach:* Stretch a balloon over a jar, tape a straw pointer, and mark a scale. Your child records readings daily for a week. Check before moving on: ask "Rising pointer means?" Look for: Higher air pressure, usually fair weather.

- **Q:** Rising pointer means?
  **A:** Higher air pressure, usually fair weather.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Microscope
*Student:* Read about Leeuwenhoek and Hooke and draw magnified objects with a magnifier.

*How to teach:* Read about Leeuwenhoek and Hooke. Your child draws magnified objects (salt, a leaf, fabric) with a magnifier. Check before moving on: ask "Hooke's discovery?" Look for: Named 'cells' after seeing cork.

- **Q:** Hooke's discovery?
  **A:** Named 'cells' after seeing cork.
- **Q:** Leeuwenhoek?
  **A:** Saw microorganisms.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Science and faith
*Student:* Discuss how many scientists held religious beliefs and how science and religion coexisted.

*How to teach:* Discuss examples of scientists with religious beliefs (Kepler, Newton) and how science and religion coexisted. Check before moving on: ask "Example?" Look for: Newton and Kepler were devout Christians.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Newton and Kepler were devout Christians.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write how scientific rationalism encouraged questioning of political authority.

*How to teach:* Your child writes how scientific rationalism encouraged questioning political authority, with an example. Check before moving on: ask "Key idea?" Look for: If nature follows laws discovered by reason, maybe government should too.

- **Q:** Key idea?
  **A:** If nature follows laws discovered by reason, maybe government should too.

**Assessment:** Written explanation.

**Answer key:** Written explanation rubric: links scientific method to questioning tradition, gives example (Enlightenment thinkers), accurate.

## Spanish 1A: Las direcciones: getting around town

**Prepare:** A printed map of a Spanish city center (e.g., Madrid's Puerta del Sol area or a textbook map). Record or prepare directions to read aloud.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing derecho (straight) and derecha (right).
- Forgetting to use the tú command forms as chunks: dobla, sigue, cruza.

### Day 1 (Monday): Direction words
*Student:* Learn derecha, izquierda, todo derecho, cuadra, esquina and follow parent's spoken directions around the house.

*How to teach:* Learn direction words; follow your spoken directions around the house.

- **Q:** What does 'Sigue todo derecho' mean?
  **A:** Go straight ahead.
- **Q:** How do you say 'turn left'?
  **A:** Dobla a la izquierda.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map practice
*Student:* Trace routes on a printed map of a Spanish city center as you listen to recorded directions.

*How to teach:* Trace routes on a city map as you read directions.

- **Q:** What does 'Camina tres cuadras' mean?
  **A:** Walk three blocks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Giving directions
*Student:* Write directions from your house to three local places using memorized chunks (dobla a la derecha, sigue derecho).

*How to teach:* Write directions to three local places.

- **Q:** Write: 'Turn right on the corner and walk two blocks.'
  **A:** Dobla a la derecha en la esquina y camina dos cuadras.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Info gap
*Student:* Play an information-gap map game with a parent: each has half the places and must ask ¿Dónde está...?

*How to teach:* Information-gap game.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Excuse me, where is the museum?'
  **A:** Perdón, ¿dónde está el museo?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a listening quiz following directions on a map.

*How to teach:* Give the direction-following listening quiz.

- **Q:** What does 'Cruza la calle' mean?
  **A:** Cross the street.

**Assessment:** Direction-following listening quiz.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz: child follows 5 sets of directions on a map and names the destination (5/5 proficient, 4/5 acceptable). Key phrases: dobla a la derecha (turn right), dobla a la izquierda (turn left), sigue todo derecho (go straight), cruza la calle (cross the street), camina dos cuadras (walk two blocks), en la esquina (on the corner).

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Stop-motion production

**Prepare:** Stop-motion app, tripod or stack of books, the storyboard, characters and set. Original composition from week 17 for soundtrack (optional).

**Watch for:**
- Bumping the camera mid-scene. Secure the camera; use a remote or timer.
- Using copyrighted songs. Use original or royalty-free music.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shoot scene 1
*Student:* Capture frames for the first scene of the animation.

*How to teach:* Shoot scene 1 following the storyboard.

- **Q:** What is a frame rate?
  **A:** The number of frames shown per second (e.g., 12 fps).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shoot scenes 2–3
*Student:* Capture frames for remaining scenes.

*How to teach:* Shoot scenes 2–3.

- **Q:** How can you make a character look like it's jumping?
  **A:** Raise it slightly each frame using clear wire or support, then lower it back down.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Edit timing and add titles and credits.

*How to teach:* Edit timing; add titles and credits.

- **Q:** What belongs in credits?
  **A:** Names of people and their roles, and music/sound sources.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sound
*Student:* Add a soundtrack or sound effects, possibly using your own composition.

*How to teach:* Add soundtrack or effects.

- **Q:** Why use your own music?
  **A:** It's original and avoids copyright problems.

### Day 5 (Friday): Premiere
*Student:* Premiere the animation for family and reflect on roles you played in production.

*How to teach:* Premiere the film; reflect on production roles.

- **Q:** Which production role did you enjoy most, and why?
  **A:** Open answer.

**Assessment:** Completed stop-motion film.

**Answer key:** Stop-motion film rubric (1–4): story (clear beginning, middle, end), animation quality (smooth movement, stable camera, consistent lighting), editing (timing, titles, credits), sound (soundtrack/effects synced). Reflection names roles (director, animator, set designer, editor, sound designer) and what was learned.

## Physical Education 7: Field events

**Prepare:** Tape measure, softball or light medicine ball (2–4 lb), sand pit or soft grass, cones for takeoff line. Throwing area clear of people.

**Watch for:**
- Measuring a long jump from where the toes landed. Measure from the takeoff line to the closest mark the body made.
- Thinking 90° (straight up) or very low throws go farthest. A release around 35–45° typically gives the most distance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Standing long jump
*Student:* Practice arm swing and takeoff and measure 3 jumps.

*How to teach:* Standing long jump: swing arms back, bend knees, explode forward swinging arms, land on two feet, fall forward. Measure 3 jumps.

- **Q:** How do arms help a standing long jump?
  **A:** A strong forward arm swing adds momentum to the jump.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Running long jump
*Student:* Practice approach and takeoff into sand or grass.

*How to teach:* Running long jump: short approach (6–10 strides), take off from one foot near the line, drive knee up, land feet first.

- **Q:** Where is the jump measured from?
  **A:** The takeoff line (board) to the nearest mark made in the landing area.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Throw
*Student:* Practice softball throw for distance or medicine-ball put.

*How to teach:* Softball throw for distance (crow hop + full throw) or medicine-ball put (push from shoulder, not thrown like a ball).

- **Q:** Why use the legs and hips in a throw or put?
  **A:** Large muscles generate more force, which transfers up through the trunk to the arm (kinetic chain).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Angles
*Student:* Experiment with release angles and record which goes farthest.

*How to teach:* Throw at three angles and record distances.

- **Q:** Which release angle gives the most distance, and why?
  **A:** About 35–45°: it balances upward speed (time in air) and forward speed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Measure best marks in each event.

*How to teach:* Measure best marks in each event.

- **Q:** What is momentum in jumping?
  **A:** Mass in motion — a faster approach gives more momentum to carry into the jump.

**Assessment:** Field event marks.

**Answer key:** Field event marks: best of 3 for standing long jump, running long jump and throw, recorded in meters/cm. Angle experiment proficient: records distance at about 3 release angles (low, ~40°, high) and concludes a medium angle (around 35–45°) goes farthest because it balances height (time in air) and forward speed.

## Health Education 7: Communicable diseases

**Prepare:** Glitter or Glo Germ lotion, soap, paper towels. Print a simple immune-system diagram. Read CDC 'How vaccines work.'

**Watch for:**
- Taking antibiotics for a cold or flu. Antibiotics kill bacteria, not viruses; misuse causes antibiotic resistance.
- Thinking hand sanitizer is always as good as soap. Soap and water for 20 seconds is best, especially when hands are visibly dirty.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pathogens
*Student:* Compare bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites.

*How to teach:* Your child compares the four pathogen types in a chart with an example disease each.

- **Q:** What is the difference between bacteria and viruses?
  **A:** Bacteria are living single cells that can reproduce on their own; viruses are much smaller and must invade a host cell to reproduce.
- **Q:** Name a disease caused by a fungus.
  **A:** Athlete's foot, ringworm or a yeast infection.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Germ spread
*Student:* Use glitter on hands to model germ spread, then test handwashing.

*How to teach:* Put glitter on your child's hand, shake hands, touch items, then see the spread. Wash with water only vs soap for 20 seconds and compare.

- **Q:** Which removed more glitter — water alone or soap and scrubbing 20 seconds?
  **A:** Soap and scrubbing for 20 seconds.
- **Q:** Name three ways germs spread.
  **A:** Direct contact, coughs/sneezes (droplets), contaminated surfaces, food or water, insect bites.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Immune system
*Student:* Diagram how the immune system fights infection.

*How to teach:* Diagram: skin/mucus barriers → inflammation/fever → white blood cells → antibodies and memory cells.

- **Q:** What are antibodies?
  **A:** Proteins made by white blood cells that recognize and help destroy specific pathogens.
- **Q:** What is the body's first line of defense?
  **A:** Skin and mucous membranes (plus tears, saliva, stomach acid).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Vaccines
*Student:* Learn how vaccines work and how herd immunity protects communities.

*How to teach:* Explain how vaccines create immune memory without the illness, and herd immunity.

- **Q:** How does a vaccine work?
  **A:** It exposes the immune system to a harmless form or part of a germ so it makes antibodies and memory cells, ready to fight the real germ quickly.
- **Q:** Who does herd immunity protect?
  **A:** People who can't be vaccinated, such as newborns or people with weak immune systems.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on communicable diseases.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key.

- **Q:** Will antibiotics cure the flu?
  **A:** No — the flu is caused by a virus; antibiotics only work against bacteria.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) pathogen = germ that causes disease 2) bacteria = single-celled living organisms (strep throat), some treated with antibiotics 3) viruses = must invade cells to reproduce (cold, flu, COVID-19); not killed by antibiotics 4) fungi = athlete's foot, ringworm 5) parasites = lice, giardia, tapeworms 6) spread: direct contact, droplets/air, contaminated food/water, vectors (mosquitoes, ticks), contaminated surfaces 7) white blood cells and antibodies fight infection 8) vaccines train the immune system using a harmless piece or weakened/inactivated form of a germ 9) herd immunity = when enough people are immune, spread is limited, protecting those who can't be vaccinated 10) wash 20 seconds.
