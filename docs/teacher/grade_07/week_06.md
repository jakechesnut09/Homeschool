# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 6

## English Language Arts 7: The Call of the Wild, chapters 1–2

**Prepare:** Get The Call of the Wild (Gutenberg or library); a map showing California, Washington, the Alaska panhandle and the Yukon; a short Klondike Gold Rush article (National Park Service 'Klondike Gold Rush' pages work). Parental preview: the novel includes dog fights, beatings with a club and animal deaths.

**Watch for:**
- Reading Buck's changes only as 'getting worse.' London presents them as adapting to survive — help your child weigh both views with evidence.
- Guessing word meanings without evidence. Model hunting for a synonym, contrast or example clue in the same or next sentence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Background: Klondike Gold Rush
*Student:* Read a short article on the 1897 Klondike Gold Rush and locate Santa Clara, Seattle, Skagway and Dawson on a map.

*How to teach:* Read the article together. Your child locates Santa Clara, Seattle, Skagway and Dawson on the map, traces the route north and explains why big dogs were valuable. Check before moving on: ask "Why were large, strong dogs stolen and sold in 1897?" Look for: Gold was found in the Klondike; prospectors needed strong, warm-coated dogs to haul sleds over snow.

- **Q:** Why were large, strong dogs stolen and sold in 1897?
  **A:** Gold was found in the Klondike; prospectors needed strong, warm-coated dogs to haul sleds over snow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 1
*Student:* Read Chapter 1 'Into the Primitive' and list three lessons Buck learns from the man in the red sweater.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 1, 'Into the Primitive', aloud or with audio. Your child lists three lessons from the man in the red sweater with quotes. Check before moving on: ask "Who sells Buck and why?" Look for: Manuel, the gardener's helper on Judge Miller's place, to pay his gambling debts.

- **Q:** Who sells Buck and why?
  **A:** Manuel, the gardener's helper on Judge Miller's place, to pay his gambling debts.
- **Q:** What does the man in the red sweater teach Buck?
  **A:** That a man with a club is a 'lawgiver' who must be obeyed — Buck is beaten until he stops attacking, though he is not broken in spirit.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Context clues
*Student:* Use context to define 10 challenging words from chapters 1–2 and check with a dictionary.

*How to teach:* Model one word with a context clue. Your child defines 10 words from chapters 1–2 (e.g., primitive, imperious, ferocity, latent, discretion), then checks a dictionary and notes which clues worked. Check before moving on: ask "What does 'primitive' mean in the chapter title?" Look for: Early, wild, basic — the uncivilized state Buck is entering.

- **Q:** What does 'primitive' mean in the chapter title?
  **A:** Early, wild, basic — the uncivilized state Buck is entering.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapter 2
*Student:* Read Chapter 2 'The Law of Club and Fang' and write a journal entry on how Curly's death changes Buck.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 2, 'The Law of Club and Fang'. Your child writes a journal entry on Curly's death and what it teaches Buck. Check before moving on: ask "How does Curly die?" Look for: She makes friendly advances to a husky, is slashed, and when she falls the other huskies swarm and kill her.

- **Q:** How does Curly die?
  **A:** She makes friendly advances to a husky, is slashed, and when she falls the other huskies swarm and kill her.
- **Q:** What lesson does Buck take from it?
  **A:** There is no fair play: once a dog is down, it is finished — so he must never go down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Discussion & check
*Student:* Discuss whether Buck is 'becoming worse' or 'adapting' and take a chapters 1–2 quiz.

*How to teach:* Discuss 'becoming worse or adapting?' — each side gives two pieces of evidence. Then give the chapters 1–2 quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Name two survival skills Buck learns in Chapter 2." Look for: Digging a sleeping hole in the snow; eating fast and stealing food (bacon) when hungry; finding water under ice.

- **Q:** Name two survival skills Buck learns in Chapter 2.
  **A:** Digging a sleeping hole in the snow; eating fast and stealing food (bacon) when hungry; finding water under ice.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and journal entries.

**Answer key:** Chapters 1–2 quiz: 1) Buck is a St. Bernard–Scotch shepherd mix living at Judge Miller's place in the Santa Clara Valley. 2) Manuel sells him to pay gambling debts. 3) The man in the red sweater teaches the law of the club. 4) Curly is killed by huskies after a friendly approach — the law of fang. 5) Perrault and François are the French-Canadian mail couriers who buy him. 6) Spitz is the lead dog Buck comes to hate. Journal rubric: 4 = cites specific events and explains the change in Buck's thinking; 2 = retells only.

## Mathematics 7: Unit rates with fractions

**Prepare:** Measuring tape (mark 1/4 mile ≈ 440 yards on a safe route or track), stopwatch, calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Dividing in the wrong order (time ÷ distance when asked for miles per hour). Say the unit: 'miles per hour' means miles ÷ hours.
- Inverting the wrong fraction in a complex fraction. Model 'keep, change, flip' only after showing why: dividing by 3/4 is asking how many 3/4's fit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Walking rates
*Student:* Time how long it takes to walk 1/4 mile and compute miles per hour using a ratio of fractions.

*How to teach:* Walk 1/4 mile and time it. Convert minutes to hours (5 min = 1/12 hr) and divide. Your child computes their own mph. Check before moving on: ask "1/4 mile in 5 minutes. Miles per hour?" Look for: (1/4) ÷ (1/12) = 3 mph.

- **Q:** 1/4 mile in 5 minutes. Miles per hour?
  **A:** (1/4) ÷ (1/12) = 3 mph.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Complex fractions
*Student:* Simplify complex fractions like (1/2)/(3/4) by multiplying by the reciprocal and connect to unit rate.

*How to teach:* Model (1/2)/(3/4) = 1/2 × 4/3 = 2/3. Your child simplifies several and explains each as a unit rate. Check before moving on: ask "Simplify (2/3)/(1/6)." Look for: 4.

- **Q:** Simplify (2/3)/(1/6).
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** Simplify (3/5)/(9/10).
  **A:** 2/3.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recipe rates
*Student:* Find cups of flour per batch and batches per cup from a recipe using 2/3 cup for 1/2 batch.

*How to teach:* Use the recipe: 2/3 cup flour for 1/2 batch. Your child finds cups per batch and batches per cup. Check before moving on: ask "Cups of flour per batch?" Look for: (2/3) ÷ (1/2) = 4/3 cups.

- **Q:** Cups of flour per batch?
  **A:** (2/3) ÷ (1/2) = 4/3 cups.
- **Q:** Batches per cup?
  **A:** 3/4 batch.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Area rates
*Student:* Find how much paint covers 1 square yard if 1/3 gallon covers 2 1/2 square yards; solve 6 similar problems.

*How to teach:* Model the paint problem as gallons ÷ square yards. Your child solves 6 similar problems with units. Check before moving on: ask "1/3 gallon covers 2 1/2 sq yd. Gallons per square yard?" Look for: (1/3) ÷ (5/2) = 2/15 gal.

- **Q:** 1/3 gallon covers 2 1/2 sq yd. Gallons per square yard?
  **A:** (1/3) ÷ (5/2) = 2/15 gal.
- **Q:** Square yards per gallon?
  **A:** 7 1/2 sq yd.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a 10-problem fraction unit-rate set and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the 10-problem set and corrects errors in a different color. Check before moving on: ask "A snail moves 3/8 ft in 1/4 min. Feet per minute?" Look for: 1 1/2 ft per minute.

- **Q:** A snail moves 3/8 ft in 1/4 min. Feet per minute?
  **A:** 1 1/2 ft per minute.

**Assessment:** Problem set on unit rates with fractions.

**Answer key:** Sample set answers: 1) (1/2)/(1/4) = 2 2) 3/4 mi in 1/3 hr = 2 1/4 mph 3) (5/6)/(2/3) = 1 1/4 4) 1 1/2 cups per 3/4 batch → 2 cups per batch 5) 2/5 gal for 1 1/5 sq yd → 1/3 gal per sq yd 6) 7 1/2 mi in 2 1/2 hr = 3 mph 7) (3/4)/(3/8) = 2 8) $1/2 for 1/4 lb → $2 per lb 9) 4/5 km in 1/5 hr = 4 km/h 10) (9/10)/(3/5) = 3/2. Score 9–10 mastery; below 7 re-teach division of fractions.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Evidence of chemical reactions

**Prepare:** Nails (new and rusty), steel wool (soap-free), jar; baking soda, vinegar, milk, lemon juice, Alka-Seltzer, ice, sugar, candle and matches (adult), bread and toaster.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any change in appearance is chemical. Melting ice and dissolving sugar are physical: no new substance.
- Thinking bubbles always mean a chemical reaction. Boiling water bubbles but is physical.

### Day 1 (Monday): Phenomenon: rusty nail
*Student:* Compare a new and rusted nail and list differences in properties; start a steel-wool rusting jar to observe all week.

*How to teach:* Compare nails: color, texture, magnetism, strength. Start the steel-wool jar (wet steel wool with vinegar, rinse, place in jar). Check before moving on: ask "What is rust?" Look for: Iron oxide — a new substance formed when iron reacts with oxygen and water.

- **Q:** What is rust?
  **A:** Iron oxide — a new substance formed when iron reacts with oxygen and water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Signs of reactions
*Student:* Run four mini-reactions (baking soda + vinegar, milk + lemon juice, Alka-Seltzer + water, steel wool + vinegar) and record evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child runs four mini-reactions and records evidence (gas, color change, precipitate/solid, temperature change). Check before moving on: ask "Evidence in milk + lemon juice?" Look for: Curdling — a new solid forms.

- **Q:** Evidence in milk + lemon juice?
  **A:** Curdling — a new solid forms.
- **Q:** Evidence in baking soda + vinegar?
  **A:** Bubbles of gas (CO2) and a temperature drop.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Physical vs. chemical
*Student:* Test melting ice, dissolving sugar, burning a candle and toasting bread; classify each with evidence.

*How to teach:* Test each change; your child classifies with evidence. Check before moving on: ask "Burning candle?" Look for: Chemical — new substances (CO2, water vapor, soot), light and heat.

- **Q:** Burning candle?
  **A:** Chemical — new substances (CO2, water vapor, soot), light and heat.
- **Q:** Melting ice?
  **A:** Physical — still water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Before and after data
*Student:* Analyze a data table of properties before and after interactions to decide if new substances formed.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes a before/after property table. Check before moving on: ask "A substance with different melting point, density and color after mixing — new substance?" Look for: Yes, changed characteristic properties mean a new substance formed.

- **Q:** A substance with different melting point, density and color after mixing — new substance?
  **A:** Yes, changed characteristic properties mean a new substance formed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER on whether rusting is a chemical reaction using the week's steel-wool observations.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER on rusting using the jar observations. Check before moving on: ask "Is rusting chemical?" Look for: Yes — new substance with different color, texture, and it's not magnetic like iron.

- **Q:** Is rusting chemical?
  **A:** Yes — new substance with different color, texture, and it's not magnetic like iron.

**Assessment:** Reaction evidence chart and CER.

**Answer key:** Reaction evidence chart: four reactions with gas/color/solid/temperature evidence. CER rubric: 4 = accurate claim, at least two pieces of specific evidence from the investigation (numbers/observations), reasoning that links evidence to the scientific principle; 3 = claim and evidence, reasoning thin; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or incorrect. Expected claim: rusting is a chemical reaction because a reddish-brown, crumbly substance with different properties forms.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Expansion of Muslim rule and trade

**Prepare:** Map of Muslim expansion 632–750; Ibn Battuta excerpts (Fordham); description of Baghdad's round city.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Islam spread only by conquest. Trade, intermarriage and missionaries spread it in Africa and Southeast Asia.
- Thinking Sunni and Shia split over beliefs about God. They split over who should lead after Muhammad.

### Day 1 (Monday): The caliphates
*Student:* Map the expansion under the Rashidun, Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates from 632 to 750.

*How to teach:* Your child maps the expansion of each caliphate. Check before moving on: ask "Capitals of the Umayyads and Abbasids?" Look for: Damascus; Baghdad.

- **Q:** Capitals of the Umayyads and Abbasids?
  **A:** Damascus; Baghdad.
- **Q:** How far did Umayyad rule reach?
  **A:** From Spain to the Indus River valley.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sunni and Shia
*Student:* Explain the origins of the Sunni-Shia division over succession.

*How to teach:* Explain the succession dispute. Check before moving on: ask "Sunni view?" Look for: The community should choose the leader (they accepted Abu Bakr).

- **Q:** Sunni view?
  **A:** The community should choose the leader (they accepted Abu Bakr).
- **Q:** Shia view?
  **A:** Leadership belonged to Ali, Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, and his descendants.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Baghdad
*Student:* Read a description of Baghdad's round city and explain why it became a world center of trade and learning.

*How to teach:* Read about Baghdad. Your child explains why it became a center. Check before moving on: ask "Why Baghdad?" Look for: Founded 762 on the Tigris near the Euphrates, on trade routes linking Asia, Africa and Europe; caliphs supported scholars.

- **Q:** Why Baghdad?
  **A:** Founded 762 on the Tigris near the Euphrates, on trade routes linking Asia, Africa and Europe; caliphs supported scholars.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ibn Battuta
*Student:* Read excerpts from Ibn Battuta's travels and trace his route across Afro-Eurasia on a map.

*How to teach:* Read Ibn Battuta excerpts. Your child traces his route. Check before moving on: ask "Who was Ibn Battuta?" Look for: A Moroccan Muslim scholar who traveled about 75,000 miles across Africa, the Middle East, India and China in the 1300s.

- **Q:** Who was Ibn Battuta?
  **A:** A Moroccan Muslim scholar who traveled about 75,000 miles across Africa, the Middle East, India and China in the 1300s.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how trade spread Islam and Arabic, with map evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph on how trade spread Islam and Arabic. Check before moving on: ask "Evidence?" Look for: Merchants carried religion and Arabic along trade routes; Arabic became the language of trade, learning and religion.

- **Q:** Evidence?
  **A:** Merchants carried religion and Arabic along trade routes; Arabic became the language of trade, learning and religion.

**Assessment:** Expansion map and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Map: caliphates shaded with dates and capitals. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate.

## Spanish 1A: ¿Qué hora es? Telling time

**Prepare:** Paper plate, brad fastener, construction paper hands. A daily schedule. Find a Spanish train timetable (Renfe) or TV listing with 24-hour times.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'son la una.' One o'clock is singular: Es la una; all other hours use son las…
- Confusing ¿Qué hora es? (What time is it now?) with ¿A qué hora es…? (At what time is…?).

### Day 1 (Monday): ¿Qué hora es?
*Student:* Build a paper-plate clock and practice es la una / son las... with y cuarto, y media, and menos diez.

*How to teach:* Build the clock; practice es la una / son las dos, y cuarto, y media, menos diez.

- **Q:** How do you say 2:30?
  **A:** Son las dos y media.
- **Q:** How do you say 3:45?
  **A:** Son las cuatro menos cuarto (or son las tres y cuarenta y cinco).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Times of day
*Student:* Add de la mañana, de la tarde, de la noche and write the time of 10 daily events on your own schedule.

*How to teach:* Add de la mañana/tarde/noche; write times of 10 daily events.

- **Q:** Write 'I eat lunch at 12:30 p.m.'
  **A:** Como el almuerzo a las doce y media de la tarde.
- **Q:** How do you say 7:00 a.m.?
  **A:** Son las siete de la mañana.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): ¿A qué hora?
*Student:* Contrast ¿Qué hora es? with ¿A qué hora es...?; ask a parent five questions about family events and record answers.

*How to teach:* Contrast ¿Qué hora es? with ¿A qué hora es…?; ask five questions.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿A qué hora es la cena? (6:00)
  **A:** La cena es a las seis.

### Day 4 (Thursday): 24-hour clock
*Student:* Read a Spanish train timetable or TV listing that uses 24-hour time and convert 10 times into spoken Spanish.

*How to teach:* Read 24-hour times and convert them.

- **Q:** A train leaves at 17:15. Say it in conversational Spanish.
  **A:** A las cinco y cuarto de la tarde.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a listening quiz (hear a time, draw the clock hands) and say the time for 10 clocks.

*How to teach:* Give the listening quiz and oral clock reading.

- **Q:** How do you say 1:10?
  **A:** Es la una y diez.

**Assessment:** Time listening quiz and oral clock reading.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz: parent reads 10 times, child draws hands. Sample key: Es la una y cuarto (1:15); Son las tres y media (3:30); Son las cinco menos diez (4:50); Son las ocho y veinte (8:20); Es mediodía (12:00 p.m.). Oral: says 10 clock times correctly (8/10 proficient), using y for minutes after and menos for minutes before the hour (past :30).

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Color theory

**Prepare:** Tempera or acrylic in red, yellow, blue, white and black; brushes; water cup; paper plate palette; paper towels; heavy paper with a pre-drawn 12-part wheel.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking black is the only way to darken. Mixing a complement produces richer darks and neutrals.
- Adding a lot of dark to light when mixing. Add dark to light in tiny amounts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Color wheel
*Student:* Mix and paint a 12-part color wheel from primaries only.

*How to teach:* Mix and paint the 12-part wheel from the three primaries only.

- **Q:** Which primaries make green?
  **A:** Blue and yellow.
- **Q:** Name the tertiary between red and violet.
  **A:** Red-violet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tints and shades
*Student:* Paint tint and shade scales for two colors.

*How to teach:* Paint tint scales (add white) and shade scales (add black) for two colors.

- **Q:** What is a tint? A shade?
  **A:** Tint = a color plus white; shade = a color plus black.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Color schemes
*Student:* Paint four small squares showing complementary, analogous, monochromatic and triadic schemes.

*How to teach:* Paint four squares: complementary, analogous, monochromatic, triadic.

- **Q:** What is the complement of blue?
  **A:** Orange.
- **Q:** What is an analogous scheme?
  **A:** Colors next to each other on the wheel, such as blue, blue-green and green.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Neutrals
*Student:* Mix browns and grays by combining complements.

*How to teach:* Mix complements (red+green, blue+orange, yellow+violet) for browns and grays.

- **Q:** Why do complements make neutral colors?
  **A:** Together they contain all three primaries, which cancel each other toward brown or gray.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Label your swatches and quiz yourself on color vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Label swatches and quiz yourself.

- **Q:** What colors make up the triadic scheme of secondaries?
  **A:** Orange, green and violet.

**Assessment:** Color wheel and scheme swatches.

**Answer key:** Color wheel: 3 primaries (red, yellow, blue), 3 secondaries (orange, green, violet), 6 tertiaries (red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet, red-violet) placed correctly. Schemes: complementary (opposites, e.g., blue/orange), analogous (3 neighbors, e.g., yellow, yellow-green, green), monochromatic (one hue with tints/shades), triadic (three equally spaced, e.g., red/yellow/blue or orange/green/violet). Quiz answers: primary can't be mixed; secondary = two primaries; tint = color + white; shade = color + black; tone = color + gray.

## Physical Education 7: Soccer: passing and receiving

**Prepare:** Soccer ball (size 4 or 5), cones, a sturdy wall, a partner. Set up a cone goal 1 m wide at 10 m for the test.

**Watch for:**
- Passing with the toe. The inside of the foot gives a bigger, flatter surface for accuracy.
- Stopping the ball dead under the feet. A good first touch cushions the ball a step away in the direction you want to go.

### Day 1 (Monday): Passing technique
*Student:* Practice inside-foot passes against a wall, 50 with each foot.

*How to teach:* Teach: plant non-kicking foot beside the ball pointing to the target, turn kicking foot out, lock ankle, strike the middle of the ball, follow through. 50 wall passes each foot.

- **Q:** Where should the plant foot point when passing?
  **A:** At the target, beside the ball.
- **Q:** Why lock the ankle?
  **A:** A firm foot gives a cleaner, more accurate pass.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Receiving
*Student:* Practice receiving with sole, inside foot and thigh and controlling in one touch.

*How to teach:* Toss or pass balls to your child to receive with sole, inside foot and thigh, cushioning into one controlled touch.

- **Q:** How do you 'cushion' the ball when receiving?
  **A:** Relax and pull the receiving surface back slightly as the ball arrives to absorb its speed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Passing on the move
*Student:* Pass with a partner while jogging through cones.

*How to teach:* Pass back and forth while jogging through cones; lead the partner by passing into their path.

- **Q:** Why pass ahead of a moving teammate?
  **A:** So they can run onto the ball without stopping.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rondo
*Student:* Play a 3-v-1 keep-away rondo focusing on quick passes.

*How to teach:* Play 3-v-1 rondo: three passers in a square keep the ball from one defender; switch when the defender wins it.

- **Q:** What helps the passers keep possession?
  **A:** Quick passes, moving to open angles, using two touches or fewer, communicating.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skill check
*Student:* Complete a passing accuracy test (10 passes into a cone goal from 10 meters).

*How to teach:* Run the accuracy test and record score.

- **Q:** Name three cues for an accurate inside-foot pass.
  **A:** Plant foot beside ball pointing at target; locked ankle with inside of foot; follow through toward target.

**Assessment:** Passing accuracy test.

**Answer key:** Passing accuracy test: 10 inside-foot passes from 10 m into a 1-m cone goal (5 each foot). Scoring: 9–10 advanced, 7–8 proficient, 5–6 developing, 0–4 beginning. Note form cues observed: plant foot beside ball pointing at target, ankle locked, strike middle of ball, follow through toward target.

## Health Education 7: Nutrition goal and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Have your child keep a 3-day food log (Thursday–Saturday before, or use recent days). Choose a balanced dinner recipe and buy ingredients. Prepare a unit test (see key) and a 3-week goal tracker.

**Watch for:**
- Writing vague goals like 'eat healthier.' A SMART goal is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.
- Comparing a single day to MyPlate and judging harshly. Look at patterns across several days.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food log
*Student:* Review a 3-day food log and compare it to MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Your child totals each food group from the 3-day log and compares with MyPlate targets, circling groups that are low or high.

- **Q:** If your target is 2.5 cups of vegetables and you averaged 1 cup, how much more do you need daily?
  **A:** 1.5 cups more per day.
- **Q:** Which food groups do most teens under-eat?
  **A:** Vegetables, fruits, whole grains and dairy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): SMART goal
*Student:* Write a SMART nutrition goal (e.g., 'eat 3 servings of vegetables 5 days a week').

*How to teach:* Teach SMART with an example. Your child writes a nutrition goal and checks each letter.

- **Q:** Is 'I will eat more fruit' a SMART goal? Fix it.
  **A:** No — not specific/measurable/time-bound. Fixed: 'I will eat one piece of fruit at breakfast 5 days a week for the next 3 weeks.'
- **Q:** What does the 'T' in SMART stand for?
  **A:** Time-bound (has a deadline or time frame).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Healthy recipe
*Student:* Plan and cook a balanced family dinner.

*How to teach:* Your child plans and cooks a balanced family dinner with your help, using food safety steps and filling half the plate with vegetables/fruit.

- **Q:** Which food groups are in your dinner?
  **A:** Answer should list at least four groups, e.g., chicken (protein), brown rice (grain), broccoli (vegetable), milk (dairy).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review unit vocabulary and concepts.

*How to teach:* Play a quick review game with the unit vocabulary (nutrient, % DV, added sugars, fad diet, cross-contamination, SMART goal).

- **Q:** Define cross-contamination.
  **A:** Transfer of harmful germs from one food or surface to another, such as raw chicken juices onto salad.
- **Q:** What is a credible source?
  **A:** A trustworthy source with expert authors, evidence and no sales motive, such as a government or medical site.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the unit 1 test and start tracking the goal for 3 weeks.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 1 test (key in assessment section). Then set up the 3-week goal tracker.

- **Q:** What is one way you'll track your SMART goal?
  **A:** A daily checkbox chart or app log reviewed each week.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test and goal tracker.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test (suggested 10 items): 1) six nutrients: carbs, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water 2) main energy nutrient: carbohydrates 3) 4 g sugar = 1 tsp 4) 20% DV or more is high, 5% or less low 5) MyPlate groups: fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy 6) half your plate fruits and vegetables 7) two signs of a fad diet (quick results, cuts food groups, sells products) 8) 60 minutes of activity daily 9) clean, separate, cook, chill 10) example of an external influence (ads, family, friends, culture). 9–10 = advanced, 7–8 proficient. Goal tracker: SMART goal written correctly with a daily checkbox chart for 3 weeks.
