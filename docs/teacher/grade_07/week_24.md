# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 24

## English Language Arts 7: Note-taking, paraphrasing and avoiding plagiarism

**Prepare:** Index cards or a digital notes doc; five paragraph passages from sources; a documentary clip on the topic.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking changing a few words is paraphrasing. Paraphrase = read, cover the source, write in your own words and structure, then cite.
- Dangling modifiers: 'Walking to school, the rain started.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Note cards
*Student:* Take 15 note cards organized by subtopic, each with source and page.

*How to teach:* Model a note card with subtopic heading, note in your own words, source and page. Your child takes 15 note cards organized by subtopic. Check before moving on: ask "Why record the source on every card?" Look for: So citations are accurate and plagiarism is avoided.

- **Q:** Why record the source on every card?
  **A:** So citations are accurate and plagiarism is avoided.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paraphrase practice
*Student:* Paraphrase five passages, then compare with originals to check for plagiarism.

*How to teach:* Model paraphrasing: read, cover the source, write from memory, then check. Your child paraphrases five passages and compares each with the original to make sure wording and structure are new. Check before moving on: ask "Is a paraphrase still cited?" Look for: Yes — ideas from a source always need a citation.

- **Q:** Is a paraphrase still cited?
  **A:** Yes — ideas from a source always need a citation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar: modifiers
*Student:* Fix 12 sentences with misplaced and dangling modifiers, then check note cards for the same errors.

*How to teach:* Teach misplaced and dangling modifiers with examples. Your child fixes 12 sentences, then checks note cards for the same errors. Check before moving on: ask "Fix: 'Walking to school, the rain started.'" Look for: 'As I was walking to school, the rain started.'

- **Q:** Fix: 'Walking to school, the rain started.'
  **A:** 'As I was walking to school, the rain started.'
- **Q:** Fix: 'She almost ate the whole pizza' (she ate nearly all of it).
  **A:** 'She ate almost the whole pizza.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Video source
*Student:* Watch a documentary clip on the topic and compare what it emphasizes versus a print source (RI.7.7).

*How to teach:* Watch a documentary clip on the topic. Your child compares what it emphasizes versus a print source and explains why each medium highlights different things. Check before moving on: ask "What can video emphasize that print can't?" Look for: Visuals, sound, emotion; print can give more detail and data.

- **Q:** What can video emphasize that print can't?
  **A:** Visuals, sound, emotion; print can give more detail and data.

### Day 5 (Friday): Outline
*Student:* Organize notes into a formal outline with thesis and three subtopics.

*How to teach:* Model a formal outline format. Your child organizes notes into an outline with a thesis and three subtopics, each with supporting points. Check before moving on: ask "Formal outline format?" Look for: Roman numerals for main sections, capital letters for subpoints.

- **Q:** Formal outline format?
  **A:** Roman numerals for main sections, capital letters for subpoints.

**Assessment:** Note cards, paraphrase set and outline.

**Answer key:** Note cards: 15 with source and page. Paraphrase set: five paraphrases in own words and structure with citations. Outline: thesis and three subtopics with supporting points.

## Mathematics 7: Solving two-step equations

**Prepare:** Paper algebra tiles (cut rectangles for x and small squares for 1; use two colors for negative).

**Watch for:**
- Distributing to only the first term: 2(x + 3) = 2x + 3 is wrong; it is 2x + 6.
- Multiplying by a fraction's reciprocal but forgetting the sign: dividing by −2/3 = multiplying by −3/2.

### Day 1 (Monday): Two methods
*Student:* Solve 2(x + 3) = 14 by dividing first and by distributing first, comparing steps.

*How to teach:* Solve 2(x + 3) = 14 both ways: divide by 2 (x + 3 = 7) and distribute (2x + 6 = 14). Your child solves 4 more both ways. Check before moving on: ask "2(x + 3) = 14?" Look for: x = 4 by either method.

- **Q:** 2(x + 3) = 14?
  **A:** x = 4 by either method.
- **Q:** 5(x − 2) = 35?
  **A:** x = 9.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Negative coefficients
*Student:* Solve equations with negative and fractional coefficients like -2/3 x + 5 = -7.

*How to teach:* Model −2/3 x + 5 = −7. Your child solves several with negative and fractional coefficients. Check before moving on: ask "−2/3 x + 5 = −7?" Look for: −2/3 x = −12, x = 18.

- **Q:** −2/3 x + 5 = −7?
  **A:** −2/3 x = −12, x = 18.
- **Q:** −4x − 3 = 9?
  **A:** x = −3.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 8 word problems leading to two-step equations and check reasonableness.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 8 word problems: define a variable, write, solve, check. Check before moving on: ask "Gym: $25 sign-up + $15/month, total $130. Months?" Look for: 25 + 15m = 130, m = 7.

- **Q:** Gym: $25 sign-up + $15/month, total $130. Months?
  **A:** 25 + 15m = 130, m = 7.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Arithmetic vs algebra
*Student:* Solve a problem by working backward arithmetically and by equation, then write which made more sense and why.

*How to teach:* Solve one problem by working backward and by equation. Your child writes which made more sense. Check before moving on: ask "A number is doubled, then 9 is subtracted to get 21. Number?" Look for: Backward: 21 + 9 = 30, ÷ 2 = 15. Equation: 2n − 9 = 21, n = 15.

- **Q:** A number is doubled, then 9 is subtracted to get 21. Number?
  **A:** Backward: 21 + 9 = 30, ÷ 2 = 15. Equation: 2n − 9 = 21, n = 15.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on two-step equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "3(x − 4) = −6?" Look for: x = 2.

- **Q:** 3(x − 4) = −6?
  **A:** x = 2.

**Assessment:** Two-step equation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 3x + 7 = 22 → 5. 2) −5x + 2 = 17 → −3. 3) 1/2 x − 4 = 6 → 20. 4) 4(x + 2.5) = 18 → 2. 5) Phone $35 + $0.10 per text = $41 → 60 texts. 6) −(x − 3) = 8 → −5.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Resource use and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Chocolate chip cookies (two types), toothpicks, graph paper; Central Valley subsidence data (USGS).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking extracting resources is free once found.
- Thinking subsidence reverses when pumping stops. Compacted clay often doesn't rebound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cookie mining
*Student:* Mine chocolate chips from a cookie with toothpicks, track 'costs' and 'profits', and discuss environmental damage.

*How to teach:* Your child 'buys' land (cookie), mines chips with toothpicks, tracks costs and profits. Check before moving on: ask "What does the crumbled cookie represent?" Look for: Environmental damage/land disturbance from mining.

- **Q:** What does the crumbled cookie represent?
  **A:** Environmental damage/land disturbance from mining.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Central Valley groundwater
*Student:* Analyze data on Central Valley land subsidence from groundwater pumping and graph the change.

*How to teach:* Your child graphs subsidence data. Check before moving on: ask "Why does land sink?" Look for: Pumping groundwater removes water from clay layers, which compact.

- **Q:** Why does land sink?
  **A:** Pumping groundwater removes water from clay layers, which compact.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Resource essay
*Student:* Write a short explanation of why one California resource is found where it is.

*How to teach:* Your child writes why one California resource is found where it is. Check before moving on: ask "Example?" Look for: Gold in the Sierra foothills from quartz veins.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Gold in the Sierra foothills from quartz veins.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review rocks, minerals, rock cycle and resources with a practice set.

*How to teach:* Review practice set. Check before moving on: ask "Igneous vs sedimentary formation?" Look for: Cooling magma vs compacted sediments.

- **Q:** Igneous vs sedimentary formation?
  **A:** Cooling magma vs compacted sediments.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Give unit 5 test. Check before moving on: ask "What fizzes with vinegar?" Look for: Limestone/calcite.

- **Q:** What fizzes with vinegar?
  **A:** Limestone/calcite.

**Assessment:** Cookie-mining analysis and unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Cookie analysis: profit calculation and environmental cost discussion. Unit 5 test: minerals, rock cycle, weathering/erosion/deposition, resources, aquifers.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Origins of the Renaissance

**Prepare:** Map of Italian city-states; Petrarch letter and Pico excerpt.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Renaissance rejected religion. Most humanists were Christian.
- Thinking the Renaissance started everywhere at once. It began in Italy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why Italy?
*Student:* Map Italian city-states and explain how trade, wealth and Roman ruins sparked the Renaissance.

*How to teach:* Your child maps city-states and explains why Italy. Check before moving on: ask "Why Italy?" Look for: Trade wealth, Roman ruins, contact with Byzantine and Muslim scholars.

- **Q:** Why Italy?
  **A:** Trade wealth, Roman ruins, contact with Byzantine and Muslim scholars.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Humanism
*Student:* Read Petrarch's letter to Cicero and Pico's Oration excerpt and define humanism.

*How to teach:* Read Petrarch's letter to Cicero and the Pico excerpt aloud. Your child writes a definition of humanism with one quote from each. Check before moving on: ask "Humanism?" Look for: Study of classical texts and focus on human potential.

- **Q:** Humanism?
  **A:** Study of classical texts and focus on human potential.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Medici Florence
*Student:* Research the Medici as bankers and patrons and list works they funded.

*How to teach:* Your child researches the Medici as bankers and patrons and lists three works they funded. Check before moving on: ask "How did the Medici get rich?" Look for: Banking.

- **Q:** How did the Medici get rich?
  **A:** Banking.
- **Q:** Works they supported?
  **A:** Donatello, Botticelli, Michelangelo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Venice and trade
*Student:* Explain how Venice's location and the revived Silk Road enriched the city.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how Venice's location and trade routes made it rich. Check before moving on: ask "Venice's advantage?" Look for: Location on the Adriatic, trade with Byzantium and Muslim lands.

- **Q:** Venice's advantage?
  **A:** Location on the Adriatic, trade with Byzantium and Muslim lands.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Renaissance origins.

*How to teach:* Give the Renaissance origins quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Father of humanism?" Look for: Petrarch.

- **Q:** Father of humanism?
  **A:** Petrarch.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: why Italy, humanism, Petrarch, Medici, Venice, Florence.

## Spanish 1A: Mi familia: family members

**Prepare:** Family photos for a collage. A fictional family tree (find one in a Spanish textbook site or draw one). Vocabulary: el padre, la madre, los padres, el hermano, la hermana, el abuelo, la abuela, el tío, la tía, el primo, la prima, el hijo, la hija, el padrastro, la madrastra, el hermanastro, la hermanastra, el esposo, la esposa, la mascota.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'los padres' means only fathers. It means parents (masculine plural covers mixed groups).
- Using 'es' + possessive with family incorrectly. 'Es la madre de Juan' (She is Juan's mother) — Spanish uses 'de' for possession.

### Day 1 (Monday): Family words
*Student:* Learn 20 family words and label a photo collage of your family.

*How to teach:* Learn 20 family words; label a photo collage.

- **Q:** How do you say 'my grandparents'?
  **A:** mis abuelos.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Relationships
*Student:* Read a family tree of a fictional Spanish-speaking family and answer 10 ¿Quién es...? questions.

*How to teach:* Read the fictional family tree; answer 10 ¿Quién es…? questions.

- **Q:** If Ana is María's mother's sister, who is Ana to María?
  **A:** Ana es la tía de María.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Describing family
*Student:* Write six sentences describing family members' personalities and appearance with ser.

*How to teach:* Write six sentences describing family members.

- **Q:** Write: 'My brother is tall and funny.'
  **A:** Mi hermano es alto y gracioso.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mascotas
*Student:* Add pets and describe them; share a short video tour of family photos in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Add pets; make a short video tour.

- **Q:** Write: 'My dog is small and lazy.'
  **A:** Mi perro es pequeño y perezoso.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a family vocabulary quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the family vocabulary quiz.

- **Q:** What is 'el primo'?
  **A:** The (male) cousin.

**Assessment:** Family vocabulary quiz.

**Answer key:** Family vocabulary quiz key samples: father → el padre; aunt → la tía; cousins (mixed) → los primos; grandparents → los abuelos; stepfather → el padrastro; half/step-sister → la medio hermana/la hermanastra; pet → la mascota. Family tree questions: answers use '___ es el/la ___ de ___.' 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Voice and movement for actors

**Prepare:** Monologue excerpts from A Midsummer Night's Dream (e.g., Puck's 'If we shadows have offended,' Helena's 'How happy some o'er other some can be,' or Bottom waking from his dream). Read and choose one with your child.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking projection means shouting. Projection uses breath support from the diaphragm to carry the voice without strain.
- Confusing stage left with audience left. Stage directions are from the actor's view facing the audience.

### Day 1 (Monday): Projection
*Student:* Practice breath support and projection reading lines across a room.

*How to teach:* Practice belly breathing and projecting lines across a room.

- **Q:** What is projection?
  **A:** Making your voice carry to the back of the audience using breath support.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Inflection
*Student:* Say one line ten ways to express ten different emotions.

*How to teach:* Say 'I can't believe you did that' ten ways (angry, proud, sarcastic…).

- **Q:** What is inflection?
  **A:** The rise and fall of pitch in the voice that changes meaning or emotion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Physical character
*Student:* Walk as five different characters, changing posture, tempo and center.

*How to teach:* Walk as five characters (king, old man, nervous kid, villain, athlete), changing posture, tempo and center (head, chest, hips).

- **Q:** How can posture show character?
  **A:** A king stands tall and slow; a nervous person hunches and moves quickly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Stage directions
*Student:* Learn stage areas (upstage, downstage left, etc.) and follow blocking commands.

*How to teach:* Teach stage areas; call out blocking commands ('Cross downstage left').

- **Q:** If you move toward the audience, which direction are you moving?
  **A:** Downstage.
- **Q:** What is blocking?
  **A:** The planned movements and positions of actors on stage.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a 1-minute monologue from A Midsummer Night's Dream with clear diction.

*How to teach:* Perform a 1-minute monologue from A Midsummer Night's Dream.

- **Q:** In 'If we shadows have offended,' who is speaking and to whom?
  **A:** Puck, speaking to the audience at the end of the play, suggesting it was all a dream.

**Assessment:** Monologue performance.

**Answer key:** Monologue rubric (1–4): memorization, projection, diction, vocal variety (inflection, pace, pauses), physical choices and focus. Stage directions: upstage = away from audience, downstage = toward audience, stage right/left = actor's right/left facing the audience; 9 areas (UR, UC, UL, R, C, L, DR, DC, DL). Proficient: clear and audible throughout with meaning conveyed.

## Physical Education 7: Team sports review

**Prepare:** Mixed equipment for basketball and softball/kickball. Prepare the unit skills test and a leadership checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Leading by only telling others what to do. Good captains demonstrate, include everyone and give specific encouragement.
- Running a drill without explaining its purpose. Players engage more when they know why a drill matters.

### Day 1 (Monday): Coach a drill
*Student:* Plan and lead a drill for basketball or softball.

*How to teach:* Your child plans and leads a 10-minute drill with a purpose, demo, cues and feedback.

- **Q:** What are the parts of a well-run drill?
  **A:** Explain the purpose, demonstrate, give 2–3 cues, practice, give feedback.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Game day 1
*Student:* Play a basketball game.

*How to teach:* Play a basketball game.

- **Q:** What should a team do on defense after a missed shot?
  **A:** Box out and rebound, then transition quickly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Game day 2
*Student:* Play a softball/kickball game.

*How to teach:* Play softball or kickball.

- **Q:** In kickball, how do you get a runner out?
  **A:** Catch a kicked ball in the air, tag/hit them below the shoulders with the ball, or touch the base on a force.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Skills test
*Student:* Complete the unit skills test.

*How to teach:* Run the unit skills test.

- **Q:** Name one cue for each: chest pass and overhand throw.
  **A:** Chest pass: step and push, thumbs down; overhand throw: step with opposite foot and follow through.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about leadership and sportsmanship during the unit.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the leadership/sportsmanship reflection.

- **Q:** Give one example of good sportsmanship from this unit.
  **A:** Open answer — e.g., helping up an opponent, accepting a call.

**Assessment:** Unit skills test and leadership reflection.

**Answer key:** Unit skills test (1–4 each): dribble course (control, both hands), shooting (10/25 proficient), passing (chest/bounce accurate), throwing (7/10), fielding (clean grounders/fly balls), batting contact (6/10). Leadership reflection: proficient gives specific examples of leading, including others and handling a conflict or loss respectfully.

## Health Education 7: Drug-free advocacy and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Phone or camera with a basic editing app. Plan a filming spot. Write the unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Packing a PSA with scary exaggerations. Accurate facts and positive messages work better and keep trust.
- Thinking 'everyone uses' — survey data show most teens do not; PSAs can use this positive norm.

### Day 1 (Monday): PSA planning
*Student:* Plan a 60-second PSA promoting a drug-free lifestyle.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a script and storyboard: hook, 2 facts, call to action.

- **Q:** What makes a good PSA hook?
  **A:** A surprising fact, question or image in the first 5 seconds that grabs the audience.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): PSA production
*Student:* Record and edit the PSA.

*How to teach:* Record and edit the PSA, checking it is under 60 seconds.

- **Q:** Name one fact you included and its source.
  **A:** Example: 'Nicotine can harm the developing brain' (CDC).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Present
*Student:* Present the PSA to family.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the PSA and asks the family for feedback.

- **Q:** What is the call to action in your PSA?
  **A:** A specific action, e.g., 'Choose to stay vape-free' or 'Talk to someone if you need help quitting.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review unit 4 concepts.

*How to teach:* Review the unit with a quiz game.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a stimulant and a depressant?
  **A:** Stimulants speed up body functions; depressants slow them down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the unit 4 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 4 test (see key).

- **Q:** What is naloxone used for?
  **A:** Reversing an opioid overdose.

**Assessment:** PSA and unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** PSA rubric: 4 = 60 seconds or less, clear drug-free message for a teen audience, 2+ accurate facts with source, positive call to action, clear audio/visuals; 3 = message and 1 fact; 2 = unclear or inaccurate; 1 = incomplete. Unit 4 test (suggested): 1) prefrontal cortex 2) dopamine 3) tolerance 4) withdrawal 5) nicotine is addictive 6) legal age 21 for tobacco/alcohol/cannabis 7) alcohol poisoning signs 8) four drug categories with examples 9) fentanyl danger / one pill can kill 10) naloxone reverses opioid overdose 11) refusal steps 12) marketing technique.
