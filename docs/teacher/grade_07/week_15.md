# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 15

## English Language Arts 7: Argument revision and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Highlighters (3 colors), revision checklist, a timer for the speech; an unseen argument text with questions (CommonLit) for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Revising = fixing spelling. Revision is improving ideas and organization; editing is fixing errors.
- Reading the speech from paper without eye contact.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revision
*Student:* Revise for logical order and transitions using a color-coding check (claim, evidence, reasoning).

*How to teach:* Your child color-codes the essay: claim (yellow), evidence (green), reasoning (blue). A paragraph with little blue needs more explanation; revise those first. Check that paragraphs are in a logical order with transitions.

- **Q:** What does a paragraph with no reasoning color show?
  **A:** Evidence is not explained — add commentary.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Editing & citations
*Student:* Edit grammar and add in-text citations, then publish the final essay.

*How to teach:* Your child edits for grammar and adds or fixes in-text citations, then creates a Works Cited page. Check two citations together for format before publishing. Check before moving on: ask "What goes on a Works Cited page?" Look for: Full source information for every source cited.

- **Q:** What goes on a Works Cited page?
  **A:** Full source information for every source cited.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Oral argument
*Student:* Deliver a 2-minute speech version of the argument with eye contact and adequate volume.

*How to teach:* Your child turns the essay into a 2-minute speech using note cards, not the full essay. Practice once with you timing; give feedback on eye contact and volume, then perform. Check before moving on: ask "Two delivery skills?" Look for: Eye contact and adequate volume.

- **Q:** Two delivery skills?
  **A:** Eye contact and adequate volume.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on which revision made the biggest difference and why.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-paragraph reflection naming the single revision that improved the essay most and explaining why. Ask for a before-and-after example. Check before moving on: ask "Good reflection includes?" Look for: A specific revision, why it mattered, what to do next time.

- **Q:** Good reflection includes?
  **A:** A specific revision, why it mattered, what to do next time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Take a unit test on an unseen argument text (claims, evidence, author's purpose, structure).

*How to teach:* Give an unseen argument text with the unit 3 questions, 45 minutes, no help. Score with the key; review evidence-evaluation answers together. Check before moving on: ask "Author's purpose in an op-ed?" Look for: To persuade.

- **Q:** Author's purpose in an op-ed?
  **A:** To persuade.

**Assessment:** Argumentative essay (rubric), speech and unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Argument essay rubric (4–1): Claim (clear, arguable), Evidence (relevant, sufficient, cited), Reasoning (explains), Counterclaim (addressed and rebutted), Organization (transitions), Conventions. Speech: eye contact, volume, pacing, within 2 minutes. Unit test: claim, evidence, purpose, structure correctly identified with quotes.

## Mathematics 7: Interest and percent error

**Prepare:** Kitchen scale, 5 household objects (an apple, a book, a can, a shoe, a phone), calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Using percent instead of decimal in I = Prt (using 3 instead of 0.03).
- Dividing error by the estimate instead of the actual value. Percent error = |estimate − actual| ÷ actual × 100.

### Day 1 (Monday): Simple interest
*Student:* Compute simple interest on savings and loans using I = Prt for different rates and years.

*How to teach:* Model I = Prt. Your child computes interest on savings and loans. Check before moving on: ask "$500 at 3% simple interest for 4 years?" Look for: I = 500 × 0.03 × 4 = $60.

- **Q:** $500 at 3% simple interest for 4 years?
  **A:** I = 500 × 0.03 × 4 = $60.
- **Q:** $2,000 loan at 6% for 2 years — total repaid?
  **A:** $240 interest, $2,240 total.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparing accounts
*Student:* Compare two savings accounts and one loan in a table and decide which is best for a goal.

*How to teach:* Your child builds a table for two savings accounts and a loan over several years and chooses the best for a goal. Check before moving on: ask "Account A: 2% for 5 yr; B: 3% for 3 yr, on $1,000. Which earns more?" Look for: A: $100; B: $90 — A earns more.

- **Q:** Account A: 2% for 5 yr; B: 3% for 3 yr, on $1,000. Which earns more?
  **A:** A: $100; B: $90 — A earns more.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Percent error lab
*Student:* Estimate the masses of 5 objects, weigh them on a kitchen scale, and compute percent error for each estimate.

*How to teach:* Your child estimates each object's mass, weighs it and computes percent error. Check before moving on: ask "Estimate 300 g, actual 250 g. Percent error?" Look for: 50 ÷ 250 = 20%.

- **Q:** Estimate 300 g, actual 250 g. Percent error?
  **A:** 50 ÷ 250 = 20%.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error and tolerance
*Student:* Solve problems about manufacturing tolerance, e.g., a 500 g bag allowed 2% error.

*How to teach:* Model tolerance with a 500 g bag, 2% allowed. Your child solves similar problems. Check before moving on: ask "Acceptable range for 500 g ± 2%?" Look for: 490 g to 510 g.

- **Q:** Acceptable range for 500 g ± 2%?
  **A:** 490 g to 510 g.
- **Q:** Is a 485 g bag acceptable?
  **A:** No, it is 3% under.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on simple interest and percent error.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Actual 40, measured 42. Percent error?" Look for: 5%.

- **Q:** Actual 40, measured 42. Percent error?
  **A:** 5%.

**Assessment:** Percent-error lab sheet and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) $800 at 5% for 3 yr: $120. 2) $1,200 at 4% for 2 yr total: $1,296. 3) Estimate 9, actual 10: 10% error. 4) Estimate 66, actual 60: 10% error. 5) 2 L ± 1%: 1.98–2.02 L. Lab sheet: all five errors computed with actual as divisor = proficient.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Resources and populations

**Prepare:** Dried beans (food, water, shelter piles), graph paper; lynx–hare data (Hudson's Bay Company pelt records, widely available online); radish seeds, 3 cups of soil, ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a population keeps growing if there's food. Other limiting factors (water, space, disease, predators) cap it at carrying capacity.
- Thinking predators wipe out their prey. In cycles, predator numbers fall after prey decline, letting prey recover.

### Day 1 (Monday): Oh Deer! simulation
*Student:* Play a tabletop version of 'Oh Deer!' with beans for food, water and shelter over 15 rounds and graph the deer population.

*How to teach:* Explain the 'Oh Deer!' rules: each 'deer' (bean) needs a resource each round; deer that find one reproduce, those that don't die. Play 15 rounds, varying resources, and your child graphs the population. Check before moving on: ask "Describe the graph's pattern." Look for: The population rises when resources are plentiful and falls when they run short, oscillating over time.

- **Q:** Describe the graph's pattern.
  **A:** The population rises when resources are plentiful and falls when they run short, oscillating over time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Population graphs
*Student:* Interpret real data on the lynx-hare population cycle and describe the pattern.

*How to teach:* Your child graphs or reads the lynx–hare data and describes the pattern. Check before moving on: ask "What pattern do you see?" Look for: Both cycle about every 10 years; lynx peaks come shortly after hare peaks.

- **Q:** What pattern do you see?
  **A:** Both cycle about every 10 years; lynx peaks come shortly after hare peaks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crowding experiment
*Student:* Plant radish seeds at 3 different densities to investigate competition for resources.

*How to teach:* Your child plants radishes at 3, 10 and 30 seeds per cup, same soil, water and light, and predicts results (check over the next weeks). Check before moving on: ask "Prediction for the 30-seed cup?" Look for: Smaller, thinner plants because they compete for light, water and nutrients.

- **Q:** Prediction for the 30-seed cup?
  **A:** Smaller, thinner plants because they compete for light, water and nutrients.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Carrying capacity
*Student:* Analyze a graph of a population reaching carrying capacity and identify limiting factors.

*How to teach:* Show an S-shaped (logistic) growth graph. Your child marks where growth is fastest and where carrying capacity is reached, and lists limiting factors. Check before moving on: ask "What is carrying capacity?" Look for: The largest population an environment can support over time with its resources.

- **Q:** What is carrying capacity?
  **A:** The largest population an environment can support over time with its resources.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on limiting factors and population graphs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Name two density-dependent limiting factors." Look for: Competition for food; disease; predation.

- **Q:** Name two density-dependent limiting factors.
  **A:** Competition for food; disease; predation.

**Assessment:** Population graph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Limiting factor = resource or condition that limits population growth (food, water, shelter, space). 2) Carrying capacity definition. 3) S-curve flattens at carrying capacity. 4) Lynx peaks lag hare peaks. 5) Crowded plants are smaller due to competition. Population graph: rounds on x-axis, deer on y-axis, labeled.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Shoguns, samurai and the warrior code

**Prepare:** Excerpts on Bushido; comparison chart (Japanese vs European feudalism); research materials on Noh, haiku or tea ceremony.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the emperor held real power under the shogun. The emperor was mostly a figurehead.
- Thinking Bushido was written down from the start. Many ideas were codified later (Edo period).

### Day 1 (Monday): Rise of the shogun
*Student:* Explain how the Minamoto clan created the Kamakura shogunate in 1192.

*How to teach:* Explain the Gempei War and Minamoto Yoritomo. Your child summarizes. Check before moving on: ask "Who became the first shogun of the Kamakura shogunate?" Look for: Minamoto Yoritomo, 1192.

- **Q:** Who became the first shogun of the Kamakura shogunate?
  **A:** Minamoto Yoritomo, 1192.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Social pyramid
*Student:* Draw Japan's feudal pyramid and describe each class's duties.

*How to teach:* Your child draws the feudal pyramid. Check before moving on: ask "Order?" Look for: Emperor (figurehead), shogun, daimyo, samurai, peasants, artisans, merchants.

- **Q:** Order?
  **A:** Emperor (figurehead), shogun, daimyo, samurai, peasants, artisans, merchants.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bushido
*Student:* Read excerpts on the samurai code and explain its values of loyalty and honor.

*How to teach:* Read excerpts. Your child explains values. Check before moving on: ask "Bushido values?" Look for: Loyalty to one's lord, honor, courage, self-discipline.

- **Q:** Bushido values?
  **A:** Loyalty to one's lord, honor, courage, self-discipline.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cultural legacy
*Student:* Research Noh theater, haiku or tea ceremony and present one to the family.

*How to teach:* Your child researches and presents a cultural legacy. Check before moving on: ask "Haiku pattern?" Look for: Three lines of 5-7-5 syllables, often about nature.

- **Q:** Haiku pattern?
  **A:** Three lines of 5-7-5 syllables, often about nature.
- **Q:** Famous haiku poet?
  **A:** Matsuo Basho.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 5 test. Check before moving on: ask "How was Japanese feudalism like European?" Look for: Land and protection exchanged for loyalty and military service.

- **Q:** How was Japanese feudalism like European?
  **A:** Land and protection exchanged for loyalty and military service.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test: geography, Shotoku, Heian culture, Buddhism types, Kamakura shogunate 1192, feudal pyramid, Bushido, cultural legacies, comparison with Europe.

## Spanish 1A: En la clase: classroom objects, gender, plurals, and hay

**Prepare:** Sticky notes, 20 school supplies and furniture items. List of classroom commands: Saquen una hoja de papel, Repitan, Escuchen, Siéntense, Levántense, Abran el libro, Cierren el libro, Escriban, ¿Cómo se dice…?, ¿Qué quiere decir…?, Levanten la mano, Miren la pizarra.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the z→c change in plurals: lápiz → lápices.
- Using 'hay' with an article for specific things. Hay means there is/are with indefinite things: Hay un lápiz; Hay dos libros.

### Day 1 (Monday): Objects
*Student:* Label 20 school supplies and furniture at home with sticky notes in Spanish with el/la.

*How to teach:* Label 20 objects with el/la.

- **Q:** Give the article: mochila, cuaderno, computadora, bolígrafo.
  **A:** la mochila, el cuaderno, la computadora, el bolígrafo.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plurals
*Student:* Learn plural rules (-s, -es, lápiz→lápices) and write the plural of 20 nouns.

*How to teach:* Teach plural rules: vowel + s; consonant + es; z → ces.

- **Q:** Make plural: el lápiz, el papel, la regla.
  **A:** los lápices, los papeles, las reglas.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hay
*Student:* Describe your backpack and desk using hay and numbers: En mi mochila hay dos cuadernos y un lápiz.

*How to teach:* Describe backpack and desk using hay + numbers.

- **Q:** Write: 'In my backpack there are two notebooks and a pencil.'
  **A:** En mi mochila hay dos cuadernos y un lápiz.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Classroom commands
*Student:* Learn and respond physically to 12 classroom expressions (Saquen una hoja, ¿Cómo se dice...?, Repitan) in a TPR game.

*How to teach:* TPR game: you give commands; child acts them out.

- **Q:** What does 'Saquen una hoja de papel' mean?
  **A:** Take out a sheet of paper.
- **Q:** How do you ask 'How do you say 'eraser'?'
  **A:** ¿Cómo se dice 'eraser'?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a vocabulary, plural, and hay quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Cuántos bolígrafos hay? (4)
  **A:** Hay cuatro bolígrafos.

**Assessment:** Classroom vocabulary quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key samples: el lápiz → los lápices; el cuaderno → los cuadernos; la carpeta → las carpetas; el papel → los papeles; el reloj → los relojes; la computadora → las computadoras. Hay sentences: 'There are three folders in my backpack' → En mi mochila hay tres carpetas. ¿Cuántos libros hay? → Hay cinco libros. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Instrument skills (ukulele or keyboard)

**Prepare:** Ukulele (tuned G-C-E-A) or keyboard, chord charts for C, Am, F, G, metronome, practice log, two songs chosen with your child (one major, one minor/different tempo).

**Watch for:**
- Stopping the strum to change chords. Keep the strumming hand moving; lift fingers a little early.
- Practicing only the parts you already know. Isolate the hard chord change and loop it slowly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strumming patterns
*Student:* Learn down-down-up-up-down-up strumming and play it on C and Am.

*How to teach:* Teach D-D-U-U-D-U strum on C and Am (count 1, 2-&, -&, 4-&... or 'down, down-up, up-down-up').

- **Q:** Write the strum pattern.
  **A:** Down, down, up, up, down, up (D-D-U-U-D-U).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chord changes
*Student:* Practice C-Am-F-G changes with a metronome at 60 bpm.

*How to teach:* Practice C-Am-F-G changes at 60 bpm, one chord per measure.

- **Q:** Name the chords in this progression as Roman numerals in C major.
  **A:** C = I, Am = vi, F = IV, G = V.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Song 1
*Student:* Learn the verse of a chosen song with the four chords.

*How to teach:* Learn the verse of the first song.

- **Q:** What is a 'pivot finger'?
  **A:** A finger that stays down or moves least between chords to make changes easier.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Song 2
*Student:* Learn a contrasting song (minor key or different tempo).

*How to teach:* Learn a contrasting song.

- **Q:** How does the contrasting song differ?
  **A:** Open answer — e.g., minor key sounds darker, slower tempo.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Record yourself playing and set practice goals with criteria.

*How to teach:* Record, listen and set practice goals.

- **Q:** Write a specific practice goal.
  **A:** Example: 'Switch F to G cleanly 10 times in a row at 70 bpm by Friday.'

**Assessment:** Recording and practice log.

**Answer key:** Recording and log rubric (1–4): steady strum pattern, clean chords (no buzzing), chord changes on time at a set tempo, practice log with daily minutes and specific goals (e.g., 'C to F at 70 bpm'). Proficient: plays both song sections with a steady beat and ≤3 stumbles.

## Physical Education 7: Net games review

**Prepare:** All net-game equipment. Prepare the net-games skills test: volleyball pass count, badminton rally count, pickleball serves in.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking net games only reward power. Placement (hitting to open space) usually wins more points.
- Staying where you last hit. Recover to base position after each shot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strategy chart
*Student:* Compare strategies in volleyball, badminton and pickleball.

*How to teach:* Chart strategies shared by volleyball, badminton and pickleball (placement, base position, varying speed/depth, attacking weak side).

- **Q:** What strategy do all three net games share?
  **A:** Hit to open space away from opponents and return to a ready base position.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Favorite game
*Student:* Play your favorite net game for the full session.

*How to teach:* Play your child's favorite net game for the session.

- **Q:** What does 'placement' mean?
  **A:** Aiming shots to specific spots where the opponent can't easily reach.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Skills test
*Student:* Complete the net games skills test.

*How to teach:* Run the skills test.

- **Q:** What is the ready position in net games?
  **A:** Feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on the balls of the feet, racket/arms up and in front.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teach a skill
*Student:* Teach one net game skill to a sibling or parent.

*How to teach:* Your child teaches one skill to a sibling or parent using 2–3 cues.

- **Q:** What makes good teaching cues?
  **A:** Short, specific and in order (e.g., 'Turn, contact in front, follow through').

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about biomechanics (follow-through, base of support) in your best skill.

*How to teach:* Your child writes about base of support and follow-through.

- **Q:** Why does a wide base of support help?
  **A:** A wider base lowers and centers your balance, making you more stable when hitting and moving.

**Assessment:** Net games skills test.

**Answer key:** Net games skills test (1–4 each): volleyball forearm pass (15 consecutive = proficient), serve (7/10 in), badminton rally (15 = proficient), pickleball serve (7/10 in) and dink rally (10). Biomechanics reflection: proficient explains how a wide, balanced base of support improves stability and how follow-through toward the target improves accuracy and reduces injury, using their best skill as an example.

## Health Education 7: Violence prevention

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards (a heated argument at a game, a friend talking about bringing a knife to school, an older teen inviting your child to 'join the crew'). Parent preview: discuss serious topics calmly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking telling an adult about a weapon is 'snitching.' Reporting prevents someone from getting hurt; safety outweighs loyalty.
- Believing fighting back is the best way to earn respect. De-escalating or walking away is the strongest and safest choice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Risk factors
*Student:* Identify risk and protective factors for violence.

*How to teach:* Explain risk factors increase the chance of violence and protective factors decrease it. Your child sorts examples.

- **Q:** Give two protective factors against violence.
  **A:** Caring family, positive friends, involvement in sports/clubs, connection to school, trusted mentors.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): De-escalation
*Student:* Practice de-escalation phrases in role-plays.

*How to teach:* Model calming phrases: 'I don't want to fight,' 'Let's take a break,' calm voice, open hands, leave. Role-play two scenarios.

- **Q:** Name three de-escalation techniques.
  **A:** Stay calm and lower your voice, give the person space, listen, avoid insults, walk away and get help.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weapons and gangs
*Student:* Discuss why to tell an adult about weapons and how gangs recruit.

*How to teach:* Discuss why reporting weapons saves lives, and how gangs recruit (belonging, money, protection) and the real costs (violence, prison, family harm).

- **Q:** A classmate shows you a weapon in his backpack. What do you do?
  **A:** Get away from the situation and tell a trusted adult or school official immediately.
- **Q:** Why might a gang appeal to a teen?
  **A:** They promise belonging, protection, respect or money — needs that can be met safely through family, teams and mentors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decision making
*Student:* Apply a decision-making model to two risky scenarios.

*How to teach:* Your child applies DECIDE to two risky scenarios and writes a choice for each.

- **Q:** Why is 'Consider consequences' important in risky situations?
  **A:** It makes you think about short- and long-term results for you and others before acting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on violence prevention.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key.

- **Q:** What is the safest response if someone tries to start a fight with you?
  **A:** Stay calm, don't respond to insults, walk away and tell an adult.

**Assessment:** Quiz and scenario responses.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) risk factors: exposure to violence, substance use, gang contact, access to weapons 2) protective factors: supportive family, school connection, positive activities, good friends 3) de-escalation: stay calm, lower voice, give space, listen, walk away 4) if you know about a weapon: tell a trusted adult immediately/use anonymous tip line 5) gangs recruit by offering belonging, protection, money. Scenario rubric: response uses a decision model, chooses a safe option, involves an adult.
