# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 19

## English Language Arts 7: Shakespeare's world and Act 1 of A Midsummer Night's Dream

**Prepare:** Folger edition of A Midsummer Night's Dream (free at folger.edu); short Globe Theatre video; character map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'wherefore' means 'where' (it means 'why'). For MND, note 'anon' = soon.
- Mixing up Hermia and Helena. Hermia loves Lysander; Helena loves Demetrius.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shakespeare's world
*Student:* Watch a short video on the Globe Theatre and Elizabethan England and take notes on how plays were staged.

*How to teach:* Watch a short video on the Globe Theatre. Your child takes notes on staging (open-air, thrust stage, groundlings, all-male casts, few props). Ask how staging would affect the way the audience imagines the forest.

- **Q:** Name two features of the Globe.
  **A:** Open-air roof, thrust stage, groundlings standing in the yard, few sets, male actors played female roles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Act 1 Scene 1
*Student:* Read Act 1.1 aloud in parts and build a character map of the four lovers, Egeus and Theseus.

*How to teach:* Read Act 1.1 aloud in parts, pausing for Folger notes. Your child builds a character map of Theseus, Egeus, Hermia, Lysander, Demetrius and Helena with arrows for who loves whom. Check before moving on: ask "What does Egeus demand?" Look for: Hermia must marry Demetrius or face death or life as a nun, under Athenian law.

- **Q:** What does Egeus demand?
  **A:** Hermia must marry Demetrius or face death or life as a nun, under Athenian law.
- **Q:** What do Hermia and Lysander plan?
  **A:** To run away through the woods to his aunt's house.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shakespeare's language
*Student:* Translate 10 lines into modern English using Folger notes and a dictionary for archaic words.

*How to teach:* Model translating one line with the Folger notes. Your child translates 10 lines into modern English and explains any archaic words. Compare two translations for accuracy.

- **Q:** Translate 'The course of true love never did run smooth.'
  **A:** Real love never goes easily.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Act 1 Scene 2
*Student:* Read Act 1.2 with the mechanicals and describe how Bottom's personality creates humor.

*How to teach:* Read Act 1.2 with the mechanicals. Your child describes Bottom's personality with three quotes and explains how it creates humor. Check before moving on: ask "Why is Bottom funny?" Look for: He wants to play every role, including the lion and Thisbe, and is overconfident.

- **Q:** Why is Bottom funny?
  **A:** He wants to play every role, including the lion and Thisbe, and is overconfident.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an Act 1 quiz and summarize the three plotlines introduced.

*How to teach:* Give the Act 1 quiz without notes. Your child then writes a short summary of the three plotlines introduced so far. Check before moving on: ask "Three plotlines?" Look for: Lovers; Theseus and Hippolyta's wedding; the mechanicals' play (fairies come in Act 2).

- **Q:** Three plotlines?
  **A:** Lovers; Theseus and Hippolyta's wedding; the mechanicals' play (fairies come in Act 2).

**Assessment:** Character map, translation and Act 1 quiz.

**Answer key:** Act 1 quiz: 1) Theseus is Duke of Athens, marrying Hippolyta. 2) Egeus wants Hermia to marry Demetrius. 3) Hermia loves Lysander. 4) Helena loves Demetrius and tells him of the elopement. 5) The mechanicals plan 'Pyramus and Thisbe' for the wedding. 6) Bottom will play Pyramus. Translation: meaning accurate in modern English.

## Mathematics 7: Multiplying rational numbers

**Prepare:** Number line, optional toy car, multiplication table grid −5 to 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking negative × negative is negative. Use the table pattern: −3 × 2 = −6, −3 × 1 = −3, −3 × 0 = 0, so −3 × −1 = 3.
- Miscounting the sign with several factors. Count negatives: even number → positive, odd → negative.

### Day 1 (Monday): Position and velocity
*Student:* Model multiplication with a toy car moving at positive or negative velocities for positive or negative time.

*How to teach:* Car moves at velocity v (positive = east, negative = west); time t positive = future, negative = past. Your child finds positions for each sign combination. Check before moving on: ask "Velocity −3 m/s, time 4 s from now. Position?" Look for: −12 m (12 m west).

- **Q:** Velocity −3 m/s, time 4 s from now. Position?
  **A:** −12 m (12 m west).
- **Q:** Velocity −3 m/s, 4 s ago (t = −4)?
  **A:** +12 m — it was east of start.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sign patterns
*Student:* Complete a multiplication table from -5 to 5 and describe the patterns that produce the sign rules.

*How to teach:* Your child fills in the −5 to 5 table and describes the sign patterns. Check before moving on: ask "What pattern gives (−)(−) = +?" Look for: In each negative row, products increase by the same amount as you move toward negatives, so they cross zero into positives.

- **Q:** What pattern gives (−)(−) = +?
  **A:** In each negative row, products increase by the same amount as you move toward negatives, so they cross zero into positives.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distributive reasoning
*Student:* Use -3(4 + -4) = 0 to justify why -3 times -4 must equal positive 12.

*How to teach:* Model −3(4 + −4) = −3(0) = 0, so −12 + (−3)(−4) = 0, so (−3)(−4) = 12. Your child writes this justification in their own words. Check before moving on: ask "Why must (−3)(−4) be 12?" Look for: −3 × 4 = −12, and the two products must add to 0, so (−3)(−4) is the opposite of −12: +12.

- **Q:** Why must (−3)(−4) be 12?
  **A:** −3 × 4 = −12, and the two products must add to 0, so (−3)(−4) is the opposite of −12: +12.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fractions and decimals
*Student:* Multiply 15 signed fraction and decimal problems including multiple factors.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 15 products with fractions, decimals and multiple factors. Check before moving on: ask "(−2/3)(9/10)?" Look for: −3/5.

- **Q:** (−2/3)(9/10)?
  **A:** −3/5.
- **Q:** (−1.5)(−4)(−2)?
  **A:** −12.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on multiplying rational numbers.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key) and collect the written justification. Check before moving on: ask "(−5)(−5)?" Look for: 25.

- **Q:** (−5)(−5)?
  **A:** 25.

**Assessment:** Multiplication quiz and written justification of sign rules.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) −6 × 7 = −42. 2) −8 × −0.5 = 4. 3) (−3/4)(8) = −6. 4) (−2)(−3)(−5) = −30. 5) (−1)^4 = 1. 6) (−0.2)(15) = −3. Justification rubric: proficient uses the distributive property or pattern argument correctly.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Ecosystem model project

**Prepare:** 2-liter bottle (adult cuts it), gravel, soil, small plants, water, pill bugs or snails from the yard; decomposition jars from week 18.

**Watch for:**
- Calling soil and water 'non-living so unimportant.' Abiotic factors determine what biotic factors can survive.
- Thinking a terrarium needs no light because it's sealed. Plants need light to supply energy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build a terrarium
*Student:* Build a bottle terrarium with plants, soil, water and small invertebrates and diagram its biotic and abiotic parts.

*How to teach:* Layer gravel, soil, plants and invertebrates; mist and close. Your child diagrams biotic and abiotic parts. Check before moving on: ask "List biotic and abiotic parts." Look for: Biotic: plants, pill bugs, microbes; abiotic: soil minerals, water, air, light, gravel.

- **Q:** List biotic and abiotic parts.
  **A:** Biotic: plants, pill bugs, microbes; abiotic: soil minerals, water, air, light, gravel.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decomposition check
*Student:* Record changes in the decomposition jars and explain which conditions sped up decomposition.

*How to teach:* Your child records changes in each jar and compares moist and dry conditions. Check before moving on: ask "Which decomposed faster and why?" Look for: Moist jars, because decomposers (fungi, bacteria) need water.

- **Q:** Which decomposed faster and why?
  **A:** Moist jars, because decomposers (fungi, bacteria) need water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Matter and energy diagram
*Student:* Draw arrows showing matter cycling and energy flowing through the terrarium.

*How to teach:* Your child draws matter-cycling and energy-flow arrows through the terrarium. Check before moving on: ask "Where does energy enter and leave?" Look for: Enters as light; leaves as heat.

- **Q:** Where does energy enter and leave?
  **A:** Enters as light; leaves as heat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review unit 4 vocabulary and practice data-analysis questions.

*How to teach:* Review vocabulary and practice data-analysis questions together. Check before moving on: ask "Define 'decomposer'." Look for: Organism that breaks down dead matter (fungi, bacteria).

- **Q:** Define 'decomposer'.
  **A:** Organism that breaks down dead matter (fungi, bacteria).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 4 test; continue terrarium observation weekly.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 4 test; continue weekly terrarium observations. Check before moving on: ask "What is a trophic cascade?" Look for: A chain of effects through a food web when one population changes.

- **Q:** What is a trophic cascade?
  **A:** A chain of effects through a food web when one population changes.

**Assessment:** Terrarium diagram and unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Terrarium diagram: labeled biotic/abiotic, matter arrows cycling, energy in as light and out as heat. Unit 4 test: interaction types, limiting factors, food web arrows, 10% rule, decomposers' role.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Crusades and the Reconquista

**Prepare:** Accounts of Urban II's speech at Clermont (Fordham has several versions); Ibn al-Athir excerpt on the 1099 capture of Jerusalem; map of Iberia for the Reconquista. Preview: sources describe massacres.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Crusades were only about religion. Motives included land, wealth, adventure and political power.
- Thinking Muslims, Christians and Jews never lived peacefully together. In al-Andalus (Córdoba), there were long periods of coexistence (convivencia).

### Day 1 (Monday): Pope Urban II
*Student:* Read accounts of Pope Urban II's 1095 speech and identify his reasons for the First Crusade.

*How to teach:* Read two versions of Urban's speech. Your child lists his reasons. Check before moving on: ask "Urban's reasons?" Look for: Help the Byzantine emperor against the Seljuk Turks, retake Jerusalem, promise forgiveness of sins, and redirect knights from fighting each other.

- **Q:** Urban's reasons?
  **A:** Help the Byzantine emperor against the Seljuk Turks, retake Jerusalem, promise forgiveness of sins, and redirect knights from fighting each other.
- **Q:** When and where?
  **A:** 1095, Clermont, France.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiple perspectives
*Student:* Compare a Christian and a Muslim account (e.g., Ibn al-Athir) of the capture of Jerusalem.

*How to teach:* Compare a Christian and Muslim account of 1099. Check before moving on: ask "How do the perspectives differ?" Look for: Christian chroniclers celebrate it as God's victory; Ibn al-Athir describes it as a slaughter of Muslims and a disaster.

- **Q:** How do the perspectives differ?
  **A:** Christian chroniclers celebrate it as God's victory; Ibn al-Athir describes it as a slaughter of Muslims and a disaster.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Effects
*Student:* Chart the effects of the Crusades on Christians, Muslims and Jews and on trade.

*How to teach:* Your child charts effects on each group and trade. Check before moving on: ask "Effect on Jews?" Look for: Crusaders massacred Jewish communities in the Rhineland (1096).

- **Q:** Effect on Jews?
  **A:** Crusaders massacred Jewish communities in the Rhineland (1096).
- **Q:** Effect on trade?
  **A:** Increased European demand for Eastern goods, enriching Italian cities like Venice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Al-Andalus and Reconquista
*Student:* Read about convivencia in Córdoba and map the Reconquista through 1492.

*How to teach:* Read about convivencia; map the Reconquista. Check before moving on: ask "When did the Reconquista end?" Look for: 1492, with the fall of Granada to Ferdinand and Isabella.

- **Q:** When did the Reconquista end?
  **A:** 1492, with the fall of Granada to Ferdinand and Isabella.
- **Q:** What happened to Spain's Jews that year?
  **A:** They were expelled unless they converted.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a perspective paragraph comparing two sources on the Crusades.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a perspective paragraph. Check before moving on: ask "What should it compare?" Look for: Each author's background, purpose and how it shapes the account.

- **Q:** What should it compare?
  **A:** Each author's background, purpose and how it shapes the account.

**Assessment:** Perspective paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate. Proficient names both authors, their perspectives, and quotes or details from each.

*How to teach:* Teach gusta/gustan, encanta/encantan; write 10 sentences.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Islamic geometric pattern art

**Prepare:** Compass, ruler, pencil, colored pencils, images of the Alhambra (Granada, Spain) tilework and mosque tiles (Smarthistory). Watch a short tutorial on constructing an 8-point star.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Islamic art never shows figures anywhere. Religious spaces generally avoid figures, so geometric, vegetal (arabesque) and calligraphic designs dominate, but figurative art appears in some secular manuscripts.
- Freehanding the pattern. Accurate construction uses compass and straightedge.

### Day 1 (Monday): Context
*Student:* View Alhambra and mosque tilework and discuss why geometric patterns are central in Islamic art.

*How to teach:* View Alhambra tilework and mosque tiles; discuss why geometry, arabesque and calligraphy are central.

- **Q:** Why are geometric patterns important in Islamic art?
  **A:** Religious art generally avoids depicting living beings, so geometry, plant forms and calligraphy are used; endless repeating patterns suggest the infinite nature of God and unity.
- **Q:** Where is the Alhambra?
  **A:** Granada, Spain — built by the Nasrid rulers in the 13th–14th centuries.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Construction
*Student:* Construct an 8-point star pattern with compass and ruler.

*How to teach:* Construct an 8-point star: draw a circle, divide it into 8 with diameters, connect points to make two overlapping squares.

- **Q:** How many degrees apart are the 8 division lines?
  **A:** 45° (360 ÷ 8).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Repeat
*Student:* Extend the pattern into a repeating tile grid.

*How to teach:* Repeat the star motif on a grid so tiles connect.

- **Q:** What is a tessellation?
  **A:** A pattern of shapes that fit together with no gaps or overlaps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Color
*Student:* Color the pattern with a planned color scheme.

*How to teach:* Color with a planned scheme, keeping symmetry.

- **Q:** What kind of symmetry does an 8-point star have?
  **A:** Radial (rotational) symmetry — and 8 lines of reflection.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write how understanding the culture changes how you see the pattern.

*How to teach:* Write the reflection.

- **Q:** What is an arabesque?
  **A:** A flowing, interlacing design based on vines, leaves and plant forms.

**Assessment:** Geometric pattern artwork.

**Answer key:** Pattern artwork rubric (1–4): accurate 8-point star construction (circle divided into 8 equal parts, two overlapping squares), repeats into a tiled grid, symmetry and tessellation, planned color scheme applied consistently, neat craftsmanship. Reflection proficient: explains that geometric patterns express ideas of unity, order and infinity in Islamic art and are used in mosques where figurative imagery is avoided.

## Physical Education 7: Flexibility, injury prevention and mid-year plan

**Prepare:** Mat, a 30-minute beginner yoga video (choose one you've previewed), a bag of frozen peas and elastic bandage for RICE demo. Pull SMART goals from week 3.

**Watch for:**
- Bouncing in stretches. Hold static stretches steadily 15–30 seconds; bouncing can strain muscles.
- Stretching to the point of pain. Stretch to mild tension only.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stretching
*Student:* Learn 8 static stretches and when to use static vs. dynamic stretching.

*How to teach:* Teach 8 static stretches (hamstring, quad, calf, hip flexor, chest, triceps, shoulder, butterfly). Discuss static vs dynamic and when to use each.

- **Q:** What is the difference between static and dynamic stretching?
  **A:** Static holds a stretch still (best after exercise); dynamic moves through a range of motion (best as a warm-up).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yoga
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute beginner yoga session.

*How to teach:* Complete a beginner yoga session together; note balance and breathing.

- **Q:** Name one fitness component yoga improves.
  **A:** Flexibility (also balance, core strength and stress reduction).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Injury prevention
*Student:* Learn the RICE method and common sports injuries.

*How to teach:* Teach common injuries (sprain = ligament; strain = muscle/tendon) and RICE.

- **Q:** What does RICE stand for?
  **A:** Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation.
- **Q:** What is the difference between a sprain and a strain?
  **A:** A sprain injures a ligament (bone to bone); a strain injures a muscle or tendon.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan
*Student:* Write a one-week FITT plan covering all fitness components.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-week FITT plan (see rubric).

- **Q:** How many days of muscle-strengthening are recommended for teens?
  **A:** At least 3 days per week (as part of the 60 daily minutes).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Follow day 1 of the plan and review progress toward SMART goals.

*How to teach:* Do day 1 of the plan and review SMART goal progress.

- **Q:** Are you on track for your SMART goal? What will you adjust?
  **A:** Open answer — compare current score to target and name a specific change.

**Assessment:** One-week fitness plan.

**Answer key:** One-week plan rubric: 4 = includes all components (cardio 3–5 days, strength 2–3 days, flexibility daily or most days), FITT details for each day, warm-up and cool-down, a rest/active recovery day, and links to SMART goals; 3 = all components but missing some FITT details; 2 = missing a component; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 7: Tobacco and vaping

**Prepare:** Print CDC e-cigarette fact sheets ('Quick Facts on the Risks of E-cigarettes'). Find 2–3 vape ad images (from news articles or Truth Initiative). Find California Healthy Kids Survey or CA Youth Tobacco Survey data online.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking vapes are just water vapor. The aerosol contains nicotine, ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals and heavy metals.
- Thinking one vape pod has little nicotine. Some pods contain as much nicotine as a pack of 20 cigarettes or more.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tobacco effects
*Student:* List short- and long-term effects of smoking on the body.

*How to teach:* Your child lists short-term (bad breath, yellow teeth, coughing, reduced stamina, higher heart rate) and long-term effects (lung cancer, heart disease, stroke, COPD/emphysema).

- **Q:** Name two long-term effects of smoking.
  **A:** Lung and other cancers, heart disease, stroke, COPD/emphysema, gum disease.
- **Q:** What is secondhand smoke?
  **A:** Smoke breathed in by nonsmokers from someone else's cigarette; it also causes disease.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Vaping
*Student:* Read CDC facts on e-cigarettes and nicotine content.

*How to teach:* Read CDC facts together and highlight nicotine content and health effects.

- **Q:** Why is nicotine especially harmful to teens?
  **A:** It is highly addictive and can harm the parts of the developing brain that control attention, learning, mood and impulse control.
- **Q:** What's in vape aerosol besides nicotine?
  **A:** Ultrafine particles, flavoring chemicals like diacetyl, volatile organic compounds and heavy metals such as nickel, tin and lead.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marketing
*Student:* Analyze how vape companies target teens with flavors and design.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes vape ads: flavors, bright colors, sleek tech look, influencers.

- **Q:** Why are candy and fruit flavors a marketing technique?
  **A:** They appeal to young people, hide harshness of nicotine and make vaping seem harmless.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teen data
*Student:* Analyze California Healthy Kids Survey data on teen vaping.

*How to teach:* Read survey data and calculate or describe trends (e.g., percent of 7th graders who have ever vaped).

- **Q:** What is the legal age to buy tobacco products, including vapes, in California?
  **A:** 21.
- **Q:** If most 7th graders have never vaped, what does that tell you about 'everyone is doing it'?
  **A:** It's a false norm — most teens do not vape.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a fact sheet on vaping for peers.

*How to teach:* Your child creates the fact sheet and you score it with the rubric.

- **Q:** Name a resource for teens who want to quit vaping.
  **A:** Truth Initiative's 'This is Quitting' (text DITCHVAPE to 88709), Kick It California, a doctor or counselor.

**Assessment:** Vaping fact sheet.

**Answer key:** Vaping fact sheet rubric: 4 = at least 4 accurate facts (most vapes contain nicotine; nicotine is highly addictive and harms the developing brain's attention/learning; aerosol has harmful chemicals and metals; one pod can equal a pack of cigarettes; flavors target youth; age 21 to buy in CA), cites CDC or similar source, peer-friendly design, includes refusal tip or quitting resource (e.g., text 'DITCHVAPE' to 88709, or Kick It California); 3 = 3 facts with source; 2 = 1–2 facts; 1 = inaccurate.
