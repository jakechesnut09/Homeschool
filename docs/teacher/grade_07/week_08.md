# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 8

## English Language Arts 7: The Call of the Wild, chapters 5–6

**Prepare:** The Call of the Wild; Venn diagram template; connotation word sets on cards.

**Watch for:**
- Treating a foil as an enemy. A foil is a character whose contrast highlights another's traits (Hal's cruelty highlights Thornton's kindness).
- Thinking synonyms are interchangeable. 'Thin', 'skinny', 'gaunt' and 'emaciated' differ in feeling and degree.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 5
*Student:* Read Chapter 5 'The Toil of Trace and Trail' and list choices Hal, Charles and Mercedes make that show ignorance.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 5 together. Your child lists at least five choices Hal, Charles and Mercedes make that show ignorance, each with a quote, and predicts what will happen to them. Ask what Buck's refusal to get up at the end shows he senses.

- **Q:** Name three foolish choices.
  **A:** Overloading the sled, overfeeding then starving the dogs, Mercedes riding on the sled, beating exhausted dogs, ignoring warnings about the melting ice.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Foils
*Student:* Compare Hal's treatment of the dogs with John Thornton's in a Venn diagram and explain how they act as foils.

*How to teach:* Define a foil (a character who contrasts with another to highlight traits). Your child fills a Venn diagram comparing how Hal and John Thornton treat the dogs, with at least three differences and one similarity, then writes two sentences explaining how the contrast makes Thornton's kindness stand out. Check before moving on: ask "How are Hal and Thornton foils?" Look for: Hal beats and starves the dogs; Thornton is gentle and saves Buck — the contrast makes Thornton's kindness stand out.

- **Q:** How are Hal and Thornton foils?
  **A:** Hal beats and starves the dogs; Thornton is gentle and saves Buck — the contrast makes Thornton's kindness stand out.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Connotation
*Student:* Rank word sets like 'thin/skinny/gaunt/emaciated' by connotation and find London's choices in chapter 5.

*How to teach:* Model ranking 'thin, skinny, gaunt, emaciated' from mildest to most extreme and discuss the feeling of each. Your child ranks four more word sets, then finds three of London's word choices in Chapter 5 describing the starving dogs and explains the effect. Check before moving on: ask "Rank thin, skinny, gaunt, emaciated from mild to extreme." Look for: Thin, skinny, gaunt, emaciated.

- **Q:** Rank thin, skinny, gaunt, emaciated from mild to extreme.
  **A:** Thin, skinny, gaunt, emaciated.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapter 6
*Student:* Read Chapter 6 'For the Love of a Man' and write about how love changes Buck.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 6. Your child lists three things Buck does out of love for Thornton (the rapids, Black Burton, the thousand-pound sled) and writes a paragraph on how this love differs from his earlier obedience to people. Ask: is this the first time Buck has truly loved a human?

- **Q:** What does Buck do for Thornton?
  **A:** Saves him from the rapids, attacks Black Burton, and wins Thornton's bet by pulling a sled with a thousand pounds of flour.

### Day 5 (Friday): Discussion & check
*Student:* Hold a discussion on loyalty in the novel, then take a chapters 5–6 quiz.

*How to teach:* Hold a 10-minute discussion on loyalty: Buck to Thornton, Dave to his work, and whether Buck owes loyalty to his old life. Each speaker must cite one page. Then give the chapters 5–6 quiz.

- **Q:** What happens to Hal's party?
  **A:** They fall through the river ice and drown with the dogs.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram, quiz and discussion.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Hal, Charles, Mercedes are inexperienced. 2) Thornton warns them about the ice. 3) Buck refuses to rise; Thornton cuts him loose. 4) Buck saves Thornton in rapids. 5) Buck wins the $1,600 bet pulling 1,000 lb. Venn diagram: at least 3 contrasts with evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Graphs of proportional relationships

**Prepare:** Graph paper, Desmos (desmos.com/calculator), six-relationship card set (write descriptions, tables, equations and graphs on index cards).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any straight line is proportional. It must also pass through (0,0).
- Reading the steeper line as slower. On distance vs time, steeper = more distance per unit time = faster.

### Day 1 (Monday): Graphing tables
*Student:* Graph three tables on the coordinate plane and identify which form straight lines through the origin.

*How to teach:* Model plotting a table. Your child graphs three tables and identifies which are straight lines through the origin. Check before moving on: ask "Table (0,0),(1,3),(2,6). Proportional graph?" Look for: Yes — straight line through the origin.

- **Q:** Table (0,0),(1,3),(2,6). Proportional graph?
  **A:** Yes — straight line through the origin.
- **Q:** Table (0,2),(1,4),(2,6)?
  **A:** Straight but does not go through (0,0), so not proportional.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Meaning of points
*Student:* Explain what (0,0), (1,k) and (5,y) mean on a graph of distance versus time for a cyclist.

*How to teach:* Model reading points on a cyclist's graph. Your child explains (0,0), (1,k) and (5,y) in context. Check before moving on: ask "A cyclist's graph passes through (1, 12). Meaning?" Look for: After 1 hour she has gone 12 miles; k = 12 mph.

- **Q:** A cyclist's graph passes through (1, 12). Meaning?
  **A:** After 1 hour she has gone 12 miles; k = 12 mph.
- **Q:** What does (5, 60) mean?
  **A:** After 5 hours, 60 miles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing graphs
*Student:* Graph two runners' distance-time lines on Desmos and decide who is faster using the constant of proportionality.

*How to teach:* Enter y = 6x and y = 8x in Desmos. Your child decides who is faster and explains with k and steepness. Check before moving on: ask "Runner A: d = 6t, Runner B: d = 8t (m/s). Who is faster?" Look for: B — 8 m/s > 6 m/s; B's line is steeper.

- **Q:** Runner A: d = 6t, Runner B: d = 8t (m/s). Who is faster?
  **A:** B — 8 m/s > 6 m/s; B's line is steeper.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Four representations
*Student:* Complete a card match linking descriptions, tables, equations and graphs for six relationships.

*How to teach:* Your child matches the 24 cards into 6 sets and explains one set aloud. Check before moving on: ask "Which matches 'apples cost $2 per pound': y = 2x or y = x + 2?" Look for: y = 2x, graph through (0,0) and (1,2).

- **Q:** Which matches 'apples cost $2 per pound': y = 2x or y = x + 2?
  **A:** y = 2x, graph through (0,0) and (1,2).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz interpreting points and steepness on proportional graphs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "A graph goes through (4, 10). What is k?" Look for: 2.5.

- **Q:** A graph goes through (4, 10). What is k?
  **A:** 2.5.

**Assessment:** Graph-interpretation quiz and card-match accuracy.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Point (1, 7) on a proportional graph: k = 7. 2) (0,0) means 0 hours, 0 miles. 3) Line through (3, 12): y = 4x. 4) Steeper of y = 2x and y = 5x: y = 5x. 5) Line through (0,3): not proportional. Card match: 6/6 = proficient.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Conservation of mass

**Prepare:** Kitchen scale (0.1 g ideal), plastic bottle, balloon, baking soda, vinegar, Alka-Seltzer, graph paper. Short reading on Lavoisier.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking mass is lost when gas escapes. It goes into the air; in a closed system the mass stays the same.
- Thinking gases have no mass.

### Day 1 (Monday): Open system
*Student:* Mass baking soda and vinegar before and after reacting in an open cup and calculate the apparent mass loss.

*How to teach:* Mass cup + vinegar + baking soda (separately on the scale) before, react in open cup, mass after. Your child calculates apparent loss. Check before moving on: ask "Why did mass decrease?" Look for: CO2 gas escaped into the air.

- **Q:** Why did mass decrease?
  **A:** CO2 gas escaped into the air.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Closed system
*Student:* Repeat the reaction in a bottle sealed with a balloon and compare before-and-after masses.

*How to teach:* Put baking soda in balloon, vinegar in bottle, mass everything, tip baking soda in, mass again. Check before moving on: ask "What happened to the mass?" Look for: It stayed the same (within measurement error) because the gas was trapped.

- **Q:** What happened to the mass?
  **A:** It stayed the same (within measurement error) because the gas was trapped.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Explaining results
*Student:* Draw particle models explaining why mass seemed to change in the open cup but not the sealed bottle.

*How to teach:* Your child draws particle models for open and closed systems. Check before moving on: ask "What's the difference?" Look for: In open system, gas particles leave; in closed system, all atoms stay.

- **Q:** What's the difference?
  **A:** In open system, gas particles leave; in closed system, all atoms stay.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lavoisier
*Student:* Read about Antoine Lavoisier's experiments and summarize how he established conservation of mass.

*How to teach:* Read about Lavoisier; your child summarizes. Check before moving on: ask "What did Lavoisier show?" Look for: In closed containers, mass is conserved during reactions — mass of reactants equals mass of products.

- **Q:** What did Lavoisier show?
  **A:** In closed containers, mass is conserved during reactions — mass of reactants equals mass of products.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a full lab report with data, graph and conclusion.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the lab report. Check before moving on: ask "State the law of conservation of mass." Look for: Matter is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

- **Q:** State the law of conservation of mass.
  **A:** Matter is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.

**Assessment:** Conservation of mass lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: question, hypothesis, data table (open and closed masses), graph, conclusion explaining gas escape vs trapped gas using conservation of mass.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Tang and Song China

**Prepare:** Map of China; foam sheet, pencil and paint for stamp; invention chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Silk Road was a single road. It was a network of routes.
- Thinking China invented the printing press like Gutenberg's. China invented woodblock printing (and movable type) earlier.

### Day 1 (Monday): Geography and reunification
*Student:* Map China's rivers, the Grand Canal and Silk Road and explain how the Sui and Tang reunified China.

*How to teach:* Show a map of China. Your child labels the Yellow and Yangtze rivers, the Grand Canal and Silk Road routes, then reads how the Sui (589) reunified China and the Tang expanded it. Ask why a canal linking north and south mattered.

- **Q:** Who reunified China in 589?
  **A:** The Sui dynasty.
- **Q:** Purpose of the Grand Canal?
  **A:** To connect the Yellow and Yangtze rivers for shipping grain north.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Spread of Buddhism
*Student:* Trace Buddhism's spread from India through China to Korea and Japan on a map.

*How to teach:* Your child traces Buddhism's spread. Check before moving on: ask "Route?" Look for: India → Central Asia via Silk Road → China → Korea → Japan.

- **Q:** Route?
  **A:** India → Central Asia via Silk Road → China → Korea → Japan.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inventions
*Student:* Make a chart of paper, printing, compass, gunpowder, tea and porcelain with their global influence.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a chart of six inventions (paper, printing, compass, gunpowder, tea, porcelain) with columns for when, what it did, and how it changed the wider world. Check before moving on: ask "Global impact of the compass?" Look for: Enabled navigation and later the Age of Exploration.

- **Q:** Global impact of the compass?
  **A:** Enabled navigation and later the Age of Exploration.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Woodblock printing
*Student:* Carve a foam stamp to model woodblock printing and explain how the Diamond Sutra (868) was produced.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a simple design, carves it into foam (reversing any letters), inks and prints several copies. Then read about the Diamond Sutra and discuss how woodblocks made copying faster. Check before moving on: ask "What is the Diamond Sutra?" Look for: The oldest dated printed book (868), a Buddhist text printed with woodblocks.

- **Q:** What is the Diamond Sutra?
  **A:** The oldest dated printed book (868), a Buddhist text printed with woodblocks.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Tang and Song China.

*How to teach:* Give the Tang and Song quiz without notes; review the invention chart together. Check before moving on: ask "Dates of the Tang and Song?" Look for: Tang 618–907; Song 960–1279.

- **Q:** Dates of the Tang and Song?
  **A:** Tang 618–907; Song 960–1279.

**Assessment:** Invention chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Sui reunification, Grand Canal, Tang and Song dates, Buddhism spread, inventions (paper, printing, compass, gunpowder, porcelain), Diamond Sutra 868.

## Spanish 1A: Subject pronouns and the verb ser

**Prepare:** Pronoun chart, Conjuguemos account (free), 20 pictures of people (yourself, a friend, groups of boys/girls/mixed).

**Watch for:**
- Using ellas for a mixed group. Any group with at least one male uses ellos/nosotros.
- Always saying the subject pronoun. Spanish often drops it because the verb shows the subject: Soy de California.

### Day 1 (Monday): Subject pronouns
*Student:* Learn the subject pronouns (including vosotros in Spain and ustedes in Latin America) and assign the correct pronoun to 20 pictures of people.

*How to teach:* Teach pronouns; assign pronouns to 20 pictures.

- **Q:** Which pronoun replaces 'Carlos y Elena'?
  **A:** Ellos.
- **Q:** Which pronoun replaces 'Elena y Sofía'?
  **A:** Ellas.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ser
*Student:* Learn soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son; complete a Conjuguemos ser drill until you score 90%.

*How to teach:* Teach ser forms; Conjuguemos drill to 90%.

- **Q:** Conjugate ser for nosotros and ellos.
  **A:** Somos; son.
- **Q:** Complete: Tú ___ simpático.
  **A:** eres.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ser for origin
*Student:* Write 10 sentences about where people are from using ser de (Shakira es de Colombia...).

*How to teach:* Write 10 origin sentences with ser de.

- **Q:** Write: 'Lionel Messi is from Argentina.'
  **A:** Lionel Messi es de Argentina.
- **Q:** Write: 'We are from California.'
  **A:** Somos de California.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pair questions
*Student:* Answer and ask ¿Eres...? yes/no questions with a parent, practicing no + verb for negatives.

*How to teach:* Ask/answer ¿Eres…? questions; practice negatives with no before the verb.

- **Q:** Answer negatively: ¿Eres de España?
  **A:** No, no soy de España.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a ser conjugation and pronoun quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the ser and pronoun quiz.

- **Q:** Which pronoun is used for 'you all' in most of Latin America?
  **A:** Ustedes.

**Assessment:** Ser and subject pronoun quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** Quiz key: ser — yo soy, tú eres, él/ella/usted es, nosotros/as somos, vosotros/as sois, ellos/ellas/ustedes son. Pronoun items: Ana → ella; Ana y yo → nosotras; Juan y María → ellos; you (formal) → usted; you all (Latin America) → ustedes; you all (Spain, informal) → vosotros. Sample sentences: Nosotros somos de Texas. ¿Tú eres de Chile? Ellas son de Perú. 80% passing.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Landscape painting

**Prepare:** Paint, canvas board or heavy paper, brushes (large flat, medium, small round), the chosen thumbnail. Protect the work surface.

**Watch for:**
- Painting distant hills as dark and detailed as nearby objects. Distant areas are lighter, cooler, less detailed (atmospheric perspective).
- Starting with details. Block in big shapes first, then refine.

### Day 1 (Monday): Underpainting
*Student:* Block in background, middle ground and foreground with a thin underpainting.

*How to teach:* Thin underpainting: block in the three grounds with diluted paint.

- **Q:** Why start with an underpainting?
  **A:** It establishes composition and values and covers the white surface so colors relate better.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Background
*Student:* Paint the sky and distant areas lighter and cooler for atmospheric perspective.

*How to teach:* Paint sky and distant areas lighter and cooler.

- **Q:** What is atmospheric perspective?
  **A:** Distant objects appear lighter, bluer/cooler, less detailed and lower in contrast because of the air between viewer and object.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Middle ground
*Student:* Paint the middle ground with more detail and saturation.

*How to teach:* Middle ground: more detail and saturation.

- **Q:** What is the middle ground?
  **A:** The area between the foreground and background.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Foreground
*Student:* Paint the foreground with the most detail and contrast.

*How to teach:* Foreground: most detail, contrast and warm/strong color.

- **Q:** Why put the most detail in the foreground?
  **A:** Nearby objects are seen most clearly, which makes them feel close.

### Day 5 (Friday): Details
*Student:* Add final details and sign the painting.

*How to teach:* Add final details and sign.

- **Q:** Where should you sign a painting?
  **A:** Usually in a lower corner, small and neatly.

**Assessment:** Landscape painting progress.

**Answer key:** Landscape progress rubric (1–4): clear foreground, middle ground and background; atmospheric perspective (background lighter, bluer, less detail); planned color scheme; focal point with most contrast/detail; craftsmanship (edges, brushwork). Proficient = all three grounds and visible atmospheric perspective.

## Physical Education 7: Ultimate frisbee: throwing and catching

**Prepare:** Flying disc (175 g ultimate disc if possible), cones at 5, 10 and 15 m, hula hoop as target.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing with the whole arm and no wrist snap. Power and spin come from stepping, rotating hips and snapping the wrist.
- Tilting the disc (letting the outside edge rise) so it curves away. Keep the disc flat or edge slightly down at release.

### Day 1 (Monday): Backhand
*Student:* Practice backhand throws to a partner at 5, 10 and 15 meters.

*How to teach:* Backhand grip: thumb on top, fingers curled under the rim. Stand sideways, reach back across body, step and snap the wrist, release flat. Throw at 5, 10 and 15 m.

- **Q:** Where does the spin come from in a backhand?
  **A:** The wrist snap at release.
- **Q:** Why does spin matter?
  **A:** It stabilizes the disc so it flies flat and straight.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forehand
*Student:* Learn the forehand (flick) grip and throw 30 times.

*How to teach:* Forehand (flick): two fingers (index and middle) against the inside rim, thumb on top; short arm motion, wrist flick like skipping a stone. 30 throws.

- **Q:** Describe the forehand grip.
  **A:** Index and middle fingers pressed against the inside rim, thumb on top, disc held flat at your side.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Catching
*Student:* Practice pancake and rim catches at different heights.

*How to teach:* Pancake catch: one hand on top, one below, clap together (chest height). Rim catch: thumb down for high catches, thumb up for low.

- **Q:** When do you use a pancake catch?
  **A:** For discs coming between the chest and waist — it's the most secure catch.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pivot and pass
*Student:* Practice pivoting to avoid a marker and completing a pass.

*How to teach:* Teach pivot: keep one foot planted and step around a marker to throw. Practice completing passes past a defender.

- **Q:** Can you run with the disc in ultimate?
  **A:** No — you must establish a pivot foot and pass it; you may pivot like in basketball.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a throwing-accuracy test into a hoop target.

*How to teach:* Run the accuracy test.

- **Q:** Why step toward the target?
  **A:** It transfers body weight into the throw for power and aim.

**Assessment:** Throwing accuracy test.

**Answer key:** Throwing accuracy test: 10 throws (5 backhand, 5 forehand) from 10 m into a hoop or to a partner's chest area. Score: 9–10 advanced, 7–8 proficient, 5–6 developing. Observe cues: sideways stance, step toward target, flat release, wrist snap, follow-through pointing at target.

## Health Education 7: Stress and coping

**Prepare:** Have a timer or breathing app ready. Print a blank weekly schedule. Write 12 coping strategies on cards (e.g., exercise, talk to a friend, journal, deep breathing, music, sleep; vs. yelling, skipping meals, isolating, all-night gaming, ignoring homework, using substances).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all stress is bad. Short-term stress (eustress) can motivate you; chronic, unmanaged stress harms health.
- Believing avoiding a problem is coping. Avoidance gives short relief but the stressor grows; healthy coping deals with it or the feelings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stress response
*Student:* Learn how the body responds to stress and list personal stressors.

*How to teach:* Explain fight-or-flight: adrenaline raises heart rate, breathing and muscle tension. Your child lists personal stressors and how their body reacts.

- **Q:** Name three physical signs of the stress response.
  **A:** Faster heart rate, rapid breathing, tense muscles, sweating, stomachache, headache.
- **Q:** What is a stressor?
  **A:** Anything that causes stress, such as a test, conflict or change.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Breathing
*Student:* Practice box breathing and progressive muscle relaxation.

*How to teach:* Practice box breathing (in 4 counts, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) for 2 minutes and progressive muscle relaxation (tense each muscle group 5 seconds, release). Ask which felt better.

- **Q:** Describe box breathing.
  **A:** Breathe in for 4 counts, hold 4, breathe out 4, hold 4, and repeat.
- **Q:** Why does slow breathing reduce stress?
  **A:** It activates the body's relaxation response, slowing heart rate and calming the fight-or-flight response.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Time management
*Student:* Make a weekly schedule to reduce stress from schoolwork.

*How to teach:* Your child fills in a weekly schedule with school, activities, sleep and free time, and breaks one big assignment into steps.

- **Q:** How does a schedule reduce stress?
  **A:** It shows what to do when, prevents last-minute cramming and makes big tasks feel manageable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Healthy vs unhealthy coping
*Student:* Sort 12 coping strategies into healthy and unhealthy.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts the 12 cards and explains why each unhealthy one makes things worse later.

- **Q:** Why is staying up all night gaming an unhealthy way to cope?
  **A:** It avoids the problem and causes sleep loss, which increases stress and irritability.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a personal stress-management plan.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a personal stress plan using the rubric checklist.

- **Q:** What should you do if stress feels overwhelming for weeks?
  **A:** Talk to a trusted adult or a counselor/doctor; long-lasting stress may need professional help.

**Assessment:** Stress plan.

**Answer key:** Stress plan rubric: 4 = lists 3+ personal stressors, body signs they notice, at least 3 healthy coping strategies (one physical, one thinking, one social), a time-management step, and a trusted adult to talk to; 3 = most parts, less specific; 2 = only 1–2 strategies; 1 = incomplete. Sort key: healthy = exercise, deep breathing, talking to someone, journaling, music, getting enough sleep, breaking tasks into steps; unhealthy = yelling/hitting, isolating, skipping meals/overeating, procrastinating, excessive screen time, using substances.
