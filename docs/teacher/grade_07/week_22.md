# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 22

## English Language Arts 7: Page vs. stage and performance

**Prepare:** A filmed production (1999 film or recorded stage version); scene script for rehearsal; Shakespeare excerpt with questions for the test.

**Watch for:**
- Treating a film as 'wrong' when it changes the script. Analyze why the change was made.
- Rushing lines in performance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Watch a production
*Student:* Watch key scenes of a filmed production and note how costumes, setting and acting change your understanding.

*How to teach:* Watch key scenes from a filmed production. Your child notes how costumes, setting and acting change their understanding of the characters. Check before moving on: ask "How can setting change meaning?" Look for: A modern setting can make the conflicts feel familiar.

- **Q:** How can setting change meaning?
  **A:** A modern setting can make the conflicts feel familiar.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparison essay
*Student:* Write a 5-paragraph comparison of the script and film's portrayal of Bottom or Puck, citing both.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 5-paragraph comparison essay on how the script and film portray Bottom or Puck. Each body paragraph cites both the script and the film. Check before moving on: ask "What must each body paragraph include?" Look for: Evidence from both script and film.

- **Q:** What must each body paragraph include?
  **A:** Evidence from both script and film.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scene rehearsal
*Student:* Choose a 30-line scene, mark blocking and rehearse with family members.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a 30-line scene, marks blocking (movement and positions) on the script, and rehearses with family members. Check before moving on: ask "What is blocking?" Look for: Planned movement and positions of actors on stage.

- **Q:** What is blocking?
  **A:** Planned movement and positions of actors on stage.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Performance
*Student:* Perform the scene for family using voice and gesture, then discuss interpretation choices.

*How to teach:* Your child performs the scene using voice and gesture. Afterward, discuss interpretation choices and how they changed meaning. Check before moving on: ask "Interpretation choice example?" Look for: Playing Bottom as kind rather than foolish.

- **Q:** Interpretation choice example?
  **A:** Playing Bottom as kind rather than foolish.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take a unit test on a Shakespeare excerpt with questions on language, structure and character.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test on a Shakespeare excerpt independently. Score with the key. Check before moving on: ask "What does 'thou' mean?" Look for: You (informal).

- **Q:** What does 'thou' mean?
  **A:** You (informal).

**Assessment:** Comparison essay, performance rubric and unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Comparison essay rubric: thesis, evidence from both, analysis. Performance rubric: memorization/preparation, voice, gesture, blocking. Unit test: answers on language (translation), structure, character with line evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Rational numbers unit test

**Prepare:** Index cards; a deck of playing cards (red = negative, black = positive, face cards = 10 or remove them).

**Watch for:**
- Undoing −3x by adding 3. The −3 multiplies x, so divide by −3.
- Writing the temperature drop as positive. A drop is a negative change.

### Day 1 (Monday): Solving simple equations
*Student:* Solve one-step equations with negative numbers like x + 4.5 = -2 and -3x = 18.

*How to teach:* Model x + 4.5 = −2 (subtract 4.5) and −3x = 18 (divide by −3). Your child solves and checks by substituting. Check before moving on: ask "x + 4.5 = −2?" Look for: x = −6.5.

- **Q:** x + 4.5 = −2?
  **A:** x = −6.5.
- **Q:** −3x = 18?
  **A:** x = −6.
- **Q:** x/−4 = 2.5?
  **A:** x = −10.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Representing contexts
*Student:* Write and solve equations for situations involving temperature drops and debt.

*How to teach:* Model writing an equation from a story. Your child writes and solves several. Check before moving on: ask "Temperature dropped 3° each hour, total −15°. Hours?" Look for: −3h = −15, h = 5.

- **Q:** Temperature dropped 3° each hour, total −15°. Hours?
  **A:** −3h = −15, h = 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Game day
*Student:* Play an integer card game (red = negative, black = positive) for sums and products and record 20 rounds.

*How to teach:* Play 20 rounds: each draws two cards, finds the sum and product; the higher value wins. Your child records each round. Check before moving on: ask "Red 7 and black 4: sum and product?" Look for: −7 + 4 = −3; (−7)(4) = −28.

- **Q:** Red 7 and black 4: sum and product?
  **A:** −7 + 4 = −3; (−7)(4) = −28.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of all four operations with rational numbers.

*How to teach:* Mixed review; one example of each operation together. Check before moving on: ask "(−3/5) ÷ (−3/10)?" Look for: 2.

- **Q:** (−3/5) ÷ (−3/10)?
  **A:** 2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the unit 5 test on rational number arithmetic.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 5 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "−0.5 × −0.5?" Look for: 0.25.

- **Q:** −0.5 × −0.5?
  **A:** 0.25.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test sample answers: 1) −9 + 14 = 5. 2) −3 − 8 = −11. 3) (−4)(−2.5) = 10. 4) −48 ÷ 6 = −8. 5) 5/8 = 0.625. 6) 2/3 = 0.6 repeating. 7) x − 7 = −12 → x = −5. 8) −2x = 9 → x = −4.5. 9) Distance from −15 to 22 = 37. 10) Debt $120 paid off at $15/week: 8 weeks.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Weathering, erosion and deposition shape the surface

**Prepare:** Baking pan or plastic bin, sand, soil, watering can, books to prop slope; ice cubes with sand frozen in, clay or play dough; photos of Yosemite Valley.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking erosion and weathering are the same. Weathering breaks rock; erosion moves the pieces.
- Thinking all landforms take millions of years. Some (landslides, floods) happen in seconds to days.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stream table
*Student:* Build a stream table in a baking pan with sand and observe erosion and delta formation as water flows.

*How to teach:* Prop the pan at low slope; pour water steadily from the top. Your child sketches channels and the delta. Check before moving on: ask "Where is sediment deposited?" Look for: Where water slows — at the bottom/outlet, forming a delta.

- **Q:** Where is sediment deposited?
  **A:** Where water slows — at the bottom/outlet, forming a delta.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Changing slope
*Student:* Repeat with steeper slope and record differences in erosion and deposition.

*How to teach:* Repeat with steeper slope. Your child compares. Check before moving on: ask "Effect of steeper slope?" Look for: Faster water, deeper channels, more erosion and larger deposits.

- **Q:** Effect of steeper slope?
  **A:** Faster water, deeper channels, more erosion and larger deposits.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Glaciers
*Student:* Drag an ice cube with embedded sand across clay to model glacial erosion and compare to Yosemite Valley.

*How to teach:* Drag the sandy ice cube over clay. Your child compares the scratches to Yosemite Valley's U-shape. Check before moving on: ask "How did glaciers shape Yosemite Valley?" Look for: Glaciers carved a V-shaped river valley into a wide U-shape and polished granite.

- **Q:** How did glaciers shape Yosemite Valley?
  **A:** Glaciers carved a V-shaped river valley into a wide U-shape and polished granite.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fast vs. slow
*Student:* Compare landslides, floods and canyon formation on a timeline from seconds to millions of years.

*How to teach:* Your child places events on a timeline from seconds to millions of years. Check before moving on: ask "Fast and slow examples?" Look for: Landslide (seconds–minutes), flood (days), canyon formation (millions of years).

- **Q:** Fast and slow examples?
  **A:** Landslide (seconds–minutes), flood (days), canyon formation (millions of years).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER on how water shaped a California landform.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER on a California landform shaped by water. Check before moving on: ask "Example claim?" Look for: The Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta formed from river sediment deposited where rivers slowed.

- **Q:** Example claim?
  **A:** The Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta formed from river sediment deposited where rivers slowed.

**Assessment:** Stream-table data and CER.

**Answer key:** Stream-table data: sketches/measurements comparing slopes. CER rubric: 4 = accurate claim, at least two pieces of specific evidence from the investigation (numbers/observations), reasoning that links evidence to the scientific principle; 3 = claim and evidence, reasoning thin; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or incorrect.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Aztec and Inca

**Prepare:** Map of Tenochtitlan; Codex Mendoza images; string for quipu.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Aztec called themselves Aztec. They called themselves Mexica.
- Thinking the Inca had writing. They used quipus to record numbers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tenochtitlan
*Student:* Study a map of Tenochtitlan and explain how chinampas fed a city of 200,000.

*How to teach:* Your child studies Tenochtitlan and chinampas. Check before moving on: ask "What were chinampas?" Look for: Artificial island farms built in the lake.

- **Q:** What were chinampas?
  **A:** Artificial island farms built in the lake.
- **Q:** Founded?
  **A:** 1325 on Lake Texcoco.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Aztec society
*Student:* Analyze Codex Mendoza tribute pages and describe Aztec social classes and warfare.

*How to teach:* Analyze Codex Mendoza tribute pages. Check before moving on: ask "What was tribute?" Look for: Goods conquered peoples had to pay the Aztec.

- **Q:** What was tribute?
  **A:** Goods conquered peoples had to pay the Aztec.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inca roads and quipu
*Student:* Map the Inca road system and make a knotted-string quipu recording family data.

*How to teach:* Map the Inca road; make a quipu. Check before moving on: ask "How long was the road?" Look for: About 25,000 miles (40,000 km).

- **Q:** How long was the road?
  **A:** About 25,000 miles (40,000 km).
- **Q:** Chasquis?
  **A:** Relay runners carrying messages.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Complete a comparison chart of Maya, Aztec and Inca government, economy, religion and achievements.

*How to teach:* Your child completes a comparison chart of Maya, Aztec and Inca government, economy, religion and achievements. Check before moving on: ask "Inca capital?" Look for: Cusco.

- **Q:** Inca capital?
  **A:** Cusco.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the Aztec and Inca.

*How to teach:* Give the Aztec and Inca quiz without notes. Check before moving on: ask "Inca labor tax?" Look for: Mit'a.

- **Q:** Inca labor tax?
  **A:** Mit'a.

**Assessment:** Comparison chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Tenochtitlan, chinampas, tribute, Aztec classes, Inca roads, quipu, mit'a, Cusco. Chart: government, economy, religion, achievements for all three.

## Spanish 1A: Culture: food traditions and markets

**Prepare:** World map, photos of dishes, ingredients for guacamole (avocados, lime, onion, tomato, cilantro, salt) or tortilla española (potatoes, eggs, onion, olive oil). A market video (e.g., Mercado de Oaxaca or La Boquería in Barcelona).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'tortilla' means the same everywhere. In Mexico it's a flat corn or flour bread; in Spain la tortilla española is a potato-and-egg omelet.
- Assuming all Latin American food is spicy. Many dishes are mild; cuisine varies widely by country.

### Day 1 (Monday): Regional foods
*Student:* Research one traditional dish each from Mexico, Spain, Venezuela, and Peru and add photos and captions to your map.

*How to teach:* Research one dish each from Mexico, Spain, Venezuela, Peru; add photos and captions.

- **Q:** What is an arepa?
  **A:** A round corn-flour flatbread from Venezuela (and Colombia), often split and filled.
- **Q:** What is ceviche?
  **A:** Peru's dish of raw fish 'cooked'/cured in lime juice with onion and chile.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tortilla: two meanings
*Student:* Compare the Mexican corn tortilla and the Spanish tortilla española; write five comparison sentences.

*How to teach:* Compare Mexican tortilla and tortilla española; write five sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'The Spanish tortilla has eggs and potatoes.'
  **A:** La tortilla española tiene huevos y papas (patatas in Spain).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): El mercado
*Student:* Watch a video of a Latin American market and list 10 foods you recognize in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Watch a market video; list 10 foods in Spanish.

- **Q:** What is 'el mercado'?
  **A:** The market.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cocina
*Student:* Prepare a simple recipe (guacamole or tortilla española) with a parent, narrating each step in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Prepare guacamole or tortilla española, narrating steps (Corto las papas, Mezclo los huevos…).

- **Q:** Say 'I cut the onion.'
  **A:** Corto la cebolla.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short reflection comparing a family meal tradition with one you studied.

*How to teach:* Write the cultural comparison reflection.

- **Q:** What is gazpacho?
  **A:** A cold tomato-based vegetable soup from Spain (Andalusia).

**Assessment:** Cultural comparison writing rubric.

**Answer key:** Cultural comparison rubric (1–4): describes a family meal tradition and one studied tradition with specific details (food, when, who, meaning), makes 2+ comparisons (similarities and differences), uses some Spanish food vocabulary. Regional foods key: Mexico — tacos, mole, tamales; Spain — paella, gazpacho, tortilla española, churros; Venezuela — arepas (corn cakes), pabellón criollo; Peru — ceviche (raw fish cured in lime), lomo saltado.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Mesoamerican and African art connections

**Prepare:** Images of an Aztec codex (e.g., Codex Borbonicus or Codex Mendoza) and adinkra symbol chart. Potatoes or foam, plastic knife (adult supervision), fabric or paper, paint/ink.

**Watch for:**
- Copying sacred or cultural symbols without understanding them. Learn meanings, credit the culture, and create your own personal symbol.
- Thinking codices are read like European books. Many Mesoamerican codices are read with pictographs, often screenfold, and some read in zigzag patterns.

### Day 1 (Monday): Codex art
*Student:* Analyze Aztec codex images for color, line and storytelling.

*How to teach:* Analyze codex images: bold outlines, flat bright colors, figures in profile, glyphs for dates/names telling stories.

- **Q:** What is a codex?
  **A:** A handmade book; Aztec codices used pictures and glyphs on bark paper or deerskin, often folded like a screen.
- **Q:** What kind of information did Aztec codices record?
  **A:** History, calendars, religious rituals, tribute lists and genealogies.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adinkra
*Student:* Learn the meanings of 8 West African adinkra symbols.

*How to teach:* Learn 8 adinkra symbols and their meanings (see key).

- **Q:** Where do adinkra symbols come from?
  **A:** The Akan people of Ghana (and Côte d'Ivoire).
- **Q:** What does Sankofa mean?
  **A:** 'Go back and get it' — learn from the past.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stamp design
*Student:* Design a personal symbol and carve it into a potato or foam stamp.

*How to teach:* Design a personal symbol; carve a potato or foam stamp (adult cuts if needed).

- **Q:** Why must stamp designs be simple?
  **A:** Fine details don't carve or print clearly; bold shapes print best.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Printed cloth
*Student:* Stamp a repeating pattern on fabric or paper with traditional and personal symbols.

*How to teach:* Stamp a repeating pattern on fabric/paper.

- **Q:** Name two kinds of repeating patterns.
  **A:** Grid (straight rows), half-drop, alternating, rotated.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about respectful inspiration versus copying cultural art.

*How to teach:* Write about respectful inspiration vs copying.

- **Q:** Give one way to be respectful when inspired by another culture's art.
  **A:** Learn the meaning and history, credit the source, avoid sacred items used out of context, and make original work.

**Assessment:** Stamped textile and reflection.

**Answer key:** Stamped textile rubric (1–4): repeating pattern (grid, alternating or rotated), includes a personal symbol and at least one adinkra symbol with written meaning, consistent printing. Adinkra meanings (Akan, Ghana): Gye Nyame — 'except God' (supremacy of God); Sankofa — 'go back and get it' (learn from the past); Dwennimmen — ram's horns (humility and strength); Akoma — heart (patience, love); Adinkrahene — chief of symbols (greatness, leadership); Funtunfunefu — Siamese crocodiles (unity, democracy); Eban — fence (safety, love, security); Ese ne tekrema — teeth and tongue (friendship, interdependence). Reflection proficient: distinguishes respectful inspiration (research, credit, understanding meaning, creating original work) from copying/appropriation.

## Physical Education 7: Softball: throwing, catching and batting

**Prepare:** Softball or wiffle ball, bat, gloves, a batting tee, cones at 15 and 25 m. Safety: swing only in a clear zone; no one stands near the batter.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing with the same-side foot forward. Step with the opposite foot toward the target.
- Turning away from grounders. Get in front of the ball, glove down, fingers pointing to the ground.

### Day 1 (Monday): Throwing
*Student:* Practice overhand throws with step and follow-through at 15 and 25 meters.

*How to teach:* Teach overhand throw: side to target, arm back with elbow at shoulder height, step with opposite foot, rotate hips, follow through across body. Crow hop for longer throws.

- **Q:** Which foot steps forward for a right-handed thrower?
  **A:** The left foot.
- **Q:** What is a crow hop?
  **A:** A hop-step that builds momentum toward the target before a long throw.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fielding
*Student:* Field grounders and fly balls hit or thrown by a partner.

*How to teach:* Roll grounders and toss fly balls. Grounders: glove down, fingers point down, body in front. Fly balls: glove up, fingers point up, call 'mine'.

- **Q:** How should the glove be for a grounder vs a fly ball?
  **A:** Grounder: open, fingers down on the ground; fly ball: above head, fingers up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Batting tee
*Student:* Practice level swings off a tee, 40 swings.

*How to teach:* Batting stance: feet shoulder-width, knuckles aligned, bat up; level swing, rotate hips, follow through. 40 swings off the tee.

- **Q:** Why is a level swing important?
  **A:** It keeps the bat in the ball's path longer, increasing contact.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Soft toss
*Student:* Hit soft-tossed balls and aim at field zones.

*How to teach:* Soft toss from the side; aim at field zones (left, center, right).

- **Q:** How do you hit to the opposite field?
  **A:** Let the ball travel deeper and contact it slightly later.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a throwing accuracy and batting contact test.

*How to teach:* Run the throwing and batting test.

- **Q:** Name a safety rule for batting.
  **A:** Only swing when no one is close; drop (don't throw) the bat after hitting.

**Assessment:** Throwing and batting test.

**Answer key:** Throwing accuracy: 10 throws to a partner's chest from 15 m (proficient 7/10). Batting contact: 10 soft-toss swings (proficient 6 contacts, advanced 6 line drives). Observe cues: side-on stance, step with opposite foot, elbow up, follow-through; batting: balanced stance, eyes on ball, level swing, hip rotation.

## Health Education 7: Medicine safety and fentanyl awareness

**Prepare:** Collect 3 OTC medicine boxes and one prescription bottle (to read only). Visit dea.gov 'One Pill Can Kill' and a naloxone info page. Plan a lockable storage spot. Parent preview: overdose topics are serious — focus on safety facts.

**Watch for:**
- Believing pills bought online or from friends are the same as pharmacy pills. Counterfeit pills often contain fentanyl and look identical.
- Thinking calling 911 for an overdose gets you in trouble. California's Good Samaritan law gives limited protection to people who seek help; saving a life comes first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Medicine labels
*Student:* Read OTC labels for dosage, warnings and expiration.

*How to teach:* Read the OTC Drug Facts label: active ingredient, uses, warnings, directions, expiration.

- **Q:** Why check the active ingredient on two medicines before taking both?
  **A:** They may contain the same drug (e.g., acetaminophen), leading to an overdose.
- **Q:** What does 'expiration date' mean?
  **A:** The date after which the medicine may not work as well or be safe.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prescription misuse
*Student:* Learn why sharing prescriptions is dangerous.

*How to teach:* Discuss why prescriptions are for one person: dose, allergies, interactions, addiction risk.

- **Q:** Why is it dangerous to take a friend's prescription?
  **A:** The dose and drug were chosen for their body and condition; it can cause allergic reactions, interactions, overdose or addiction — and it's illegal.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fentanyl
*Student:* Learn about counterfeit pills laced with fentanyl and the 'one pill can kill' message.

*How to teach:* Explain counterfeit pills: they look real but may contain fentanyl, which can be deadly in tiny amounts (about 2 mg). Discuss 'One Pill Can Kill.'

- **Q:** Why is fentanyl so dangerous?
  **A:** It is extremely potent; an amount the size of a few grains of salt (about 2 mg) can be fatal, and it is hidden in fake pills and other drugs.
- **Q:** Where is the only safe source of medicine?
  **A:** A licensed pharmacy, given by a doctor or a trusted adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Naloxone
*Student:* Learn what naloxone (Narcan) is and how to get help in an overdose.

*How to teach:* Teach overdose signs (unresponsive, slow/no breathing, blue lips, pinpoint pupils) and the response: call 911, give naloxone, rescue breathing if trained, stay with them.

- **Q:** What does naloxone (Narcan) do?
  **A:** It quickly reverses an opioid overdose by blocking opioids in the brain; it is a nasal spray anyone can give.
- **Q:** Name two signs of an opioid overdose.
  **A:** Not responding, slow or stopped breathing, blue/gray lips or fingertips, tiny pupils, gurgling sounds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Inventory and safely store household medicines with a parent.

*How to teach:* With a parent, inventory home medicines and lock up or dispose of expired ones.

- **Q:** How should you dispose of old medicines?
  **A:** Take them to a drug take-back site or pharmacy kiosk (or follow FDA disposal instructions).

**Assessment:** Medicine safety inventory.

**Answer key:** Medicine safety inventory: lists each household medicine with name, purpose, expiration date and whether stored safely; expired meds set aside for a take-back site or kiosk; prescription meds locked. Proficient work also identifies the active ingredient and correct dose from the label for one OTC product.
