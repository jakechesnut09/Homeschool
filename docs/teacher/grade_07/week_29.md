# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 29

## English Language Arts 7: Roll of Thunder, chapters 7–9

**Prepare:** Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry; theme tracker.

**Watch for:**
- Missing the symbol: the fig tree survives among bigger trees.
- Thinking Cassie's revenge is just mean.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 7
*Student:* Read Chapter 7 and analyze Papa's story about the fig tree as a symbol.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 7. Your child explains Papa's fig tree story and what the tree symbolizes for the Logans. Check before moving on: ask "What does the fig tree symbolize?" Look for: The Logans: small among big trees, but deep roots keep it alive and bearing fruit.

- **Q:** What does the fig tree symbolize?
  **A:** The Logans: small among big trees, but deep roots keep it alive and bearing fruit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 8
*Student:* Read Chapter 8 and explain Cassie's plan against Lillian Jean and what it reveals.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 8. Your child explains Cassie's plan against Lillian Jean and what it reveals about her. Check before moving on: ask "What is Cassie's plan?" Look for: She pretends to be Lillian Jean's friend, learns her secrets, then fights her in the woods; Lillian Jean won't tell because Cassie knows her secrets.

- **Q:** What is Cassie's plan?
  **A:** She pretends to be Lillian Jean's friend, learns her secrets, then fights her in the woods; Lillian Jean won't tell because Cassie knows her secrets.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 9
*Student:* Read Chapter 9 and track the boycott and its consequences.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 9. Your child tracks the boycott and its consequences in a cause-effect chart. Check before moving on: ask "What happens to Papa?" Look for: His wagon is attacked; his leg is broken.

- **Q:** What happens to Papa?
  **A:** His wagon is attacked; his leg is broken.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme tracking
*Student:* Add evidence to a theme tracker for 'family strength' and 'standing up to injustice'.

*How to teach:* Your child adds evidence to a theme tracker for 'family strength' and 'standing up to injustice'. Check before moving on: ask "Evidence for 'family strength'?" Look for: The family works together to protect the land.

- **Q:** Evidence for 'family strength'?
  **A:** The family works together to protect the land.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Discuss the fig tree symbol and take a chapters 7–9 quiz.

*How to teach:* Discuss the fig tree symbol, then give the chapters 7–9 quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Why does Mama lose her job?" Look for: She teaches about slavery and covered the textbook chart; school board fires her.

- **Q:** Why does Mama lose her job?
  **A:** She teaches about slavery and covered the textbook chart; school board fires her.

**Assessment:** Theme tracker and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Fig tree symbol. 2) Cassie's plan. 3) Mama fired. 4) Boycott of Wallace store. 5) Papa's broken leg. Theme tracker: 3+ entries per theme.

## Mathematics 7: Angle relationships

**Prepare:** Protractor, paper strips, brads (paper fasteners).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up complementary (90°) and supplementary (180°). Mnemonic: C comes before S, 90 before 180.
- Thinking vertical angles are angles that look vertical. They are opposite each other at an intersection.

### Day 1 (Monday): Angles on a line
*Student:* Measure angles formed by intersecting lines with a protractor and record which pairs sum to 180 or are equal.

*How to teach:* Draw two intersecting lines. Your child measures all four angles and records sums and equal pairs. Check before moving on: ask "One angle is 65°. The adjacent angle on the line?" Look for: 115°.

- **Q:** One angle is 65°. The adjacent angle on the line?
  **A:** 115°.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Complementary and supplementary
*Student:* Find unknown complements and supplements and write equations like x + 47 = 90.

*How to teach:* Model x + 47 = 90. Your child finds complements and supplements. Check before moving on: ask "Complement of 47°?" Look for: 43°.

- **Q:** Complement of 47°?
  **A:** 43°.
- **Q:** Supplement of 47°?
  **A:** 133°.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Vertical angles
*Student:* Build intersecting lines with paper strips and a brad to show vertical angles stay equal as you rotate.

*How to teach:* Build an X with strips and a brad and rotate. Your child records vertical-angle pairs. Check before moving on: ask "One angle is 130°. Its vertical angle?" Look for: 130°.

- **Q:** One angle is 130°. Its vertical angle?
  **A:** 130°.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Angle equations
*Student:* Solve 10 problems like (2x + 10) + 3x = 180 from diagrams.

*How to teach:* Model (2x + 10) + 3x = 180. Your child solves 10. Check before moving on: ask "Solve." Look for: 5x + 10 = 180, x = 34; angles 78° and 102°.

- **Q:** Solve.
  **A:** 5x + 10 = 180, x = 34; angles 78° and 102°.
- **Q:** Vertical angles 4x − 10 and 2x + 30?
  **A:** x = 20, each 70°.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on angle relationships and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Complementary angles x and 2x?" Look for: x = 30°, 60°.

- **Q:** Complementary angles x and 2x?
  **A:** x = 30°, 60°.

**Assessment:** Angle relationships quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Complement of 28° = 62°. 2) Supplement of 95° = 85°. 3) Vertical angle of 54° = 54°. 4) x + 3x = 180 → x = 45. 5) (x + 20) + 2x = 90 → x = 70/3 ≈ 23.3. 6) Three angles on a line: 40, x, 75 → x = 65.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Plate tectonics and California

**Prepare:** Graham crackers, frosting, wax paper; USGS 'Latest Earthquakes' map; world map; calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking California will 'fall into the ocean.' The Pacific Plate slides northwest past North America.
- Thinking earthquakes are random.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plate boundaries
*Student:* Model convergent, divergent and transform boundaries with graham crackers on frosting.

*How to teach:* Spread frosting on wax paper (the asthenosphere) and lay two graham-cracker halves on it. Your child models convergent (push together — crackers buckle or one slides under), divergent (pull apart — frosting shows the gap) and transform (slide past — edges catch and jerk) boundaries and sketches each. Check before moving on: ask "San Andreas boundary type?" Look for: Transform.

- **Q:** San Andreas boundary type?
  **A:** Transform.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Earthquake map
*Student:* Plot recent earthquakes from the USGS map and identify plate boundaries from the patterns.

*How to teach:* Open the USGS Latest Earthquakes map (past 30 days, magnitude 2.5+). Your child plots or marks 20–30 quakes on a world map and traces the lines where they cluster, then compares with a plate boundary map. Check before moving on: ask "Pattern?" Look for: Earthquakes cluster along plate boundaries (Ring of Fire).

- **Q:** Pattern?
  **A:** Earthquakes cluster along plate boundaries (Ring of Fire).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): San Andreas Fault
*Student:* Study how the Pacific and North American plates slide along the San Andreas and calculate how far LA moves in 1,000 years at 5 cm/yr.

*How to teach:* Explain that the Pacific Plate (with LA) slides northwest relative to the North American Plate along the San Andreas. Your child calculates movement at 5 cm/yr over 1,000 years and converts to meters. Check before moving on: ask "5 cm/yr × 1,000 years?" Look for: 5,000 cm = 50 m.

- **Q:** 5 cm/yr × 1,000 years?
  **A:** 5,000 cm = 50 m.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mountains and volcanoes
*Student:* Explain the formation of the Sierra Nevada and Mount Shasta in terms of plate motions.

*How to teach:* Show maps of the Sierra Nevada and Cascades. Your child explains how subduction produced the magma for Sierra granite and Mount Shasta, and how later faulting uplifted the Sierra. Check before moving on: ask "Sierra Nevada?" Look for: Granite from subduction magma, later uplifted and tilted by faulting.

- **Q:** Sierra Nevada?
  **A:** Granite from subduction magma, later uplifted and tilted by faulting.
- **Q:** Mount Shasta?
  **A:** Volcano from Juan de Fuca Plate subducting under North America (Cascade arc).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on plate boundaries and California geology.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on boundary types and California geology without notes. Review the earthquake map together. Check before moving on: ask "Divergent example?" Look for: Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

- **Q:** Divergent example?
  **A:** Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

**Assessment:** Plotted earthquake map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: three boundary types with examples, San Andreas transform, 50 m in 1,000 yrs, Shasta from subduction, earthquakes at boundaries. Map: plotted quakes with boundaries identified.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Counter-Reformation and missionaries

**Prepare:** Council of Trent summaries; Calisphere images of California missions; timeline sorting cards. Preview: missions discussion includes harm to Native Californians.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Counter-Reformation only fought Protestants. It also reformed abuses inside the Catholic Church.
- Thinking Jesuits founded California's missions. Franciscans (Junípero Serra) founded the 21 Alta California missions (1769–1823).

### Day 1 (Monday): Council of Trent
*Student:* Read summaries of the Council of Trent's reforms and chart what changed and what stayed.

*How to teach:* Your child charts changes and continuities from Trent. Check before moving on: ask "What changed?" Look for: Ended sale of indulgences abuses, required training for priests (seminaries).

- **Q:** What changed?
  **A:** Ended sale of indulgences abuses, required training for priests (seminaries).
- **Q:** What stayed?
  **A:** Seven sacraments, authority of pope and tradition.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jesuits
*Student:* Research Ignatius of Loyola and Jesuit schools and missions to Asia and the Americas.

*How to teach:* Research Loyola and Jesuits. Check before moving on: ask "Founded?" Look for: 1540, Ignatius of Loyola.

- **Q:** Founded?
  **A:** 1540, Ignatius of Loyola.
- **Q:** Famous missionary?
  **A:** Francis Xavier in Asia.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Missions
*Student:* Analyze Calisphere images of early California missions and discuss impacts on Native Californians.

*How to teach:* Analyze mission images. Check before moving on: ask "Impacts on Native Californians?" Look for: Forced labor, loss of culture and land, deadly diseases; population declined sharply.

- **Q:** Impacts on Native Californians?
  **A:** Forced labor, loss of culture and land, deadly diseases; population declined sharply.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review Renaissance and Reformation with a timeline sorting game.

*How to teach:* Play the timeline sorting game. Check before moving on: ask "Order: Gutenberg Bible, 95 Theses, Council of Trent begins." Look for: 1455, 1517, 1545.

- **Q:** Order: Gutenberg Bible, 95 Theses, Council of Trent begins.
  **A:** 1455, 1517, 1545.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 8 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 8 test. Check before moving on: ask "Who was Ignatius of Loyola?" Look for: Founder of the Jesuits.

- **Q:** Who was Ignatius of Loyola?
  **A:** Founder of the Jesuits.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test: Renaissance origins, humanism, art, printing, Reformation causes, Luther, other reformers, Henry VIII, Council of Trent, Jesuits.

## Spanish 1A: Lugares en la ciudad

**Prepare:** Hand-drawn town map, Google Maps Street View of the Plaza de la Constitución/Zócalo in Oaxaca or the Plaza Mayor in Salamanca. Places: la biblioteca, el parque, el centro comercial, la iglesia, la plaza, el cine, el gimnasio, la playa, el restaurante, el supermercado, el banco, el museo, la escuela, el hospital, el correo, la piscina.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'el iglesia' because it starts with a vowel. It's la iglesia (feminine).
- Thinking a plaza is like a mall. A Spanish/Latin American plaza is an open central square for gathering, often with a church and government buildings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Places
*Student:* Learn 16 places in a town and label a hand-drawn map of your neighborhood.

*How to teach:* Learn 16 places; label a neighborhood map.

- **Q:** How do you say 'the library' and 'the mall'?
  **A:** la biblioteca; el centro comercial.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map questions
*Student:* Answer ¿Dónde está la biblioteca? questions using prepositions on your map.

*How to teach:* Answer ¿Dónde está…? using map prepositions.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Dónde está el parque? (across from/in front of the school)
  **A:** El parque está delante de la escuela (or enfrente de la escuela).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): La plaza
*Student:* Read about the central plaza in Spanish and Latin American towns and compare it with a California downtown.

*How to teach:* Read about the central plaza; compare with a California downtown.

- **Q:** What buildings are usually around a main plaza?
  **A:** A church/cathedral, the town hall (ayuntamiento), shops and cafés.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Virtual tour
*Student:* Use Google Maps Street View to tour a plaza in Oaxaca or Salamanca and write 8 sentences about what is there.

*How to teach:* Virtual tour; write 8 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'In the plaza there is a church and many people.'
  **A:** En la plaza hay una iglesia y mucha gente.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a places vocabulary quiz with a map-reading section.

*How to teach:* Give the places and map quiz.

- **Q:** What is 'el cine'?
  **A:** The movie theater.

**Assessment:** Places vocabulary and map quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 16 places with articles; map section answers '¿Dónde está…?' with estar + preposition (e.g., La biblioteca está al lado del parque; El cine está entre el banco y el restaurante). Plaza culture: central square (plaza mayor/zócalo) surrounded by church, town hall, shops and cafés; center of social life, festivals and the paseo. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Photo essay and digital ethics

**Prepare:** Camera, free photo editor (e.g., Snapseed, Photos app, GIMP). Read a short explainer of Creative Commons licenses.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking anything online is free to use. Most images are copyrighted; use your own, public domain or Creative Commons images with attribution.
- Photographing people without asking. Ask permission, especially of children and before sharing online.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a 6–8 photo essay about a day in your family or neighborhood.

*How to teach:* Plan a 6–8 photo story with a shot list (wide establishing, medium, close-up details).

- **Q:** What is a photo essay?
  **A:** A series of photos that together tell a story or explore a theme.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shoot
*Student:* Take photos for the essay, asking permission before photographing people.

*How to teach:* Shoot, asking permission before photographing people.

- **Q:** Why ask permission before photographing people?
  **A:** It respects their privacy and consent, especially before sharing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Crop and adjust exposure in a free photo editor.

*How to teach:* Crop and adjust exposure.

- **Q:** What does 'exposure' mean?
  **A:** How light or dark the photo is, based on the light reaching the sensor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ethics
*Student:* Learn about copyright, Creative Commons and consent, and write sharing rules for your work.

*How to teach:* Teach copyright (automatic for creators), Creative Commons (creator lets others use with conditions like attribution) and consent; write sharing rules.

- **Q:** What does 'CC BY' mean?
  **A:** Creative Commons Attribution — you may use the work if you credit the creator.
- **Q:** Is a photo you found on Google free to use?
  **A:** Not necessarily — it's usually copyrighted unless marked public domain or licensed for reuse.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present the photo essay as a slideshow with captions.

*How to teach:* Present as a captioned slideshow.

- **Q:** What story does your essay tell?
  **A:** Open answer.

**Assessment:** Photo essay.

**Answer key:** Photo essay rubric (1–4): 6–8 photos telling a story with beginning, middle and end; variety of shots (wide, medium, close-up); captions; editing improves without distorting; permission obtained for people. Sharing rules include consent, privacy (no addresses/faces of kids without permission), credit and a license choice.

## Physical Education 7: Track: sprints and starts

**Prepare:** Open field or track, stopwatch, cones at 0, 20 and 50 m, a baton (rolled paper or tube), phone for video.

**Watch for:**
- Running on the heels in sprints. Sprint on the balls of the feet.
- Swinging arms across the body. Arms swing forward and back ('cheek to pocket'), elbows ~90°.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sprint drills
*Student:* Practice A-skips, B-skips and high knees for sprint form.

*How to teach:* Teach A-skips (high knee with a skip), B-skips (extend lower leg and paw back), high knees.

- **Q:** Why practice sprint drills?
  **A:** They train correct sprint mechanics: high knees, foot strike under the hips, quick ground contact.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Starts
*Student:* Practice standing and three-point starts over 20 meters.

*How to teach:* Practice standing and three-point starts: low forward lean, push hard, short powerful steps (drive phase), gradually rise.

- **Q:** What is the drive phase?
  **A:** The first several steps after the start when the body leans forward and pushes hard to accelerate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timed 50m
*Student:* Run and time three 50-meter sprints.

*How to teach:* Time three 50 m sprints with full recovery (2–3 minutes) between.

- **Q:** Why rest fully between sprints?
  **A:** Sprinting uses the fast anaerobic energy system, which needs time to recover so each sprint is at top speed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relay handoffs
*Student:* Practice baton handoffs with a partner.

*How to teach:* Teach handoffs: incoming runner says 'Stick!', outgoing runner reaches back with palm open (upsweep or downsweep) while running.

- **Q:** Why does the outgoing runner start running before the handoff?
  **A:** So both are moving at speed when the baton is passed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Analyze sprint form on video.

*How to teach:* Video sprints and analyze form.

- **Q:** Describe correct sprint arm action.
  **A:** Elbows bent about 90°, swing forward and back (not across), hands relaxed, from cheek to hip pocket.

**Assessment:** 50m times and video analysis.

**Answer key:** 50 m times: best of three recorded to 0.1 s. Video analysis proficient: notes body lean in drive phase, knee lift, ball-of-foot contact, arm swing front-to-back at 90°, relaxed face/shoulders; and one specific correction.

## Health Education 7: Identity and family

**Prepare:** Make 20 values cards (honesty, family, faith, friendship, health, achievement, kindness, creativity, fairness, fun, courage, loyalty, independence, learning, responsibility, respect, adventure, generosity, humor, service). Poster paper for identity map. Schedule a 20-minute family meeting.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking values are just rules from parents. Values are what you personally believe is important; they guide your choices even when no one is watching.
- Assuming identity is fixed. Identity grows and changes through experiences.

### Day 1 (Monday): Values
*Student:* Rank 20 values cards and identify your top five.

*How to teach:* Your child ranks the cards and selects the top five, explaining each.

- **Q:** What are values?
  **A:** Beliefs about what is important that guide your decisions and actions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Identity map
*Student:* Create an identity map with interests, culture and goals.

*How to teach:* Create an identity map with interests, culture, roles, strengths and goals.

- **Q:** What parts make up a person's identity?
  **A:** Interests, culture/heritage, family, beliefs and values, roles, strengths and goals.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family roles
*Student:* Discuss family responsibilities and how they change in adolescence.

*How to teach:* Discuss how family responsibilities change in adolescence; list current and new jobs.

- **Q:** Name two new responsibilities a teen can take on.
  **A:** Cooking a meal, laundry, watching younger siblings, managing own schedule, yard work.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family meeting
*Student:* Hold a family meeting practicing respectful communication.

*How to teach:* Hold a family meeting with an agenda, using I-messages and active listening.

- **Q:** What makes a family meeting respectful?
  **A:** Everyone gets to speak without interruption, uses I-messages, listens, and works toward a fair decision.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a reflection on how values guide decisions.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a reflection on how values guide decisions; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Give an example of a value guiding a decision.
  **A:** If you value honesty, you tell a parent you broke something rather than hiding it.

**Assessment:** Identity map and reflection.

**Answer key:** Identity map: includes interests, culture/heritage, family, values, strengths and goals with at least 2 items each. Reflection rubric: 4 = names top values, explains with a specific example how a value guided (or should guide) a real decision, and connects to future goals; 3 = example but little reflection; 2 = lists values only; 1 = incomplete.
