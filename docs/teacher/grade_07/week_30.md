# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 30

## English Language Arts 7: Roll of Thunder, chapters 10–12

**Prepare:** Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry; whole-novel test (prepare items from the key). Parental preview: Chapter 11 includes a mob attack on T.J. and the threat of lynching; Chapter 12 ends with T.J. facing a likely death sentence. Plan time to talk afterward.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Papa sets the fire by accident or that it was lightning only. The text leads readers (and finally Cassie) to understand Papa set it to stop the mob.
- Seeing the ending as 'happy because the land is saved.' It is bittersweet: the land is saved at the cost of crops, and T.J.'s fate is grim.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 10
*Student:* Read Chapter 10 and explain how the Logans try to keep their land.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 10. Your child explains how the Logans try to keep their land. Check before moving on: ask "How does Harlan Granger pressure the Logans?" Look for: He has the bank call in the mortgage note early.

- **Q:** How does Harlan Granger pressure the Logans?
  **A:** He has the bank call in the mortgage note early.
- **Q:** How do they pay it?
  **A:** Uncle Hammer sells his car to pay the note.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 11
*Student:* Read Chapter 11 and analyze the night T.J. is attacked and Papa's decision.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 11. Your child analyzes the night T.J. is attacked and Papa's decision. Check before moving on: ask "What happened at the Barnett Mercantile?" Look for: T.J. went with R.W. and Melvin Simms to steal a pistol; the Simms brothers attacked Mr. Barnett and blamed T.J.

- **Q:** What happened at the Barnett Mercantile?
  **A:** T.J. went with R.W. and Melvin Simms to steal a pistol; the Simms brothers attacked Mr. Barnett and blamed T.J.
- **Q:** What does Papa do to stop the mob?
  **A:** He sets fire to his own cotton field so everyone, white and Black, must fight the fire, which stops the lynching.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 12
*Student:* Read Chapter 12 and explain what Cassie understands at the end that she did not before.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 12. Your child explains what Cassie understands at the end that she did not before. Check before moving on: ask "What does Cassie realize?" Look for: That Papa set the fire, that T.J. will likely die, and she cries for T.J. and for the land.

- **Q:** What does Cassie realize?
  **A:** That Papa set the fire, that T.J. will likely die, and she cries for T.J. and for the land.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Objective summary
*Student:* Write an objective summary of the novel and a theme statement.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an objective summary of the novel and a theme statement. Check before moving on: ask "Theme example?" Look for: Family strength and owning land give people dignity and the power to resist injustice, though at great cost.

- **Q:** Theme example?
  **A:** Family strength and owning land give people dignity and the power to resist injustice, though at great cost.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a whole-novel test with multiple choice and two constructed responses.

*How to teach:* Give the whole-novel test with multiple choice and two constructed responses. Check before moving on: ask "What does the title refer to?" Look for: A spiritual/song the family sings, 'Roll of thunder, hear my cry,' expressing defiance and endurance.

- **Q:** What does the title refer to?
  **A:** A spiritual/song the family sings, 'Roll of thunder, hear my cry,' expressing defiance and endurance.

**Assessment:** Novel test.

**Answer key:** Novel test multiple choice: Cassie narrator; 1933 Mississippi; textbook; bus ditch; Barnett Mercantile; fig tree; Mama fired; Papa's leg; Hammer sells car; Papa's fire. CR1: theme with 3 pieces of evidence. CR2: how Cassie changes (from not understanding to recognizing the danger and cost of injustice).

## Mathematics 7: Drawing triangles

**Prepare:** Straws, scissors, ruler, protractor, compass.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any three lengths form a triangle. The two shorter sides must add to more than the longest.
- Thinking three angles determine one triangle. They fix the shape, not the size.

### Day 1 (Monday): Building with straws
*Student:* Cut straws to given lengths and test which sets of three form a triangle; state the triangle inequality.

*How to teach:* Cut straw sets (e.g., 3-4-5, 3-4-8, 4-4-8, 5-5-5). Your child tests each and states the triangle inequality. Check before moving on: ask "3, 4, 8 cm?" Look for: No: 3 + 4 = 7 < 8.

- **Q:** 3, 4, 8 cm?
  **A:** No: 3 + 4 = 7 < 8.
- **Q:** 4, 4, 8?
  **A:** No: 4 + 4 = 8 collapses flat.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Angle conditions
*Student:* Try to draw triangles with angles 50, 60, 70 and note that many sizes exist (not unique).

*How to teach:* Your child draws triangles with angles 50°, 60°, 70° of different sizes. Check before moving on: ask "Are they the same?" Look for: Same shape (scaled copies), different sizes — not unique.

- **Q:** Are they the same?
  **A:** Same shape (scaled copies), different sizes — not unique.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Side-angle-side
*Student:* Construct triangles from two sides and the included angle using ruler and protractor and compare with a sibling's copy.

*How to teach:* Model SAS with ruler and protractor. Your child constructs and compares with someone else's. Check before moving on: ask "Sides 5 and 7 cm with 40° between: unique?" Look for: Yes — SAS gives one triangle.

- **Q:** Sides 5 and 7 cm with 40° between: unique?
  **A:** Yes — SAS gives one triangle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unique or not?
*Student:* Classify 8 sets of conditions as giving no triangle, one unique triangle, or many triangles.

*How to teach:* Your child classifies 8 condition sets. Check before moving on: ask "Angles 90°, 60°, 40°?" Look for: No triangle — sum is 190°.

- **Q:** Angles 90°, 60°, 40°?
  **A:** No triangle — sum is 190°.
- **Q:** Sides 6, 8, 10?
  **A:** Exactly one triangle.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on triangle constructions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Third side of a triangle with sides 5 and 9 must be between…?" Look for: 4 and 14.

- **Q:** Third side of a triangle with sides 5 and 9 must be between…?
  **A:** 4 and 14.

**Assessment:** Construction quiz and straw-lab table.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 2, 3, 6: no triangle. 2) 5, 6, 7: one. 3) Angles 30, 60, 90: many (any size). 4) Two sides + included angle: one. 5) Angles 100, 100, x: none. Straw table: correct yes/no for every set.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Changes at many scales and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Poster paper; toothpicks, mini marshmallows, gelatin pan (shake table); landform research materials.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking big changes need big events.
- Thinking tall buildings are always less safe.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scale chart
*Student:* Create a chart of Earth processes from fast/small (landslide) to slow/large (mountain building).

*How to teach:* Brainstorm Earth processes together. Your child makes a chart placing each on axes of time (seconds to millions of years) and size (local to continental). Check before moving on: ask "Example?" Look for: Landslide (minutes), river canyon (millions of years), mountain building (tens of millions).

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Landslide (minutes), river canyon (millions of years), mountain building (tens of millions).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Earthquake-safe design
*Student:* Build a structure from toothpicks and marshmallows and test it on a gelatin 'shake table'.

*How to teach:* Make gelatin in a pan the night before. Your child builds a toothpick-and-marshmallow structure, tests it by shaking the pan, then improves it with triangles or a wider base and retests. Check before moving on: ask "Design features that help?" Look for: Triangles (bracing), wide base, low center of mass.

- **Q:** Design features that help?
  **A:** Triangles (bracing), wide base, low center of mass.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story of a landform
*Student:* Write the geologic history of a California landform (e.g., Yosemite, Death Valley) across time scales.

*How to teach:* Your child picks a California landform and writes its geologic history in order, naming processes at different time scales. Check that each step names a process (deposition, uplift, erosion, glaciation). Check before moving on: ask "Yosemite example?" Look for: Granite formed underground, uplifted, eroded by rivers, carved by glaciers.

- **Q:** Yosemite example?
  **A:** Granite formed underground, uplifted, eroded by rivers, carved by glaciers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Review unit 6 concepts with a practice set.

*How to teach:* Your child works a unit 6 practice set; review answers together and re-teach any missed topic. Check before moving on: ask "What is a transform boundary?" Look for: Plates slide past.

- **Q:** What is a transform boundary?
  **A:** Plates slide past.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 6 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 6 test independently. Check before moving on: ask "Oldest layer?" Look for: Bottom.

- **Q:** Oldest layer?
  **A:** Bottom.

**Assessment:** Landform history and unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Landform history rubric: sequence at multiple time scales. Unit 6 test: relative dating, time scale, fossils, plate tectonics, California geology.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Scientific Revolution

**Prepare:** Flashlight or lamp, balls; Galileo's Starry Messenger excerpt; objects of different masses.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster. Without air resistance, they fall at the same rate.
- Thinking the Church opposed all science. It supported much astronomy but objected to Galileo's challenge.

### Day 1 (Monday): Roots
*Student:* Trace ideas from Greek, Muslim and Renaissance scholars that set up the Scientific Revolution.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a chain showing Greek, Muslim and Renaissance contributions leading to the Scientific Revolution. Check before moving on: ask "Contributions?" Look for: Greek observation, Muslim scholars (Ibn al-Haytham's optics), Renaissance questioning.

- **Q:** Contributions?
  **A:** Greek observation, Muslim scholars (Ibn al-Haytham's optics), Renaissance questioning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Copernicus and Kepler
*Student:* Model geocentric and heliocentric systems with a lamp and balls and explain Kepler's elliptical orbits.

*How to teach:* Model geocentric and heliocentric systems. Check before moving on: ask "Who proposed heliocentric model?" Look for: Copernicus (1543).

- **Q:** Who proposed heliocentric model?
  **A:** Copernicus (1543).
- **Q:** Kepler's discovery?
  **A:** Planets move in elliptical orbits.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Galileo
*Student:* Read Galileo's Starry Messenger observations of Jupiter's moons and explain the conflict with the Church.

*How to teach:* Read Galileo's observations. Check before moving on: ask "What did Galileo see?" Look for: Four moons orbiting Jupiter, mountains on the Moon.

- **Q:** What did Galileo see?
  **A:** Four moons orbiting Jupiter, mountains on the Moon.
- **Q:** Result?
  **A:** Tried in 1633, forced to recant, house arrest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Newton
*Student:* Drop objects of different masses and explain Newton's law of universal gravitation.

*How to teach:* Drop objects of different masses from the same height together. Your child observes and explains Newton's law of universal gravitation. Check before moving on: ask "Newton's law of universal gravitation?" Look for: Every mass attracts every other mass.

- **Q:** Newton's law of universal gravitation?
  **A:** Every mass attracts every other mass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the Scientific Revolution.

*How to teach:* Give the Scientific Revolution quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Newton's major work?" Look for: Principia (1687).

- **Q:** Newton's major work?
  **A:** Principia (1687).

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: geocentric vs heliocentric, Copernicus, Kepler, Galileo, Newton, roots.

## Spanish 1A: ¿Adónde vas? The verb ir and question words

**Prepare:** Weekly planner. Question words: ¿Qué?, ¿Quién?, ¿Dónde?, ¿Adónde?, ¿Cuándo?, ¿Con quién?, ¿Por qué?, ¿Cómo?, ¿Cuántos?

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'a el parque.' a + el contracts to al: Voy al parque (a la is not contracted: Voy a la playa).
- Using ¿Dónde? with ir. With motion use ¿Adónde vas?

### Day 1 (Monday): Ir
*Student:* Learn voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van and the contraction a + el = al.

*How to teach:* Teach ir forms and a + el = al.

- **Q:** Conjugate ir for nosotros.
  **A:** vamos.
- **Q:** Complete: Mis padres van ___ (a + el) cine.
  **A:** al.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Question words
*Student:* Review ¿Qué? ¿Quién? ¿Dónde? ¿Adónde? ¿Cuándo? ¿Con quién? ¿Por qué? and write 10 questions about weekend plans.

*How to teach:* Review question words; write 10 questions.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Who do you go to the movies with?'
  **A:** ¿Con quién vas al cine?
- **Q:** What's the difference between ¿Dónde? and ¿Adónde?
  **A:** ¿Dónde? = where (location); ¿Adónde? = to where (destination with motion).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): My week
*Student:* Fill a planner with where you go each day and write 7 sentences with ir and time expressions.

*How to teach:* Fill a planner; write 7 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'On Saturdays I go to the gym.'
  **A:** Los sábados voy al gimnasio.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a family member about where they go during the week and write a short report.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member; write a report.

- **Q:** Report: 'My mom goes to the supermarket on Fridays.'
  **A:** Mi mamá va al supermercado los viernes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an ir and question words quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the ir and question words quiz.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Adónde vas mañana? (library)
  **A:** Mañana voy a la biblioteca.

**Assessment:** Ir and question words quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: ir — voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van. Samples: I go to the park → Voy al parque; We go to the beach on weekends → Vamos a la playa los fines de semana; Where are you going? → ¿Adónde vas?; With whom do you go? → ¿Con quién vas? Question words matching. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Stop-motion animation planning

**Prepare:** Clay or LEGO, a free stop-motion app (e.g., Stop Motion Studio), phone stand, storyboard template. Find a clip of Wallace & Gromit or a classic stop-motion short.

**Watch for:**
- Moving characters too much between frames. Small moves create smooth motion.
- Shooting near a window. Changing daylight causes flicker; use steady lamp light.

### Day 1 (Monday): Study
*Student:* Watch short clips of stop-motion and analyze how frames create motion.

*How to teach:* Watch stop-motion clips; discuss how still frames create motion (persistence of vision).

- **Q:** How does stop-motion create the illusion of movement?
  **A:** Many still photos, each with a tiny change, play quickly in sequence so the brain sees continuous motion.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test
*Student:* Make a 2-second test animation of a ball bouncing at 12 frames per second.

*How to teach:* Make a 2-second ball bounce at 12 fps.

- **Q:** How many frames do you need for 2 seconds at 12 fps?
  **A:** 24 frames.
- **Q:** Why are frames closer together at the top of the bounce?
  **A:** The ball slows down at the top, so it moves less between frames.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Storyboard
*Student:* Storyboard a 30-second story with beginning, middle and end.

*How to teach:* Storyboard a 30-second story.

- **Q:** How many frames for 30 seconds at 12 fps?
  **A:** 360 frames.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Characters
*Student:* Build characters from clay or LEGO.

*How to teach:* Build characters from clay or LEGO.

- **Q:** Why add a wire armature to clay characters?
  **A:** It supports the figure and holds poses between frames.

### Day 5 (Friday): Set
*Student:* Build a set and plan lighting to avoid flicker.

*How to teach:* Build a set and plan lighting.

- **Q:** What causes flicker in stop-motion?
  **A:** Changing light (sunlight, shadows) or camera exposure between frames.

**Assessment:** Storyboard and test animation.

**Answer key:** Test animation: ball bounce of about 24 frames (2 s × 12 fps) with spacing showing slow at top, fast near bottom and squash on impact. Storyboard rubric: 6–9 panels with beginning, middle, end; camera angle and action notes; estimated frames (30 s × 12 fps = 360 frames).

## Physical Education 7: Track: distance and pacing

**Prepare:** Track (400 m per lap) or measured route, stopwatch. Pull baseline mile/PACER from week 1.

**Watch for:**
- Starting too fast. Even pace (or negative split) produces a faster overall time.
- Thinking every run must be hard. Easy, conversational runs build the aerobic base.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pacing
*Student:* Run 4 × 400m at an even pace, recording splits.

*How to teach:* Run 4 × 400 m at an even pace with 2 minutes rest; record each split.

- **Q:** Your 400 m splits are 2:10, 2:12, 2:08, 2:10. What is the average?
  **A:** 2:10 (130 seconds average).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fartlek
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute fartlek run.

*How to teach:* Fartlek ('speed play'): 20 minutes mixing jogging with faster bursts to landmarks.

- **Q:** What is fartlek training?
  **A:** Swedish for 'speed play' — continuous running mixing fast and slow segments.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Long run
*Student:* Run or run/walk 2 miles at a conversational pace.

*How to teach:* Run/walk 2 miles at a conversational pace (can talk in full sentences).

- **Q:** Why run long runs slowly?
  **A:** Easy pace builds aerobic endurance with less injury risk.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Timed mile
*Student:* Run a timed mile and compare to baseline.

*How to teach:* Run a timed mile with an even-pace plan; compare to baseline.

- **Q:** What is a negative split?
  **A:** Running the second half faster than the first half.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Graph mile progress and reflect on your SMART goal.

*How to teach:* Graph mile progress and reflect on the SMART goal.

- **Q:** Baseline mile 11:20, now 10:05. How much faster?
  **A:** 1 minute 15 seconds (75 seconds).

**Assessment:** Mile time comparison.

**Answer key:** Mile comparison: baseline vs current time with difference in seconds (e.g., 10:30 → 9:45 = 45 s faster). Proficient reflection explains pacing strategy and whether the SMART goal was met, with next step.

## Health Education 7: Unit 5 review and assessment

**Prepare:** Make review cards for weeks 25–29. Write the unit 5 test. Pull out the sleep log and hygiene routine from week 26.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up hormone/gland pairs. Review: pituitary = master gland; testes = testosterone; ovaries = estrogen/progesterone; thyroid = metabolism.
- Thinking boundaries don't apply to family or close friends. Everyone has the right to boundaries, with anyone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review 1
*Student:* Review changes, hygiene and sleep concepts.

*How to teach:* Review changes, hygiene and sleep with flash cards.

- **Q:** Name one physical, one emotional and one social change of adolescence.
  **A:** Physical: growth spurt; emotional: mood swings; social: more time with friends/independence.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review 2
*Student:* Review boundaries and communication skills.

*How to teach:* Review boundaries and communication with quick role-plays.

- **Q:** Give an assertive boundary statement.
  **A:** 'I don't want to talk about that. Please stop.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Goal check
*Student:* Check sleep and hygiene goals.

*How to teach:* Your child checks progress on sleep and hygiene goals and adjusts.

- **Q:** If you averaged 7.5 hours and your goal was 9, what change could help?
  **A:** Move bedtime earlier by 30–60 minutes, set a screen curfew, keep a consistent wake time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 5 test (see key).

- **Q:** What is the endocrine system?
  **A:** Glands that release hormones into the blood to control growth, metabolism and development.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Discuss the unit with a parent.

*How to teach:* Talk with your child about what they learned and any lingering questions.

- **Q:** What's one thing you learned that you'll use?
  **A:** Open answer — look for a specific skill such as a sleep routine or boundary statement.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test (suggested): 1) puberty is caused by hormones 2) timing varies (about ages 8–14 start) 3) teens need 8–10 hours of sleep 4) screens/blue light reduce melatonin 5) acne caused by oil, clogged pores, bacteria, hormones 6) brush twice, floss once daily 7) pituitary = master gland 8) three boundary types (physical, emotional, digital) 9) consent = freely given, can be withdrawn 10) two valid resources. 9–10 advanced, 7–8 proficient.
