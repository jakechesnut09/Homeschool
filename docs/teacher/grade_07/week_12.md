# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 12

## English Language Arts 7: Evaluating arguments

**Prepare:** A CommonLit or newspaper op-ed on later school start times; a persuasive ad or speech video (1–3 minutes); rating chart (relevant? sufficient?).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any statistic is good evidence. It must be relevant to the claim and enough to support it.
- Confusing counterclaim with the author's claim.

### Day 1 (Monday): Anatomy of an argument
*Student:* Read an op-ed on a school-start-time debate and label claim, reasons, evidence and counterclaim.

*How to teach:* Read the op-ed aloud. Model labeling the claim, then your child labels reasons, evidence and the counterclaim in different colors. Ask your child to state the claim in one sentence.

- **Q:** What is a counterclaim?
  **A:** The opposing view the author acknowledges (and usually rebuts).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sound reasoning?
*Student:* Evaluate whether each piece of evidence in the op-ed is relevant and sufficient using a rating chart.

*How to teach:* Make a chart with each piece of evidence and columns 'relevant?' and 'sufficient?'. Your child rates each one and explains, e.g., a single anecdote is weak while a study of thousands of students is strong. Ask which piece of evidence is most convincing.

- **Q:** Is 'my cousin is always tired' sufficient evidence for later start times?
  **A:** No — it is one anecdote; studies of many students would be stronger.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Author's point of view
*Student:* Analyze how the author distinguishes her view from opponents' using specific phrases.

*How to teach:* Your child finds phrases where the author introduces and responds to opponents ('Critics claim…', 'Yet…', 'However…') and explains how the author makes her own view sound more reasonable. Ask: did she treat opponents fairly? Check before moving on: ask "Why acknowledge opponents?" Look for: It shows fairness and lets the author rebut them, making the argument stronger.

- **Q:** Why acknowledge opponents?
  **A:** It shows fairness and lets the author rebut them, making the argument stronger.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Listening to arguments
*Student:* Watch a short persuasive speech or ad and delineate its claims, noting any bandwagon or loaded-language fallacies.

*How to teach:* Watch a 1–3 minute ad or speech twice. Your child lists each claim and any bandwagon appeals or loaded words, then rates the reasoning. Discuss how the techniques try to persuade without real evidence.

- **Q:** What is bandwagon?
  **A:** Arguing you should do something because everyone else does.
- **Q:** Loaded language?
  **A:** Emotionally charged words meant to sway feelings rather than prove a point.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete an argument-evaluation quiz on a new text.

*How to teach:* Give a new argument text with the quiz items in the key. Your child works independently for 30 minutes. Review answers together, focusing on evidence evaluation.

- **Q:** What makes reasoning sound?
  **A:** Evidence is relevant, sufficient and logically connected to the claim.

**Assessment:** Argument evaluation chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (for any argument text): identify claim (one sentence), two reasons, one piece of evidence and whether it is relevant/sufficient, counterclaim and rebuttal, and one rhetorical technique. Proficient = all identified correctly with quoted text. Chart: each piece of evidence rated with a reason.

## Mathematics 7: Composite circular figures & unit test

**Prepare:** Grid paper, calculator, compass.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the straight edge (diameter) when finding a semicircle's perimeter.
- Rounding π too early. Keep answers in terms of π until the last step.

### Day 1 (Monday): Semicircles and quarters
*Student:* Find perimeter and area of semicircles and quarter circles, including the straight edges.

*How to teach:* Model a semicircle with d = 10: arc = 5π, perimeter = 5π + 10, area = 12.5π. Your child solves semicircle and quarter-circle problems. Check before moving on: ask "Semicircle d = 10. Perimeter and area?" Look for: 5π + 10 ≈ 25.71; 12.5π ≈ 39.27.

- **Q:** Semicircle d = 10. Perimeter and area?
  **A:** 5π + 10 ≈ 25.71; 12.5π ≈ 39.27.
- **Q:** Quarter circle r = 4. Area and perimeter?
  **A:** 4π ≈ 12.57; 2π + 8 ≈ 14.28.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Composite figures
*Student:* Find areas of a running track shape and a window shaped like a rectangle topped with a semicircle.

*How to teach:* Sketch a window: 4 ft wide rectangle, 6 ft tall, with a semicircle on top. Your child decomposes and adds areas, then does the track (rectangle + two semicircles). Check before moving on: ask "Window area?" Look for: 24 + 2π ≈ 30.28 ft².

- **Q:** Window area?
  **A:** 24 + 2π ≈ 30.28 ft².
- **Q:** Track: rectangle 100 m by 60 m with semicircles on the 60 m ends. Area?
  **A:** 6,000 + 900π ≈ 8,827 m².

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Expressing in terms of pi
*Student:* Write answers both as exact expressions with pi and as decimal approximations and compare.

*How to teach:* Your child writes each answer as an exact π expression and a decimal, and discusses which is more precise. Check before moving on: ask "Circle r = 6: exact area and approximation?" Look for: 36π, ≈ 113.1.

- **Q:** Circle r = 6: exact area and approximation?
  **A:** 36π, ≈ 113.1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Solve a review set of 10 problems on circumference, area and composite shapes.

*How to teach:* Review set: work one composite problem together, rest independently. Check before moving on: ask "Circumference of a circle with area 49π?" Look for: r = 7, C = 14π ≈ 43.98.

- **Q:** Circumference of a circle with area 49π?
  **A:** r = 7, C = 14π ≈ 43.98.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the unit 3 test on circles.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 3 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Area of a semicircle with r = 2?" Look for: 2π ≈ 6.28.

- **Q:** Area of a semicircle with r = 2?
  **A:** 2π ≈ 6.28.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample answers (π ≈ 3.14): 1) C of d = 12: 37.68. 2) A of r = 9: 254.34. 3) Semicircle r = 5 perimeter: 15.7 + 10 = 25.7. 4) Quarter circle r = 10 area: 78.5. 5) Rectangle 8×4 + semicircle d = 4 on top: 32 + 6.28 = 38.28. 6) r from C = 50.24: r = 8. 7) Area vs circumference choice explained correctly.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Cellular respiration

**Prepare:** Dry yeast, sugar, warm water, 3 bottles, 3 balloons, string and ruler; red cabbage indicator (boil chopped red cabbage, cool, strain — adult) or bromothymol blue; straws.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only animals do respiration.
- Confusing breathing with cellular respiration. Breathing moves air; cellular respiration releases energy from food in cells.

### Day 1 (Monday): Yeast balloons
*Student:* Feed yeast different amounts of sugar in bottles topped with balloons and measure balloon circumference every 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Set up bottles with 0, 1 and 2 tbsp sugar and yeast in warm water, balloons on top. Your child measures circumference every 10 minutes. Check before moving on: ask "Which balloon inflates most?" Look for: The one with the most sugar — more food for respiration means more CO2.

- **Q:** Which balloon inflates most?
  **A:** The one with the most sugar — more food for respiration means more CO2.
- **Q:** What gas fills the balloon?
  **A:** Carbon dioxide.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Breath test
*Student:* Blow through a straw into red-cabbage indicator before and after exercise and compare color changes as evidence of CO2.

*How to teach:* Your child blows gently through a straw into indicator at rest, then after 1 minute of jumping jacks, and compares. Check before moving on: ask "What color change shows CO2?" Look for: Red cabbage shifts toward pink/red (more acidic); bromothymol blue turns green to yellow.

- **Q:** What color change shows CO2?
  **A:** Red cabbage shifts toward pink/red (more acidic); bromothymol blue turns green to yellow.
- **Q:** Why faster after exercise?
  **A:** Muscles do more respiration and release more CO2.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Respiration equation
*Student:* Compare photosynthesis and respiration equations and draw a diagram of how they are linked.

*How to teach:* Write both equations. Your child diagrams how they cycle. Check before moving on: ask "Respiration equation?" Look for: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy.

- **Q:** Respiration equation?
  **A:** C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy.
- **Q:** How are they linked?
  **A:** Products of one are reactants of the other.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Food to body
*Student:* Model how digested food molecules are rearranged to build body tissue or release energy.

*How to teach:* Model food broken into smaller molecules that cells rebuild. Check before moving on: ask "What happens to proteins you eat?" Look for: Broken into amino acids then rebuilt into your body's proteins.

- **Q:** What happens to proteins you eat?
  **A:** Broken into amino acids then rebuilt into your body's proteins.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on respiration and its relationship to photosynthesis.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Where does cellular respiration occur?" Look for: In the mitochondria of cells.

- **Q:** Where does cellular respiration occur?
  **A:** In the mitochondria of cells.

**Assessment:** Yeast data table and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Respiration equation. 2) Plants do photosynthesis AND respiration. 3) CO2 test with indicator. 4) Yeast with more sugar made more CO2. 5) Mitochondria. Yeast data table: circumference readings with units.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Mali and the spread of Islam

**Prepare:** Retelling of the Epic of Sundiata; Catalan Atlas image (1375); readings on Timbuktu; Ibn Battuta's account of Mali.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Mansa Musa's gift-giving only helped Cairo. Flooding Cairo with gold lowered its value for years.
- Thinking Timbuktu was a myth or tiny. It was a major center of Islamic scholarship with thousands of manuscripts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sundiata
*Student:* Read a retelling of the Epic of Sundiata and identify the qualities of a leader it celebrates.

*How to teach:* Read the epic retelling. Your child lists Sundiata's leadership qualities. Check before moving on: ask "What challenge did Sundiata overcome as a child?" Look for: He could not walk, but grew strong and became a great leader.

- **Q:** What challenge did Sundiata overcome as a child?
  **A:** He could not walk, but grew strong and became a great leader.
- **Q:** What did he achieve?
  **A:** Defeated Sumanguru at Kirina (c. 1235) and founded the Mali Empire.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mansa Musa's hajj
*Student:* Analyze the Catalan Atlas image of Mansa Musa and explain how his 1324 hajj affected Cairo's economy.

*How to teach:* Show the Catalan Atlas. Your child explains what it shows and the effect of the hajj on Cairo. Check before moving on: ask "What does the atlas show?" Look for: Mansa Musa on a throne holding a gold nugget — Europeans saw Mali as fabulously rich.

- **Q:** What does the atlas show?
  **A:** Mansa Musa on a throne holding a gold nugget — Europeans saw Mali as fabulously rich.
- **Q:** Effect on Cairo?
  **A:** He gave away so much gold in 1324 that its value fell in Cairo for years.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timbuktu
*Student:* Research Timbuktu's mosques and the Sankore scholars and its manuscript tradition.

*How to teach:* Your child researches Timbuktu. Check before moving on: ask "What made Timbuktu famous?" Look for: Sankore and Djinguereber mosques, scholars and manuscripts on law, science and religion.

- **Q:** What made Timbuktu famous?
  **A:** Sankore and Djinguereber mosques, scholars and manuscripts on law, science and religion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ibn Battuta in Mali
*Student:* Read Ibn Battuta's account of Mali and evaluate what he admired and criticized.

*How to teach:* Read Ibn Battuta's account. Your child lists admired and criticized things. Check before moving on: ask "Admired?" Look for: Safety on roads, justice, devotion to prayer.

- **Q:** Admired?
  **A:** Safety on roads, justice, devotion to prayer.
- **Q:** Criticized?
  **A:** Some customs, like women servants' dress and the ruler's small gift to him.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on how trade spread Islam and Arabic to West Africa.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph on how trade spread Islam and Arabic. Check before moving on: ask "Example evidence?" Look for: Muslim merchants traveled trans-Saharan routes; rulers like Mansa Musa adopted Islam; Arabic became the language of scholarship in Timbuktu.

- **Q:** Example evidence?
  **A:** Muslim merchants traveled trans-Saharan routes; rulers like Mansa Musa adopted Islam; Arabic became the language of scholarship in Timbuktu.

**Assessment:** Paragraph and source analysis.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate. Source analysis: identifies the author's perspective (Ibn Battuta as an outside Muslim scholar).

## Spanish 1A: ¿Qué te gusta hacer? Activities and gustar

**Prepare:** Activity picture cards for 18 infinitives (nadar, bailar, cantar, dibujar, leer, escribir, montar en bicicleta, jugar videojuegos, practicar deportes, correr, tocar la guitarra, usar la computadora, ver la tele, escuchar música, patinar, cocinar, hablar por teléfono, pasar tiempo con amigos). A teen hobby podcast or video in Spanish (e.g., from a textbook site or YouTube).

**Watch for:**
- Conjugating the activity after gustar: 'Me gusta nado.' Use the infinitive: Me gusta nadar.
- Saying 'Yo gusto…' Use 'Me gusta…' (literally, it pleases me).

### Day 1 (Monday): Actividades
*Student:* Learn 18 activity infinitives with picture cards and act them out for a parent to guess.

*How to teach:* Learn 18 activities; act them out for guessing.

- **Q:** What does 'montar en bicicleta' mean?
  **A:** To ride a bike.
- **Q:** How do you say 'to swim'?
  **A:** nadar.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Me gusta / no me gusta
*Student:* Write 10 sentences: Me gusta nadar, No me gusta cantar, Me gusta más leer.

*How to teach:* Write 10 sentences with me gusta / no me gusta / me gusta más.

- **Q:** Write: 'I like to read but I don't like to write.'
  **A:** Me gusta leer pero no me gusta escribir.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): ¿Te gusta...?
*Student:* Ask a family member ten ¿Te gusta...? questions and record answers in a chart.

*How to teach:* Ask a family member 10 ¿Te gusta…? questions; record answers.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Te gusta cocinar? (yes)
  **A:** Sí, me gusta cocinar.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Listening
*Student:* Listen to two teens on a Spanish podcast or video describe hobbies and note each one's likes and dislikes.

*How to teach:* Listen to two teens; note likes and dislikes.

- **Q:** If you hear 'No me gusta nada correr,' what does it mean?
  **A:** I don't like running at all.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a vocabulary and gustar quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the vocabulary and gustar quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Do you like to play video games?'
  **A:** ¿Te gusta jugar videojuegos?

**Assessment:** Activities vocabulary and gustar quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: vocabulary matching (18 activities) and sentences. Key samples: I like to dance → Me gusta bailar; I don't like to sing → No me gusta cantar; Do you like to read? → ¿Te gusta leer?; I like to draw more → Me gusta más dibujar. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Listening: medieval to Renaissance music

**Prepare:** Find recordings: Gregorian chant, Hildegard von Bingen (e.g., 'O virtus Sapientiae'), a troubadour song, Palestrina's Missa Papae Marcelli (Kyrie), an English madrigal (e.g., Morley's 'Now Is the Month of Maying'), Japanese gagaku ('Etenraku') and kora music (e.g., Toumani Diabaté).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'medieval' music is all the same. Chant is monophonic and sacred; Renaissance polyphony layers independent voices; secular songs were about love and daily life.
- Calling chant 'harmony.' Chant is a single melody line (monophony), even with many singers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chant
*Student:* Listen to Gregorian chant and Hildegard von Bingen and describe texture and melody.

*How to teach:* Listen to chant and Hildegard; describe melody (smooth, stepwise), texture (one line) and rhythm (free).

- **Q:** What is monophony?
  **A:** A single melodic line with no accompaniment or harmony.
- **Q:** Who was Hildegard von Bingen?
  **A:** A 12th-century German abbess, writer and composer of sacred music.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Polyphony
*Student:* Listen to a Palestrina mass movement and identify the interweaving voices.

*How to teach:* Listen to Palestrina; raise a hand each time a new voice enters.

- **Q:** What is polyphony?
  **A:** Two or more independent melodic lines sounding at the same time.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Secular music
*Student:* Listen to a troubadour song and an English madrigal and compare to sacred music.

*How to teach:* Compare a troubadour song and a madrigal with sacred music.

- **Q:** How is a madrigal different from chant?
  **A:** A madrigal is secular, polyphonic, in the everyday language and about topics like love; chant is sacred, monophonic and in Latin.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Global connections
*Student:* Listen to Japanese gagaku and West African kora music and compare to European music of the same era.

*How to teach:* Listen to gagaku and kora music and compare.

- **Q:** What is the kora?
  **A:** A West African harp-lute with about 21 strings played by griots (storyteller-musicians).
- **Q:** What is gagaku?
  **A:** Ancient Japanese imperial court music played by wind, string and percussion ensembles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a listening log comparing three pieces using musical vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Write the listening log on three pieces.

- **Q:** Name one way all these traditions are similar.
  **A:** Example: music served religious or courtly purposes and was passed on by trained musicians/oral tradition.

**Assessment:** Listening log.

**Answer key:** Listening log rubric (1–4): for three pieces, names composer/tradition, time period, texture (monophony/polyphony), instruments or voices, language/purpose (sacred/secular), tempo/mood; compares using vocabulary. Key facts: Gregorian chant — monophonic, Latin, sacred, unaccompanied, free rhythm; Hildegard — 12th-century German abbess and composer; Palestrina — 16th-century Italian, polyphonic masses; madrigal — secular, polyphonic, vernacular (English/Italian) part-song; troubadours — medieval French poet-musicians singing of courtly love; gagaku — Japanese imperial court music; kora — 21-string harp-lute of West African griots.

## Physical Education 7: Volleyball: serving and game play

**Prepare:** Volleyball, net or rope, cones or tape to mark court zones (6 zones on one side). Explain court rotation with a diagram.

**Watch for:**
- Tossing the ball too high on the serve. Underhand: hold ball low and hit from the hand; overhand: toss slightly in front about arm's length.
- Thinking players rotate after every point. Teams rotate clockwise only when they win back the serve (side out).

### Day 1 (Monday): Underhand serve
*Student:* Practice underhand serves over a net, 20 attempts.

*How to teach:* Underhand serve: opposite foot forward, hold ball low in front, swing hitting arm like a pendulum, contact with heel of hand. 20 attempts; track makes.

- **Q:** Which foot is forward for a right-handed underhand serve?
  **A:** The left foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Overhand serve
*Student:* Try overhand serves and compare success rates.

*How to teach:* Overhand (float) serve: toss in front of hitting shoulder, elbow high, contact with heel of hand. Compare success rate with underhand.

- **Q:** Your underhand made 16/20 and overhand 9/20. What are the percentages?
  **A:** Underhand 80%, overhand 45%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rules
*Student:* Learn rotation and scoring and play 2-v-2 with three contacts.

*How to teach:* Teach rotation and scoring; play 2-v-2 with three contacts.

- **Q:** How many contacts is a team allowed on one side?
  **A:** Three (a block doesn't count in indoor rules).
- **Q:** When does a team rotate?
  **A:** When it wins the serve back from the other team (side out), rotating clockwise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play doubles volleyball games.

*How to teach:* Play doubles games; encourage bump-set-hit.

- **Q:** Why aim to use all three contacts?
  **A:** Passing to a setter then hitting creates a controlled, stronger attack.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a serving accuracy test into court zones.

*How to teach:* Run the serving accuracy test.

- **Q:** What is rally scoring?
  **A:** A point is scored on every rally regardless of which team served.

**Assessment:** Serving test and game observation.

**Answer key:** Serving test: 10 serves into marked zones. Score 1 point for in, 2 for the called zone. Proficient = 7+ serves in; advanced = 4+ zone hits. Game observation: uses three contacts (bump-set-hit), calls 'mine', rotates correctly. Rules key: rally scoring (point every rally), games to 25 win by 2 (15 in a deciding game), rotate clockwise on side out, ball may be played up to 3 times per side, same player cannot hit twice in a row.

## Health Education 7: Promoting positive climate; unit assessment

**Prepare:** Poster paper or a phone for a short video; stationery for gratitude letters. Write a unit 2 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing empathy with sympathy. Sympathy feels sorry for someone; empathy tries to understand how they feel from their point of view.
- Thinking advocacy means just telling people what to do. Effective advocacy uses facts, a clear message and a positive call to action.

### Day 1 (Monday): Empathy
*Student:* Practice perspective-taking with three scenarios.

*How to teach:* Read three short scenarios (new kid, friend who lost a game, sibling who is left out). Your child describes what each person might feel and what they need.

- **Q:** What is empathy?
  **A:** Understanding and sharing another person's feelings by imagining their point of view.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Advocacy campaign
*Student:* Design a kindness or anti-bullying campaign poster or video.

*How to teach:* Your child designs a kindness or anti-bullying poster/video for a specific audience with a call to action.

- **Q:** What is a call to action? Give one.
  **A:** A specific thing you want people to do, e.g., 'Invite someone new to sit with you this week.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Gratitude
*Student:* Start a gratitude practice and write three letters of thanks.

*How to teach:* Explain research linking gratitude to better mood. Your child writes three thank-you letters and starts a daily 'three good things' list.

- **Q:** How can practicing gratitude affect mental health?
  **A:** It increases positive emotions, reduces stress and improves relationships.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review unit 2 concepts and skills.

*How to teach:* Review unit 2 with a quick quiz game using vocabulary cards.

- **Q:** What is the difference between an upstander and a bystander?
  **A:** A bystander watches and does nothing; an upstander takes safe action to help.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the unit 2 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test (see key).

- **Q:** Name one way to promote inclusion in your community.
  **A:** Invite someone new, start a club open to all, speak up against exclusion, learn about others' cultures.

**Assessment:** Campaign product and unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Campaign rubric: 4 = clear positive message, a specific call to action (e.g., 'Sit with someone new'), accurate facts, aimed at a specific audience, creative design; 3 = clear message, weak call to action; 2 = unclear; 1 = incomplete. Unit 2 test (suggested): 1) resilience = bouncing back 2) three body signs of stress 3) two healthy coping strategies 4) 988 Lifeline 5) two signs of depression 6) I-message formula 7) four types of bullying (physical, verbal, social, cyber) 8) upstander actions 9) DECIDE steps 10) empathy definition. 9–10 advanced, 7–8 proficient.
