# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 35

## English Language Arts 7: Timed writing and year review

**Prepare:** Timed-writing prompt with two short sources; grammar review sheet; roots/figurative language review game; final exam.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping planning in timed writing. Spend 5 minutes outlining.
- Forgetting to cite both sources.

### Day 1 (Monday): Timed argument
*Student:* Write a 45-minute argument essay responding to a prompt with two short sources.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 45-minute argument essay responding to a prompt with two short sources. Check before moving on: ask "How to budget 45 minutes?" Look for: 5 plan, 35 write, 5 proofread.

- **Q:** How to budget 45 minutes?
  **A:** 5 plan, 35 write, 5 proofread.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Self-scoring
*Student:* Score the timed essay with the rubric and revise one paragraph.

*How to teach:* Your child scores the timed essay with the rubric and revises one paragraph. Check before moving on: ask "What to revise first?" Look for: Weakest rubric area.

- **Q:** What to revise first?
  **A:** Weakest rubric area.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of phrases, clauses, sentence types, modifiers and commas.

*How to teach:* Your child completes a mixed review of phrases, clauses, sentence types, modifiers and commas. Check before moving on: ask "Add commas if needed: 'It was a cold dark stormy night.'" Look for: 'It was a cold, dark, stormy night.' — coordinate adjectives pass the 'and' test (cold and dark and stormy).

- **Q:** Add commas if needed: 'It was a cold dark stormy night.'
  **A:** 'It was a cold, dark, stormy night.' — coordinate adjectives pass the 'and' test (cold and dark and stormy).
- **Q:** Fix the dangling modifier: 'Running for the bus, my backpack fell.'
  **A:** 'Running for the bus, I dropped my backpack.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word study review
*Student:* Review Greek/Latin roots and figurative language with a 40-item review game.

*How to teach:* Review Greek/Latin roots and figurative language with a 40-item review game. Check before moving on: ask "'bene' means?" Look for: Good.

- **Q:** 'bene' means?
  **A:** Good.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the year-end ELA exam (reading passages, grammar and a short written response).

*How to teach:* Give the year-end ELA exam: reading passages, grammar and a short written response. Check before moving on: ask "Theme vs topic?" Look for: Topic = subject; theme = message about it.

- **Q:** Theme vs topic?
  **A:** Topic = subject; theme = message about it.

**Assessment:** Timed essay and final exam.

**Answer key:** Timed essay rubric: claim, evidence from both sources, reasoning, organization, conventions. Final exam: reading passages scored by key; grammar items; short written response proficient with claim and two pieces of evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Sampling and comparing populations

**Prepare:** A long novel, random number generator, graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a large sample is unbiased. A large biased sample is still biased.
- Expecting every sample to give the same mean.

### Day 1 (Monday): Populations and samples
*Student:* Identify populations and samples in 8 survey scenarios and judge which are biased.

*How to teach:* Your child identifies population and sample in 8 scenarios and judges bias. Check before moving on: ask "Surveying soccer fans about the favorite sport — biased?" Look for: Yes, not representative.

- **Q:** Surveying soccer fans about the favorite sport — biased?
  **A:** Yes, not representative.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Random sampling
*Student:* Use random page numbers to sample word lengths in a novel and estimate the mean word length.

*How to teach:* Your child samples random pages and computes mean word length. Check before moving on: ask "Why random pages?" Look for: Every page has an equal chance, giving a representative sample.

- **Q:** Why random pages?
  **A:** Every page has an equal chance, giving a representative sample.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Variation in samples
*Student:* Take 5 random samples of 10 words, compare sample means and describe the variability.

*How to teach:* Your child takes 5 samples of 10 words and compares means. Check before moving on: ask "What do differing means show?" Look for: Sampling variability.

- **Q:** What do differing means show?
  **A:** Sampling variability.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing groups
*Student:* Compare two dot plots (e.g., heights of two plant groups) using means and MAD and decide if the difference is meaningful.

*How to teach:* Model MAD. Your child compares two plant groups. Check before moving on: ask "Means 12 and 18, MADs about 2. Meaningful?" Look for: Yes — difference is 3 MADs, more than 2, so it is meaningful.

- **Q:** Means 12 and 18, MADs about 2. Meaningful?
  **A:** Yes — difference is 3 MADs, more than 2, so it is meaningful.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on sampling and comparing populations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Population for a survey of 50 of 1,000 students?" Look for: All 1,000 students.

- **Q:** Population for a survey of 50 of 1,000 students?
  **A:** All 1,000 students.

**Assessment:** Sampling quiz and word-length sampling report.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Survey of 7th graders: population = all 7th graders at the school. 2) Random sample best. 3) Mean of 2, 4, 6, 8 = 5; MAD = 2. 4) Means differ by 3 MADs: meaningful. Report: proficient includes method, mean, variability.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Evaluating competing solutions for biodiversity

**Prepare:** Articles on wildlife crossings (e.g., Wallis Annenberg Wildlife Crossing on US-101) and fish ladders; decision matrix template.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the 'best' solution is the cheapest.
- Ignoring stakeholders.

### Day 1 (Monday): Case study
*Student:* Research solutions to protect a California species (e.g., wildlife crossings for mountain lions, fish ladders for salmon).

*How to teach:* Your child researches solutions for a California species. Check before moving on: ask "Why do mountain lions need crossings?" Look for: Freeways isolate populations, causing inbreeding and roadkill.

- **Q:** Why do mountain lions need crossings?
  **A:** Freeways isolate populations, causing inbreeding and roadkill.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decision matrix
*Student:* Rate three competing solutions on cost, effectiveness, community impact and ecosystem services.

*How to teach:* Your child rates three solutions in a decision matrix. Check before moving on: ask "Criteria?" Look for: Cost, effectiveness, community impact, ecosystem services.

- **Q:** Criteria?
  **A:** Cost, effectiveness, community impact, ecosystem services.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Filter redesign
*Student:* Redesign the water filter based on test data and compare versions.

*How to teach:* Your child redesigns the filter based on test data (changing one variable, such as more charcoal) and compares versions using the same muddy-water sample. Check before moving on: ask "What to compare?" Look for: Clarity and flow rate before and after.

- **Q:** What to compare?
  **A:** Clarity and flow rate before and after.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Stakeholder views
*Student:* Role-play a town meeting with a farmer, developer and biologist debating a solution.

*How to teach:* Assign roles (farmer, developer, biologist). Each prepares a 1-minute statement; your child plays the biologist and responds to concerns. Check before moving on: ask "Farmer's concern?" Look for: Land use and water access.

- **Q:** Farmer's concern?
  **A:** Land use and water access.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a recommendation letter to a city council supporting the best solution with evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a recommendation letter. Check before moving on: ask "Letter must include?" Look for: Recommended solution, evidence, response to concerns.

- **Q:** Letter must include?
  **A:** Recommended solution, evidence, response to concerns.

**Assessment:** Decision matrix and recommendation letter.

**Answer key:** Matrix: three solutions scored with justification. Letter rubric: clear recommendation, evidence from research, addresses trade-offs, formal tone.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: From Magna Carta to the Declaration of Independence

**Prepare:** Magna Carta, English Bill of Rights, Declaration of Independence (National Archives); five DBQ documents.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Declaration created the government. It declared independence; the Constitution created the government.
- Thinking democracy ideas came only from America.

### Day 1 (Monday): English Bill of Rights
*Student:* Read key clauses of the 1689 English Bill of Rights and compare with Magna Carta.

*How to teach:* Read key clauses of the 1689 English Bill of Rights. Your child compares them with Magna Carta in a chart. Check before moving on: ask "Key protections?" Look for: No taxes without Parliament, free elections, no cruel and unusual punishment.

- **Q:** Key protections?
  **A:** No taxes without Parliament, free elections, no cruel and unusual punishment.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Declaration of Independence
*Student:* Read the Declaration's preamble and find Locke's ideas.

*How to teach:* Read the Declaration's preamble. Your child highlights Locke's ideas. Check before moving on: ask "Locke's ideas?" Look for: Unalienable rights, consent of the governed, right to alter or abolish government.

- **Q:** Locke's ideas?
  **A:** Unalienable rights, consent of the governed, right to alter or abolish government.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): DBQ planning
*Student:* Plan an essay answering 'How did ideas from 1215 to 1776 shape democracy?' using 5 documents.

*How to teach:* Your child plans the DBQ essay using 5 documents: thesis, document groupings and outline. Check before moving on: ask "Thesis example?" Look for: Limits on rulers, protection of rights and consent of the governed built over 1215–1776.

- **Q:** Thesis example?
  **A:** Limits on rulers, protection of rights and consent of the governed built over 1215–1776.

### Day 4 (Thursday): DBQ writing
*Student:* Write the DBQ essay with a thesis and evidence from each document.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the DBQ essay with a thesis and evidence from each document. Check before moving on: ask "Each paragraph?" Look for: Topic sentence, document evidence, explanation.

- **Q:** Each paragraph?
  **A:** Topic sentence, document evidence, explanation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Revise and submit the DBQ essay.

*How to teach:* Your child revises and submits the DBQ essay. Check before moving on: ask "What to check?" Look for: Thesis, all documents used, citations.

- **Q:** What to check?
  **A:** Thesis, all documents used, citations.

**Assessment:** DBQ essay.

**Answer key:** DBQ rubric: thesis, evidence from all 5 documents, chronological development, explanation, conventions.

## Spanish 1A: Spanish 1A review

**Prepare:** All unit tests, flashcards, Conjuguemos, three short texts (menu, family profile, town brochure).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up ser and estar under pressure. Remember: ser = origin, description, identity; estar = location and feelings.
- Forgetting verb endings by type. Review -ar, -er, -ir charts side by side.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: units 1–5
*Student:* Review greetings, calendar, ser, gustar, school, and -ar verbs with a practice test.

*How to teach:* Review units 1–5 with a practice test.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Today is Friday, it's 10:30 and it's sunny.'
  **A:** Hoy es viernes, son las diez y media y hace sol.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: units 6–9
*Student:* Review food, -er/-ir verbs, family, tener, estar, ir, and ir a + infinitive with Conjuguemos drills.

*How to teach:* Review units 6–9 with Conjuguemos drills.

- **Q:** Conjugate vivir, tener and ir for nosotros.
  **A:** vivimos, tenemos, vamos.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading practice
*Student:* Read three short Spanish texts (menu, family profile, town brochure) and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Read three short texts; answer questions.

- **Q:** On a menu, what is 'el postre'?
  **A:** Dessert.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Speaking practice
*Student:* Do a mock 3-minute interview covering all topics of the year.

*How to teach:* Mock 3-minute interview on all topics.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Qué vas a hacer el fin de semana?
  **A:** Voy a ___ (infinitive), e.g., Voy a jugar al fútbol con mis amigos.

### Day 5 (Friday): Writing practice
*Student:* Write a practice paragraph about yourself, your family, and your weekend plans.

*How to teach:* Write a practice paragraph about yourself, family and weekend plans.

- **Q:** Write: 'I have two brothers and we live in California.'
  **A:** Tengo dos hermanos y vivimos en California.

**Assessment:** Self-scored practice test.

**Answer key:** Self-scored practice test: student checks answers with the unit keys (greetings, calendar, ser/adjectives, gustar, school, -ar/-er/-ir, food, family/tener, estar, ir, ir a + infinitive). Goal 80%+; any section under 80% gets a targeted review. Writing practice: paragraph with 8+ sentences using at least 5 different verbs correctly.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Exhibition and showcase

**Prepare:** Exhibition space, hooks/tape, the song, monologue and animation file, response cards, invitations to family/friends.

**Watch for:**
- Hanging work too high. Hang 2-D art with the center at about eye level (around 57–60 inches).
- Thinking only museums give art value. Context changes how art is viewed, but meaning comes from the work and its audience.

### Day 1 (Monday): Install
*Student:* Install the exhibition in the home or community space.

*How to teach:* Install the exhibition.

- **Q:** At about what height should the center of a framed artwork hang?
  **A:** Eye level — about 57–60 inches from the floor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse a song, monologue and gallery talk for showcase night.

*How to teach:* Rehearse song, monologue and gallery talk.

- **Q:** What is a gallery talk?
  **A:** A short talk explaining artworks to visitors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Showcase
*Student:* Host the arts showcase: gallery, music performance and animation screening.

*How to teach:* Host the showcase.

- **Q:** Which work got the strongest reaction and why?
  **A:** Open answer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Collect visitor comments on response cards.

*How to teach:* Collect visitor response cards.

- **Q:** What makes feedback useful?
  **A:** It is specific about what worked and what could change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Compare viewing art at home versus a museum and how location changes value.

*How to teach:* Reflect on home vs museum viewing.

- **Q:** How can location change how people value art?
  **A:** A museum setting suggests importance and expertise; home settings feel personal; lighting and labels affect attention.

**Assessment:** Showcase and reflection.

**Answer key:** Showcase rubric: exhibition installed neatly with labels; performances of a song, monologue and animation screening; gallery talk explains at least 3 works with vocabulary. Reflection proficient: compares home vs museum viewing (lighting, labels, scale, crowds, prestige, cost) and how location affects perceived value.

## Physical Education 7: Personal fitness plan

**Prepare:** Fitness journal, calendar. Pull post-test results.

**Watch for:**
- Planning only workouts you dislike. Adherence depends on enjoyment; include favorite activities.
- Leaving out rest days. Recovery is part of training.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Design a 4-week summer plan using FITT for all fitness components.

*How to teach:* Your child designs a 4-week summer plan with FITT details.

- **Q:** How would you apply progression across 4 weeks for jogging?
  **A:** Increase time or distance gradually, e.g., 20 → 22 → 25 → 28 minutes (about 10% per week).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trial day 1
*Student:* Complete day 1 of the plan.

*How to teach:* Complete day 1 of the plan.

- **Q:** What RPE did you feel? Was it what you planned?
  **A:** Open answer — compare actual RPE with planned intensity.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trial day 2
*Student:* Complete day 2 of the plan and adjust intensity.

*How to teach:* Complete day 2 and adjust intensity based on RPE or heart rate.

- **Q:** If a workout felt like RPE 9/10 when you planned 6, what should you change?
  **A:** Lower the intensity — slower pace, fewer reps or more rest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Barriers
*Student:* Identify barriers to staying active and plan solutions.

*How to teach:* List barriers (travel, heat, boredom) and solutions.

- **Q:** Barrier: hot summer afternoons. Solution?
  **A:** Exercise early morning or evening, swim, exercise indoors, hydrate.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present the plan to a parent.

*How to teach:* Present the plan to a parent.

- **Q:** What is adherence?
  **A:** Sticking with a plan over time.

**Assessment:** Personal fitness plan.

**Answer key:** Personal fitness plan rubric: 4 = 4 weeks; covers cardio (3–5 days), strength (2–3 days), flexibility (most days) with FITT for each; progression across weeks; warm-up/cool-down; rest days; ties to post-test weaknesses; barriers and solutions; 3 = all components, some FITT gaps; 2 = missing a component; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 7: Community health advocacy project

**Prepare:** Help your child brainstorm local issues (vaping near parks, lack of bike lanes, air quality, water bottle refill stations, food access). Look up who makes decisions (city council, school board). Poster paper or slides.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking one young person can't make a difference. Councils and boards respond to clear, polite, fact-based requests, and youth voices stand out.
- Writing an advocacy piece with only opinions. Strong advocacy uses accurate facts, a personal connection and a specific request.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose an issue
*Student:* Choose a community health issue and research with valid sources.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses an issue and gathers 3 facts from valid sources (cite them).

- **Q:** Name two valid sources for community health data.
  **A:** County public health department, CDC, state health department, EPA/AirNow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Plan an advocacy product (letter to city council, poster or video).

*How to teach:* Choose the format and audience; outline the message: issue, facts, personal connection, specific request.

- **Q:** What is a stakeholder?
  **A:** A person or group affected by or able to influence the issue, such as residents, council members, parents, businesses.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Create the advocacy product.

*How to teach:* Create the product. Check facts and tone.

- **Q:** Why should an advocacy letter be polite and specific?
  **A:** Decision-makers are more likely to listen and act on a respectful, concrete request.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present or send the product.

*How to teach:* Present to family or send the letter/email to the decision-maker.

- **Q:** What is your call to action?
  **A:** A specific request, e.g., 'Please install a water refill station at Central Park by next summer.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Reflect on the impact of advocacy.

*How to teach:* Your child reflects on what went well and possible impact.

- **Q:** How could you measure whether your advocacy worked?
  **A:** Track replies, changes made, people who signed on or changed behavior.

**Assessment:** Advocacy project.

**Answer key:** Advocacy project rubric: 4 = clear health issue with 3+ facts from valid sources, identifies the audience/decision-maker, takes a clear stand, includes a specific, realistic request (call to action), polished and respectful; 3 = clear stand with 2 facts and a request; 2 = opinion with weak evidence; 1 = incomplete.
