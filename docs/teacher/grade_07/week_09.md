# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 9

## English Language Arts 7: The Call of the Wild, chapter 7 and theme

**Prepare:** The Call of the Wild; one film adaptation clip (the 2020 film is widely available; preview it — it changes the plot and uses a CGI Buck); theme tracker.

**Watch for:**
- Including opinions or minor details in the summary. Check every sentence: 'Could someone who hated the book agree this happened?'
- Writing a theme as a single word. Coach: 'What does the book say ABOUT survival?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 7
*Student:* Read Chapter 7 'The Sounding of the Call' and discuss the ending's meaning.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 7 together. Your child notes the two pulls on Buck (Thornton and the wild) and what finally decides between them. Discuss whether the ending is happy, sad or both, citing the final paragraphs.

- **Q:** What happens to John Thornton?
  **A:** While Buck is away in the forest, the Yeehats attack the camp and kill Thornton, Hans, Pete and the dogs.
- **Q:** How does the novel end?
  **A:** Buck kills several Yeehats, joins the wolf pack, becomes the legendary 'Ghost Dog', and returns each summer to mourn at the place Thornton died.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Objective summary
*Student:* Write a 200-word objective summary of the whole novel without opinions.

*How to teach:* Model a two-sentence chunk. Your child writes a 200-word objective summary from Buck's kidnapping to his joining the wolves. Check before moving on: ask "Which sentence is objective: 'Hal was a cruel fool' or 'Hal beat the starving dogs'?" Look for: 'Hal beat the starving dogs' — it reports an event without judgment.

- **Q:** Which sentence is objective: 'Hal was a cruel fool' or 'Hal beat the starving dogs'?
  **A:** 'Hal beat the starving dogs' — it reports an event without judgment.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Theme tracking
*Student:* Trace a theme (survival of the fittest, nature vs. civilization or loyalty) through 6 quotations from different chapters.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses one theme and finds six quotations from at least four chapters that develop it, writing one sentence of commentary for each. Check that the final theme statement is a complete sentence, not one word. Check before moving on: ask "Write a complete theme statement about nature vs. civilization." Look for: Example: 'When the comforts of civilization are stripped away, instincts hidden deep inside can take over.'

- **Q:** Write a complete theme statement about nature vs. civilization.
  **A:** Example: 'When the comforts of civilization are stripped away, instincts hidden deep inside can take over.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Film comparison
*Student:* Watch a clip from a film adaptation and note two ways it changes Buck's story and why.

*How to teach:* Watch 10–15 minutes of an adaptation covering a scene you've read. Your child lists two changes (plot, characters, tone) and explains a possible reason for each (audience, rating, length, visual effect). Check before moving on: ask "Why might a film soften the violence?" Look for: To reach a family audience and earn a lower rating.

- **Q:** Why might a film soften the violence?
  **A:** To reach a family audience and earn a lower rating.

### Day 5 (Friday): Essay planning
*Student:* Choose a thesis for a literary analysis essay on how setting shapes Buck and outline three body paragraphs with evidence.

*How to teach:* Model turning a topic into a thesis about how setting shapes Buck. Your child writes the thesis and outlines three body paragraphs, each with a topic sentence and two quotations with page numbers. Check that every topic sentence supports the thesis.

- **Q:** Example thesis?
  **A:** 'The brutal Northland setting transforms Buck from a pampered pet into a leader of the wild by teaching him the law of club and fang, the need for cunning, and the pull of his ancestors.'

**Assessment:** Objective summary and essay outline.

**Answer key:** Summary rubric: 4 = 180–220 words, all major stages (Judge Miller's → kidnapping → club/fang lessons → Spitz → leader → Hal's party → Thornton → wolves), no opinions; 3 = one stage missing; 2 = opinions or major gaps; 1 = retells a single part. Outline: arguable thesis about setting; three topic sentences each tied to the thesis; two cited quotes per paragraph.

## Mathematics 7: Proportional reasoning project & unit test

**Prepare:** Phone timer, measuring cup with mL markings, graph paper, calculator. Set the faucet to a slow drip (ask permission; collect the water to reuse on plants).

**Watch for:**
- Predicting yearly waste by multiplying wrong units (minutes vs hours). Convert step by step: per minute → per hour (×60) → per day (×24) → per year (×365).
- Claiming data that are 'close' to a line are exactly proportional. Discuss measurement error and use the average rate for k.

### Day 1 (Monday): Collecting data
*Student:* Measure how much water drips from a faucet in 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 minutes and record in a table.

*How to teach:* Set up the drip and measure water collected at 1–5 minutes. Your child records in a table with units. Check before moving on: ask "Why measure several times instead of once?" Look for: To see the pattern and reduce the effect of measurement error.

- **Q:** Why measure several times instead of once?
  **A:** To see the pattern and reduce the effect of measurement error.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Modeling
*Student:* Graph the drip data, find k, write an equation, and predict water wasted in a day and a year.

*How to teach:* Your child graphs the data, finds k (mL per minute), writes w = kt and predicts daily and yearly totals. Check before moving on: ask "If k = 30 mL/min, water wasted in one day?" Look for: 30 × 60 × 24 = 43,200 mL = 43.2 L.

- **Q:** If k = 30 mL/min, water wasted in one day?
  **A:** 30 × 60 × 24 = 43,200 mL = 43.2 L.
- **Q:** In one year?
  **A:** 43.2 × 365 = 15,768 L.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Report
*Student:* Write a one-page report explaining whether the relationship is proportional and what the constant means.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-page report: data table, graph, equation, whether it is proportional and what k means. Read it and ask one clarifying question. Check before moving on: ask "What does k mean in w = 30t?" Look for: 30 mL of water drips each minute.

- **Q:** What does k mean in w = 30t?
  **A:** 30 mL of water drips each minute.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review on tables, equations, graphs and unit rates with fractions.

*How to teach:* Work two review problems together, then independent practice. Re-teach any weak representation. Check before moving on: ask "Unit rate for 3/4 lb costing $1.50?" Look for: $2 per lb.

- **Q:** Unit rate for 3/4 lb costing $1.50?
  **A:** $2 per lb.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the unit 2 test covering all representations of proportional relationships.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Does y = 3x + 0 represent a proportional relationship?" Look for: Yes — it is y = 3x.

- **Q:** Does y = 3x + 0 represent a proportional relationship?
  **A:** Yes — it is y = 3x.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and dripping-faucet modeling report.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test sample answers: 1) (3,12),(5,20) → k = 4, y = 4x. 2) (2,5),(4,9) not proportional. 3) (1/2)/(1/8) = 4. 4) Graph through (2,7): k = 3.5. 5) y = 0.75x, x = 12 → 9. 6) Point (0,0) means zero input gives zero output. Report rubric: 4 = accurate table/graph, correct k with units, equation, valid daily/yearly prediction, clear proportional judgment; 3 = one error; 2 = missing parts; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Energy in reactions: hot and cold

**Prepare:** Thermometer (digital ideal), baking soda, vinegar, 3% hydrogen peroxide, dry yeast, steel wool, jar, calcium chloride (ice melt; gloves).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a 'cold' reaction adds cold. Endothermic reactions absorb thermal energy from surroundings, so the surroundings get colder.
- Thinking all reactions get hot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Measuring change
*Student:* Measure temperature change for baking soda + vinegar every 30 seconds and graph it.

*How to teach:* Your child records temperature every 30 seconds for baking soda + vinegar and graphs. Check before moving on: ask "What happens to the temperature?" Look for: It drops a few degrees — the reaction absorbs energy (endothermic).

- **Q:** What happens to the temperature?
  **A:** It drops a few degrees — the reaction absorbs energy (endothermic).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hot reaction
*Student:* Measure temperature change for yeast + hydrogen peroxide and for steel wool + vinegar in a jar.

*How to teach:* Your child tests yeast + peroxide and steel wool + vinegar in a jar. Check before moving on: ask "Temperature change in yeast + peroxide?" Look for: It rises — exothermic (releases energy) as peroxide breaks into water and oxygen.

- **Q:** Temperature change in yeast + peroxide?
  **A:** It rises — exothermic (releases energy) as peroxide breaks into water and oxygen.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing reactions
*Student:* Compare graphs and classify each reaction as exothermic or endothermic.

*How to teach:* Compare graphs and classify. Check before moving on: ask "Steel wool rusting?" Look for: Exothermic — temperature rises slowly.

- **Q:** Steel wool rusting?
  **A:** Exothermic — temperature rises slowly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hand warmers & cold packs
*Student:* Read how commercial hand warmers and instant cold packs work and identify their chemicals.

*How to teach:* Read about hand warmers and cold packs. Check before moving on: ask "What makes hand warmers hot?" Look for: Iron powder oxidizing (rusting) quickly with air, helped by salt.

- **Q:** What makes hand warmers hot?
  **A:** Iron powder oxidizing (rusting) quickly with air, helped by salt.
- **Q:** Cold packs?
  **A:** Ammonium nitrate (or urea) dissolving in water absorbs energy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on exothermic and endothermic reactions with graph interpretation.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Define exothermic." Look for: A reaction that releases thermal energy to its surroundings.

- **Q:** Define exothermic.
  **A:** A reaction that releases thermal energy to its surroundings.

**Assessment:** Temperature graphs and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Exothermic releases energy (temp rises). 2) Endothermic absorbs energy (temp falls). 3) Baking soda + vinegar = endothermic. 4) Hand warmer = exothermic iron oxidation. 5) Graph rising = exothermic.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Confucianism and the scholar-officials

**Prepare:** Analects excerpts; mock civil service exam (10 questions on the Analects).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Confucianism is mainly about gods. It focuses on ethics, relationships and good government.
- Thinking the exams were open to everyone equally. Studying took years, so mostly wealthy families' sons passed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Confucian values
*Student:* Read selected Analects passages and explain filial piety and the five relationships.

*How to teach:* Read passages. Your child explains filial piety and the five relationships. Check before moving on: ask "The five relationships?" Look for: Ruler–subject, father–son, husband–wife, elder brother–younger brother, friend–friend.

- **Q:** The five relationships?
  **A:** Ruler–subject, father–son, husband–wife, elder brother–younger brother, friend–friend.
- **Q:** Filial piety?
  **A:** Respect and obedience to parents and ancestors.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Civil service exams
*Student:* Take a mock civil service exam with Analects questions and discuss fairness of the system.

*How to teach:* Give a 10-question mock exam on the Analects under timed, silent conditions. Discuss: who could afford to prepare, and was the system fair? Check before moving on: ask "Why might exams be unfair?" Look for: Only those who could afford years of study could pass.

- **Q:** Why might exams be unfair?
  **A:** Only those who could afford years of study could pass.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Scholar-officials
*Student:* Explain how the exam created a scholar-official class and compare to modern government hiring.

*How to teach:* Explain how passing exams created a class of scholar-officials. Your child compares this to modern civil service tests and job interviews. Check before moving on: ask "How were officials chosen?" Look for: By passing exams on Confucian classics — merit, not birth.

- **Q:** How were officials chosen?
  **A:** By passing exams on Confucian classics — merit, not birth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Neo-Confucianism
*Student:* Read about Zhu Xi and how Neo-Confucianism blended Buddhist and Daoist ideas.

*How to teach:* Read a short passage about Zhu Xi. Your child lists which ideas came from Confucianism, Buddhism and Daoism. Check before moving on: ask "What did Neo-Confucianism blend?" Look for: Confucian ethics with Buddhist and Daoist ideas about the universe and the mind.

- **Q:** What did Neo-Confucianism blend?
  **A:** Confucian ethics with Buddhist and Daoist ideas about the universe and the mind.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph evaluating whether merit-based exams strengthened China.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph evaluating whether merit-based exams strengthened China, using two pieces of evidence and one counterargument. Check before moving on: ask "One argument for?" Look for: Educated, capable officials; stable government.

- **Q:** One argument for?
  **A:** Educated, capable officials; stable government.

**Assessment:** Evaluation paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate. Proficient addresses both strengths (educated officials, stability) and weaknesses (favored wealthy, rigid focus on classics).

## Spanish 1A: ¿Cómo eres? Personality adjectives and agreement

**Prepare:** Flashcards for 16 adjectives: simpático, antipático, gracioso, serio, trabajador, perezoso, inteligente, paciente, impaciente, deportista, artístico, ordenado, desordenado, talentoso, tímido, sociable.

**Watch for:**
- Using -o adjectives for girls. Feminine: simpática, seria; adjectives ending in -e or -ista don't change for gender (inteligente, deportista).
- Putting adjectives before nouns like English. Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun: una chica simpática.

### Day 1 (Monday): Personality words
*Student:* Learn 16 personality adjectives with flashcards and describe yourself in five sentences using soy and no soy.

*How to teach:* Learn adjectives; describe self with soy / no soy.

- **Q:** A girl says 'I am funny but not lazy.'
  **A:** Soy graciosa pero no soy perezosa.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Agreement
*Student:* Learn masculine/feminine and singular/plural adjective endings (-o/-a/-os/-as, -e, consonant) and complete a 20-item agreement worksheet.

*How to teach:* Teach -o/-a/-os/-as, -e → -es, consonant → -es (trabajador → trabajadora/trabajadores).

- **Q:** Make 'serio' agree with 'las chicas'.
  **A:** Las chicas son serias.
- **Q:** Make 'inteligente' agree with 'los chicos'.
  **A:** Los chicos son inteligentes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Intensifiers
*Student:* Add muy, un poco, bastante, and pero/y to describe three family members in connected sentences.

*How to teach:* Use muy, un poco, bastante and y/pero to describe three family members.

- **Q:** Write: 'My dad is very hardworking and a little serious.'
  **A:** Mi papá es muy trabajador y un poco serio.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Entrevista
*Student:* Interview a parent: ¿Cómo eres? ¿Eres...? and write a five-sentence description of them from the answers.

*How to teach:* Interview a parent with ¿Cómo eres? ¿Eres…?; write five sentences.

- **Q:** Ask your mom if she is patient.
  **A:** ¿Eres paciente? (or formal: ¿Es usted paciente?)

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an adjective agreement quiz and record a 30-second self-description.

*How to teach:* Give the agreement quiz; record a self-description.

- **Q:** Make 'trabajador' agree with 'mi hermana'.
  **A:** Mi hermana es trabajadora.

**Assessment:** Agreement quiz and oral self-description.

**Answer key:** Agreement quiz key (sample): 1) Ella es (trabajador) → trabajadora 2) Ellos son (gracioso) → graciosos 3) Mis amigas son (inteligente) → inteligentes 4) Él es (deportista) → deportista 5) Nosotras somos (serio) → serias 6) Tú (a girl) eres (perezoso) → perezosa 7) Las chicas son (sociable) → sociables. 80% proficient. Oral self-description: 30 seconds with soy/no soy, 4+ adjectives with correct agreement.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Painting critique

**Prepare:** Finished painting. Image of a famous landscape (e.g., Hokusai, Monet's Impression, Sunrise, or Thomas Cole's The Oxbow). Clear a display area.

**Watch for:**
- Jumping straight to 'I like it' or 'it's bad.' Feldman's method saves judgment for last.
- Treating critique as only negative. Critique names strengths and specific improvements.

### Day 1 (Monday): Feldman critique
*Student:* Use the describe-analyze-interpret-judge method to critique a famous landscape.

*How to teach:* Model Feldman's four steps on a famous landscape; your child completes each step.

- **Q:** What are the four steps of Feldman's method?
  **A:** Describe, Analyze, Interpret, Judge.
- **Q:** At which step do you give your opinion of quality?
  **A:** Judge — the last step.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Self-critique
*Student:* Critique your own painting with both personal and established criteria and note differences.

*How to teach:* Critique own painting using personal and established criteria.

- **Q:** What is the difference between personal and established criteria?
  **A:** Personal criteria are your own goals for the piece; established criteria are standards used in the art world (composition, technique, use of elements).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise
*Student:* Make one revision to strengthen the focal point.

*How to teach:* Make one revision to strengthen the focal point (more contrast, detail or brighter color).

- **Q:** Name two ways to emphasize a focal point.
  **A:** Increase contrast, add detail, use the brightest or complementary color, place it on a rule-of-thirds point, use leading lines.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Artist statement
*Student:* Write an artist statement for the painting.

*How to teach:* Write an artist statement for the painting.

- **Q:** What place inspired your painting and how did you show its mood?
  **A:** Open answer — names the place and specific color/brushwork choices.

### Day 5 (Friday): Mini show
*Student:* Display drawings and painting for family and explain one choice in each.

*How to teach:* Display drawings and painting; your child explains one choice in each.

- **Q:** What did you learn from a family member's comment?
  **A:** Open answer.

**Assessment:** Written critique and mini show.

**Answer key:** Written critique rubric: Describe (subject, colors, objects — facts only), Analyze (elements and principles: color scheme, contrast, focal point, balance, depth), Interpret (meaning or mood with evidence), Judge (success with reasons based on criteria). Proficient: all four steps in order with evidence. Mini show: child explains one specific choice per work.

## Physical Education 7: Ultimate frisbee: game play and Spirit of the Game

**Prepare:** Disc, cones for endzones (about 20 m apart for small games), a few players. Print or read the 'Spirit of the Game' summary from USA Ultimate.

**Watch for:**
- Running in a straight line to get open. Use a hard cut away, then cut sharply back (change of direction and speed).
- Thinking self-officiating means you can make calls in your favor. Spirit of the Game means honest calls and respect even in competition.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cutting
*Student:* Practice cutting to get open and receiving passes in a 2-v-1 drill.

*How to teach:* Teach cutting: jog away, plant and sprint back toward the thrower. Practice 2-v-1 cut-and-catch.

- **Q:** What is a cut in ultimate?
  **A:** A sudden sprint and change of direction to get open for a pass.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Marking
*Student:* Practice marking a thrower with a 10-second stall count.

*How to teach:* Marker stands an arm's length away, no contact, counts out loud 'Stalling one…' to ten. Thrower must release before ten.

- **Q:** What happens if the stall count reaches 10?
  **A:** It's a turnover — the other team gets the disc.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rules & Spirit
*Student:* Learn ultimate rules and the Spirit of the Game, then play a 3-v-3 game.

*How to teach:* Teach rules: score by catching in the endzone; turnovers on dropped, out-of-bounds, intercepted or stalled throws; no contact; self-officiated. Play 3-v-3.

- **Q:** Name two ways the disc changes possession.
  **A:** Dropped pass, out of bounds, interception, block or stall.
- **Q:** What is the Spirit of the Game?
  **A:** Ultimate's principle that players are responsible for fair play, honest calls and respect, with no referees.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play a full game of ultimate and self-officiate calls.

*How to teach:* Play a full game with self-officiated calls; pause to discuss any calls.

- **Q:** What do you do when two players disagree on a foul?
  **A:** Discuss calmly; if unresolved, the disc goes back to the last undisputed thrower.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Rate your own and teammates' spirit and strategy on a scorecard.

*How to teach:* Fill in spirit scorecards for self and teammates.

- **Q:** What is a stack?
  **A:** An offensive formation where players line up (usually down the middle) to open space for cuts.

**Assessment:** Game play and spirit scorecard.

**Answer key:** Spirit scorecard (WFDF style, 0–4 each): rules knowledge, fouls and body contact, fair-mindedness, attitude and self-control, communication. Game play: completes passes, makes cuts to get open, marks with legal stall count (10 seconds, arm's length, no contact). Proficient: average 2–3 spirit scores with examples and correct use of rules.

## Health Education 7: Depression, anxiety and getting help

**Prepare:** Read KidsHealth teen articles on depression and anxiety first. Parent preview: Day 2 covers suicide warning signs — keep it calm and factual, say you always want to know if your child or a friend is struggling. Save 988 (call or text) in phones. Index card for help card.

**Watch for:**
- Believing talking about suicide puts the idea in someone's head. Research shows asking directly and caringly does not increase risk and can help.
- Thinking depression is just being sad or weak. It is a medical condition lasting two weeks or more that affects mood, sleep, appetite and energy, and it is treatable.
- Promising to keep a friend's dangerous secret. Safety comes before secrets — tell a trusted adult.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mental illness facts
*Student:* Read KidsHealth articles on depression and anxiety and list symptoms.

*How to teach:* Read the articles together and make a two-column symptom list. Emphasize both are common and treatable.

- **Q:** Name three symptoms of depression.
  **A:** Persistent sadness or irritability, loss of interest in activities, changes in sleep or appetite, tiredness, trouble concentrating, feelings of worthlessness.
- **Q:** Name two symptoms of anxiety.
  **A:** Excessive worry, restlessness, racing heart, stomachaches, avoiding situations, trouble sleeping.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Warning signs
*Student:* Learn suicide warning signs and the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

*How to teach:* Calmly teach warning signs (talking about wanting to die or being a burden, giving away possessions, withdrawing, sudden mood changes) and the 988 Lifeline. Save 988 together.

- **Q:** What number do you call or text in a mental health crisis?
  **A:** 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call 911 if someone is in immediate danger).
- **Q:** Name two suicide warning signs.
  **A:** Talking about death or wanting to die, saying they are a burden, giving away belongings, withdrawing from friends, extreme mood swings.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trusted adults
*Student:* Identify five trusted adults and how to reach them.

*How to teach:* Your child lists five trusted adults with how to reach each (in person, phone).

- **Q:** What makes an adult 'trusted'?
  **A:** They listen, keep you safe, take you seriously and would help you get support.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Helping a friend
*Student:* Role-play encouraging a friend to talk to an adult.

*How to teach:* Role-play: you play a friend who seems down; your child encourages talking to an adult. Switch roles.

- **Q:** Your friend says 'Promise you won't tell anyone.' What do you say?
  **A:** 'I care about you too much to keep this secret. Let's talk to an adult together.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a help card with resources to keep in your wallet or phone.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a wallet/phone help card and you check it against the key.

- **Q:** Besides 988, give one other resource.
  **A:** Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), a school counselor, a doctor, or 911 in an emergency.

**Assessment:** Help card and role-play.

**Answer key:** Help card: includes 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988), Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), 911 for emergencies, names and numbers of at least 3 trusted adults, and one coping strategy. Role-play rubric: 4 = notices signs, expresses care using 'I' statements, listens without judging, urges talking to an adult and offers to go with them, does not promise secrecy; 3 = most elements; 2 = advice only; 1 = does not involve an adult.
