# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 21

## English Language Arts 7: Acts 4–5 and play-within-a-play

**Prepare:** A Midsummer Night's Dream; summary of Ovid's Pyramus and Thisbe.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all spells are removed. Demetrius stays enchanted, loving Helena.
- Missing that Act 5 comedy comes from the bad acting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Act 4
*Student:* Read Act 4 and explain how each conflict is resolved.

*How to teach:* Read Act 4 in parts. Your child explains how each conflict (lovers, fairy king and queen, Egeus's demand) is resolved and by whom. Check before moving on: ask "How is the lovers' conflict resolved?" Look for: At Oberon’s command Puck removes the spell from Lysander at the end of Act 3 (Demetrius stays enchanted), so Lysander loves Hermia and Demetrius loves Helena; Theseus overrules Egeus.

- **Q:** How is the lovers' conflict resolved?
  **A:** At Oberon’s command Puck removes the spell from Lysander at the end of Act 3 (Demetrius stays enchanted), so Lysander loves Hermia and Demetrius loves Helena; Theseus overrules Egeus.
- **Q:** How is the fairy conflict resolved?
  **A:** Titania gives up the boy; Oberon releases her.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pyramus and Thisbe
*Student:* Read a summary of Ovid's myth and explain how Shakespeare's allusion parodies the lovers' story.

*How to teach:* Read a summary of Ovid's Pyramus and Thisbe. Your child compares the myth with the lovers' story and explains how the mechanicals' play parodies it. Check before moving on: ask "How are Pyramus and Thisbe like the lovers?" Look for: Forbidden young lovers who meet secretly — but their story ends in tragedy while the Athenians' ends happily.

- **Q:** How are Pyramus and Thisbe like the lovers?
  **A:** Forbidden young lovers who meet secretly — but their story ends in tragedy while the Athenians' ends happily.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Act 5
*Student:* Read Act 5 and analyze the audience's comments during the mechanicals' play.

*How to teach:* Read Act 5 in parts. Your child records three audience comments by the nobles and explains what they show about the play and the nobles. Check before moving on: ask "What do the nobles' comments show?" Look for: They mock the actors, adding comedy and showing class differences.

- **Q:** What do the nobles' comments show?
  **A:** They mock the actors, adding comedy and showing class differences.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme
*Student:* Write a theme statement about love or imagination with three supporting quotes.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a theme statement about love or imagination supported by three quotes from different acts. Check that the theme is a complete sentence. Check before moving on: ask "Theme example?" Look for: Love is irrational and can change suddenly.

- **Q:** Theme example?
  **A:** Love is irrational and can change suddenly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a full-play quiz on plot, structure and theme.

*How to teach:* Give the full-play quiz on plot, structure and theme. Review together. Check before moving on: ask "How does the play end?" Look for: Puck's epilogue: if the play offended, think it was a dream.

- **Q:** How does the play end?
  **A:** Puck's epilogue: if the play offended, think it was a dream.

**Assessment:** Theme paragraph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Full-play quiz: plot (sequence of acts), structure (three plotlines merge in Act 5), theme (love irrational, imagination). Theme paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two or more accurately quoted and cited pieces of evidence, explanation that connects each quote to the claim; 3 = claim and evidence but thin explanation; 2 = evidence summarized rather than quoted, or weak link to claim; 1 = opinion without evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Four operations with rational numbers in context

**Prepare:** Check register template (print or draw columns: date, description, deposit, withdrawal, balance), calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Ignoring order of operations with negatives: −3² = −9 but (−3)² = 9.
- Forgetting the sign of a rate (descending = negative).

### Day 1 (Monday): Money in and out
*Student:* Track a month of deposits and withdrawals in a check register using signed decimals.

*How to teach:* Model logging deposits (+) and withdrawals (−). Your child tracks a month of transactions. Check before moving on: ask "Start $50, spend $62.75, deposit $20. Balance?" Look for: $7.25.

- **Q:** Start $50, spend $62.75, deposit $20. Balance?
  **A:** $7.25.
- **Q:** Start $10, withdraw $15.50. Balance?
  **A:** −$5.50 (overdrawn).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Order of operations
*Student:* Evaluate expressions like -2(3 - 7)^2 / 4 + (-1.5) step by step.

*How to teach:* Model −2(3 − 7)²/4 + (−1.5) step by step. Your child evaluates several. Check before moving on: ask "Value?" Look for: (3 − 7) = −4; squared 16; −2·16 = −32; ÷4 = −8; + (−1.5) = −9.5.

- **Q:** Value?
  **A:** (3 − 7) = −4; squared 16; −2·16 = −32; ÷4 = −8; + (−1.5) = −9.5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rate problems
*Student:* Solve problems about a diver descending at -1.5 m/s and a tank draining at -2.25 gal/min.

*How to teach:* Model distance = rate × time with signed rates. Your child solves diver and tank problems. Check before moving on: ask "Diver at −1.5 m/s for 12 s?" Look for: −18 m (18 m deeper).

- **Q:** Diver at −1.5 m/s for 12 s?
  **A:** −18 m (18 m deeper).
- **Q:** Tank drains −2.25 gal/min. Time to lose 45 gal?
  **A:** −45 ÷ −2.25 = 20 minutes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Estimation
*Student:* Estimate answers to 8 multistep problems before solving and compare estimate to exact answer.

*How to teach:* Your child rounds to estimate each problem first, then solves and compares. Check before moving on: ask "Estimate −4.9 × 6.1." Look for: About −30 (exact −29.89).

- **Q:** Estimate −4.9 × 6.1.
  **A:** About −30 (exact −29.89).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on multistep rational-number problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "−12 + 3 × (−2)?" Look for: −18.

- **Q:** −12 + 3 × (−2)?
  **A:** −18.

**Assessment:** Multistep problem quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) (−4)(3) − (−10) = −2. 2) (−6 + 2)² ÷ 8 = 2. 3) Temperature falls 2.5° per hour for 4 hours from 3°: −7°. 4) Balance $40 then four $12.50 charges: −$10. 5) Submarine at −120 m rises 15 m/min for 5 min: −45 m.

## Science 7 (Integrated): The rock cycle and energy that drives it

**Prepare:** Crayons (3 colors), pencil sharpener or grater, aluminum foil, hot water (adult); sugar cubes, jar with lid, timer; rock cycle diagram.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the rock cycle is a fixed circle. Any rock type can become any other by different processes.
- Forgetting energy sources. The sun drives weathering/erosion; Earth's internal heat drives melting and metamorphism.

### Day 1 (Monday): Crayon rock cycle
*Student:* Shave crayons into sediment, press into 'sedimentary rock', heat-press into 'metamorphic', and melt into 'igneous'.

*How to teach:* Your child shaves crayons (weathering), presses layers in foil (compaction → sedimentary), presses with warm hands/hot water (metamorphic), adult melts in foil boat in hot water (igneous after cooling). Check before moving on: ask "What did pressing represent?" Look for: Compaction and cementation forming sedimentary rock.

- **Q:** What did pressing represent?
  **A:** Compaction and cementation forming sedimentary rock.
- **Q:** Melting and cooling?
  **A:** Igneous rock forming from magma.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Energy sources
*Student:* Label a rock cycle diagram with energy from the sun (weathering, erosion) and Earth's interior (melting, metamorphism).

*How to teach:* Your child labels a rock-cycle diagram with energy source for each process. Check before moving on: ask "Energy source for erosion?" Look for: The sun (drives water cycle and wind) and gravity.

- **Q:** Energy source for erosion?
  **A:** The sun (drives water cycle and wind) and gravity.
- **Q:** For melting?
  **A:** Earth's internal heat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weathering lab
*Student:* Shake sugar cubes in a jar for different times to model mechanical weathering and graph the results.

*How to teach:* Shake sugar cubes for 1, 2, 3 and 4 minutes. Your child weighs remaining cube mass or counts fragments and graphs. Check before moving on: ask "Result?" Look for: Longer shaking breaks more pieces off — more mechanical weathering.

- **Q:** Result?
  **A:** Longer shaking breaks more pieces off — more mechanical weathering.

### Day 4 (Thursday): California rocks
*Student:* Research California's state rock serpentine and granite of the Sierra Nevada and place each in the rock cycle.

*How to teach:* Research serpentine and Sierra granite. Your child places each in the cycle. Check before moving on: ask "Serpentine rock type?" Look for: Metamorphic — formed from ocean-floor rock altered by water and pressure.

- **Q:** Serpentine rock type?
  **A:** Metamorphic — formed from ocean-floor rock altered by water and pressure.
- **Q:** Sierra Nevada granite?
  **A:** Igneous — magma cooled slowly underground, later uplifted and exposed by erosion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw and explain a complete rock cycle model with at least 8 processes.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a full rock cycle with 8+ processes. Check before moving on: ask "Name 8 processes." Look for: Weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, cementation, metamorphism, melting, cooling/crystallization, uplift.

- **Q:** Name 8 processes.
  **A:** Weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, cementation, metamorphism, melting, cooling/crystallization, uplift.

**Assessment:** Rock-cycle model.

**Answer key:** Rock-cycle model rubric: 4 = all three rock types, 8+ correctly labeled processes, arrows showing multiple paths, energy sources noted; 3 = 6–7 processes; 2 = only circular path; 1 = incomplete.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Maya

**Prepare:** Map of Mesoamerica; Maya number chart; Tzolk'in calendar converter (online).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Maya disappeared. Millions of Maya people live today.
- Thinking Maya numbers are base 10. They are base 20 and include zero.

### Day 1 (Monday): Geography
*Student:* Map the Yucatán, highlands and rainforest and explain how the Maya farmed with slash-and-burn and terraces.

*How to teach:* Your child maps the Yucatán, highlands and lowland rainforest and explains slash-and-burn farming and terraces. Check before moving on: ask "Farming methods?" Look for: Slash-and-burn (milpa), terraces, raised fields.

- **Q:** Farming methods?
  **A:** Slash-and-burn (milpa), terraces, raised fields.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): City-states
*Student:* Explore images of Tikal and Chichén Itzá and describe pyramids, ball courts and plazas.

*How to teach:* View images of Tikal and Chichén Itzá. Your child describes pyramids, ball courts and plazas and what they suggest about Maya life. Check before moving on: ask "Purpose of ball courts?" Look for: Ritual ball games with religious meaning.

- **Q:** Purpose of ball courts?
  **A:** Ritual ball games with religious meaning.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Maya math
*Student:* Write numbers 1–100 in Maya base-20 notation, including zero.

*How to teach:* Teach dots (1), bars (5), shell (0). Your child writes 1–100. Check before moving on: ask "Write 17." Look for: Three bars and two dots.

- **Q:** Write 17.
  **A:** Three bars and two dots.
- **Q:** Write 20.
  **A:** One dot in the twenties place over a shell in the ones place.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Calendar and glyphs
*Student:* Calculate your birthday in the Maya Tzolk'in calendar and write your name in glyphs.

*How to teach:* Your child uses an online converter to find their birthday in the Tzolk'in and writes their name using syllable glyphs. Check before moving on: ask "How many days in the Tzolk'in?" Look for: 260.

- **Q:** How many days in the Tzolk'in?
  **A:** 260.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on the Maya.

*How to teach:* Give the Maya quiz without notes. Check before moving on: ask "Maya Classic period?" Look for: About 250–900 CE.

- **Q:** Maya Classic period?
  **A:** About 250–900 CE.

**Assessment:** Math and calendar work, quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: geography, farming, city-states, base 20 with zero, 260-day Tzolk'in and 365-day Haab', glyph writing. Math: 1–100 correct.

## Spanish 1A: Para mantener la salud: healthy eating and plural adjectives

**Prepare:** MyPlate graphic, a Spanish nutrition label (información nutricional — many U.S. products have bilingual labels), short healthy-eating article in Spanish. Vocabulary: las verduras, las zanahorias, la lechuga, los tomates, los guisantes, las judías verdes, el pollo, el pescado, la carne de res, el arroz, los frijoles, la grasa.

**Watch for:**
- Agreement with plural foods: 'Las zanahorias son sabrosas' (not sabroso).
- Using 'debe' with a conjugated verb. Deber + infinitive: Debes comer verduras.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comida sana
*Student:* Learn 12 vegetable and protein words and classify foods as bueno/malo para la salud.

*How to teach:* Learn 12 vegetable/protein words; classify bueno/malo para la salud.

- **Q:** Is 'el pescado' bueno or malo para la salud?
  **A:** Bueno para la salud.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plural adjectives
*Student:* Describe 10 foods with ser and agreeing adjectives: Las zanahorias son sabrosas.

*How to teach:* Describe 10 foods with ser + agreeing adjectives.

- **Q:** Write: 'The peas are horrible.'
  **A:** Los guisantes son horribles.
- **Q:** Write: 'Chicken is tasty.'
  **A:** El pollo es sabroso.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Nutrition labels
*Student:* Read a Spanish nutrition label (información nutricional) and answer questions about grasa, azúcar, and calorías.

*How to teach:* Read a Spanish nutrition label; answer about grasa, azúcar, calorías.

- **Q:** What does 'grasa total' mean?
  **A:** Total fat.
- **Q:** What does 'azúcares añadidos' mean?
  **A:** Added sugars.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mi plato
*Student:* Draw a balanced plate and write a 6-sentence description of a healthy lunch, using para mantener la salud debes...

*How to teach:* Draw a balanced plate; write 6 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'To stay healthy you should eat vegetables.'
  **A:** Para mantener la salud debes comer verduras.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a reading quiz on a short healthy-eating article and present your plate orally.

*How to teach:* Give the reading quiz; present the plate.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Ice cream is bad for your health.'
  **A:** El helado es malo para la salud.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and oral presentation.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz: comprehension questions on the article (e.g., which foods are good for health, how much water to drink). Oral plate presentation rubric (1–4): 6 sentences, names foods by group, uses 'Para mantener la salud debes…', adjective agreement, pronunciation.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Illuminated manuscripts and calligraphy

**Prepare:** Chisel-tip calligraphy marker, gold marker or metallic paint, guide-lined paper. Images of the Book of Kells (c. 800, Insular Gospel book, Trinity College Dublin) and a Persian manuscript page (e.g., the Shahnameh of Shah Tahmasp, 16th century).

**Watch for:**
- Holding the chisel tip at random angles. Keep a consistent pen angle (about 30–45°) for even thick-thin strokes.
- Thinking illumination means just colorful. Illuminated manuscripts are decorated with gold or silver that literally 'lights up' the page.

### Day 1 (Monday): Manuscripts
*Student:* Study images of the Book of Kells and a Persian manuscript and compare them.

*How to teach:* Compare the Book of Kells and a Persian manuscript page.

- **Q:** What is the Book of Kells?
  **A:** An illuminated Latin manuscript of the four Gospels made by Irish/Scottish monks around 800 CE.
- **Q:** What material were medieval manuscripts written on?
  **A:** Vellum or parchment (prepared animal skin).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Calligraphy
*Student:* Practice a gothic or italic alphabet with a chisel-tip marker.

*How to teach:* Practice an italic or gothic alphabet with a chisel marker at a steady angle.

- **Q:** What is calligraphy?
  **A:** Decorative, skilled handwriting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Illuminated letter
*Student:* Design an illuminated initial with border decoration.

*How to teach:* Design an illuminated initial with border decoration.

- **Q:** Why were initials decorated?
  **A:** To mark the start of important sections and to glorify the text.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish page
*Student:* Write a short quote in calligraphy and add metallic accents.

*How to teach:* Write a short quote in calligraphy; add metallic accents.

- **Q:** What does 'illumination' refer to?
  **A:** Decoration with gold or silver (and bright colors) that makes the page shine.

### Day 5 (Friday): Preservation
*Student:* Compare how manuscripts were preserved then and how museums digitize art now.

*How to teach:* Compare preservation then and now.

- **Q:** How does digitizing manuscripts help?
  **A:** It lets anyone view them online while the fragile originals are protected from light and handling.

**Assessment:** Illuminated page.

**Answer key:** Illuminated page rubric (1–4): consistent calligraphic hand (gothic or italic) with even spacing and pen angle, decorated initial with border motifs, metallic accents, layout balance. Manuscript comparison: Book of Kells — Christian Gospels, Celtic knots, interlace, animals, Latin script; Persian manuscript — poetry/epic, detailed figures and landscapes, floral borders, Persian script. Preservation: then — vellum, bindings, monastery libraries, copying by scribes; now — climate control, limited light, digitization for online access.

## Physical Education 7: Basketball: shooting and game play

**Prepare:** Basketball and hoop. Mark 5 shooting spots about 8–10 ft from the basket (two corners, two wings, top).

**Watch for:**
- Shooting with two hands pushing equally. The shooting hand pushes; the guide hand only steadies the ball.
- Jumping off the wrong foot on layups. Right-hand layup: take off from the left foot; left-hand layup: take off from the right foot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shooting form
*Student:* Practice BEEF shooting form from close range, 50 shots.

*How to teach:* Teach BEEF: Balance (feet shoulder-width), Eyes (on the rim), Elbow (under the ball), Follow-through (wrist snap, 'hand in the cookie jar'). 50 close shots.

- **Q:** What does BEEF stand for?
  **A:** Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Layups
*Student:* Practice right- and left-handed layups.

*How to teach:* Right-hand layup: right-left steps, jump off the left foot, lift right knee, use the backboard square. Then left side.

- **Q:** Which foot do you jump off for a right-handed layup?
  **A:** The left foot.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Give-and-go
*Student:* Practice give-and-go and defensive slides.

*How to teach:* Give-and-go: pass, cut to the basket, receive return pass, lay up. Defensive slides: low stance, step-slide, don't cross feet.

- **Q:** What is a give-and-go?
  **A:** Pass to a teammate, cut toward the basket and get the ball back for a shot.
- **Q:** Why shouldn't defenders cross their feet when sliding?
  **A:** It reduces balance and slows changes of direction.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game
*Student:* Play 2-v-2 or 3-v-3 half-court games.

*How to teach:* Play 2-v-2 or 3-v-3 half-court; use man-to-man defense.

- **Q:** What is man-to-man defense?
  **A:** Each defender guards one specific opponent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a shooting test from 5 spots.

*How to teach:* Run the 5-spot shooting test.

- **Q:** You made 3, 2, 4, 1, 3 from the five spots. What is your total and percentage?
  **A:** 13 of 25 = 52%.

**Assessment:** Shooting test.

**Answer key:** Shooting test: 5 shots from each of 5 spots (25 total). Proficient = 10+, advanced = 15+. Note BEEF cues. Layup check: 3/5 made each side with correct takeoff foot.

## Health Education 7: Cannabis and other drugs

**Prepare:** NIDA for Teens pages on marijuana, stimulants, inhalants and opioids. Chart paper for 4-category sort.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cannabis is harmless because it's legal for adults in California. It is illegal under 21 and THC can affect memory, attention and learning in teens; today's products are much more potent.
- Thinking inhalants aren't 'real drugs' because they're household products. Inhaling them can cause sudden death even the first time (sudden sniffing death).

### Day 1 (Monday): Cannabis
*Student:* Learn effects of THC on the developing brain and California law for minors.

*How to teach:* Read about THC's effects (memory, attention, coordination, mental health risks) and California law (21+, illegal for minors).

- **Q:** What is THC?
  **A:** Tetrahydrocannabinol, the main mind-altering chemical in cannabis.
- **Q:** Name two effects of cannabis on the teen brain.
  **A:** Problems with memory, attention and learning; slower reaction time; increased risk of anxiety or psychosis with heavy use.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drug categories
*Student:* Sort drugs into stimulants, depressants, hallucinogens and opioids with effects.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts drug names into four categories with effects (see key).

- **Q:** What category is alcohol?
  **A:** Depressant.
- **Q:** What do opioids do to breathing in an overdose?
  **A:** They slow or stop breathing, which can cause death.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inhalants
*Student:* Learn about the dangers of inhalants.

*How to teach:* Explain inhalants (glue, aerosols, markers) and their dangers.

- **Q:** What is 'sudden sniffing death'?
  **A:** Heart failure that can happen the very first time someone inhales chemicals.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Drug-free benefits
*Student:* List benefits of staying drug-free for goals and activities.

*How to teach:* Your child lists personal goals and how staying drug-free supports them.

- **Q:** Name two benefits of staying drug-free.
  **A:** Better health and school performance, keep trust of family, stay eligible for sports, save money, avoid legal trouble, reach goals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on drug categories.

*How to teach:* Give the category quiz.

- **Q:** Classify: caffeine, LSD, heroin, alcohol.
  **A:** Caffeine = stimulant; LSD = hallucinogen; heroin = opioid; alcohol = depressant.

**Assessment:** Drug category chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Drug category chart: stimulants (nicotine, caffeine, cocaine, methamphetamine, misused ADHD meds) speed up the body — raise heart rate, alertness; depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates) slow the brain — drowsiness, slowed breathing; hallucinogens (LSD, psilocybin mushrooms, PCP) distort perception; opioids (heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, morphine) relieve pain, cause euphoria and can stop breathing. Cannabis has mixed effects (often listed separately). Quiz: matching each drug to category; THC = main psychoactive chemical in cannabis; legal age for cannabis in CA = 21; inhalant danger = sudden sniffing death, brain damage.
