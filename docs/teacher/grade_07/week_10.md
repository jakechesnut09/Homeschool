# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 10

## English Language Arts 7: Literary analysis essay

**Prepare:** Essay outline; Purdue OWL pages on quotations and MLA in-text citations; print 'To Build a Fire' (public domain) excerpt for the test. Parental preview: in 'To Build a Fire' the man freezes to death.

**Watch for:**
- Dropping quotes in without introduction ('plop quotes'). Teach the sandwich: introduce, quote, explain.
- Putting the period before the parenthetical citation. MLA: "...the club" (London 12).

### Day 1 (Monday): Introduction
*Student:* Draft an introduction with a hook, brief novel context and a clear thesis statement.

*How to teach:* Show three hook types (question, vivid image, quotation). Your child drafts an introduction with a hook, two to three sentences of context (author, title, situation), and the thesis as the last sentence. Read it aloud and ask: does the thesis say how setting shapes Buck?

- **Q:** What three parts should the introduction have?
  **A:** Hook, brief context (author, title, basic plot), and thesis.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body paragraphs
*Student:* Draft three body paragraphs using claim-evidence-commentary with embedded quotations and page citations.

*How to teach:* Model one claim-evidence-commentary paragraph. Your child drafts three body paragraphs with embedded, cited quotes. Check before moving on: ask "Punctuate: London writes the club was a revelation (page 14)." Look for: London writes that the club 'was a revelation' (14).

- **Q:** Punctuate: London writes the club was a revelation (page 14).
  **A:** London writes that the club 'was a revelation' (14).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conclusion & revision
*Student:* Draft a conclusion and revise transitions between paragraphs for cohesion.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts a conclusion that restates the thesis in new words and ends with a larger insight. Then they add or improve transitions at the start of each body paragraph ('Beyond physical survival…', 'Most importantly…'). Read the essay aloud together to test flow.

- **Q:** What should a conclusion do?
  **A:** Restate the thesis in new words, sum up the reasoning, and leave a final insight — not new evidence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Editing
*Student:* Edit for sentence variety, quotation punctuation and spelling, then publish the essay.

*How to teach:* Your child edits in three passes: sentence variety (start some sentences with a phrase or clause), quotation punctuation, then spelling. Check two quotes together for correct MLA format before your child prints the final essay. Check before moving on: ask "Fix: Buck learns 'the law of club and fang.' (London 20)" Look for: Buck learns 'the law of club and fang' (London 20).

- **Q:** Fix: Buck learns 'the law of club and fang.' (London 20)
  **A:** Buck learns 'the law of club and fang' (London 20).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Take a unit test with passages from The Call of the Wild and an unseen excerpt from London's 'To Build a Fire'.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test: Call of the Wild passages plus the 'To Build a Fire' excerpt, 50 minutes, no book. Score with the key and discuss any constructed response below proficient. Check before moving on: ask "In 'To Build a Fire,' why does the man's fire go out?" Look for: He builds it under a snow-laden spruce tree; his movements shake the branches and snow falls and smothers it.

- **Q:** In 'To Build a Fire,' why does the man's fire go out?
  **A:** He builds it under a snow-laden spruce tree; his movements shake the branches and snow falls and smothers it.

**Assessment:** Literary analysis essay (rubric) and unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric (4–1): Thesis is arguable and about setting; each body paragraph has claim, 2 embedded quotes with citations, and commentary; logical transitions; conclusion; correct MLA punctuation. Unit test answers: The Call of the Wild items as in prior quizzes; 'To Build a Fire': the man ignores the old-timer from Sulphur Creek's advice not to travel alone in extreme cold; the dog's instinct is wiser than the man's judgment; the man dies and the dog heads to camp; theme — nature is indifferent and human pride/lack of imagination can be fatal.

## Mathematics 7: Circumference

**Prepare:** String, ruler, compass, 6 round objects (cans, lids, plates, a bike wheel if possible), calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing radius and diameter. Write d = 2r on the board and always circle which is given.
- Thinking π is exactly 3.14. It is a never-ending decimal; 3.14 and 22/7 are approximations.

### Day 1 (Monday): Parts of a circle
*Student:* Use a compass to draw circles and label center, radius, diameter and circumference; note d = 2r.

*How to teach:* Model drawing a circle with a compass and labeling center, radius, diameter, circumference. Your child draws three circles and labels each. Check before moving on: ask "Radius 4.5 cm. Diameter?" Look for: 9 cm.

- **Q:** Radius 4.5 cm. Diameter?
  **A:** 9 cm.
- **Q:** Diameter 15 in. Radius?
  **A:** 7.5 in.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measuring around
*Student:* Measure diameter and circumference of 6 household circles with string, record in a table and graph circumference versus diameter.

*How to teach:* Model wrapping string around a can and measuring it. Your child measures all 6 objects, records d and C, and graphs C vs d. Check before moving on: ask "What does the graph look like?" Look for: Points fall close to a straight line through the origin — C is proportional to d.

- **Q:** What does the graph look like?
  **A:** Points fall close to a straight line through the origin — C is proportional to d.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Discovering pi
*Student:* Compute C/d for each object, average the ratios, and compare to 3.14 and 22/7.

*How to teach:* Your child computes C/d for each object and averages. Check before moving on: ask "A lid has d = 8 cm, C = 25 cm. C/d?" Look for: 3.125, close to π ≈ 3.14.

- **Q:** A lid has d = 8 cm, C = 25 cm. C/d?
  **A:** 3.125, close to π ≈ 3.14.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Applying circumference
*Student:* Solve problems such as how far a bike wheel travels in 10 rotations and finding diameter from circumference.

*How to teach:* Model the bike wheel problem. Your child solves several, including finding d from C. Check before moving on: ask "A 26-inch wheel turns 10 times. Distance?" Look for: 26π × 10 ≈ 816.8 in ≈ 68 ft.

- **Q:** A 26-inch wheel turns 10 times. Distance?
  **A:** 26π × 10 ≈ 816.8 in ≈ 68 ft.
- **Q:** C = 31.4 cm. Diameter?
  **A:** 10 cm (31.4 ÷ 3.14).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on circle vocabulary, C = pi d and C = 2 pi r.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Radius 3 m. Circumference?" Look for: 6π ≈ 18.84 m.

- **Q:** Radius 3 m. Circumference?
  **A:** 6π ≈ 18.84 m.

**Assessment:** Measurement lab table and circumference quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (use π ≈ 3.14): 1) d = 10 → C = 31.4. 2) r = 5 → C = 31.4. 3) r = 7 → C ≈ 43.96. 4) C = 62.8 → d = 20, r = 10. 5) d = 2r, C = πd = 2πr. Lab: complete table with C/d values between about 3.0 and 3.3 = proficient.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Engineering challenge: design a hand warmer or cold pack

**Prepare:** Zip bags, calcium chloride, baking soda, citric acid, water, thermometer, scale, gloves, goggles.

**Watch for:**
- Changing more than one variable between prototypes.
- Ignoring constraints (safety, cost) once something works.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define the problem
*Student:* Write criteria (e.g., warms 10 °C for 5 minutes) and constraints (safe, cheap, under 50 g) for a chemical warmer or cooler.

*How to teach:* Your child writes criteria (e.g., changes 10 °C for 5 minutes) and constraints (safe, under 50 g, cheap). Check before moving on: ask "Criteria vs constraints?" Look for: Criteria = what success looks like; constraints = limits.

- **Q:** Criteria vs constraints?
  **A:** Criteria = what success looks like; constraints = limits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prototype 1
*Student:* Build and test two designs with different amounts of reactants and record temperature over time.

*How to teach:* Build and test two designs with different amounts of reactants. Check before moving on: ask "Which reactants for a warmer?" Look for: Calcium chloride + water (exothermic).

- **Q:** Which reactants for a warmer?
  **A:** Calcium chloride + water (exothermic).
- **Q:** For a cooler?
  **A:** Citric acid + baking soda + water (endothermic).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Analyze and improve
*Student:* Compare test data in a decision matrix and redesign to better meet criteria.

*How to teach:* Your child compares tests in a decision matrix and redesigns. Check before moving on: ask "What's a decision matrix?" Look for: A table scoring designs on each criterion to pick the best.

- **Q:** What's a decision matrix?
  **A:** A table scoring designs on each criterion to pick the best.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prototype 2
*Student:* Test the improved design three times and calculate the average temperature change.

*How to teach:* Test improved design three times and average. Check before moving on: ask "Why test three times?" Look for: To check reliability and reduce effect of errors.

- **Q:** Why test three times?
  **A:** To check reliability and reduce effect of errors.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Present the final design with data and take the unit 2 test.

*How to teach:* Present design; unit 2 test. Check before moving on: ask "Why does more calcium chloride make it warmer?" Look for: More reacting particles release more total energy.

- **Q:** Why does more calcium chloride make it warmer?
  **A:** More reacting particles release more total energy.

**Assessment:** Design portfolio and unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Portfolio: criteria/constraints, two prototypes with data, decision matrix, improved design average. Unit 2 test: signs of chemical change, balancing, conservation of mass, endo/exothermic.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Mongols and Ming: open and closed

**Prepare:** Marco Polo excerpt on Kublai Khan's court; map of Zheng He's voyages; Mongol Empire map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Mongols only destroyed. The Pax Mongolica made trade safer across Eurasia.
- Thinking Zheng He's voyages were to conquer. They displayed Ming power and gathered tribute.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mongol conquests
*Student:* Map the Mongol Empire and explain how the Pax Mongolica revived Silk Road trade.

*How to teach:* Your child maps the Mongol Empire and explains the Pax Mongolica. Check before moving on: ask "Who founded the empire?" Look for: Genghis Khan (1206).

- **Q:** Who founded the empire?
  **A:** Genghis Khan (1206).
- **Q:** Pax Mongolica?
  **A:** Period of relative peace under Mongol rule that made Silk Road trade safer.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Marco Polo
*Student:* Read Marco Polo's description of Kublai Khan's court and evaluate it as a source.

*How to teach:* Read Marco Polo's description of Kublai Khan's court. Your child lists details and evaluates the source's reliability (who, when, purpose). Check before moving on: ask "Why question Polo?" Look for: He dictated it in prison years later, possibly exaggerating; some details are missing.

- **Q:** Why question Polo?
  **A:** He dictated it in prison years later, possibly exaggerating; some details are missing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Zheng He
*Student:* Map Zheng He's seven voyages and compare his ships with Columbus's.

*How to teach:* Your child maps Zheng He's seven voyages and compares the size of his fleet and ships with Columbus's three ships in a chart. Check before moving on: ask "Dates?" Look for: 1405–1433.

- **Q:** Dates?
  **A:** 1405–1433.
- **Q:** Ship size comparison?
  **A:** Zheng He's treasure ships were several times longer than Columbus's Santa María.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Why stop?
*Student:* Analyze reasons the Ming ended the voyages and built the Great Wall, and debate the decision.

*How to teach:* Your child lists reasons the Ming ended the voyages and rebuilt the Great Wall, then debates you: was it a wise decision? Check before moving on: ask "Reasons?" Look for: Cost, Confucian disdain for trade, threats from the north requiring Great Wall rebuilding.

- **Q:** Reasons?
  **A:** Cost, Confucian disdain for trade, threats from the north requiring Great Wall rebuilding.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 3 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 3 test independently. Check before moving on: ask "Kublai Khan founded which dynasty?" Look for: Yuan dynasty.

- **Q:** Kublai Khan founded which dynasty?
  **A:** Yuan dynasty.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test: Sui/Tang/Song, Buddhism spread, inventions, Confucianism and exams, Mongols and Yuan, Marco Polo, Zheng He 1405–1433, Ming end voyages.

## Spanish 1A: Physical descriptions, articles, and nationalities

**Prepare:** Magazine photos or printed images of people; famous-person cards. Nationality list: mexicano, español, argentino, colombiano, peruano, chileno, cubano, puertorriqueño, dominicano, venezolano, estadounidense, guatemalteco.

**Watch for:**
- Using ser for hair and eyes. Use tener: Tiene el pelo castaño y los ojos azules.
- Capitalizing nationalities. Nationalities are lowercase in Spanish: mexicana.

### Day 1 (Monday): Physical traits
*Student:* Learn alto, bajo, rubio, moreno, pelirrojo, tiene el pelo/los ojos... and describe 6 magazine photos.

*How to teach:* Learn alto, bajo, rubio, moreno, pelirrojo, tiene el pelo / los ojos…; describe 6 photos.

- **Q:** Write: 'She is tall and has brown eyes.'
  **A:** Ella es alta y tiene los ojos castaños (or marrones/cafés).
- **Q:** Write: 'He has red hair.'
  **A:** Él es pelirrojo (or tiene el pelo rojo).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Articles
*Student:* Learn el/la/los/las and un/una/unos/unas and complete a sorting activity with 20 nouns.

*How to teach:* Teach definite and indefinite articles; sort 20 nouns.

- **Q:** Give the definite and indefinite article for 'mochila'.
  **A:** la mochila, una mochila.
- **Q:** Which article goes with 'día'?
  **A:** el (el día — an exception ending in -a).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Nationalities
*Student:* Learn 12 nationality adjectives and their agreement (español/española) and write who each famous person is and where they are from.

*How to teach:* Learn 12 nationalities and agreement; write sentences about famous people.

- **Q:** Write: 'Shakira is Colombian.'
  **A:** Shakira es colombiana.
- **Q:** Make 'español' feminine plural.
  **A:** españolas.

### Day 4 (Thursday): ¿Quién es?
*Student:* Play a guess-who game with a parent using only Spanish yes/no questions about appearance and personality.

*How to teach:* Play guess-who with yes/no questions (¿Es alta? ¿Tiene el pelo rubio?).

- **Q:** Ask: 'Does he have blue eyes?'
  **A:** ¿Tiene los ojos azules?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a reading quiz: read four short profiles and match each to the correct picture.

*How to teach:* Give the reading quiz.

- **Q:** What does 'Es mexicano, bajo y muy gracioso' mean?
  **A:** He is Mexican, short and very funny.

**Assessment:** Reading comprehension quiz and guess-who participation.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz: matches four profiles to pictures (4/4 proficient). Articles key: el libro, la mesa, los chicos, las chicas, un perro, una casa, unos lápices, unas mochilas; el día and el mapa are masculine, la mano feminine. Nationality agreement: español/española/españoles/españolas; estadounidense (no gender change).

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Rhythm reading

**Prepare:** Free musictheory.net lessons (Note Duration, Measures and Time Signatures), metronome app, bucket/hand drum and sticks or wooden spoons. Write 8 short rhythms.

**Watch for:**
- Counting rests as silence without counting. Rests take up beats exactly like notes.
- Thinking a dot adds one beat. A dot adds half the note's value (dotted half = 3 beats in 4/4).

### Day 1 (Monday): Note values
*Student:* Complete musictheory.net lessons on note and rest durations and clap 8 rhythms.

*How to teach:* Complete the note-duration lesson; clap and count 8 rhythms aloud ('1, 2-and, 3, 4').

- **Q:** In 4/4, how many beats does a half note get?
  **A:** 2 beats.
- **Q:** How many eighth notes equal one half note?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Time signatures
*Student:* Identify meter in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 excerpts and conduct each pattern.

*How to teach:* Identify meter in 2/4, 3/4 and 4/4 excerpts; conduct each pattern (2: down-up; 3: down-right-up; 4: down-left-right-up).

- **Q:** What does the top number in a time signature tell you?
  **A:** How many beats are in each measure.
- **Q:** What does the bottom number 4 mean?
  **A:** A quarter note gets one beat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sixteenth notes
*Student:* Count and clap sixteenth-note rhythms with 1-e-and-a syllables.

*How to teach:* Count sixteenths as 1-e-&-a, 2-e-&-a; clap patterns slowly with a metronome.

- **Q:** How many sixteenth notes are in one quarter-note beat?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bucket drumming
*Student:* Perform a 16-bar bucket drumming rhythm with a metronome.

*How to teach:* Perform a 16-bar bucket rhythm with the metronome (start at 70 bpm).

- **Q:** Why practice with a metronome?
  **A:** It keeps a steady beat and shows if you rush or drag.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a rhythm-reading quiz on musictheory.net exercises.

*How to teach:* Take the rhythm quiz.

- **Q:** How many beats does a dotted half note get in 4/4?
  **A:** 3 beats (2 + 1).

**Assessment:** Rhythm-reading quiz and performance.

**Answer key:** Rhythm quiz: in 4/4 whole note = 4 beats, half = 2, quarter = 1, eighth = ½, sixteenth = ¼, dotted half = 3, dotted quarter = 1½; rests equal matching notes. Meter identification: 2/4 = 2 quarter beats per measure, 3/4 = 3 (waltz), 4/4 = 4. Proficient: 80% on musictheory.net exercises. Performance: 16 bars steady with metronome, ≤2 errors = proficient.

## Physical Education 7: Invasion games tournament

**Prepare:** Soccer ball, disc, basketball if available, cones. Arrange a homeschool group or family for two mini tournaments. Prepare the skills test stations.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking each sport is totally different. Invasion games share principles: keep possession, create space, move after passing, defend goal-side.
- Standing still after losing the ball. Transition immediately to defense.

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare games
*Student:* Make a chart comparing strategies in soccer, ultimate and basketball.

*How to teach:* Your child charts offense/defense strategies in soccer, ultimate and basketball.

- **Q:** Name one offensive strategy that all three share.
  **A:** Spread out to create space; move to open space after passing; quick passing to advance.
- **Q:** What does 'transition' mean?
  **A:** Switching from offense to defense (or vice versa) when possession changes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tournament 1
*Student:* Play a mini soccer tournament with a homeschool group or family.

*How to teach:* Run a mini soccer tournament (short games, rotating teams).

- **Q:** What is 'goal-side' defending?
  **A:** Staying between the attacker and the goal you're defending.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tournament 2
*Student:* Play a mini ultimate tournament.

*How to teach:* Run a mini ultimate tournament.

- **Q:** Why is a quick throw after a turnover helpful?
  **A:** The defense hasn't organized yet, so receivers are open.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Skills test
*Student:* Complete a unit skills test (passing, dribbling, throwing, catching).

*How to teach:* Run the skills test stations.

- **Q:** Name a cue for a secure catch.
  **A:** Watch the object into your hands; for a disc use a pancake catch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write about which strategies transferred between games.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the strategy-transfer reflection.

- **Q:** Give one example of a strategy that transferred between games.
  **A:** Example: give-and-go/moving after passing works in soccer and ultimate.

**Assessment:** Unit skills test and strategy reflection.

**Answer key:** Unit skills test (score 1–4 per skill): soccer passing (inside foot to target, 7/10 proficient), dribbling (slalom with control both feet), disc throwing (7/10 to partner), catching (8/10 secure catches). Reflection: proficient names 2 strategies that transfer (e.g., spreading wide, moving into space after passing, man-to-man defense, quick transition) with an example from two games.

## Health Education 7: Healthy relationships and communication

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards: 9 relationship behaviors (e.g., listening, respecting 'no', checking your phone constantly, name-calling, hitting, threatening, supporting goals, jealousy, pressuring) and 3 peer-pressure situations.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing assertive with aggressive. Assertive = clear, respectful, firm; aggressive = hostile or blaming; passive = giving in.
- Thinking an I-message is any sentence that starts with 'I'. It must state your feeling, the specific behavior and why/what you need, not blame.

### Day 1 (Monday): Healthy relationships
*Student:* Sort relationship behaviors into healthy, unhealthy and abusive.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts the cards into healthy, unhealthy, abusive and explains each.

- **Q:** Is a friend checking your phone without asking healthy?
  **A:** No — unhealthy, it shows lack of trust and respect for privacy (controlling).
- **Q:** Name three signs of a healthy relationship.
  **A:** Respect, trust, honesty, good communication, support, accepting boundaries.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): I-messages
*Student:* Rewrite five blaming statements as I-messages.

*How to teach:* Teach the formula: 'I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I would like ___.' Your child rewrites five 'you' statements.

- **Q:** Rewrite: 'You always ruin my stuff!'
  **A:** 'I feel upset when my things get broken because they matter to me. I'd like you to ask before borrowing them.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Active listening
*Student:* Practice active listening with a family member and summarize what they said.

*How to teach:* Your child listens to a family member talk for 3 minutes, then summarizes what they heard. The speaker rates accuracy.

- **Q:** List two active listening behaviors.
  **A:** Eye contact, not interrupting, nodding, paraphrasing ('So you're saying…'), asking questions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peer pressure
*Student:* Role-play responding to peer pressure with assertive communication.

*How to teach:* Role-play peer-pressure situations; coach your child to use an assertive voice and body language.

- **Q:** A friend says 'Just skip practice with us, everyone is.' Give an assertive response.
  **A:** 'No thanks, I'm going to practice. Want to hang out after?'
- **Q:** What is the difference between passive, aggressive and assertive?
  **A:** Passive gives in; aggressive attacks or blames; assertive states your view clearly and respectfully.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on relationship and communication skills.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the assessment key and score one final role-play with the rubric.

- **Q:** Why are I-messages better than you-messages in a conflict?
  **A:** They express feelings without blaming, so the other person is less defensive and more likely to listen.

**Assessment:** Role-play and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) healthy relationship traits: respect, trust, honesty, good communication, support 2) I-message formula: 'I feel ___ when ___ because ___; I'd like ___' 3) active listening: eye contact, don't interrupt, nod, paraphrase, ask questions 4) assertive = stating needs respectfully and firmly 5) example of unhealthy: jealousy/controlling, put-downs; abusive: hitting, threats. Role-play rubric: 4 = firm 'no', assertive body language (eye contact, steady voice), gives reason or alternative, leaves if needed; 3 = says no but hesitates; 2 = gives in partly; 1 = gives in.
