# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 18

## English Language Arts 7: Writing and performing poetry

**Prepare:** Poetry notebook; mentor poems from weeks 16–17; semester checkpoint (choose a passage with questions + 10 grammar items).

**Watch for:**
- Writing imagery of sight only.
- Memorizing lines without pauses for meaning.

### Day 1 (Monday): Imagery poem
*Student:* Write a free-verse poem about a place using imagery of all five senses.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm a place with your child for each sense. Your child writes a free-verse poem of 12+ lines with at least one image for each of the five senses. Read it aloud and identify each sense together.

- **Q:** Example sound image?
  **A:** 'The screen door slaps against its frame.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Form poem
*Student:* Write a sonnet, haiku sequence or ballad stanza modeled on a mentor poem.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a form (sonnet, haiku sequence or ballad stanza) and studies the mentor poem's pattern. They write a poem that follows the form; check syllables, rhyme or meter together. Check before moving on: ask "Haiku pattern?" Look for: Three lines of 5, 7, 5 syllables.

- **Q:** Haiku pattern?
  **A:** Three lines of 5, 7, 5 syllables.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Figurative poem
*Student:* Write an extended-metaphor poem modeled on 'Mother to Son'.

*How to teach:* Reread 'Mother to Son.' Your child chooses an object for life or a challenge (a road, a river, a game) and writes a poem that extends that one metaphor across the whole poem. Check before moving on: ask "What is an extended metaphor?" Look for: A comparison carried through several lines or the whole poem.

- **Q:** What is an extended metaphor?
  **A:** A comparison carried through several lines or the whole poem.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recitation practice
*Student:* Memorize 14+ lines of a studied poem and rehearse with attention to pacing and emphasis.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a studied poem and memorizes at least 14 lines, a stanza at a time. Rehearse with you, focusing on pauses at punctuation (not at line ends) and emphasis on key words. Check before moving on: ask "Tip for memorizing?" Look for: Learn a stanza at a time; practice daily.

- **Q:** Tip for memorizing?
  **A:** Learn a stanza at a time; practice daily.

### Day 5 (Friday): Poetry café & semester check
*Student:* Perform the memorized poem and one original poem for family, then take a semester 1 reading and grammar checkpoint.

*How to teach:* Host a poetry café: your child performs the memorized poem and one original poem. Then give the semester checkpoint independently and note weak areas for review. Check before moving on: ask "Clause or phrase: 'after the storm'?" Look for: Prepositional phrase.

- **Q:** Clause or phrase: 'after the storm'?
  **A:** Prepositional phrase.

**Assessment:** Poetry portfolio (3 poems), recitation rubric and semester checkpoint.

**Answer key:** Portfolio (3 poems): imagery with 5 senses; form poem follows its form; extended metaphor sustained. Recitation rubric: accuracy, volume, pacing, expression. Checkpoint: score by key; note clause, comma and theme errors for review.

## Mathematics 7: Subtracting rational numbers

**Prepare:** Number line, two-color counters, weekly weather table (look up a week of daily highs/lows).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking subtraction always makes smaller. 5 − (−3) = 8.
- Changing only the operation, not the second number's sign, when 'adding the opposite'. a − b = a + (−b): change both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Subtracting with counters
*Student:* Model 3 - 5 and -2 - (-6) with counters, adding zero pairs when needed.

*How to teach:* Model 3 − 5 with counters: you need to remove 5 positives but have 3, so add 2 zero pairs. Your child models −2 − (−6). Check before moving on: ask "3 − 5?" Look for: −2.

- **Q:** 3 − 5?
  **A:** −2.
- **Q:** −2 − (−6)?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add the opposite
*Student:* Rewrite 15 subtraction problems as addition of the opposite and solve.

*How to teach:* Model rewriting a − b as a + (−b). Your child rewrites and solves 15. Check before moving on: ask "−7 − 4?" Look for: −7 + (−4) = −11.

- **Q:** −7 − 4?
  **A:** −7 + (−4) = −11.
- **Q:** −1.5 − (−3.5)?
  **A:** 2.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distance
*Student:* Find distances between elevations like Death Valley (-282 ft) and Mt. Whitney (14,505 ft) using |a - b|.

*How to teach:* Model distance as |a − b|. Your child finds elevation differences. Check before moving on: ask "Mt. Whitney (14,505 ft) to Death Valley (−282 ft)?" Look for: |14,505 − (−282)| = 14,787 ft.

- **Q:** Mt. Whitney (14,505 ft) to Death Valley (−282 ft)?
  **A:** |14,505 − (−282)| = 14,787 ft.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Changes in temperature
*Student:* Compute temperature changes over a week from a weather table and graph them.

*How to teach:* Your child computes day-to-day temperature changes as later − earlier and graphs them. Check before moving on: ask "From −3°C to 5°C, change?" Look for: +8°C.

- **Q:** From −3°C to 5°C, change?
  **A:** +8°C.
- **Q:** From 4°C to −2°C?
  **A:** −6°C.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester review & check
*Student:* Take a cumulative semester 1 checkpoint covering units 1–5 so far.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative checkpoint (see key). Note weak units for review. Check before moving on: ask "8 − (−8)?" Look for: 16.

- **Q:** 8 − (−8)?
  **A:** 16.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 cumulative checkpoint.

**Answer key:** Checkpoint sample answers: 1) Scale factor 6→9: 1.5. 2) 1 cm = 20 km, 4.5 cm: 90 km. 3) (3/4)/(1/2) = 1.5. 4) y = 6x through (1,6). 5) Area r = 4: 16π ≈ 50.24. 6) $90 with 20% off: $72. 7) −8 + 5 = −3. 8) 4 − 9 = −5. 9) −6 − (−2) = −4. 10) Percent change 40 → 30 = 25% decrease. Note any unit below 70% for review.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Decomposers and cycling matter

**Prepare:** Clear jars with lids, soil, bread, apple slices, leaves, spray bottle; magnifier. Do not open moldy jars indoors — mold spores can trigger allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking decomposition destroys matter. Decomposers break it into simpler substances that return to soil and air.
- Thinking mold is a plant. Molds are fungi; they don't photosynthesize.

### Day 1 (Monday): Decomposition jars
*Student:* Set up decomposition jars with bread, apple and leaves under moist and dry conditions to observe over weeks.

*How to teach:* Your child sets up jars with bread, apple and leaves — some moist, some dry — labels and predicts which decompose faster. Check before moving on: ask "Which conditions should speed decomposition?" Look for: Moist and warm — decomposers need water and grow faster when warm.

- **Q:** Which conditions should speed decomposition?
  **A:** Moist and warm — decomposers need water and grow faster when warm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mold growth
*Student:* Observe moldy bread with a magnifier and draw hyphae and spores.

*How to teach:* Through a sealed bag, your child views moldy bread with a magnifier and draws thread-like hyphae and spore heads. Check before moving on: ask "What are hyphae?" Look for: The thread-like filaments of a fungus that grow into food and absorb nutrients.

- **Q:** What are hyphae?
  **A:** The thread-like filaments of a fungus that grow into food and absorb nutrients.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Nitrogen and carbon
*Student:* Diagram how carbon and nitrogen cycle among plants, animals, decomposers and soil.

*How to teach:* Draw the carbon and nitrogen cycles with arrows. Your child labels each step including decomposers. Check before moving on: ask "Role of decomposers in the carbon cycle?" Look for: They break down dead matter and release CO2 through respiration.

- **Q:** Role of decomposers in the carbon cycle?
  **A:** They break down dead matter and release CO2 through respiration.
- **Q:** In the nitrogen cycle?
  **A:** They return nitrogen compounds to the soil for plants to absorb.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compost design
*Student:* Design a small compost bin and explain how it returns matter to the soil.

*How to teach:* Your child designs a compost bin and explains how it returns matter to soil. Check before moving on: ask "What does compost need?" Look for: Green (nitrogen-rich) and brown (carbon-rich) materials, moisture, and air.

- **Q:** What does compost need?
  **A:** Green (nitrogen-rich) and brown (carbon-rich) materials, moisture, and air.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester review & check
*Student:* Take a cumulative semester 1 checkpoint on units 1–4.

*How to teach:* Give the semester checkpoint covering units 1–4. Check before moving on: ask "Why does a balloon of yeast and sugar inflate?" Look for: Yeast respiration produces CO2.

- **Q:** Why does a balloon of yeast and sugar inflate?
  **A:** Yeast respiration produces CO2.

**Assessment:** Decomposition observations and semester checkpoint.

**Answer key:** Decomposition observations: dated entries with sketches. Semester checkpoint: particles; elements/compounds; density; chemical vs physical; balancing; conservation of mass; endo/exothermic; photosynthesis/respiration; food webs and 10% rule. Note any topic below 70% for review.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Church and state; Magna Carta

**Prepare:** Magna Carta translation (British Library); U.S. Bill of Rights.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Magna Carta created democracy immediately. It mainly protected barons at first.
- Thinking habeas corpus came directly in 1215. It developed later (1679 Act).

### Day 1 (Monday): Popes and kings
*Student:* Read about Henry IV and Pope Gregory VII at Canossa and explain the investiture controversy.

*How to teach:* Read about Canossa. Your child explains investiture. Check before moving on: ask "What was the investiture controversy?" Look for: Conflict over who appointed bishops — pope or king.

- **Q:** What was the investiture controversy?
  **A:** Conflict over who appointed bishops — pope or king.
- **Q:** What happened at Canossa (1077)?
  **A:** Henry IV stood in the snow seeking the pope's forgiveness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Magna Carta
*Student:* Read clauses 39 and 40 of the Magna Carta and restate them in modern English.

*How to teach:* Read clauses 39 and 40. Your child restates them. Check before moving on: ask "Clause 39 in modern words?" Look for: No free man can be arrested or punished except by lawful judgment of his peers or the law of the land.

- **Q:** Clause 39 in modern words?
  **A:** No free man can be arrested or punished except by lawful judgment of his peers or the law of the land.
- **Q:** Clause 40?
  **A:** Justice won't be sold, denied or delayed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Parliament
*Student:* Trace the development of Parliament and habeas corpus from Magna Carta.

*How to teach:* Your child traces a timeline from Magna Carta (1215) to the Model Parliament (1295) to the Habeas Corpus Act (1679), explaining each step. Check before moving on: ask "Model Parliament?" Look for: 1295, Edward I.

- **Q:** Model Parliament?
  **A:** 1295, Edward I.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Legacy
*Student:* Connect Magna Carta clauses to the U.S. Bill of Rights in a chart.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a chart connecting Magna Carta clauses to U.S. Bill of Rights amendments. Check before moving on: ask "Clause 39 connects to?" Look for: 5th Amendment due process, 6th Amendment jury trial.

- **Q:** Clause 39 connects to?
  **A:** 5th Amendment due process, 6th Amendment jury trial.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester review & check
*Student:* Take a semester 1 cumulative checkpoint on units 1–6 so far.

*How to teach:* Give the semester 1 checkpoint covering units 1–6. Check before moving on: ask "When was Magna Carta signed?" Look for: 1215, King John at Runnymede.

- **Q:** When was Magna Carta signed?
  **A:** 1215, King John at Runnymede.

**Assessment:** Magna Carta chart and semester checkpoint.

**Answer key:** Magna Carta chart: clause, modern meaning, Bill of Rights connection. Semester checkpoint key: Rome/Byzantium, Islam, China, West Africa, Japan, Europe facts as in prior quizzes.

## Physical Education 7: Strength training with body weight

**Prepare:** Mat, optional resistance band, form checklist for squat, lunge, push-up, plank, glute bridge. Pull the week-2 curl-up and push-up scores.

**Watch for:**
- Letting knees cave inward on squats or lunges. Knees track over toes.
- Holding the breath during strength work. Exhale on the effort (pushing/standing up), inhale on the lowering.

### Day 1 (Monday): Muscle groups
*Student:* Match 10 body-weight exercises to the muscle groups they train.

*How to teach:* Your child matches 10 exercises to muscle groups (see key).

- **Q:** Which muscles does a push-up mainly work?
  **A:** Chest (pectorals), triceps and front shoulders, with the core stabilizing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Form
*Student:* Practice squats, lunges, push-ups, planks and glute bridges with a form checklist.

*How to teach:* Teach form for squats, lunges, push-ups, planks, glute bridges with the checklist; your child coaches you.

- **Q:** Give two cues for a good squat.
  **A:** Feet shoulder-width, sit hips back, chest up, knees track over toes, weight in heels, thighs to about parallel.
- **Q:** When should you exhale during a push-up?
  **A:** On the way up (the effort).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design
*Student:* Design a 6-station strength circuit for all major muscle groups.

*How to teach:* Design a 6-station circuit covering major muscle groups.

- **Q:** Define a rep and a set.
  **A:** A rep is one complete movement; a set is a group of reps done without rest (e.g., 3 sets of 10).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform your circuit for 2 sets and record reps.

*How to teach:* Perform the circuit for 2 sets and record reps.

- **Q:** Why rest 30–60 seconds between strength sets?
  **A:** To let muscles partly recover so you can keep good form.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Re-test push-ups and curl-ups for mid-year progress.

*How to teach:* Re-test push-ups and curl-ups; compare to baseline.

- **Q:** Baseline 12 push-ups, now 17. What is the percent increase?
  **A:** 5 ÷ 12 ≈ 42% increase.

**Assessment:** Circuit design and mid-year re-test.

**Answer key:** Muscle matching key: squat — quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings; lunge — quads, glutes, hamstrings; push-up — chest (pectorals), triceps, shoulders (deltoids), core; plank — abdominals/core; glute bridge — glutes, hamstrings; superman — lower back (erector spinae); calf raise — calves; tricep dip — triceps; wall sit — quadriceps; bicycle crunch — abdominals/obliques. Circuit design proficient: 6 stations covering legs, chest/arms, back, core, with reps/time and rest. Mid-year re-test: compare with baseline and state change.

## Health Education 7: Drugs and the brain

**Prepare:** Explore NIDA for Teens 'Brain and Addiction' pages. Prepare 10 myth/fact cards. Parent preview: units 18–24 cover drugs — make clear your family expectations.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking addiction only happens to 'weak' people. Addiction is a brain disease; teens are more vulnerable because the brain is still developing.
- Believing natural or prescription means safe. Cannabis, tobacco and prescription opioids can all be harmful or addictive.

### Day 1 (Monday): The teen brain
*Student:* Learn how the prefrontal cortex develops into the mid-20s.

*How to teach:* Explain the prefrontal cortex (planning, judgment, impulse control) matures into the mid-20s while the reward system develops earlier.

- **Q:** What does the prefrontal cortex do?
  **A:** Controls decision-making, planning, judgment and impulse control.
- **Q:** At about what age is the brain fully developed?
  **A:** Around the mid-20s (about age 25).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reward pathway
*Student:* Diagram the dopamine reward pathway and how drugs hijack it.

*How to teach:* Your child diagrams the reward pathway (VTA → nucleus accumbens → prefrontal cortex) and labels dopamine.

- **Q:** How do drugs 'hijack' the reward pathway?
  **A:** They cause a much larger dopamine surge than natural rewards, teaching the brain to crave the drug and making everyday pleasures feel less rewarding.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Addiction
*Student:* Explain tolerance, dependence and withdrawal.

*How to teach:* Define tolerance, dependence, withdrawal, addiction with a nicotine example.

- **Q:** What is tolerance?
  **A:** Needing more of a drug to get the same effect.
- **Q:** What is withdrawal?
  **A:** Uncomfortable physical and mental symptoms (irritability, cravings, headache) when a person stops using a drug they depend on.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Myths vs facts
*Student:* Sort 10 drug myths and facts.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 10 cards into myths and facts and explains each.

- **Q:** Myth or fact: 'Prescription drugs are safe to share because a doctor prescribed them.'
  **A:** Myth — they're prescribed for one person's body and condition; misuse can cause overdose or addiction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on drugs and the brain.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key.

- **Q:** Why are teens more likely than adults to become addicted?
  **A:** Their brains are still developing; the reward system is mature but impulse control isn't, and early use changes brain wiring.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) prefrontal cortex controls judgment, planning, impulse control; develops into mid-20s 2) dopamine = brain chemical linked to pleasure/reward and motivation 3) drugs flood the reward pathway with dopamine, so natural rewards feel less satisfying 4) tolerance = needing more for the same effect 5) dependence = body needs the drug to feel normal 6) withdrawal = unpleasant symptoms when stopping 7) addiction = compulsive use despite harm 8) why teens are at higher risk — developing brain. Myth/fact examples: 'You can't get addicted to vaping' — myth; 'Most teens don't use drugs' — fact.
