# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 13

## English Language Arts 7: Paired texts: same topic, different authors

**Prepare:** Two articles on zoos (one pro, one con — CommonLit, Newsela or Britannica ProCon); highlighters in three colors (statistics, expert, anecdote).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking both authors must disagree on facts. They often use different facts or emphasize different ones.
- Writing a position with no counterclaim.

### Day 1 (Monday): Text A
*Student:* Read an article arguing zoos protect endangered species and highlight evidence types (statistics, expert, anecdote).

*How to teach:* Read the pro-zoo article together. Your child highlights statistics in one color, expert quotes in another and anecdotes in a third, then counts each type. Ask which type the author relies on most.

- **Q:** Example of expert evidence?
  **A:** A quote from a zoologist or conservation scientist.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Text B
*Student:* Read an article arguing zoos harm animals and highlight its evidence the same way.

*How to teach:* Read the anti-zoo article and highlight it the same way. Your child compares the counts with Text A and notes which facts appear in only one article. Check before moving on: ask "Example of anecdotal evidence?" Look for: A story about a single animal showing stress behaviors.

- **Q:** Example of anecdotal evidence?
  **A:** A story about a single animal showing stress behaviors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare authors
*Student:* Write a comparison paragraph on how each author emphasizes different evidence and facts.

*How to teach:* Model a contrast sentence ('While A emphasizes…, B focuses on…'). Your child writes a paragraph comparing how the two authors use different evidence and what effect each choice has on the reader. Check before moving on: ask "Model sentence?" Look for: 'While Author A emphasizes breeding programs for endangered species, Author B focuses on animals' stress in captivity.'

- **Q:** Model sentence?
  **A:** 'While Author A emphasizes breeding programs for endangered species, Author B focuses on animals' stress in captivity.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Taking a position
*Student:* Choose a position on 'Should zoos exist?' and fill an evidence organizer with at least 5 pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a position on 'Should zoos exist?' and fills the organizer with at least five pieces of evidence from both articles, noting the source and type. Check that each piece directly supports the position. Check before moving on: ask "What makes evidence strong?" Look for: Specific, credible source, directly supports the claim.

- **Q:** What makes evidence strong?
  **A:** Specific, credible source, directly supports the claim.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draft a claim and counterclaim statement and get feedback.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts a one-sentence claim and a counterclaim-with-rebuttal sentence. Give feedback: is the claim arguable, and does the rebuttal answer the opposing reason with evidence? Check before moving on: ask "Example counterclaim sentence?" Look for: 'Some argue that zoos keep animals in unnatural conditions; however, accredited zoos…'

- **Q:** Example counterclaim sentence?
  **A:** 'Some argue that zoos keep animals in unnatural conditions; however, accredited zoos…'

**Assessment:** Comparison paragraph and evidence organizer.

**Answer key:** Comparison paragraph rubric: 4 = names each author's claim, contrasts specific evidence types with examples, explains the effect of each emphasis. Evidence organizer: 5+ items with source and type.

## Mathematics 7: Percent increase and decrease

**Prepare:** Store ads or flyers, grid paper for tape diagrams, calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Finding percent change using the new value as the base. Always divide the change by the ORIGINAL amount.
- Thinking a 25% increase followed by a 25% decrease returns the original. Try $100 → $125 → $93.75.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lots of fractions
*Student:* Use tape diagrams to show 1/4 more and 1/4 less than a quantity and connect to 125% and 75%.

*How to teach:* Draw a tape of 4 equal parts for a quantity; add a part (5/4 = 125%) and remove a part (3/4 = 75%). Your child draws diagrams for several quantities. Check before moving on: ask "1/4 more than 60?" Look for: 75 (125% of 60).

- **Q:** 1/4 more than 60?
  **A:** 75 (125% of 60).
- **Q:** 1/4 less than 60?
  **A:** 45 (75% of 60).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Percent increase
*Student:* Compute new prices after increases of 10%, 15% and 120% using multiplication by 1.10, 1.15 and 2.20.

*How to teach:* Model multiplying by 1.10 for a 10% increase. Your child computes new prices for 10%, 15% and 120% increases. Check before moving on: ask "$40 increased by 15%?" Look for: 40 × 1.15 = $46.

- **Q:** $40 increased by 15%?
  **A:** 40 × 1.15 = $46.
- **Q:** $40 increased by 120%?
  **A:** 40 × 2.20 = $88.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Percent decrease
*Student:* Compute sale prices after discounts and find the original price given the sale price.

*How to teach:* Model 25% off as × 0.75 and working backward. Your child finds sale and original prices. Check before moving on: ask "$80 jacket, 30% off?" Look for: $56.

- **Q:** $80 jacket, 30% off?
  **A:** $56.
- **Q:** Sale price $36 after 25% off. Original?
  **A:** 36 ÷ 0.75 = $48.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finding percent change
*Student:* Find percent change from old to new values for populations, prices and test scores.

*How to teach:* Model percent change = change ÷ original × 100. Your child computes changes for populations, prices and scores. Check before moving on: ask "80 → 100?" Look for: 25% increase.

- **Q:** 80 → 100?
  **A:** 25% increase.
- **Q:** 100 → 80?
  **A:** 20% decrease.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on percent increase and decrease with tape diagrams and equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Price went from $50 to $65. Percent increase?" Look for: 30%.

- **Q:** Price went from $50 to $65. Percent increase?
  **A:** 30%.

**Assessment:** Percent change quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) $60 + 20% = $72. 2) $250 − 40% = $150. 3) Sale $45 after 10% off → original $50. 4) 40 → 50 = 25% increase. 5) 50 → 40 = 20% decrease. 6) Tape diagram for 150% of 8 = 12.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Food, energy and the body

**Prepare:** Iodine (adult), brown paper bag, bread, potato, oil, butter, cracker; five nutrition labels; cheese puff, soda can, thermometer, paper clip stand, matches (adult only, outdoors or over sink).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Calories are an ingredient. A Calorie is a unit of energy.
- Thinking all food energy is used immediately.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nutrient tests
*Student:* Test foods for starch with iodine and for fat with brown paper and record results.

*How to teach:* Your child tests foods with iodine (blue-black = starch) and brown paper (translucent spot = fat). Check before moving on: ask "Potato with iodine?" Look for: Turns blue-black — contains starch.

- **Q:** Potato with iodine?
  **A:** Turns blue-black — contains starch.
- **Q:** Butter on brown paper?
  **A:** Leaves a translucent grease spot — fat.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Nutrition labels
*Student:* Analyze five nutrition labels to compare calories from carbohydrates, fats and proteins.

*How to teach:* Your child compares five labels; note fat = 9 Cal/g, carbs and protein = 4 Cal/g. Check before moving on: ask "A snack with 10 g fat — Calories from fat?" Look for: 90 Calories.

- **Q:** A snack with 10 g fat — Calories from fat?
  **A:** 90 Calories.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Energy in a peanut (demo)
*Student:* With an adult, burn a cheese puff under a can of water and calculate the temperature change as evidence of stored energy.

*How to teach:* Adult burns a cheese puff under a can with 50 mL water. Your child calculates. Check before moving on: ask "Water 50 g rises 10 °C. Energy?" Look for: 50 × 10 = 500 calories = 0.5 food Calories (about 2,090 J).

- **Q:** Water 50 g rises 10 °C. Energy?
  **A:** 50 × 10 = 500 calories = 0.5 food Calories (about 2,090 J).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Growth and repair
*Student:* Read about how proteins are broken into amino acids and rebuilt into body proteins and summarize in a flowchart.

*How to teach:* Read and flowchart protein to amino acids to body proteins. Check before moving on: ask "Why can't the body use whole proteins from food?" Look for: They must be broken down into amino acids to be absorbed and rearranged.

- **Q:** Why can't the body use whole proteins from food?
  **A:** They must be broken down into amino acids to be absorbed and rearranged.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an explanation of how a sandwich becomes both energy and muscle.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how a sandwich becomes energy and muscle. Check before moving on: ask "Key answer?" Look for: Digestion breaks food into small molecules; cells use sugars in respiration for energy and rebuild amino acids into muscle.

- **Q:** Key answer?
  **A:** Digestion breaks food into small molecules; cells use sugars in respiration for energy and rebuild amino acids into muscle.

**Assessment:** Food-testing results and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Results table: starch in bread/potato, fat in butter/oil. Explanation rubric: digestion, absorption, respiration for energy, synthesis for growth; mentions atoms rearranged.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Oral traditions and griots

**Prepare:** Recorded griot performance with kora (search 'griot kora'); 8 West African proverbs; interview questions for a relative.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking oral history is unreliable compared with written history. Griots trained for years to memorize and preserve accurate histories and genealogies.
- Thinking proverbs are just sayings. They pass on values and wisdom.

### Day 1 (Monday): Griots
*Student:* Listen to a recorded griot performance with kora and explain the griot's role as historian.

*How to teach:* Listen to a griot. Your child explains the griot's role. Check before moving on: ask "What is a griot?" Look for: A West African storyteller, historian and musician who preserves family histories, genealogies and events orally.

- **Q:** What is a griot?
  **A:** A West African storyteller, historian and musician who preserves family histories, genealogies and events orally.
- **Q:** What is a kora?
  **A:** A 21-string harp-lute.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Proverbs
*Student:* Interpret 8 West African proverbs and write two of your own family proverbs.

*How to teach:* Your child interprets 8 proverbs and writes two family proverbs. Check before moving on: ask "Interpret: 'Rain does not fall on one roof alone.'" Look for: Troubles come to everyone; we share hardships.

- **Q:** Interpret: 'Rain does not fall on one roof alone.'
  **A:** Troubles come to everyone; we share hardships.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Oral history project
*Student:* Interview an older relative about family history and retell it as a griot would.

*How to teach:* Your child interviews a relative and retells it as a griot would. Check before moving on: ask "What makes it griot-like?" Look for: Spoken aloud from memory, with names, ancestors and a lesson.

- **Q:** What makes it griot-like?
  **A:** Spoken aloud from memory, with names, ancestors and a lesson.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Songhai preview
*Student:* Read briefly about Songhai's rise after Mali and add it to the timeline.

*How to teach:* Read about Songhai's rise. Check before moving on: ask "Who expanded Songhai?" Look for: Sunni Ali and Askia Muhammad.

- **Q:** Who expanded Songhai?
  **A:** Sunni Ali and Askia Muhammad.
- **Q:** When did Songhai fall?
  **A:** 1591, to a Moroccan army with guns.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 4 test and perform the oral family story.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 4 test; your child performs the oral story. Check before moving on: ask "Three West African empires in order?" Look for: Ghana, Mali, Songhai.

- **Q:** Three West African empires in order?
  **A:** Ghana, Mali, Songhai.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test and oral history performance.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test: vegetation zones, gold-salt trade, Ghana, Sundiata, Mansa Musa 1324, Timbuktu, Ibn Battuta, griots, Songhai. Oral history rubric: memorized, includes names and events, clear lesson, expressive delivery.

## Spanish 1A: A mí también, a mí tampoco: agreeing, disagreeing, and le gusta

**Prepare:** Survey sheet, 15 statements to read aloud (Me gusta nadar, No me gusta cocinar…).

**Watch for:**
- Answering a negative statement with 'a mí también.' Agree with a negative using 'a mí tampoco'; disagree with 'a mí sí.'
- Saying 'Ella gusta…' Use 'A ella le gusta…'

### Day 1 (Monday): También/tampoco
*Student:* Learn a mí también, a mí tampoco, a mí sí/no and practice reacting to 15 statements read by a parent.

*How to teach:* React to 15 statements: positive + agree → a mí también; positive + disagree → a mí no; negative + agree → a mí tampoco; negative + disagree → a mí sí.

- **Q:** Statement: 'Me gusta nadar.' You also like it. React.
  **A:** A mí también.
- **Q:** Statement: 'No me gusta correr.' You do like it. React.
  **A:** A mí sí.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Le gusta
*Student:* Use a él/a ella le gusta to report what three relatives like; write a paragraph about each.

*How to teach:* Report relatives' likes with a él/a ella le gusta.

- **Q:** Write: 'My grandmother likes to cook.'
  **A:** A mi abuela le gusta cocinar.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Negatives
*Student:* Practice no...nada, ni...ni, and tampoco in 10 written sentences.

*How to teach:* Practice no…nada, ni…ni, tampoco in 10 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'I don't like to run or swim.'
  **A:** No me gusta ni correr ni nadar.
- **Q:** Write: 'I don't like to do anything.'
  **A:** No me gusta hacer nada.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Encuesta
*Student:* Survey five people (in person or by video call) on their favorite activities and graph the results with Spanish labels.

*How to teach:* Survey five people; graph results with Spanish labels.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What do you like to do?'
  **A:** ¿Qué te gusta hacer?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold a 2-minute unscripted conversation about likes and dislikes and take a short writing check.

*How to teach:* Hold a 2-minute conversation; give the writing check.

- **Q:** Statement: 'No me gusta dibujar.' You don't either. React.
  **A:** A mí tampoco.

**Assessment:** Interpersonal conversation rubric and writing check.

**Answer key:** Conversation rubric (1–4): asks and answers about likes, reacts with también/tampoco/sí/no correctly, keeps going 2 minutes, comprehensibility. Writing check key samples: 'My brother likes to skate' → A mi hermano le gusta patinar; 'I don't like to sing or dance' → No me gusta ni cantar ni bailar; 'I don't either' → A mí tampoco.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Musical form and expression

**Prepare:** Recordings of Beethoven's Für Elise and Mozart's Twelve Variations on 'Ah vous dirai-je, Maman' (K. 265, the 'Twinkle' tune). Two different performances of one piece. Colored paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'piano' means slow. Piano means soft; tempo terms (adagio, allegro) describe speed.
- Thinking a variation is a new song. Each variation keeps the theme's structure but changes rhythm, harmony, texture or style.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dynamics and tempo
*Student:* Learn Italian dynamic and tempo terms and mark them while listening to a piece.

*How to teach:* Teach Italian terms; mark them on a listening chart as the music plays.

- **Q:** What does 'forte' mean?
  **A:** Loud.
- **Q:** What does 'allegro' mean?
  **A:** Fast, lively.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rondo
*Student:* Map the form of 'Für Elise' (ABACA) with colored blocks.

*How to teach:* Map Für Elise with colored blocks: A (famous theme), B, A, C, A.

- **Q:** What is rondo form?
  **A:** A recurring main theme (A) alternating with contrasting sections: A-B-A-C-A.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Variations
*Student:* Listen to Mozart's 'Twelve Variations on Ah vous dirai-je, Maman' and describe how each variation changes.

*How to teach:* Listen to several Mozart variations; describe the change in each (faster notes, minor key, melody in the left hand…).

- **Q:** What famous tune is the theme of Mozart's variations?
  **A:** The melody known as 'Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star' (also the ABC song).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpretation
*Student:* Compare two performances of the same piece and explain how performers' choices change expression.

*How to teach:* Compare two performances (tempo, dynamics, articulation).

- **Q:** How can two performers make the same piece sound different?
  **A:** By choosing different tempos, dynamics, articulation (staccato/legato) and phrasing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a form and expression quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the form and expression quiz.

- **Q:** What is the difference between staccato and legato?
  **A:** Staccato is short and detached; legato is smooth and connected.

**Assessment:** Form maps and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: dynamics pp (pianissimo, very soft), p (piano, soft), mp (mezzo piano), mf (mezzo forte), f (forte, loud), ff (fortissimo, very loud), crescendo (gradually louder), decrescendo/diminuendo (gradually softer); tempo largo (very slow), adagio (slow), andante (walking), moderato (moderate), allegro (fast), presto (very fast); articulation staccato (short, detached), legato (smooth, connected). Für Elise form: rondo A-B-A-C-A. Theme and variations: a theme followed by altered versions.

## Physical Education 7: Badminton

**Prepare:** Badminton rackets, shuttlecocks, a net or rope at about 5 ft. Mark a simple court with cones if outdoors (avoid wind).

**Watch for:**
- Gripping the racket like a frying pan with the whole fist. Use a 'handshake' grip and relaxed fingers.
- Hitting every shot hard. Mixing deep clears and soft drop shots moves the opponent and creates openings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grips and serve
*Student:* Learn forehand and backhand grips and practice short and long serves.

*How to teach:* Teach forehand handshake grip and backhand (thumb on the back of the handle). Practice short serves (low over net to front) and long serves (high and deep).

- **Q:** Where must the serve be contacted?
  **A:** Below the waist, hit underhand, diagonally into the opponent's service court.
- **Q:** When would you use a long serve?
  **A:** In singles to push the opponent to the back of the court.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Clear
*Student:* Practice overhead clears to the back of the court.

*How to teach:* Teach the overhead clear: side-on, racket back, reach high, contact the shuttle above and in front, hit up and deep.

- **Q:** What is the purpose of a clear?
  **A:** To hit the shuttle high and deep to the back of the opponent's court, giving you time and pushing them back.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Drop shot
*Student:* Practice drop shots and discuss when to use them.

*How to teach:* Teach the drop shot: same preparation as a clear, but softly guide the shuttle to just over the net.

- **Q:** When should you use a drop shot?
  **A:** When your opponent is deep in the back court, so the shuttle lands far from them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game play
*Student:* Play singles games applying clears and drops to move the opponent.

*How to teach:* Play singles; coach returning to the center base after each shot.

- **Q:** Why return to base position?
  **A:** The center gives the shortest distance to reach any next shot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a rally-count test with a partner.

*How to teach:* Run the rally-count test.

- **Q:** What is a smash?
  **A:** A powerful downward overhead shot used to end a rally when the shuttle is high and short.

**Assessment:** Rally test and game strategy observation.

**Answer key:** Rally-count test: partners count longest controlled rally in 3 tries (clears and drops). 15+ proficient, 25+ advanced. Strategy observation: uses clears to push opponent back, drops when opponent is deep, returns to base position (center) after each shot. Rules key: serve underhand below the waist diagonally; rally scoring to 21, win by 2; shuttle landing on the line is in.

## Health Education 7: Injury prevention

**Prepare:** Look up CDC WISQARS or a CDC fact sheet on teen injuries (leading causes for ages 10–14: motor-vehicle crashes, suffocation, drowning, falls; for 15–19 motor-vehicle crashes lead). Print a home safety checklist; have a bike helmet ready.

**Watch for:**
- Calling injuries 'accidents' that can't be prevented. Most unintentional injuries are predictable and preventable.
- Thinking you must lose consciousness to have a concussion. Most concussions do not involve passing out.

### Day 1 (Monday): Leading causes
*Student:* Analyze CDC data on leading causes of teen injury.

*How to teach:* Look at CDC data together. Your child identifies the top causes of injury death for teens and one prevention for each.

- **Q:** What is the leading cause of injury death for U.S. teens?
  **A:** Motor-vehicle crashes.
- **Q:** Name one way to prevent motor-vehicle injuries as a passenger.
  **A:** Always wear a seat belt; ride in the back seat until age 13; don't distract the driver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Home safety audit
*Student:* Complete a home safety audit for falls, fire and poisoning.

*How to teach:* Your child walks the house with the checklist, testing a smoke alarm and noting hazards.

- **Q:** How often should smoke alarms be tested?
  **A:** Monthly (replace batteries at least yearly or as directed).
- **Q:** Where should medicines and cleaners be stored?
  **A:** Locked or high, out of children's reach, in original containers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sports safety
*Student:* Check helmet fit and learn about concussion signs.

*How to teach:* Fit a helmet: level on head, about two finger-widths above the eyebrows, side straps forming a V under the ears, chin strap snug (one finger fits). Teach concussion signs.

- **Q:** Name three concussion signs.
  **A:** Headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea/vomiting, blurred vision, sensitivity to light, memory problems.
- **Q:** What should an athlete do if a concussion is suspected?
  **A:** Stop playing immediately ('when in doubt, sit it out') and see a doctor before returning.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Water and bike safety
*Student:* Learn water safety and bike/scooter rules of the road.

*How to teach:* Teach water safety (swim with a buddy, lifeguarded areas, life jackets, never dive into unknown water) and bike rules (ride with traffic, obey signals, be visible).

- **Q:** Which direction should you ride a bike on the road?
  **A:** With traffic, on the right side.
- **Q:** Why never dive into shallow or unknown water?
  **A:** Risk of hitting the bottom and causing head or spinal cord injury.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a family injury-prevention plan.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a family injury-prevention plan using the audit results.

- **Q:** Give one fix for a fall hazard.
  **A:** Secure loose rugs with backing, add lighting/handrails on stairs, clean up clutter.

**Assessment:** Home safety audit and plan.

**Answer key:** Home audit: checks smoke detectors (test monthly), fire extinguisher, escape routes, medicines and cleaners locked/high, rugs and stairs for fall hazards, carbon monoxide detector; lists 3 hazards found with fixes. Family plan rubric: 4 = addresses falls, fire, poisoning, vehicle/bike/water safety with specific actions and who is responsible; 3 = three areas; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
