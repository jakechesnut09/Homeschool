# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 16

## English Language Arts 7: Sound and figurative language in poetry

**Prepare:** Print 'Paul Revere's Ride' (Longfellow), 'Annabel Lee' (Poe), 'Mother to Son' (Hughes); colored pencils; 10 word analogies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every poem must rhyme.
- Confusing alliteration (repeated initial consonant sounds) with any repetition.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rhythm and story
*Student:* Read 'Paul Revere's Ride' aloud, tap the galloping meter, and mark rhyme scheme in two stanzas.

*How to teach:* Read the first stanzas of 'Paul Revere's Ride' aloud while your child taps the galloping rhythm. Model labeling a rhyme scheme with letters; your child labels two stanzas. Ask how the rhythm matches the story.

- **Q:** Rhyme scheme of 'Listen, my children, and you shall hear / Of the midnight ride of Paul Revere, / On the eighteenth of April, in Seventy-five; / Hardly a man is now alive / Who remembers that famous day and year.'
  **A:** AABBA.
- **Q:** What is the signal?
  **A:** 'One if by land, and two if by sea' — lanterns in the Old North Church tower.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sound devices
*Student:* Annotate 'Annabel Lee' for repetition, internal rhyme and alliteration and explain how they create a haunting tone.

*How to teach:* Read 'Annabel Lee' aloud slowly. Your child annotates repetition ('kingdom by the sea', 'Annabel Lee'), internal rhyme and alliteration, then writes how these sounds create a haunting, mournful tone. Check before moving on: ask "Internal rhyme example?" Look for: 'For the moon never beams, without bringing me dreams'.

- **Q:** Internal rhyme example?
  **A:** 'For the moon never beams, without bringing me dreams'.
- **Q:** What does the speaker claim killed Annabel Lee?
  **A:** A wind from a cloud, sent because the angels envied their love.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Extended metaphor
*Student:* Analyze the staircase metaphor in Hughes' 'Mother to Son' and write what it means for life's struggles.

*How to teach:* Read 'Mother to Son' aloud. Your child lists each part of the staircase (tacks, splinters, boards torn up, no carpet) and what hardship it could represent, then writes what the poem says about facing life's struggles. Check before moving on: ask "What does 'Life for me ain't been no crystal stair' mean?" Look for: Her life has not been easy or elegant; it has been hard.

- **Q:** What does 'Life for me ain't been no crystal stair' mean?
  **A:** Her life has not been easy or elegant; it has been hard.
- **Q:** What does she urge her son?
  **A:** Not to turn back or give up — keep climbing.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Allusions & analogies
*Student:* Learn common mythological and biblical allusions (Achilles' heel, Pandora's box, Good Samaritan) and complete 10 word analogies.

*How to teach:* Tell the stories behind Achilles' heel, Pandora's box and the Good Samaritan. Your child explains each allusion's modern meaning, then completes 10 analogies, saying the relationship aloud ('A is to B as…'). Check before moving on: ask "Achilles' heel?" Look for: A weak spot.

- **Q:** Achilles' heel?
  **A:** A weak spot.
- **Q:** Pandora's box?
  **A:** Something that unleashes many troubles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a poetry-terms quiz and analyze an unseen short poem.

*How to teach:* Give the poetry-terms quiz, then an unseen short poem; your child identifies two devices and explains their effect on tone. Review together. Check before moving on: ask "What is meter?" Look for: The rhythmic pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.

- **Q:** What is meter?
  **A:** The rhythmic pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.

**Assessment:** Annotations and poetry quiz.

**Answer key:** Poetry quiz: alliteration, internal rhyme, meter, rhyme scheme, extended metaphor, allusion, tone — each defined with an example from the week. Unseen poem proficient answer identifies two devices and explains their effect on tone.

## Mathematics 7: Percent project & unit test

**Prepare:** Store flyers or online pages; spreadsheet or paper; local sales tax rate.

**Watch for:**
- Calculating percent saved from the sale price instead of the original. Savings ÷ original total.
- Applying a percent coupon to an already-applied dollar coupon in the wrong order. Follow the store's stated order.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shopping project
*Student:* Choose a $200 back-to-school budget and select items from ads, applying discounts and coupons.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses items within $200, listing original price and any discounts. Check before moving on: ask "A $60 backpack at 25% off?" Look for: $45.

- **Q:** A $60 backpack at 25% off?
  **A:** $45.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adding tax
*Student:* Add sales tax, compute total savings as a percent of original price, and adjust to stay within budget.

*How to teach:* Your child adds tax to the discounted subtotal and computes total savings percent, adjusting to stay within budget. Check before moving on: ask "Subtotal $180 at 8% tax?" Look for: $194.40.

- **Q:** Subtotal $180 at 8% tax?
  **A:** $194.40.
- **Q:** Original $230, paid (before tax) $180. Percent saved?
  **A:** 50 ÷ 230 ≈ 21.7%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentation
*Student:* Create a one-page receipt-style summary showing every calculation and the percent saved.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a receipt-style summary with every calculation. Check before moving on: ask "Why show every calculation?" Look for: So each step can be checked and errors found.

- **Q:** Why show every calculation?
  **A:** So each step can be checked and errors found.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of percent change, tax, tips, interest and error.

*How to teach:* Mixed review — one problem of each type; independent practice. Check before moving on: ask "$150 → $120. Percent change?" Look for: 20% decrease.

- **Q:** $150 → $120. Percent change?
  **A:** 20% decrease.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the unit 4 test on percentages.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 4 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "$300 at 2% for 3 years simple interest?" Look for: $18.

- **Q:** $300 at 2% for 3 years simple interest?
  **A:** $18.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test and budget project.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test sample answers: 1) $75 + 12% = $84. 2) $120 − 35% = $78. 3) Sale $68 after 15% off: original $80. 4) 25 → 30: 20% increase. 5) 18% tip on $50: $9. 6) $400 at 3.5% for 2 yr: $28. 7) Estimate 12, actual 15: 20% error. Project rubric: 4 = within $200 with tax, every discount and tax correct, percent saved correct; 3 = one error; 2 = several; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Interactions among organisms

**Prepare:** 20 organism-pair cards (e.g., bee/flower, tick/dog, remora/shark, lion/zebra, two trees for light), notebook, timer for outdoor survey.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up mutualism (both benefit) and commensalism (one benefits, other unaffected).
- Thinking competition only happens between different species. Members of the same species compete too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interaction types
*Student:* Sort 20 organism-pair cards into predation, competition, mutualism, commensalism and parasitism.

*How to teach:* Define the five interaction types with an example each. Your child sorts the 20 cards and justifies each. Check before moving on: ask "Bee and flower?" Look for: Mutualism — the bee gets nectar, the flower gets pollinated.

- **Q:** Bee and flower?
  **A:** Mutualism — the bee gets nectar, the flower gets pollinated.
- **Q:** Barnacles on a whale?
  **A:** Commensalism — barnacles get a ride and food; the whale is mostly unaffected.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Backyard survey
*Student:* Observe a yard or park for 30 minutes and record organisms and any interactions seen.

*How to teach:* Spend 30 quiet minutes outside. Your child records organisms, counts and any interactions (a bird eating seeds, ants on a plant). Check before moving on: ask "Why sit still and quiet?" Look for: Animals return and behave naturally when not disturbed.

- **Q:** Why sit still and quiet?
  **A:** Animals return and behave naturally when not disturbed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Across ecosystems
*Student:* Compare interactions in a kelp forest, desert and tropical rainforest and identify repeating patterns.

*How to teach:* Your child compares one example of each interaction in a kelp forest, desert and rainforest. Check before moving on: ask "What pattern repeats across ecosystems?" Look for: All have predation, competition and mutualism, even though the organisms differ.

- **Q:** What pattern repeats across ecosystems?
  **A:** All have predation, competition and mutualism, even though the organisms differ.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sea otters
*Student:* Read about California sea otters, urchins and kelp and explain the predator-prey pattern.

*How to teach:* Read about California sea otters. Your child explains the otter–urchin–kelp relationship. Check before moving on: ask "What happens to kelp if sea otters disappear?" Look for: Urchins multiply and overgraze kelp, creating 'urchin barrens'.

- **Q:** What happens to kelp if sea otters disappear?
  **A:** Urchins multiply and overgraze kelp, creating 'urchin barrens'.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an explanation predicting what would happen if a predator were removed from an ecosystem.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a prediction of what happens when a predator is removed from an ecosystem. Check before moving on: ask "Possible prediction?" Look for: Prey population rises, overeats its food, then may crash; other species are affected too (trophic cascade).

- **Q:** Possible prediction?
  **A:** Prey population rises, overeats its food, then may crash; other species are affected too (trophic cascade).

**Assessment:** Survey log and written prediction.

**Answer key:** Survey log: date, time, location, organisms with counts, at least two interactions classified. Prediction rubric: 4 = names predator/prey, predicts prey increase and resource decline with reasoning and a real example (otters/urchins/kelp or wolves/elk in Yellowstone); 2 = prediction without reasoning.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Geography of Europe and the early Middle Ages

**Prepare:** Physical map of Europe; Rule of St. Benedict excerpt; Book of Kells images; gold marker; Einhard's Life of Charlemagne excerpt.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Middle Ages were 'dark' with no learning. Monasteries preserved books and learning.
- Thinking Charlemagne was French or German only. His empire covered much of both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Europe's geography
*Student:* Map Europe's rivers, mountains, plains and seas and explain their effects on trade and settlement.

*How to teach:* Your child labels a physical map of Europe: Rhine, Danube, Alps, Pyrenees, North European Plain, Mediterranean, North and Baltic seas, then explains how each affected trade and settlement. Check before moving on: ask "How did rivers help?" Look for: Rhine and Danube were trade routes.

- **Q:** How did rivers help?
  **A:** Rhine and Danube were trade routes.
- **Q:** Mountain barriers?
  **A:** Alps and Pyrenees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Monasteries
*Student:* Read the Rule of St. Benedict excerpt and describe daily life in a monastery.

*How to teach:* Read the Rule. Your child describes monastery life. Check before moving on: ask "Daily life?" Look for: Prayer at set hours, manual work, reading, obedience — 'pray and work'.

- **Q:** Daily life?
  **A:** Prayer at set hours, manual work, reading, obedience — 'pray and work'.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Illuminated manuscripts
*Student:* Study images of the Book of Kells and create an illuminated first letter of your name.

*How to teach:* Show Book of Kells images. Your child designs an illuminated first letter of their name with interlace patterns and gold marker. Check before moving on: ask "What is an illuminated manuscript?" Look for: Handwritten book decorated with gold, color and designs.

- **Q:** What is an illuminated manuscript?
  **A:** Handwritten book decorated with gold, color and designs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Charlemagne
*Student:* Read Einhard's Life of Charlemagne excerpt and list his achievements.

*How to teach:* Read Einhard. Your child lists achievements. Check before moving on: ask "When was Charlemagne crowned emperor?" Look for: 800 CE by Pope Leo III.

- **Q:** When was Charlemagne crowned emperor?
  **A:** 800 CE by Pope Leo III.
- **Q:** Achievements?
  **A:** Unified much of western Europe, promoted learning and schools, spread Christianity.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on geography and the early Middle Ages.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Why were monasteries important?" Look for: Preserved learning, copied books, helped the poor.

- **Q:** Why were monasteries important?
  **A:** Preserved learning, copied books, helped the poor.

**Assessment:** Map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: rivers, mountains, plains; Benedict's Rule; monks copied manuscripts; Charlemagne crowned 800; Carolingian Renaissance.

## Spanish 1A: Mi horario: school subjects and numbers to 100

**Prepare:** Blank weekly schedule, price ads, bingo cards with numbers 32–100. Read about school in Mexico or Chile.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 'treintaidos.' From 31 on, tens and units are separate words with y: treinta y dos.
- Adjective agreement with subjects: 'las matemáticas son difíciles' (plural).

### Day 1 (Monday): Subjects
*Student:* Learn the school subjects and ordinal numbers 1st–8th and fill out your weekly schedule in Spanish with times.

*How to teach:* Learn subjects and ordinals 1st–8th; fill a schedule.

- **Q:** Say: 'In first period I have science.'
  **A:** En la primera hora tengo ciencias.
- **Q:** What is the ordinal for 3rd?
  **A:** tercero/tercera (tercer before a masculine singular noun).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Números 32–100
*Student:* Learn tens and combinations (treinta y dos ... cien) and play number bingo with a parent.

*How to teach:* Learn tens (treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien); play bingo.

- **Q:** Write 76 in words.
  **A:** setenta y seis.
- **Q:** Write 100.
  **A:** cien.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Opinions
*Student:* Write opinions about each subject using es interesante/difícil/aburrida and me gusta.

*How to teach:* Write opinions about each subject.

- **Q:** Write: 'Math is difficult but interesting.'
  **A:** Las matemáticas son difíciles pero interesantes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Culture: la escuela
*Student:* Read about school in Mexico or Chile (uniforms, recreo, schedules) and list four differences from your homeschool day.

*How to teach:* Read about school in Mexico/Chile; list 4 differences.

  **A:** Recess/break.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer schedule questions in a short interview and take a numbers-to-100 dictation.

*How to teach:* Interview and give the number dictation.

  **A:** 89.

**Assessment:** Schedule interview and number dictation.

**Answer key:** Schedule interview: answers ¿Qué clase tienes en la primera hora? (En la primera hora tengo matemáticas), ¿A qué hora es el almuerzo?, ¿Qué clase te gusta más? Ordinals: primero, segundo, tercero, cuarto, quinto, sexto, séptimo, octavo (primer/tercer before masculine nouns). Numbers dictation key: 32 treinta y dos, 45 cuarenta y cinco, 58 cincuenta y ocho, 67 sesenta y siete, 74 setenta y cuatro, 83 ochenta y tres, 96 noventa y seis, 100 cien. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Composition: melody

**Prepare:** Chrome Music Lab Song Maker (free) and/or MuseScore. Set the key to C major.

**Watch for:**
- Writing random notes without repetition. Unity comes from repeating and developing a motif.
- Ending on any note. Ending on the tonic (C in C major) sounds finished.

### Day 1 (Monday): Motifs
*Student:* Create three 2-bar motifs using Song Maker and choose the strongest.

*How to teach:* Create three 2-bar motifs in Song Maker; choose the strongest.

- **Q:** What is a motif?
  **A:** A short musical idea that repeats and develops through a piece.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Phrases
*Student:* Extend the motif into a 4-bar question phrase and 4-bar answer phrase.

*How to teach:* Extend into a 4-bar question (ends unresolved) and 4-bar answer (ends resolved).

- **Q:** What is a phrase?
  **A:** A musical 'sentence,' often 4 bars long, ending with a cadence.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cadences
*Student:* End the melody on the tonic for a strong cadence and test alternatives.

*How to teach:* End on the tonic; compare with ending on other notes.

- **Q:** What is a cadence?
  **A:** The ending of a phrase that gives a sense of rest or pause.
- **Q:** What is the tonic in C major?
  **A:** C.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Notation
*Student:* Notate the 8-bar melody with rhythm and dynamics.

*How to teach:* Notate the 8-bar melody in MuseScore with rhythm and dynamics.

- **Q:** In 4/4, does a bar of half note + quarter note + two eighths add up?
  **A:** Yes: 2 + 1 + ½ + ½ = 4 beats.

### Day 5 (Friday): Self-evaluate
*Student:* Evaluate the melody using criteria of unity and variety and revise one bar.

*How to teach:* Evaluate for unity (repetition) and variety (change); revise a bar.

- **Q:** Why does music need both unity and variety?
  **A:** Unity makes it hold together; variety keeps it interesting.

**Assessment:** Notated 8-bar melody.

**Answer key:** 8-bar melody rubric (1–4): clear motif that repeats/varies, question phrase (bars 1–4, ending on a non-tonic note such as G/D) and answer phrase (bars 5–8, ending on tonic), mostly stepwise with some leaps, correct notation (measures add up), dynamics marked. Proficient: 4 measures of 4 beats each per phrase, ends on tonic, shows unity and variety.

## Physical Education 7: The FITT principle

**Prepare:** Fitness journal. Write three sample workout plans (one missing intensity, one too much too soon, one balanced). Stopwatch.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking more is always better. Overload must be gradual (about 10% per week); too much too soon causes injury.
- Assuming any exercise improves every component. Training is specific — running builds endurance, not upper-body strength.

### Day 1 (Monday): FITT
*Student:* Learn the FITT principle and analyze three sample workout plans.

*How to teach:* Teach FITT and analyze three sample plans, identifying what each is missing.

- **Q:** What does FITT stand for?
  **A:** Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type.
- **Q:** Plan: 'Run 5 days a week.' Which FITT parts are missing?
  **A:** Intensity and time (how hard and how long).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cardio session
*Student:* Complete a 25-minute jog/walk interval and record FITT details.

*How to teach:* Do a 25-minute jog/walk interval (e.g., 2 min jog, 1 min walk) and record F, I, T and T.

- **Q:** Record the FITT for today's session.
  **A:** Example: F = 3x/week, I = moderate-vigorous (RPE 6–7), T = 25 min, T = jog/walk intervals.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Overload & progression
*Student:* Plan a 4-week progression for push-ups and try week 1.

*How to teach:* Plan a 4-week push-up progression (e.g., 3 sets of 6 → 8 → 10 → 12), then do week 1.

- **Q:** What is progressive overload?
  **A:** Gradually increasing the demands (reps, sets, intensity, time) so the body keeps adapting.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Specificity
*Student:* Match exercises to fitness components and to sports.

*How to teach:* Match exercises to fitness components and sports.

- **Q:** Which fitness component does a soccer player need most for running the whole game?
  **A:** Cardiorespiratory endurance.
- **Q:** What is the principle of specificity?
  **A:** You improve the specific component or skill you train.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a FITT quiz and log the week's MVPA minutes.

*How to teach:* Give the FITT quiz and total the week's MVPA minutes.

- **Q:** If you did 45, 60, 30, 70 and 55 minutes this week over 5 days, what is the average?
  **A:** (45+60+30+70+55) ÷ 5 = 260 ÷ 5 = 52 minutes per day.

**Assessment:** FITT quiz and activity log.

**Answer key:** FITT quiz key (suggested): 1) F = frequency (how often), I = intensity (how hard), T = time (how long), T = type (what kind) 2) overload = working harder than normal to improve 3) progression = gradually increasing 4) specificity = training matches the goal 5) teens need 60 min MVPA daily 6) aerobic example — running, swimming; strength — push-ups 7) 10% rule. Activity log: daily MVPA minutes totaled.

## Health Education 7: Emergency preparedness

**Prepare:** Visit Ready.gov/kids. Gather kit supplies: water (1 gallon per person per day for 3 days), nonperishable food, flashlight, batteries, first aid kit, medicines, radio, phone charger, copies of documents, masks (N95 for smoke). Check your county's alert system (e.g., sign up for local emergency alerts).

**Watch for:**
- Running outside or standing in a doorway during an earthquake. Drop, Cover and Hold On under sturdy furniture; doorways aren't safer in modern homes.
- Thinking a cloth mask protects from wildfire smoke. Only well-fitted N95/KN95 respirators filter fine smoke particles; staying indoors with filtered air is best.

### Day 1 (Monday): Earthquakes
*Student:* Practice Drop, Cover and Hold On and identify safe spots at home.

*How to teach:* Practice Drop, Cover, Hold On; your child identifies safe spots (under sturdy tables) and dangers (windows, tall furniture).

- **Q:** What are the three steps during an earthquake?
  **A:** Drop to your hands and knees, Cover your head and neck (under a sturdy table), Hold On until shaking stops.
- **Q:** If you're in bed during an earthquake, what should you do?
  **A:** Stay in bed, cover your head and neck with a pillow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wildfires
*Student:* Learn about wildfire evacuation and air-quality alerts.

*How to teach:* Learn evacuation terms (warning = go now; watch = be ready) and check the AirNow AQI scale.

- **Q:** At what AQI level is air 'unhealthy for sensitive groups'?
  **A:** 101–150 (orange).
- **Q:** Name two ways to protect yourself from wildfire smoke.
  **A:** Stay indoors with windows closed, use air filters/HEPA, wear an N95, limit outdoor exercise.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family plan
*Student:* Make a family emergency communication plan.

*How to teach:* Fill out a family communication plan with contacts and meeting places.

- **Q:** Why choose an out-of-area contact?
  **A:** Long-distance calls/texts often go through when local lines are overloaded, and that person can relay messages.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kit
*Student:* Build or update a 72-hour emergency kit.

*How to teach:* Build or update the 72-hour kit using the checklist.

- **Q:** How much water should a 4-person family store for 3 days?
  **A:** 4 people × 1 gallon × 3 days = 12 gallons.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Run a family evacuation drill.

*How to teach:* Run an evacuation drill: alarm, grab kit, meet outside. Time it and discuss improvements.

- **Q:** What should you do if your exit is blocked by smoke?
  **A:** Use your second exit; stay low under smoke; feel doors for heat before opening.

**Assessment:** Emergency plan and kit.

**Answer key:** Emergency plan: lists out-of-area contact, two meeting places (near home and outside neighborhood), evacuation routes, how to get alerts, pets and medical needs. Kit checklist: at least 3 days' water (1 gal/person/day), food, flashlight, batteries, radio, first aid kit, medications, phone charger, important document copies, cash, whistle, N95 masks. Drill completed in a set time with everyone at the meeting spot.
