# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 7

## English Language Arts 7: The Call of the Wild, chapters 3–4

**Prepare:** The Call of the Wild; a character tracker (columns: trait gained/lost, evidence, page).

**Watch for:**
- Calling 'Buck pulled hard, and Spitz watched' complex. It's compound — two independent clauses joined by 'and'. Complex needs a dependent clause (because, when, although…).
- Seeing the Buck–Spitz rivalry as random violence. London frames it as a struggle for leadership of the team.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 3
*Student:* Read Chapter 3 and chart each clash between Buck and Spitz leading to their final fight.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 3, 'The Dominant Primordial Beast'. Your child charts each clash (Spitz taking Buck's sleeping nest at Lake Le Barge, Buck defending Pike from Spitz, the rabbit chase) leading to the fight. Check before moving on: ask "What triggers the final fight?" Look for: During a rabbit chase Spitz cuts across and kills the rabbit Buck is chasing; Buck attacks him.

- **Q:** What triggers the final fight?
  **A:** During a rabbit chase Spitz cuts across and kills the rabbit Buck is chasing; Buck attacks him.
- **Q:** How does Buck win?
  **A:** He uses imagination as well as strength — feints high and goes low, breaking Spitz's front legs; then the circle of dogs closes in on Spitz.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Character tracker
*Student:* Update a Buck character tracker with traits gained and lost, citing a page for each.

*How to teach:* Model one tracker row: 'Gained: cunning — he sides with Pike to undermine Spitz (p. __).' Your child adds at least four traits gained and two lost, each with a page citation. Ask which change matters most for his survival and why.

- **Q:** Give a trait Buck gains and evidence.
  **A:** Cunning — he deliberately undermines Spitz's authority by siding with dogs Spitz punishes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar: sentence types
*Student:* Identify simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences in a London passage, then write one of each about Buck.

*How to teach:* Teach the four sentence types with one example each. Your child labels sentences in a London passage and writes one of each about Buck. Check before moving on: ask "Write a compound-complex sentence about Buck." Look for: Example: 'Because Spitz stole his nest, Buck attacked, and the fight was on.' (one dependent + two independent clauses)

- **Q:** Write a compound-complex sentence about Buck.
  **A:** Example: 'Because Spitz stole his nest, Buck attacked, and the fight was on.' (one dependent + two independent clauses)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapter 4
*Student:* Read Chapter 4 'Who Has Won to Mastership' and explain how Buck leads differently than Spitz.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 4 aloud or with audio. Your child lists how Buck takes the lead position and two ways he leads differently than Spitz, with quotes. Discuss whether Buck is a better leader or just a stronger one.

- **Q:** How does Buck get the lead position?
  **A:** He refuses to let Sol-leks take it and stays out of reach of François's club until they give him the lead.
- **Q:** How is he different as leader?
  **A:** He enforces discipline firmly (on Pike and Joe) so the team works together and makes record time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on chapters 3–4 and revise five choppy sentences into compound-complex sentences.

*How to teach:* Give the chapters 3–4 quiz without the book. Then model combining one set of choppy sentences into a compound-complex sentence; your child combines the other four and labels the independent and dependent clauses. Check before moving on: ask "Combine: The trail was hard. Dave was sick. He would not leave the traces." Look for: 'Although Dave was sick and the trail was hard, he would not leave the traces, and he pulled until he collapsed.' (any correct compound-complex)

- **Q:** Combine: The trail was hard. Dave was sick. He would not leave the traces.
  **A:** 'Although Dave was sick and the trail was hard, he would not leave the traces, and he pulled until he collapsed.' (any correct compound-complex)

**Assessment:** Quiz and sentence-combining exercise.

**Answer key:** Chapters 3–4 quiz: 1) Spitz steals Buck's nest at Lake Le Barge. 2) Buck sides with Pike against Spitz to undermine him. 3) The rabbit chase sparks the fight; Spitz dies when the pack closes in. 4) François tries Sol-leks as leader; Buck refuses until he gets the lead. 5) Under Buck the team improves. 6) Dave, sick, insists on pulling until he is shot. Combining exercise: each of 5 rewrites must contain at least one dependent and two independent clauses with correct commas.

## Mathematics 7: Equations for proportional relationships

**Prepare:** Collect 3–4 grocery or gas receipts and a pay stub example (or make one: $15/hour).

**Watch for:**
- Writing y = x + k instead of y = kx. Test with a table: add-a-constant relationships do not have a constant y/x.
- Thinking y = 4/x is proportional because it has a 4. Make a table: (1,4), (2,2) — y/x changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): From table to equation
*Student:* Write y = kx equations for tables of gas cost, hourly pay and unit price taken from real receipts.

*How to teach:* Turn a receipt into a table (lb vs cost) and model writing y = kx. Your child writes equations for gas, pay and unit price. Check before moving on: ask "Gas costs $4.80/gallon. Equation?" Look for: c = 4.80g.

- **Q:** Gas costs $4.80/gallon. Equation?
  **A:** c = 4.80g.
- **Q:** You earn $15 per hour. Equation and pay for 6 hours?
  **A:** p = 15h; $90.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Using equations
*Student:* Use y = 3.25x to answer forward and backward questions about cost and quantity.

*How to teach:* Model forward (x → y) and backward (y → x) with y = 3.25x. Your child answers several of each. Check before moving on: ask "Cost of 4 lb?" Look for: $13.

- **Q:** Cost of 4 lb?
  **A:** $13.
- **Q:** How many lb for $26?
  **A:** 8 lb (26 ÷ 3.25).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two equations
*Student:* Write both y = kx and x = (1/k)y for a recipe and explain what each tells you.

*How to teach:* For a recipe of 3 cups flour per 2 cups sugar, write f = 1.5s and s = (2/3)f. Your child explains each in words. Check before moving on: ask "What does s = (2/3)f tell you?" Look for: Cups of sugar needed per cup of flour: 2/3 cup of sugar for each cup of flour.

- **Q:** What does s = (2/3)f tell you?
  **A:** Cups of sugar needed per cup of flour: 2/3 cup of sugar for each cup of flour.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Proportional or not?
*Student:* Decide whether equations like y = 4x, y = x + 4 and y = 4/x are proportional and justify with tables.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a table for y = 4x, y = x + 4 and y = 4/x and justifies which is proportional. Check before moving on: ask "Which is proportional and why?" Look for: Only y = 4x; its y/x is always 4. y = x + 4 gives (1,5),(2,6) with changing ratios; y = 4/x gives (1,4),(2,2).

- **Q:** Which is proportional and why?
  **A:** Only y = 4x; its y/x is always 4. y = x + 4 gives (1,5),(2,6) with changing ratios; y = 4/x gives (1,4),(2,2).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz writing and using equations for proportional relationships.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key) and correct. Check before moving on: ask "Is d = 60t proportional? What is k?" Look for: Yes; k = 60 (e.g., miles per hour).

- **Q:** Is d = 60t proportional? What is k?
  **A:** Yes; k = 60 (e.g., miles per hour).

**Assessment:** Quiz on writing and interpreting y = kx.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Table (2,9),(4,18): y = 4.5x. 2) Using y = 2.4x, x = 5 → 12. 3) y = 2.4x, y = 18 → x = 7.5. 4) y = 7x + 1 proportional? No. 5) Write x in terms of y for y = 5x: x = 0.2y. 6) In c = 1.25n, 1.25 means dollars per item.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Atoms rearrange: chemical equations

**Prepare:** LEGO bricks in 3 colors (or gumdrops); PhET 'Balancing Chemical Equations'.

**Watch for:**
- Changing subscripts to balance. Subscripts change the substance; only change coefficients.
- Thinking atoms are destroyed in a reaction. Atoms rearrange; the same atoms appear on both sides.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading equations
*Student:* Identify reactants, products, coefficients and subscripts in five chemical equations.

*How to teach:* Model labeling reactants, products, coefficients and subscripts in 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Your child labels five equations. Check before moving on: ask "In 2H2O, how many hydrogen atoms?" Look for: 4 (2 molecules × 2 H).

- **Q:** In 2H2O, how many hydrogen atoms?
  **A:** 4 (2 molecules × 2 H).
- **Q:** What are the reactants in CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O?
  **A:** Methane and oxygen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Modeling with LEGO
*Student:* Use colored LEGO bricks to model methane burning (CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O) and count atoms before and after.

*How to teach:* Build methane and 2 oxygen molecules from LEGO, take them apart, rebuild into CO2 and 2 H2O. Your child counts atoms before and after. Check before moving on: ask "Atom counts both sides?" Look for: 1 C, 4 H, 4 O on each side.

- **Q:** Atom counts both sides?
  **A:** 1 C, 4 H, 4 O on each side.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Balancing simulation
*Student:* Complete PhET 'Balancing Chemical Equations' intro and game levels 1–2.

*How to teach:* Your child completes PhET intro and game levels 1–2. Check before moving on: ask "Balance H2 + O2 → H2O." Look for: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.

- **Q:** Balance H2 + O2 → H2O.
  **A:** 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Baking soda and vinegar equation
*Student:* Model the baking soda + vinegar reaction and explain where the gas comes from.

*How to teach:* Model NaHCO3 + CH3COOH → NaCH3COO + H2O + CO2. Your child explains where the gas comes from. Check before moving on: ask "Where does the gas come from?" Look for: Carbon and oxygen atoms from the baking soda rearrange into carbon dioxide gas.

- **Q:** Where does the gas come from?
  **A:** Carbon and oxygen atoms from the baking soda rearrange into carbon dioxide gas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: count atoms and balance three simple equations with models.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Balance N2 + H2 → NH3." Look for: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3.

- **Q:** Balance N2 + H2 → NH3.
  **A:** N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3.

**Assessment:** Balancing quiz and model photos.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 3CO2 has 3 C, 6 O. 2) Balance Mg + O2 → MgO: 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO. 3) Balance H2 + Cl2 → HCl: H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl. 4) Balance N2 + H2 → NH3: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3. 5) Coefficient vs subscript explained.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Islamic Golden Age

**Prepare:** Paper plate, protractor, straw, string and washer for astrolabe; compass, ruler for geometric pattern; reading on the House of Wisdom.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Muslim scholars only preserved Greek knowledge. They added original work in algebra, medicine, optics and astronomy.
- Thinking an astrolabe tells time only. It measures angles of stars to find latitude and time.

### Day 1 (Monday): House of Wisdom
*Student:* Read about the House of Wisdom and how scholars translated Greek texts into Arabic.

*How to teach:* Read about the House of Wisdom. Your child lists what scholars translated and why. Check before moving on: ask "What was the House of Wisdom?" Look for: A library and center in Baghdad where scholars translated Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic.

- **Q:** What was the House of Wisdom?
  **A:** A library and center in Baghdad where scholars translated Greek, Persian and Indian works into Arabic.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Math and science
*Student:* Solve a simple equation al-Khwarizmi's way and research Ibn Sina's Canon of Medicine.

*How to teach:* Show al-Khwarizmi's balancing method. Your child solves a simple equation and researches Ibn Sina. Check before moving on: ask "What word comes from al-Khwarizmi's book?" Look for: Algebra (from al-jabr); 'algorithm' comes from his name.

- **Q:** What word comes from al-Khwarizmi's book?
  **A:** Algebra (from al-jabr); 'algorithm' comes from his name.
- **Q:** Ibn Sina's major work?
  **A:** The Canon of Medicine, used in European universities for centuries.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Astrolabe
*Student:* Build a paper-plate astrolabe and use it to measure the angle of the North Star.

*How to teach:* Build the astrolabe. At night, your child sights the North Star and reads the angle. Check before moving on: ask "What does the North Star's angle tell you?" Look for: Your latitude.

- **Q:** What does the North Star's angle tell you?
  **A:** Your latitude.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Art and geometry
*Student:* Draw an Islamic geometric star pattern using compass and ruler.

*How to teach:* Model constructing an 8-point star with a compass. Your child draws a repeating pattern. Check before moving on: ask "Why geometric patterns?" Look for: They express infinite order and avoid images of living beings in religious spaces.

- **Q:** Why geometric patterns?
  **A:** They express infinite order and avoid images of living beings in religious spaces.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 2 test with a short constructed response on Golden Age contributions.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test. Check before moving on: ask "Two Golden Age contributions?" Look for: Algebra; Ibn Sina's medical encyclopedia; improved astrolabes; optics (Ibn al-Haytham).

- **Q:** Two Golden Age contributions?
  **A:** Algebra; Ibn Sina's medical encyclopedia; improved astrolabes; optics (Ibn al-Haytham).

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and astrolabe/pattern projects.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test: Arabia geography; Muhammad timeline; Five Pillars; Sunni-Shia; caliphates; Baghdad; Golden Age. Constructed response proficient: two contributions with scholar names and impact.

## Spanish 1A: ¿Qué tiempo hace? Weather and seasons

**Prepare:** A Spanish-language weather site (e.g., AccuWeather en español, Meteored), world map, phone to record.

**Watch for:**
- Using 'es' with weather. Use hace (hace calor), está (está nublado), or verbs llueve/nieva.
- Thinking seasons are the same everywhere. Southern Hemisphere countries (Argentina, Chile, Uruguay) have summer in December–February.

### Day 1 (Monday): El tiempo
*Student:* Learn hace calor/frío/sol/viento, llueve, nieva, está nublado and give a daily weather report for your town.

*How to teach:* Learn weather expressions; give a daily report.

- **Q:** How do you say 'It's hot and sunny'?
  **A:** Hace calor y hace sol.
- **Q:** How do you say 'It's raining'?
  **A:** Llueve (or Está lloviendo).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Las estaciones
*Student:* Learn the four seasons and match months to seasons for California and for Argentina, explaining why they are reversed.

*How to teach:* Match months to seasons for California and Argentina; explain why reversed.

- **Q:** What season is it in Argentina in January?
  **A:** Verano (summer).
- **Q:** Why are the seasons reversed?
  **A:** Argentina is in the Southern Hemisphere, which tilts toward the sun when the Northern Hemisphere tilts away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Weather report
*Student:* Read a Spanish-language weather forecast for Mexico City and Buenos Aires and record the temperature and conditions for both.

*How to teach:* Read forecasts for Mexico City and Buenos Aires; record temperature and conditions.

- **Q:** What does 'está nublado' mean?
  **A:** It's cloudy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): El pronóstico
*Student:* Write and record a 45-second TV-style weather report for three Spanish-speaking capitals, using the date and time.

*How to teach:* Write and record a 45-second weather report.

- **Q:** Write: 'Today is Monday, March 3. In Madrid it is cold and windy.'
  **A:** Hoy es lunes, el tres de marzo. En Madrid hace frío y hace viento.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit test on days, months, dates, time, and weather, including a listening section.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 2 test.

- **Q:** Name the four seasons in Spanish.
  **A:** La primavera, el verano, el otoño, el invierno.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and weather report recording.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test: days, months, dates, time, weather with listening. Seasons key: primavera, verano, otoño, invierno. California: invierno = diciembre–febrero, primavera = marzo–mayo, verano = junio–agosto, otoño = septiembre–noviembre; Argentina reversed (verano = diciembre–febrero). Weather report rubric: names 3 capitals, date and time, conditions and temperature in Spanish, 45 seconds, clear pronunciation.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Color and mood

**Prepare:** Images of Van Gogh's The Starry Night (1889) and Picasso's The Old Guitarist (1903–04, Blue Period). Paints, sketchbook.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking warm colors always mean happy and cool always sad. Context, value and brushwork also shape mood; cool can be calm or peaceful.
- Choosing colors only for realism. Expressionist artists chose colors for feeling.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analyze mood
*Student:* Compare Van Gogh's 'Starry Night' and Picasso's 'The Old Guitarist' and describe how color creates mood.

*How to teach:* Compare the two paintings: Starry Night uses swirling brushstrokes and contrasting yellow against blue for energy and emotion; The Old Guitarist uses mostly blue for sadness and isolation.

- **Q:** How does Picasso use color to create mood in The Old Guitarist?
  **A:** Almost entirely blue (monochromatic, cool) to express sadness, poverty and loneliness.
- **Q:** How do Van Gogh's brushstrokes add to the mood?
  **A:** Thick, swirling, rhythmic strokes create movement and intense emotion in the sky.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Same scene, two moods
*Student:* Paint the same simple landscape twice, once warm and once cool.

*How to teach:* Paint the same simple landscape twice: warm and cool.

- **Q:** Name the warm colors and the cool colors.
  **A:** Warm: red, orange, yellow; cool: blue, green, violet.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan
*Student:* Choose a personal place and sketch three thumbnails for a landscape painting.

*How to teach:* Choose a personal place and sketch three thumbnails.

- **Q:** What is a thumbnail sketch?
  **A:** A small, quick sketch to plan composition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Set criteria
*Student:* Write criteria for the painting (color scheme, focal point, depth).

*How to teach:* Write painting criteria.

- **Q:** Give one criterion for a successful landscape.
  **A:** Example: clear foreground, middle ground and background to show depth.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Discuss which thumbnail best meets the criteria and why.

*How to teach:* Discuss which thumbnail best meets the criteria.

- **Q:** What is Expressionism?
  **A:** Art that uses distorted forms and bold colors to express emotions rather than realistic appearance.

**Assessment:** Mood studies and painting plan.

**Answer key:** Mood studies: two versions of the same landscape — one in warm colors (reds, oranges, yellows), one in cool (blues, greens, violets) — and a written sentence for each mood. Painting plan: three thumbnails and written criteria (color scheme, off-center focal point, foreground/middle/background for depth). Proficient: criteria are specific and the chosen thumbnail is justified by them.

## Physical Education 7: Soccer: dribbling, shooting and strategy

**Prepare:** Soccer ball, cones for slalom, a goal or targets (two cones or a wall chart) at 12 m. Small group for 2-v-1 and 3-v-3.

**Watch for:**
- Looking down at the ball the whole time while dribbling. Keep the ball close and glance up to see space and teammates.
- Leaning back when shooting, which sends the ball high. Lean over the ball, plant beside it, strike with laces.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dribbling
*Student:* Dribble through a cone slalom using both feet and three different touches.

*How to teach:* Dribble a cone slalom using inside, outside and sole touches with both feet. Time it.

- **Q:** Why use small touches when dribbling in traffic?
  **A:** They keep the ball close so you can change direction and protect it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shooting
*Student:* Practice instep shots at targets from 12 meters, focusing on plant foot and follow-through.

*How to teach:* Teach the instep (laces) shot: approach at a slight angle, plant beside ball, toe pointed down, strike with laces, land on the kicking foot. Shoot at targets from 12 m.

- **Q:** Which part of the foot strikes a powerful shot?
  **A:** The laces (instep), with toe pointed down and ankle locked.
- **Q:** What happens if you lean back?
  **A:** The ball rises over the goal.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Creating space
*Student:* Play 2-v-1 games learning to spread wide and move into open space.

*How to teach:* Play 2-v-1: attackers spread wide and move into space; defender tries to win the ball.

- **Q:** What does 'width' mean in soccer?
  **A:** Spreading players toward the sidelines to stretch the defense and create space.
- **Q:** What is 'depth'?
  **A:** Players positioned behind and ahead of the ball to provide support and passing options.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Small-sided game
*Student:* Play 3-v-3 soccer applying width and support.

*How to teach:* Play 3-v-3 small-sided games; pause to point out width and support.

- **Q:** What should you do right after passing?
  **A:** Move into open space to be available for a return pass (support).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Self-assess dribbling and shooting with a skill rubric.

*How to teach:* Your child self-assesses with the rubric.

- **Q:** Name one strength and one goal.
  **A:** Open answer — tied to rubric cues.

**Assessment:** Skill rubric and game observation.

**Answer key:** Skill rubric (1–4 each): dribbling (close control, both feet, head up, change of direction), shooting (plant foot, laces contact, toe down, follow-through, accuracy), game play (spreads wide for width, supports ball carrier, moves into open space). Proficient = 3s across; game observation notes at least two times your child created width or support.

## Health Education 7: Emotions and mental health

**Prepare:** Print or display an emotion wheel (free online, e.g., Plutchik or 'feelings wheel'). Choose a short true story of resilience (e.g., a biography article about an athlete or person who overcame hardship).

**Watch for:**
- Believing mental health means always being happy. Healthy people feel the full range of emotions and manage them.
- Thinking self-esteem comes only from others' praise. It grows from your own effort, values and strengths.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is mental health?
*Student:* Define mental health and list traits of emotionally healthy people.

*How to teach:* Define mental health: how we think, feel and act, cope with stress, relate to others and make choices. Your child lists 5 traits of emotionally healthy people.

- **Q:** List three traits of good mental health.
  **A:** Handles stress, expresses emotions in healthy ways, has positive relationships, asks for help when needed, accepts mistakes, feels good about self.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Emotion wheel
*Student:* Use an emotion wheel to name precise feelings from three recent situations.

*How to teach:* Show how the wheel moves from basic feelings (mad, sad, scared, happy) to precise ones. Your child names precise feelings for three recent events.

- **Q:** Why is it helpful to name a precise emotion like 'embarrassed' instead of 'bad'?
  **A:** Naming feelings precisely calms the brain and helps you choose the right coping strategy and explain it to others.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Self-esteem
*Student:* List 10 personal strengths and ask family to add three.

*How to teach:* Your child lists 10 strengths (skills, character traits); family members add three. Discuss which surprised them.

- **Q:** What is self-esteem?
  **A:** How much you value and respect yourself.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Resilience
*Student:* Read a story of someone overcoming adversity and identify resilience skills.

*How to teach:* Read the resilience story together. Your child lists the setbacks and the skills used to bounce back.

- **Q:** What is resilience?
  **A:** The ability to recover or adapt after difficulty or setbacks.
- **Q:** Name two resilience skills.
  **A:** Positive self-talk, asking for support, problem-solving, setting small goals, persistence, learning from mistakes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Journal about a time you bounced back from a setback.

*How to teach:* Your child journals about a personal setback and how they recovered. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What could you say to yourself after failing a test?
  **A:** Example: 'This was hard, but I can study differently and ask for help. One test doesn't define me.'

**Assessment:** Journal entries.

**Answer key:** Journal rubric (score each entry 1–4): 4 = names specific emotions precisely, explains the situation and thoughts, identifies at least one coping or resilience skill used (asking for help, positive self-talk, problem-solving, persistence), and reflects on what they learned; 3 = clear but less reflective; 2 = brief, emotion vague ('bad'); 1 = incomplete.
