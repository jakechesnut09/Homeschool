# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 27

## English Language Arts 7: Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry: background and chapters 1–3

**Prepare:** Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry (library); Library of Congress FSA photos of 1930s Mississippi (search 'sharecroppers Mississippi 1936'). Parental preview: the novel depicts racism, a racial slur (in the textbook scene and elsewhere), night riders, burning of Black men, and later a lynch mob; discuss the history before reading.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking racism in the novel is only individual meanness. Help your child see the system: segregated schools, sharecropping debt, unequal law.
- Forgetting that Cassie is nine and her view is limited — readers often understand more than she does.

### Day 1 (Monday): Historical context
*Student:* Examine Library of Congress FSA photographs of 1930s Mississippi sharecroppers and list observations.

*How to teach:* Look at 4–6 FSA photographs together. Your child lists observations (housing, clothing, work, children) and questions, then you briefly explain sharecropping and segregation in 1930s Mississippi. Check before moving on: ask "What was sharecropping?" Look for: Farmers worked land owned by someone else and paid with a share of the crop, often staying in debt.

- **Q:** What was sharecropping?
  **A:** Farmers worked land owned by someone else and paid with a share of the crop, often staying in debt.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 1
*Student:* Read Chapter 1 and analyze how the textbook incident reveals Cassie's and Little Man's characters.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 1 together. Your child explains what Little Man and Cassie do about the textbooks and what this reveals about their characters, citing two quotes. Check before moving on: ask "Why does Little Man refuse the book?" Look for: The inside chart shows it was a worn book passed down from white students, with the condition column listing the Black students' race in an insulting word; he stomps on it.

- **Q:** Why does Little Man refuse the book?
  **A:** The inside chart shows it was a worn book passed down from white students, with the condition column listing the Black students' race in an insulting word; he stomps on it.
- **Q:** What does this show about Little Man and Cassie?
  **A:** They are proud and refuse to accept humiliation, even when they are punished.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapters 2–3
*Student:* Read Chapters 2–3 and explain how the bus incident and the night men create tension.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 2–3. Your child explains how the bus incident and the night men create tension, citing specific moments. Discuss why the children are so afraid that night.

- **Q:** What do the children do to the bus?
  **A:** They dig a ditch in the road that fills with rain; the bus breaks an axle in it.
- **Q:** Why are they afraid that night?
  **A:** The night men ride; the children fear they are coming because of the bus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Narrator
*Student:* Analyze how Cassie's first-person point of view shapes what readers learn.

*How to teach:* Discuss first-person point of view. Your child finds two moments where Cassie doesn't fully understand what adults know, and explains how her narration shapes what readers learn. Check before moving on: ask "How does Cassie's narration affect what readers learn?" Look for: We see events through a nine-year-old's eyes: her anger and confusion; adults' reasons are revealed slowly.

- **Q:** How does Cassie's narration affect what readers learn?
  **A:** We see events through a nine-year-old's eyes: her anger and confusion; adults' reasons are revealed slowly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Discuss the importance of land to the Logans and take a chapters 1–3 quiz.

*How to teach:* Discuss why land matters to the Logans, citing Papa's and Big Ma's words. Then give the chapters 1–3 quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Why is land important to the Logans?" Look for: Owning land gives them independence; they don't have to sharecrop for Harlan Granger.

- **Q:** Why is land important to the Logans?
  **A:** Owning land gives them independence; they don't have to sharecrop for Harlan Granger.

**Assessment:** Quiz and discussion notes.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Cassie Logan narrates; setting 1933 Mississippi. 2) Siblings Stacey, Christopher-John, Little Man. 3) Textbook incident: books from white school. 4) Mama is a teacher and pastes paper over the chart. 5) Bus splashing and ditch. 6) Mr. Morrison comes with Papa as protection. Discussion notes: two ideas with evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Expressions and equations in context

**Prepare:** visualpatterns.org (pick two patterns), calculator, graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Writing an expression that only fits step 1. Test the expression on at least three steps.
- Treating break-even as revenue alone. Break-even is where revenue = cost.

### Day 1 (Monday): Visual patterns
*Student:* Write expressions for the nth step of two visual patterns and show the expressions are equivalent.

*How to teach:* Show a visual pattern; describe how it grows. Your child writes two different-looking expressions and shows they are equivalent. Check before moving on: ask "Pattern: 5, 8, 11, 14 tiles. Expression for step n?" Look for: 3n + 2 (check n = 1: 5).

- **Q:** Pattern: 5, 8, 11, 14 tiles. Expression for step n?
  **A:** 3n + 2 (check n = 1: 5).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fundraiser problem
*Student:* Model a bake sale with costs and revenue using expressions and find the break-even point.

*How to teach:* Model: supplies cost $30 plus $0.50 per cookie; cookies sell for $1.25. Your child finds break-even. Check before moving on: ask "Break-even?" Look for: 1.25c = 30 + 0.50c → 0.75c = 30 → c = 40 cookies.

- **Q:** Break-even?
  **A:** 1.25c = 30 + 0.50c → 0.75c = 30 → c = 40 cookies.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Phone plans
*Student:* Compare two phone plans with equations and an inequality to decide when one is cheaper.

*How to teach:* Plan A: $30 + $5 per GB; Plan B: $50 flat. Your child writes equations and an inequality for when A is cheaper. Check before moving on: ask "When is A cheaper?" Look for: 30 + 5g < 50 → g < 4 GB.

- **Q:** When is A cheaper?
  **A:** 30 + 5g < 50 → g < 4 GB.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Find and correct errors in 6 worked solutions to equations and inequalities.

*How to teach:* Your child finds and corrects errors in 6 worked solutions, writing the error in words. Check before moving on: ask "Error: −3x < 12 → x < −4." Look for: Dividing by −3 must reverse: x > −4.

- **Q:** Error: −3x < 12 → x < −4.
  **A:** Dividing by −3 must reverse: x > −4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Short quiz combining expressions, equations and inequalities.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key) and collect the fundraiser write-up. Check before moving on: ask "Simplify 2(3n + 1) − n." Look for: 5n + 2.

- **Q:** Simplify 2(3n + 1) − n.
  **A:** 5n + 2.

**Assessment:** Quiz and fundraiser model write-up.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Pattern 4, 7, 10 → 3n + 1. 2) 2(x − 3) = 3x − 4 → x = −2. 3) 4x − 5 > 11 → x > 4. 4) Break-even: $20 costs + $2/item, sells $6 → 5 items. Write-up rubric: correct cost/revenue expressions, correct break-even, interpretation.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Fossils and the history of life

**Prepare:** Plaster of Paris or salt dough, shells, leaves, petroleum jelly, paper cups; Smithsonian Learning Lab fossil images; La Brea Tar Pits website.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fossils are always actual bones. Most are mineral replacements, molds, casts or traces.
- Thinking the fossil record is complete. Fossilization is rare.

### Day 1 (Monday): Making fossils
*Student:* Make mold and cast fossils of shells and leaves in salt dough and plaster.

*How to teach:* Coat a shell with petroleum jelly, press into dough (mold), fill with plaster (cast). Your child labels each. Check before moving on: ask "Mold vs cast?" Look for: Mold is the hollow impression; cast is the filled replica.

- **Q:** Mold vs cast?
  **A:** Mold is the hollow impression; cast is the filled replica.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fossil types
*Student:* Compare body, trace, mold, cast and amber fossils using Smithsonian Learning Lab images.

*How to teach:* Compare body, trace, mold, cast and amber fossils. Check before moving on: ask "Footprint?" Look for: Trace fossil.

- **Q:** Footprint?
  **A:** Trace fossil.
- **Q:** Insect in amber?
  **A:** Preserved remains in hardened tree resin.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): La Brea Tar Pits
*Student:* Explore the La Brea Tar Pits fossils and explain what they reveal about Ice Age Los Angeles.

*How to teach:* Explore La Brea Tar Pits. Your child explains what it reveals about Ice Age LA. Check before moving on: ask "Animals found?" Look for: Saber-toothed cats, dire wolves (most common), mammoths, giant ground sloths.

- **Q:** Animals found?
  **A:** Saber-toothed cats, dire wolves (most common), mammoths, giant ground sloths.
- **Q:** Why so many predators?
  **A:** Predators came to eat trapped animals and got stuck too.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fossil record data
*Student:* Analyze a chart of first and last appearances of life forms and identify patterns of diversification and extinction.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes a chart of appearances and extinctions. Check before moving on: ask "Pattern?" Look for: Life diversified over time with sudden losses at mass extinctions, followed by new diversification.

- **Q:** Pattern?
  **A:** Life diversified over time with sudden losses at mass extinctions, followed by new diversification.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on fossil formation and the fossil record.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Where do fossils usually form?" Look for: Sedimentary rock.

- **Q:** Where do fossils usually form?
  **A:** Sedimentary rock.

**Assessment:** Fossil models and data analysis quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: fossil types, why sedimentary, La Brea evidence, diversification/extinction pattern, fossil record incomplete. Models labeled mold and cast.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Causes of the Reformation; Luther

**Prepare:** Excerpts of the 95 Theses; account of the Diet of Worms; Reformation pamphlet images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Luther wanted to start a new church. He first wanted to reform the Catholic Church.
- Thinking indulgences were 'permission to sin.' They were believed to reduce punishment for sins already forgiven; abuses arose when they were sold.

### Day 1 (Monday): Problems in the Church
*Student:* Read about the Great Schism of the papacy, corruption and indulgences and chart the causes of discontent.

*How to teach:* Your child charts causes of discontent. Check before moving on: ask "Causes?" Look for: Western Schism (1378–1417) with rival popes, wealthy and corrupt clergy, sale of indulgences.

- **Q:** Causes?
  **A:** Western Schism (1378–1417) with rival popes, wealthy and corrupt clergy, sale of indulgences.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The 95 Theses
*Student:* Read 10 of Luther's 95 Theses and restate each in modern English.

*How to teach:* Read 10 theses; restate. Check before moving on: ask "Thesis 86 restated?" Look for: Why doesn't the rich pope build St. Peter's Basilica with his own money instead of poor people's?

- **Q:** Thesis 86 restated?
  **A:** Why doesn't the rich pope build St. Peter's Basilica with his own money instead of poor people's?
- **Q:** When and where?
  **A:** 1517, Wittenberg.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diet of Worms
*Student:* Read Luther's 'Here I stand' speech and explain why he refused to recant.

*How to teach:* Read Luther at Worms. Check before moving on: ask "Why did Luther refuse to recant?" Look for: His conscience was bound by Scripture; he would recant only if proven wrong by Scripture and reason.

- **Q:** Why did Luther refuse to recant?
  **A:** His conscience was bound by Scripture; he would recant only if proven wrong by Scripture and reason.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Printing and Reformation
*Student:* Explain how the printing press spread Luther's ideas, using pamphlet examples.

*How to teach:* Explain printing and Reformation. Check before moving on: ask "How did printing help Luther?" Look for: Pamphlets and his German Bible spread his ideas quickly to many people.

- **Q:** How did printing help Luther?
  **A:** Pamphlets and his German Bible spread his ideas quickly to many people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Reformation causes and Luther.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Diet of Worms year?" Look for: 1521.

- **Q:** Diet of Worms year?
  **A:** 1521.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: causes of discontent, indulgences, Tetzel, 95 Theses 1517, Diet of Worms 1521, printing press role, Luther's key beliefs (faith alone, Bible authority).

## Spanish 1A: Unit 7 project: Mi árbol genealógico

**Prepare:** Poster board, family photos, index cards, phone to record.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the second surname is a middle name. In Spanish naming, the first surname is the father's first surname and the second is the mother's first surname.
- Reading the full script. Use key-word index cards.

### Day 1 (Monday): Apellidos
*Student:* Learn how Spanish double surnames (apellido paterno y materno) work and write your name in that format.

*How to teach:* Explain double surnames; write your name in that format.

- **Q:** If a father is Juan López Ruiz and the mother is Ana Pérez Gómez, what are their child's surnames?
  **A:** López Pérez (father's first surname + mother's first surname).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build the tree
*Student:* Create a family tree poster with at least 12 relatives, labels, and ages.

*How to teach:* Build the tree with 12+ relatives, labels and ages.

- **Q:** What does 'materno' mean?
  **A:** On the mother's side (maternal).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Script
*Student:* Write a 10–12 sentence presentation of your family using tener, ser, possessives, and gustar.

*How to teach:* Write a 10–12 sentence presentation.

- **Q:** Write: 'My aunt is 40 years old and she likes to read.'
  **A:** Mi tía tiene cuarenta años y le gusta leer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice presenting with notes on index cards (key words only) and record a practice run.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with key-word cards; record.

- **Q:** Say: 'These are my paternal grandparents.'
  **A:** Estos son mis abuelos paternos.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Present the family tree (2 minutes) and take the unit 7 written test.

*How to teach:* Present and give the unit 7 test.

- **Q:** How do you say 'married'?
  **A:** casado/casada.

**Assessment:** Family tree presentation rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Family tree presentation rubric (1–4 each): content (12+ relatives with relationships and ages), language (tener, ser, possessives, gustar; agreement), fluency/pronunciation (2 minutes with notes), visual (labels in Spanish). Unit 7 test: family vocabulary, tener and tener expressions, possessives, ages. Surname example: Gabriel García Márquez → father's surname García, mother's surname Márquez.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Performance and theatre critique

**Prepare:** Set a performance time, audience seating. Find a filmed production (e.g., a filmed Shakespeare, National Theatre at Home, or a local theater show).

**Watch for:**
- Writing a review that only summarizes the plot. Reviews evaluate acting, design and directing with evidence.
- Breaking character when something goes wrong. Stay in the moment and keep going.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Hold a full dress rehearsal.

*How to teach:* Hold a full dress rehearsal with costumes and props.

- **Q:** Why is a dress rehearsal important?
  **A:** It's the final full run with all elements, catching problems before the audience sees it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Performance
*Student:* Perform the play for family or a homeschool group.

*How to teach:* Perform the play for family or group; take a curtain call.

- **Q:** What is a curtain call?
  **A:** When actors bow at the end of a performance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Watch a production
*Student:* Watch a filmed or local live theatre production.

*How to teach:* Watch a filmed or live production, taking notes.

- **Q:** Name one design element to note.
  **A:** Set, costumes, lighting, sound, makeup, props.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique
*Student:* Write a review of the production with evidence about acting, design and directing choices.

*How to teach:* Write a review with evidence.

- **Q:** Give an example of evidence in a review.
  **A:** Example: 'When the lights shifted to blue during the forest scene, the mood became dreamlike.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on your own performance and the artistic choices you made.

*How to teach:* Reflect on own performance.

- **Q:** What choice are you proudest of?
  **A:** Open answer with a specific moment.

**Assessment:** Performance and written review.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric (1–4): memorization, projection/diction, characterization, blocking, ensemble work. Review rubric: introduces the production, evaluates acting, design (set, costume, lighting, sound) and directing with specific examples, and gives an overall judgment with reasons. Self-reflection: names choices made and their effect.

## Physical Education 7: Choreography project

**Prepare:** Music (clean edit), space, partners if possible, a heart-rate method. Print a choreography rubric.

**Watch for:**
- Making every move at the same level and facing forward. Use levels (high, middle, low), pathways (straight, curved, zigzag) and directions.
- Choreographing without counting. Write moves by 8-counts so the routine fits the music.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose music
*Student:* Choose a song and map its sections.

*How to teach:* Choose a song; map intro, verse, chorus and count 8-counts in each.

- **Q:** Why map the song's sections?
  **A:** So movements change with the music and repeat on the chorus.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choreograph
*Student:* Choreograph a 32-count routine using levels and pathways.

*How to teach:* Choreograph 32 counts (four 8-counts) using levels and pathways; write it down.

- **Q:** Give an example of each level.
  **A:** Low: kneeling or floor move; middle: normal standing; high: jumping or on toes with arms up.
- **Q:** What is canon?
  **A:** Dancers perform the same movement starting at different times, like a round.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse and refine transitions with partners.

*How to teach:* Rehearse; smooth transitions between 8-counts.

- **Q:** How can you smooth a transition?
  **A:** End one move in a position that easily flows into the next, and practice the switch slowly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the routine for family.

*How to teach:* Perform for family.

- **Q:** How many 8-counts are in 32 counts?
  **A:** Four.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Reflect on dance as cardiovascular exercise using heart-rate data.

*How to teach:* Use heart-rate data to reflect on dance as exercise.

- **Q:** Your HR went from 78 to 150 bpm. What does that show?
  **A:** The routine was moderate-to-vigorous cardio exercise, within the target zone.

**Assessment:** Choreography performance rubric.

**Answer key:** Choreography rubric (1–4 each): structure (32 counts written in 8-count sections that match the music), use of levels and pathways (at least 2 levels and 2 pathways), transitions (smooth), performance (on beat, memorized, energy). Reflection proficient: uses pre/post heart-rate data to show dance raised HR into the target zone and explains it as cardio exercise.

## Health Education 7: Body systems and reproductive health (parent-led)

**Prepare:** Parent-led week: choose resources (KidsHealth reproductive system pages for teens or a book you approve). Prepare an anonymous-question box. Make or print a labeling worksheet matching what you teach (e.g., endocrine glands: pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, ovaries, testes).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking hormones only affect the reproductive system. Hormones also control growth, metabolism, stress response and blood sugar.
- Feeling embarrassed questions are 'weird.' Every question about the body is normal and deserves accurate answers from a trusted adult or valid source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Endocrine system
*Student:* Learn the endocrine glands and hormones that drive puberty.

*How to teach:* Teach the endocrine glands and their hormones (see key).

- **Q:** Which gland is called the 'master gland'?
  **A:** The pituitary gland.
- **Q:** Which hormones drive puberty?
  **A:** Estrogen and progesterone (made mainly in the ovaries) and testosterone (made mainly in the testes), triggered by pituitary hormones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reproductive anatomy
*Student:* Study diagrams of reproductive systems with parent guidance.

*How to teach:* Use your chosen diagrams to discuss the reproductive systems at the level you choose.

- **Q:** What is the main job of the reproductive system?
  **A:** To produce sex cells (eggs or sperm) and hormones, and make reproduction possible.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Questions
*Student:* Write anonymous questions and discuss them with a parent.

*How to teach:* Your child writes questions anonymously; answer them together calmly or look them up in your chosen resource.

- **Q:** Why is it important to get answers from trusted adults or valid sources rather than friends or random websites?
  **A:** Friends and random sites often have wrong information; trusted adults and medical sources are accurate.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Valid resources
*Student:* Identify trusted adults and valid sources for health questions.

*How to teach:* List trusted adults and valid sources (pediatrician, school nurse, KidsHealth).

- **Q:** Name two valid sources of health information.
  **A:** A doctor or nurse, parents, KidsHealth, CDC or other .gov sites.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a labeling worksheet.

*How to teach:* Complete the labeling worksheet and check with the key.

- **Q:** What does the thyroid gland control?
  **A:** Metabolism — how fast the body uses energy.

**Assessment:** Labeling worksheet.

**Answer key:** Labeling worksheet (endocrine version): pituitary (master gland, at base of brain — releases growth hormone and signals puberty), thyroid (neck — metabolism), adrenal glands (on kidneys — adrenaline, stress response), pancreas (insulin, blood sugar), ovaries (estrogen, progesterone; egg cells) and testes (testosterone; sperm cells). For the reproductive diagram, check labels against the parent-chosen resource. 90% correct = proficient.
