# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 17

## English Language Arts 7: Form and meaning: sonnets, free verse and Frost

**Prepare:** Sonnet 18, 'The Road Not Taken', 'Nothing Gold Can Stay', 'O Captain! My Captain!', a free-verse poem (e.g., Whitman 'When I Heard the Learn'd Astronomer'); audio recording of a poem reading.

**Watch for:**
- Reading 'The Road Not Taken' as simply 'take the road less traveled.' The speaker says the roads were 'really about the same'; the poem is about how we tell our choices later.
- Thinking free verse has no craft. It uses line breaks, repetition and imagery.

### Day 1 (Monday): The sonnet
*Student:* Read Sonnet 18, map its three quatrains and couplet, and explain how the couplet turns the argument.

*How to teach:* Read Sonnet 18 aloud. Your child brackets the three quatrains and the couplet and writes one sentence summarizing each. Ask where the poem turns and how the couplet answers the opening question.

- **Q:** What does the couplet say?
  **A:** 'So long as men can breathe or eyes can see, / So long lives this, and this gives life to thee' — the poem keeps the beloved alive forever.
- **Q:** Where is the turn?
  **A:** Line 9, 'But thy eternal summer shall not fade'.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Frost
*Student:* Read 'The Road Not Taken' and 'Nothing Gold Can Stay' and write a theme statement for each.

*How to teach:* Read both Frost poems aloud twice. Your child writes a theme statement for each with two supporting lines. Discuss the common misreading of 'The Road Not Taken' using the line about the roads being 'really about the same.' Check before moving on: ask "Theme of 'Nothing Gold Can Stay'?" Look for: Beauty and innocence are fleeting.

- **Q:** Theme of 'Nothing Gold Can Stay'?
  **A:** Beauty and innocence are fleeting.
- **Q:** Theme of 'The Road Not Taken'?
  **A:** People shape the meaning of their choices when they look back.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Free verse vs. rhyme
*Student:* Compare Whitman's 'O Captain! My Captain!' with a free-verse poem and explain how form affects mood.

*How to teach:* Read 'O Captain! My Captain!' and a free-verse poem aloud. Your child compares rhyme and rhythm and explains how the steady rhyme of 'O Captain!' creates a formal, mournful mood while free verse feels more conversational.

- **Q:** What is 'O Captain! My Captain!' about?
  **A:** An elegy for Abraham Lincoln, the 'captain' who died after the 'ship' (the nation) survived the Civil War.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poem vs. performance
*Student:* Listen to a recorded reading of a poem and compare how the reader's voice changes its meaning (RL.7.7).

*How to teach:* Listen to a recorded reading of one poem twice. Your child notes where the reader pauses, speeds up or emphasizes words and how this changes meaning compared with reading silently. Check before moving on: ask "How can pace change meaning?" Look for: Slow reading can stress grief or seriousness; fast reading can stress excitement.

- **Q:** How can pace change meaning?
  **A:** Slow reading can stress grief or seriousness; fast reading can stress excitement.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 1-page analysis of how form shapes meaning in one poem from the week.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses one poem and writes a one-page analysis with a claim about how its form shapes meaning, citing at least two specific lines. Check the claim before they draft. Check before moving on: ask "Example claim?" Look for: The sonnet's final couplet resolves the argument by claiming poetry makes beauty eternal.

- **Q:** Example claim?
  **A:** The sonnet's final couplet resolves the argument by claiming poetry makes beauty eternal.

**Assessment:** Poetry analysis page.

**Answer key:** Analysis rubric: 4 = names the form accurately, cites at least two specific lines, explains how form (rhyme, structure, line breaks) shapes meaning; 3 = form and meaning linked loosely; 2 = summary only; 1 = incomplete.

## Mathematics 7: Positive and negative numbers; adding rational numbers

**Prepare:** Two-color counters (or coins: heads = +, tails = −), number line taped to floor (−10 to 10), outdoor thermometer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking −8 is greater than −3. On the number line, farther left is smaller; −8 is colder.
- Adding the absolute values when signs differ. With different signs, subtract absolute values and keep the sign of the larger.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interpreting negatives
*Student:* Describe temperatures, elevations and bank balances with signed numbers and order them on a number line.

*How to teach:* Discuss temperatures, elevations and bank balances. Your child writes signed numbers for contexts and orders them on a number line. Check before moving on: ask "Order −3, 2, −7.5, 0, 1/2." Look for: −7.5, −3, 0, 1/2, 2.

- **Q:** Order −3, 2, −7.5, 0, 1/2.
  **A:** −7.5, −3, 0, 1/2, 2.
- **Q:** A balance of −$20 means?
  **A:** You owe $20.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adding with arrows
*Student:* Model sums such as -5 + 8 and 3 + (-7) with arrows on a number line and with two-color counters.

*How to teach:* Model −5 + 8 with arrows: start at 0, move 5 left, then 8 right. Then model with counters. Your child does several each way. Check before moving on: ask "−5 + 8?" Look for: 3.

- **Q:** −5 + 8?
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** 3 + (−7)?
  **A:** −4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Zero pairs
*Student:* Use zero pairs to explain why a number plus its opposite is 0 and solve 12 addition problems.

*How to teach:* Show a positive and negative counter canceling. Your child explains why a + (−a) = 0 and solves 12 problems. Check before moving on: ask "−9 + 9?" Look for: 0 — they are opposites (zero pairs).

- **Q:** −9 + 9?
  **A:** 0 — they are opposites (zero pairs).
- **Q:** −6 + (−4)?
  **A:** −10.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Adding fractions and decimals
*Student:* Add signed fractions and decimals such as -2.5 + 1.75 and -3/4 + (-1/2).

*How to teach:* Model with a number line using decimals and fractions. Your child solves signed fraction and decimal sums. Check before moving on: ask "−2.5 + 1.75?" Look for: −0.75.

- **Q:** −2.5 + 1.75?
  **A:** −0.75.
- **Q:** −3/4 + (−1/2)?
  **A:** −5/4 = −1 1/4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on adding rational numbers in context.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Temperature is −4°F and rises 10°. New temperature?" Look for: 6°F.

- **Q:** Temperature is −4°F and rises 10°. New temperature?
  **A:** 6°F.

**Assessment:** Addition quiz and number-line explanations.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) −7 + 3 = −4. 2) 6 + (−11) = −5. 3) −2.4 + (−1.6) = −4. 4) 1/3 + (−5/6) = −1/2. 5) Diver at −30 ft rises 12 ft: −18 ft. 6) Number line drawing correct for one problem.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Food webs and energy flow

**Prepare:** Yarn, index cards; optional sterilized owl pellet kit (or virtual pellet online), tweezers, gloves; calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing arrows from predator to prey. Arrows show energy flow: from the organism eaten to the organism that eats it.
- Thinking all energy moves up a level. Only about 10% is passed on; the rest is used for life processes and lost as heat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food chains
*Student:* Build food chains for a California chaparral ecosystem and label producers, consumers and decomposers.

*How to teach:* Build two chaparral food chains with your child, labeling producers, consumers and decomposers. Check before moving on: ask "Give a chaparral food chain." Look for: Chamise (producer) → brush rabbit (primary consumer) → coyote (secondary consumer); fungi and bacteria decompose them.

- **Q:** Give a chaparral food chain.
  **A:** Chamise (producer) → brush rabbit (primary consumer) → coyote (secondary consumer); fungi and bacteria decompose them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yarn web
*Student:* Connect organism cards with yarn into a food web and observe the effect of 'removing' one organism.

*How to teach:* Connect organism cards with yarn into a web. Remove one organism and have your child predict and trace the effects. Check before moving on: ask "If rabbits are removed, what happens?" Look for: Coyotes and hawks lose a food source and decline or switch prey; plants rabbits ate may increase.

- **Q:** If rabbits are removed, what happens?
  **A:** Coyotes and hawks lose a food source and decline or switch prey; plants rabbits ate may increase.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Owl pellet
*Student:* Dissect an owl pellet (or virtual pellet) and identify prey bones to build the owl's food web.

*How to teach:* Your child dissects the pellet, sorts bones, uses a chart to identify prey and builds the owl's food web. Check before moving on: ask "What prey bones are typical?" Look for: Voles, mice and shrews (skulls, jaws, leg bones).

- **Q:** What prey bones are typical?
  **A:** Voles, mice and shrews (skulls, jaws, leg bones).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Energy pyramid
*Student:* Calculate energy available at each level using the 10% rule and draw an energy pyramid.

*How to teach:* Model the 10% rule. Your child calculates energy at each level and draws a pyramid. Check before moving on: ask "Producers have 10,000 kcal. Energy at the third consumer level?" Look for: 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10 kcal.

- **Q:** Producers have 10,000 kcal. Energy at the third consumer level?
  **A:** 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10 kcal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on food webs and energy pyramids.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Why are there fewer top predators than herbivores?" Look for: Less energy is available at each higher level.

- **Q:** Why are there fewer top predators than herbivores?
  **A:** Less energy is available at each higher level.

**Assessment:** Food-web model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Arrow direction = energy flow. 2) Producer, consumer, decomposer examples. 3) 10% rule: 5,000 kcal producers → 50 kcal secondary consumers. 4) Pyramid widest at producers. 5) Removing a species affects others.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Feudalism and manor life

**Prepare:** Manor diagram; oath of fealty (Fordham); guild charter template.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking serfs were slaves. Serfs were bound to the land but could not be sold separately.
- Thinking knights owned land outright. They held fiefs in exchange for service.

### Day 1 (Monday): Feudal pyramid
*Student:* Draw the feudal pyramid and explain the exchange of land for loyalty.

*How to teach:* Draw the feudal pyramid together (king, lords, knights, peasants/serfs). Your child explains the exchange of land for loyalty and service. Check before moving on: ask "What did vassals receive and give?" Look for: Received land (fief) and protection; gave loyalty and military service.

- **Q:** What did vassals receive and give?
  **A:** Received land (fief) and protection; gave loyalty and military service.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Oath of fealty
*Student:* Read a medieval oath of fealty (Fordham) and role-play a homage ceremony.

*How to teach:* Read an oath of fealty aloud. Role-play a homage ceremony with your child as vassal; then discuss what each side promised. Check before moving on: ask "What does homage involve?" Look for: Vassal kneels, places hands between lord's, swears loyalty.

- **Q:** What does homage involve?
  **A:** Vassal kneels, places hands between lord's, swears loyalty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The manor
*Student:* Label a manor diagram and calculate how three-field rotation increased crop yields.

*How to teach:* Your child labels a manor diagram (manor house, church, fields, mill, village, commons) and calculates the gain from three-field rotation compared with two-field. Check before moving on: ask "How much more land was planted?" Look for: From one-half to two-thirds — a one-third increase in planted area.

- **Q:** How much more land was planted?
  **A:** From one-half to two-thirds — a one-third increase in planted area.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Towns and guilds
*Student:* Read about medieval guilds and design a guild charter for a craft.

*How to teach:* Read about guilds. Your child designs a guild charter for a craft with rules for apprentices, journeymen, masters, prices and quality. Check before moving on: ask "Guild stages?" Look for: Apprentice, journeyman, master.

- **Q:** Guild stages?
  **A:** Apprentice, journeyman, master.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 'day in the life' diary entry of a serf, knight or guild apprentice with accurate details.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a diary entry of a serf, knight or apprentice with at least five accurate historical details. Check before moving on: ask "Accurate serf details?" Look for: Work lord's fields several days a week, pay fees, live in a one-room hut.

- **Q:** Accurate serf details?
  **A:** Work lord's fields several days a week, pay fees, live in a one-room hut.

**Assessment:** Diary entry rubric.

**Answer key:** Diary rubric: 4 = accurate role details, at least five historical facts, first-person voice, vivid; 3 = some details; 2 = generic; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish 1A: Present tense of -ar verbs

**Prepare:** Verb chart, Conjuguemos -ar practice. Verbs: hablar, estudiar, trabajar, usar, necesitar, enseñar, practicar, tocar, escuchar, bailar, cantar, nadar.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving the -ar on: 'yo hablaro.' Drop -ar, add the ending: hablo.
- Forgetting accents in vosotros: habláis.

### Day 1 (Monday): -ar endings
*Student:* Learn infinitive, stem, and endings (-o, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an) and conjugate hablar and estudiar in a chart.

*How to teach:* Teach infinitive, stem, endings; chart hablar and estudiar.

- **Q:** Conjugate estudiar for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** estudio; estudiamos.
- **Q:** What is the stem of 'trabajar'?
  **A:** trabaj-.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drill
*Student:* Complete Conjuguemos -ar verb practice until you score 90%, then write 10 original sentences.

*How to teach:* Conjuguemos drill to 90%; write 10 sentences.

- **Q:** Write: 'We practice soccer.'
  **A:** Practicamos fútbol.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Who does what?
*Student:* Describe what each family member does on a weekday using -ar verbs and time expressions.

*How to teach:* Describe family members' weekdays.

- **Q:** Write: 'My mom works in the morning.'
  **A:** Mi mamá trabaja por la mañana.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Questions
*Student:* Form yes/no and question-word questions (¿Qué estudias? ¿Cuándo trabajas?) and interview a parent.

*How to teach:* Form questions (¿Qué estudias? ¿Cuándo trabajas?); interview a parent.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What do you need for class?'
  **A:** ¿Qué necesitas para la clase?
- **Q:** Answer: ¿Tocas la guitarra? (no)
  **A:** No, no toco la guitarra.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an -ar verb conjugation and sentence-writing quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the -ar quiz.

- **Q:** Conjugate 'escuchar' for ellos.
  **A:** escuchan.

**Assessment:** -ar verb quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** -ar quiz: hablar → hablo, hablas, habla, hablamos, habláis, hablan. Sample items: Nosotros (estudiar) → estudiamos; Ellos (trabajar) → trabajan; Tú (necesitar) → necesitas; Ella (enseñar) → enseña; Yo (tocar) → toco; Ustedes (usar) → usan. Sentence-writing: 5 sentences with correct subject-verb agreement. 80% passing.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Composition: arranging

**Prepare:** MuseScore or Song Maker, keyboard/ukulele, the 8-bar melody from week 16, criteria checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Making the B section too similar to A. Contrast in key, rhythm, register or mood.
- Picking chords that clash with the melody. Choose the chord (I, IV or V) that contains the melody's main note in each bar.

### Day 1 (Monday): Harmonize
*Student:* Add I-IV-V chords under the melody.

*How to teach:* Add chords: for each bar choose I (C-E-G), IV (F-A-C) or V (G-B-D) containing the melody's main notes.

- **Q:** A bar's melody uses mostly F and A. Which chord fits?
  **A:** IV (F major: F-A-C).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): B section
*Student:* Compose a contrasting 8-bar B section to create ABA form.

*How to teach:* Compose an 8-bar B section with contrast.

- **Q:** What is ABA form?
  **A:** A section, contrasting B section, then A returns.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Arrange
*Student:* Add a rhythm part or bass line.

*How to teach:* Add a rhythm part or bass line (roots of chords).

- **Q:** What is an accompaniment?
  **A:** Music that supports the melody, such as chords, bass line or rhythm.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse performing the composition and the two songs, using a criteria checklist.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the composition and two songs with a checklist.

- **Q:** Name two items on a performance checklist.
  **A:** Steady tempo, correct notes/chords, dynamics, posture, confident start and ending.

### Day 5 (Friday): Peer feedback
*Student:* Play the composition for a family member and record their feedback.

*How to teach:* Play for a family member and record feedback.

- **Q:** What feedback will you act on?
  **A:** Open answer.

**Assessment:** ABA composition draft.

**Answer key:** ABA composition rubric (1–4): A section 8 bars with I-IV-V harmony matching melody notes, contrasting 8-bar B section, return of A, added rhythm or bass part, notation readable. Feedback recorded and one revision planned. Proficient: clear ABA with harmony that fits most bars.

## Physical Education 7: Heart rate and intensity

**Prepare:** Stopwatch or watch with seconds, optional heart-rate monitor, graph paper. Teach pulse-taking with index and middle fingers (not the thumb).

**Watch for:**
- Taking a pulse with the thumb. The thumb has its own pulse; use index and middle fingers.
- Pressing hard on both sides of the neck. Press gently on one side only.

### Day 1 (Monday): Taking a pulse
*Student:* Measure resting heart rate at the wrist and neck for 3 mornings.

*How to teach:* Teach radial (wrist, thumb side) and carotid (neck, beside windpipe) pulses. Count 15 seconds × 4. Your child measures resting HR on 3 mornings before getting up.

- **Q:** You count 18 beats in 15 seconds. What is the heart rate?
  **A:** 18 × 4 = 72 beats per minute.
- **Q:** Why measure resting heart rate in the morning before getting up?
  **A:** That is when the body is most at rest, giving a consistent baseline.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Target zone
*Student:* Calculate maximum heart rate (220 − age) and a 60–85% target zone.

*How to teach:* Calculate max HR and target zone for your child's age.

- **Q:** For a 12-year-old, what are max HR and the 60–85% target zone?
  **A:** Max = 220 − 12 = 208 bpm; 60% = about 125 bpm, 85% = about 177 bpm, so the zone is about 125–177 bpm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Zone workout
*Student:* Do a 20-minute workout, checking pulse every 5 minutes to stay in zone.

*How to teach:* Do a 20-minute workout (jog, jump rope, dance) checking pulse every 5 minutes and adjusting effort to stay in zone.

- **Q:** Your HR is 190 at minute 10. What should you do?
  **A:** Slow down — you're above the target zone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recovery heart rate
*Student:* Measure heart rate recovery 1 and 3 minutes after exercise and graph it.

*How to teach:* Measure HR immediately after exercise, then at 1 and 3 minutes; graph it.

- **Q:** What does a fast drop in heart rate after exercise show?
  **A:** Better cardiorespiratory fitness — the heart recovers quickly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write an explanation of how heart rate shows fitness gains.

*How to teach:* Your child writes how heart rate shows fitness gains (see key).

- **Q:** Why does a fit person's resting heart rate go down?
  **A:** The heart muscle is stronger and pumps more blood per beat, so it needs fewer beats.

**Assessment:** Heart-rate data and explanation.

**Answer key:** Heart-rate data: 3 resting pulse readings averaged; max HR = 220 − age (age 12: 208; age 13: 207); target zone 60–85% (age 12: about 125–177 bpm; age 13: about 124–176). Recovery graph shows HR at 0, 1 and 3 minutes post-exercise. Explanation (proficient): as fitness improves, resting HR decreases, the heart pumps more blood per beat (stroke volume), the same workout feels easier at a lower HR, and HR returns to resting faster after exercise.

## Health Education 7: First aid and unit assessment

**Prepare:** First aid kit, bandages, gloves, a pillow for choking practice. Consider a Red Cross or AHA course for hands-on certification. Write the unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Putting butter or ice on burns. Cool a minor burn under cool running water for about 10–20 minutes, then cover loosely.
- Hanging up when calling 911. Stay on the line until the dispatcher says to hang up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Calling 911
*Student:* Practice what to say when calling 911.

*How to teach:* Practice a mock call: location, what happened, how many hurt, your name/number, stay on the line.

- **Q:** What is the most important information to give a 911 dispatcher first?
  **A:** Your exact location (address or landmarks).
- **Q:** What does a Good Samaritan law do?
  **A:** It protects people who give reasonable emergency help in good faith from being sued.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cuts and burns
*Student:* Practice treating cuts, scrapes and minor burns.

*How to teach:* Practice with gloves: press a clean cloth on a 'cut', clean and bandage; cool a 'burn' under running water.

- **Q:** How do you stop bleeding from a cut?
  **A:** Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or gauze; raise it if possible; get help if bleeding doesn't stop.
- **Q:** How should you treat a minor burn?
  **A:** Cool under cool running water about 10–20 minutes, don't pop blisters, cover loosely with sterile gauze; no ice or butter.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Choking
*Student:* Learn the universal choking sign and abdominal thrust technique on a pillow.

*How to teach:* Teach the universal sign (hands at throat). Practice abdominal thrusts on a pillow — never on a person.

- **Q:** What is the universal choking sign?
  **A:** Hands clutched at the throat.
- **Q:** Where do you place your fist for abdominal thrusts?
  **A:** Just above the navel, thumb side in, below the ribcage, then quick inward-and-upward thrusts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review unit 3 concepts.

*How to teach:* Review unit 3 with flash cards and a mock 911 call.

- **Q:** If a choking person can cough forcefully, what should you do?
  **A:** Encourage them to keep coughing; be ready to help if they can't breathe, speak or cough.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the unit 3 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 3 test (see key).

- **Q:** Name one emergency where you call 911.
  **A:** Someone is unconscious, not breathing, severely bleeding, choking and unable to breathe, a fire, a serious crash.

**Assessment:** First-aid skills check and unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Skills check: 911 call states location first, what happened, number of people hurt, stays on line; cut care: gloves, pressure with clean cloth, wash small cuts, bandage; burn: cool water 10–20 min, no ice/butter, cover; choking: recognizes universal sign, asks 'Are you choking?', gives abdominal thrusts (fist above navel, quick inward-upward thrusts) and calls 911. Unit 3 test (suggested): 1) leading cause of teen injury death — motor vehicle crashes 2) helmet fit 3) concussion signs 4) digital footprint 5) phishing clues 6) grooming signs 7) Drop, Cover, Hold On 8) 1 gallon water/person/day 9) burn care 10) universal choking sign.
