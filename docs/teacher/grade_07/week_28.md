# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 28

## English Language Arts 7: Roll of Thunder, chapters 4–6

**Prepare:** Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry; perspective chart (character, response to injustice, evidence).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Big Ma makes Cassie apologize because she agrees with Mr. Simms. She does it to protect Cassie from danger.
- Reading T.J. as only a villain; he's also lonely and manipulated.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapter 4
*Student:* Read Chapter 4 and explain T.J.'s choices and how others react to him.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 4 together. Your child explains T.J.'s choices and how Stacey and others react, citing evidence. Check before moving on: ask "What does T.J. do about the test?" Look for: He brings cheat notes; when Mama catches Stacey with them, Stacey takes the blame.

- **Q:** What does T.J. do about the test?
  **A:** He brings cheat notes; when Mama catches Stacey with them, Stacey takes the blame.
- **Q:** How do others respond?
  **A:** Stacey goes after T.J. at the Wallace store; Papa's family disapproves of the store.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 5
*Student:* Read Chapter 5 (the trip to Strawberry) and write a journal entry from Cassie's perspective about the Barnett Mercantile.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 5. Your child writes a journal entry from Cassie's perspective about what happened at the Barnett Mercantile and how she felt. Check before moving on: ask "What happens at the Mercantile?" Look for: Mr. Barnett keeps serving white customers ahead of T.J.'s order; when Cassie protests, he orders her out.

- **Q:** What happens at the Mercantile?
  **A:** Mr. Barnett keeps serving white customers ahead of T.J.'s order; when Cassie protests, he orders her out.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 6
*Student:* Read Chapter 6 and compare how Big Ma, Mama and Uncle Hammer each respond to injustice.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 6. Your child completes a chart comparing how Big Ma, Mama and Uncle Hammer each respond to injustice, citing evidence for each. Check before moving on: ask "How does Uncle Hammer respond to what happened to Cassie?" Look for: He wants to confront Mr. Simms; Mr. Morrison stops him.

- **Q:** How does Uncle Hammer respond to what happened to Cassie?
  **A:** He wants to confront Mr. Simms; Mr. Morrison stops him.
- **Q:** How does Mama respond?
  **A:** She explains the history and danger of racism to Cassie calmly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word connotations
*Student:* Analyze how Taylor's word choices carry connotations that show characters' attitudes in five passages.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes five passages for word connotations that show characters' attitudes, explaining the effect of each word. Check before moving on: ask "What does Mr. Simms forcing Cassie to say 'Miss Lillian Jean' show?" Look for: That he expects Black people to show deference to white people.

- **Q:** What does Mr. Simms forcing Cassie to say 'Miss Lillian Jean' show?
  **A:** That he expects Black people to show deference to white people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a chapters 4–6 quiz with a constructed response on contrasting perspectives.

*How to teach:* Give the chapters 4–6 quiz with a constructed response on contrasting perspectives. Review together. Check before moving on: ask "What does Cassie learn in Strawberry?" Look for: How deeply unfair the system is.

- **Q:** What does Cassie learn in Strawberry?
  **A:** How deeply unfair the system is.

**Assessment:** Perspective chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) T.J.'s cheat notes. 2) Mercantile incident. 3) Lillian Jean, Mr. Simms. 4) Big Ma makes Cassie apologize to protect her. 5) Uncle Hammer wants to confront Simms. CR rubric: compares two perspectives with evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Unit 6 review & test

**Prepare:** Index cards for the equation sort; Illustrative Mathematics unit 6 practice problems.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to check solutions — require substitution.
- Writing an inequality where an equation is meant (look for 'at least', 'more than').

### Day 1 (Monday): Equation sort
*Student:* Sort 15 equations and inequalities by solution and solving strategy.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 15 cards by solution and strategy and explains groupings. Check before moving on: ask "Which have the same solution: 2x + 4 = 10, 3(x − 1) = 6, x/3 = 1?" Look for: All have x = 3.

- **Q:** Which have the same solution: 2x + 4 = 10, 3(x − 1) = 6, x/3 = 1?
  **A:** All have x = 3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Create a problem
*Student:* Write two original word problems—one equation and one inequality—and solve them with full work.

*How to teach:* Your child writes one equation and one inequality word problem with full solutions. Check before moving on: ask "What makes a good word problem?" Look for: Clear quantities, a question, and a unique answer that fits the context.

- **Q:** What makes a good word problem?
  **A:** Clear quantities, a question, and a unique answer that fits the context.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner swap
*Student:* Solve a parent's or sibling's version of the original problems and check each other's answers.

*How to teach:* Swap problems with a parent or sibling; each solves and checks. Check before moving on: ask "How do you check an inequality answer?" Look for: Substitute a value from the solution set and one outside it.

- **Q:** How do you check an inequality answer?
  **A:** Substitute a value from the solution set and one outside it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of unit 6 problems from the IM practice set.

*How to teach:* Mixed review from IM practice set. Check before moving on: ask "−3(x + 2) = 12?" Look for: x = −6.

- **Q:** −3(x + 2) = 12?
  **A:** x = −6.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the unit 6 test on expressions, equations and inequalities.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 6 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "6 − 2x ≥ 0?" Look for: x ≤ 3.

- **Q:** 6 − 2x ≥ 0?
  **A:** x ≤ 3.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test sample answers: 1) 4x − 9 = 15 → 6. 2) −2(x − 5) = 4 → 3. 3) 2/3 x + 1 = 9 → 12. 4) 7 − x < 2 → x > 5. 5) Expand −3(2x − 1) = −6x + 3. 6) Simplify 5x − 2 − 3x + 8 = 2x + 6. 7) $15 per hour plus $20 tips, at least $110 → h ≥ 6. 8) Equation from tape diagram correct.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Continental drift and seafloor evidence

**Prepare:** Printed continent outlines with fossil and rock evidence; scissors; paper strips, shoebox; seafloor age map and ocean depth map (NOAA).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking continents float on the ocean. Plates are slabs of lithosphere moving over the asthenosphere.
- Thinking Wegener's idea was accepted immediately.

### Day 1 (Monday): Puzzle continents
*Student:* Cut out continents with fossil and rock evidence marked and fit them into Pangaea.

*How to teach:* Your child cuts and fits continents using fossil and rock clues. Check before moving on: ask "Mesosaurus fossils in South America and Africa show?" Look for: The continents were once joined.

- **Q:** Mesosaurus fossils in South America and Africa show?
  **A:** The continents were once joined.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wegener's evidence
*Student:* Read about Alfred Wegener and list four lines of evidence and why scientists first rejected his idea.

*How to teach:* Read about Wegener. Your child lists four evidence lines and why rejected. Check before moving on: ask "Four lines?" Look for: Continent fit, matching fossils, matching rock formations/mountains, past climate evidence (glacial deposits in tropics, coal in Antarctica).

- **Q:** Four lines?
  **A:** Continent fit, matching fossils, matching rock formations/mountains, past climate evidence (glacial deposits in tropics, coal in Antarctica).
- **Q:** Why rejected?
  **A:** He could not explain the mechanism for movement.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Seafloor spreading model
*Student:* Pull paper strips through a slit in a shoebox to model seafloor spreading and magnetic striping.

*How to teach:* Pull paper strips up through a slit to model spreading; color magnetic stripes. Check before moving on: ask "What do symmetric stripes show?" Look for: New seafloor forms at ridges and spreads both ways, recording magnetic reversals.

- **Q:** What do symmetric stripes show?
  **A:** New seafloor forms at ridges and spreads both ways, recording magnetic reversals.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ocean data
*Student:* Analyze maps of seafloor age and ocean depth to identify ridges and trenches.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes seafloor age and depth maps. Check before moving on: ask "Where is youngest seafloor?" Look for: At mid-ocean ridges.

- **Q:** Where is youngest seafloor?
  **A:** At mid-ocean ridges.
- **Q:** Oldest?
  **A:** Near trenches/continents.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER on whether continents have moved, using three types of evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER on whether continents have moved. Check before moving on: ask "What would a strong claim and evidence look like?" Look for: Claim: the continents have moved over millions of years. Evidence: Mesosaurus/Glossopteris fossils match across oceans; seafloor is youngest at ridges and older away from them; magnetic stripes are symmetric on both sides of ridges.

- **Q:** What would a strong claim and evidence look like?
  **A:** Claim: the continents have moved over millions of years. Evidence: Mesosaurus/Glossopteris fossils match across oceans; seafloor is youngest at ridges and older away from them; magnetic stripes are symmetric on both sides of ridges.

**Assessment:** CER on continental drift.

**Answer key:** CER rubric: 4 = accurate claim, at least two pieces of specific evidence from the investigation (numbers/observations), reasoning that links evidence to the scientific principle; 3 = claim and evidence, reasoning thin; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or incorrect. Proficient uses three types: fossil/rock matches, seafloor age pattern, magnetic striping.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Spread of Protestantism

**Prepare:** Religious map of Europe c. 1600; four-column reformer chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Protestants agreed. Lutherans, Calvinists and Anglicans differed.
- Thinking Henry VIII changed doctrines a lot. He mainly changed who led the church.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reformers compared
*Student:* Compare the ideas of Erasmus, Luther, Calvin and Tyndale in a four-column chart.

*How to teach:* Your child compares four reformers. Check before moving on: ask "Erasmus?" Look for: Criticized corruption but stayed Catholic.

- **Q:** Erasmus?
  **A:** Criticized corruption but stayed Catholic.
- **Q:** Calvin?
  **A:** Predestination; led Geneva.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Henry VIII
*Student:* Explain how Henry VIII created the Church of England and why the decision was political.

*How to teach:* Explain Henry VIII. Check before moving on: ask "Why did Henry break with Rome?" Look for: The pope wouldn't annul his marriage to Catherine of Aragon; Act of Supremacy (1534) made him head of the Church of England.

- **Q:** Why did Henry break with Rome?
  **A:** The pope wouldn't annul his marriage to Catherine of Aragon; Act of Supremacy (1534) made him head of the Church of England.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Church self-government
*Student:* Explain how Protestant congregations' self-government influenced democratic practices.

*How to teach:* Explain congregational self-government. Check before moving on: ask "How did it influence democracy?" Look for: Members elected leaders and governed themselves, practicing self-rule.

- **Q:** How did it influence democracy?
  **A:** Members elected leaders and governed themselves, practicing self-rule.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Religious map
*Student:* Color a map of Catholic and Protestant regions c. 1600 and predict effects on colonization of the Americas.

*How to teach:* Color the map; predict colonization effects. Check before moving on: ask "Catholic regions c. 1600?" Look for: Spain, Portugal, Italy, France (mostly), southern Germany.

- **Q:** Catholic regions c. 1600?
  **A:** Spain, Portugal, Italy, France (mostly), southern Germany.
- **Q:** Protestant?
  **A:** England, Scotland, Scandinavia, northern Germany, Netherlands.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on how the Reformation influenced ideas about government.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph. Check before moving on: ask "Key idea?" Look for: Questioning church authority encouraged questioning political authority.

- **Q:** Key idea?
  **A:** Questioning church authority encouraged questioning political authority.

**Assessment:** Map and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Map: correct shading and legend. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate.

## Spanish 1A: ¿Dónde está? Estar and location

**Prepare:** Stuffed animal, household objects. Prepositions: encima de, debajo de, al lado de, delante de, detrás de, cerca de, lejos de, entre, dentro de, a la derecha de, a la izquierda de.

**Watch for:**
- Using ser for location. Location of things and people uses estar: El libro está en la mesa (events use ser: La fiesta es en mi casa).
- Forgetting de + el = del: al lado del sofá.

### Day 1 (Monday): Estar
*Student:* Learn estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están and when to use estar for location and feelings.

*How to teach:* Teach estar forms and uses (location, feelings).

- **Q:** Conjugate estar for tú and ellos.
  **A:** estás; están.
- **Q:** Say 'I am tired' (boy).
  **A:** Estoy cansado.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prepositions
*Student:* Place a stuffed animal in 12 locations and describe each: El gato está debajo de la mesa.

*How to teach:* Place a stuffed animal in 12 spots and describe.

- **Q:** Say: 'The bear is behind the chair.'
  **A:** El oso está detrás de la silla.
- **Q:** Say: 'The cat is on top of the bed.'
  **A:** El gato está encima de la cama.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Where is it?
*Student:* Hide five objects and give a parent Spanish clues to find them; then switch roles.

*How to teach:* Hide objects and give clues; switch roles.

- **Q:** Give a clue: 'It's near the door.'
  **A:** Está cerca de la puerta.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ser vs. estar intro
*Student:* Sort 15 sentences into ser (origin, description) or estar (location, feelings) and explain each choice.

*How to teach:* Sort 15 sentences into ser or estar and explain.

- **Q:** Choose: Mi madre (es/está) de Chile.
  **A:** es — origin.
- **Q:** Choose: Mi madre (es/está) en la cocina.
  **A:** está — location.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an estar and prepositions quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the estar and prepositions quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'The backpack is next to the sofa.'
  **A:** La mochila está al lado del sofá.

**Assessment:** Estar and prepositions quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: estar — estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están. Samples: The cat is under the table → El gato está debajo de la mesa; The books are next to the computer → Los libros están al lado de la computadora; We are near the park → Estamos cerca del parque. Ser vs estar sort: origin, description, profession, time → ser; location, feelings/conditions → estar. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Photography composition

**Prepare:** Phone or camera. Image of Dorothea Lange's 'Migrant Mother' (1936, Nipomo, California) and a modern advertisement.

**Watch for:**
- Centering every subject. The rule of thirds places subjects along lines/intersections for more dynamic images.
- Thinking photos are always objective. Photographers choose framing, angle and moment, shaping the message.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analyze photos
*Student:* Analyze Dorothea Lange's 'Migrant Mother' and a modern ad for composition and audience influence.

*How to teach:* Analyze Migrant Mother (close framing, worried expression, children turned away, made for the Farm Security Administration during the Great Depression) and an ad.

- **Q:** What was the purpose of 'Migrant Mother'?
  **A:** To document poverty of migrant farm workers during the Great Depression and build support for government aid.
- **Q:** How does an ad use composition to influence viewers?
  **A:** It places the product at the focal point, uses lighting, angles and attractive models to make it desirable.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rule of thirds
*Student:* Take 10 photos using the rule of thirds.

*How to teach:* Take 10 photos with the rule of thirds (turn on grid lines).

- **Q:** What is the rule of thirds?
  **A:** Dividing the frame into a 3×3 grid and placing key subjects along the lines or at intersections.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lines and framing
*Student:* Take 10 photos using leading lines and natural frames.

*How to teach:* Take 10 photos with leading lines and natural frames.

- **Q:** What are leading lines?
  **A:** Lines in the image (roads, fences, rivers) that guide the eye to the subject.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Angles
*Student:* Photograph one object from 5 angles (bird's-eye, worm's-eye, etc.) and compare effect.

*How to teach:* Photograph one object from 5 angles.

- **Q:** What effect does a worm's-eye view have?
  **A:** Shooting from below makes the subject look large, powerful or imposing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Select
*Student:* Choose your best 5 photos and caption each with the technique used.

*How to teach:* Select and caption best 5.

- **Q:** What is depth of field?
  **A:** How much of the photo, front to back, is in sharp focus.

**Assessment:** Photo portfolio of 5.

**Answer key:** Photo portfolio of 5 (1–4): each photo clearly shows a named technique (rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, angle, depth of field), is in focus and well exposed, and has a caption. Proficient: 5 strong photos showing at least 4 different techniques.

## Physical Education 7: Jump rope skills

**Prepare:** Jump rope sized correctly (stand on the middle; handles reach the armpits). Smooth surface, supportive shoes.

**Watch for:**
- Jumping too high. Small jumps on the balls of the feet, about 1 inch off the ground.
- Swinging the rope with the whole arm. Turn with the wrists, elbows close to the sides.

### Day 1 (Monday): Basics
*Student:* Practice basic bounce, alternate foot and side swing for 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Teach basic bounce, alternate foot step and side swing; 10 minutes of practice.

- **Q:** How should you size a jump rope?
  **A:** Stand on the middle; the handles should reach about the armpits.
- **Q:** Where should your elbows be?
  **A:** Close to your sides, turning the rope with your wrists.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tricks
*Student:* Learn criss-cross and boxer step.

*How to teach:* Teach criss-cross (arms cross in front on the jump) and boxer step (shift weight side to side, tapping the other foot).

- **Q:** What's the key to a criss-cross?
  **A:** Cross arms wide in front so the loop opens, then uncross on the next jump.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Endurance
*Student:* Complete 5 one-minute jump rounds with 30-second rests.

*How to teach:* 5 one-minute rounds with 30-second rests; count jumps per round.

- **Q:** Which energy system and component does this train?
  **A:** Cardiorespiratory endurance (aerobic) and muscular endurance in calves.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Routine
*Student:* Create a 1-minute jump rope routine with 5 skills.

*How to teach:* Create a 1-minute routine with 5 skills; practice.

- **Q:** Why do jump rope skills help other sports?
  **A:** They build footwork, coordination, timing and endurance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform the routine and record continuous-jump time.

*How to teach:* Perform the routine and record longest continuous-jump time.

- **Q:** What is a double under?
  **A:** The rope passes under the feet twice in one jump.

**Assessment:** Jump rope routine.

**Answer key:** Routine rubric (1–4): includes 5 skills (e.g., basic bounce, alternate foot, side swing, criss-cross, boxer step), smooth transitions, 1 minute long, control (few misses). Record continuous-jump time from the endurance test. Proficient: 5 skills with no more than 2 misses.

## Health Education 7: Boundaries and consent

**Prepare:** Write boundary scenario cards (a hug from a relative, a friend reading your texts, someone posting your photo, a friend borrowing things). Parent preview: discuss consent in age-appropriate, everyday terms.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking setting boundaries is rude. Boundaries are healthy and help relationships; you can state them kindly.
- Thinking 'no' only counts if said loudly. Silence, hesitation or pulling away is not a yes; check in and respect it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Types of boundaries
*Student:* Identify physical, emotional and digital boundaries.

*How to teach:* Define physical, emotional and digital boundaries with examples; your child lists one personal boundary of each type.

- **Q:** Give an example of a digital boundary.
  **A:** Not sharing passwords, asking before posting someone's photo, not texting after 9 p.m.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Saying no
*Student:* Practice assertive ways to state boundaries.

*How to teach:* Practice: 'I'm not comfortable with that,' 'Please stop,' 'I'd prefer a high five.'

- **Q:** State a boundary with an I-statement for a friend who keeps grabbing your phone.
  **A:** 'I don't like it when you take my phone. Please ask first.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Respecting others
*Student:* Practice asking permission and accepting 'no'.

*How to teach:* Practice asking ('Can I…?') and accepting 'no' without pressure or sulking.

- **Q:** What is consent?
  **A:** Freely given permission; it can be changed or taken back at any time, and pressure means it isn't freely given.
- **Q:** How do you respond respectfully if someone says no?
  **A:** Accept it right away ('Okay, no problem') without arguing or guilt-tripping.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Online boundaries
*Student:* Discuss sharing photos and messages and when to tell an adult.

*How to teach:* Discuss: never share photos without permission; never send or forward revealing photos (it can be illegal for minors); tell an adult if asked.

- **Q:** Someone asks you to send a private photo. What should you do?
  **A:** Refuse, don't send it, save the request and tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Is it okay to forward a photo someone sent you privately?
  **A:** No — you need their permission; forwarding can hurt them and may be illegal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Role-play boundary-setting scenarios.

*How to teach:* Run scenario role-plays and score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What should you do if someone keeps ignoring your boundary?
  **A:** Repeat it firmly, leave the situation and tell a trusted adult.

**Assessment:** Role-play.

**Answer key:** Role-play rubric: 4 = states boundary clearly with an I-statement, uses assertive body language, holds firm if pushed, and in reverse role accepts a 'no' gracefully; 3 = clear but needs prompting; 2 = unclear or gives in; 1 = does not set boundary.
