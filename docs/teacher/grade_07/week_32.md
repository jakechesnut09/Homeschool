# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 32

## English Language Arts 7: Famous speeches: rhetoric and purpose

**Prepare:** Five printed ads; Gettysburg Address text and recording; Susan B. Anthony's 'On Women's Right to Vote' (1873); Chief Joseph's 1877 surrender speech.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pathos is the strongest appeal. A strong argument balances all three.
- Thinking parallelism is just repetition. It is repeated grammatical structure.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ethos, pathos, logos
*Student:* Learn the three appeals and find examples in five advertisements.

*How to teach:* Teach ethos, pathos and logos with examples. Your child finds examples in five advertisements. Check before moving on: ask "A dentist recommends a toothpaste." Look for: Ethos (credibility).

- **Q:** A dentist recommends a toothpaste.
  **A:** Ethos (credibility).
- **Q:** '9 out of 10 users saw results.'
  **A:** Logos (logic/statistics).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gettysburg Address
*Student:* Read and listen to the Gettysburg Address and annotate parallelism and purpose.

*How to teach:* Read and listen to the Gettysburg Address. Your child annotates parallelism and purpose. Check before moving on: ask "Example of parallelism?" Look for: 'We can not dedicate—we can not consecrate—we can not hallow—this ground'; 'government of the people, by the people, for the people'.

- **Q:** Example of parallelism?
  **A:** 'We can not dedicate—we can not consecrate—we can not hallow—this ground'; 'government of the people, by the people, for the people'.
- **Q:** Purpose?
  **A:** To honor the fallen soldiers and rededicate the nation to equality and democracy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Susan B. Anthony
*Student:* Read 'On Women's Right to Vote' and trace her claims and evidence.

*How to teach:* Read 'On Women's Right to Vote'. Your child traces Anthony's claims and evidence. Check before moving on: ask "What is her main claim?" Look for: Women are citizens, so denying them the vote violates the Constitution.

- **Q:** What is her main claim?
  **A:** Women are citizens, so denying them the vote violates the Constitution.
- **Q:** What evidence does she use?
  **A:** The Preamble: 'We, the people' — not 'We, the white male citizens'.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chief Joseph
*Student:* Read Chief Joseph's surrender speech and analyze how word choice conveys his point of view.

*How to teach:* Read Chief Joseph's surrender speech. Your child analyzes how word choice conveys his point of view. Check before moving on: ask "What words show his point of view?" Look for: 'I am tired; my heart is sick and sad'; 'the little children are freezing to death'; 'I will fight no more forever' — grief and exhaustion.

- **Q:** What words show his point of view?
  **A:** 'I am tired; my heart is sick and sad'; 'the little children are freezing to death'; 'I will fight no more forever' — grief and exhaustion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph comparing how two speakers use appeals and take a rhetoric quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph comparing how two speakers use appeals, then takes the rhetoric quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Which appeal does Chief Joseph mainly use?" Look for: Pathos.

- **Q:** Which appeal does Chief Joseph mainly use?
  **A:** Pathos.

**Assessment:** Rhetoric quiz and comparison paragraph.

**Answer key:** Rhetoric quiz: ethos = credibility, pathos = emotion, logos = logic; parallelism example from Gettysburg; Anthony's claim and evidence; Chief Joseph's tone (sorrowful, weary). Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two or more accurately quoted and cited pieces of evidence, explanation that connects each quote to the claim; 3 = claim and evidence but thin explanation; 2 = evidence summarized rather than quoted, or weak link to claim; 1 = opinion without evidence.

## Mathematics 7: Surface area & unit test

**Prepare:** Cardstock, tape, ruler, scissors.

**Watch for:**
- Counting only 3 faces of a rectangular prism. There are 6 (3 pairs).
- Using cubic units for surface area. Surface area is square units.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nets
*Student:* Draw and cut out nets of a triangular and a rectangular prism and fold them to check.

*How to teach:* Your child draws, cuts and folds nets of triangular and rectangular prisms. Check before moving on: ask "How many faces in a triangular prism net?" Look for: 5 (2 triangles, 3 rectangles).

- **Q:** How many faces in a triangular prism net?
  **A:** 5 (2 triangles, 3 rectangles).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Surface area
*Student:* Find surface area of prisms from nets and from dimensions, including a prism with a hole.

*How to teach:* Model SA as the sum of face areas. Your child finds SA including a prism with a hole. Check before moving on: ask "2 × 3 × 4 prism SA?" Look for: 2(6 + 8 + 12) = 52 units².

- **Q:** 2 × 3 × 4 prism SA?
  **A:** 2(6 + 8 + 12) = 52 units².
- **Q:** Cube side 5?
  **A:** 150 units².

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Which measure?
*Student:* Decide whether 8 situations (wrapping a gift, filling a pool) need surface area or volume, then solve them.

*How to teach:* Your child classifies 8 situations then solves. Check before moving on: ask "Wrapping a gift?" Look for: Surface area.

- **Q:** Wrapping a gift?
  **A:** Surface area.
- **Q:** Filling a pool?
  **A:** Volume.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Mixed review of angles, triangles, cross sections, surface area and volume.

*How to teach:* Mixed review; one of each topic. Check before moving on: ask "Supplement of 72°?" Look for: 108°.

- **Q:** Supplement of 72°?
  **A:** 108°.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the unit 7 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 7 test (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Cube with volume 27. Surface area?" Look for: Side 3; SA = 54.

- **Q:** Cube with volume 27. Surface area?
  **A:** Side 3; SA = 54.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test sample answers: 1) Complement of 35 = 55. 2) x + 2x + 30 = 180 → 50. 3) 4, 5, 10: no triangle. 4) Prism 4 × 5 × 6 volume 120, SA 148. 5) Triangular prism (3-4-5 right triangle, length 10): V = 60, SA = 12 + 120 = 132. 6) Cross section of cylinder parallel to base: circle.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Biodiversity and ecosystem services

**Prepare:** Hula hoop or string square (1 m²), field guide or iNaturalist app, clipboard; reading on pollinator decline.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking biodiversity means only number of species. It includes how evenly individuals are spread among species.
- Thinking ecosystem services are free and unlimited.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ecosystem services
*Student:* List services ecosystems provide (pollination, water filtering, flood control) and give a California example of each.

*How to teach:* Your child lists services with California examples. Check before moving on: ask "Pollination example?" Look for: Bees pollinate Central Valley almonds.

- **Q:** Pollination example?
  **A:** Bees pollinate Central Valley almonds.
- **Q:** Water filtering?
  **A:** Wetlands filter runoff before it reaches the Bay.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Biodiversity survey
*Student:* Survey a 1-square-meter plot in two locations and count species and individuals.

*How to teach:* Survey two 1-m² plots (lawn vs garden/wild). Your child counts species and individuals. Check before moving on: ask "Why compare two locations?" Look for: To see how habitat affects biodiversity.

- **Q:** Why compare two locations?
  **A:** To see how habitat affects biodiversity.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Biodiversity index
*Student:* Calculate a simple biodiversity index for each plot and compare.

*How to teach:* Your child calculates species ÷ individuals for each plot. Check before moving on: ask "8 species, 40 individuals?" Look for: 0.2.

- **Q:** 8 species, 40 individuals?
  **A:** 0.2.
- **Q:** Which plot is more diverse?
  **A:** The one with the higher index.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pollinators
*Student:* Read about declining pollinators and explain how that affects food crops.

*How to teach:* Read about pollinators. Your child explains food crop effects. Check before moving on: ask "Why worry about bee decline?" Look for: About one-third of our food crops depend on animal pollinators.

- **Q:** Why worry about bee decline?
  **A:** About one-third of our food crops depend on animal pollinators.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a report comparing the two plots' biodiversity.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a report comparing plots. Check before moving on: ask "Report sections?" Look for: Question, method, data, comparison, conclusion.

- **Q:** Report sections?
  **A:** Question, method, data, comparison, conclusion.

**Assessment:** Biodiversity survey report.

**Answer key:** Report rubric: data tables, index calculation, comparison, explanation of habitat differences.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Age of Exploration

**Prepare:** World map; Columbus's journal excerpt (Oct 1492); Library of Congress maps from 1450 and 1570.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Columbus proved the Earth was round. Educated Europeans already knew.
- Thinking Columbus 'discovered' empty land. Millions of people lived there.

### Day 1 (Monday): Motives
*Student:* Explain the motives for exploration: God, gold and glory, and the new technologies that enabled it.

*How to teach:* Your child lists motives for exploration (God, gold, glory) and the technologies that made it possible. Check before moving on: ask "God, gold and glory?" Look for: Spread Christianity, gain wealth, win fame.

- **Q:** God, gold and glory?
  **A:** Spread Christianity, gain wealth, win fame.
- **Q:** Technologies?
  **A:** Caravel, astrolabe, magnetic compass, better maps.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mapping voyages
*Student:* Map the voyages of Dias, da Gama, Columbus, Magellan and Cabrillo on a world map.

*How to teach:* Your child maps the voyages of Dias, da Gama, Columbus, Magellan and Cabrillo with dates. Check before moving on: ask "Dias?" Look for: Rounded Cape of Good Hope 1488.

- **Q:** Dias?
  **A:** Rounded Cape of Good Hope 1488.
- **Q:** Cabrillo?
  **A:** Explored California coast 1542.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Columbus's journal
*Student:* Read Columbus's journal entry from October 1492 and analyze his descriptions of the Taíno.

*How to teach:* Read Columbus's October 1492 journal entry. Your child analyzes his descriptions of the Taíno and what they reveal about his intentions. Check before moving on: ask "How does he describe the Taíno?" Look for: As gentle and generous, but thinks they'd make good servants and easily be converted.

- **Q:** How does he describe the Taíno?
  **A:** As gentle and generous, but thinks they'd make good servants and easily be converted.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Changing maps
*Student:* Compare maps from 1450 and 1570 (Library of Congress) and explain how the European worldview changed.

*How to teach:* Compare maps from 1450 and 1570. Your child lists what changed and how it reflects a new worldview. Check before moving on: ask "Change?" Look for: Americas appear; Europeans realized a new continent existed.

- **Q:** Change?
  **A:** Americas appear; Europeans realized a new continent existed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on explorers and routes.

*How to teach:* Give the explorers and routes quiz. Check before moving on: ask "First circumnavigation?" Look for: Magellan's expedition (1519–1522), completed by Elcano.

- **Q:** First circumnavigation?
  **A:** Magellan's expedition (1519–1522), completed by Elcano.

**Assessment:** Voyage map and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: motives, technologies, Dias 1488, da Gama 1498, Columbus 1492, Magellan 1519–22, Cabrillo 1542. Map: routes labeled.

## Spanish 1A: Unit 8 assessment: Mi ciudad

**Prepare:** Phone/camera, town map, a short tourist brochure in Spanish (many city tourism sites have Spanish versions).

**Watch for:**
- Narrating with only 'hay.' Vary: está, la gente va, me gusta, se puede…
- Forgetting the tourist role uses usted with strangers: ¿Puede usted ayudarme?

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose five places in your town and write two sentences about each: where it is and what people do there.

*How to teach:* Choose five places; write two sentences each.

- **Q:** Write: 'The park is near my house. People go to the park to walk.'
  **A:** El parque está cerca de mi casa. La gente va al parque para caminar.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Record
*Student:* Film or photograph the places and record a narrated tour in Spanish (2 minutes).

*How to teach:* Film and narrate a 2-minute tour.

  **A:** Este es el supermercado.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading
*Student:* Read a short tourist brochure about a Spanish-speaking city and answer comprehension questions.

*How to teach:* Read a tourist brochure; answer questions.

- **Q:** What does 'el ayuntamiento' mean?
  **A:** City hall.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversation
*Student:* Role-play a tourist asking a local for directions to three places.

*How to teach:* Role-play a tourist asking directions (usted).

- **Q:** Ask politely: 'Where is the plaza, please?'
  **A:** Perdone, ¿dónde está la plaza, por favor?

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the unit 8 test on estar, ir, places, and directions.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 8 test.

- **Q:** Translate: 'We are going to the museum.'
  **A:** Vamos al museo.

**Assessment:** Town tour video rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Town tour video rubric (1–4 each): 5 places with location (estar + prepositions) and activities, 2 minutes, pronunciation, visuals. Unit 8 test: estar conjugation and location, ir conjugation and al/a la, places vocabulary, directions. Model: 'Esta es la biblioteca. Está al lado del parque. La gente va a la biblioteca para leer.'

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Additive sculpture

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay, aluminum foil, wire, clay tools (toothpicks, forks). Images of Donatello's David (bronze, c. 1440s) and Michelangelo's David (marble, 1501–1504) from several angles.

**Watch for:**
- Building thick, solid clay forms. Use a foil armature so the clay is thinner and dries without cracking.
- Only making the front interesting. Sculpture in the round should work from every view.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sculpture study
*Student:* Compare Donatello's 'David' and Michelangelo's 'David' from multiple viewpoints online.

*How to teach:* Compare the two Davids from multiple viewpoints.

- **Q:** Which David was carved from marble and which was cast in bronze?
  **A:** Michelangelo's is marble (carved); Donatello's is bronze (cast).
- **Q:** What moment does Michelangelo's David show?
  **A:** The moment before the fight with Goliath — tense and focused.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Armature
*Student:* Build a foil and wire armature for a small figure or animal.

*How to teach:* Build a foil and wire armature.

- **Q:** What is an armature?
  **A:** An internal support framework for a sculpture.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Clay
*Student:* Add clay over the armature, building major forms.

*How to teach:* Add clay over the armature, building major forms.

- **Q:** Is adding clay additive or subtractive sculpture?
  **A:** Additive.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Detail
*Student:* Add texture and detail with tools.

*How to teach:* Add texture and details with tools.

- **Q:** What does 'in the round' mean?
  **A:** A freestanding sculpture meant to be viewed from all sides.

### Day 5 (Friday): Dry
*Student:* Smooth and let the sculpture dry; sketch it from three views.

*How to teach:* Smooth; let dry; sketch from three views.

- **Q:** What is contrapposto?
  **A:** A pose with weight on one leg so hips and shoulders tilt in opposite directions, looking natural and relaxed.

**Assessment:** Clay sculpture progress.

**Answer key:** Sculpture progress rubric (1–4): stable armature, forms built additively over armature, texture and details, works in the round; three-view sketches (front, side, back). Comparison key: Donatello's David — bronze, smaller, youthful, wearing a hat and boots, standing over Goliath's head after victory, relaxed contrapposto; Michelangelo's David — marble, about 17 ft tall, muscular, shown before the battle with focused, tense expression and sling over the shoulder, carved (subtractive).

## Physical Education 7: Track meet

**Prepare:** Track or field, stopwatch, tape measure, a schedule for each day's events, water and snacks.

**Watch for:**
- Skipping the warm-up on competition day. A warm-up improves performance and prevents injury.
- Comparing to others only. Focus on personal records (PRs).

### Day 1 (Monday): Prep
*Student:* Plan events and warm-up for the meet.

*How to teach:* Plan events, order and warm-up; create a results sheet.

- **Q:** Why plan the event order?
  **A:** To allow rest between hard events and warm-up time before each.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Meet day 1
*Student:* Compete in sprints and long jump.

*How to teach:* Compete in sprints and long jump.

- **Q:** What is a heat?
  **A:** A preliminary race round when there are too many runners for one race.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Meet day 2
*Student:* Compete in distance run and throw.

*How to teach:* Compete in distance run and throw.

- **Q:** What pacing strategy will you use?
  **A:** Even pace or slight negative split.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recovery
*Student:* Do a light recovery walk and stretch.

*How to teach:* Light recovery walk and stretching.

- **Q:** Why do a recovery day after competition?
  **A:** Muscles repair and soreness eases with light movement.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Record personal records and reflect on improvement.

*How to teach:* Record PRs and reflect.

- **Q:** What is a personal record (PR)?
  **A:** Your best-ever performance in an event.

**Assessment:** Track meet results.

**Answer key:** Track meet results: times/marks for each event (50 m or 100 m sprint, long jump, distance run, throw), with PRs marked. Reflection proficient: compares to earlier marks with numbers, explains why improvement happened (training, technique), and sets a next goal.

## Health Education 7: Noncommunicable diseases

**Prepare:** Find CDC or American Heart/Diabetes Association pages on heart disease, type 2 diabetes, cancer and asthma. Print a family health history form (e.g., CDC 'My Family Health Portrait'). Talk with relatives about health history in advance.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking chronic diseases only affect old people. Habits formed now (diet, activity, not smoking) affect risk later, and type 2 diabetes and asthma occur in teens.
- Believing a family history means you will definitely get the disease. It raises risk, but healthy behaviors can lower it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chronic diseases
*Student:* Research heart disease, type 2 diabetes, cancer and asthma.

*How to teach:* Your child researches four chronic diseases: cause, symptoms, risk factors.

- **Q:** What happens in type 2 diabetes?
  **A:** The body doesn't use insulin well (insulin resistance), so blood sugar stays too high.
- **Q:** What is asthma?
  **A:** A chronic disease where airways become inflamed and narrow, causing wheezing, coughing and shortness of breath; triggers include smoke, pollen, exercise and cold air.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Risk factors
*Student:* Sort risk factors into controllable and uncontrollable.

*How to teach:* Sort risk factors: uncontrollable (age, genes, family history, sex) vs controllable (diet, activity, smoking, sleep, sun exposure).

- **Q:** Is family history a controllable risk factor?
  **A:** No — it is uncontrollable.
- **Q:** Name three controllable risk factors for heart disease.
  **A:** Smoking, inactivity, unhealthy diet high in saturated fat and sodium, high blood pressure, excess weight.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family history
*Student:* Complete a family health history with a parent.

*How to teach:* Complete the family health history with a parent.

- **Q:** Why does a doctor want your family health history?
  **A:** It shows which diseases you may be at higher risk for so you can screen earlier and prevent them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prevention
*Student:* Connect nutrition and activity to preventing chronic disease.

*How to teach:* Connect MyPlate and 60 minutes of activity to lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers.

- **Q:** How does physical activity lower diabetes risk?
  **A:** Muscles use glucose, helping control blood sugar and improving insulin sensitivity; it also helps maintain a healthy weight.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a prevention plan for one chronic disease.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a prevention plan; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Name one way to lower skin cancer risk.
  **A:** Use SPF 30+ sunscreen, wear hats/protective clothing, avoid tanning beds, seek shade midday.

**Assessment:** Prevention plan.

**Answer key:** Prevention plan rubric: 4 = names the disease and accurate description, lists 2 uncontrollable and 3 controllable risk factors, gives 3+ specific SMART prevention behaviors (e.g., 60 min activity daily, limit sugary drinks, never smoke, sunscreen), mentions screenings/checkups; 3 = most elements; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
