# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 25

## English Language Arts 7: Drafting the research report

**Prepare:** Outline and note cards; word processor; a chart, image or diagram from a credible source for one section.

**Watch for:**
- Stringing quotes together ('quote dumping'). Each body paragraph should be mostly your child's words, with quotes as support.
- Adding an image without a caption or credit. Every visual needs a caption explaining what it shows and its source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Introduction
*Student:* Draft an introduction that hooks the reader and states the focus of the report.

*How to teach:* Model a hook for an informational report (a surprising fact). Your child drafts an introduction ending with a focus statement. Check before moving on: ask "How is a report's focus statement different from an argument's claim?" Look for: It states what the report will explain ('This report explains how…'), not a position to defend.

- **Q:** How is a report's focus statement different from an argument's claim?
  **A:** It states what the report will explain ('This report explains how…'), not a position to defend.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body section 1
*Student:* Draft the first body section integrating at least two sources with citations.

*How to teach:* Model integrating a source: introduce, quote or paraphrase, cite, explain. Your child drafts body section 1 using at least two sources. Check before moving on: ask "Integrate this fact correctly: NASA says sea level rose about 4 inches since 1993." Look for: According to NASA, global sea level has risen about four inches since 1993 (NASA). — introduced, paraphrased, cited.

- **Q:** Integrate this fact correctly: NASA says sea level rose about 4 inches since 1993.
  **A:** According to NASA, global sea level has risen about four inches since 1993 (NASA). — introduced, paraphrased, cited.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Body sections 2–3
*Student:* Draft remaining body sections, adding a chart, image or diagram with caption.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts sections 2–3 and inserts one visual with a caption ('Figure 1: … Source: …'). Check before moving on: ask "Why include a visual?" Look for: It shows data or a process more clearly than words alone and supports the reader's understanding.

- **Q:** Why include a visual?
  **A:** It shows data or a process more clearly than words alone and supports the reader's understanding.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conclusion & works cited
*Student:* Draft a conclusion and format the Works Cited page.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts a conclusion (restate focus, key takeaways, why it matters) and formats Works Cited (alphabetical, hanging indent, double-spaced). Check before moving on: ask "How is Works Cited ordered?" Look for: Alphabetically by the first word of each entry (usually the author's last name).

- **Q:** How is Works Cited ordered?
  **A:** Alphabetically by the first word of each entry (usually the author's last name).

### Day 5 (Friday): Self-check
*Student:* Highlight every citation and verify each matches a Works Cited entry.

*How to teach:* Your child highlights each in-text citation and checks it against a Works Cited entry; fix any mismatches. Check before moving on: ask "An in-text citation says (Lopez) but Works Cited has no Lopez. What's wrong?" Look for: The source is missing from Works Cited (or the citation is wrong); add or correct the entry.

- **Q:** An in-text citation says (Lopez) but Works Cited has no Lopez. What's wrong?
  **A:** The source is missing from Works Cited (or the citation is wrong); add or correct the entry.

**Assessment:** Full research report draft.

**Answer key:** Draft checklist: introduction with focus statement; three body sections each citing at least two sources with explanation; one captioned, credited visual; conclusion; Works Cited in MLA format; every in-text citation matches an entry. Return with 2–3 specific revision comments.

## Mathematics 7: Inequalities

**Prepare:** Number line, colored pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Not reversing the inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative. Test a value to check.
- Open vs closed circles: ≤, ≥ use closed; <, > use open.

### Day 1 (Monday): Representing situations
*Student:* Write inequalities for situations like 'at least 13 to ride' and graph them on number lines.

*How to teach:* Model 'at least 13' as h ≥ 13 with a closed circle. Your child writes and graphs several. Check before moving on: ask "'No more than 8 people'?" Look for: p ≤ 8, closed circle at 8, shade left (whole numbers only in context).

- **Q:** 'No more than 8 people'?
  **A:** p ≤ 8, closed circle at 8, shade left (whole numbers only in context).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finding solutions
*Student:* Test values in inequalities and discover the reverse when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

*How to teach:* Test values in −2x > 6. Your child discovers solutions are x < −3 and states the reversal rule. Check before moving on: ask "Is x = −5 a solution to −2x > 6?" Look for: Yes: 10 > 6.

- **Q:** Is x = −5 a solution to −2x > 6?
  **A:** Yes: 10 > 6.
- **Q:** Is x = 0?
  **A:** No: 0 > 6 is false.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solving inequalities
*Student:* Solve 10 inequalities like 5x - 3 ≥ 22 and -2x + 4 < 10 and graph the solutions.

*How to teach:* Model 5x − 3 ≥ 22. Your child solves 10 and graphs. Check before moving on: ask "5x − 3 ≥ 22?" Look for: x ≥ 5.

- **Q:** 5x − 3 ≥ 22?
  **A:** x ≥ 5.
- **Q:** −2x + 4 < 10?
  **A:** x > −3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Inequalities in context
*Student:* Solve a budget problem: earning $8 per hour with $40 saved, how many hours to have more than $150? Interpret the solution.

*How to teach:* Solve the budget problem together, discussing the meaning of a decimal answer. Check before moving on: ask "$8/hour with $40 saved, more than $150?" Look for: 40 + 8h > 150 → h > 13.75; she must work at least 14 whole hours.

- **Q:** $8/hour with $40 saved, more than $150?
  **A:** 40 + 8h > 150 → h > 13.75; she must work at least 14 whole hours.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing, solving and graphing inequalities.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "−x ≤ 4?" Look for: x ≥ −4.

- **Q:** −x ≤ 4?
  **A:** x ≥ −4.

**Assessment:** Inequality quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 'At least 18' → a ≥ 18, closed circle. 2) 3x + 2 < 14 → x < 4. 3) −4x ≥ 20 → x ≤ −5. 4) x/2 − 1 > 3 → x > 8. 5) Graph x > −2: open circle, shade right. 6) $5 per hour with $12, at least $60 → h ≥ 9.6 → 10 hours.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Rock layers and relative dating

**Prepare:** Colored play dough, plastic knife, clear jar with lid, sand, soil, gravel, water; Grand Canyon cross-section diagram (NPS); two cliff diagrams with index fossils.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the top layer is oldest. By superposition, in undisturbed layers the bottom is oldest.
- Thinking a fault or intrusion is older than the layers it cuts. Cross-cutting features are younger.

### Day 1 (Monday): Layers in a jar
*Student:* Pour sand, soil and gravel into a jar of water and observe how layers settle.

*How to teach:* Shake sand, soil and gravel in water and let settle. Your child sketches the layers. Check before moving on: ask "Which settles first?" Look for: Gravel (largest, heaviest), then sand, then fine soil/clay on top.

- **Q:** Which settles first?
  **A:** Gravel (largest, heaviest), then sand, then fine soil/clay on top.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Play-dough strata
*Student:* Build play-dough layers, add a 'fault' and an 'intrusion', and have a family member sequence the events.

*How to teach:* Build 4 play-dough layers, cut a fault, push in an 'intrusion'. A family member sequences events; your child checks. Check before moving on: ask "Order of events?" Look for: Layers deposited bottom to top, then fault and intrusion (whichever cuts the other is younger).

- **Q:** Order of events?
  **A:** Layers deposited bottom to top, then fault and intrusion (whichever cuts the other is younger).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grand Canyon
*Student:* Analyze a Grand Canyon cross-section and order the layers and events from oldest to youngest.

*How to teach:* Your child orders Grand Canyon layers and events. Check before moving on: ask "Oldest rocks at the bottom of the Grand Canyon?" Look for: Vishnu Schist (metamorphic, about 1.7 billion years old).

- **Q:** Oldest rocks at the bottom of the Grand Canyon?
  **A:** Vishnu Schist (metamorphic, about 1.7 billion years old).
- **Q:** Top layer?
  **A:** Kaibab Limestone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Index fossils
*Student:* Use index fossils to correlate layers between two cliff diagrams.

*How to teach:* Model correlating layers using an index fossil. Your child matches layers between two cliffs. Check before moving on: ask "What makes a good index fossil?" Look for: Widespread, lived a short time, easy to recognize.

- **Q:** What makes a good index fossil?
  **A:** Widespread, lived a short time, easy to recognize.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on relative dating principles.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Law of superposition?" Look for: In undisturbed layers, the oldest is at the bottom.

- **Q:** Law of superposition?
  **A:** In undisturbed layers, the oldest is at the bottom.

**Assessment:** Strata-sequencing quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Superposition. 2) Original horizontality (layers deposited flat). 3) Cross-cutting relationships (feature cutting layers is younger). 4) Index fossils correlate layers. 5) Sequence a diagram correctly.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Renaissance art, science and literature

**Prepare:** Smarthistory images; ruler, sketchbook; Dante or Machiavelli excerpt.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking perspective existed before the Renaissance. Linear perspective was developed by Brunelleschi.
- Thinking Machiavelli advocated evil. He described how rulers keep power.

### Day 1 (Monday): Linear perspective
*Student:* Learn one-point perspective and draw a hallway or street using a vanishing point.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate a horizon line and one vanishing point. Your child draws a hallway or street with lines converging to the point. Check before moving on: ask "What is a vanishing point?" Look for: The point where parallel lines appear to meet.

- **Q:** What is a vanishing point?
  **A:** The point where parallel lines appear to meet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Leonardo
*Student:* Study Leonardo's notebooks and anatomical sketches and design an invention page in his style.

*How to teach:* Show Leonardo's notebook pages. Your child designs an invention page with mirror-writing labels and sketches. Check before moving on: ask "Leonardo's interests?" Look for: Anatomy, flight, engineering, painting.

- **Q:** Leonardo's interests?
  **A:** Anatomy, flight, engineering, painting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Michelangelo and Raphael
*Student:* Analyze the Sistine Chapel and The School of Athens for classical and humanist themes.

*How to teach:* Show the Sistine Chapel ceiling and The School of Athens. Your child identifies classical and humanist themes in each. Check before moving on: ask "Who is at the center of The School of Athens?" Look for: Plato and Aristotle.

- **Q:** Who is at the center of The School of Athens?
  **A:** Plato and Aristotle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Literature
*Student:* Read an excerpt from Dante's Inferno or Machiavelli's The Prince and discuss its ideas.

*How to teach:* Read an excerpt from Dante's Inferno or The Prince together. Discuss its main ideas and whether they still apply. Check before moving on: ask "Machiavelli's famous idea?" Look for: It is safer for a ruler to be feared than loved if he cannot be both.

- **Q:** Machiavelli's famous idea?
  **A:** It is safer for a ruler to be feared than loved if he cannot be both.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a Renaissance 'museum card' for one artwork explaining its significance.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a museum card (title, artist, date, medium, significance) for one artwork. Check before moving on: ask "Museum card includes?" Look for: Title, artist, date, significance.

- **Q:** Museum card includes?
  **A:** Title, artist, date, significance.

**Assessment:** Perspective drawing and museum card.

**Answer key:** Perspective drawing: one vanishing point, converging lines. Museum card: accurate facts and significance.

## Spanish 1A: The verb tener and possessive adjectives

**Prepare:** Conjuguemos tener practice.

**Watch for:**
- Using ser for age. Spanish uses tener: Tengo doce años.
- Using 'su' only for 'his.' Su/sus can mean his, her, its, your (usted/ustedes) or their; context clarifies.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tener
*Student:* Learn the forms of tener (tengo, tienes...) and complete a Conjuguemos tener drill.

*How to teach:* Teach tener forms; Conjuguemos drill.

- **Q:** Conjugate tener for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** tengo; tenemos.
- **Q:** Complete: Mis primos ___ un perro.
  **A:** tienen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ages
*Student:* Ask and answer ¿Cuántos años tiene...? for 10 family members and friends.

*How to teach:* Ask and answer ¿Cuántos años tiene…? for 10 people.

- **Q:** Answer: ¿Cuántos años tiene tu abuelo? (70)
  **A:** Mi abuelo tiene setenta años.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Possessives
*Student:* Learn mi/tu/su/nuestro/vuestro and their plurals and complete a 20-item practice.

*How to teach:* Teach possessive adjectives; 20-item practice.

- **Q:** Translate: 'our cousins (girls)'.
  **A:** nuestras primas.
- **Q:** Translate: 'her brothers'.
  **A:** sus hermanos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversation
*Student:* Discuss your family with a parent: how many siblings, cousins, and pets, and who is older or younger.

*How to teach:* Discuss family: siblings, cousins, pets, older/younger (mayor/menor).

- **Q:** Say: 'I have a younger sister.'
  **A:** Tengo una hermana menor.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a tener and possessives quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the tener and possessives quiz.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Do you have pets?'
  **A:** ¿Tienes mascotas?

**Assessment:** Tener and possessives quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** Quiz key: tener — tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen. Possessives: mi/mis, tu/tus, su/sus, nuestro/a/os/as, vuestro/a/os/as, su/sus. Samples: (our) house → nuestra casa; (my) cousins → mis primos; (their) dog → su perro; (your, tú) sisters → tus hermanas; How old is your grandma? → ¿Cuántos años tiene tu abuela? 80% passing.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Script analysis and design

**Prepare:** Script of 'Pyramus and Thisbe' (A Midsummer Night's Dream, Act 5, Scene 1) or another public-domain one-act. Read it first. Household materials for design sketches.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking subtext is the same as the lines. Subtext is what the character really thinks or wants under the words.
- Assuming Shakespeare's plays used elaborate sets. Elizabethan public theatres had little scenery; words, costumes and props set the scene.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read the script
*Student:* Read a short one-act (e.g., the Pyramus and Thisbe scene) and summarize plot and characters.

*How to teach:* Read the scene aloud together; summarize plot and characters.

- **Q:** Why is the mechanicals' 'tragedy' funny?
  **A:** They are amateur actors who overact, explain everything literally (a man plays the Wall), forget lines and mangle words, turning a sad story into comedy.
- **Q:** Who plays Pyramus?
  **A:** Nick Bottom, the weaver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Playwright intent
*Student:* Research how the play was originally staged and discuss the playwright's intentions.

*How to teach:* Research original staging (Globe-era open-air theatre, daylight, male actors for all roles, little scenery).

- **Q:** Who played women's roles in Shakespeare's time?
  **A:** Boys or young men; women were not allowed on the public stage in England.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Character analysis
*Student:* Write a character profile with objectives and subtext for your role.

*How to teach:* Write a character profile with objective and subtext.

- **Q:** What is subtext?
  **A:** The unspoken thoughts and motives beneath a character's lines.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design plan
*Student:* Sketch set, costume and prop designs using household materials.

*How to teach:* Sketch set, costume and prop designs from household materials.

- **Q:** How could you show the 'Wall' character simply?
  **A:** An actor holding cardboard painted like bricks, with fingers making the chink.

### Day 5 (Friday): Patterns
*Student:* Compare comic patterns in this play with a sitcom episode.

*How to teach:* Compare comic patterns with a sitcom (misunderstanding, physical comedy, characters unaware they're funny).

- **Q:** Name one comic technique shared with sitcoms.
  **A:** Dramatic irony, slapstick physical comedy, exaggeration, a 'show within a show,' running gags.

**Assessment:** Character profile and design sketches.

**Answer key:** Character profile rubric: name, role, relationships, traits with line evidence, objective, obstacles, subtext for 2 lines, voice/movement choices. Design sketches: set, costume and prop sketches labeled with materials and reasons. Key facts for Pyramus and Thisbe: performed by the 'rude mechanicals' for Theseus's wedding; Bottom plays Pyramus, Flute plays Thisbe, Snout plays Wall, Starveling plays Moonshine, Snug plays Lion, Quince speaks the Prologue; the lovers talk through a chink in a Wall, plan to meet at Ninus' tomb, Thisbe flees a Lion leaving her bloody mantle, Pyramus thinks she's dead and kills himself, Thisbe finds him and kills herself — a tragic story performed so badly it's comic.

## Physical Education 7: Line dances and rhythm

**Prepare:** Music player, three songs of different tempos, videos of the Cupid Shuffle and Electric Slide. Clear space.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing ahead of the music. Count '5, 6, 7, 8' before starting and step on the beat.
- Treating dance as not 'real' exercise. Check heart rate — dancing can reach the target zone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Counting music
*Student:* Clap and step to 8-counts in three songs of different tempos.

*How to teach:* Clap and step to the beat of three songs; count in 8-counts and identify the tempo (slow, medium, fast).

- **Q:** What is tempo?
  **A:** The speed of the music's beat.
- **Q:** What is an 8-count?
  **A:** A phrase of 8 beats dancers count to structure movements.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Basic steps
*Student:* Learn grapevine, step-touch, pivot turn and box step.

*How to teach:* Teach grapevine (side, behind, side, touch), step-touch, pivot turn and box step.

- **Q:** Describe a grapevine to the right.
  **A:** Step right, cross left behind, step right, touch (or tap) left beside it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Line dance 1
*Student:* Learn and perform the 'Cupid Shuffle' or 'Electric Slide'.

*How to teach:* Learn the Cupid Shuffle (right 4 steps, left 4, kick 4 counts, 'walk it out' turning a quarter turn left) or the Electric Slide.

- **Q:** In the Cupid Shuffle, which way do you turn after 'walk it by yourself'?
  **A:** A quarter turn to the left to face a new wall.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Line dance 2
*Student:* Learn and perform a second line dance and check heart rate afterward.

*How to teach:* Learn a second line dance; take pulse afterward.

- **Q:** Your pulse is 32 in 15 seconds after dancing. What is your heart rate, and is it in a 60–85% zone for a 12-year-old?
  **A:** 128 bpm — yes, within about 125–177 bpm.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a line dance for family without the video.

*How to teach:* Perform for family without the video.

- **Q:** What is a 'wall' in line dancing?
  **A:** The direction dancers face; many line dances rotate to a new wall each sequence.

**Assessment:** Line dance performance.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric (1–4): timing (on the beat), accuracy of steps and sequence, transitions/turns, energy and expression. Proficient = completes the full dance from memory with minor errors and stays with the music.

## Health Education 7: Adolescent changes

**Prepare:** Parent preview: read KidsHealth's puberty articles ('Everything you wanted to know about puberty') first and decide what to discuss. This is a parent-led conversation; keep it private and respectful. Journal.

**Watch for:**
- Believing everyone goes through puberty at the same time. It can start anywhere from about 8 to 14 and each person's timing is normal for them.
- Thinking mood swings mean something is wrong. Hormone changes and brain development cause stronger emotions; coping skills help.

### Day 1 (Monday): Physical changes
*Student:* Read about physical changes of puberty and discuss with a parent.

*How to teach:* Read about physical changes (growth spurt, body hair, skin changes, voice changes, body shape) and discuss with your child.

- **Q:** What causes the changes of puberty?
  **A:** Hormones released by the pituitary gland and the ovaries or testes.
- **Q:** What is a growth spurt?
  **A:** A period of rapid growth in height and weight during puberty.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Emotional changes
*Student:* List emotional changes like mood swings and strategies to manage them.

*How to teach:* Your child lists emotional changes and a strategy for each.

- **Q:** Give one healthy strategy for mood swings.
  **A:** Get enough sleep, exercise, journal, talk to someone, take a break before responding.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Social changes
*Student:* Discuss changing friendships and independence.

*How to teach:* Discuss friendships changing and wanting more independence; agree on ways to earn trust.

- **Q:** How can a teen earn more independence?
  **A:** Show responsibility: keep promises, do chores and schoolwork, communicate plans honestly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Timing
*Student:* Learn that puberty timing varies and why comparisons aren't helpful.

*How to teach:* Teach that timing varies widely; comparing causes worry.

- **Q:** Why aren't comparisons helpful during puberty?
  **A:** Everyone's body follows its own schedule; comparing creates worry about something normal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Journal on one change and a healthy way to handle it.

*How to teach:* Your child journals about one change; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Who can you ask questions about puberty?
  **A:** Parents, a doctor or nurse, a trusted relative, a school counselor, and valid sites like KidsHealth.

**Assessment:** Journal.

**Answer key:** Journal rubric: 4 = describes one change accurately, explains how it feels or affects daily life, names a healthy way to handle it (talking to a trusted adult, hygiene routine, sleep, exercise, coping strategy) and a resource; 3 = change plus strategy; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
