# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 23

## English Language Arts 7: Research questions and sources

**Prepare:** Library card for public library databases (e.g., Britannica School, Gale); CRAAP test checklist; Purdue OWL MLA citation page.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a topic too broad ('climate change'). Narrow to a question: 'How do sea walls protect California's coast?'
- Trusting a site because it looks professional. Check author, date and purpose.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a topic
*Student:* Brainstorm topics on an invention, scientist or environmental issue and narrow to one focused research question.

*How to teach:* Your child brainstorms topics on an invention, scientist or environmental issue and narrows to one focused research question. Check that it is open-ended and researchable in about four weeks. Check before moving on: ask "Narrow 'Thomas Edison'." Look for: 'How did Edison's research lab change the way inventions were made?'

- **Q:** Narrow 'Thomas Edison'.
  **A:** 'How did Edison's research lab change the way inventions were made?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluating sources
*Student:* Apply the CRAAP test (currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose) to five websites on the topic.

*How to teach:* Explain each CRAAP letter with an example. Your child scores five websites on a checklist and explains which are trustworthy. Check before moving on: ask "What does each letter mean?" Look for: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.

- **Q:** What does each letter mean?
  **A:** Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Library databases
*Student:* Search the public library's student databases and find three credible sources.

*How to teach:* Search the library's student databases together. Your child finds three credible sources and notes why each is credible. Check before moving on: ask "Why are databases more reliable than random sites?" Look for: Their articles are edited/reviewed and from known publishers.

- **Q:** Why are databases more reliable than random sites?
  **A:** Their articles are edited/reviewed and from known publishers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Working bibliography
*Student:* Create MLA citations for at least four sources using Purdue OWL.

*How to teach:* Model one MLA citation using Purdue OWL. Your child creates at least four citations in alphabetical order with a hanging indent. Check before moving on: ask "MLA order for a website?" Look for: Author. 'Title.' Site Name, Publisher (if different), Date, URL.

- **Q:** MLA order for a website?
  **A:** Author. 'Title.' Site Name, Publisher (if different), Date, URL.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit research question, source evaluations and working bibliography for feedback.

*How to teach:* Your child submits the research question, source evaluations and bibliography. Give feedback on focus and source quality. Check before moving on: ask "Good research question test?" Look for: Open-ended, researchable, focused enough for 1,000 words.

- **Q:** Good research question test?
  **A:** Open-ended, researchable, focused enough for 1,000 words.

**Assessment:** Research proposal and bibliography.

**Answer key:** Proposal: focused question; CRAAP scores for 5 sites with reasons; 3 credible sources. Bibliography: 4 MLA citations, alphabetized, hanging indent, all required elements.

## Mathematics 7: Tape diagrams and equations

**Prepare:** Grid paper; draw hanger diagrams (a balanced mobile with shapes).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing p(x + q) with px + q. In p(x + q), each of p groups contains x + q.
- Removing from only one side of a hanger. Whatever you remove from one side, remove from the other to stay balanced.

### Day 1 (Monday): Relationships and diagrams
*Student:* Match 6 stories to tape diagrams showing px + q = r or p(x + q) = r.

*How to teach:* Show tape diagrams: 3 equal parts of x plus a piece 5 = total 20 (3x + 5 = 20) vs 3 equal parts each (x + 5) = 20. Your child matches 6 stories. Check before moving on: ask "Diagram: 4 boxes each 'x + 2', total 28. Equation?" Look for: 4(x + 2) = 28.

- **Q:** Diagram: 4 boxes each 'x + 2', total 28. Equation?
  **A:** 4(x + 2) = 28.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Writing equations
*Student:* Write equations for situations such as 4 friends each buying a $2.50 snack plus a shared $6 pizza.

*How to teach:* Model the snack problem. Your child writes equations for several situations. Check before moving on: ask "4 friends each buy a $2.50 snack and share a $6 pizza. Total cost?" Look for: 4(2.50) + 6 = $16.

- **Q:** 4 friends each buy a $2.50 snack and share a $6 pizza. Total cost?
  **A:** 4(2.50) + 6 = $16.
- **Q:** If the total is t and snack cost is s: equation?
  **A:** 4s + 6 = t.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hanger diagrams
*Student:* Use balanced hanger diagrams to reason about solving 3x + 2 = 11.

*How to teach:* Draw a hanger: 3 triangles and 2 circles (each circle = 1) on the left, 11 circles on the right. Your child removes 2 from each side, then splits into 3. Check before moving on: ask "Solve 3x + 2 = 11." Look for: x = 3.

- **Q:** Solve 3x + 2 = 11.
  **A:** x = 3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Solving with balance
*Student:* Solve 10 equations by doing the same to each side and checking solutions.

*How to teach:* Model 'same to both sides' with written steps. Your child solves 10 and checks each. Check before moving on: ask "5x − 4 = 21?" Look for: x = 5.

- **Q:** 5x − 4 = 21?
  **A:** x = 5.
- **Q:** 4(x + 1) = 24?
  **A:** x = 5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing equations from diagrams and stories.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Equation for 'twice a number plus 7 is 19'?" Look for: 2n + 7 = 19; n = 6.

- **Q:** Equation for 'twice a number plus 7 is 19'?
  **A:** 2n + 7 = 19; n = 6.

**Assessment:** Quiz on tape diagrams and equation writing.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Diagram 5 parts of x and 3 = 23 → 5x + 3 = 23, x = 4. 2) 2(x + 6) = 20, x = 4. 3) Story: 3 tickets plus $4 parking is $31 → 3t + 4 = 31, t = 9. 4) 6x + 1 = 25, x = 4. 5) Match diagram to 3(x − 2) = 12, x = 6.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Earth's resources: minerals, energy, groundwater

**Prepare:** Clear cup, gravel, sand, clay, water, food coloring, sponge; short readings on California gold and Central Valley oil; USGS mineral resource map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking groundwater is an underground lake. It fills spaces between sediment grains and cracks in rock.
- Thinking resources are spread evenly. They form only where specific geologic processes happened.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gold Rush geology
*Student:* Read about how California gold formed in quartz veins and concentrated in streams, then explain the geologic processes.

*How to teach:* Read about California gold. Your child explains how gold formed and concentrated. Check before moving on: ask "How did gold form?" Look for: Hot fluids from magma deposited gold in quartz veins in the Sierra foothills.

- **Q:** How did gold form?
  **A:** Hot fluids from magma deposited gold in quartz veins in the Sierra foothills.
- **Q:** Why in streams?
  **A:** Weathering freed gold; since it's very dense, it settled in stream gravels (placer deposits).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Aquifer model
*Student:* Build an aquifer in a cup with gravel, sand and clay layers and add colored 'pollution' to see it spread.

*How to teach:* Layer gravel, sand, clay in a cup; add water and colored 'pollution'. Your child observes spreading. Check before moving on: ask "Why does pollution spread?" Look for: Water moves through connected pores in sand and gravel, carrying pollution.

- **Q:** Why does pollution spread?
  **A:** Water moves through connected pores in sand and gravel, carrying pollution.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Oil and gas
*Student:* Read about how oil formed in California's Central Valley and map where fossil fuels are found.

*How to teach:* Read about oil in the Central Valley. Your child maps fossil fuel locations. Check before moving on: ask "How did oil form?" Look for: Tiny marine organisms buried in sediment were heated and pressurized over millions of years.

- **Q:** How did oil form?
  **A:** Tiny marine organisms buried in sediment were heated and pressurized over millions of years.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Resource map
*Student:* Analyze a USGS map of mineral and groundwater resources and explain uneven distribution.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes the USGS map. Check before moving on: ask "Why are resources unevenly distributed?" Look for: They form only where specific past geologic conditions occurred.

- **Q:** Why are resources unevenly distributed?
  **A:** They form only where specific past geologic conditions occurred.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on resource formation and distribution.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "What is an aquifer?" Look for: Underground layer of permeable rock/sediment holding groundwater.

- **Q:** What is an aquifer?
  **A:** Underground layer of permeable rock/sediment holding groundwater.

**Assessment:** Aquifer model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Aquifer definition. 2) Gold in quartz veins, concentrated as placer. 3) Oil from buried marine organisms. 4) Uneven distribution due to geology. 5) Clay is less permeable.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Spanish conquest

**Prepare:** Excerpt of Cortés's second letter; Broken Spears excerpt; 6 documents for DBQ.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a few hundred Spaniards defeated the Aztec alone. Thousands of Indigenous allies (Tlaxcalans) joined.
- Thinking guns were the main factor. Disease and allies mattered more.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cortés
*Student:* Read an excerpt of Cortés's letter to Charles V describing Tenochtitlan.

*How to teach:* Read Cortés's letter. Check before moving on: ask "How does Cortés describe Tenochtitlan?" Look for: As a magnificent city with a huge market, compared to Spanish cities.

- **Q:** How does Cortés describe Tenochtitlan?
  **A:** As a magnificent city with a huge market, compared to Spanish cities.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Aztec perspective
*Student:* Read an Aztec account from the Florentine Codex (Broken Spears) and compare points of view.

*How to teach:* Read the Aztec account; compare. Check before moving on: ask "Aztec perspective?" Look for: Grief, devastation, Spanish greed for gold.

- **Q:** Aztec perspective?
  **A:** Grief, devastation, Spanish greed for gold.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pizarro and the Inca
*Student:* Explain how civil war, disease and steel helped Pizarro defeat Atahualpa.

*How to teach:* Explain Pizarro's victory. Check before moving on: ask "Factors?" Look for: Inca civil war (Atahualpa vs Huáscar), smallpox, steel weapons, horses, capturing Atahualpa at Cajamarca 1532.

- **Q:** Factors?
  **A:** Inca civil war (Atahualpa vs Huáscar), smallpox, steel weapons, horses, capturing Atahualpa at Cajamarca 1532.

### Day 4 (Thursday): DBQ
*Student:* Analyze 6 documents to answer 'How were so few Spanish able to conquer so many?'

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes 6 documents, noting each one's author, date and the cause it supports, then groups them into categories. Check before moving on: ask "Common causes?" Look for: Disease, allies, technology, internal divisions.

- **Q:** Common causes?
  **A:** Disease, allies, technology, internal divisions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Write the DBQ essay and take a short unit 7 test.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the DBQ essay, then takes the short unit 7 test. Check before moving on: ask "When did Tenochtitlan fall?" Look for: 1521.

- **Q:** When did Tenochtitlan fall?
  **A:** 1521.

**Assessment:** DBQ essay and unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** DBQ rubric: thesis with 3 causes, evidence from 4+ documents, explanation, sourcing. Unit 7 test: Maya, Aztec, Inca, conquest.

## Spanish 1A: Unit 6 assessment: a restaurant menu

**Prepare:** Paper or slides for a menu; play money in pesos or euros.

**Watch for:**
- Ordering with 'Quiero' only. 'Quisiera…' (I would like) is more polite.
- Forgetting agreement in menu descriptions: 'una ensalada fresca', 'unos tacos deliciosos'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Menu design
*Student:* Design a bilingual café menu with 12 items and prices in pesos or euros.

*How to teach:* Design a 12-item bilingual menu with prices.

- **Q:** Say '35 pesos'.
  **A:** treinta y cinco pesos.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Descriptions
*Student:* Write a one-sentence Spanish description for each menu item using adjectives.

*How to teach:* Write a description for each item.

- **Q:** Write: 'Delicious chicken tacos.'
  **A:** Tacos de pollo deliciosos.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ordering chunks
*Student:* Learn the chunks ¿Qué quieres comer? and Quisiera... and practice ordering from your menu.

*How to teach:* Learn ¿Qué quieres comer? / Quisiera… / ¿Cuánto cuesta? / La cuenta, por favor.

- **Q:** Order a sandwich and water politely.
  **A:** Quisiera un sándwich y un agua, por favor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Role-play
*Student:* Run a café role-play with a family member as customer, using numbers for prices.

*How to teach:* Run the café role-play.

- **Q:** Ask for the check.
  **A:** La cuenta, por favor.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the unit 6 test: food listening, -er/-ir verbs, and a short writing on your eating habits.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 6 test.

- **Q:** Write: 'We always drink water at lunch.'
  **A:** Siempre bebemos agua en el almuerzo.

**Assessment:** Menu project rubric and unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Menu project rubric (1–4): 12 items in categories, prices, a Spanish description per item with correct adjective agreement, attractive layout; role-play uses ¿Qué quieres comer/beber?, Quisiera…, ¿Cuánto cuesta?, La cuenta, por favor, numbers for prices. Unit 6 test: food listening, -er/-ir conjugations, short writing on eating habits (e.g., 'En el desayuno como cereal y bebo leche. Nunca como…').

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Theatre games and improvisation

**Prepare:** Open space, prompt cards (characters, settings, objectives). Know Magna Carta context (1215, King John and barons at Runnymede).

**Watch for:**
- Blocking a scene partner's idea ('No, that's not a dragon'). In improv, accept offers and build: 'Yes, and…'
- Thinking a tableau is just standing still. Use levels, facial expressions and focus to tell a story.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm-ups
*Student:* Do vocal and physical warm-ups (tongue twisters, mirror exercise).

*How to teach:* Warm up: tongue twisters ('Red leather, yellow leather'), stretches, mirror exercise.

- **Q:** Why do actors warm up?
  **A:** To prepare voice and body, improve diction and focus, and build ensemble trust.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yes, and
*Student:* Play 'Yes, and' story-building improv with a family member.

*How to teach:* Play 'Yes, and' story building, one sentence each.

- **Q:** What does 'Yes, and' mean in improv?
  **A:** Accept your partner's idea and add to it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tableaux
*Student:* Create frozen tableaux for scenes from history (e.g., signing Magna Carta).

*How to teach:* Create frozen tableaux of historical moments like King John sealing Magna Carta.

- **Q:** What is a tableau?
  **A:** A frozen stage picture showing a moment with bodies, levels and expressions.
- **Q:** What happened at the Magna Carta in 1215?
  **A:** English barons forced King John to agree to a charter limiting royal power at Runnymede.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Objectives
*Student:* Improvise short scenes where each character has a clear objective and obstacle.

*How to teach:* Improvise scenes where each character has an objective and an obstacle.

- **Q:** What is an objective?
  **A:** What a character wants in the scene.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Journal on which game built confidence and why.

*How to teach:* Journal on which game built confidence.

- **Q:** What is an ensemble?
  **A:** A group of performers working together as a team.

**Assessment:** Participation checklist and journal.

**Answer key:** Participation checklist (yes/no): warms up fully, accepts and builds on offers, makes clear choices, uses levels in tableaux, plays a clear objective, supports partners. Journal proficient: names a game, describes a specific moment, explains how it built confidence.

## Physical Education 7: Softball: game strategy

**Prepare:** Softball equipment, four bases (60 ft apart for full size, shorter for kids), a group for games.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every runner must be tagged. In a force out, the fielder only needs to touch the base before the runner arrives.
- Leaving the base early on a fly ball. If the fly is caught, the runner must tag up (return to the base) before advancing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Base running
*Student:* Practice running through first and rounding bases.

*How to teach:* Practice running through first base (don't slow down) and rounding bases (curve out, touch inside corner).

- **Q:** Why run through first base instead of stopping on it?
  **A:** You may overrun first base without being tagged out, so running through is faster.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fielding decisions
*Student:* Practice where to throw in force-out situations.

*How to teach:* Practice situations: runner on first, ground ball → throw to second for a force out (or first for the sure out).

- **Q:** With a runner on first and a ground ball to shortstop, where is the force play?
  **A:** Second base (and first base for the batter).
- **Q:** What is a force out?
  **A:** When a runner must advance because the batter became a runner, the fielder can get them out by touching the base before they arrive.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rules
*Student:* Learn basic rules and play a modified game of 3-pitch softball.

*How to teach:* Teach basic rules (3 outs per half-inning, 3 strikes, fair/foul) and play 3-pitch softball (own team pitches, 3 pitches to hit).

- **Q:** How many outs end a half-inning?
  **A:** Three.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play a full game with a homeschool group or family.

*How to teach:* Play a full game.

- **Q:** What does 'tag up' mean?
  **A:** After a fly ball is caught, a runner must touch their original base before advancing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write about one strategic decision you made in the game.

*How to teach:* Your child writes about one strategic decision.

- **Q:** What is the cutoff player's job?
  **A:** To catch a long throw from the outfield and relay it quickly to the right base.

**Assessment:** Game play and reflection.

**Answer key:** Game play: runs through first base, rounds bases correctly, makes correct force-out throw choices, knows when to tag up. Reflection rubric: 4 = describes the situation (outs, runners), the decision made and why, and evaluates the outcome; 3 = situation and decision; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.

## Health Education 7: Refusal skills

**Prepare:** Write scenario cards (vape offer at the park, drinks at a party, pressure from an older cousin). Agree on a family code word/text in advance.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you need a long explanation to say no. A clear 'No thanks' repeated calmly is enough.
- Worrying that refusing will lose all your friends. Real friends respect a no; suggest an alternative or leave.

### Day 1 (Monday): Refusal steps
*Student:* Learn refusal steps: say no, give a reason, suggest an alternative, leave.

*How to teach:* Teach the four steps. Model several 'no' phrases and reasons ('I have a game tomorrow,' 'My parents would kill me,' 'Not my thing').

- **Q:** What are the steps of refusal?
  **A:** Say no clearly, give a reason, suggest an alternative, leave if needed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice 1
*Student:* Role-play refusing a vape offer from a friend.

*How to teach:* Role-play: friend offers a vape; your child refuses using all steps. Add pressure in round 2.

- **Q:** Your friend says 'Come on, just one hit.' Give a strong response.
  **A:** 'No thanks, I'm not into that. Let's go shoot hoops instead.' Then walk away if they keep pushing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice 2
*Student:* Role-play refusing alcohol at a party.

*How to teach:* Role-play alcohol at a party, including how to leave safely.

- **Q:** Give one exit strategy at a party.
  **A:** Text a parent the code word, say you have to go home, stay with a friend who doesn't drink, leave together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Exit plans
*Student:* Create a family code word for getting picked up.

*How to teach:* Create a family code word/text (e.g., 'X') that means 'come get me, no questions now.'

- **Q:** Why does a code word help?
  **A:** You can get out of an uncomfortable situation without losing face in front of others.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform a refusal-skills assessment scenario.

*How to teach:* Run an unrehearsed scenario and score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Describe assertive body language.
  **A:** Stand tall, eye contact, calm firm voice, matching facial expression.

**Assessment:** Refusal skills rubric.

**Answer key:** Refusal skills rubric: 4 = clear 'No' with assertive body language (eye contact, firm voice), gives a reason, suggests an alternative, repeats if pressured, leaves if needed; 3 = says no and gives reason but no alternative or exit; 2 = hesitant or vague; 1 = gives in.
