# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 20

## English Language Arts 7: Acts 2–3: the forest and the mix-ups

**Prepare:** A Midsummer Night's Dream; love chart (arrows for who loves whom).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking dramatic irony means sarcasm. It is when the audience knows something characters don't.
- Thinking Puck enchants the wrong man on purpose. He mistakes Lysander for Demetrius because of the Athenian clothes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Act 2
*Student:* Read Act 2 and explain the conflict between Oberon and Titania and Puck's role.

*How to teach:* Read Act 2 in parts. Your child explains the Oberon–Titania quarrel and Puck's role in carrying out Oberon's plan. Ask what goes wrong with Puck's mission. Check before moving on: ask "Why do Oberon and Titania quarrel?" Look for: Over a changeling Indian boy Titania refuses to give Oberon.

- **Q:** Why do Oberon and Titania quarrel?
  **A:** Over a changeling Indian boy Titania refuses to give Oberon.
- **Q:** What does Oberon send Puck to get?
  **A:** The flower 'love-in-idleness', whose juice makes a sleeper love the first creature seen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dramatic irony
*Student:* List moments when the audience knows more than the lovers and explain how this creates comedy.

*How to teach:* Define dramatic irony with an example. Your child lists at least three moments when the audience knows more than the lovers and explains how each creates comedy. Check before moving on: ask "Example?" Look for: The audience knows Lysander loves Helena only because of the potion; Hermia does not.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** The audience knows Lysander loves Helena only because of the potion; Hermia does not.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Act 3
*Student:* Read Act 3 and track who loves whom on a 'love chart' before and after the potion.

*How to teach:* Read Act 3 in parts. Your child makes two love charts with arrows: before the potion and after Act 3.2. Discuss why the lovers' quarrel becomes so heated. Check before moving on: ask "After the potion in 3.2, whom do Lysander and Demetrius love?" Look for: Both love Helena.

- **Q:** After the potion in 3.2, whom do Lysander and Demetrius love?
  **A:** Both love Helena.
- **Q:** Whom does Titania love?
  **A:** Bottom, who has a donkey's head.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Soliloquy
*Student:* Analyze Helena's soliloquy at the end of Act 1 and what it reveals that dialogue does not.

*How to teach:* Reread Helena's soliloquy at the end of 1.1. Your child explains what a soliloquy is and what Helena reveals that she wouldn't say in dialogue, citing two lines. Check before moving on: ask "What does she reveal?" Look for: She is jealous and plans to tell Demetrius of the elopement to win his favor; 'Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind.'

- **Q:** What does she reveal?
  **A:** She is jealous and plans to tell Demetrius of the elopement to win his favor; 'Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an Acts 2–3 quiz with a constructed response on dramatic irony.

*How to teach:* Give the Acts 2–3 quiz with the constructed response on dramatic irony. Review together. Check before moving on: ask "Famous Puck line in 3.2?" Look for: 'Lord, what fools these mortals be!'

- **Q:** Famous Puck line in 3.2?
  **A:** 'Lord, what fools these mortals be!'

**Assessment:** Love chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Changeling boy. 2) Love-in-idleness. 3) Puck anoints Lysander by mistake. 4) Puck gives Bottom a donkey head. 5) Titania loves Bottom. CR (dramatic irony): proficient names one instance, explains what the audience knows that characters don't, and how it creates comedy.

## Mathematics 7: Dividing rational numbers and decimals

**Prepare:** Calculator, paper for long division.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 0 ÷ 5 is undefined. 0 ÷ 5 = 0; 5 ÷ 0 is undefined.
- Stopping long division too soon and missing the repeat. Keep dividing until the remainder repeats.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dividing signed numbers
*Student:* Use multiplication facts to determine sign rules for division and solve 15 division problems.

*How to teach:* Use 'if (−4)(−9) = 36, then 36 ÷ (−4) = −9'. Your child states sign rules and solves 15. Check before moving on: ask "−36 ÷ −4?" Look for: 9.

- **Q:** −36 ÷ −4?
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** 45 ÷ (−9)?
  **A:** −5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dividing by zero
*Student:* Explore why division by zero is undefined using related multiplication sentences and write an explanation.

*How to teach:* Ask: 5 ÷ 0 = ? means ? × 0 = 5. Your child writes why no number works. Check before moving on: ask "Why is 7 ÷ 0 undefined?" Look for: No number times 0 equals 7, since anything × 0 = 0.

- **Q:** Why is 7 ÷ 0 undefined?
  **A:** No number times 0 equals 7, since anything × 0 = 0.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Long division to decimals
*Student:* Convert 1/8, 3/11, 5/6 and 7/16 to decimals by long division and mark repeating parts with bar notation.

*How to teach:* Model 3 ÷ 11 by long division. Your child converts the four fractions and marks repeating parts. Check before moving on: ask "1/8?" Look for: 0.125.

- **Q:** 1/8?
  **A:** 0.125.
- **Q:** 3/11?
  **A:** 0.272727… = 0.27 with a bar over 27.
- **Q:** 5/6?
  **A:** 0.8333… = 0.83 with a bar over the 3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Which fractions terminate?
*Student:* Investigate which denominators give terminating decimals and state a rule based on factors of 2 and 5.

*How to teach:* Your child tests denominators 2–20, factoring each, and states the rule. Check before moving on: ask "Will 7/16 terminate?" Look for: Yes — 16 = 2⁴; 7/16 = 0.4375.

- **Q:** Will 7/16 terminate?
  **A:** Yes — 16 = 2⁴; 7/16 = 0.4375.
- **Q:** Will 2/15 terminate?
  **A:** No — 15 has a factor of 3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on division and fraction-to-decimal conversion.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "−4.8 ÷ 0.6?" Look for: −8.

- **Q:** −4.8 ÷ 0.6?
  **A:** −8.

**Assessment:** Division and decimal conversion quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) −56 ÷ 7 = −8. 2) −1.2 ÷ −0.3 = 4. 3) (−3/4) ÷ (1/2) = −3/2. 4) 4/9 = 0.4 repeating. 5) 3/8 = 0.375. 6) 1/12 = 0.083 repeating (bar over 3). 7) Terminates: 7/20 yes, 1/6 no.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Minerals and rocks

**Prepare:** Rock samples from yard or a store rock kit, steel nail, copper penny, unglazed tile (back of a bathroom tile), glass plate (adult), vinegar, dropper; salt or sugar, jar, string, pencil.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking minerals and rocks are the same. A mineral is a naturally formed, inorganic solid with a definite composition and crystal structure; rocks are made of one or more minerals.
- Thinking streak color always equals the sample's color. Pyrite looks gold but leaves a greenish-black streak.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mineral properties
*Student:* Test samples for luster, streak and hardness using a fingernail, penny and nail on the Mohs scale.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate luster, streak and the scratch test. Your child tests each sample: fingernail (~2.5), penny (~3.5), nail (~5.5), records hardness range. Check before moving on: ask "A mineral is scratched by a nail but not by a penny. Hardness?" Look for: Between about 3.5 and 5.5.

- **Q:** A mineral is scratched by a nail but not by a penny. Hardness?
  **A:** Between about 3.5 and 5.5.
- **Q:** What is streak?
  **A:** The color of a mineral's powder when rubbed on unglazed tile.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Grow crystals
*Student:* Grow salt or sugar crystals from a supersaturated solution and connect to how minerals form.

*How to teach:* Dissolve salt (or sugar) in hot water until no more dissolves (adult heats), hang a string, and let it sit. Your child records crystal growth daily. Check before moving on: ask "How does this model mineral formation?" Look for: As water evaporates or cools, dissolved minerals come out of solution and form crystals.

- **Q:** How does this model mineral formation?
  **A:** As water evaporates or cools, dissolved minerals come out of solution and form crystals.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rock types
*Student:* Classify yard and store-bought rocks into igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic with an ID key.

*How to teach:* Teach the three rock types. Your child classifies samples with an ID key. Check before moving on: ask "Granite?" Look for: Igneous (intrusive, cooled slowly underground — big crystals).

- **Q:** Granite?
  **A:** Igneous (intrusive, cooled slowly underground — big crystals).
- **Q:** Sandstone?
  **A:** Sedimentary.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fizz test
*Student:* Test rocks with vinegar to identify limestone/calcite and explain the chemical reaction.

*How to teach:* Drop vinegar on each rock. Your child identifies fizzers and explains. Check before moving on: ask "What does fizzing show?" Look for: Calcium carbonate (calcite/limestone) reacting with acid to make CO2 gas.

- **Q:** What does fizzing show?
  **A:** Calcium carbonate (calcite/limestone) reacting with acid to make CO2 gas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mineral properties and rock types.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Marble — rock type?" Look for: Metamorphic (from limestone).

- **Q:** Marble — rock type?
  **A:** Metamorphic (from limestone).

**Assessment:** Rock and mineral ID chart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Mineral definition. 2) Mohs hardness: talc 1, diamond 10. 3) Streak test. 4) Igneous = cooled magma/lava; sedimentary = compacted sediments; metamorphic = changed by heat and pressure. 5) Limestone fizzes. ID chart: each sample with luster, streak, hardness, type.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Black Death and the Church's influence

**Prepare:** Plague spread map; Boccaccio's Decameron introduction excerpt; population data; images of Gothic cathedrals. Preview: Boccaccio describes death vividly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking people knew germs caused the plague. They blamed bad air, God's punishment, or scapegoated Jews.
- Thinking the plague had only negative effects. Labor shortages raised wages and weakened serfdom.

### Day 1 (Monday): Spread of the plague
*Student:* Map the spread of the Black Death from Central Asia along trade routes to Europe, 1330s–1350s.

*How to teach:* Your child maps the plague's spread from Central Asia to Caffa to Sicily (1347) across Europe. Check before moving on: ask "How did it spread?" Look for: Along trade routes by fleas on rats and through people.

- **Q:** How did it spread?
  **A:** Along trade routes by fleas on rats and through people.
- **Q:** What fraction of Europe died?
  **A:** About one-third to one-half.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eyewitness
*Student:* Read Boccaccio's description of plague in Florence and list social effects.

*How to teach:* Read Boccaccio. Your child lists social effects. Check before moving on: ask "Effects?" Look for: People abandoned sick family members, mass graves, some fled or partied, others isolated themselves.

- **Q:** Effects?
  **A:** People abandoned sick family members, mass graves, some fled or partied, others isolated themselves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Consequences
*Student:* Analyze population data and explain how the plague weakened feudalism.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how the plague weakened feudalism. Check before moving on: ask "Why?" Look for: Fewer workers meant peasants could demand higher wages or leave manors.

- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** Fewer workers meant peasants could demand higher wages or leave manors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Universities and cathedrals
*Student:* Read about Thomas Aquinas and Gothic cathedrals and explain how the Church shaped learning and art.

*How to teach:* Read about Aquinas and Gothic cathedrals. Check before moving on: ask "Aquinas's goal?" Look for: Show faith and reason (Aristotle) work together.

- **Q:** Aquinas's goal?
  **A:** Show faith and reason (Aristotle) work together.
- **Q:** Gothic features?
  **A:** Pointed arches, flying buttresses, stained glass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 6 test.

*How to teach:* Give the unit 6 test. Check before moving on: ask "Effect of Magna Carta?" Look for: Limited the king's power.

- **Q:** Effect of Magna Carta?
  **A:** Limited the king's power.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test: geography, monasteries, Charlemagne, feudalism, manors, guilds, investiture, Magna Carta, Crusades, Reconquista, Black Death, Aquinas, Gothic architecture.

## Spanish 1A: Present tense of -er and -ir verbs

**Prepare:** Verb chart, Conjuguemos -er/-ir practice.

**Watch for:**
- Using -amos with -er/-ir verbs. -er → -emos (comemos); -ir → -imos (vivimos).
- Mixing up siempre/nunca placement. 'Nunca como…' or 'No como nunca…'

### Day 1 (Monday): -er verbs
*Student:* Learn the -er endings and conjugate comer, beber, and leer in a chart.

*How to teach:* Teach -er endings; chart comer, beber, leer.

- **Q:** Conjugate beber for tú and ellos.
  **A:** bebes; beben.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): -ir verbs
*Student:* Learn the -ir endings, noting nosotros -imos, and conjugate vivir, escribir, compartir.

*How to teach:* Teach -ir endings (same as -er except nosotros -imos, vosotros -ís); chart vivir, escribir, compartir.

- **Q:** Conjugate escribir for nosotros.
  **A:** escribimos.
- **Q:** Which forms differ between -er and -ir verbs?
  **A:** Nosotros (-emos/-imos) and vosotros (-éis/-ís).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Drill
*Student:* Complete Conjuguemos -er/-ir practice to 90%, then write 10 sentences with frequency words (siempre, a veces, nunca).

*How to teach:* Drill to 90%; write 10 sentences with siempre, a veces, nunca.

- **Q:** Write: 'I never drink coffee.'
  **A:** Nunca bebo café.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a parent about their eating habits with eight questions using -er/-ir verbs.

*How to teach:* Interview a parent: eight questions with -er/-ir verbs.

- **Q:** Ask: 'What do you eat for lunch?'
  **A:** ¿Qué comes en el almuerzo?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take an -ar/-er/-ir conjugation quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the -ar/-er/-ir quiz.

- **Q:** Conjugate leer for nosotros and compartir for ella.
  **A:** leemos; comparte.

**Assessment:** Regular present-tense quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** Quiz key: comer — como, comes, come, comemos, coméis, comen; vivir — vivo, vives, vive, vivimos, vivís, viven. Sample items: Yo (beber) → bebo; Nosotros (escribir) → escribimos; Ellas (leer) → leen; Tú (compartir) → compartes; Nosotros (comprender) → comprendemos; Ustedes (vivir) → viven; Mi hermano (hablar) → habla. 80% passing.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Relief printmaking

**Prepare:** Foam printing plates (or flat erasers), dull pencil, water-based block ink, brayer, a tray, paper. Image of Hokusai's The Great Wave off Kanagawa (c. 1831).

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting prints are reversed. Letters and directional images must be drawn backwards on the plate.
- Using too much ink. Roll a thin, even layer that makes a soft hissing sound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Design
*Student:* Design a print inspired by Japanese woodblock prints (Hokusai) with strong positive and negative space.

*How to teach:* Design a print with bold shapes and positive/negative space, inspired by Hokusai.

- **Q:** What is negative space?
  **A:** The empty space around and between the subject.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transfer and carve
*Student:* Transfer the design onto a foam plate and incise lines.

*How to teach:* Transfer the design to foam and press lines in (incise) with a dull pencil.

- **Q:** Will a word on your plate print correctly?
  **A:** No — it will print backwards unless you carve it reversed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): First prints
*Student:* Ink with a brayer and pull test prints, adjusting pressure.

*How to teach:* Ink with a brayer and pull test prints; adjust pressure.

- **Q:** What is a brayer?
  **A:** A small hand roller used to spread ink evenly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edition
*Student:* Pull an edition of 4 prints, numbering and signing each.

*How to teach:* Pull 4 prints; sign and number them.

- **Q:** What does '3/4' on a print mean?
  **A:** This is the third print in an edition of 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Compare your print process to Hokusai's 'The Great Wave' production.

*How to teach:* Compare your process to The Great Wave.

- **Q:** In relief printing, do the carved-away areas print?
  **A:** No — the raised surface takes ink; carved (incised) areas stay the paper color.

**Assessment:** Numbered print edition.

**Answer key:** Edition rubric (1–4): strong positive/negative space design, clear incised lines, consistent inking, 4 matching prints, each signed and numbered in pencil below the image (1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 left, title center, signature right). Reflection proficient: explains that Hokusai's print was made by a team — the artist drew the design, a carver cut it into multiple woodblocks (one per color), a printer inked and printed, and a publisher sold many copies — unlike your single-person foam process.

## Physical Education 7: Basketball: dribbling and passing

**Prepare:** Basketball, hoop (or a target), cones, a partner. Set up a timed dribble course (e.g., 6 cones, down and back).

**Watch for:**
- Slapping the ball with the palm. Dribble with finger pads, pushing the ball, keeping it waist-high or lower.
- Looking at the ball. Keep eyes up to see teammates and defenders.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dribbling
*Student:* Practice stationary and moving dribbles with each hand and crossovers through cones.

*How to teach:* Practice stationary dribbles (high, low, each hand), then moving dribbles and crossovers through cones.

- **Q:** What part of the hand controls the dribble?
  **A:** The finger pads.
- **Q:** Why dribble with the hand away from the defender?
  **A:** Your body protects the ball.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Passing
*Student:* Practice chest, bounce and overhead passes with a partner, 20 each.

*How to teach:* Chest pass (thumbs down follow-through), bounce pass (bounce about two-thirds of the way to the partner), overhead pass. 20 each.

- **Q:** Where should a bounce pass hit the floor?
  **A:** About two-thirds of the way to the receiver.
- **Q:** When is a bounce pass useful?
  **A:** To get the ball under a defender's arms.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Triple threat
*Student:* Practice triple-threat position and jab steps.

*How to teach:* Teach triple threat (ready to shoot, pass or dribble) and jab steps.

- **Q:** Describe triple-threat position.
  **A:** Knees bent, ball held at hip on the shooting side, eyes on the basket, pivot foot set — ready to shoot, pass or dribble.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Keep-away
*Student:* Play 2-v-1 keep-away with dribble and pass.

*How to teach:* Play 2-v-1 keep-away using dribble and pass.

- **Q:** What is a pivot?
  **A:** Keeping one foot planted while stepping with the other to turn without traveling.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a timed dribble course.

*How to teach:* Time the dribble course.

- **Q:** What is traveling?
  **A:** Moving the pivot foot or taking steps while holding the ball without dribbling.

**Assessment:** Dribble course time.

**Answer key:** Dribble course: time to weave 6 cones down and back, using right hand on the way down and left on the way back, with crossovers at each cone; add 2 seconds per lost ball. Compare to a baseline trial. Proficient: completes with control, head up, using the correct hand.

## Health Education 7: Alcohol

**Prepare:** NIDA for Teens alcohol pages, 2–3 alcohol ads from magazines/online. Write a short quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Believing coffee, cold showers or sleep 'sober someone up' quickly. Only time lowers BAC — the liver processes alcohol at about one standard drink per hour.
- Thinking someone passed out from drinking should 'sleep it off.' They may have alcohol poisoning; call 911 and keep them on their side.

### Day 1 (Monday): Alcohol effects
*Student:* Learn how alcohol affects the brain, liver and judgment.

*How to teach:* Explain alcohol is a depressant that slows the brain. Your child labels effects on brain, liver, heart, stomach.

- **Q:** Is alcohol a stimulant or a depressant?
  **A:** A depressant — it slows down the central nervous system.
- **Q:** Which organ breaks down most alcohol?
  **A:** The liver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): BAC
*Student:* Learn what BAC is and factors that affect it.

*How to teach:* Define BAC and list factors; discuss why teens are affected more.

- **Q:** What is BAC?
  **A:** Blood alcohol concentration — the percent of alcohol in a person's blood.
- **Q:** Name three factors that affect BAC.
  **A:** Amount drunk, how fast, body weight/size, sex, whether there's food in the stomach.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Alcohol poisoning
*Student:* Learn signs of alcohol poisoning and when to call 911.

*How to teach:* Teach the signs of alcohol poisoning and the response: call 911, stay with the person, keep them on their side, don't let them 'sleep it off.'

- **Q:** Name three signs of alcohol poisoning.
  **A:** Confusion, vomiting, seizures, slow or irregular breathing, blue or pale skin, low body temperature, can't be awakened.
- **Q:** Why place a vomiting, drowsy person on their side?
  **A:** To prevent choking on vomit.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ad analysis
*Student:* Analyze alcohol ads and identify missing information.

*How to teach:* Your child analyzes ads and lists what's left out (health risks, accidents, addiction, cost).

- **Q:** What do alcohol ads usually show, and what do they leave out?
  **A:** They show fun, friends, success and attractive people; they leave out hangovers, injuries, crashes, addiction and health problems.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on alcohol.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key.

- **Q:** What is the legal drinking age in the U.S.?
  **A:** 21.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) alcohol is a depressant 2) affects judgment, coordination, reaction time, memory 3) liver breaks down most alcohol; heavy use causes liver disease 4) BAC = percent of alcohol in the blood 5) factors: amount, how fast, body size, sex, food in stomach 6) signs of alcohol poisoning: confusion, vomiting, seizures, slow or irregular breathing, pale/blue skin, low body temperature, can't be awakened 7) call 911 8) legal drinking age 21 9) binge drinking definition (for adults 4+ drinks women/5+ men in ~2 hours).
