# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 34

## English Language Arts 7: Debate and persuasive speech

**Prepare:** Timer; evidence cards; recording device (phone); debate and speech rubrics.

**Watch for:**
- Interrupting during cross-examination.
- Using only emotion in a persuasive speech.

### Day 1 (Monday): Debate rehearsal
*Student:* Rehearse the opening statement and cross-examination questions with a parent.

*How to teach:* Your child rehearses the opening statement and cross-examination questions with you. Check before moving on: ask "Good cross-examination question?" Look for: One that reveals a weakness: 'What evidence shows 16-year-olds are less informed than 18-year-olds?'

- **Q:** Good cross-examination question?
  **A:** One that reveals a weakness: 'What evidence shows 16-year-olds are less informed than 18-year-olds?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Debate
*Student:* Hold a structured debate with family (opening, rebuttal, cross-examination, closing).

*How to teach:* Hold a structured debate with family: opening, rebuttal, cross-examination, closing. Check before moving on: ask "Debate order?" Look for: Opening, cross-examination, rebuttal, closing for each side.

- **Q:** Debate order?
  **A:** Opening, cross-examination, rebuttal, closing for each side.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Evaluate the debate
*Student:* Watch a recording of the debate and evaluate both sides' reasoning with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Watch a recording of the debate. Your child evaluates both sides' reasoning with a rubric. Check before moving on: ask "What makes reasoning strong?" Look for: Relevant, sufficient evidence linked to claims.

- **Q:** What makes reasoning strong?
  **A:** Relevant, sufficient evidence linked to claims.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Persuasive speech
*Student:* Write a 3-minute persuasive speech on a community issue using ethos, pathos and logos.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 3-minute persuasive speech on a community issue using ethos, pathos and logos. Check before moving on: ask "Example ethos?" Look for: 'As someone who rides this bike lane every day…'

- **Q:** Example ethos?
  **A:** 'As someone who rides this bike lane every day…'

### Day 5 (Friday): Deliver & check
*Student:* Deliver the persuasive speech and receive rubric feedback.

*How to teach:* Your child delivers the persuasive speech. Give rubric feedback. Check before moving on: ask "What's a call to action?" Look for: A request for audience to do something specific.

- **Q:** What's a call to action?
  **A:** A request for audience to do something specific.

**Assessment:** Debate and speech rubrics.

**Answer key:** Debate rubric: argument, evidence, rebuttal, delivery, respect. Speech rubric: claim, ethos/pathos/logos each used, organization, delivery (eye contact, volume, 3 minutes).

## Mathematics 7: Compound events and simulation

**Prepare:** Two dice, coins, random number generator (random.org or a calculator).

**Watch for:**
- Counting a sum like 7 as one outcome instead of 6 ordered pairs.
- Too few simulation trials. Emphasize more trials give better estimates.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tree diagrams
*Student:* Draw tree diagrams for outfit choices and coin-die combinations and count outcomes.

*How to teach:* Model a tree for 3 shirts × 2 pants. Your child draws trees for outfits and coin-die combos. Check before moving on: ask "Coin and die outcomes?" Look for: 12.

- **Q:** Coin and die outcomes?
  **A:** 12.
- **Q:** 3 shirts, 2 pants, 2 shoes?
  **A:** 12 outfits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tables of outcomes
*Student:* Make a 6x6 table of sums of two dice and find P(sum = 7) and P(doubles).

*How to teach:* Your child completes the 6 × 6 sum table. Check before moving on: ask "P(sum = 7)?" Look for: 6/36 = 1/6.

- **Q:** P(sum = 7)?
  **A:** 6/36 = 1/6.
- **Q:** P(doubles)?
  **A:** 6/36 = 1/6.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Designing a simulation
*Student:* Design a coin-flip simulation for 'a family with 3 children has at least 2 girls' and run 30 trials.

*How to teach:* Design: H = girl, flip 3 coins per trial. Your child runs 30 trials. Check before moving on: ask "Theoretical P(at least 2 girls)?" Look for: 4/8 = 1/2.

- **Q:** Theoretical P(at least 2 girls)?
  **A:** 4/8 = 1/2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Simulating with technology
*Student:* Use a random number generator to simulate a 30% free-throw shooter taking 5 shots and estimate P(at least 2 makes).

*How to teach:* Model: digits 1–3 = make, 4–10 = miss. Your child simulates 5 shots many times. Check before moving on: ask "Theoretical answer?" Look for: About 0.47.

- **Q:** Theoretical answer?
  **A:** About 0.47.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on compound events and simulation design.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "P(two heads in two flips)?" Look for: 1/4.

- **Q:** P(two heads in two flips)?
  **A:** 1/4.

**Assessment:** Compound probability quiz and simulation write-up.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) Outfits from 4 shirts, 3 pants = 12. 2) P(sum 8) = 5/36. 3) P(HH) = 1/4. 4) P(at least one head in 2 flips) = 3/4. 5) Simulation design must state model, trial, success, number of trials.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Engineering to monitor and reduce human impact

**Prepare:** Coffee filters, sand, gravel, activated charcoal (aquarium store), cut plastic bottles, muddy water; petroleum jelly, index cards, string. Do NOT drink filtered water.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking clear water is safe. Filters remove particles but not all germs or dissolved chemicals.
- Changing many variables at once.

### Day 1 (Monday): Problem definition
*Student:* Choose a local impact (stormwater runoff, plastic waste, air quality) and write criteria and constraints for a solution.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses an impact and writes criteria and constraints. Check before moving on: ask "Criteria example?" Look for: Water clarity improves; flow at least 50 mL per minute.

- **Q:** Criteria example?
  **A:** Water clarity improves; flow at least 50 mL per minute.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water filter design
*Student:* Build a water filter from sand, gravel, charcoal and coffee filters and test it on muddy water.

*How to teach:* Build the filter: gravel, sand, charcoal, filter. Your child tests muddy water. Check before moving on: ask "Role of charcoal?" Look for: Adsorbs some dissolved substances and odors.

- **Q:** Role of charcoal?
  **A:** Adsorbs some dissolved substances and odors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Monitoring tool
*Student:* Design a method to monitor the impact (e.g., petroleum-jelly air particle cards placed around the house).

*How to teach:* Show how petroleum-jelly cards catch airborne particles. Your child designs a monitoring plan: card locations (kitchen, near a door, bedroom, outside), duration and how to count particles. Check before moving on: ask "Why several locations?" Look for: Compare particle levels.

- **Q:** Why several locations?
  **A:** Compare particle levels.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Testing
*Student:* Collect data from the filter and monitoring cards and record results.

*How to teach:* Collect the cards after 2–3 days and count particles in a marked square under a magnifier. Run more muddy-water filter tests and record clarity and flow in a table. Check before moving on: ask "How to measure clarity?" Look for: Compare to a scale or count visible particles.

- **Q:** How to measure clarity?
  **A:** Compare to a scale or count visible particles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Summarize results and propose improvements.

*How to teach:* Your child summarizes results in a paragraph with numbers and proposes two improvements with reasons. Check before moving on: ask "Why test improvements?" Look for: To check they work.

- **Q:** Why test improvements?
  **A:** To check they work.

**Assessment:** Design notebook entries.

**Answer key:** Design notebook: problem, criteria/constraints, design sketch, data table, conclusion, improvements.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: The Enlightenment

**Prepare:** Locke's Second Treatise excerpt; Montesquieu excerpt; costumes or name cards for salon.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Enlightenment thinkers all agreed.
- Thinking separation of powers is the same as federalism.

### Day 1 (Monday): Roots of the Enlightenment
*Student:* Create a flowchart linking Greek, Roman, Christian, Renaissance, Reformation and Scientific Revolution ideas to the Enlightenment.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a flowchart linking Greek, Roman, Christian, Renaissance, Reformation and Scientific Revolution ideas to the Enlightenment. Check before moving on: ask "Scientific Revolution's link?" Look for: Reason and natural laws could apply to society.

- **Q:** Scientific Revolution's link?
  **A:** Reason and natural laws could apply to society.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Locke
*Student:* Read excerpts from Locke's Second Treatise and explain natural rights.

*How to teach:* Read Locke's Second Treatise excerpts. Your child explains natural rights in their own words. Check before moving on: ask "Natural rights?" Look for: Life, liberty and property.

- **Q:** Natural rights?
  **A:** Life, liberty and property.
- **Q:** If government violates rights?
  **A:** People may change or overthrow it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Montesquieu
*Student:* Read Montesquieu on separation of powers and diagram the three branches.

*How to teach:* Read Montesquieu on separation of powers. Your child diagrams three branches with checks. Check before moving on: ask "Three branches?" Look for: Legislative, executive, judicial — to prevent tyranny.

- **Q:** Three branches?
  **A:** Legislative, executive, judicial — to prevent tyranny.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Enlightenment salon
*Student:* Role-play a salon conversation among Locke, Voltaire, Rousseau and Montesquieu.

*How to teach:* Role-play a salon among Locke, Voltaire, Rousseau and Montesquieu; your child plays two roles. Check before moving on: ask "Voltaire's ideas?" Look for: Freedom of speech and religion.

- **Q:** Voltaire's ideas?
  **A:** Freedom of speech and religion.
- **Q:** Rousseau?
  **A:** Social contract, general will.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Enlightenment thinkers.

*How to teach:* Give the Enlightenment thinkers quiz. Check before moving on: ask "Locke's work?" Look for: Two Treatises of Government (1689).

- **Q:** Locke's work?
  **A:** Two Treatises of Government (1689).

**Assessment:** Quiz and salon performance.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Locke natural rights, Montesquieu separation of powers, Voltaire free speech, Rousseau social contract. Salon rubric: accurate views, interaction.

## Spanish 1A: Invitations: accepting and declining

**Prepare:** Phone for texting/calling practice. Read about punctuality norms (e.g., social events often start later than the stated time in much of Latin America and Spain; business meetings expect punctuality).

**Watch for:**
- Declining without a reason. Polite decline: Lo siento, no puedo. Tengo que…
- Overgeneralizing 'everyone is always late' as a stereotype. Norms vary by country, setting and person.

### Day 1 (Monday): Invitation chunks
*Student:* Learn ¿Quieres ir...?, ¡Claro que sí!, Lo siento, no puedo, tengo que... and practice in pairs.

*How to teach:* Practice invitation chunks in pairs.

- **Q:** Invite a friend to the movies Saturday.
  **A:** ¿Quieres ir al cine el sábado?
- **Q:** Decline because you have to study.
  **A:** Lo siento, no puedo. Tengo que estudiar.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Texting
*Student:* Write a Spanish text-message exchange inviting a friend to a game, including time and place.

*How to teach:* Write a text exchange with time and place.

- **Q:** Ask 'At what time?' and answer 'At 4:00.'
  **A:** ¿A qué hora? — A las cuatro.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Phone call
*Student:* Make a simulated phone call to invite a family member to an event and respond to their excuse.

*How to teach:* Simulated phone call; respond to an excuse.

- **Q:** Your friend says she's busy. React sympathetically.
  **A:** ¡Qué pena! Otro día, entonces.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cultural norms
*Student:* Read about punctuality and social plans in Latin America and Spain and discuss differences.

*How to teach:* Read and discuss punctuality and social plans.

- **Q:** Name one cultural difference in social plans you read about.
  **A:** Example: in Spain dinner and social events often start much later (9–10 p.m.); friends may arrive after the stated time for informal parties.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold a recorded unscripted invitation conversation.

*How to teach:* Record an unscripted invitation conversation.

- **Q:** Accept enthusiastically.
  **A:** ¡Claro que sí! ¡Me encantaría!

**Assessment:** Interpersonal invitation rubric.

**Answer key:** Interpersonal invitation rubric (1–4): invites with ¿Quieres ir…? including place and time, accepts (¡Claro que sí! / ¡Qué bueno!) or declines (Lo siento, no puedo, tengo que… / Estoy ocupado/a), asks ¿A qué hora? / ¿Dónde?, reacts (¡Qué pena!), keeps the conversation going naturally.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Curating a portfolio

**Prepare:** All the year's artwork, poster board, cardstock, glue, boxes for pedestals. Google Arts & Culture account-free virtual tour (e.g., the Uffizi or the Met).

**Watch for:**
- Including everything. Curating means selecting the best and most representative works.
- Labels that only give a title. Museum labels include title, date, medium, size and a short interpretive text.

### Day 1 (Monday): Virtual museum
*Student:* Take a Google Arts & Culture virtual tour and note how works are displayed and labeled.

*How to teach:* Take a virtual tour; note how works are spaced, grouped and labeled.

- **Q:** What does a curator do?
  **A:** Selects, organizes and presents artworks for an exhibition and writes interpretive information.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Select
*Student:* Select 8–10 works representing each unit.

*How to teach:* Select 8–10 works representing each unit.

- **Q:** Why choose works from every unit?
  **A:** To show the range of skills and growth across the year.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prepare
*Student:* Mat or mount 2-D work and create pedestals for sculptures.

*How to teach:* Mat 2-D work and make pedestals for sculptures.

- **Q:** What is a mat?
  **A:** A border (usually cardboard) around a 2-D artwork that frames and protects it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Labels
*Student:* Write museum-style labels for each piece.

*How to teach:* Write museum-style labels.

- **Q:** What goes on a museum label?
  **A:** Artist name, title, date, medium, dimensions, and a short description.

### Day 5 (Friday): Layout
*Student:* Plan the exhibition layout in a room.

*How to teach:* Plan the exhibition layout.

- **Q:** How might you group works?
  **A:** By unit, medium, theme or chronology.

**Assessment:** Curated portfolio.

**Answer key:** Curated portfolio rubric (1–4): 8–10 works representing every unit, mounted/matted neatly, museum labels (artist, title, date, medium, dimensions, 2–3 sentence description), logical layout plan. Proficient: all units represented and labels complete.

## Physical Education 7: FITNESSGRAM post-test

**Prepare:** Same setup as weeks 1–2: PACER audio and 20 m cones (or mile course), mat, curl-up strip, cadence audio, trunk-lift ruler, sit-and-reach box. Pull baseline and mid-year scores.

**Watch for:**
- Changing test procedures from the baseline. Use exactly the same protocol so the comparison is fair.
- Thinking a lower score means failure. Growth, sleep and effort affect scores; reflect on why and what to adjust.

### Day 1 (Monday): PACER
*Student:* Retake the PACER or mile.

*How to teach:* Retake PACER or mile with the same protocol.

- **Q:** Baseline PACER 28 laps, now 41. What is the change?
  **A:** 13 more laps (about a 46% increase).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength tests
*Student:* Retake curl-ups and push-ups.

*How to teach:* Retake curl-ups and push-ups at cadence.

- **Q:** Why use the cadence for both tests?
  **A:** So reps are counted the same way each time, making results comparable.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility tests
*Student:* Retake trunk lift and sit-and-reach.

*How to teach:* Retake trunk lift and sit-and-reach.

- **Q:** What is the maximum score recorded for the trunk lift?
  **A:** 12 inches.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Active play
*Student:* Play a favorite game from the year.

*How to teach:* Play a favorite game from the year.

- **Q:** Which fitness component does your favorite game train most?
  **A:** Open answer — e.g., soccer: cardiorespiratory endurance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Compare
*Student:* Graph baseline vs. post-test scores and evaluate SMART goals.

*How to teach:* Graph baseline vs post-test and evaluate SMART goals.

- **Q:** What does it mean if a score is in the Healthy Fitness Zone?
  **A:** It meets the standard associated with good health for your age and sex.

**Assessment:** Post-test results and graph.

**Answer key:** Post-test graph: bar or line graph showing baseline vs post-test for PACER/mile, curl-ups, push-ups, trunk lift and sit-and-reach, with HFZ lines. Evaluation proficient: for each SMART goal states met/not met with numbers, and explains one reason for each result.

## Health Education 7: Environmental health

**Prepare:** Bookmark AirNow.gov. Find your water provider's annual Consumer Confidence (water quality) Report. Optional water test strips. A bag for a 1-day waste audit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking clear water is always safe. Lead and bacteria can't be seen; water reports and testing show safety.
- Thinking sunscreen is only needed at the beach. UV rays reach you on cloudy days and during everyday outdoor activities.

### Day 1 (Monday): Air quality
*Student:* Check the Air Quality Index for a week and learn health actions for each level.

*How to teach:* Check AirNow daily for a week; your child records AQI and the recommended action.

- **Q:** What does an orange AQI day mean and who should act?
  **A:** Unhealthy for sensitive groups (101–150); people with asthma or heart/lung disease, children and older adults should reduce long or intense outdoor exercise.
- **Q:** Name a source of air pollution.
  **A:** Vehicle exhaust, wildfire smoke, factories, power plants.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water
*Student:* Learn where your tap water comes from and read your local water report.

*How to teach:* Read the local water report: source (river, groundwater, imported) and any contaminants listed.

- **Q:** Why is lead in water dangerous?
  **A:** Lead damages the brain and nervous system, especially in children, and has no safe level.
- **Q:** Where does lead in water usually come from?
  **A:** Old lead pipes, solder or fixtures in plumbing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Noise and sun
*Student:* Learn about noise-induced hearing loss and sun protection.

*How to teach:* Teach noise-induced hearing loss (85 dB+, the 60/60 rule for earbuds: 60% volume, 60 minutes) and sun protection.

- **Q:** What is the 60/60 rule for headphones?
  **A:** Listen at no more than 60% volume for no more than 60 minutes at a time.
- **Q:** How often should sunscreen be reapplied?
  **A:** Every 2 hours, and after swimming or sweating.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reduce, reuse, recycle
*Student:* Audit household waste and plan reductions.

*How to teach:* Audit one day's household trash; sort into landfill, recycling, compost; plan 3 reductions.

- **Q:** Why is 'reduce' better than 'recycle'?
  **A:** Reducing prevents waste from being made at all, saving the energy and materials recycling still uses.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on environmental health.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key.

- **Q:** What does AQI stand for?
  **A:** Air Quality Index.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) AQI colors: green good (0–50), yellow moderate (51–100), orange unhealthy for sensitive groups (101–150), red unhealthy (151–200), purple very unhealthy (201–300), maroon hazardous (301+) 2) on red days limit outdoor exercise 3) lead harms the brain and development, especially in children; found in old pipes and old paint 4) noise above about 85 dB over long periods damages hearing 5) sunscreen SPF 30+, reapply every 2 hours and after swimming 6) reduce, reuse, recycle in order of best impact.
