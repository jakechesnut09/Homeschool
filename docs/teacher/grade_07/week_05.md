# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 5

## English Language Arts 7: Narrative revision, 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi' and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Print 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi' (Project Gutenberg); revision checklist; choose an unseen CommonLit short story with its questions for Day 5 (read it yourself first).

**Watch for:**
- Writing a theme as one word ('courage'). A theme is a complete sentence about life: 'True courage means protecting others even when you are afraid.'
- Putting opinions ('This awesome story…') into an objective summary. Summaries report only what happens.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revision: pacing
*Student:* Revise the narrative using a checklist: add transitions, cut one slow section, expand the climax.

*How to teach:* Your child revises with the checklist: add 3+ transitions, cut or shorten one slow section, expand the climax. Compare before and after aloud. Check before moving on: ask "Which transitions show time passing?" Look for: 'Later that afternoon', 'by the time', 'meanwhile', 'suddenly'.

- **Q:** Which transitions show time passing?
  **A:** 'Later that afternoon', 'by the time', 'meanwhile', 'suddenly'.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Editing & publishing
*Student:* Edit for clauses, commas and spelling, then type and publish the final narrative.

*How to teach:* Your child edits the narrative for one thing at a time: commas after introductory clauses, run-ons, then spelling. Read it aloud together to catch missing words, then your child types and prints the final copy with a title. Check before moving on: ask "Correct: 'When the whistle blew I ran.'" Look for: 'When the whistle blew, I ran.' — comma after an introductory dependent clause.

- **Q:** Correct: 'When the whistle blew I ran.'
  **A:** 'When the whistle blew, I ran.' — comma after an introductory dependent clause.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Read 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi'
*Student:* Read Kipling's 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi' and track how Rikki's courage develops through each conflict.

*How to teach:* Read the story together, taking turns by section. Your child records each conflict (Karait, Nag, the eggs, Nagaina) in a chart: what happens, what Rikki does, and what it shows about his courage. Ask how Rikki's confidence grows after each fight.

- **Q:** How does Rikki come to live with Teddy's family?
  **A:** A flood washes him out of his burrow; the family finds and revives him.
- **Q:** List Rikki's battles in order.
  **A:** Karait (small snake in the dust), Nag (in the bathroom, with the father's shotgun finishing him), then Nagaina (after he destroys her eggs and she threatens Teddy; he follows her into her hole and kills her).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme statement
*Student:* Write an objective summary of the story and a theme statement supported by three pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* Model one objective-summary sentence. Your child writes a summary and a theme statement with three pieces of evidence. Check before moving on: ask "A strong theme statement with evidence?" Look for: 'Courage grows through action': Rikki is young but kills Karait, then Nag, then faces Nagaina in her burrow.

- **Q:** A strong theme statement with evidence?
  **A:** 'Courage grows through action': Rikki is young but kills Karait, then Nag, then faces Nagaina in her burrow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Take a unit test on an unseen short story (CommonLit) with questions on plot, character, point of view and theme.

*How to teach:* Give the unseen CommonLit story and its questions with a 45-minute limit and no help. Score with the CommonLit key and the constructed-response guidance below; note weak skills (theme, point of view) for review. Check before moving on: ask "What does an objective summary leave out?" Look for: Opinions, judgments and minor details.

- **Q:** What does an objective summary leave out?
  **A:** Opinions, judgments and minor details.

**Assessment:** Published personal narrative (rubric) and unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (4–1 each): Focus/ideas (one clear moment, meaningful reflection); Organization (strong lead, logical sequence, transitions); Development (dialogue, sensory detail, slowed climax); Language/conventions (clause commas, spelling). Proficient = mostly 3s. Unit test: score selected responses with the CommonLit key; constructed responses proficient if they state an answer, quote the story accurately, and explain; theme answers must be complete sentences.

## Mathematics 7: Proportional relationships in tables

**Prepare:** Measuring cups, water, lemonade mix or lemon juice and sugar. Prepare 2–3 recipe cards: e.g., 2 cups water + 3 tbsp mix, 4 cups + 6 tbsp, 6 cups + 8 tbsp.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any table with increasing numbers is proportional. Divide y by x for every row — the quotient must be the same each time.
- Forgetting units on k. Insist on 'dollars per pound', 'miles per hour'.

### Day 1 (Monday): One of these things
*Student:* Compare recipes for lemonade that taste the same versus different and organize the amounts in tables.

*How to teach:* Mix two recipes that taste the same and one that doesn't. Your child records cups of water and tablespoons of mix in tables and explains which match using multiplication. Check before moving on: ask "2 cups water to 3 tbsp mix. How much mix for 6 cups?" Look for: 9 tbsp (×3).

- **Q:** 2 cups water to 3 tbsp mix. How much mix for 6 cups?
  **A:** 9 tbsp (×3).
- **Q:** Does 6 cups to 8 tbsp taste the same as 2 to 3?
  **A:** No — it should be 9 tbsp; 8/6 ≠ 3/2, so it is weaker.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Constant of proportionality
*Student:* Find k in tables of cost versus pounds and miles versus hours, writing k as a unit rate with units.

*How to teach:* Model k = y ÷ x for a cost-per-pound table. Your child finds k for each table and writes it as a unit rate with units. Check before moving on: ask "3 lb cost $7.50, 5 lb cost $12.50. k?" Look for: $2.50 per pound.

- **Q:** 3 lb cost $7.50, 5 lb cost $12.50. k?
  **A:** $2.50 per pound.
- **Q:** 120 miles in 2 hours, 300 in 5. k?
  **A:** 60 miles per hour.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Both directions
*Student:* Find the constant of proportionality from x to y and y to x in a currency-conversion table and note they are reciprocals.

*How to teach:* Model a table of dollars to euros (e.g., $1 → €0.90). Your child computes k each direction and checks that they multiply to 1. Check before moving on: ask "If euros = 0.9 × dollars, what is k from euros to dollars?" Look for: 1/0.9 = 10/9 ≈ 1.11 dollars per euro.

- **Q:** If euros = 0.9 × dollars, what is k from euros to dollars?
  **A:** 1/0.9 = 10/9 ≈ 1.11 dollars per euro.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Not proportional
*Student:* Sort 8 tables into proportional and not proportional, explaining with ratios that are or are not equivalent.

*How to teach:* Your child divides y by x in every row of all 8 tables and sorts them, writing one sentence of evidence per table. Check before moving on: ask "Table (1,5), (2,10), (4,18). Proportional?" Look for: No — 18/4 = 4.5, not 5.

- **Q:** Table (1,5), (2,10), (4,18). Proportional?
  **A:** No — 18/4 = 4.5, not 5.
- **Q:** Table (2,3), (4,6), (10,15). Proportional?
  **A:** Yes — every y/x = 1.5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: identify proportional tables, find k, and fill in missing table values.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key), then check and correct. Check before moving on: ask "In a proportional table x = 4, y = 10. What is y when x = 6?" Look for: 15 (k = 2.5).

- **Q:** In a proportional table x = 4, y = 10. What is y when x = 6?
  **A:** 15 (k = 2.5).

**Assessment:** Quiz on tables and constant of proportionality.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) (2,7),(4,14),(6,21): proportional, k = 3.5. 2) (1,4),(3,10),(5,16): not proportional. 3) $9 for 3 lb: k = 3 dollars per lb; 7 lb costs $21. 4) Missing value: x = 5, k = 1.2 → y = 6. 5) Reciprocal of k = 4/5 is 5/4.

## Science 7 (Integrated): Natural resources to synthetic materials

**Prepare:** Milk, white vinegar, strainer, paper towels; white school glue (PVA), borax, water (adult mixes borax solution; wash hands); 15 household items.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'synthetic' means not from nature at all. Synthetics are made from natural resources (often petroleum) changed by chemical processes.
- Thinking slime is a new element. It's a polymer: long chains linked by borate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Natural vs. synthetic
*Student:* Sort 15 household items as natural or synthetic and trace three synthetics back to their raw resources.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 15 items (cotton shirt, wool, nylon backpack, plastic bottle, wood, rubber band…) and traces three synthetics to raw resources. Check before moving on: ask "What raw resource are most plastics made from?" Look for: Petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas.

- **Q:** What raw resource are most plastics made from?
  **A:** Petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas.
- **Q:** Nylon or cotton — which is synthetic?
  **A:** Nylon.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Milk plastic
*Student:* Make casein plastic from warm milk and vinegar, mold it and let it dry.

*How to teach:* Warm 1 cup milk (not boiling), stir in 4 tsp vinegar, strain the curds, press dry, mold and let dry 48 hours. Your child records observations. Check before moving on: ask "What new substance formed?" Look for: Casein, a protein polymer that clumps when acid is added — an early plastic.

- **Q:** What new substance formed?
  **A:** Casein, a protein polymer that clumps when acid is added — an early plastic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Polymer slime
*Student:* Make glue-and-borax slime and compare its properties to the starting materials.

*How to teach:* Mix glue and water, then add borax solution while stirring. Your child compares slime with glue and borax solution. Check before moving on: ask "Why is slime stretchy?" Look for: Borate links PVA polymer chains loosely into a network.

- **Q:** Why is slime stretchy?
  **A:** Borate links PVA polymer chains loosely into a network.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Research a material
*Student:* Research nylon, polyester or plastic bags and write how they are made and how they affect society and the environment.

*How to teach:* Your child researches one material and writes about how it's made and its impacts. Check before moving on: ask "Environmental issue with plastic bags?" Look for: They last hundreds of years, break into microplastics and harm wildlife.

- **Q:** Environmental issue with plastic bags?
  **A:** They last hundreds of years, break into microplastics and harm wildlife.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Take the unit 1 test and present a one-page infographic on a synthetic material.

*How to teach:* Give unit 1 test; infographic presentation. Check before moving on: ask "Example of a synthetic material from a natural resource?" Look for: Nylon from petroleum.

- **Q:** Example of a synthetic material from a natural resource?
  **A:** Nylon from petroleum.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test and synthetic-material infographic.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test: particles move faster when hot; element/compound/mixture classification; count atoms in formulas; density calculations; synthetic materials come from natural resources via chemical reactions. Infographic: raw resource, process, uses, impact.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Beliefs and practices of Islam

**Prepare:** Translated Qur'an excerpts on charity and justice (Fordham); Five Pillars organizer; images of the Dome of the Rock and the Great Mosque of Córdoba (Smarthistory).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Muslims are Arab. Most Muslims today live outside the Arab world (e.g., Indonesia).
- Confusing the Qur'an (believed by Muslims to be God's word revealed to Muhammad) with the Hadith (reports of Muhammad's sayings and actions).

### Day 1 (Monday): The Qur'an
*Student:* Read short translated passages from the Qur'an on charity and justice and summarize their teachings.

*How to teach:* Read passages together. Your child summarizes each teaching in one sentence. Check before moving on: ask "What do the passages teach about charity?" Look for: Believers should give to the poor, orphans and travelers; wealth is a trust from God.

- **Q:** What do the passages teach about charity?
  **A:** Believers should give to the poor, orphans and travelers; wealth is a trust from God.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Five Pillars
*Student:* Create a graphic organizer of the Five Pillars with a description and example practice for each.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a graphic organizer of the Five Pillars. Check before moving on: ask "Name the Five Pillars." Look for: Shahada (declaration of faith), Salat (prayer five times daily facing Mecca), Zakat (charity), Sawm (fasting during Ramadan), Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca once if able).

- **Q:** Name the Five Pillars.
  **A:** Shahada (declaration of faith), Salat (prayer five times daily facing Mecca), Zakat (charity), Sawm (fasting during Ramadan), Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca once if able).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sunnah and law
*Student:* Explain how the Sunnah and Sharia guided daily life, commerce and family law.

*How to teach:* Explain Sunnah and Sharia. Your child lists examples in daily life, commerce and family. Check before moving on: ask "What is the Sunnah?" Look for: The example of Muhammad's words and actions, recorded in the Hadith.

- **Q:** What is the Sunnah?
  **A:** The example of Muhammad's words and actions, recorded in the Hadith.
- **Q:** Example of Sharia in commerce?
  **A:** Honest weights and measures; rules against charging interest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mosque architecture
*Student:* Examine images of the Dome of the Rock and Great Mosque of Córdoba and identify features like minaret, mihrab and arabesque.

*How to teach:* Show images. Your child identifies minaret, mihrab, arabesque and calligraphy. Check before moving on: ask "What is a mihrab?" Look for: A niche in the wall showing the direction of Mecca (qibla) for prayer.

- **Q:** What is a mihrab?
  **A:** A niche in the wall showing the direction of Mecca (qibla) for prayer.
- **Q:** Why geometric and plant patterns rather than people?
  **A:** Islamic religious art generally avoids depicting people or animals in sacred spaces.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Islamic beliefs and practices.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Check before moving on: ask "What happens during Ramadan?" Look for: Muslims fast from dawn to sunset.

- **Q:** What happens during Ramadan?
  **A:** Muslims fast from dawn to sunset.

**Assessment:** Five Pillars organizer and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Five Pillars named and described; Qur'an vs. Hadith; Sunnah; Sharia; mosque features (minaret, mihrab, dome, calligraphy); Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, Great Mosque in Córdoba.

## Spanish 1A: Los días, los meses y la fecha

**Prepare:** Printable calendar, markers, list of family birthdays. Read about 16 de septiembre, 12 de octubre and 6 de enero.

**Watch for:**
- Capitalizing days and months. In Spanish they are lowercase: lunes, enero.
- Saying 'el uno de enero.' The first of the month is 'el primero de enero'; other dates use cardinal numbers (el dos, el tres…).

### Day 1 (Monday): Los días
*Student:* Learn the days of the week (lowercase, week starts on lunes) and write tomorrow/today/yesterday statements: Hoy es martes, mañana es miércoles.

*How to teach:* Learn days (week starts on lunes); write today/tomorrow statements.

- **Q:** If today is Tuesday, write 'Today is Tuesday, tomorrow is Wednesday.'
  **A:** Hoy es martes, mañana es miércoles.
- **Q:** Which day starts the week on Spanish calendars?
  **A:** Lunes (Monday).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Los meses
*Student:* Learn the 12 months and fill in a blank Spanish calendar for this month with family events.

*How to teach:* Learn the 12 months; fill a calendar with family events.

- **Q:** Spell the month after julio.
  **A:** agosto.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): La fecha
*Student:* Learn the date format (el 5 de mayo, el primero de enero) and write 10 family birthdays: El cumpleaños de mi mamá es el....

*How to teach:* Teach date format; write 10 family birthdays.

- **Q:** Write: 'My mom's birthday is May 5.'
  **A:** El cumpleaños de mi mamá es el cinco de mayo.
- **Q:** How do you say January 1?
  **A:** el primero de enero.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Culture: días festivos
*Student:* Read about three holidays (16 de septiembre, 12 de octubre, 6 de enero Día de Reyes) and mark them on your calendar with a one-line note each.

*How to teach:* Read about three holidays; mark them.

- **Q:** What is celebrated on el 16 de septiembre?
  **A:** Mexican Independence Day (Grito de Dolores, 1810).
- **Q:** What is el Día de Reyes (6 de enero)?
  **A:** Three Kings Day (Epiphany), when children receive gifts and families eat rosca de reyes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer ¿Qué día es hoy? and ¿Cuál es la fecha? orally for five random dates and take a written dates quiz.

*How to teach:* Ask ¿Qué día es hoy? and ¿Cuál es la fecha? for five dates; give the written quiz.

- **Q:** Say the date 10/12 (October 12).
  **A:** Es el doce de octubre.

**Assessment:** Dates quiz and oral calendar questions.

**Answer key:** Dates quiz: days lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo; months enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre. Sample items: 4/7 → el cuatro de julio; 1/1 → el primero de enero; 25/12 → el veinticinco de diciembre; 14/2 → el catorce de febrero. Format: day before month (el 5 de mayo). 80% proficient. Oral: answers '¿Qué día es hoy?' (Hoy es ___) and '¿Cuál es la fecha?' (Es el ___ de ___).

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Still life composition and artist statement

**Prepare:** 4–5 household objects of different heights and textures, a drawing paper, a paper viewfinder (rectangle cut from cardstock). An image of a Dutch Golden Age still life by Pieter Claesz.

**Watch for:**
- Lining objects up in a row facing forward. Overlap objects and vary heights for depth and interest.
- Placing the focal point dead center. Off-center (e.g., rule of thirds) is usually more dynamic.

### Day 1 (Monday): Arranging
*Student:* Arrange 4–5 objects and use a paper viewfinder to make three thumbnail compositions.

*How to teach:* Arrange objects with overlap; make three thumbnails through the viewfinder.

- **Q:** What is a focal point?
  **A:** The area that draws the viewer's eye first.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drawing
*Student:* Draw the strongest composition lightly with accurate proportion and overlap.

*How to teach:* Lightly draw the best thumbnail using sighting to check proportions (hold pencil at arm's length to compare sizes).

- **Q:** How does overlap create depth?
  **A:** An object in front blocks part of the one behind, showing which is closer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shading
*Student:* Shade the still life fully with a range of values.

*How to teach:* Shade fully with a range of values and a consistent light source.

- **Q:** Why include both very light and very dark values?
  **A:** Strong contrast makes objects look three-dimensional and creates emphasis.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique
*Student:* Analyze a Dutch still life (e.g., Claesz) and compare its composition to yours.

*How to teach:* Analyze a Claesz still life (muted tones, strong light, objects arranged on a diagonal, symbols of transience). Compare to your composition.

- **Q:** What message did many Dutch still lifes carry?
  **A:** Vanitas — the shortness of life and the passing nature of earthly pleasures, shown with objects like skulls, extinguished candles, watches or half-eaten food.

### Day 5 (Friday): Artist statement
*Student:* Write a 150-word artist statement explaining your choices.

*How to teach:* Write the 150-word artist statement.

- **Q:** What belongs in an artist statement?
  **A:** What the work is, materials, the choices you made and why, and what you want viewers to notice or feel.

**Assessment:** Still life drawing and artist statement (rubric).

**Answer key:** Still life rubric (1–4 each): composition (overlap, varied sizes, clear off-center focal point, fills the page), proportion, value range (white to near black), craftsmanship. Artist statement (~150 words) proficient: names subject and medium, explains composition and value choices, connects to the Dutch still-life influence, reflects on challenge/success.

## Physical Education 7: Leadership and fair play

**Prepare:** Cones, a frisbee/flag, pinnies or colored shirts for two teams, a few friends or family members. Print a sportsmanship checklist (shakes hands, follows rules, accepts calls, encourages, includes everyone).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking leaders are the loudest or best players. Leaders organize, include others and model fair play.
- Arguing every close call. In friendly games, call it fairly, offer a redo if unsure, and move on.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lead a warm-up
*Student:* Plan and lead the family through a 5-minute warm-up.

*How to teach:* Your child plans and leads a 5-minute warm-up (moves, counts, demonstrations).

- **Q:** Name two qualities of a good warm-up leader.
  **A:** Clear loud instructions, demonstrates moves, watches participants, keeps it moving, encourages.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Capture the flag
*Student:* Play capture the flag with a group and practice calling fair play.

*How to teach:* Play capture the flag (each team guards a flag; tagged players go to 'jail' until freed). Players call their own tags honestly.

- **Q:** What is sportsmanship?
  **A:** Playing fair, following rules, respecting opponents and officials, and being gracious whether winning or losing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conflict scenarios
*Student:* Role-play 4 game conflicts (disputed call, ball hog) and solve them respectfully.

*How to teach:* Role-play four conflicts (disputed call, ball hog, sore loser, rule-bender) and solve them with I-messages or rock-paper-scissors redo.

- **Q:** Two players disagree on whether a tag happened. What is a fair solution?
  **A:** Discuss calmly, and if still unclear, replay the point (do-over) or use rock-paper-scissors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Create a game
*Student:* Design a new tag game with rules that include everyone, then teach and play it.

*How to teach:* Design a new tag game with an inclusion rule; teach and play it, then revise.

- **Q:** Give an example of an inclusive rule.
  **A:** Players who are tagged can rejoin after 10 jumping jacks, so no one sits out long.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Self-assess sportsmanship with a checklist.

*How to teach:* Your child self-assesses with the checklist.

- **Q:** How should you act after losing a game?
  **A:** Shake hands, congratulate the winners, think about what to improve, no excuses or blaming.

**Assessment:** Game design and sportsmanship checklist.

**Answer key:** Game design rubric: 4 = clear objective, boundaries, safety rule, rules that include all players (e.g., everyone must touch the ball/tag rotates), tested and revised after play; 3 = playable with minor gaps; 2 = unclear rules; 1 = incomplete. Sportsmanship checklist self-assessment with honest examples.

## Health Education 7: Physical activity and food safety

**Prepare:** Gather a food thermometer (or plan to read the fridge thermometer), ingredients for a simple healthy snack, and an activity/screen-time log sheet for 7 days.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 60 minutes of activity must be done all at once. Activity can add up across the day in shorter bouts.
- Believing you can tell food is safe by smell or look. Bacteria that cause illness often can't be seen or smelled; use a thermometer and the fridge rules.

### Day 1 (Monday): Activity guidelines
*Student:* Learn the 60-minutes-a-day guideline and log a week's activity and screen time.

*How to teach:* Teach the guideline: ages 6–17 need 60+ minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily, with vigorous, muscle- and bone-strengthening activity at least 3 days each. Your child starts a 7-day log of activity and screen time.

- **Q:** How can you tell if activity is moderate vs vigorous?
  **A:** Moderate: you can talk but not sing. Vigorous: you can only say a few words before needing a breath.
- **Q:** Give one bone-strengthening activity.
  **A:** Jumping rope, running, basketball, hopscotch — anything with impact.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Benefits and barriers
*Student:* List benefits of activity and personal barriers with solutions.

*How to teach:* Your child lists 5 benefits of activity and 3 personal barriers, each with a solution.

- **Q:** Name three benefits of regular activity.
  **A:** Stronger heart and lungs, stronger muscles and bones, better sleep, less stress, better mood and focus, healthy weight.
- **Q:** Barrier: 'No time.' Give a solution.
  **A:** Break activity into 10-minute bouts, walk/bike for errands, active family time, move during screen breaks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Food safety
*Student:* Learn clean, separate, cook, chill and check the fridge temperature.

*How to teach:* Teach the four steps: clean, separate, cook, chill. Your child checks the fridge temperature and finds where raw meat should be stored (bottom shelf).

- **Q:** What temperature should a refrigerator be?
  **A:** 40°F (4°C) or below.
- **Q:** Why store raw meat on the bottom shelf?
  **A:** So its juices can't drip onto ready-to-eat foods (prevents cross-contamination).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cook a snack
*Student:* Prepare a healthy snack using food safety steps.

*How to teach:* Your child washes hands 20 seconds, cleans the counter, uses separate boards if needed, and prepares a snack (e.g., veggie wrap). Ask them to narrate each safety step.

- **Q:** How long should you scrub hands with soap?
  **A:** At least 20 seconds.
- **Q:** To what temperature must chicken be cooked?
  **A:** 165°F internal temperature.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on food safety and activity guidelines.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the assessment key and total the activity log.

- **Q:** What is the 2-hour rule?
  **A:** Perishable food should not sit at room temperature more than 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F).
- **Q:** Name two symptoms of foodborne illness.
  **A:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomach cramps, fever.

**Assessment:** Activity log and quiz.

**Answer key:** Activity log: 7 days of activity minutes and screen time, with total MVPA and a note whether the 60-minute daily goal was met. Quiz key (suggested): 1) 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily 2) vigorous activity, muscle-strengthening and bone-strengthening each at least 3 days a week 3) clean, separate, cook, chill 4) fridge at 40°F (4°C) or below, freezer 0°F 5) cook poultry to 165°F, ground beef to 160°F 6) refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour if over 90°F) 7) cross-contamination = spreading germs from raw meat to ready-to-eat food.
