# Teacher's Guide — 7th Grade, Week 26

## English Language Arts 7: Research revision and multimedia presentation

**Prepare:** Google Slides or poster board; a TED-Ed talk (5 min) on any topic; presentation rubric printed.

**Watch for:**
- Overloaded slides that the speaker reads aloud. Keep slides to images and a few key words; the speaker explains.
- Thinking revision is just spelling. Revising means improving clarity, word choice and flow.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revision
*Student:* Revise the report for clarity, precise vocabulary and smooth integration of quotes.

*How to teach:* Your child revises for clarity, precise vocabulary and smooth quote integration; read one paragraph aloud together to catch awkward spots. Check before moving on: ask "Replace 'a lot of people' with something precise." Look for: 'More than 40,000 Californians' or 'most coastal residents' — a specific, sourced amount.

- **Q:** Replace 'a lot of people' with something precise.
  **A:** 'More than 40,000 Californians' or 'most coastal residents' — a specific, sourced amount.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final edit
*Student:* Edit and publish the final 1,000–1,200-word report.

*How to teach:* Your child does a final edit (spelling, citations, formatting) and publishes 1,000–1,200 words. Check before moving on: ask "Name three things to check in a final edit." Look for: Spelling/grammar, citation punctuation, and Works Cited formatting.

- **Q:** Name three things to check in a final edit.
  **A:** Spelling/grammar, citation punctuation, and Works Cited formatting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build presentation
*Student:* Create a 6–8 slide presentation with images and minimal text emphasizing key findings.

*How to teach:* Your child builds 6–8 slides: title, 4–6 key findings with images, sources slide. Check before moving on: ask "What makes a slide effective?" Look for: One idea per slide, a clear visual, few words, readable font.

- **Q:** What makes a slide effective?
  **A:** One idea per slide, a clear visual, few words, readable font.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Deliver a 5-minute presentation and answer two audience questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents for 5 minutes and answers two audience questions. Check before moving on: ask "How should you answer a question you can't answer?" Look for: Say you don't know yet and how you would find out.

- **Q:** How should you answer a question you can't answer?
  **A:** Say you don't know yet and how you would find out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection & check
*Student:* Write a reflection on the research process and listen to a TED-Ed talk, summarizing its main ideas and supporting details.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a reflection, then watches a TED-Ed talk and summarizes its main idea with three supporting details. Check before moving on: ask "What is the difference between a main idea and a supporting detail?" Look for: The main idea is the speaker's central point; details are facts, examples or explanations that support it.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a main idea and a supporting detail?
  **A:** The main idea is the speaker's central point; details are facts, examples or explanations that support it.

**Assessment:** Research report and presentation rubrics.

**Answer key:** Report rubric (4–1): focus; research (3+ credible sources, accurately represented); organization; integration and citation; conventions. Presentation rubric: content accuracy, slide design, delivery (eye contact, volume, pace, 5 min), answers to questions. TED-Ed summary: main idea + 3 accurate details.

## Mathematics 7: Equivalent expressions

**Prepare:** Area model templates (rectangles split into parts).

**Watch for:**
- Combining unlike terms (3x + 2 = 5x).
- Dropping a negative sign when distributing: −2(x − 4) = −2x + 8, not −2x − 8.

### Day 1 (Monday): Subtraction in expressions
*Student:* Rewrite expressions with subtraction as adding the opposite and evaluate to test equivalence.

*How to teach:* Rewrite 5 − 3x as 5 + (−3x). Your child tests equivalence by substituting x = 2 in both forms. Check before moving on: ask "Is 8 − 2x equivalent to −2x + 8?" Look for: Yes — commutative after writing as 8 + (−2x).

- **Q:** Is 8 − 2x equivalent to −2x + 8?
  **A:** Yes — commutative after writing as 8 + (−2x).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Distributive property
*Student:* Expand and factor expressions like 6(2x - 5) and 12x + 18 using area models.

*How to teach:* Model 6(2x − 5) with an area model. Your child expands and factors. Check before moving on: ask "Expand 6(2x − 5)." Look for: 12x − 30.

- **Q:** Expand 6(2x − 5).
  **A:** 12x − 30.
- **Q:** Factor 12x + 18.
  **A:** 6(2x + 3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Combining like terms
*Student:* Simplify expressions such as 3x - 4 - 7x + 2.5 and check by substituting values.

*How to teach:* Model 3x − 4 − 7x + 2.5. Your child simplifies and checks with x = 1. Check before moving on: ask "Simplify." Look for: −4x − 1.5. Check x = 1: original 3 − 4 − 7 + 2.5 = −5.5; simplified −4 − 1.5 = −5.5.

- **Q:** Simplify.
  **A:** −4x − 1.5. Check x = 1: original 3 − 4 − 7 + 2.5 = −5.5; simplified −4 − 1.5 = −5.5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rewriting for meaning
*Student:* Explain why 'increase by 5%' can be written as a + 0.05a or 1.05a and solve 4 similar problems.

*How to teach:* Model a + 0.05a = 1.05a. Your child solves 4 similar problems. Check before moving on: ask "Write a 20% decrease of p two ways." Look for: p − 0.20p and 0.80p.

- **Q:** Write a 20% decrease of p two ways.
  **A:** p − 0.20p and 0.80p.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on equivalent expressions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz (see key). Check before moving on: ask "Simplify −2(x − 4) + 3x." Look for: x + 8.

- **Q:** Simplify −2(x − 4) + 3x.
  **A:** x + 8.

**Assessment:** Equivalent expressions quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) 4(3x − 2) = 12x − 8. 2) Factor 15x − 10 = 5(3x − 2). 3) 2x + 5 − 6x − 1 = −4x + 4. 4) Is 3(x + 2) equivalent to 3x + 2? No. 5) 8% increase on c: 1.08c. 6) −(5 − x) = x − 5.

## Science 7 (Integrated): The geologic time scale

**Prepare:** Roll of receipt paper or toilet paper, meter stick, markers; 100 pennies, cup, graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking humans and dinosaurs lived together. Non-avian dinosaurs died out about 66 million years ago; humans appeared about 300,000 years ago.
- Thinking half-life means all is gone after two half-lives. Half of what remains decays each half-life.

### Day 1 (Monday): Toilet-paper timeline
*Student:* Build a 46-meter (or 46-square) timeline of Earth's history and mark major events to scale.

*How to teach:* Lay out 46 m (1 m = 100 million years). Your child marks Earth's formation (4.6 bya), first life (~3.5–3.8 bya), Cambrian explosion (~539 mya), dinosaurs (~230–66 mya), humans. Check before moving on: ask "How far from the end is the dinosaur extinction?" Look for: 66 million years = 0.66 m.

- **Q:** How far from the end is the dinosaur extinction?
  **A:** 66 million years = 0.66 m.
- **Q:** Where do humans go?
  **A:** About 3 mm from the end (300,000 years).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reflecting on scale
*Student:* Calculate what fraction of the timeline includes humans and write a reflection.

*How to teach:* Your child calculates humans' fraction of Earth's history and writes a reflection. Check before moving on: ask "Fraction?" Look for: 300,000 / 4,600,000,000 ≈ 0.0065% of Earth's history.

- **Q:** Fraction?
  **A:** 300,000 / 4,600,000,000 ≈ 0.0065% of Earth's history.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Absolute dating
*Student:* Model radioactive half-life with 100 pennies flipped and removed each round, and graph the decay.

*How to teach:* Flip 100 pennies; remove tails each round. Your child records and graphs. Check before moving on: ask "About how many remain after 3 rounds?" Look for: About 12–13 (100 → 50 → 25 → 12.5).

- **Q:** About how many remain after 3 rounds?
  **A:** About 12–13 (100 → 50 → 25 → 12.5).
- **Q:** How does this date rocks?
  **A:** Measure the ratio of parent to daughter atoms; knowing the half-life gives the age.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Eras and extinctions
*Student:* Read about the five mass extinctions and label them on the timeline.

*How to teach:* Read about the five mass extinctions. Your child labels them. Check before moving on: ask "Largest?" Look for: End-Permian (~252 mya), 'the Great Dying'.

- **Q:** Largest?
  **A:** End-Permian (~252 mya), 'the Great Dying'.
- **Q:** Most recent?
  **A:** End-Cretaceous (~66 mya), asteroid impact, killed non-avian dinosaurs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an explanation of how rock strata and fossils were used to build the geologic time scale.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how strata and fossils built the time scale. Check before moving on: ask "Key idea?" Look for: Relative dating ordered layers and fossil changes; radiometric dating added absolute ages.

- **Q:** Key idea?
  **A:** Relative dating ordered layers and fossil changes; radiometric dating added absolute ages.

**Assessment:** Timeline project and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Timeline: scale correct (1 m = 100 million years), events placed accurately, five extinctions labeled (end-Ordovician ~445, Late Devonian ~372, end-Permian ~252, end-Triassic ~201, end-Cretaceous ~66 mya). Explanation rubric: superposition, index fossils, radiometric dating.

## World History & Geography: Medieval and Early Modern Times: Printing press and Northern Renaissance

**Prepare:** Letter stamps or potato-carved letters, ink pad, paper; map of European printing presses by 1500; images of Dürer and van Eyck works.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Gutenberg invented printing. He invented a movable-metal-type press in Europe (c. 1450); China had woodblock printing and movable type centuries earlier.
- Thinking literacy rose overnight. It grew gradually as books became cheaper.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gutenberg
*Student:* Read about Gutenberg's press and compare the time to copy a book by hand vs. by press.

*How to teach:* Read about Gutenberg. Your child compares copying by hand vs printing. Check before moving on: ask "When was the Gutenberg Bible printed?" Look for: About 1455.

- **Q:** When was the Gutenberg Bible printed?
  **A:** About 1455.
- **Q:** Effect on book cost and time?
  **A:** A scribe took months or more per book; presses produced many copies quickly and cheaply.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Movable type
*Student:* Use letter stamps to print a short message and reflect on the process.

*How to teach:* Your child sets type with letter stamps (reversed!) and prints a message. Check before moving on: ask "Why must type be reversed?" Look for: It prints as a mirror image.

- **Q:** Why must type be reversed?
  **A:** It prints as a mirror image.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spread of ideas
*Student:* Map printing presses in Europe by 1500 and explain effects on literacy and vernacular texts.

*How to teach:* Your child maps presses and explains effects. Check before moving on: ask "Effects?" Look for: More books, higher literacy, books in vernacular languages, faster spread of ideas.

- **Q:** Effects?
  **A:** More books, higher literacy, books in vernacular languages, faster spread of ideas.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Northern artists and Shakespeare
*Student:* Compare Dürer and van Eyck with Italian artists and preview Shakespeare's place in the Renaissance.

*How to teach:* Compare northern and Italian artists. Check before moving on: ask "Van Eyck's contribution?" Look for: Oil painting with fine detail (Arnolfini Portrait).

- **Q:** Van Eyck's contribution?
  **A:** Oil painting with fine detail (Arnolfini Portrait).
- **Q:** Dürer?
  **A:** Woodcuts and engravings, self-portraits.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a cause-effect paragraph on the printing press.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a cause-effect paragraph. Check before moving on: ask "Strong cause-effect chain?" Look for: Press → cheaper books → more readers → ideas (like Luther's) spread fast.

- **Q:** Strong cause-effect chain?
  **A:** Press → cheaper books → more readers → ideas (like Luther's) spread fast.

**Assessment:** Cause-effect paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence answering the prompt, at least two accurate specific facts or document references, explanation of how they support the claim, concluding sentence; 3 = accurate but thin explanation; 2 = general statements, few facts; 1 = off-topic or inaccurate.

## Spanish 1A: Tener expressions and celebrations

**Prepare:** Pictures for captioning (someone shivering, eating, sleepy, scared, running late). Recording of 'Las mañanitas.' Read about the quinceañera.

**Watch for:**
- Translating 'I am hungry' with estar or ser. Spanish uses tener: Tengo hambre.
- Saying 'Tengo mucho frío' is right, but 'Tengo muy frío' is wrong — tener expressions use mucho/mucha (nouns).

### Day 1 (Monday): Expressions
*Student:* Learn tener hambre, sed, frío, calor, sueño, miedo, prisa, razón and caption 10 pictures.

*How to teach:* Learn 8 expressions; caption 10 pictures.

- **Q:** Caption a picture of a boy shivering.
  **A:** Él tiene frío.
- **Q:** Say 'I'm very hungry.'
  **A:** Tengo mucha hambre.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tener que
*Student:* Write a to-do list using tener que + infinitive for each family member this week.

*How to teach:* Write a to-do list with tener que + infinitive.

- **Q:** Write: 'My brother has to clean his room.'
  **A:** Mi hermano tiene que limpiar su cuarto.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Celebrations
*Student:* Read about the quinceañera and the cumpleaños tradition of las mañanitas; listen to Las mañanitas and write down five words you hear.

*How to teach:* Read about the quinceañera and las mañanitas; listen and note five words.

- **Q:** What is a quinceañera?
  **A:** A celebration of a girl's 15th birthday marking her passage to young womanhood, often with a Mass, a formal dress, a waltz and a party.
- **Q:** When is 'Las mañanitas' sung?
  **A:** On birthdays (and saints' days), often early in the morning, in Mexico.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mi fiesta
*Student:* Describe your last family party in present-tense sentences: who is there, what they eat, what they have.

*How to teach:* Describe a family party in present tense.

- **Q:** Write: 'At the party we eat cake.'
  **A:** En la fiesta comemos pastel.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a tener expressions quiz with a listening section.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz with listening.

- **Q:** Which expression: 'Tienes ___' when you are right?
  **A:** Tienes razón.

**Assessment:** Tener expressions quiz.

**Answer key:** Tener expressions quiz key: tener hambre (to be hungry), sed (thirsty), frío (cold), calor (hot), sueño (sleepy), miedo (afraid), prisa (in a hurry), razón (right), ___ años (to be __ years old); tener que + infinitive (to have to). Samples: 'We are very thirsty' → Tenemos mucha sed; 'I have to study' → Tengo que estudiar; 'She is afraid' → Ella tiene miedo. Listening: identify expression from a short audio description. 80% proficient.

## Visual & Performing Arts 7: Rehearsal process

**Prepare:** Script copies, pencil, props and costumes from the design plan, family actors, phone for sound effects.

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing lines without cue lines. Learn the line before yours so you know when to speak.
- Saving blocking notes in your head. Write them in pencil in the script.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read-through
*Student:* Hold a read-through with family members playing roles.

*How to teach:* Hold a read-through with family playing roles.

- **Q:** What is a read-through?
  **A:** The first rehearsal where the cast reads the script aloud together.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Blocking
*Student:* Block the scene and write blocking notes in the script.

*How to teach:* Block the scene; write notes like 'X DSL' (cross downstage left).

- **Q:** What does 'cheat out' mean?
  **A:** Turn your body partly toward the audience so they can see your face.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Memorize
*Student:* Work toward off-book with cue-line drills.

*How to teach:* Drill cue lines.

- **Q:** What does 'off-book' mean?
  **A:** Having lines memorized without the script.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Run-through
*Student:* Run the full scene with props and give notes.

*How to teach:* Run the full scene with props; give notes.

- **Q:** What is a run-through?
  **A:** Performing the whole scene/play without stopping.

### Day 5 (Friday): Tech
*Student:* Add costumes, sound effects or lighting cues and run again.

*How to teach:* Add costumes, sound or lighting cues; run again.

- **Q:** What is a cue?
  **A:** A signal (line, action or sound) for an actor or technician to do something.

**Assessment:** Rehearsal journal.

**Answer key:** Rehearsal journal (each day): what was rehearsed, director's notes received, personal goal, progress. Proficient: 5 entries with specific notes (e.g., 'Cheat out downstage on the death speech') and evidence of being off-book by day 4.

## Physical Education 7: Cultural and folk dances

**Prepare:** Music and videos for La Raspa, the Hora (or Virginia Reel) and a West African dance (e.g., Kuku or Lamban) — preview so you know the formations.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking folk dances are just steps. They carry cultural meaning — celebrations, harvest, community.
- Ignoring the formation. Circle, line and longways formations are part of the dance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mexican folk dance
*Student:* Learn basic steps of a Mexican folk dance like 'La Raspa'.

*How to teach:* Teach La Raspa's hop step (hop and switch feet forward like a bleking step) and partner swing.

- **Q:** Where does La Raspa come from?
  **A:** Mexico (Veracruz).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Israeli/European folk dance
*Student:* Learn 'Hora' or 'Virginia Reel' formations.

*How to teach:* Learn the Hora (circle, step-cross-step with kicks moving left/right) or the Virginia Reel (two lines, partners do turns and a reel down the middle).

- **Q:** What formation is the Hora danced in?
  **A:** A circle with hands joined or on shoulders.
- **Q:** What formation is the Virginia Reel?
  **A:** A longways set — two lines facing each other.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): West African dance
*Student:* Follow a West African dance video and learn about its connection to drumming.

*How to teach:* Follow a West African dance video; discuss how dancers respond to drum rhythms (call and response).

- **Q:** How are dance and drumming connected in West African dance?
  **A:** Drummers lead and change rhythms and dancers respond; drum calls signal changes in movement.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare formations and purposes of the three dances.

*How to teach:* Compare formations and purposes of the three dances.

- **Q:** What is one purpose many folk dances share?
  **A:** Celebrating community events like weddings, harvests or festivals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Perform one folk dance with family members.

*How to teach:* Perform one folk dance with family.

- **Q:** What made the group dance work well?
  **A:** Open answer — e.g., counting together, holding formation, watching partners.

**Assessment:** Folk dance performance.

**Answer key:** Folk dance performance rubric (1–4): correct formation, basic steps, timing with music, cooperation with partners. Comparison chart proficient: for each dance names origin (La Raspa — Mexico; Hora — Jewish/Israeli and Eastern European (Romania); Virginia Reel — American, from English country dance; West African dance — e.g., Guinea/Mali), formation (La Raspa — partners/scattered; Hora — circle; Virginia Reel — two facing lines/longways set; West African — often circle or lines facing drummers), and purpose (celebration, social, community).

## Health Education 7: Hygiene and sleep

**Prepare:** Sleep log sheet for 7 days. Gather a few hygiene products to discuss. Decide your family screen curfew range.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking acne comes from dirty skin or chocolate. Acne is mainly caused by hormones increasing oil, clogged pores and bacteria; scrubbing hard makes it worse.
- Believing you can 'catch up' all lost sleep on weekends. Irregular sleep disrupts the body clock; a consistent schedule works best.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hygiene
*Student:* Create a daily hygiene routine (skin, hair, teeth, deodorant).

*How to teach:* Your child writes a daily hygiene routine: shower, wash face twice, deodorant, brush teeth twice and floss, clean clothes.

- **Q:** How often should you brush and floss?
  **A:** Brush twice a day for 2 minutes; floss once a day.
- **Q:** Why does body odor increase in puberty?
  **A:** Sweat glands become more active and bacteria break down sweat.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Acne
*Student:* Learn causes of acne and myths vs. facts.

*How to teach:* Explain acne causes and myths vs facts.

- **Q:** Myth or fact: Popping pimples makes them heal faster.
  **A:** Myth — it can push bacteria deeper and cause scarring.
- **Q:** What helps prevent acne?
  **A:** Gently washing twice daily, oil-free products, not picking, washing after sweating; see a doctor for severe acne.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sleep needs
*Student:* Learn teens need 8–10 hours and start a one-week sleep log.

*How to teach:* Teach sleep needs and the circadian rhythm; start the 7-day log.

- **Q:** How much sleep do teens 13–18 need?
  **A:** 8–10 hours per night (ages 6–12 need 9–12).
- **Q:** What is the circadian rhythm?
  **A:** The body's internal 24-hour clock that controls sleep and wake cycles.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Screens and sleep
*Student:* Analyze how screens affect sleep and set a screen curfew.

*How to teach:* Explain blue light suppresses melatonin and screens are stimulating. Your child sets a screen curfew (e.g., 1 hour before bed).

- **Q:** How do screens affect sleep?
  **A:** Blue light reduces melatonin and the content keeps the brain alert, making it harder to fall asleep.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Evaluate the sleep log and set a sleep goal.

*How to teach:* Average the log and write a SMART sleep goal.

- **Q:** Bedtime 10:30 p.m., wake 6:30 a.m. How many hours?
  **A:** 8 hours.

**Assessment:** Sleep log and goal.

**Answer key:** Sleep log: 7 nights with bedtime, wake time and hours, averaged correctly. Proficient sleep goal: SMART format, e.g., 'Lights out by 9:30 on school nights for 2 weeks to reach 9 hours,' with a screen curfew. Recommended: 9–12 hours for ages 6–12, 8–10 hours for ages 13–18.
