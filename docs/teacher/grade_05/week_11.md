# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 11

## ELA 5: Multiple accounts: Columbus and the Taíno

**Prepare:** Find an excerpt of Columbus's journal from October 1492 (Library of Congress or National Archives education sites) and the Smithsonian NMAI article on the Taíno. Preview both — the journal describes the Taíno as potential servants, which should be discussed honestly.

**Watch for:**
- Believing primary sources are always objective. A primary source shows the author's view and goals.
- Thinking the Taíno disappeared entirely. Their population was devastated by disease, violence and forced labor, but Taíno descendants and culture continue today in the Caribbean and elsewhere.
- Mixing up Greek roots graph (write) and gram (letter/written).

### Day 1 (Monday): Primary source
*Student:* Read an excerpt of Columbus's journal from October 1492 and note how he describes the land and the Taíno people.

*How to teach:* Read the journal excerpt; your child notes how Columbus describes the land and the Taíno and what he seems to want. Read it together slowly, defining old words; your child highlights descriptions of people in one color and of land in another.

- **Q:** How does Columbus describe the Taíno?
  **A:** As gentle, friendly, well-built people who had no iron weapons; he writes that they would make good servants and could easily be converted to Christianity.
- **Q:** What does that show about his goals?
  **A:** He was looking for gold, lands and people to control for Spain.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Another perspective
*Student:* Read a secondary source about Taíno life and the effects of contact, noting the author's point of view.

*How to teach:* Read the secondary source about Taíno life (farming, villages, canoes, cassava) and the effects of contact; note the author's point of view. Ask what the author wants readers to understand and which words reveal that view.

- **Q:** What happened to the Taíno after contact?
  **A:** Many died from European diseases, violence and forced labor under Spanish rule.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing accounts
*Student:* Complete a chart comparing what each account emphasizes and leaves out, quoting both.

*How to teach:* Chart what each account emphasizes and leaves out, quoting both. Your child needs at least one exact quotation from each source. Ask, 'Whose voice is missing from the journal?'

- **Q:** What does Columbus's journal leave out?
  **A:** The Taíno point of view and the harm that followed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Discussion
*Student:* Discuss 'Why do accounts of the same event differ?' and learn Greek roots graph, tele, geo, chron.

*How to teach:* Discuss why accounts differ (purpose, audience, experience). Teach graph (write), tele (far), geo (earth), chron (time).

- **Q:** What does 'chronology' mean? 'telegraph'?
  **A:** The order of events in time; a device that 'writes from far away' (sends messages over distance).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph analyzing two points of view with quotations, and take a Greek roots quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a point-of-view paragraph with quotations and takes a Greek roots quiz. Review the paragraph against the rubric, then grade the roots quiz.

- **Q:** What does 'geography' mean by its roots?
  **A:** geo (earth) + graph (write) = writing about/describing the earth.

**Assessment:** Point-of-view comparison paragraph; Greek roots quiz.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = explains both points of view, two accurate quotations, explains why they differ (purpose/perspective); 3 = both views with one quotation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate. Greek roots quiz key: graph = write; tele = far; geo = earth; chron = time; e.g., autograph, telescope, geology, chronological.

## Math 5: Dividing decimals

**Prepare:** Place value disks (ones, tenths, hundredths) and hundredths grids. Practice 7.5 ÷ 6 yourself so you can model annexing zeros.

**Watch for:**
- Misplacing the decimal point in the quotient. Write the point in the quotient directly above the dividend's point before dividing.
- Stopping with a remainder. Annex zeros (7.5 = 7.50) and keep dividing.
- Thinking dividing always gives a smaller answer. 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4: there are 4 halves in 2.

### Day 1 (Monday): Decimal / whole number
*Student:* Use disks to share 4.8 among 4 and solve 12 problems like 6.24 / 3 with the written method.

*How to teach:* Share 4.8 (4 ones and 8 tenths disks) among 4 groups: 1 one and 2 tenths each = 1.2. Then show the written method for 6.24 ÷ 3. The child solves 12 problems.

- **Q:** 4.8 ÷ 4 = ?
  **A:** 1.2
- **Q:** 6.24 ÷ 3 = ?
  **A:** 2.08

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decimal / whole number with zeros
*Student:* Solve 10 problems like 7.5 / 6 where zeros must be annexed to the dividend to finish.

*How to teach:* Divide 7.5 ÷ 6: 6 goes into 7 once (1 left), 15 tenths ÷ 6 = 2 R3, annex a zero: 30 hundredths ÷ 6 = 5. The child solves 10 such problems.

- **Q:** 7.5 ÷ 6 = ?
  **A:** 1.25
- **Q:** 3 ÷ 4 = ?
  **A:** 0.75

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dividing by tenths
*Student:* Model 2 / 0.5 as 'how many halves in 2' and solve 10 problems like 3.6 / 0.4 using grids.

*How to teach:* Ask 'how many halves in 2?' and shade it: 4. Then 3.6 ÷ 0.4 as 'how many 4 tenths in 36 tenths' = 9. The child solves 10 problems with grids.

- **Q:** 2 ÷ 0.5 = ?
  **A:** 4
- **Q:** 3.6 ÷ 0.4 = ?
  **A:** 9

### Day 4 (Thursday): Equivalent division
*Student:* Multiply dividend and divisor by 10 or 100 to rewrite 1.44 / 0.12 as 144 / 12 and solve 10 such problems.

*How to teach:* Show that multiplying dividend and divisor by the same power of 10 keeps the quotient: 1.44 ÷ 0.12 = 144 ÷ 12 = 12. The child rewrites and solves 10 problems.

- **Q:** 1.44 ÷ 0.12 = ?
  **A:** 12
- **Q:** 4.5 ÷ 0.15 = ?
  **A:** 450 ÷ 15 = 30

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on 10 decimal division problems and check two of them with multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-problem quiz; the child checks two answers by multiplying quotient × divisor. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Check 6.24 ÷ 3 = 2.08.
  **A:** 2.08 × 3 = 6.24 ✓

**Assessment:** Quiz on decimal division with multiplication checks.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 9.6 ÷ 3 = 3.2 2) 8.45 ÷ 5 = 1.69 3) 0.72 ÷ 8 = 0.09 4) 5.4 ÷ 4 = 1.35 5) 9 ÷ 0.3 = 30 6) 2.4 ÷ 0.6 = 4 7) 6.3 ÷ 0.07 = 90 8) 1.2 ÷ 0.04 = 30 9) 13.5 ÷ 0.5 = 27 10) 0.96 ÷ 0.12 = 8. Two checks shown with multiplication that returns the dividend.

## Science 5: Testing and improving the filter

**Prepare:** Filter prototype, dirty water, measuring cup, timer, graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Changing more than one variable in version 2.
- Believing a clear filter makes water drinkable. Germs remain; disinfection is needed.
- Thinking failure is bad. Failure points guide improvement.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fair test
*Student:* Run the same amount of dirty water through the prototype, measuring clarity, time and volume recovered.

*How to teach:* Run the same volume (e.g., 200 mL) through the prototype. Your child records clarity (1–5), time and volume recovered.

- **Q:** Why use the same amount of dirty water each time?
  **A:** It keeps the test fair so only the design affects the results.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Failure points
*Student:* Identify where the filter failed (leaks, cloudy water, slow flow) and plan one change.

*How to teach:* Your child identifies failure points and plans one change. Look at the data and the filter together. Your child names the one biggest problem and the single change to try.

- **Q:** Water flows too slowly. What could you change?
  **A:** Use less fine material or a thinner sand layer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Redesign
*Student:* Build version 2 changing only one variable and test it under the same conditions.

*How to teach:* Build version 2, changing only one variable, and test under the same conditions. Use the same dirty water mix and volume as the first test. Your child records results in the same table.

- **Q:** Why change only one variable?
  **A:** So you know that change caused any difference in results.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing results
*Student:* Graph results of both versions and discuss why filtered water is still not safe to drink without disinfection.

*How to teach:* Your child graphs both versions and discusses why filtered water still isn't safe. Graph clarity for each version side by side. Explain that germs are too small to be caught by sand and charcoal, so the water still needs boiling or treatment.

- **Q:** Why isn't filtered water safe to drink?
  **A:** Bacteria, viruses and dissolved chemicals can pass through; water needs boiling or disinfection.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the final filter design and write a reflection on how fair tests guided improvements.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the final design and writes a reflection on how fair tests guided improvements. Your child presents in 3–5 minutes with the graph. Ask, 'What would version 3 change?'

- **Q:** What made your tests fair?
  **A:** Same water amount, same dirty water mix, same timing, one change at a time.

**Assessment:** Engineering presentation and data comparison graph.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = explains design, data for both versions graphed, identifies the one change and its effect, explains safety limitation; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = little data; 1 = incomplete. Graph: clarity/time per version with labels.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: New England colonies and religious dissent

**Prepare:** Map of New England colonies, colonial economy cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Puritans believed in religious freedom for everyone.
- Thinking New England was mainly farming.
- Confusing Hooker and Hutchinson.

### Day 1 (Monday): Roger Williams
*Student:* Read about Roger Williams's banishment and the founding of Rhode Island on ideas of religious freedom.

*How to teach:* Read about Roger Williams. Ask: Why would leaders banish someone for his ideas? Your child records his beliefs and how Rhode Island was different.

- **Q:** Why was Williams banished?
  **A:** He argued for separation of church and government and fair dealings with Native peoples.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Anne Hutchinson and Thomas Hooker
*Student:* Compare Anne Hutchinson's and Thomas Hooker's disagreements with Puritan leaders.

*How to teach:* Read short bios of Anne Hutchinson and Thomas Hooker. Your child makes a Venn diagram of their disagreements with Puritan leaders and what each did next.

- **Q:** What did Hooker found?
  **A:** Hartford, Connecticut (1636).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geography and economy
*Student:* Map New England's rocky soil, forests and harbors and explain how they led to fishing, shipbuilding and trade.

*How to teach:* Your child shades rocky soil, forests and harbors on a New England map. Then they explain in writing how these led to fishing, shipbuilding and trade.

- **Q:** Why fishing and shipbuilding?
  **A:** Rocky soil, forests, good harbors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Colonial diary
*Student:* Write a diary entry as a child in a New England town, using 5 accurate details.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm details first (school with a hornbook, chores, Sunday meeting). Your child writes a diary entry with at least 5 accurate details.

- **Q:** Example detail.
  **A:** Attending a long Sunday meeting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label the New England colonies and take a quiz on their founders and reasons.

*How to teach:* Your child labels the New England colonies, then takes the quiz on founders and reasons. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Name the four New England colonies.
  **A:** Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New Hampshire.

**Assessment:** Diary entry and map quiz.

**Answer key:** Diary rubric: 5 accurate details. Map quiz key: MA, RI, CT, NH.

## Art & Music 5: Painting a landscape

**Prepare:** Gather watercolor paper, watercolors, brushes (one large flat, one round), masking tape, salt, and a reference photo or outdoor view. Tape paper edges to the board.

**Watch for:**
- Painting dark colors first in watercolor. Work light to dark; you can't easily lighten watercolor.
- Overworking wet paint. Let layers dry before adding detail.

### Day 1 (Monday): Watercolor techniques
*Student:* Practice wash, wet-on-wet, dry brush and salt texture on a technique sampler.

*How to teach:* On a sampler: flat wash, wet-on-wet (paint on wet paper), dry brush (little water on brush), and salt on wet paint for texture.

- **Q:** What happens when you sprinkle salt on wet watercolor?
  **A:** The salt absorbs pigment and water, creating a speckled starburst texture.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planning
*Student:* Sketch a landscape from a photo or outdoors with foreground, middle ground and background.

*How to teach:* Sketch the landscape lightly with foreground, middle ground and background. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Which ground is closest to the viewer?
  **A:** The foreground.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Background washes
*Student:* Paint the sky and distant layers with light, cool washes.

*How to teach:* Paint the sky and distant layers with light, cool washes. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why use cool, light colors for the background?
  **A:** Atmospheric perspective makes distant things appear lighter and bluer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Foreground detail
*Student:* Paint the middle ground and foreground with warmer, darker colors and detail.

*How to teach:* Paint the middle ground and foreground with warmer, darker colors and detail. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Where should the most detail go?
  **A:** In the foreground, closest to the viewer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Evaluate progress with a checklist and note one area to refine.

*How to teach:* Your child checks: light-to-dark layering, three grounds, atmospheric perspective; notes one area to refine.

- **Q:** What would you refine?
  **A:** Answer varies; e.g., 'darken the foreground trees for more contrast.'

**Assessment:** Technique sampler and painting progress check.

**Answer key:** Technique sampler: four techniques labeled and visible. Progress check: three grounds, background light and cool, foreground darker/warmer with more detail. Proficient = all present.

## PE 5: Shooting, punting and goalkeeping

**Prepare:** Gather a ball, a goal (or two cones 4 m apart), targets for the corners (cones or buckets), and a tape measure. Gloves optional for goalkeeping; use gentle shots. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Shooting with the toe. Strike with the laces (instep) with the toe pointed down for power and control.
- Leaning back when shooting. Leaning back sends the ball high; keep the chest over the ball.

### Day 1 (Monday): Instep shot
*Student:* Practice the instep drive at a goal from 10 m, aiming for corners.

*How to teach:* Model the instep drive: approach at a slight angle, plant foot beside the ball, toe pointed down, strike with the laces, follow through. Shoot from 10 m aiming at corners.

- **Q:** What part of the foot strikes the ball in an instep drive?
  **A:** The laces (top of the foot), toe pointed down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Punting
*Student:* Practice punting for distance and height, measuring the best of 5.

*How to teach:* Teach the punt: hold ball out, drop it, kick with laces before it bounces. Measure the best of 5.

- **Q:** Why must the toe be pointed when punting?
  **A:** So the hard, flat laces make contact and the ball goes up and far instead of off to the side.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Goalkeeping
*Student:* Practice the W catch and scoop as a partner shoots gently.

*How to teach:* Teach the W catch (thumbs and index fingers form a W behind the ball for chest-high or higher shots) and the scoop (low ball: bend, hands behind it, scoop to the chest). Shoot gently at your child.

- **Q:** When do you use a W catch versus a scoop?
  **A:** W catch for balls at chest height or higher; scoop for low or rolling balls.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shooting game
*Student:* Play a 1-v-1 shooting game, rotating goalkeeper.

*How to teach:* Play 1-v-1: one shoots from 10 m, one keeps goal; switch every 3 shots. Walk through the rules and boundaries first, then play; pause once to ask a quick strategy question ('Where is the open space?') and praise fair play and effort as much as skill.

- **Q:** Where is the hardest place for a keeper to save?
  **A:** Low in the corners, far from the keeper's hands.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Shoot 10 times at target zones and record accuracy.

*How to teach:* Your child shoots 10 times at labeled zones (corners and middle) and records hits. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** If 6 of 10 shots hit the target, what is your accuracy?
  **A:** 60%.

**Assessment:** Shooting accuracy record.

**Answer key:** Shooting accuracy record: zones hit out of 10 with percentage calculated correctly. Form cues: plant foot beside the ball, laces contact, toe down, follow-through toward the target. Proficient = 50% or higher and 3 of 4 cues.

## Health 5: Activity, communication and safety

**Prepare:** Get your child's bike helmet and water bottle. Check the heat index if exercising outdoors. Prepare a points chart template.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a helmet just needs to be on your head. It must fit: level on the forehead (two fingers above eyebrows), straps form a V under each ear, chin strap snug (one finger).
- Waiting until thirsty to drink in heat. Drink before, during and after activity.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ask for activity
*Student:* Role-play asking a parent for a family walk or park trip using a clear, respectful request.

*How to teach:* Role-play asking for a family walk: use a clear, respectful request with a reason and a specific time ('Could we walk to the park after dinner? I want to reach my activity goal.').

- **Q:** What makes a request clear and respectful?
  **A:** Calm tone, eye contact, a specific request and reason, and listening to the answer.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Exercise safety
*Student:* List safety rules for heat, hydration, sun and protective gear; check your own helmet fit.

*How to teach:* List safety rules for heat, hydration, sun and protective gear; check helmet fit with the 2-V-1 rule.

- **Q:** Describe the 2-V-1 helmet check.
  **A:** Two fingers above the eyebrows, straps form a V under each ear, one finger fits under the chin strap.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family challenge
*Student:* Design a one-week family activity challenge with a points chart.

*How to teach:* Design a one-week family activity challenge with points. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why might a family challenge help you reach goals?
  **A:** It adds support, fun and accountability.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Retest
*Student:* Retest one fitness baseline from week 9 and compare results.

*How to teach:* Retest one baseline from week 9 and compare. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** If push-ups went from 8 to 11, how much improvement?
  **A:** 3 more push-ups.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a reflection on your activity goal progress and pass a fitness-unit quiz.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on goal progress; take the fitness quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Name two signs of heat illness.
  **A:** Dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps, heavy sweating or confusion.

**Assessment:** Fitness unit quiz and reflection.

**Answer key:** Fitness quiz key: 1) five components 2) 60 minutes 3) talk test 4) helmet 2-V-1 5) heat illness signs and action (stop, shade, water, tell adult). Reflection rubric: 4 = compares data, explains why, and sets next step; 3 = compares data; 2 = general; 1 = missing.
