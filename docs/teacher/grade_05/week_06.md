# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 6

## ELA 5: Hatchet chapters 5–9: first days of survival

**Prepare:** Preview Hatchet chapters 5–9 and a ReadWorks article on wilderness survival. Sticky notes for marking decisions.

**Watch for:**
- Calling every comparison a simile. Simile uses like/as; metaphor says something is something else; personification gives human traits.
- Main idea = topic. The main idea is what the author says about the topic.
- Explaining figurative language literally. Ask 'what does this show about Brian's situation or feelings?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 5–6
*Student:* Read chapters 5–6 and mark three moments where Brian makes a decision; rate each as smart or risky with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5–6; your child marks three decisions Brian makes (drinking lake water, eating berries, choosing a shelter) and rates each smart or risky with evidence. Model one rating with a page number, then your child does the other two. Ask, 'What would a safer choice have been?'

- **Q:** Was it smart for Brian to drink so much lake water?
  **A:** Risky — he drank too much at first and got sick; drinking slowly was safer.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Figurative language
*Student:* Collect 6 similes, metaphors or personifications from chapters 5–7 and explain what each shows about Brian's situation.

*How to teach:* Your child collects 6 examples of figurative language from chapters 5–7 and explains what each shows. Check each label (simile/metaphor/personification).

- **Q:** Label: The wind whispered through the trees.
  **A:** Personification — wind cannot whisper; it suggests a quiet, eerie setting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paired informational text
*Student:* Read a ReadWorks wilderness survival article, identify two main ideas and compare its advice to Brian's choices.

*How to teach:* Read the survival article; identify two main ideas and compare its advice (shelter, water, fire, signaling) with Brian's choices. Have your child underline each piece of advice in the article and put a check if Brian followed it.

- **Q:** How does Brian's choice of shelter compare with survival advice?
  **A:** Survival guides stress finding shelter early; Brian builds a lean-to under a rock overhang near the lake, which follows that advice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapters 8–9
*Student:* Read chapters 8–9 (the porcupine and fire) and write a journal entry on why the hatchet becomes so important.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 8–9 (porcupine and fire). Your child writes a journal entry explaining why the hatchet becomes so important.

- **Q:** How does Brian discover how to make fire?
  **A:** He throws the hatchet at a porcupine in the dark, it hits the rock wall and makes sparks; he then strikes the hatchet on the rock to light birch bark.
- **Q:** Why does the hatchet become so important?
  **A:** It is his only tool: it makes fire and lets him build and hunt.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on chapters 5–9 and figurative language, and practice verb tense to show sequence in 5 sentences.

*How to teach:* Give a chapters 5–9 quiz with figurative language items. Then practice verb tense for sequence in 5 sentences (e.g., After he had found the berries, he ate them).

- **Q:** Combine with tense showing sequence: He found the berries. He got sick.
  **A:** After he had eaten the berries, he got sick.

**Assessment:** Figurative language chart; journal entry using evidence; chapters 5–9 quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (suggested): 1) Brian builds his shelter under a rock overhang 2) the 'gut cherries' make him sick 3) he finds raspberries later 4) a porcupine quills his leg 5) sparks from the hatchet on rock make fire 6) simile, metaphor, personification correctly labeled in 3 examples. Figurative language chart: 6 examples, correct labels, explanation of meaning. Journal entry: claim about the hatchet with at least one quotation.

## Math 5: Numerical expressions and multiplication word problems

**Prepare:** Make expression cards (e.g., (6 + 4) × 3, 6 + 4 × 3, 2 × {5 + [12 − (3 × 2)]}) and gather 4 dice for 'Target 24'. Review order of operations.

**Watch for:**
- Working left to right and ignoring grouping symbols. Highlight the innermost group and do it first.
- Writing 'add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2' as 8 + 7 × 2. Parentheses are needed: (8 + 7) × 2.
- Thinking you must calculate to compare expressions. Point out the shared part (18,932 + 921) and what is done to it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grouping symbols
*Student:* Evaluate 15 expressions with parentheses such as (6 + 4) x 3 vs 6 + 4 x 3 and discuss why the answers differ.

*How to teach:* Evaluate (6 + 4) × 3 and 6 + 4 × 3 side by side and discuss why one is 30 and one is 18. The child evaluates 15 expressions, circling what to do first.

- **Q:** (6 + 4) × 3 vs 6 + 4 × 3?
  **A:** 30 vs 18. Parentheses make you add first; without them you multiply first.
- **Q:** 20 − (8 − 3) = ?
  **A:** 15

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brackets and braces
*Student:* Evaluate nested expressions like 2 x {5 + [12 - (3 x 2)]}, working from the inside out on 12 problems.

*How to teach:* Model working inside out: parentheses, then brackets, then braces. The child rewrites each step on a new line for 12 problems.

- **Q:** 2 × {5 + [12 − (3 × 2)]} = ?
  **A:** 3 × 2 = 6; 12 − 6 = 6; 5 + 6 = 11; 2 × 11 = 22.
- **Q:** [(9 + 3) ÷ 4] × 5 = ?
  **A:** 15

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Writing expressions
*Student:* Translate 12 phrases like 'add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2' into expressions and write phrases for given expressions.

*How to teach:* Translate words to expressions and back, underlining action words (add, then, multiply). The child writes 12 expressions and phrases for given expressions.

- **Q:** Write: add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2.
  **A:** (8 + 7) × 2, which equals 30.
- **Q:** Write a phrase for 4 × (9 − 2).
  **A:** Subtract 2 from 9, then multiply by 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpreting expressions
*Student:* Explain without calculating why 3 x (18,932 + 921) is three times as large as 18,932 + 921, then solve 5 multi-step multiplication word problems.

*How to teach:* Ask why 3 × (18,932 + 921) is three times as large as 18,932 + 921 without calculating (it is the same sum tripled). Then the child solves 5 multi-step multiplication word problems with labeled answers.

- **Q:** Without calculating, compare 5 × (234 + 76) to 234 + 76.
  **A:** It is 5 times as large because the same sum is multiplied by 5.
- **Q:** Tickets cost $14. A family buys 3 adult and 4 child tickets at the same price. Write and solve an expression.
  **A:** 14 × (3 + 4) = 14 × 7 = $98.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play 'Target 24' with dice to build expressions, then take a 12-item quiz on evaluating and writing expressions.

*How to teach:* Play 'Target 24': roll four dice and build an expression with grouping symbols equal to 24. Then give the 12-item quiz.

- **Q:** Using 2, 3, 4, 6 once each, make 24.
  **A:** One answer: 6 × 4 × (3 − 2) = 24. Another: (6 + 2) × (4 − 3) × 3 = 24. Accept any correct expression that uses each number once.

**Assessment:** Quiz on evaluating and writing numerical expressions; word problem solutions with labeled answers.

**Answer key:** Suggested 12-item key: 1) 4 + 5 × 2 = 14 2) (4 + 5) × 2 = 18 3) 30 − (12 + 6) = 12 4) 48 ÷ (4 × 3) = 4 5) 3 × [10 − (2 + 5)] = 9 6) {20 − [3 × (8 − 5)]} × 2 = 22 7) 'multiply 6 by 5, then add 4' = 6 × 5 + 4 8) 'subtract 3 from 11, then divide by 2' = (11 − 3) ÷ 2 9) phrase for 7 × (2 + 9): add 2 and 9, then multiply by 7 10) (1,284 + 37) × 4 is 4 times 1,284 + 37 11) 2 × (15 + 25) = 80 12) [6 × (4 + 1)] − 10 = 20. Word problems: full credit requires an expression, correct computation and a labeled unit.

## Science 5: Conservation of weight when melting and freezing

**Prepare:** Kitchen scale (grams), ice cubes, sealed zip bags (double-bag), chocolate chips, warm-water bowl, graph paper. Wipe bags dry before every weighing.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking melted water weighs less because it 'shrinks.' Mass stays the same; ice actually takes up more space than the same water.
- Counting condensation on the outside of the bag. Dry the bag first or the scale shows extra water.
- Treating a 1 g difference as proof. Small differences are measurement error.

### Day 1 (Monday): Prediction
*Student:* Predict whether a sealed bag of ice will weigh more, less or the same after melting, and explain the prediction.

*How to teach:* Ask: Will a sealed bag of ice weigh more, less or the same after melting? Your child writes a prediction and reason.

- **Q:** Predict the weight of a sealed bag of ice after it melts.
  **A:** The same — no particles enter or leave the sealed bag.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Melting test
*Student:* Weigh 3 sealed bags of ice, let them melt and reweigh, recording data in a table.

*How to teach:* Weigh 3 sealed bags of ice, let them melt, dry the outsides, and reweigh. Record in a table.

- **Q:** A bag of ice weighs 152 g; after melting it weighs 152 g. What does this show?
  **A:** Mass is conserved during melting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Freezing test
*Student:* Weigh sealed bags of water, freeze overnight and reweigh; also melt chocolate chips in a sealed bag in warm water and reweigh.

*How to teach:* Weigh bags of water and freeze overnight. Also melt chocolate chips in a sealed bag in warm water and reweigh.

- **Q:** Does freezing change weight?
  **A:** No — the water freezes but the weight stays the same.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graphing
*Student:* Create a bar graph comparing before and after weights for all trials and calculate any differences.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a bar graph comparing before and after weights for all trials and calculates differences. Help set a y-axis scale that starts at a sensible number so small differences are visible. Ask: 'Are any differences bigger than 1–2 g? What could cause them?'

- **Q:** If one trial shows a 1 g difference, what could explain it?
  **A:** Measurement error: a water drop on the outside, scale precision, or a tiny leak.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER on whether weight is conserved during melting and freezing, explaining small measurement errors.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER on whether weight is conserved, explaining small errors. Prompt: 'What evidence from your table supports your claim?' The reasoning should say the same particles stayed sealed in the bag.

- **Q:** Write the claim.
  **A:** Weight is conserved during melting and freezing.

**Assessment:** Bar graph and CER on conservation.

**Answer key:** CER rubric: 4 = clear, accurate claim; specific evidence with numbers from the data; reasoning that links evidence to the science idea (particles, conservation, etc.); 3 = accurate claim and evidence, reasoning partial; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or inaccurate. Bar graph: paired bars before/after for each trial, labeled axes in grams, title. Proficient CER explains that the same particles remain in the sealed bag.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Columbus and the Spanish in the Americas

**Prepare:** Columbus's October 12, 1492 journal excerpt (National Archives or Library of Congress education pages) and a map of Coronado's and de Soto's routes. Preview materials on forced labor and disease.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Columbus 'discovered' an empty land. Millions of people already lived in the Americas.
- Thinking the Columbian Exchange was only goods. It included diseases that devastated Native populations.
- Confusing Coronado (Southwest) and de Soto (Southeast).

### Day 1 (Monday): Primary source: Columbus's journal
*Student:* Read the October 12, 1492 journal entry and note what Columbus observed and what he wanted.

*How to teach:* Read the journal excerpt aloud. Your child lists what Columbus observed and what he wanted.

- **Q:** What did Columbus want?
  **A:** Gold, land for Spain, and to convert people to Christianity; he wrote the Taíno would make good servants.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Taíno perspective
*Student:* Read about Taíno society and the effects of disease and forced labor after contact, and compare to Columbus's account.

*How to teach:* Read about Taíno society and the impact of contact. Your child compares to Columbus's account in a T-chart.

- **Q:** What happened to the Taíno population?
  **A:** It fell drastically due to disease, violence and forced labor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coronado and de Soto
*Student:* Trace the routes of Coronado and de Soto on a map and record their goals, obstacles and results.

*How to teach:* Trace Coronado's (1540–42) and de Soto's (1539–42) routes. Your child records goals, obstacles and results.

- **Q:** What was Coronado searching for?
  **A:** The legendary Seven Cities of Gold (Cíbola); he found none.
- **Q:** What did de Soto reach?
  **A:** The Mississippi River; he died on the expedition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Columbian Exchange
*Student:* Sort plants, animals and diseases that crossed the Atlantic in each direction and explain one lasting effect.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts items that crossed the Atlantic each way and explains one lasting effect. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Name items from the Americas to Europe and from Europe to the Americas.
  **A:** Americas → Europe: corn, potatoes, tomatoes, cacao. Europe → Americas: horses, cattle, wheat, smallpox.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph evaluating Columbus's legacy from two perspectives.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph evaluating Columbus's legacy from two perspectives with evidence. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Why do people disagree about Columbus?
  **A:** He began lasting contact between continents, but his actions led to suffering for Native peoples.

**Assessment:** Perspective paragraph and route map.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence, three or more accurate, specific details or quotations from sources, explanation of how they support the point, conclusion; 3 = accurate with two details; 2 = general or partly inaccurate; 1 = off topic or incomplete. Route map: Coronado (Southwest to Kansas) and de Soto (Southeast across the Mississippi) correctly traced.

## Art & Music 5: Syncopation, dotted rhythms and body percussion

**Prepare:** Open Chrome Music Lab Rhythm. Gather buckets or pots and wooden spoons. Find recordings of 'America the Beautiful' and Scott Joplin's 'The Entertainer' or 'Maple Leaf Rag.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a dot adds one beat. A dot adds half of the note's value (dotted half = 3 beats; dotted quarter = 1½ beats).
- Rushing improvised solos. An improvised solo must still fit the steady beat and the length (4 measures).

### Day 1 (Monday): Dotted rhythms
*Student:* Learn dotted half and dotted quarter notes and clap patterns from 'America the Beautiful.'

*How to teach:* Teach dotted half (3 beats) and dotted quarter (1½ beats, usually followed by an eighth). Clap 'O beau-ti-ful for spa-cious skies' noticing the long-short pattern.

- **Q:** How many beats is a dotted half note? A dotted quarter?
  **A:** Dotted half = 3 beats; dotted quarter = 1½ beats.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Syncopation
*Student:* Clap and say syncopated patterns (ta ti-ta ti) and find syncopation in a song like 'Ragtime' by Scott Joplin.

*How to teach:* Clap syncopated patterns (ti-ta-ti: eighth-quarter-eighth); listen for off-beat accents in Joplin's rag.

- **Q:** What is syncopation?
  **A:** Accenting a weak beat or off-beat instead of the strong beat, which gives music a 'bouncy' feel.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Body percussion ostinato
*Student:* Create a 4-beat body percussion ostinato (stomp, pat, clap, snap) and perform it with a recording.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a 4-beat body percussion ostinato (e.g., stomp-pat-clap-snap) and performs it with a recording.

- **Q:** What is an ostinato?
  **A:** A short pattern repeated over and over.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bucket drumming
*Student:* Improvise 4-measure rhythm solos on a bucket or pot in a call-and-response with a family member.

*How to teach:* Take turns: you play 4 measures, your child answers with an improvised 4 measures on a bucket.

- **Q:** What is improvisation?
  **A:** Making up music on the spot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the ostinato and an improvised solo and evaluate using a steady-beat rubric.

*How to teach:* Your child performs the ostinato and an improvised solo; evaluate with a steady-beat rubric.

- **Q:** Why is a steady beat important in a performance with others?
  **A:** It keeps everyone together in time.

**Assessment:** Performance of ostinato and improvisation.

**Answer key:** Steady-beat rubric: 4 = ostinato repeats correctly at least 8 times on beat, solo is exactly 4 measures with a steady beat and varied rhythms; 3 = minor slips; 2 = beat drifts often; 1 = no steady beat.

## PE 5: Advanced rope tricks and long rope

**Prepare:** Gather individual ropes plus a long rope (about 4–5 m; or tie two ropes together). If you lack a second turner, tie one end to a fence or tree at waist height. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Crossing the arms at the elbows only for criss-cross. Cross deeply so the hands reach the opposite hips, making a big loop.
- Running straight at the long rope. Enter from the side at an angle, right after the rope passes the top and hits the ground.

### Day 1 (Monday): Criss-cross
*Student:* Learn the criss-cross and side swing open, practicing with feedback cues.

*How to teach:* Model criss-cross: on the downswing, cross arms to opposite hips, jump through, then open. Teach the side swing (rope swings beside the body with hands together) followed by opening into a jump.

- **Q:** Where should your hands go in a criss-cross?
  **A:** Cross deeply so each hand is near the opposite hip.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Double under attempts
*Student:* Practice high jumps with fast wrists to attempt double unders.

*How to teach:* Practice 'power jumps' (high, tight jumps) with fast wrist turns. Try one double under after several singles; celebrate attempts.

- **Q:** What two things make a double under possible?
  **A:** Jumping a bit higher and turning the rope much faster with the wrists, so it passes under twice in one jump.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Long rope
*Student:* With two turners (family or a tree tie-off), practice entering, jumping 10 and exiting a long rope.

*How to teach:* With two turners, practice: stand near a turner, wait for the rope to hit the ground and go up, run in to the middle, jump 10, and run out the side diagonally.

- **Q:** When is the right moment to enter a long rope?
  **A:** Right after the rope hits the ground and starts moving away/up ('front door' entry).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peer coaching
*Student:* Coach a family member on one trick using critical cues and encouragement.

*How to teach:* Your child picks one trick and coaches you using two cues and encouragement ('Elbows in! Great rhythm!').

- **Q:** Give one cue and one encouraging comment for the skier.
  **A:** Cue: 'Keep feet together and jump side to side over a line.' Encouragement: 'Nice steady rhythm—keep going!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform 5 different tricks and record which ones you mastered.

*How to teach:* Your child performs five different tricks; mark mastered (5 in a row) or developing on a chart.

- **Q:** Which trick was hardest, and what will you practice?
  **A:** Answer varies; a strong answer names the trick and a specific cue to practice (e.g., 'criss-cross—cross deeper').

**Assessment:** Trick mastery chart.

**Answer key:** Trick mastery chart: list ≥5 tricks (e.g., basic, skier, bell, criss-cross, side swing, long-rope entry). Mastered = 5 consecutive successful jumps. 4 = 5+ tricks mastered; 3 = 4 mastered; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.

## Health 5: Reading Nutrition Facts labels

**Prepare:** Collect 5 cereal boxes and a measuring cup. Print a blank Nutrition Facts label for labeling. Review names for sugar: sucrose, corn syrup, high-fructose corn syrup, dextrose, cane juice, honey, molasses.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the label amounts are for the whole box. Amounts are per serving; multiply if you eat more.
- Thinking 'wheat flour' means whole grain. Look for the word 'whole' (whole wheat, whole grain oats).

### Day 1 (Monday): Label anatomy
*Student:* Label every part of a Nutrition Facts panel on a printout and define each in your own words.

*How to teach:* Your child labels each part: serving size, servings per container, calories, %DV, nutrients to limit, nutrients to get more of, footnote.

- **Q:** Where should you look first on a Nutrition Facts label?
  **A:** The serving size and servings per container.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Serving size test
*Student:* Pour your usual cereal, measure it, and compare it to the label's serving size; recalculate sugar for your real portion.

*How to teach:* Pour a usual bowl of cereal, measure it, compare to serving size and recalculate sugar. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Serving size is 1 cup with 12 g sugar. You eat 2 cups. How much sugar?
  **A:** 24 g (about 6 teaspoons).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ingredient lists
*Student:* Read ingredient lists in order of weight and identify whole grains and different names for sugar.

*How to teach:* Read ingredient lists, which are in order by weight (most first); circle whole grains and sugars.

- **Q:** What does it mean if sugar is the first ingredient?
  **A:** There is more sugar by weight than any other ingredient.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cereal compare
*Student:* Compare 5 cereals for fiber, added sugar and sodium and use the decision steps to choose the healthiest.

*How to teach:* Compare 5 cereals for fiber, sugar and sodium; use the decision steps to choose. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Which cereal is healthiest: high fiber/low sugar or low fiber/high sugar?
  **A:** High fiber and low added sugar.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a label-reading test using two unfamiliar products.

*How to teach:* Give a test with two unfamiliar products. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** A label says 2.5 servings per container. If you eat the whole container with 150 calories per serving, how many calories?
  **A:** 375 calories.

**Assessment:** Label-reading test.

**Answer key:** Label test: correctly identifies serving size, calories, %DV, nutrients to limit (saturated fat, sodium, added sugars), nutrients to get more of (fiber, vitamin D, calcium, iron, potassium), recalculates for a different portion, names whole grains and sugars in ingredients. Proficient = 80%.
