# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 26

## ELA 5: Publishing and debating

**Prepare:** Computer, debate note cards, timer. Arrange a family member or co-op partner to debate.

**Watch for:**
- Attacking the person instead of the argument.
- Reading the essay word for word as a speech.
- Using slang in a formal debate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final copy
*Student:* Type and format the final opinion essay with a title and source list.

*How to teach:* Your child types the essay in one sitting, adds a title and a source list at the end, and proofreads a printed copy.

- **Q:** Where does the source list go?
  **A:** At the end.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Debate prep
*Student:* Turn the essay into a 2-minute opening statement on note cards and prepare rebuttals to 2 likely counterarguments.

*How to teach:* Show how to boil each paragraph into a key phrase on a card. Your child practices the 2-minute opening with a timer and writes a rebuttal for 2 counterarguments.

- **Q:** What is a rebuttal?
  **A:** A response that answers the other side's argument.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Debate
*Student:* Debate the question with a family member or co-op partner using formal English and timed turns.

*How to teach:* Set rules: opening statements, rebuttals, closings, each timed. Model formal phrases ('I respectfully disagree because…') and note your child's use of evidence.

- **Q:** Formal: 'That's dumb.'
  **A:** I disagree with that point because…

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection
*Student:* Summarize the opponent's best reasons and write whether they changed your thinking.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the opponent's 2 best reasons fairly, then a paragraph on whether their own thinking changed and why.

- **Q:** Why summarize the other side fairly?
  **A:** It shows understanding and helps you respond better.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take a cold-read assessment analyzing reasons and evidence and write an on-demand opinion paragraph in 30 minutes.

*How to teach:* Give an unfamiliar opinion article with questions, then set a 30-minute timer for an on-demand opinion paragraph. Do not help; score both with the rubric.

- **Q:** What does a strong on-demand paragraph include?
  **A:** A clear opinion, 2–3 reasons with evidence, and a concluding sentence.

**Assessment:** Opinion essay rubric; debate observation; Unit 6 on-demand writing.

**Answer key:** Opinion essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = clear opinion, 3 well-supported reasons, counterclaim, logical transitions, strong conclusion, source list, few errors; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = weak support; 1 = incomplete. Debate observation: formal language, stayed in time, rebuttals relevant. Unit 6 on-demand: opinion + 2 reasons with evidence + conclusion = proficient.

## Math 5: Composite figures and volume design project

**Prepare:** Cardstock, tape, rulers, unit cubes. Draw 8 L-shaped/step figures with dimensions labeled. Write Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Double-counting the overlap when decomposing. Draw a line to split the figure into two non-overlapping prisms.
- Using the whole figure's length for both parts. Find each part's own dimensions by subtracting.
- Thinking same volume means same amount of cardboard. Compare surface area of designs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Composite figures
*Student:* Decompose 8 L-shaped and step-shaped figures into two prisms and add their volumes.

*How to teach:* Split an L-shape into two rectangular prisms, find each volume and add. Show it can be split two ways with the same total.

- **Q:** An L-shape: one prism 5 × 4 × 2 and another 3 × 4 × 2. Total volume?
  **A:** 40 + 24 = 64 cubic units.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Real-world composite volume
*Student:* Find the volume of a two-level planter box and a staircase made of blocks.

*How to teach:* Find volumes of a two-level planter (e.g., bottom 6 × 2 × 1 ft, top 3 × 2 × 1 ft) and a staircase of blocks. Have your child sketch each figure and draw a line where it splits into two prisms. They label each part's dimensions before computing.

- **Q:** Planter: bottom 6 × 2 × 1 ft, top 3 × 2 × 1 ft. Volume?
  **A:** 12 + 6 = 18 cubic ft.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Design challenge
*Student:* Design 3 different boxes with a volume of exactly 72 cubic inches and choose the one using the least cardboard.

*How to teach:* List boxes with V = 72 cubic inches (6 × 4 × 3, 6 × 6 × 2, 8 × 3 × 3, 9 × 4 × 2…). Compute the cardboard (surface area: 2 × (lw + lh + wh)) and choose the least.

- **Q:** Which uses less cardboard: 6 × 4 × 3 or 6 × 6 × 2?
  **A:** 6 × 4 × 3 uses 108 sq in; 6 × 6 × 2 uses 120 sq in. The 6 × 4 × 3 box.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build and present
*Student:* Build the chosen box from cardstock and present its dimensions, volume calculation and design reasoning.

*How to teach:* Build the chosen box from cardstock and present dimensions, volume and reasoning. Your child draws a net of the box first, then cuts, folds and tapes it. In the presentation they show the 3 designs and why they chose this one.

- **Q:** Show your box holds 72 cubic inches.
  **A:** Example: 6 × 4 × 3 = 72.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on volume concepts, formulas and composite figures.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Volume of a 4 × 4 × 4 cube plus a 4 × 2 × 4 prism?
  **A:** 64 + 32 = 96 cubic units.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test and box design project rubric.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test key (suggested): 1) 3 × 5 × 2 = 30 2) B = 18, h = 4 → 72 3) V = 150, l = 6, w = 5 → h = 5 4) composite 4×3×2 + 2×3×2 = 24 + 12 = 36 5) cube edge 3 = 27 6) explain volume as cubic units. Project rubric (4 levels): 4 = box measures exactly 72 cu in, three designs compared with surface area, clear presentation; 3 = correct volume, comparison mostly correct; 2 = volume error or no comparison; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 5: Human impact on land, water and air

**Prepare:** NASA Climate Kids and EPA kids pages; index cards and petroleum jelly; gloves for trash audit; scale.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking renewable resources can't be overused.
- Thinking recycling solves all waste.
- Thinking air pollution is only from factories.

### Day 1 (Monday): Resources
*Student:* Sort 15 resources as renewable or nonrenewable and discuss how each is used in your home.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 15 resources as renewable or nonrenewable. Use resource cards. Your child sorts and explains how each is used at home.

- **Q:** Is natural gas renewable?
  **A:** No — it's nonrenewable (fossil fuel).
- **Q:** Is solar energy renewable?
  **A:** Yes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Air quality
*Student:* Coat index cards with petroleum jelly, place them indoors and outdoors, and check particulates at week's end.

*How to teach:* Coat cards with petroleum jelly; place indoors and outdoors. Place one card indoors and one outdoors for the week. Your child predicts which will collect more.

- **Q:** What will the cards collect?
  **A:** Dust, pollen, soot — particulates in the air.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trash audit
*Student:* Weigh and sort one day of household trash into recycling, compost, landfill and hazardous categories.

*How to teach:* Weigh and sort one day of trash. Wear gloves; weigh each category on a scale. Your child records in a table and graphs.

- **Q:** Which category is largest?
  **A:** Varies; discuss how to reduce it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reading about impacts
*Student:* Read two articles about human impacts (plastic in oceans, groundwater overuse) and take notes.

*How to teach:* Read two impact articles; take notes. Read together. Your child notes the problem, cause and a possible solution for each.

- **Q:** How does plastic harm the ocean?
  **A:** Animals eat it or get tangled; it breaks into microplastics.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Examine the particulate cards with a magnifier and write a summary of human impacts on two spheres.

*How to teach:* Examine cards; write a summary of human impacts on two spheres. Examine cards with a magnifier. Your child writes a summary of human impacts on two spheres.

- **Q:** Name one human impact on the atmosphere.
  **A:** Burning fossil fuels releases pollution and carbon dioxide.

**Assessment:** Trash audit data and impact summary.

**Answer key:** Trash audit: categories weighed. Summary rubric: 4 = two spheres, specific impacts with evidence; 3 = mostly; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Women, families and the home front

**Prepare:** Abigail Adams's 'Remember the Ladies' letter (1776); Continental currency image.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking women were absent from the war.
- Thinking Molly Pitcher is documented.
- Thinking inflation doesn't matter.

### Day 1 (Monday): Remember the Ladies
*Student:* Read Abigail Adams's 1776 letter and John's reply and discuss what she asked for.

*How to teach:* Read Abigail's letter and John's reply; discuss. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** What did Abigail ask?
  **A:** To remember women's rights in new laws.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Women in the war
*Student:* Research Martha Washington, Molly Pitcher, Phillis Wheatley and Mercy Otis Warren and create a 'women of the Revolution' chart.

*How to teach:* Your child researches four women and fills a chart. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Who was Mercy Otis Warren?
  **A:** A writer and historian.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inflation
*Student:* Use a simple price table to model how Continental dollars lost value ('not worth a Continental').

*How to teach:* Use a price table to model inflation. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** What does 'not worth a Continental' mean?
  **A:** Worthless.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Freedom for whom?
*Student:* Read about Elizabeth Freeman (Mum Bett) suing for freedom in Massachusetts and discuss how Declaration ideals changed views on slavery.

*How to teach:* Read about Elizabeth Freeman; discuss ideals and slavery. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** What did she win?
  **A:** Her freedom in 1781.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a letter as a family member on the home front describing hardships with accurate details.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a home-front letter. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Hardship?
  **A:** Shortages, high prices.

**Assessment:** Home-front letter and women's chart.

**Answer key:** Letter: accurate details. Chart: roles.

## Art & Music 5: Clay or salt dough sculpture: planning and building

**Prepare:** Gather air-dry clay (or salt dough: 2 cups flour, 1 cup salt, about ¾ cup water, kneaded smooth), a plastic knife, fork or toothpick for scoring, a water cup, a placemat, and plastic wrap or a zip bag. Make a little 'slip' by mixing clay with water to a yogurt-like paste.

**Watch for:**
- Joining pieces by just pressing them together. Without scoring and slip, pieces crack apart as they dry.
- Making walls very uneven. Thin spots crack and thick spots dry slowly; aim for walls about as thick as a pencil.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pinch pot
*Student:* Make a pinch pot and practice even wall thickness.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate: roll a ball, press your thumb into the center, then pinch while turning the ball a little each time. Your child makes a pinch pot and checks wall thickness by feel.

- **Q:** How do you keep pinch-pot walls even?
  **A:** Pinch with the same pressure while rotating the pot a little after each pinch, working from the bottom up.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Coils and slabs
*Student:* Make a coil and a slab test piece and join pieces using score and slip.

*How to teach:* Roll a coil (even 'snake') and a slab (flattened with a rolling pin). Join two pieces: scratch both surfaces, add slip, press and smooth the seam.

- **Q:** What are the steps of score and slip?
  **A:** Scratch (score) both surfaces, add slip (wet clay paste), press together, and smooth the seam.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Planning
*Student:* Sketch a sculpture (animal vessel or expressive figure) combining ideas from two inspirations.

*How to teach:* Your child sketches an animal vessel or expressive figure that combines ideas from two inspirations (e.g., a pinch-pot owl inspired by Pueblo pottery and a pet), labeling which method builds each part.

- **Q:** Which method would you use for a tall, round vessel?
  **A:** Stacked coils (or a pinch pot base with coils added on).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Building
*Student:* Build the sculpture's main form using the chosen methods.

*How to teach:* Your child builds the main form, joining every attachment with score and slip. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why should solid parts be no thicker than about an inch?
  **A:** Thick parts dry slowly and may crack; thinner, even parts dry evenly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Add texture and details and cover with plastic; note what to finish next week.

*How to teach:* Add texture with a fork, toothpick or pressed objects; wrap tightly in plastic and write next steps.

- **Q:** Why wrap the unfinished sculpture in plastic?
  **A:** To keep it from drying out so you can keep working on it next time.

**Assessment:** Technique test pieces and sculpture plan.

**Answer key:** Technique test pieces: 4 = pinch pot with even walls, smooth coil, flat slab, and a joint that holds when gently tugged; 3 = one weakness; 2 = joints fail or walls very uneven; 1 = incomplete. Sculpture plan: sketch shows two inspirations and labels building methods.

## PE 5: Paddle and racket skills

**Prepare:** Gather paddles (or paper plates taped to paint sticks), foam or wiffle balls, and tape a line on a wall about 1 m high. Set a low rope about 60 cm high for partner rallies. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Gripping like a frying pan. Use the 'shake hands' grip so you can hit both forehand and backhand.
- Swinging hard. Control comes from a short, smooth swing and a firm wrist.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grip and control
*Student:* Learn the shake-hands grip and bounce a ball on the paddle 25 times.

*How to teach:* Teach the shake-hands grip; bounce the ball up on the paddle 25 times. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What is the shake-hands grip?
  **A:** Hold the handle as if shaking hands, with the paddle face like an extension of the palm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forehand
*Student:* Hit forehands against a wall above a tape line, counting consecutive hits.

*How to teach:* Forehands against the wall above the line; count consecutive hits. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** For a forehand, which side of the body is the ball on?
  **A:** The same side as your hitting hand (palm-side).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Backhand
*Student:* Practice backhands against the wall and alternate forehand and backhand.

*How to teach:* Backhands against the wall, then alternate forehand and backhand. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Which side of the paddle hits a backhand?
  **A:** The back-of-hand side, with the ball on the non-dominant side of the body.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner rally
*Student:* Rally with a partner over a low rope, focusing on soft control.

*How to teach:* Rally with a partner over the low rope, soft control. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why use soft hits in a cooperative rally?
  **A:** Softer controlled hits are easier to return, so the rally lasts longer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how paddle angle changes the ball's direction and demonstrate.

*How to teach:* Your child explains and demonstrates how paddle angle changes direction. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** If the paddle face is tilted up when you hit, where does the ball go?
  **A:** Upward/higher; tilted down sends it lower; angled sideways sends it that direction.

**Assessment:** Wall-hit counts and demonstration.

**Answer key:** Wall-hit counts (forehand, backhand, alternating) recorded. Demonstration: open face → ball goes up, closed face → down, angled face → sideways in that direction. Proficient = 10+ consecutive forehands and correct explanation.

## Health 5: Tobacco advertising and refusal skills

**Prepare:** Find historical cigarette ads (Stanford Research into the Impact of Tobacco Advertising collection) and modern vape ads/social media images. Write 4 refusal role-play scenarios (older kid at the park offers a vape; friend at a sleepover; cousin; online).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking saying no means losing friends. Real friends respect your choices; you can suggest another activity.
- Confusing assertive with aggressive. Assertive is firm and respectful; aggressive is hostile.

### Day 1 (Monday): Historic ads
*Student:* Compare historical cigarette ads with today's vape marketing and identify persuasion techniques.

*How to teach:* Compare old cigarette ads with today's vape marketing; identify techniques (celebrity, glamour, doctors, flavors, freedom).

- **Q:** What technique did old cigarette ads use by showing doctors?
  **A:** Health claims/authority (testimonial) to suggest smoking was safe.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Refusal steps
*Student:* Learn refusal strategies: say no clearly, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away.

*How to teach:* Teach refusal steps: say no clearly, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Give an example of a complete refusal.
  **A:** 'No thanks, I don't vape. It's bad for my lungs. Let's go shoot hoops instead.' Then walk away if needed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Role-play
*Student:* Role-play 4 scenarios refusing a vape from an older kid or friend.

*How to teach:* Role-play the 4 scenarios; switch roles. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What if they keep pressuring you?
  **A:** Repeat 'no' firmly (broken record) and leave.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Assertive vs. passive
*Student:* Practice assertive body language and tone compared with passive and aggressive responses.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate passive, aggressive and assertive tone and body language. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Describe assertive body language.
  **A:** Stand tall, eye contact, calm firm voice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a refusal role-play for a parent and take the tobacco unit test.

*How to teach:* Perform a refusal role-play and take the unit test. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Why is nicotine harmful to young people?
  **A:** It is highly addictive and harms the developing brain (attention, learning, impulse control).

**Assessment:** Refusal role-play and unit test.

**Answer key:** Role-play rubric (1–4 each): clear 'no', reason, alternative, assertive voice/body, walks away if needed. Unit test key: nicotine addictive, tar, CO, vape aerosol contents, flavors target youth, most teens don't vape, secondhand smoke, refusal steps, legal age 21. Proficient = 80%.
