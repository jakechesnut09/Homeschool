# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 19

## ELA 5: Esperanza Rising: arrival in California – Los Melones through Las Almendras

**Prepare:** Preview chapters 'Los Melones' through 'Las Almendras' in Esperanza Rising. Venn diagram ready.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking correlative conjunctions can be mixed (either/nor). They come in fixed pairs.
- Seeing Esperanza's attitude change as sudden. It is gradual and has setbacks.
- Subject-verb agreement with neither/nor: the verb agrees with the closer subject.

### Day 1 (Monday): Los Melones
*Student:* Read 'Los Melones' (the train journey) and discuss how Esperanza treats Isabel and the peasant girl.

*How to teach:* Read 'Los Melones' (the train). Discuss Esperanza's treatment of the peasant girl and her attitude toward riding with poor people.

- **Q:** How does Esperanza treat the peasant girl who wants to touch her doll?
  **A:** She pulls the doll away; Mama is ashamed and makes her see that she was unkind.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Las Cebollas
*Student:* Read 'Las Cebollas' and compare Esperanza's old and new homes in a Venn diagram.

*How to teach:* Read 'Las Cebollas'; compare old and new homes in a Venn diagram. Your child fills each side of the Venn diagram with at least 3 details and finds 2 things that stayed the same.

- **Q:** How is her new home different?
  **A:** A small cabin in a farm labor camp with shared bathrooms, instead of a large ranch house with servants.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Correlative conjunctions
*Student:* Learn either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also and write 8 sentences about the novel using them.

*How to teach:* Teach either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also; your child writes 8 sentences about the novel. Write the four pairs on cards; your child matches them, then writes 8 sentences about the novel using each pair twice.

- **Q:** Complete: Esperanza knew neither how to sweep ___ how to change a diaper.
  **A:** nor

### Day 4 (Thursday): Las Almendras
*Student:* Read 'Las Almendras' and write a paragraph on what Esperanza learns from sweeping the platform.

*How to teach:* Read 'Las Almendras'; your child writes a paragraph on what Esperanza learns from sweeping the platform. Discuss how it felt to be laughed at; then your child writes a paragraph with a claim and two pieces of evidence.

- **Q:** What does Esperanza learn?
  **A:** She learns that she lacks practical skills and that she must work and learn to survive; embarrassment teaches her humility.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Discuss in a book talk how Esperanza is changing, then take a quiz on chapters and correlative conjunctions.

*How to teach:* Book talk on how Esperanza is changing; quiz on chapters and correlative conjunctions. In the book talk, your child names one way Esperanza has changed and one way she has not yet changed.

- **Q:** Who is Isabel?
  **A:** Hortensia and Alfonso's niece — Juan and Josefina's young daughter who admires Esperanza.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram; paragraph with evidence; grammar quiz.

**Answer key:** Venn: 3+ details each side and shared. Paragraph: claim about what she learns, 2 pieces of evidence. Grammar quiz key: either/or, neither/nor, both/and, not only/but also used correctly in 8 sentences.

## Math 5: Multiplying a fraction and a whole number

**Prepare:** 24 counters (beans, coins), paper for tape diagrams.

**Watch for:**
- Dividing by the numerator instead of the denominator (3/4 of 24 = 24 ÷ 3). Split into 4 equal groups (denominator), take 3 of them.
- Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the whole number (6 × 2/3 = 12/18). Only the numerator is multiplied.
- Leaving improper answers. Simplify 12/3 to 4.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fraction of a set
*Student:* Use 24 counters to find 1/3, 2/3, 3/4 and 5/6 of the set and write each as multiplication.

*How to teach:* Split 24 counters into 3 equal groups: 1/3 = 8, 2/3 = 16. Repeat with fourths and sixths. Write each as multiplication (2/3 × 24 = 16).

- **Q:** 3/4 of 24?
  **A:** 18
- **Q:** 5/6 of 24?
  **A:** 20

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tape diagrams
*Student:* Draw tape diagrams to solve 10 problems like 3/5 x 40.

*How to teach:* Draw a tape for 40 split into 5 parts of 8; shade 3 parts = 24. The child solves 10 problems.

- **Q:** 3/5 × 40 = ?
  **A:** 24
- **Q:** 2/7 × 35 = ?
  **A:** 10

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Whole x fraction
*Student:* Solve 12 problems like 6 x 2/3 using repeated addition and then (6 x 2)/3.

*How to teach:* Show 6 × 2/3 as 2/3 added 6 times = 12/3 = 4, then (6 × 2)/3. The child solves 12 problems both ways.

- **Q:** 6 × 2/3 = ?
  **A:** 12/3 = 4
- **Q:** 5 × 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 15/4 = 3 3/4

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems such as 'Maria read 3/4 of a 220-page book; how many pages did she read?'

*How to teach:* Draw a tape diagram for each story problem. The child solves 6 problems with labeled answers.

- **Q:** Maria read 3/4 of a 220-page book. How many pages?
  **A:** 220 ÷ 4 = 55; 55 × 3 = 165 pages.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on fraction-by-whole-number multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently; let the child use tape diagrams. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** 2/3 × 18 = ?
  **A:** 12

**Assessment:** Quiz on multiplying fractions and whole numbers.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 1/4 × 32 = 8 2) 3/8 × 40 = 15 3) 4 × 2/5 = 8/5 = 1 3/5 4) 7 × 1/3 = 2 1/3 5) 5/6 × 36 = 30 6) 9 × 2/3 = 6 7) 3/10 of 150 students = 45 8) 3 × 3/4 cup = 2 1/4 cups.

## Science 5: Ecosystem balance and invasive species

**Prepare:** Articles on quagga mussels or Argentine ants in California; graph paper; space for an 'Oh Deer' game (cards labeled food, water, shelter). Write the Unit 5 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking invasive species are always bigger predators. Many are small and outcompete natives.
- Thinking populations stay constant.
- Reading predator-prey graphs as unrelated.

### Day 1 (Monday): Invasive species
*Student:* Read about an invasive species in California and diagram how it disrupts the food web.

*How to teach:* Read about an invasive species; diagram how it disrupts the food web. Read together; your child draws the food web before and after the invasive species arrived. Ask what native species lost.

- **Q:** How do quagga mussels harm lakes?
  **A:** They filter out plankton that native species need and clog pipes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Population data
*Student:* Graph sample population data for predators and prey over 10 years and describe the pattern.

*How to teach:* Graph sample predator and prey data; describe the pattern. Provide sample data (e.g., rabbits and foxes over 10 years). Your child graphs both lines and describes how predator peaks follow prey peaks.

- **Q:** What pattern do predator and prey populations show?
  **A:** They rise and fall in cycles; predator peaks follow prey peaks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ecosystem game
*Student:* Play an 'Oh Deer' style game with family modeling food, water and shelter and record population changes each round.

*How to teach:* Play 'Oh Deer' with family and record populations each round. Explain rules: 'deer' pick a needed resource, 'resource' players pick one; deer who match survive and reproduce. Your child records population each round and graphs it.

- **Q:** What happens when resources are limited?
  **A:** The deer population drops; when it drops, resources recover and it rises again.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Terrarium update
*Student:* Observe the terrarium and explain whether matter is cycling successfully.

*How to teach:* Observe the terrarium; explain whether matter is cycling. Your child looks for condensation, new growth and decomposing leaves, writing a short claim about whether matter is cycling.

- **Q:** What evidence shows matter cycling?
  **A:** Condensation, healthy plants, decomposing leaves.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 assessment on food webs, decomposers and matter cycling.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test. Give the test without help, then review any missed vocabulary with the food web cards.

- **Q:** Name the three roles in a food web.
  **A:** Producers, consumers, decomposers.

**Assessment:** Population graph; Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Population graph: both lines labeled, pattern described. Unit 5 test key (suggested): 1) producers make food 2) arrows show energy flow 3) removing otters increases urchins 4) decomposers return matter to soil 5) invasive species disrupt webs 6) predator-prey cycles 7) terrarium water cycle.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: The French and Indian War

**Prepare:** Franklin's 'Join, or Die' cartoon (1754); maps of 1754 and 1763. West flashcards. Write the Unit 5 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the war was against Native peoples. It was Britain vs. France, each with Native allies.
- Thinking colonists welcomed the Proclamation.
- Thinking the war ended in 1754.

### Day 1 (Monday): Competition for the Ohio Valley
*Student:* Map British, French and Native claims to the Ohio Valley and explain why war began in 1754.

*How to teach:* Map British, French and Native claims to the Ohio Valley. Explain why the war began in 1754.

- **Q:** Why did war start?
  **A:** Britain and France both claimed the Ohio Valley.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Primary source: 'Join, or Die'
*Student:* Analyze Franklin's 'Join, or Die' cartoon and explain its message about colonial unity.

*How to teach:* Look at the cartoon together. Your child explains the snake segments and the message of unity.

- **Q:** Message?
  **A:** Colonies must unite or be destroyed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Alliances and outcome
*Student:* Chart which Native nations allied with France or Britain and compare 1754 and 1763 maps.

*How to teach:* Chart Native nations' alliances. Compare 1754 and 1763 maps.

- **Q:** What did France lose in 1763?
  **A:** Canada and land east of the Mississippi.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pontiac and the Proclamation
*Student:* Read about Pontiac's resistance and the Proclamation of 1763 and explain why colonists were angry.

*How to teach:* Read about Pontiac's resistance and the Proclamation of 1763. Your child explains colonists' anger.

- **Q:** Why were colonists angry?
  **A:** It banned settlement west of the Appalachians.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test on cooperation and conflict and review West states and capitals.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test and review West states and capitals. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** When did the war end?
  **A:** 1763.

**Assessment:** Cartoon analysis and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Cartoon analysis: segments as colonies, unity message. Unit 5 test key: cooperation, wars, French and Indian War, Proclamation. West capitals: Montana–Helena, Idaho–Boise, Wyoming–Cheyenne, Nevada–Carson City, Utah–Salt Lake City, Colorado–Denver, Arizona–Phoenix, New Mexico–Santa Fe, Washington–Olympia, Oregon–Salem, California–Sacramento, Alaska–Juneau, Hawaii–Honolulu.

## Art & Music 5: Printmaking and persuasive images

**Prepare:** Find Paul Revere's engraving 'The Bloody Massacre' (1770) and Benjamin Franklin's 'Join, or Die' (1754). Gather foam trays or craft foam, dull pencil, washable ink or tempera, a brayer or roller, and paper. Cover the table.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the print reverses. Letters and words must be carved backward.
- Carving too shallow. Lines must be pressed deeply so ink doesn't fill them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Prints that persuade
*Student:* Study Paul Revere's Boston Massacre engraving and Franklin's 'Join, or Die' and explain how prints spread messages.

*How to teach:* Study the two prints; explain how prints spread messages. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How was Revere's Boston Massacre engraving propaganda?
  **A:** It showed British soldiers firing in an orderly line at peaceful colonists, exaggerating events to stir anger against Britain.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Designing a print
*Student:* Design a simple image with a message about a cause you care about, remembering the print will be reversed.

*How to teach:* Design an image with a message, remembering it reverses. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** If you want 'HELP' to read correctly in the print, how must you carve it?
  **A:** Backward (mirror image).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Carving
*Student:* Press the design into a foam plate with a dull pencil, checking line depth.

*How to teach:* Press the design into foam with a dull pencil, checking depth. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why must lines be deep?
  **A:** So ink stays on the raised surface and the lines print white.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Printing an edition
*Student:* Roll ink and pull an edition of 4 prints, numbering and signing them.

*How to teach:* Roll ink, pull an edition of 4 prints, number and sign them (1/4, 2/4...). Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What does '2/4' written on a print mean?
  **A:** The second print in an edition of four.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Choose your best print and explain its message and the care taken with tools.

*How to teach:* Choose the best print and explain its message and tool care. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What did 'Join, or Die' mean?
  **A:** The colonies (snake pieces) had to unite or be destroyed.

**Assessment:** Print edition and message explanation.

**Answer key:** Print edition: 4 consistent prints, numbered and signed, clear image with reversed text correct. Explanation: message clearly stated; tool care (clean brayer, wipe ink). Proficient = all.

## PE 5: Offense and defense in basketball

**Prepare:** Gather a basketball and hoop. Draw a simple court diagram for Friday (paper or whiteboard). Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Crossing the feet on defense. Slide the feet without crossing so you can change direction quickly.
- Standing still after passing. In a give-and-go, cut to the basket immediately after the pass.

### Day 1 (Monday): Defensive stance
*Student:* Practice defensive slides and stance for 5 x 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* Teach the defensive stance: feet wide, knees bent, hands active, stay between the player and basket. Do 5 × 30-second slides.

- **Q:** Why don't defenders cross their feet when sliding?
  **A:** Crossing makes you off-balance and slow to change direction.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Give-and-go
*Student:* Practice the give-and-go with a partner and finish with a layup.

*How to teach:* Teach the give-and-go: pass to a partner, cut toward the basket, get the ball back and finish with a layup.

- **Q:** What does the 'go' mean in give-and-go?
  **A:** After giving (passing) the ball, you go (cut) toward the basket to get it back.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 2-v-1
*Student:* Play 2-v-1 drills deciding when to pass or shoot.

*How to teach:* Two attackers vs. you on defense: if you guard the ball carrier, they pass; if you stay back, they shoot or drive.

- **Q:** In 2-v-1, when should the ball carrier pass?
  **A:** When the defender commits to them, leaving the teammate open.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Half-court game
*Student:* Play a 2-v-2 half-court game applying offensive and defensive strategies.

*How to teach:* Play 2-v-2 half court; pause to point out open lanes and help defense. Walk through the rules and boundaries first, then play; pause once to ask a quick strategy question ('Where is the open space?') and praise fair play and effort as much as skill.

- **Q:** What is help defense?
  **A:** Leaving your player slightly to stop a driving ball carrier, then recovering.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Diagram a give-and-go and explain how it creates an open lane.

*How to teach:* Your child diagrams a give-and-go with arrows and explains it. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How does a give-and-go create an open lane?
  **A:** The defender turns to watch the pass, and the cutter moves quickly past them toward the basket into open space.

**Assessment:** Strategy diagram and game observation.

**Answer key:** Strategy diagram: shows player A passing to B (dashed arrow), A cutting to the basket (solid arrow), B passing back, and A shooting; explanation mentions the defender watching the ball. Game observation: uses defensive stance, moves without the ball, passes to open teammates. Proficient = accurate diagram plus 2 of 3 game behaviors.

## Health 5: The reproductive systems

**Prepare:** Print labeled and blank diagrams of the female and male reproductive systems (KidsHealth or a health textbook). Preview AMAZE videos on reproductive anatomy. PARENT PREVIEW: watch the videos and read materials before your child does; decide how you'll answer personal or values questions, and keep the anonymous question box open.

**Watch for:**
- Using slang or nicknames for body parts. Correct names help when talking to doctors and keep kids safer.
- Thinking the reproductive system is 'gross' or shameful. It's a normal body system like any other.

### Day 1 (Monday): Correct names
*Student:* Learn medically accurate names for body parts and why using them matters for health and safety.

*How to teach:* Explain why correct names matter (clear communication with doctors and trusted adults, safety). Practice saying them matter-of-factly.

- **Q:** Why is it important to use medically correct names?
  **A:** So you can communicate clearly with doctors and trusted adults about health or safety.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Female system
*Student:* Label a diagram of the female reproductive system and write the function of each structure.

*How to teach:* Label the female diagram: ovaries (make eggs and estrogen), fallopian tubes (carry egg), uterus (where a baby can grow), vagina (birth canal).

- **Q:** What is the function of the ovaries?
  **A:** To make eggs (ova) and the hormone estrogen.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Male system
*Student:* Label a diagram of the male reproductive system and write the function of each structure.

*How to teach:* Label the male diagram: testes (make sperm and testosterone), scrotum (holds testes), penis, urethra.

- **Q:** What is the function of the testes?
  **A:** To make sperm and the hormone testosterone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Create a T-chart comparing changes in both systems during puberty.

*How to teach:* Create a T-chart of puberty changes in each system. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Give one change in each system during puberty.
  **A:** Female: ovaries begin releasing eggs, menstruation begins. Male: testes begin producing sperm.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a diagram-labeling quiz and submit any questions to the question box.

*How to teach:* Diagram-labeling quiz; collect question-box questions. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Where does a fertilized egg develop into a baby?
  **A:** In the uterus.

**Assessment:** Diagram quiz.

**Answer key:** Diagram quiz: female — ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina; male — testes, scrotum, penis, urethra, sperm duct (vas deferens). Proficient = 80% labeled correctly with functions.
