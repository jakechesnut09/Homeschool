# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 33

## ELA 5: Writing an original myth

**Prepare:** Myth planning sheet; art supplies.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to explain the phenomenon.
- Rushing the key scene.
- Weak verbs (went, got).

### Day 1 (Monday): Planning
*Student:* Choose a phenomenon (why the moon changes shape, why cats purr) and plan a myth with characters, conflict and explanation.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm phenomena together. Your child fills the planning sheet: characters, setting, conflict, and the explanation at the end.

- **Q:** What must a myth explain?
  **A:** A natural phenomenon or custom.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drafting
*Student:* Draft the myth with dialogue and a god, hero or trickster character.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts the myth with at least 4 lines of dialogue and a god, hero or trickster. Check dialogue punctuation.

- **Q:** Punctuate: you will pay said Zeus
  **A:** “You will pay,” said Zeus.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revising
*Student:* Revise for pacing (summarize travel, slow down the key scene) and vivid verbs.

*How to teach:* Your child highlights the travel or less important parts to summarize and the key scene to stretch. Replace 5 weak verbs with vivid ones.

- **Q:** Replace 'went': The hero went up the mountain.
  **A:** The hero scrambled/climbed up the mountain.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publishing
*Student:* Type and illustrate the myth as a small book.

*How to teach:* Your child types the final copy and illustrates key scenes, assembling a small book with a cover.

- **Q:** What makes illustrations helpful?
  **A:** They match key scenes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Read the myth aloud to an audience and take a Greek/Latin roots cumulative quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the myth to an audience. Then give the cumulative roots quiz.

- **Q:** Meaning of 'tele' and 'port'?
  **A:** Far; carry.

**Assessment:** Original myth narrative rubric; roots cumulative quiz.

**Answer key:** Myth rubric: 4 = explains a phenomenon, god/hero/trickster, dialogue, paced key scene, vivid verbs; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = little explanation; 1 = incomplete. Roots quiz key: spect see, port carry, struct build, rupt break, graph write, tele far, geo earth, chron time; prefixes un-, re-, dis-, mis-, pre-.

## Math 5: Hierarchy of quadrilaterals

**Prepare:** Geoboard and rubber bands or the free Math Learning Center Geoboard app; chart paper. Note: some books define trapezoid as 'exactly one' pair of parallel sides and others 'at least one'; check which definition your materials use and stay consistent.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a square is not a rectangle. A square has all rectangle properties (4 right angles), so it is a special rectangle.
- Reversing the hierarchy (all rhombuses are squares). Only squares that have right angles; a rhombus is sometimes a square.
- Mixing trapezoid definitions. Use the one your materials state.

### Day 1 (Monday): Quadrilateral family
*Student:* Build each quadrilateral type on a geoboard and list its defining properties.

*How to teach:* Build each quadrilateral on the geoboard and list defining properties (sides, parallel pairs, angles). Your child builds a square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezoid and kite and lists sides, parallel pairs and angles for each.

- **Q:** What makes a rhombus?
  **A:** 4 equal sides (opposite sides parallel).
- **Q:** What makes a rectangle?
  **A:** 4 right angles (opposite sides parallel and equal).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hierarchy diagram
*Student:* Create a hierarchy diagram from quadrilateral down to square with arrows and property labels.

*How to teach:* Draw a hierarchy: quadrilateral → parallelogram → rectangle and rhombus → square, labeling the property gained at each arrow. Your child draws the diagram on chart paper. Ask: 'What does a square have that a rectangle might not?'

- **Q:** Where does a square belong in the hierarchy?
  **A:** Under both rectangle and rhombus — it has 4 right angles and 4 equal sides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Always, sometimes, never
*Student:* Decide whether 12 statements like 'a rhombus is a square' are always, sometimes or never true, with drawings as proof.

*How to teach:* Decide always/sometimes/never for 12 statements, drawing proof. Your child marks each statement always, sometimes or never and draws a figure as proof, especially for 'sometimes'.

- **Q:** A rhombus is a square.
  **A:** Sometimes — only when it has right angles.
- **Q:** A square is a rectangle.
  **A:** Always.
- **Q:** A rectangle is a parallelogram.
  **A:** Always.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a review of triangle and quadrilateral classification using a Venn diagram.

*How to teach:* Review triangles and quadrilaterals using a Venn diagram (e.g., rectangles and rhombuses overlap in squares). Your child places shapes in a Venn diagram (e.g., rectangles and rhombuses overlapping in squares) and explains each placement.

- **Q:** What goes in the overlap of rectangles and rhombuses?
  **A:** Squares.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on classifying two-dimensional figures.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test and collect the hierarchy diagram. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** A parallelogram has a right angle — always, sometimes, never?
  **A:** Sometimes (when it's a rectangle).

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test covering 5.G.B.3–B.4; hierarchy diagram.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test key (suggested): 1) A square is a rhombus: always 2) A rhombus is a rectangle: sometimes 3) A parallelogram is a quadrilateral: always 4) A triangle can have two right angles: never 5) A rectangle has 4 right angles: always 6) Squares are in both rectangle and rhombus categories 7) a kite is a quadrilateral 8) classify a figure with 2 pairs of parallel sides, no right angles, unequal adjacent sides = parallelogram. Hierarchy diagram: correct order quadrilateral → parallelogram → rectangle/rhombus → square with property labels.

## Science 5: Day length and seasons

**Prepare:** Sunrise/sunset data table for your city, graph paper, globe, lamp.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking seasons are caused by distance from the sun. They're caused by Earth's tilt.
- Thinking day length is the same everywhere.
- Thinking the longest day is in July.

### Day 1 (Monday): Daylight data
*Student:* Collect sunrise and sunset times for the first day of each month and calculate hours of daylight.

*How to teach:* Collect sunrise and sunset times for the first of each month; calculate daylight hours. Use an almanac or website for sunrise/sunset times. Your child calculates daylight hours.

- **Q:** Sunrise 6:00 a.m., sunset 8:00 p.m. — daylight hours?
  **A:** 14 hours.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Graphing day length
*Student:* Graph daylight hours across the year and identify the longest and shortest days.

*How to teach:* Graph daylight across the year; find longest and shortest. Your child graphs months on the x-axis and hours on the y-axis.

- **Q:** When are the longest and shortest days?
  **A:** Around June 21 and December 21.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tilt model
*Student:* Use a lamp and tilted globe to model why California has longer days in summer.

*How to teach:* Model with a lamp and tilted globe. Tilt the globe about 23.5° and orbit the lamp. Your child observes the Northern Hemisphere in summer and winter.

- **Q:** Why are summer days longer?
  **A:** Northern Hemisphere tilts toward the sun, so more of its daily path is in sunlight.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing cities
*Student:* Compare day-length graphs for Los Angeles and Anchorage and describe the differences.

*How to teach:* Compare Los Angeles and Anchorage. Your child compares graphs for both cities and describes differences.

- **Q:** How does Anchorage differ?
  **A:** Much longer summer days (about 19 hours) and shorter winter days (about 5.5 hours).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer data-analysis questions about day length patterns and take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Answer data questions; take a quiz. Give the data-analysis questions and quiz.

- **Q:** What causes seasons?
  **A:** Earth's tilted axis as it orbits the sun.

**Assessment:** Day length graph and quiz.

**Answer key:** Day length graph: monthly points, labeled. Quiz key: tilt causes seasons; longest ~June 21; shortest ~Dec 21; higher latitudes have bigger changes.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: The Louisiana Purchase and western explorers

**Prepare:** Lewis and Clark journal excerpts; expedition route map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Sacagawea was a guide.
- Thinking York was free.
- Thinking the Purchase was small.

### Day 1 (Monday): Louisiana Purchase
*Student:* Read about Jefferson's 1803 purchase and calculate its size compared to the existing U.S.

*How to teach:* Read about the 1803 Purchase; calculate size comparison. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Size?
  **A:** About 828,000 square miles, doubling the U.S.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lewis and Clark journals
*Student:* Read journal excerpts describing animals and landscapes and sketch one like a naturalist.

*How to teach:* Read journal excerpts; sketch like a naturalist. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Why sketch?
  **A:** To record new species.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sacagawea and York
*Student:* Research the contributions of Sacagawea and York to the expedition.

*How to teach:* Research Sacagawea and York. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Sacagawea's role?
  **A:** Interpreter; helped get horses from the Shoshone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pike and Frémont
*Student:* Trace Pike's and Frémont's routes and record what each found, including Frémont in California.

*How to teach:* Trace Pike's and Frémont's routes. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Frémont in California?
  **A:** Explored and mapped; involved in Bear Flag Revolt 1846.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a journal entry as a member of the Corps of Discovery with accurate geography.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a Corps of Discovery journal entry. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Where did they winter on the Pacific?
  **A:** Fort Clatsop.

**Assessment:** Journal entry and route map.

**Answer key:** Journal entry and route map: accurate geography.

## Art & Music 5: Curating the art portfolio

**Prepare:** Gather the year's artwork, index cards, and access to Google Arts & Culture for a virtual museum tour.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a portfolio is all your work. A curated portfolio selects pieces that show growth and variety.
- Choosing only favorites. Include pieces showing progress, even early ones.

### Day 1 (Monday): What curators do
*Student:* Take a virtual museum tour on Google Arts & Culture and list a curator's responsibilities.

*How to teach:* Take a virtual tour; list curator responsibilities (choose works, arrange, write labels, care for art).

- **Q:** What does a curator do?
  **A:** Selects, organizes and presents artworks for an exhibition and cares for them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Selecting work
*Student:* Review the year's artwork and select 6 pieces that show growth and variety.

*How to teach:* Select 6 pieces showing growth and variety. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why include an early piece?
  **A:** It shows how your skills grew over the year.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection for each piece on what was learned and what you would change.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection for each: learned and would change. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What should a reflection include?
  **A:** What you learned, techniques used, and what you'd change.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Exhibit theme
*Student:* Choose an exhibition title and theme and arrange the order of artworks.

*How to teach:* Choose an exhibition title and theme; order the artworks. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** How can the order of artworks tell a story?
  **A:** Arranging by time, theme or technique leads viewers through growth or an idea.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the curated selection plan to a parent and explain the choices.

*How to teach:* Present the plan to a parent. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** Why does an exhibition need a theme?
  **A:** It connects the works and helps viewers understand the show.

**Assessment:** Portfolio selection and reflections.

**Answer key:** Portfolio selection: 6 pieces showing variety (drawing, painting, printmaking, sculpture) and growth. Reflections: 4 = each piece has specific learning and revision ideas with art terms; 3 = most; 2 = brief; 1 = missing.

## PE 5: Training week: aerobic and strength

**Prepare:** Gather stopwatch, mat, activity log. Plan a capture-the-flag game (two flags, boundaries). Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking intervals are 'cheating.' Alternating jog and walk lets you work longer and build endurance.
- Rushing reps. Good form counts more than speed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interval run
*Student:* Do a 20-minute interval run (1 min jog, 1 min walk) and check heart rate.

*How to teach:* 20-minute interval run (1 min jog, 1 min walk); check heart rate at the end. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why check heart rate during training?
  **A:** To see how hard you're working and whether you're in a target zone.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength circuit
*Student:* Complete a 3-round strength circuit targeting curl-ups and push-ups.

*How to teach:* 3-round circuit: curl-ups, push-ups, squats, plank. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What's the difference between a set and a repetition?
  **A:** A repetition is one movement; a set is a group of repetitions done in a row.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Active game
*Student:* Play a vigorous game (capture the flag) for 20 minutes.

*How to teach:* Play capture the flag for 20 minutes. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** Is capture the flag moderate or vigorous? How do you know?
  **A:** Vigorous—breathing hard and hard to talk.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flexibility and core
*Student:* Complete a yoga flow and trunk lift practice.

*How to teach:* Yoga flow and trunk lift practice. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Which fitness component does yoga mainly build?
  **A:** Flexibility (also balance and core strength).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Review the activity log and rate effort for each day.

*How to teach:* Review the log and rate effort each day. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How can you tell if your plan is working?
  **A:** Log shows consistent activity; heart rate recovers faster; reps or times improve.

**Assessment:** Activity log and heart-rate entries.

**Answer key:** Activity log: daily entries with activity, minutes, and heart rate. Proficient = 5 entries with heart rate and effort ratings.

## Health 5: Drug-free goal and refusal practice

**Prepare:** Prepare a goal card and a recording device (optional). List the 6 refusal strategies: broken record, humor, change the subject, leave, give facts, suggest an alternative.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you need a perfect excuse to say no. 'No thanks' is enough.
- Thinking stress or boredom requires something special. Healthy activities manage feelings well.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drug-free goal
*Student:* Write a personal drug-free goal with reasons and supports.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a drug-free goal with reasons and supports. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Write a drug-free goal.
  **A:** Example: 'I will stay drug-free through high school because I want to play soccer and stay healthy; my parents and coach will support me.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Refusal toolbox
*Student:* Practice 6 refusal strategies (broken record, humor, change subject, leave, give facts, suggest alternative).

*How to teach:* Practice 6 refusal strategies with quick role-plays. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Give an example of the 'broken record' strategy.
  **A:** Repeating 'No thanks' calmly each time someone asks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Record a skit
*Student:* Write and record a 1-minute refusal skit.

*How to teach:* Write and record a 1-minute refusal skit. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Which strategies did you use in your skit?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name at least two (e.g., give facts, suggest an alternative).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coping without drugs
*Student:* List healthy ways to deal with stress, boredom and sadness.

*How to teach:* List healthy ways to deal with stress, boredom and sadness. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name a healthy way to cope with boredom.
  **A:** Play a sport, read, draw, call a friend, learn a new skill.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a refusal skill demonstration scored with a rubric.

*How to teach:* Perform a refusal skill demonstration scored with a rubric. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What body language shows you mean 'no'?
  **A:** Eye contact, calm firm voice, straight posture.

**Assessment:** Refusal skill rubric.

**Answer key:** Refusal rubric (1–4 each): clear 'no', uses at least two strategies, assertive voice and body language, suggests an alternative or leaves. Proficient = 3 or higher in each.
