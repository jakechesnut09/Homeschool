# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 7

## ELA 5: Hatchet chapters 10–13: the new Brian

**Prepare:** Preview chapters 10–13 of Hatchet. Parental note: at Brian's low point after the search plane passes without seeing him, he tries to harm himself with the hatchet (described briefly). Read this section first and be ready to talk about it calmly and to emphasize that he chooses to live and that people should always reach out for help.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking character change happens all at once. Show it builds over many small experiences.
- Shifting tense mid-paragraph. Pick past tense for the scene and keep it.
- Confusing roots with prefixes: 'port' is a root meaning carry; 'trans-' is a prefix.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 10–11
*Student:* Read chapters 10–11 and compare 'old Brian' and 'new Brian' on a T-chart with quotations.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 10–11. Your child builds a T-chart: 'old Brian' (city boy, self-pity) vs. 'new Brian' (observant, patient) with quotations.

- **Q:** Give one example of the 'new Brian.'
  **A:** He learns to watch and listen closely, gathers turtle eggs, and makes a fish spear — he solves problems instead of feeling sorry for himself.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Roots: spect, port, struct, rupt
*Student:* Learn 4 Latin roots and build 16 words; find any of them in Hatchet and define them in context.

*How to teach:* Teach spect (see), port (carry), struct (build), rupt (break). Your child builds 16 words and defines any found in Hatchet.

- **Q:** What do 'inspect,' 'transport,' 'construct' and 'erupt' mean?
  **A:** Look at closely; carry across; build; break out.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapters 12–13
*Student:* Read chapters 12–13 (the plane passing, the low point) and discuss how this turning point changes Brian.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 12–13. Discuss the turning point: the search plane passes without seeing him, his despair, and his decision to keep going as a tougher 'new' person.

- **Q:** How does the passing plane change Brian?
  **A:** He loses hope and hits his lowest point, but afterward he becomes determined and tough, realizing self-pity doesn't help.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Narrative scene
*Student:* Write a one-page scene from the point of view of an animal watching Brian, avoiding shifts in verb tense.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-page scene from an animal's view watching Brian, staying in one tense. Before writing, list what the animal could see, hear and smell. After writing, your child circles every verb to check the tense stays the same.

- **Q:** Which tense works best for a story scene?
  **A:** Usually past tense, kept consistent from beginning to end.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Correct 10 sentences with inappropriate tense shifts and take a roots quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child corrects 10 sentences with tense shifts and takes a roots quiz. For each fix, have your child say which word signaled the time (yesterday, then) and which tense it requires.

- **Q:** Fix: Brian sharpens the spear and then he threw it.
  **A:** Brian sharpened the spear and then he threw it.

**Assessment:** Character change T-chart; animal-POV scene; roots and tense-shift quiz.

**Answer key:** Roots quiz key: spect = see (spectator, inspect); port = carry (portable, import); struct = build (structure, instruct); rupt = break (rupture, interrupt). T-chart: 3+ contrasting traits with quotations. Animal scene rubric: consistent point of view and tense, sensory detail, accurate to events in Hatchet.

## Math 5: Division with two-digit divisors: models

**Prepare:** Grid paper, base-ten blocks or quick drawings (squares, sticks, dots). Review multiplication facts for 2-digit multiples (24 × 10, × 5, × 2).

**Watch for:**
- Dropping zeros: 2,400 ÷ 60 = 4. Rewrite as 240 tens ÷ 6 tens = 40, then check 60 × 40 = 2,400.
- Remainder larger than the divisor. If the remainder ≥ divisor, the quotient can go up by 1.
- Forgetting to add the partial quotients at the end. Circle each one and total them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Division by multiples of 10
*Student:* Use basic facts and place value to solve 15 problems like 2,400 / 60 and 420 / 30.

*How to teach:* Use basic facts: 2,400 ÷ 60 is like 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then think 60 × 40 = 2,400. The child solves 15 problems, checking each by multiplying.

- **Q:** 2,400 ÷ 60 = ?
  **A:** 40
- **Q:** 420 ÷ 30 = ?
  **A:** 14 (30 × 14 = 420)

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Area model division
*Student:* Solve 8 problems such as 1,512 / 24 by building an area model with known parts of the dividend.

*How to teach:* Draw a rectangle with height 24 and an unknown width; take off chunks such as 24 × 60 = 1,440, leaving 72 = 24 × 3. Add the widths. The child solves 8 problems this way.

- **Q:** 1,512 ÷ 24 = ?
  **A:** 24 × 60 = 1,440; 1,512 − 1,440 = 72; 24 × 3 = 72. Quotient 63.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partial quotients
*Student:* Use partial quotients with friendly multiples (10x, 5x, 2x the divisor) to solve 8 four-digit by two-digit problems.

*How to teach:* List friendly multiples of the divisor (10×, 5×, 2×) at the side, then subtract chunks. The child solves 8 four-digit by two-digit problems.

- **Q:** 3,456 ÷ 27 = ?
  **A:** 27 × 100 = 2,700 (756 left); 27 × 20 = 540 (216 left); 27 × 8 = 216. 100 + 20 + 8 = 128.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Checking with multiplication
*Student:* Solve 6 division problems and check each with multiplication plus remainder; write how the check confirms the answer.

*How to teach:* Solve each problem, then check: quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend. The child writes one sentence on why that proves the answer.

- **Q:** 845 ÷ 23 = ? Check it.
  **A:** 36 R17; 23 × 36 = 828 and 828 + 17 = 845.
- **Q:** Why does the check work?
  **A:** Division undoes multiplication: equal groups times the number of groups, plus leftovers, rebuilds the total.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz of 8 division problems showing a model or partial quotients for each.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz of 8 problems; the child shows an area model or partial quotients for every one. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** 1,950 ÷ 65 = ?
  **A:** 30

**Assessment:** Quiz on division with two-digit divisors using models.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 3,600 ÷ 90 = 40 2) 1,488 ÷ 31 = 48 3) 2,856 ÷ 42 = 68 4) 4,368 ÷ 52 = 84 5) 945 ÷ 15 = 63 6) 2,047 ÷ 23 = 89 7) 5,000 ÷ 25 = 200 8) 1,000 ÷ 37 = 27 R1. Full credit needs a correct answer and a model or partial quotients that matches it.

## Science 5: Mixtures and solutions

**Prepare:** Sand, salt, paper clips, rice, coffee filter, sieve, magnet, water, kitchen scale, cups, trail mix ingredients.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a solution is not a mixture. A solution is a mixture where one substance dissolves evenly in another.
- Thinking salt is destroyed when dissolved. It can be recovered by evaporation.
- Expecting to recover exactly 100% of the salt. Some sticks to the filter and cups.

### Day 1 (Monday): Making mixtures
*Student:* Make a trail-mix and a sand-and-salt mixture, weigh each part and the whole, and record whether weight adds up.

*How to teach:* Make trail mix and a sand-salt mix. Weigh each part and the whole and check that weights add up.

- **Q:** 20 g of sand + 15 g of salt = ? g of mixture.
  **A:** 35 g.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Separation plan
*Student:* Plan how to separate sand, salt, paper clips and rice using properties (magnetism, size, solubility).

*How to teach:* Your child plans a separation of sand, salt, paper clips and rice using properties: magnetism, particle size, and solubility. Ask questions rather than giving the order: 'What property does only the paper clip have? What can water do to salt but not sand?' Your child writes numbered steps.

- **Q:** Give the order of steps.
  **A:** Magnet removes paper clips; sieve removes rice; add water to dissolve the salt; filter out the sand; evaporate the water to recover salt.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Separating
*Student:* Carry out the separation plan with a magnet, sieve, water and coffee filter, then set salt water to evaporate.

*How to teach:* Carry out the plan. Pour the salt water through the coffee filter and set it to evaporate on a plate.

- **Q:** Why does the sand stay in the filter but the salt doesn't?
  **A:** Sand doesn't dissolve and its grains are too large; dissolved salt particles pass through with the water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recovering the salt
*Student:* Weigh the recovered salt and compare with the starting amount; explain any differences.

*How to teach:* Weigh the recovered salt and compare with the starting amount. Discuss differences.

- **Q:** Why might you recover less salt than you started with?
  **A:** Some salt stays in the filter, sand or cups, or water hadn't fully evaporated.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a procedure for separating a new mixture and take a quiz on mixtures vs. solutions.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a procedure for separating a new mixture (e.g., iron filings, sugar and pebbles) and takes a mixtures quiz. Read the procedure as if you were following it literally; if a step is unclear, your child revises it. Then give the quiz.

- **Q:** Mixture or solution: salt water? sand and water?
  **A:** Salt water is a solution; sand and water is a mixture that is not a solution.

**Assessment:** Separation procedure and data; mixtures quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) mixture = substances combined but not chemically changed 2) solution = one substance dissolved evenly in another 3) magnet separates iron/steel 4) sieve separates by size 5) filtering separates undissolved solids 6) evaporation recovers dissolved solids. Procedure rubric: numbered steps, uses properties, materials listed.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: French, English and Dutch explorers

**Prepare:** Outline map of North America, explorer cards (Cabot, Cartier, Champlain, Hudson, Columbus, Coronado).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Northwest Passage existed as hoped. No practical route through North America was found.
- Confusing Hudson's sponsors. He sailed for the Dutch (1609) and the English (1610).
- Coloring claims as if borders were firm.

### Day 1 (Monday): John Cabot and Jacques Cartier
*Student:* Trace Cabot's and Cartier's voyages and record their sponsors and findings.

*How to teach:* Read short bios of Cabot (England, 1497) and Cartier (France, 1534–36). Your child traces both routes on the outline map in different colors and notes each sponsor and finding.

- **Q:** What did Cabot's voyage give England?
  **A:** A claim to North America.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Champlain and Hudson
*Student:* Trace Champlain's and Hudson's routes and explain the search for the Northwest Passage.

*How to teach:* Read about Champlain (founded Quebec, 1608) and Hudson (Dutch voyage 1609, English voyage 1610). Ask why so many explorers looked for a Northwest Passage and whether they found one.

- **Q:** What was the Northwest Passage?
  **A:** A hoped-for water route through North America to Asia.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Explorer comparison
*Student:* Complete an explorer comparison matrix (sponsor, goal, obstacles, accomplishments) for 6 explorers.

*How to teach:* Your child fills a matrix (sponsor, goal, obstacles, accomplishments) for Columbus, Coronado, Cabot, Cartier, Champlain and Hudson. Check each row against the readings.

- **Q:** Who founded Quebec?
  **A:** Samuel de Champlain, 1608.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Land claims map
*Student:* Color a map showing land claimed by Spain, France, England, Portugal, the Netherlands, Sweden and Russia.

*How to teach:* Use an atlas showing about 1650 claims. Your child colors each nation's claim with a key and labels one settlement for each.

- **Q:** Which country claimed most of South America's east coast?
  **A:** Portugal (Brazil).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a map quiz on explorer routes and European land claims.

*How to teach:* Give the map quiz without the atlas; review any missed route by retracing it. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Which river did Cartier explore?
  **A:** The St. Lawrence River.

**Assessment:** Explorer matrix and land claims map quiz.

**Answer key:** Matrix: sponsor, goal, obstacles, accomplishments for 6 explorers. Map quiz key: Spain–Southwest, Florida, Mexico; France–St. Lawrence and Mississippi; England–Atlantic coast; Netherlands–Hudson River; Russia–Alaska; Sweden–Delaware River; Portugal–Brazil.

## Art & Music 5: Beginning recorder: B, A, G

**Prepare:** Get a soprano recorder (baroque or German fingering — check which, since a few notes differ) and its fingering chart, or a free virtual recorder/keyboard app. Print flashcards of B, A and G on the treble staff and the music for 'Hot Cross Buns' (B A G, B A G, G G G G A A A A, B A G) and 'Gently Sleep.' Fingerings: B = left thumb + finger 1; A = thumb + 1, 2; G = thumb + 1, 2, 3.

**Watch for:**
- Blowing too hard, which makes squeaks. Use gentle 'warm' breath, as if blowing on soup to cool it.
- Leaky fingers. Cover holes with the pads of the fingers (not tips) so no air escapes; squeaks often mean a hole isn't fully covered.

### Day 1 (Monday): Recorder basics
*Student:* Learn posture, left-hand position and gentle 'too' tonguing; play B with steady tone.

*How to teach:* Show posture (sit or stand tall), left hand on top, lips gently around the mouthpiece (not teeth). Start each note with a soft 'too.' Play B (thumb + finger 1) with a steady tone.

- **Q:** Which hand goes on top of the recorder?
  **A:** The left hand.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): A and G
*Student:* Learn A and G and play 4-note patterns from a fingering chart.

*How to teach:* Teach A (thumb + 1, 2) and G (thumb + 1, 2, 3). Play 4-note patterns: B-A-G-A, G-A-B-B. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What changes when you go from B to G?
  **A:** You cover more holes (add fingers 2 and 3), and the pitch gets lower.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading the staff
*Student:* Identify B, A and G on the staff with flashcards and play 'Hot Cross Buns' and 'Gently Sleep.'

*How to teach:* Flashcards: B on the middle line, A in the second space, G on the second line. Then play 'Hot Cross Buns' and 'Gently Sleep.'

- **Q:** Which line of the treble staff is B on?
  **A:** The middle (third) line.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice and record
*Student:* Practice 10 minutes and record a performance of 'Hot Cross Buns' to listen back.

*How to teach:* Practice 10 minutes, then record 'Hot Cross Buns.' Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why record yourself?
  **A:** So you can hear your tone and rhythm the way an audience does and find what to fix.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-evaluate the recording for tone and rhythm, then play a B-A-G song for an audience.

*How to teach:* Listen back and rate tone (no squeaks, steady) and rhythm (steady beat); then perform a B-A-G song for an audience.

- **Q:** Your recording has squeaks. What two things should you check?
  **A:** Breath (blow more gently) and whether fingers fully cover the holes.

**Assessment:** Recorded B-A-G performance with self-evaluation.

**Answer key:** Recorded performance rubric: 4 = correct notes, steady beat, clear tone with no squeaks, tonguing each note; 3 = 1–2 errors; 2 = several errors or uneven rhythm; 1 = cannot complete. Self-evaluation proficient if it names one strength and one specific fix that matches the recording.

## PE 5: Locomotor patterns and rhythmic sequences

**Prepare:** Gather music at several tempos, cones, and lummi sticks (or two wooden spoons/paper towel tubes per person). Learn one simple lummi pattern ahead of time (tap floor, tap together, tap partner's sticks). Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up a gallop and a slide. A gallop goes forward with the same lead foot; a slide goes sideways.
- Rushing the beat. Clap the beat first, then move, so steps land on the beat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Locomotor review
*Student:* Practice skip, slide, gallop, leap and hop to changing music tempos.

*How to teach:* Call out skip, slide, gallop, leap and hop while music plays; change the tempo and have your child match it.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a hop and a jump?
  **A:** A hop takes off and lands on the same one foot; a jump lands on two feet (or takes off from two).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rhythm sticks
*Student:* Learn a lummi stick routine in 4/4 time with a partner.

*How to teach:* Sit facing each other. Teach a 4-count lummi pattern: tap ends on floor (1), tap sticks together (2), tap partner's right stick (3), tap together (4). Repeat to 4/4 music.

- **Q:** How many beats are in each measure of 4/4 time?
  **A:** Four beats.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 8-count sequences
*Student:* Combine four 8-count locomotor sequences into one pattern.

*How to teach:* Build four 8-count sequences (e.g., 8 skips, 8 slides right, 4 hops + 4 jumps, 8 gallops) and string them together.

- **Q:** How many counts are in four 8-count sequences?
  **A:** 32 counts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Designing a routine
*Student:* Design a 32-count routine mixing locomotor moves and jump rope tricks.

*How to teach:* Your child designs a 32-count routine mixing locomotor moves and jump-rope tricks and writes it down by counts.

- **Q:** Your routine has 8 skier jumps, 8 skips and 8 slides. How many more counts do you need for 32?
  **A:** 8 more counts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the routine to music and explain how it follows the beat.

*How to teach:* Your child performs the routine to music, then explains where the moves change. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** How did your routine follow the beat?
  **A:** Each move lasted a set number of counts (8) and changes happened at the start of a new 8-count phrase on the beat.

**Assessment:** Rhythmic routine performance.

**Answer key:** Rhythmic routine (4 = 32 counts, at least 4 different moves, stays on beat throughout, smooth changes on count 1; 3 = minor beat slips; 2 = loses the beat often or fewer moves; 1 = incomplete). Child explains that changes happen at the start of 8-count phrases.

## Health 5: Healthy breakfasts, snacks and beverages

**Prepare:** Gather yogurt, fruit, granola, vegetables and hummus for Wednesday; make 20 snack cards (apple, cheese stick, chips, candy bar, carrots, crackers, nuts, cookies, popcorn, fruit snacks, etc.). Find a short kids' article on breakfast and school performance (e.g., KidsHealth 'Break for Breakfast').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking skipping breakfast saves time with no cost. Without breakfast, energy and concentration drop by mid-morning.
- Thinking all snacks are 'junk.' Nutrient-dense snacks (fruit, yogurt, nuts, veggies) help growing bodies; 'sometimes' foods give empty calories.
- Thinking you only need water when thirsty. Drink regularly through the day and more when active or hot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Breakfast matters
*Student:* Read a short article on breakfast and concentration, then plan three quick balanced breakfasts.

*How to teach:* Read the article; your child plans three quick breakfasts, each with at least three food groups (e.g., whole-grain toast + peanut butter + banana).

- **Q:** Why does breakfast help you at school?
  **A:** It refuels the body and brain after the night, giving energy to concentrate, remember and stay alert.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Snack sorting
*Student:* Sort 20 snack cards into everyday vs. sometimes foods and explain your reasoning with nutrients.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts snack cards into everyday vs. sometimes and justifies with nutrients (fiber, protein, added sugar, sodium).

- **Q:** Why is a cheese stick an everyday snack but fruit snacks a sometimes snack?
  **A:** Cheese gives protein and calcium; fruit snacks are mostly added sugar with few nutrients (empty calories).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Make a snack
*Student:* Prepare a yogurt parfait or veggie-and-hummus plate with adult help and record its food groups.

*How to teach:* Wash hands, then your child makes a parfait or veggie-and-hummus plate and records food groups.

- **Q:** What food groups are in a yogurt parfait with berries and granola?
  **A:** Dairy (yogurt), fruit (berries), grains (granola).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hydration
*Student:* Track your water intake for a day and design a water-bottle reminder chart.

*How to teach:* Your child tracks every drink for a day and designs a reminder chart (e.g., a bottle with time markings).

- **Q:** Name two signs you need more water.
  **A:** Thirst, dark yellow urine, headache, tiredness or dizziness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a one-week healthy snack menu and justify three choices using label data.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-week snack menu and justifies three choices using label data (fiber, added sugar, protein, %DV).

- **Q:** What does 'nutrient-dense' mean?
  **A:** A food with lots of vitamins, minerals, fiber or protein for its calories.

**Assessment:** Snack menu with justifications.

**Answer key:** Snack menu rubric: 4 = 7 days of mostly nutrient-dense snacks across several food groups, three justifications citing specific label numbers (e.g., '5 g fiber, 0 g added sugar'); 3 = justifications general; 2 = mostly 'sometimes' foods or no justification; 1 = incomplete.
