# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 31

## ELA 5: Greek myths: gods, heroes and lessons

**Prepare:** Borrow D'Aulaires' Book of Greek Myths; download Hawthorne's A Wonder-Book (Project Gutenberg) for 'The Golden Touch' and 'The Paradise of Children.'

**Watch for:**
- Confusing myths with history.
- Thinking a lesson is a summary. A lesson is advice for life.
- Not knowing allusions come from myths (Herculean task).

### Day 1 (Monday): The Olympians
*Student:* Read the D'Aulaires' chapters on Zeus and the Olympians and make a family tree with each god's domain.

*How to teach:* Read the D'Aulaires' chapters on the Olympians together. Your child draws a family tree with each god's name and domain.

- **Q:** Name 3 Olympians and their domains.
  **A:** Zeus (sky, king of gods), Poseidon (sea), Athena (wisdom), Hera (marriage), Apollo (sun, music).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): 'The Golden Touch'
*Student:* Read Hawthorne's 'The Golden Touch' (King Midas) and write the lesson in one sentence with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Golden Touch' aloud. Your child writes the lesson in one sentence and supports it with an event from the story.

- **Q:** What happens to Midas's daughter Marygold?
  **A:** She turns to gold when he hugs/kisses her.
- **Q:** Lesson?
  **A:** Greed leads to unhappiness; love is worth more than gold.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 'The Paradise of Children'
*Student:* Read Hawthorne's Pandora story and compare how it explains troubles in the world.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Paradise of Children.' Your child compares it with 'The Golden Touch' — both show a wish or curiosity causing trouble — and explains how it accounts for troubles in the world.

- **Q:** What escapes and what remains?
  **A:** The Troubles fly out into the world and sting Pandora and Epimetheus; then Hope, which was also shut inside the box, flies out to comfort them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Words from myths
*Student:* Learn 12 words and phrases from mythology (Midas touch, panic, echo, Herculean) and explain their origins.

*How to teach:* Introduce each word with the myth it comes from in 1–2 sentences. Your child writes the word, the myth, and a sentence using it today.

- **Q:** Meaning of 'Herculean'?
  **A:** Requiring great strength or effort, from Hercules.
- **Q:** Origin of 'panic'?
  **A:** From Pan, god of the wild, who caused sudden fear.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Summarize a myth aloud in 1 minute and take a quiz on myth vocabulary and lessons.

*How to teach:* Your child practices a 1-minute summary of a myth, then delivers it to you. Give the quiz afterward.

- **Q:** What does 'Midas touch' mean today?
  **A:** The ability to make money from everything one does.

**Assessment:** Myth lesson statements; allusion vocabulary quiz; oral summary.

**Answer key:** Allusion quiz key: Midas touch = makes money easily; panic (Pan); echo (nymph Echo); Herculean = huge task; narcissism (Narcissus); Achilles' heel = weakness; Pandora's box = source of many troubles; titanic = huge; nemesis = enemy. Lesson statements: full sentences with evidence. Oral summary: beginning, conflict, resolution in 1 minute.

## Math 5: Numerical patterns and ordered pairs

**Prepare:** Graph paper, pattern tables with two columns.

**Watch for:**
- Reading only one rule's pattern. Compare corresponding terms (same position) in both lists.
- Swapping x and y when making pairs. First pattern = x, second = y.
- Thinking 'add 6' is 'add 3' plus 3 each time in the corresponding terms. Corresponding terms are doubled, not +3.

### Day 1 (Monday): Two rules
*Student:* Generate patterns 'add 3' and 'add 6' starting at 0 in a table and describe how corresponding terms compare.

*How to teach:* Generate 'add 3' and 'add 6' from 0 in a table; compare terms in the same position. Make a two-column table with five rows. Your child fills both columns, then compares terms in the same row.

- **Q:** List 5 terms of each starting at 0.
  **A:** Add 3: 0, 3, 6, 9, 12. Add 6: 0, 6, 12, 18, 24.
- **Q:** How do corresponding terms compare?
  **A:** Each term in the add-6 list is twice the add-3 term.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Graphing patterns
*Student:* Form ordered pairs from 4 pairs of patterns and graph them, noting the straight-line pattern.

*How to teach:* Form ordered pairs (0,0), (3,6), (6,12), (9,18) and graph them; notice a straight line through the origin. Your child plots each pair and connects the points with a ruler. Ask: 'Why is it a straight line?'

- **Q:** Rules 'add 2' and 'add 8' from 0 — what is the 4th ordered pair?
  **A:** (6, 24)

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Real-world patterns
*Student:* Create tables for 'cost of tickets at $4' vs '$8' and graph to compare.

*How to teach:* Build tables for tickets at $4 and $8 per ticket and graph both. Compare.

- **Q:** 5 tickets cost? at $4 and $8
  **A:** $20 and $40; the $8 cost is always double.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of coordinate plane, graphing and patterns.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of coordinate plane, graphing and patterns. Include plotting, distance, interpreting a graph and generating two patterns. Check each section before moving on.

- **Q:** Distance from (0, 4) to (0, 11)?
  **A:** 7

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on coordinate graphing and numerical patterns.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Rule 'add 5' starting at 0, 4th term?
  **A:** 15 (0, 5, 10, 15)

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test covering 5.G.A.1–A.2 and 5.OA.B.3.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test key (suggested): 1) (4, 2) is 4 right, 2 up 2) name a plotted point correctly 3) (0, 7) lies on the y-axis 4) distance (5,1) to (5,8) = 7 5) 'add 4' from 0: 0,4,8,12; 'add 12' from 0: 0,12,24,36 6) each y is 3 times x 7) pairs (0,0),(4,12),(8,24),(12,36) plotted 8) graph point (3, 45) on a pages-read graph means 45 pages after 3 days.

## Science 5: Scale of the solar system and stars

**Prepare:** Toilet paper roll, measuring tape, markers, balls of different sizes. Write the Unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking planets are close together.
- Thinking orbits need no force.
- Thinking all stars are at the same distance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Toilet paper solar system
*Student:* Build a toilet-paper scale model of planet distances from the sun along a hallway.

*How to teach:* Build a toilet-paper model: Mercury 1, Venus 1.8, Earth 2.5, Mars 3.8, Jupiter 13, Saturn 24, Uranus 48, Neptune 75 sheets from the sun. Unroll toilet paper along a hallway; mark planets with sticky notes. Ask what surprised them.

- **Q:** What do you notice about outer planets?
  **A:** They are much farther apart.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Size model
*Student:* Compare the sizes of the sun and planets with balls and seeds and discuss scale.

*How to teach:* Compare sizes with balls and seeds. Use balls and seeds to compare sizes. Discuss why distance and size models are hard to combine.

- **Q:** How many Earths fit across the sun?
  **A:** About 109.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Gravity and orbits
*Student:* Swing a ball on a string to model how gravity keeps planets in orbit and explain what happens if the string is cut.

*How to teach:* Swing a ball on a string; discuss gravity and orbits. Swing a ball on a string in a safe area. Ask what happens when you let go.

- **Q:** What happens if the string is cut?
  **A:** The ball flies off in a straight line.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Brightness data
*Student:* Analyze a table of star brightness and distance data and identify the pattern.

*How to teach:* Analyze a star brightness table. Your child reads a brightness and distance table and identifies a pattern.

- **Q:** What pattern?
  **A:** Closer stars generally appear brighter, but bigger or hotter stars can be bright far away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 assessment on gravity and star brightness.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test. Give the test without help.

- **Q:** What force keeps planets in orbit?
  **A:** Gravity.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test; scale model reflection.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test key (suggested): gravity pulls toward Earth's center; objects fall at same rate; sun is a star; brightness depends on distance; gravity keeps planets in orbit. Reflection: scale model insight.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Ratification, the Bill of Rights and the American creed

**Prepare:** Bill of Rights transcript; song lyrics. Write the Unit 8 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Bill of Rights was in the original Constitution.
- Thinking rights have no limits.
- Confusing amendments.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ratification debate
*Student:* Role-play a debate between a Federalist and an Anti-Federalist about ratifying the Constitution.

*How to teach:* Role-play a Federalist vs. Anti-Federalist debate.

- **Q:** Anti-Federalist worry?
  **A:** Too much central power, no bill of rights.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bill of Rights
*Student:* Paraphrase the first 10 amendments and create an illustrated Bill of Rights booklet.

*How to teach:* Paraphrase the 10 amendments; make a booklet. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** First Amendment?
  **A:** Speech, religion, press, assembly, petition.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rights in action
*Student:* Read 6 scenarios and decide which amendment protects the person in each.

*How to teach:* Read 6 scenarios; identify amendments. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Police search without a warrant?
  **A:** Fourth Amendment.

### Day 4 (Thursday): American creed and songs
*Student:* Read and sing 'The Star-Spangled Banner' and 'America the Beautiful' and explain which ideals each expresses.

*How to teach:* Read and sing the songs; discuss ideals. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Who wrote the Star-Spangled Banner?
  **A:** Francis Scott Key, 1814.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on the Constitution and Bill of Rights.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** When was the Bill of Rights ratified?
  **A:** 1791.

**Assessment:** Bill of Rights booklet and Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Booklet: 10 amendments paraphrased. Unit 8 test key: branches, checks, federalism, ratification, Bill of Rights.

## Art & Music 5: Composing a melody with Chrome Music Lab

**Prepare:** Open Chrome Music Lab Song Maker (set to 4 bars, C major) and find the opening of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony. Print staff paper and a criteria checklist: memorable motif, question and answer phrases, ends on the tonic (C), repetition with variation, steady rhythm, accompaniment.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a composition needs many different ideas. Strong melodies repeat and develop a few motifs.
- Ending on a random note. An 'answer' phrase sounds finished when it ends on the tonic (home note).

### Day 1 (Monday): Motifs
*Student:* Study how Beethoven's Fifth Symphony grows from a 4-note motif and invent 3 motifs of your own.

*How to teach:* Listen to Beethoven's Fifth opening (short-short-short-long); show how the motif repeats at different pitches. Your child invents 3 motifs of 3–5 notes.

- **Q:** What is a motif?
  **A:** A short musical idea that is repeated and developed through a piece.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Phrases
*Student:* Compose a 4-measure question phrase and a 4-measure answer phrase that ends on the tonic in Song Maker.

*How to teach:* Compose a 4-measure 'question' phrase ending on a note other than C, and a 4-measure 'answer' ending on C.

- **Q:** Why does the answer phrase end on the tonic?
  **A:** The tonic sounds like 'home,' so the music feels finished (a cadence).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adding rhythm and harmony
*Student:* Add a percussion pattern and a simple two-chord accompaniment.

*How to teach:* Add a percussion pattern and I–V accompaniment (C and G chords) in Song Maker. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What two chords make a simple accompaniment in C major?
  **A:** C major (I) and G major (V).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Notation
*Student:* Write the melody on staff paper or save and print the Song Maker link and screenshot.

*How to teach:* Write the melody on staff paper or save the Song Maker link and screenshot. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why write the music down?
  **A:** So it can be performed again by you or others exactly as composed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share the draft composition and collect feedback with a criteria checklist.

*How to teach:* Share the draft; family uses the criteria checklist. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Give one criterion of a strong melody.
  **A:** A memorable motif, repetition, ending on the tonic, or a clear phrase structure.

**Assessment:** Composition draft and notation.

**Answer key:** Composition draft: 8 measures (question + answer), motif repeated or varied, ends on tonic C, percussion and I–V accompaniment, notation or saved link. Proficient = 5 of 6 criteria.

## PE 5: Modified softball games

**Prepare:** Gather foam bat, wiffle balls, tee, and bases. Print a diagram of the 9 fielding positions. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Every fielder chases the ball. Each position covers an area and a base.
- Thinking one strong player wins the game. Teamwork and positive talk matter.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hit and run
*Student:* Play 'hit the bat and run' where batters run bases while fielders return the ball.

*How to teach:* Play 'hit the bat and run' — batter hits, runs bases; fielders get the ball back to home. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** What decides how many bases you can run?
  **A:** How quickly the fielders return the ball to home.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Modified game
*Student:* Play a modified softball game off a tee with everyone batting each inning.

*How to teach:* Play a modified game off a tee, everyone bats each inning. Walk through the rules and boundaries first, then play; pause once to ask a quick strategy question ('Where is the open space?') and praise fair play and effort as much as skill.

- **Q:** How many outs in a half-inning?
  **A:** Three.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fielding positions
*Student:* Learn the 9 fielding positions and responsibilities with scenarios.

*How to teach:* Learn the 9 positions: pitcher, catcher, first base, second base, shortstop, third base, left field, center field, right field.

- **Q:** Name the 9 fielding positions.
  **A:** Pitcher, catcher, first baseman, second baseman, shortstop, third baseman, left fielder, center fielder, right fielder.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play a full modified game and practice positive talk to teammates.

*How to teach:* Play a full modified game; practice positive talk. Walk through the rules and boundaries first, then play; pause once to ask a quick strategy question ('Where is the open space?') and praise fair play and effort as much as skill.

- **Q:** Give an example of positive teammate talk.
  **A:** 'Nice try! You'll get the next one.' or 'Great hustle!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Complete a striking and fielding skills test and reflect on sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Skills test (tee hits, grounders, flies); reflect on sportsmanship. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Why does sportsmanship matter in team games?
  **A:** It keeps the game fair, safe and fun, and builds trust among teammates and opponents.

**Assessment:** Striking and fielding skills test.

**Answer key:** Skills test: tee hits into fair territory /10, grounders /10, flies /10. Proficient = 6+ each. Reflection includes one specific example of good sportsmanship.

## Health 5: Influences: family, peers and media

**Prepare:** Print an influence web template (you in the center; family, friends, media, culture, community around). Write 6 peer-pressure scenarios (spoken, unspoken, online). Find an alcohol ad (magazine or online) to analyze.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking peer pressure is always spoken. Unspoken pressure (everyone doing something) and online pressure are powerful too.
- Thinking all influences are negative. Family rules, coaches, and goals are positive influences (protective factors).

### Day 1 (Monday): Influence web
*Student:* Make a web of influences (family, friends, media, culture, community) on decisions about drugs.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the influence web, noting positive and negative influences on decisions about drugs.

- **Q:** Give one positive and one negative influence on decisions about drugs.
  **A:** Positive: family rules, a coach, friends who don't use. Negative: ads that glamorize drinking, older kids who vape.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Peer pressure types
*Student:* Identify spoken, unspoken and online peer pressure in 6 scenarios.

*How to teach:* Your child labels 6 scenarios as spoken, unspoken or online pressure. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Everyone at a party is vaping, but no one asks you. What kind of pressure is that?
  **A:** Unspoken peer pressure.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Alcohol ads
*Student:* Analyze an alcohol advertisement for target audience and hidden messages.

*How to teach:* Analyze an alcohol ad: who is it targeting, what does it suggest, what does it leave out? Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What do alcohol ads usually leave out?
  **A:** Health risks, addiction, impaired driving, hangovers, and that it's illegal under 21.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Protective factors
*Student:* List protective factors in your life (family rules, activities, goals) and how they help.

*How to teach:* List protective factors in your child's life and how each helps. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a protective factor?
  **A:** Something in your life that lowers the chance of risky behavior, like strong family relationships, activities, goals or supportive friends.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph analyzing the strongest influence on you and how to respond.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph on the strongest influence and how to respond. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How can you respond to a negative influence?
  **A:** Recognize it, think about your values and goals, use refusal skills, and choose friends who support you.

**Assessment:** Analysis paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = names the strongest influence, explains how it affects choices with an example, and gives a specific healthy response; 3 = names and explains; 2 = names only; 1 = missing.
