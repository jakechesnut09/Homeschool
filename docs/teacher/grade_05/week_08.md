# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 8

## ELA 5: Hatchet chapters 14–17: the moose and the tornado

**Prepare:** Preview Hatchet chapters 14–17 (the moose attack and the tornado). Draw a plot mountain. Set up a phone to record the fluency reading.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking theme equals a story event. Theme is the lesson about life (e.g., resilience), not what happened.
- Choosing evidence that only retells. Pick quotations that show Brian's attitude.
- Reading dramatic passages too fast. Model pausing at commas and slowing for important moments.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 14–15
*Student:* Read chapters 14–15 and plot the major events of the novel so far on a plot mountain.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 14–15. Your child plots major events so far on a plot mountain (crash, fire, low point, new Brian…).

- **Q:** What is the climax-building pattern of the novel?
  **A:** Each problem (hunger, fire, shelter) is followed by a small victory, building toward greater challenges.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapter 16
*Student:* Read chapter 16 (the tornado) and list what Brian lost and what he still has, explaining his reaction.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 16. Your child lists what Brian lost and what he still has after the moose attack and the tornado, and explains his reaction.

- **Q:** How does Brian react to losing his shelter and supplies?
  **A:** He is hurt and discouraged but does not give up; he starts rebuilding, showing he has changed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapter 17
*Student:* Read chapter 17 and discuss the theme of resilience in a Socratic-style conversation, citing two page numbers.

*How to teach:* Read chapter 17; hold a conversation on resilience. Your child cites two page numbers.

- **Q:** What does Brian notice after the tornado that gives him new hope?
  **A:** The tail of the plane is now sticking up out of the lake, and he remembers there may be a survival pack inside.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fluency and expression
*Student:* Practice reading a dramatic passage from chapter 16 three times with expression and record the final reading.

*How to teach:* Your child reads a dramatic passage from chapter 16 three times with expression, then records the best reading. Model one reading with expression yourself first. Compare the first and final recordings together and name one improvement.

- **Q:** What makes reading 'with expression'?
  **A:** Changing pace, volume and tone to match the action and feelings; pausing at punctuation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a theme paragraph: 'What does Hatchet teach about mistakes?' with two quotations and explanation.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a theme paragraph on 'What does Hatchet teach about mistakes?' with two quotations. Remind your child to use the quote sandwich (introduce, quote, explain). Score with the rubric and point to one strong sentence.

- **Q:** State a theme about mistakes.
  **A:** Mistakes are how we learn: Brian fails often (sick from berries, porcupine quills) but each mistake teaches him to survive.

**Assessment:** Theme paragraph scored on claim, evidence and explanation; fluency recording.

**Answer key:** Theme paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two exact quotations with page numbers, each explained, concluding sentence; 3 = claim and evidence with partial explanation; 2 = summary with weak claim; 1 = no claim/evidence. Fluency: expressive reading with appropriate phrasing and pace.

## Math 5: Estimating quotients, interpreting remainders, unit assessment

**Prepare:** Word problem cards (write 8 problems such as 230 students on buses holding 48; 150 pencils in packs of 24). Prepare the Unit 2 test from the key.

**Watch for:**
- Always rounding remainders down. Ask 'what does the leftover mean?' — extra students still need a bus (round up).
- Picking non-compatible estimates (3,476 ÷ 58 ≈ 3,500 ÷ 60). Choose numbers that divide easily: 3,600 ÷ 60.
- Not adjusting a trial quotient that's too big. If the product is larger than the part being divided, lower the digit by one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Compatible numbers
*Student:* Estimate 15 quotients like 3,476 / 58 ≈ 3,600 / 60 and decide whether the actual quotient is greater or less.

*How to teach:* Model compatible numbers: 3,476 ÷ 58 ≈ 3,600 ÷ 60 = 60. Since 3,476 < 3,600, the real quotient is a bit less. The child estimates 15 quotients and predicts greater/less.

- **Q:** Estimate 3,476 ÷ 58. Is the real answer greater or less?
  **A:** ≈ 3,600 ÷ 60 = 60; the real quotient is less (59 R54).
- **Q:** Estimate 2,712 ÷ 39.
  **A:** ≈ 2,800 ÷ 40 = 70.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adjusting estimates
*Student:* Divide 8 problems where the first estimate is too high or too low and adjust the trial quotient.

*How to teach:* Model a trial quotient that is too high: 245 ÷ 35, try 8 → 280 is too big, try 7 → 245. The child solves 8 problems, showing crossed-out trials.

- **Q:** 245 ÷ 35 = ?
  **A:** 7 (8 × 35 = 280 is too high).
- **Q:** 612 ÷ 18 = ?
  **A:** 34

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interpreting remainders
*Student:* Solve 8 word problems (buses for 230 students, packs of 24 pencils) and decide whether to round up, drop or use the remainder.

*How to teach:* Read each problem and ask what the remainder means. Decide: round up, drop it, or use it as the answer. The child solves 8 word problems and writes the decision.

- **Q:** 230 students ride buses holding 48 each. How many buses?
  **A:** 230 ÷ 48 = 4 R38, so 5 buses (round up).
- **Q:** 150 pencils are packed 24 to a box. How many full boxes?
  **A:** 6 R6, so 6 full boxes (drop the remainder).
- **Q:** How many pencils are left over?
  **A:** 6 (use the remainder).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of multiplication, expressions and division and write your own word problem for each.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of multiplication, expressions and division. For each problem type, the child writes and solves an original word problem.

- **Q:** Evaluate (125 × 12) ÷ 25.
  **A:** 1,500 ÷ 25 = 60

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on multi-digit multiplication, division and numerical expressions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test independently, then review mistakes together. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** A theater has 1,248 seats in rows of 32. How many rows?
  **A:** 39

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test covering 5.NBT.B.5, 5.NBT.B.6, 5.OA.A.1–A.2.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test key (suggested): 1) 345 × 26 = 8,970 2) 1,207 × 43 = 51,901 3) 268 × 154 = 41,272 4) (9 + 6) × 4 − 10 = 50 5) 3 × [20 − (4 × 2)] = 36 6) 'add 12 and 8, then divide by 4' = (12 + 8) ÷ 4 = 5 7) 7 × (45 + 13) is 7 times 45 + 13 8) 1,680 ÷ 40 = 42 9) 2,914 ÷ 31 = 94 10) 6,525 ÷ 75 = 87 11) 4,000 ÷ 64 = 62 R32 12) 230 students, vans of 12 → 230 ÷ 12 = 19 R2, so 20 vans. Scoring: 11–12 mastery, 9–10 proficient, 6–8 developing.

## Science 5: Chemical reactions: making new substances

**Prepare:** Baking soda, vinegar, milk, lemon juice, steel wool (wear gloves), 3 jars, balloons, red cabbage juice, 6 household liquids (lemon juice, vinegar, water, baking soda water, soap water, milk). Do not mix bleach or ammonia.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any change is a chemical reaction. Melting, dissolving and tearing are not; new substances must form.
- Thinking bubbles always mean a reaction. Boiling water bubbles but makes no new substance.
- Thinking rust is dirt on the metal. Rust is a new substance (iron oxide) formed from iron, oxygen and water.

### Day 1 (Monday): Signs of reaction
*Student:* Learn the signs of a new substance (gas, color change, temperature change, new solid) and mix baking soda and vinegar, recording observations.

*How to teach:* Teach the four signs: gas, color change, temperature change, new solid. Mix baking soda and vinegar in a cup; your child records observations.

- **Q:** What sign of a chemical reaction do you see with baking soda and vinegar?
  **A:** Bubbles of a new gas (carbon dioxide); the cup also feels slightly cooler.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Milk and lemon
*Student:* Add lemon juice to warm milk and observe curds forming; compare to milk mixed with water.

*How to teach:* Add lemon juice to warm milk and stir; compare with milk mixed with water. Adult warms the milk (not boiling). Your child records what they see in both cups and names which sign of a new substance appeared.

- **Q:** What forms and is it a new substance?
  **A:** Solid curds form — evidence of a new substance; milk mixed with water just gets thinner.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rust investigation
*Student:* Set up steel wool samples (dry, wet, wet with vinegar) in jars to observe rusting over the week.

*How to teach:* Set up steel wool in three jars: dry, wet with water, wet with vinegar. Your child predicts which rusts most.

- **Q:** Which do you predict will rust fastest?
  **A:** Steel wool with vinegar, then water; dry steel wool rusts very little.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cabbage indicator
*Student:* Make red cabbage juice indicator and test 6 household liquids, recording color changes.

*How to teach:* Make cabbage juice indicator; test 6 liquids and record colors. Adult chops and simmers red cabbage, then strains the purple juice. Your child adds a spoonful of juice to each liquid and records the color in a table, labeling acid or base.

- **Q:** What color does cabbage juice turn in vinegar and in baking soda water?
  **A:** Pink/red in vinegar (acid); green/blue in baking soda water (base).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort 10 changes as making new substances or not, using evidence from the investigations, and check the rust jars.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 10 changes (melting ice, burning wood, rusting, dissolving sugar, baking a cake, cutting paper…) as new substance or not, with evidence. Check the rust jars.

- **Q:** Is burning wood a chemical change? Is melting chocolate?
  **A:** Burning wood is chemical (new ash, gases, heat, light); melting chocolate is physical (same substance).

**Assessment:** Observation tables and change sorting with evidence.

**Answer key:** Sorting key: new substance — burning wood, rusting, baking a cake, baking soda + vinegar, milk + lemon curdling, fireworks; not new — melting ice, dissolving sugar, cutting paper, boiling water, freezing juice. Observation tables: sign(s) of reaction recorded for each test.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: The Atlantic world and unit review

**Prepare:** Map of Atlantic trade routes; an age-appropriate excerpt from Olaudah Equiano's narrative (1789). Preview it — it describes the Middle Passage. Midwest flashcards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the 'triangle' was a single fixed route. Many routes and goods.
- Thinking enslaved Africans came willingly.
- Mixing up Midwest capitals.

### Day 1 (Monday): Atlantic trade routes
*Student:* Draw the Atlantic trade routes and label goods carried on each leg.

*How to teach:* Draw a triangle connecting Europe, West Africa and the Americas on a map. Your child labels the goods on each leg and the direction of travel.

- **Q:** What goods went on each leg?
  **A:** Europe → Africa: cloth, guns, rum; Africa → Americas: enslaved people; Americas → Europe: sugar, tobacco, cotton.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Middle Passage
*Student:* Read an age-appropriate excerpt from Olaudah Equiano's narrative (1789) and discuss what it reveals about the Middle Passage.

*How to teach:* Read the excerpt aloud together and pause to discuss feelings and facts. Ask what Equiano's account shows that a trade map cannot.

- **Q:** What does Equiano describe?
  **A:** Crowded, filthy holds, sickness, chains and terror.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): States strand: Midwest
*Student:* Learn the 12 Midwest states and capitals with a blank map and flashcards.

*How to teach:* Introduce the 12 Midwest states and capitals on the blank map, then drill with flashcards. Your child labels the map from memory at the end.

- **Q:** Capital of Missouri? South Dakota?
  **A:** Jefferson City; Pierre.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Create a timeline of key explorations from 1492 to 1610 and review vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Your child places 6–8 explorations from 1492 to 1610 on a timeline in order. Review vocabulary words (sponsor, colony, Northwest Passage, Columbian Exchange) with quick definitions.

- **Q:** Put in order: Cartier, Columbus, Champlain.
  **A:** Columbus (1492), Cartier (1534), Champlain (1608).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on exploration and the Midwest states.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test independently and review mistakes using the timeline and maps. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Which country did Hudson sail for in 1609?
  **A:** The Netherlands.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and timeline.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test key: exploration motives; astrolabe; Columbus and the Taíno; Coronado, de Soto; Cabot, Cartier, Champlain, Hudson; Columbian Exchange; Atlantic trade; Midwest capitals: Ohio–Columbus, Indiana–Indianapolis, Illinois–Springfield, Michigan–Lansing, Wisconsin–Madison, Minnesota–Saint Paul, Iowa–Des Moines, Missouri–Jefferson City, North Dakota–Bismarck, South Dakota–Pierre, Nebraska–Lincoln, Kansas–Topeka.

## Art & Music 5: Recorder: C, D and E; ensemble playing

**Prepare:** Recorder, fingering chart and a metronome app. Print 'Merrily We Roll Along,' a simplified 'Ode to Joy' (B B C D D C B A G G A B B A A) and a 5-note warm-up. Fingerings (baroque and German are the same here): high C = thumb + finger 2; high D = finger 2 only (no thumb); low E = thumb + 1, 2, 3 (left) + 1, 2 (right hand).

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to lift the thumb for high D. High D uses only finger 2 of the left hand.
- Playing louder to reach low notes. Low notes need gentler, slower breath.

### Day 1 (Monday): High C and D
*Student:* Learn high C and D and play 'Merrily We Roll Along' and 'Old MacDonald' sections.

*How to teach:* Teach high C (thumb + 2) and high D (2 only). Play 'Merrily We Roll Along' and sections of 'Old MacDonald.'

- **Q:** What is the fingering for high D?
  **A:** Only the second finger of the left hand; no thumb.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Low E
*Student:* Learn low E and play a 5-note warm-up scale with a metronome.

*How to teach:* Teach low E using the right hand; play a 5-note warm-up (E-G-A-B-C or G-A-B-C-D) with a metronome at 60.

- **Q:** Why use a metronome?
  **A:** To keep a steady tempo and notice rushing or slowing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ensemble
*Student:* Play 'Ode to Joy' (simplified) with a sibling, parent or recording, staying together.

*How to teach:* Play 'Ode to Joy' with a sibling, parent or recording; start together after a count-off. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What is an ensemble?
  **A:** A group of musicians performing together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice log
*Student:* Practice the unit songs and write a practice log noting a tricky measure and how you fixed it.

*How to teach:* Your child keeps a practice log: what they practiced, a tricky measure, and how they fixed it (slow practice, repeat 5 times).

- **Q:** What's a good strategy for a tricky measure?
  **A:** Practice it slowly, alone, several times, then put it back in the song at tempo.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Perform two recorder songs and a rhythm reading for family, then evaluate with a performance rubric.

*How to teach:* Perform two recorder songs and a rhythm reading for family; evaluate with the rubric. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is a musical phrase?
  **A:** A musical 'sentence' — a group of notes that forms a complete idea, often ending at a breath.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 recorder and rhythm performance rubric.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric (1–4 each): correct notes; steady rhythm; clear tone; phrasing/breathing at phrase ends; stage presence. Rhythm reading: correct counting of 4 measures. Proficient = 3+ in each category.

## PE 5: Jump rope routine and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Gather ropes, music, and a phone/tablet to record. Optional: look up 'Jump Rope for Heart' (American Heart Association) for ideas. Invite an audience for Friday. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a routine is just a list of tricks. Transitions (how you move from one trick to the next without stopping) are part of the routine.
- Giving only general feedback ('good job'). Feedback should name a specific cue: 'Your criss-cross was on beat; open the arms sooner.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Routine planning
*Student:* Plan a 1-minute jump rope routine with at least 6 tricks and transitions.

*How to teach:* Help your child write a 1-minute routine with at least 6 tricks and how they'll transition (e.g., 8 bounces → 8 skiers).

- **Q:** About how many 8-counts fill 1 minute of music at 120 beats per minute?
  **A:** 120 beats ÷ 8 = 15 eight-counts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice
*Student:* Practice the routine with music, focusing on staying on beat.

*How to teach:* Practice the routine with music three times, counting aloud at first, then silently. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What should you do if you miss a jump in the middle?
  **A:** Restart the rope quickly and rejoin on the next 8-count without stopping the routine.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Record and review
*Student:* Record the routine and identify two spots to improve.

*How to teach:* Record the routine and watch together. Your child names two specific spots to improve. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Give an example of a specific improvement note.
  **A:** e.g., 'At count 33 I stopped to switch to skier—add a bounce step to transition smoothly.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Jump-a-thon
*Student:* Do a family 'Jump Rope for Heart' style challenge, counting total minutes jumped.

*How to teach:* Family jump-a-thon: everyone jumps in 1–2 minute turns; total the minutes. Talk about how jumping is good for heart health.

- **Q:** Why is jumping rope good for the heart?
  **A:** It is vigorous aerobic exercise that makes the heart and lungs work harder, which strengthens them over time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Perform the routine for an audience and give feedback on another performer's routine.

*How to teach:* Your child performs for an audience, then watches another performer and gives one praise and one cue-based suggestion.

- **Q:** What makes feedback helpful?
  **A:** It is specific, uses a skill cue, is kind, and gives one thing to try next.

**Assessment:** Routine rubric (tricks, rhythm, transitions).

**Answer key:** Routine rubric (each 1–4): Tricks — 6+ different tricks performed correctly; Rhythm — on the beat throughout; Transitions — no stops between tricks; Feedback — gives specific, cue-based, kind feedback. Proficient = 3 or higher in each.

## Health 5: Meal planning and food tracking

**Prepare:** Print a one-day food log (columns: time, food/drink, amount, food group) and gather grocery flyers. Set a realistic one-day budget with your child. Plan which meal you'll cook together on Thursday and buy ingredients; review knife safety (claw grip, cut away from body, cutting board on a damp towel).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a balanced day must be expensive. Beans, eggs, oats, bananas, frozen vegetables and canned fish are low-cost and nutritious.
- Forgetting drinks in a food log. Beverages (juice, soda, milk) count toward sugar and food groups.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food log
*Student:* Log everything you eat and drink for one day by food group.

*How to teach:* Your child logs everything eaten and drunk for a day, including amounts, by food group. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why record amounts and not just foods?
  **A:** To compare with MyPlate's recommended amounts (cups, ounces) and see if you got enough or too much.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Analyze the log
*Student:* Compare your log with MyPlate recommendations and highlight food groups that were over or under.

*How to teach:* Compare the log with MyPlate recommendations; highlight over and under groups. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** MyPlate says 2½ cups of vegetables and you had 1 cup. How much more do you need?
  **A:** 1½ cups.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a day
*Student:* Plan breakfast, lunch, dinner and two snacks for one day that meet MyPlate amounts within a set budget using grocery flyers.

*How to teach:* Your child plans three meals and two snacks within the budget using flyer prices. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name two inexpensive protein foods.
  **A:** Any two: dried or canned beans, eggs, peanut butter, canned tuna, lentils.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cook a meal
*Student:* Help prepare one planned meal with an adult, practicing handwashing and safe knife use.

*How to teach:* Cook the planned meal together: wash hands 20 seconds with soap, use the claw grip, separate raw meat from produce.

- **Q:** Why keep raw meat away from vegetables you'll eat raw?
  **A:** Raw meat can carry germs (like Salmonella or E. coli) that can spread to other food (cross-contamination).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the meal plan and complete the Nutrition unit test.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the plan, then takes the unit test. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** What does MyPlate recommend about grains?
  **A:** Make at least half of your grains whole grains.

**Assessment:** Meal plan presentation and unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test answer key: 1) six nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water 2) fruits and vegetables fill half the plate 3) 5% DV low / 20% DV high 4) 4 g sugar ≈ 1 teaspoon 5) check serving size first 6) ingredients listed by weight, most first 7) calcium builds bones; iron carries oxygen 8) choose water/milk over sugary drinks 9) wash hands 20 seconds 10) avoid cross-contamination. Meal plan rubric: 4 = meets MyPlate amounts for all groups, within budget, variety, clear presentation; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = major groups missing; 1 = incomplete.
