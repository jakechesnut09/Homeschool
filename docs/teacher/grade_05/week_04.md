# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 4

## ELA 5: 'All Summer in a Day' by Ray Bradbury; revising and publishing narratives

**Prepare:** Find 'All Summer in a Day' by Ray Bradbury (library anthology or CommonLit) and read it first. Note: the children lock Margot in a closet — a bullying scene worth discussing. Prepare a revision checklist and colored pens. Choose an unfamiliar short story for the Friday cold read.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking setting is just the place. Setting includes time and conditions; here the constant rain on Venus causes the conflict.
- Revising means just fixing spelling. Revising changes content (adding detail, slowing a moment); editing fixes conventions.
- Shifting verb tense in the middle of a narrative. Pick past tense and keep it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Setting and mood
*Student:* Read 'All Summer in a Day' and list Bradbury's descriptions of rain and sun; explain how the Venus setting causes the conflict.

*How to teach:* Read the story and list words describing rain and sun. Discuss how the setting — Venus, where it has rained for seven years and the sun appears for only about two hours every seven years — causes the conflict with Margot.

- **Q:** Why are the children jealous of Margot?
  **A:** She came from Earth (Ohio) only five years ago and remembers the sun; they were too young to remember it.
- **Q:** How does the setting cause the conflict?
  **A:** The endless rain makes the sun precious; Margot's memory of it sets her apart, and the children lock her in a closet just before the sun comes out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revising for pacing
*Student:* Revise the narrative by slowing down the most important moment with thoughts, feelings and sensory details (stretch it to at least a full paragraph).

*How to teach:* Your child finds the most important moment in the narrative and stretches it into at least a full paragraph with thoughts, feelings and sensory details. Model first with a dull sentence ('I was scared') stretched into several sentences of heartbeat, thoughts and sounds. Then have your child highlight the stretched moment in the draft.

- **Q:** Name three things you can add to slow down a moment.
  **A:** Thoughts, feelings, sensory details (sounds, smells), small actions, dialogue.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Editing
*Student:* Edit with a checklist for dialogue punctuation, commas in series, spelling and verb tense, then read it aloud to catch errors.

*How to teach:* Edit with the checklist (dialogue punctuation, series commas, spelling, consistent tense), then your child reads aloud to catch errors. Have your child mark each fix in colored pen so you can see what they caught; catch only patterns they missed, not every error.

- **Q:** Fix the tense: I ran to the door and I see my dad.
  **A:** I ran to the door and I saw my dad.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publishing
*Student:* Type the final narrative (aim for two pages in one sitting) and read it aloud to family as an author's chair.

*How to teach:* Your child types the final narrative and reads it aloud in an 'author's chair' to family who respond with one compliment and one question. Aim for one sitting; afterward ask, 'Which part are you proudest of and why?'

- **Q:** What makes a strong ending to a personal narrative?
  **A:** It resolves the moment and shows what the narrator felt, learned, or realized.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Take a cold-read assessment on an unfamiliar short story (theme, character, quoting evidence) and a root/prefix vocabulary quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the cold-read assessment on an unfamiliar story (theme, character, quoting evidence) and a vocabulary quiz on prefixes and roots from weeks 1–3. Do not help during the cold read; afterward score with the rubric and review one question together.

- **Q:** At the end of 'All Summer in a Day,' how do the children feel when they let Margot out?
  **A:** Ashamed and guilty — they can't look at each other and move slowly to unlock the door.

**Assessment:** Published personal narrative scored with a narrative rubric; Unit 1 cold-read assessment.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (4 levels): 4 = focused small moment, engaging lead, the key moment slowed with thoughts/sensory detail, correctly punctuated dialogue, satisfying ending, few errors; 3 = all parts present with minor gaps; 2 = summary-like, little detail or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Cold-read rubric (choose an unfamiliar grade-5 story from CommonLit): 4 = states a precise theme/central idea, explains character with 2+ exact quotations correctly punctuated and explained; 3 = accurate answer with at least one relevant quotation and explanation; 2 = accurate but general answer, evidence paraphrased or loosely connected; 1 = inaccurate or no evidence. Vocabulary quiz: prefixes un-/re-/dis-/mis-/pre- defined correctly.

## Math 5: Rounding decimals and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Vertical number line template (draw one), a grocery receipt with 6–10 items, Unit 1 test (write from the key below). Review weeks 1–3 notes.

**Watch for:**
- Rounding by looking at the wrong digit. Circle the rounding place, then underline the digit just to its right.
- Writing 7.96 rounded to the tenth as 7.10 or 7.9. Show on a number line it falls between 7.9 and 8.0; the answer is 8.0.
- Dropping trailing zeros that show the place (8.0 vs 8). Explain 8.0 tells that it was rounded to tenths.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rounding on a number line
*Student:* Round 12 decimals to the nearest tenth by placing them on a vertical number line between two benchmarks and marking the midpoint.

*How to teach:* Draw a vertical number line from 2.3 to 2.4 with 2.35 marked as the midpoint. Place 2.37, decide which benchmark it is closer to, and round. The child rounds 12 decimals this way.

- **Q:** Round 2.37 to the nearest tenth.
  **A:** 2.4 (2.37 is above the midpoint 2.35).
- **Q:** Round 6.349 to the nearest tenth.
  **A:** 6.3

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rounding to any place
*Student:* Round the same 10 numbers (like 4.386) to the nearest whole, tenth and hundredth and compare results in a table.

*How to teach:* Make a table with columns whole / tenth / hundredth. Round 4.386 together, then the child completes 10 rows and notes how the answers differ.

- **Q:** Round 4.386 to the nearest whole, tenth and hundredth.
  **A:** 4; 4.4; 4.39
- **Q:** Round 7.96 to the nearest tenth.
  **A:** 8.0

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rounding in context
*Student:* Round prices on a grocery receipt to the nearest dollar and estimate the total, then compare to the actual total.

*How to teach:* Round each receipt price to the nearest dollar and add for an estimate, then compare with the real total. Discuss whether the estimate was over or under and why.

- **Q:** Estimate $3.49 + $5.72 + $1.15 by rounding to the nearest dollar.
  **A:** $3 + $6 + $1 = $10 (actual $10.36).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of place value, powers of 10, comparing and rounding, and make a one-page 'decimal place value' reference sheet.

*How to teach:* Do a mixed review of the unit's skills, then the child builds a one-page reference sheet with a place value chart, powers of 10, comparing steps and a rounding example. Quiz orally as they go: 'What is 10 times 0.3? Round 2.65 to the tenth.' The reference sheet should include one worked example of each skill.

- **Q:** Write 4.6 × 10^2 in standard form, then round 3.482 to the nearest hundredth.
  **A:** 460; 3.48.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test (place value, exponents, comparing, rounding) and complete a self-reflection on the hardest item.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test independently. Then the child writes a short reflection on the hardest item and what helped.

- **Q:** Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.599? Explain.
  **A:** 0.6, because 6 tenths > 5 tenths.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test covering 5.NBT.A.1–A.4.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test key (suggested 15 items): 1) Value of 4 in 3.245 = 0.04 2) 0.8 is 10 times 0.08 3) 1/10 of 6 = 0.6 4) 5.3 × 100 = 530 5) 72 ÷ 1,000 = 0.072 6) 10^4 = 10,000 7) 9 × 10^3 = 9,000 8) 'six and fourteen thousandths' = 6.014 9) 0.42 > 0.402 10) Order 1.1, 1.01, 1.101 → 1.01, 1.1, 1.101 11) Round 3.649 to the tenth = 3.6 12) Round 3.649 to the hundredth = 3.65 13) Round 12.5 to the nearest whole = 13 14) Round 0.996 to the hundredth = 1.00 15) Explain why 2.5 × 10 = 25 (digits shift one place left). Scoring: 14–15 mastery, 11–13 proficient, 8–10 developing, below 8 reteach.

## Science 5: Identifying mystery powders

**Prepare:** Baking soda, salt, sugar, cornstarch, flour, vinegar, red cabbage juice (boil chopped red cabbage in water, strain) or iodine (adult only — poisonous, stains), magnifier, muffin tin, labels. Safety: no tasting powders in this lab.

**Watch for:**
- Identifying a powder by look alone. Several white powders look alike; use multiple tests.
- Thinking fizzing means boiling. Fizzing is a gas (carbon dioxide) being produced by a reaction.
- Mixing up dissolving and settling. Dissolved powders disappear; undissolved ones settle or cloud the water.

### Day 1 (Monday): Observing powders
*Student:* Examine 5 known white powders with a magnifying glass and record texture, crystal shape and feel.

*How to teach:* Your child examines each powder with a magnifier and records texture, crystal shape and feel (rubbed between fingers, then washed). Model the first powder by thinking aloud ('tiny cubes, gritty'). Remind your child to wash hands after touching and never taste.

- **Q:** How do salt and sugar look under a magnifier?
  **A:** Salt crystals look like small cubes; sugar crystals are larger and more irregular, glassy grains.
- **Q:** Which powders feel smooth and squeaky?
  **A:** Cornstarch (very fine and silky); flour is soft too.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water test
*Student:* Add each powder to water and record whether it dissolves, clumps or settles.

*How to teach:* Add a spoonful of each powder to water and stir. Record dissolves, clumps or settles.

- **Q:** Which powders dissolve?
  **A:** Salt and sugar dissolve; baking soda mostly dissolves; cornstarch and flour make cloudy water and settle.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Vinegar and indicator tests
*Student:* Test each powder with vinegar (fizzing) and an indicator (adult-supervised iodine or cabbage juice) and record results.

*How to teach:* Test each powder with vinegar and with the indicator. Record fizzing and color change.

- **Q:** Which powder fizzes with vinegar?
  **A:** Baking soda — it reacts to make carbon dioxide gas.
- **Q:** What does iodine do with cornstarch and flour?
  **A:** Turns them blue-black because they contain starch. (With cabbage juice, baking soda turns it green-blue because it is a base.)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mystery mixture
*Student:* Identify an adult-prepared mystery powder (or mixture of two) using the test results chart.

*How to teach:* Prepare a mystery powder (or a mix of two, e.g., baking soda + cornstarch). Your child runs the tests and compares with the chart.

- **Q:** A mystery powder fizzes with vinegar and turns iodine blue-black. What is it?
  **A:** A mixture of baking soda and a starch (cornstarch or flour).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER identifying the mystery powder and take a quiz on material properties.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER identifying the mystery powder and takes a properties quiz. Have your child state the claim aloud first, then point to the rows of the chart that are the evidence. Score the CER with the rubric before the quiz.

- **Q:** Why use several tests instead of one?
  **A:** Different powders share some properties; combining test results narrows it down to one identity.

**Assessment:** Powder test chart, CER and properties quiz.

**Answer key:** CER rubric: 4 = clear, accurate claim; specific evidence with numbers from the data; reasoning that links evidence to the science idea (particles, conservation, etc.); 3 = accurate claim and evidence, reasoning partial; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or inaccurate. Quiz key (suggested): 1) baking soda fizzes with vinegar 2) cornstarch/flour turn iodine blue-black 3) salt and sugar dissolve in water 4) cornstarch does not dissolve 5) baking soda turns cabbage juice green/blue (base) 6) properties are characteristics used to identify a substance.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Comparing First Peoples; unit project

**Prepare:** Poster board, art supplies, and research sources from NMAI Native Knowledge 360 or a nation's official website. Southeast flashcards.

**Watch for:**
- Copying facts without connecting them to geography.
- Thinking Native nations ended long ago. Many have governments, languages and cultural programs today.
- Mixing up Southeast capitals.

### Day 1 (Monday): Research
*Student:* Choose one nation (e.g., Hopi, Chinook, Lakota, Cherokee) and research its homeland, food, shelter, government and stories.

*How to teach:* Help your child pick a nation and find 2–3 reliable sources (NMAI Native Knowledge 360, the nation's own website, a library book). They take notes in five labeled boxes: homeland, food, shelter, government, stories.

- **Q:** Where is the Hopi homeland?
  **A:** Northeastern Arizona, on mesas.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Today's nations
*Student:* Learn that these nations still exist today and find one fact about the nation's present-day government or culture.

*How to teach:* Help your child find the nation's official website. They record one fact about its present-day government or culture.

- **Q:** Name a present-day Native nation government.
  **A:** The Cherokee Nation (Tahlequah, Oklahoma) has an elected Principal Chief and council.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create the project
*Student:* Create an illustrated poster or museum card set showing how geography shaped the nation's life.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a poster or museum card set showing how geography shaped the nation's life, with pictures and captions. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** What should each caption explain?
  **A:** How the environment shaped that part of life.

### Day 4 (Thursday): States strand: Southeast
*Student:* Learn Southeast states and capitals and locate the homelands of the Cherokee, Creek and Seminole on a map.

*How to teach:* Say each Southeast state and capital aloud while pointing to the map, then quiz with flashcards. Your child shades the Cherokee, Creek (Muscogee) and Seminole homelands and labels them.

- **Q:** Where were the Seminole?
  **A:** Florida.
- **Q:** What is Tennessee's capital?
  **A:** Nashville.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Present the project and take the Unit 1 test on First Peoples and Southeast states.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the poster in 2–3 minutes while you ask one follow-up question. Then give the Unit 1 test without notes.

- **Q:** Name one region and its main shelter.
  **A:** Southwest–adobe pueblos; Pacific Northwest–cedar plank houses; Plains–tipis; Eastern Woodlands–longhouses.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 project rubric and test.

**Answer key:** Project rubric: 4 = accurate homeland, food, shelter, government, stories, clear geography connection, present-day fact, neat; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing categories; 1 = incomplete. Unit 1 test: regions matched to peoples and shelters; Great Law of Peace; Southeast capitals: Virginia–Richmond, West Virginia–Charleston, Kentucky–Frankfort, Tennessee–Nashville, North Carolina–Raleigh, South Carolina–Columbia, Georgia–Atlanta, Florida–Tallahassee, Alabama–Montgomery, Mississippi–Jackson.

## Art & Music 5: Drawing project: a detailed observational drawing

**Prepare:** Cut a cardboard viewfinder (a 2 × 3 inch window in cardstock). Gather drawing paper and pencils. Learn the 'I notice, I wonder' feedback format.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the first drawing should be the final. Thumbnails let you try compositions quickly before committing.
- Giving every area equal detail. A focal point is where the most contrast and detail go, guiding the viewer's eye.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a view
*Student:* Use a cardstock viewfinder to choose a composition of a corner of your home and make 3 thumbnail sketches.

*How to teach:* Your child uses the viewfinder to frame three views of a corner of the home and makes a quick thumbnail of each, then picks one.

- **Q:** What is a composition?
  **A:** The arrangement of the parts of an artwork.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drawing
*Student:* Lightly block in the composition with contour lines and perspective.

*How to teach:* Lightly block in the chosen view with contour lines and perspective, keeping lines light for changes.

- **Q:** Why draw lightly at first?
  **A:** So you can erase and change without leaving marks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shading
*Student:* Add value and texture to the drawing, establishing a focal point.

*How to teach:* Add value and texture; make the focal point have the strongest contrast and most detail. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How can you make a focal point stand out?
  **A:** Use the strongest contrast, most detail, or place it where lines lead the eye.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique and revise
*Student:* Get feedback using 'I notice, I wonder' and make one revision.

*How to teach:* Give 'I notice, I wonder' feedback; your child makes one revision. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Give an example of 'I notice, I wonder' feedback.
  **A:** 'I notice the chair has strong shadows. I wonder if the background could be lighter to make the chair stand out.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an artist statement describing your choices and revision, and file the drawing in your portfolio.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an artist statement (what, how, why, revision) and files the drawing. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What belongs in an artist statement?
  **A:** What the artwork shows, techniques and choices made, the focal point and why, and what was revised.

**Assessment:** Drawing project rubric and artist statement.

**Answer key:** Drawing rubric (1–4 each): composition (thumbnail-based, clear focal point); accurate contour and perspective; full value range and texture; craftsmanship; revision shown. Artist statement: proficient names subject, two techniques, focal point choice and the revision made.

## PE 5: Baseline FITNESSGRAM assessment

**Prepare:** Print a FITNESSGRAM Healthy Fitness Zone (HFZ) chart for your child's age and sex (available from FITNESSGRAM/the California Department of Education), a score sheet, the PACER audio, stopwatch and the curl-up strip. Test on rested days with water nearby; space tests across the week. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the goal is to beat other kids. FITNESSGRAM compares you to a health standard (the Healthy Fitness Zone), not to other people.
- Believing one low score means 'unfit.' A baseline just shows where you start; scores rise with training.

### Day 1 (Monday): Aerobic test
*Student:* Complete the PACER (or one-mile run) and record laps or time.

*How to teach:* Warm up, then run the PACER until your child misses the line twice (or run/walk one mile and time it). Record laps or time immediately.

- **Q:** When does the PACER end for a runner?
  **A:** The second time they fail to reach the line before the beep.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength tests
*Student:* Complete the curl-up and push-up tests and record scores.

*How to teach:* Curl-up test at 1 every 3 seconds until two form errors or 75 reached; rest; then the push-up test at the same cadence until two form errors. Record both.

- **Q:** What is the cadence for the curl-up and push-up tests?
  **A:** One repetition every 3 seconds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility tests
*Student:* Complete the trunk lift and sit-and-reach and record scores.

*How to teach:* Trunk lift: two trials, record the best (max 12 in). Back-saver sit-and-reach: measure each side to the nearest half inch (max 12 in).

- **Q:** Why do we measure the sit-and-reach on both sides?
  **A:** Flexibility can differ between the right and left legs, so each side is tested separately.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing to HFZ
*Student:* Compare your scores to the Healthy Fitness Zone chart for your age and highlight strengths and areas to improve.

*How to teach:* Look up each score on the HFZ chart together. Your child highlights scores in the HFZ green and circles any 'Needs Improvement.'

- **Q:** If your score is in the Healthy Fitness Zone, what does that mean?
  **A:** Your fitness in that area meets the level associated with good health for your age and sex.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write one fitness goal for the year and an activity to support it, then play an active game of choice.

*How to teach:* Your child writes one goal ('I will increase my PACER from 18 to 25 laps by May') plus an activity to reach it. Finish with an active game of their choice.

- **Q:** Make this goal specific: 'Get better at push-ups.'
  **A:** Example: 'I will go from 6 to 10 push-ups by the spring test by doing push-ups 3 days a week.'

**Assessment:** Baseline FITNESSGRAM score sheet and goal statement.

**Answer key:** Score sheet complete for all five items (PACER laps or mile time, curl-ups, push-ups, trunk lift inches, sit-and-reach L/R inches), each compared correctly to the HFZ chart. Goal statement rubric: 4 = specific number, deadline, and a matching activity with frequency; 3 = specific and measurable but no plan; 2 = vague goal; 1 = missing.

## Health 5: Nutrients and MyPlate for a growing body

**Prepare:** Print MyPlate (MyPlate.gov / MyPlate Kids' Place) and gather food magazines or grocery flyers, scissors and glue. Use the MyPlate Plan tool to get amounts for your child's age, sex and activity level.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fat is always bad. The body needs some fat (especially unsaturated) for energy and to absorb vitamins A, D, E and K.
- Forgetting water is a nutrient. Water is essential for every body process.

### Day 1 (Monday): Six nutrients
*Student:* Make a 6-panel chart of carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals and water with each job and two food sources.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a 6-panel chart with each nutrient's job and two foods. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is the main job of carbohydrates and of protein?
  **A:** Carbohydrates are the body's main energy source; protein builds and repairs tissues like muscle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Nutrients for growth
*Student:* Read about calcium, iron and protein needs during growth spurts and list foods rich in each.

*How to teach:* Read about calcium (bones), iron (carries oxygen in blood), and protein (growth) during growth spurts; list rich foods.

- **Q:** Name two foods rich in calcium and two rich in iron.
  **A:** Calcium: milk, yogurt, cheese, fortified soy milk, broccoli. Iron: lean meat, beans, spinach, fortified cereal.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): MyPlate check
*Student:* Use MyPlate Kids to find daily food-group amounts for your age and activity level and record them.

*How to teach:* Use MyPlate to look up daily amounts and record them. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Which food group should fill about half the plate?
  **A:** Fruits and vegetables.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plate collage
*Student:* Cut magazine pictures to build a balanced MyPlate collage that meets the recommended amounts.

*How to teach:* Build a balanced plate collage from magazine pictures. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why is variety important on MyPlate?
  **A:** Different foods provide different nutrients, so variety helps you get all you need.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer a 10-question nutrients quiz and explain your collage to a family member.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-question quiz; your child explains the collage. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Which nutrient helps digestion and is found in whole grains, beans and vegetables?
  **A:** Fiber (a type of carbohydrate).

**Assessment:** Nutrients quiz and MyPlate collage.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) carbohydrates — energy 2) protein — build/repair 3) fats — stored energy, vitamin absorption 4) vitamins — help body processes 5) minerals — e.g., calcium for bones 6) water — temperature, transport 7) calcium foods: dairy 8) iron foods: meat, beans 9) half plate: fruits & veg 10) fiber sources: whole grains, beans. Collage: all five groups with vegetables and fruits ≈ half the plate, grains (half whole), protein, dairy.
