# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 35

## ELA 5: Building a multimedia presentation

**Prepare:** Computer with free slideshow tool; images with credits.

**Watch for:**
- Too much text on slides.
- Media that doesn't support a main idea.
- Forgetting image credits.

### Day 1 (Monday): Storyboarding
*Student:* Storyboard 8 slides: title, 5 content slides, conclusion and sources.

*How to teach:* Your child sketches 8 boxes: title, 5 content slides, conclusion and sources, with notes for each.

- **Q:** Name the 8 slides.
  **A:** Title, 5 content, conclusion, sources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Writing slide text
*Student:* Write concise slide text and a full speaker script for each slide.

*How to teach:* Your child writes short slide text (bullets, not paragraphs) and a full speaker script for each slide.

- **Q:** How much text per slide?
  **A:** A few key words or short bullets.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adding media
*Student:* Add a map, captioned photos and one audio or video element that supports a main idea, crediting sources.

*How to teach:* Your child adds a map, captioned photos and one audio or video clip, crediting each source on the slide or sources page.

- **Q:** Why credit images?
  **A:** To respect creators and show sources.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Editing
*Student:* Edit script and slides for spelling and conventions, and practice timing to 5 minutes.

*How to teach:* Your child proofreads slides and script, then times a practice run to about 5 minutes, trimming as needed.

- **Q:** How to stay in time?
  **A:** Rehearse with a timer and trim.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Do a full rehearsal for a family member and revise based on feedback.

*How to teach:* Your child gives a full rehearsal for a family member who gives feedback on clarity, pace and visuals; revise accordingly.

- **Q:** What feedback to ask for?
  **A:** Clarity, pace, visuals.

**Assessment:** Storyboard, script and slide deck checklist.

**Answer key:** Checklist: 8 slides storyboarded; concise text; full script; map, captioned photos, one media element; sources credited; ~5 minutes; edited.

## Math 5: Cumulative review: fractions, volume and geometry

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, graph paper, review packet; write the end-of-year assessment from the key (about 25 items across domains).

**Watch for:**
- Using a common denominator to multiply fractions.
- Adding instead of multiplying dimensions for volume.
- Forgetting x comes first in ordered pairs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fraction add/subtract
*Student:* Solve 12 mixed number addition and subtraction problems and 2 word problems.

*How to teach:* Solve 12 mixed-number add/subtract problems and 2 word problems. Your child solves the problems with estimates. Check every answer as they finish each set.

- **Q:** 3 2/3 + 2 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 6 5/12
- **Q:** 5 1/6 − 2 1/2 = ?
  **A:** 2 2/3

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fraction multiply/divide
*Student:* Solve 12 fraction multiplication and unit-fraction division problems and explain 1 with a model.

*How to teach:* Solve 12 multiplication and unit-fraction division problems; explain one with a model. Your child solves the problems and draws a model (area model or number line) for one, explaining it aloud.

- **Q:** 3/4 × 2/5 = ?
  **A:** 3/10
- **Q:** 4 ÷ 1/3 = ?
  **A:** 12

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Volume and geometry
*Student:* Find volumes of 6 prisms and composite figures and classify 6 quadrilaterals.

*How to teach:* Find volumes of prisms and composite figures; classify 6 quadrilaterals. Your child shows the formula used for each volume and lists the properties behind each classification.

- **Q:** Volume of 7 × 3 × 2?
  **A:** 42 cubic units.
- **Q:** 4 equal sides, no right angles?
  **A:** Rhombus.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graphing
*Student:* Graph and interpret a pattern table and a real-world data set on a coordinate plane.

*How to teach:* Graph a pattern table and a data set; interpret points. Your child graphs one pattern table and one data set, then explains what two points mean.

- **Q:** Rules 'add 2' and 'add 6' from 0: 3rd pair?
  **A:** (4, 12)

### Day 5 (Friday): End-of-year assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year cumulative assessment covering all fifth-grade domains.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year cumulative assessment. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** 7/8 − 1/4 = ?
  **A:** 5/8

**Assessment:** End-of-year cumulative assessment.

**Answer key:** End-of-year key (suggested sample items): 1) value of 5 in 2.354 = 0.05 2) 0.38 × 100 = 38 3) round 6.947 to tenth = 6.9 4) 307 × 24 = 7,368 5) 2,592 ÷ 48 = 54 6) 2 × [3 + (10 − 4)] = 18 7) 5.6 + 3.75 = 9.35 8) 1.2 × 0.4 = 0.48 9) 6.3 ÷ 0.9 = 7 10) 2/3 + 3/5 = 1 4/15 11) 4 1/2 − 1 2/3 = 2 5/6 12) 3/4 × 16 = 12 13) 2/3 × 3/8 = 1/4 14) 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 15) 5 ÷ 1/4 = 20 16) 9 ÷ 4 = 2 1/4 17) volume 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 18) composite 3×2×2 + 4×2×2 = 12 + 16 = 28 19) 6 ft = 72 in 20) 3.5 kg = 3,500 g 21) (5, 2) is 5 right, 2 up 22) a square is always a rhombus 23) 'add 3'/'add 9' pairs: (3, 9) 24) 3/4 × 8 < 8. Scoring: 90%+ mastery, 75–89% proficient, 60–74% developing.

## Science 5: Capstone investigation: planning and testing

**Prepare:** Materials for the chosen investigation; science notebook.

**Watch for:**
- Testable questions must be measurable.
- Too many variables.
- Too few trials.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a question
*Student:* Choose a testable question connected to this year's science (e.g., Does temperature affect how fast compost decomposes?).

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a testable question. Brainstorm questions together. Your child picks one that is measurable.

- **Q:** Is 'Do plants like music?' testable?
  **A:** Rewrite to: 'Does music affect bean plant height in 2 weeks?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planning
*Student:* Write a hypothesis, list variables and controls and write a step-by-step procedure.

*How to teach:* Write a hypothesis, variables and procedure. Your child writes the hypothesis, variables, controls and steps.

- **Q:** Name the independent and dependent variables.
  **A:** Independent = what you change; dependent = what you measure.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Running trials
*Student:* Conduct the investigation with at least 3 trials and record data.

*How to teach:* Run at least 3 trials and record data. Your child runs at least 3 trials and records data.

- **Q:** Why 3 trials?
  **A:** To make results reliable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): More trials
*Student:* Finish trials and organize data in a table.

*How to teach:* Finish trials; organize data. Your child finishes trials and organizes data in a table with averages.

- **Q:** How to organize data?
  **A:** A table with trials and averages.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a graph of the results and check it with a parent for accuracy.

*How to teach:* Make a graph; check accuracy with a parent. Your child graphs results; check scales and labels together.

- **Q:** Which graph fits?
  **A:** Bar graph for categories; line graph for change over time.

**Assessment:** Investigation plan and data table.

**Answer key:** Plan rubric: testable question, hypothesis, variables, procedure, 3 trials, data table.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: The overland trails

**Prepare:** Trail maps; pioneer diary excerpts; masking tape. Preview Donner Party content. Write the Unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pioneers rode in wagons.
- Thinking trails were easy.
- Thinking the Donner Party was typical.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trails map
*Student:* Map the Oregon, California, Mormon and Santa Fe Trails and label rivers, mountains and landmarks like Chimney Rock.

*How to teach:* Map the four trails and landmarks. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Where is Chimney Rock?
  **A:** Nebraska.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Packing the wagon
*Student:* Tape a 4-by-10-foot wagon outline on the floor and choose supplies that fit, explaining each choice.

*How to teach:* Tape a 4-by-10-foot wagon; choose supplies. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Essential?
  **A:** Flour, water barrel, tools.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pioneer diaries
*Student:* Read excerpts of pioneer women's diaries and list hardships caused by terrain, rivers and climate.

*How to teach:* Read diary excerpts; list hardships. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Hardship?
  **A:** River crossings, disease, weather.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Arrival in California
*Student:* Read about the Donner Party crossing the Sierra Nevada and life at the end of the California Trail.

*How to teach:* Read about the Donner Party and arrival in California. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** What happened?
  **A:** Trapped by snow in the Sierra Nevada 1846–47; about half died.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on westward movement.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Where did the Oregon Trail start?
  **A:** Independence, Missouri.

**Assessment:** Wagon-packing justification and Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Wagon justification: realistic choices. Unit 9 test key: trails, landmarks, hardships, Donner Party, Louisiana Purchase, removal.

## Art & Music 5: Concert preparation

**Prepare:** Gather recorder, song sheets and the composition. Prepare a performance rubric (accuracy, tone, rhythm, expression, stage presence).

**Watch for:**
- Practicing only the parts you know. Focus slow practice on problem spots.
- Ignoring transitions between pieces. Plan introductions and bows.

### Day 1 (Monday): Selecting repertoire
*Student:* Choose 4 pieces (a recorder song, a patriotic or folk song, a round and your composition) and explain each choice.

*How to teach:* Choose 4 pieces and explain each choice. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why choose varied repertoire?
  **A:** It shows different skills and keeps the audience interested.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearsal 1
*Student:* Rehearse each piece, recording and noting problem spots.

*How to teach:* Rehearse; record and note problem spots. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What should you do after finding a problem spot?
  **A:** Practice it slowly, then gradually speed up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearsal 2
*Student:* Fix problem spots with slow practice and rehearse transitions.

*How to teach:* Fix problem spots with slow practice; rehearse transitions. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is a transition in a concert?
  **A:** Moving smoothly from one piece to the next, including introductions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Concert program
*Student:* Design a printed concert program with piece titles, composers and short notes.

*How to teach:* Design a printed concert program. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What goes in a concert program?
  **A:** Piece titles, composers, performers and short notes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Do a dress rehearsal and evaluate it with a performance rubric.

*How to teach:* Dress rehearsal; evaluate with a rubric. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is stage presence?
  **A:** Confident posture, eye contact, and engaging the audience.

**Assessment:** Dress rehearsal rubric.

**Answer key:** Dress rehearsal rubric (1–4 each): accurate notes and rhythm, tone, expression, stage presence and bows, transitions. Proficient = 3+ each.

## PE 5: FITNESSGRAM post-assessment

**Prepare:** Gather the HFZ chart, the baseline score sheet from week 4, PACER audio, curl-up strip, ruler and sit-and-reach box. Test under the same conditions as the baseline (same surface, time of day, rested and hydrated). Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Comparing scores with other kids. Compare the post-test only with your own baseline and with the Healthy Fitness Zone.
- Thinking no improvement means failure. Note effort and consistency, and look for other gains (better form, faster heart-rate recovery).

### Day 1 (Monday): Aerobic test
*Student:* Complete the PACER or mile run post-test.

*How to teach:* Warm up, then run the PACER (or mile) exactly as in week 4 and record laps or time. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What pacing strategy should you use on the post-test?
  **A:** Start at the beep's pace rather than sprinting, saving energy for the faster later levels.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength tests
*Student:* Complete curl-up and push-up post-tests.

*How to teach:* Curl-up and push-up post-tests at one rep every 3 seconds; stop after the second form error.

- **Q:** Why must the cadence and form rules be the same as the baseline?
  **A:** So the comparison is fair — a change in score reflects a change in fitness, not a change in the test.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility tests
*Student:* Complete trunk lift and sit-and-reach post-tests.

*How to teach:* Trunk lift (best of two, max 12 in) and back-saver sit-and-reach (each side, max 12 in). Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Your sit-and-reach was 7 in at baseline and 9 in now. What changed?
  **A:** Flexibility in that leg/lower back improved by 2 inches.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing results
*Student:* Graph baseline vs. post-test results and identify improvements.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a double bar graph (baseline vs. post-test) for each test and marks which are in the HFZ.

- **Q:** Your PACER went from 18 to 26 laps. How many laps did you improve, and by what percent?
  **A:** 8 laps; 8 ÷ 18 ≈ 44% improvement.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write whether you met your goals and why.

*How to teach:* Your child writes whether each goal from week 32 was met and why, citing their log. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Give two reasons a score might not improve.
  **A:** Any two: not enough training (frequency/time), low intensity, illness or injury, poor sleep or hydration on test day, or already near the top of the range.

**Assessment:** FITNESSGRAM post-test and comparison graph.

**Answer key:** Post-test sheet complete for all items with HFZ status. Comparison graph: correctly labeled double bars for baseline and post-test. Written reflection rubric: 4 = states each goal, whether met, numbers, and specific reasons tied to the training log; 3 = goals and numbers with general reasons; 2 = incomplete; 1 = missing.

## Health 5: Creating the campaign

**Prepare:** Gather art supplies or a camera for the final product. Schedule a dress rehearsal with a parent on Friday. Pull out the year's goal charts (nutrition, activity, drug-free) for Thursday.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a call to action is optional. Without it, the audience doesn't know what to do.
- Exaggerating facts to persuade. Accuracy builds trust; exaggeration weakens the message.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final product
*Student:* Create the final poster, brochure or video.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the final poster, brochure or video. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How do visuals make a campaign more persuasive?
  **A:** They grab attention and make the message easy to understand and remember.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Call to action
*Student:* Add a clear call to action and a source list.

*How to teach:* Add a clear call to action and a source list. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Give an example of a call to action.
  **A:** 'Swap one sugary drink for water every day this week!'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse a 3-minute presentation of the campaign.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a 3-minute presentation. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What makes a presentation effective?
  **A:** Eye contact, clear voice, accurate facts, and a confident call to action.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Goal check
*Student:* Review your nutrition, activity and drug-free goals from the year and record progress.

*How to teach:* Review nutrition, activity and drug-free goals and record progress. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Did you meet your goals? How do you know?
  **A:** Answer varies; strong answers use data from tracking charts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present a dress rehearsal to a parent and revise based on feedback.

*How to teach:* Dress rehearsal for a parent; revise based on feedback. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** Why is feedback useful before presenting?
  **A:** It helps you fix unclear parts and improve before the real audience.

**Assessment:** Rehearsal feedback.

**Answer key:** Rehearsal feedback rubric (1–4 each): message clear, facts accurate with sources, persuasive visuals, confident delivery, call to action. Proficient = 3+ in each, plus at least one revision made.
