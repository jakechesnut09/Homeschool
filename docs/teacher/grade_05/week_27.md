# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 27

## ELA 5: The Secret Garden chapters 1–7: Mary arrives at Misselthwaite

**Prepare:** Get The Secret Garden by Frances Hodgson Burnett (Project Gutenberg). Preview chapters 1–7. Note: Mary's parents die of cholera in chapter 1.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Mary is simply mean. She is neglected and lonely.
- Skipping dialect. Read it aloud slowly; it gets easier.
- Thinking 'contrary' means sad. It means stubborn and disagreeable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 1–2
*Student:* Read chapters 1–2 and list 5 details showing Mary is 'contrary'; discuss why she behaves that way.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2 (aloud or together). Your child lists 5 details that show Mary is contrary and then discusses why: think about how she was raised.

- **Q:** Why is Mary so disagreeable?
  **A:** Her parents ignored her and servants gave her whatever she wanted, so she never learned kindness.
- **Q:** What happens to her parents?
  **A:** They die in a cholera outbreak in India.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 3–4
*Student:* Read chapters 3–4 and describe Misselthwaite Manor and the moor; draw the setting with labels.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 3–4. Your child sketches the manor, the moor and the gardens, labeling details from the text (e.g., the moor's heather and endless wild land).

- **Q:** Where is Misselthwaite Manor?
  **A:** On the edge of the moor in Yorkshire, England — a huge, gloomy house with about a hundred rooms, most shut up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Yorkshire dialect
*Student:* Translate 8 lines of Martha's Yorkshire dialect into standard English and discuss why Burnett includes dialect.

*How to teach:* Read Martha's lines aloud slowly. Your child rewrites 8 lines in standard English and discusses what dialect adds to the story.

- **Q:** Translate 'Canna' tha' dress thysen?'
  **A:** Can't you dress yourself?
- **Q:** Why include dialect?
  **A:** It makes characters realistic and shows Yorkshire's culture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapters 5–7
*Student:* Read chapters 5–7 (the cry in the corridor, the robin, finding the key) and predict what the key opens.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5–7 together. Your child writes a prediction of what the key opens and who might be crying in the corridor, with clues from the text.

- **Q:** What does the robin help Mary find?
  **A:** The buried key to the locked garden.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on chapters 1–7 and read a dialect passage aloud for fluency.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz, then have your child practice a Martha passage twice before reading it aloud for fluency.

- **Q:** Why was the garden locked?
  **A:** Mr. Craven locked it after his wife died following an accident there ten years before.

**Assessment:** Setting drawing; dialect translation; chapter quiz.

**Answer key:** Chapter quiz (suggested): 1) Mary is from India 2) parents died of cholera 3) she goes to live with her uncle, Mr. Craven 4) Martha is the cheerful Yorkshire maid 5) Ben Weatherstaff is the gardener 6) the robin shows Mary the key 7) the garden has been locked ten years. Setting drawing: house, moor, gardens labeled. Dialect translation: 6 of 8 accurate.

## Math 5: Converting customary units

**Prepare:** Measuring tape, kitchen scale (ounces/pounds), measuring cups, pint/quart containers, a gallon jug.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying when converting to a larger unit. Big unit → small unit: multiply; small → big: divide.
- Borrowing 10 instead of 12 inches (or 16 oz). 1 ft = 12 in; 1 lb = 16 oz.
- Confusing fluid ounces with weight ounces.

### Day 1 (Monday): Length conversions
*Student:* Measure the room in feet and convert to inches and yards; complete 15 length conversion problems.

*How to teach:* Measure room length in feet; convert to inches (× 12) and yards (÷ 3). The child solves 15 conversions.

- **Q:** 12 ft = ? in = ? yd
  **A:** 144 in; 4 yd.
- **Q:** 5 yd = ? ft
  **A:** 15 ft

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Weight conversions
*Student:* Weigh items on a kitchen scale in ounces and convert to pounds; solve 12 problems including fractional pounds.

*How to teach:* Weigh items in ounces and convert to pounds (÷ 16). Solve 12 problems including fractional pounds.

- **Q:** 2 1/4 lb = ? oz
  **A:** 36 oz
- **Q:** 40 oz = ? lb
  **A:** 2 1/2 lb

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Capacity conversions
*Student:* Fill a gallon jug using cups, pints and quarts and create a conversion chart; solve 12 capacity problems.

*How to teach:* Fill a gallon jug using cups, pints, quarts; build the chart 1 gal = 4 qt = 8 pt = 16 c. Solve 12 problems.

- **Q:** How many cups in 3 quarts?
  **A:** 12 cups
- **Q:** 10 pints = ? quarts
  **A:** 5 quarts

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mixed units
*Student:* Convert and compute with mixed units like 5 ft 8 in - 3 ft 10 in and 2 lb 6 oz x 3.

*How to teach:* Compute with mixed units, regrouping 1 ft as 12 in or 1 lb as 16 oz. Model regrouping: borrow 1 ft as 12 in, or carry 16 oz as 1 lb. Your child solves 6 problems, checking that inches stay under 12 and ounces under 16.

- **Q:** 5 ft 8 in − 3 ft 10 in = ?
  **A:** 4 ft 20 in − 3 ft 10 in = 1 ft 10 in.
- **Q:** 2 lb 6 oz × 3 = ?
  **A:** 6 lb 18 oz = 7 lb 2 oz.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on customary conversions with 5 word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz including 5 word problems. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** A 3/4 lb bag of nuts is how many ounces?
  **A:** 12 oz

**Assessment:** Quiz on customary unit conversions.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 7 ft = 84 in 2) 48 in = 4 ft 3) 2 mi = 10,560 ft 4) 3 lb = 48 oz 5) 1 1/2 lb = 24 oz 6) 2 gal = 32 c 7) 6 c = 3 pt 8) 3 qt 1 pt = 7 pt 9) Word: 4 ribbons of 2 ft 9 in = 11 ft 10) Word: 3 gal of punch = 48 one-cup servings 11) Word: 64 oz of flour = 4 lb 12) Word: 6 ft − 4 ft 7 in = 1 ft 5 in 13) Word: 5 lb ÷ 8 bags = 10 oz each.

## Science 5: Communities protecting the environment

**Prepare:** Articles on Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary, water-wise landscaping, California condor recovery; city website; note sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking conservation is only government work.
- Using one source only.
- Copying facts without credit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Case studies
*Student:* Read about two California conservation projects and record the problem, science used and results.

*How to teach:* Read two case studies; record problem, science, results. Read the case studies together. Your child fills a chart with problem, science used, result.

- **Q:** How was the condor saved?
  **A:** By 1987 all 27 remaining wild condors were captured for breeding; captive-bred birds were released and now over 500 exist.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Local research
*Student:* Find what your city does with recycling, stormwater and water conservation using its website.

*How to teach:* Find city recycling, stormwater and conservation practices. Browse the city website together. Your child writes three facts about local programs.

- **Q:** What does storm drain water flow into?
  **A:** Usually directly to creeks and the ocean without treatment.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a family member or neighbor about one way they conserve resources and record their answers.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about conservation. Your child prepares 3–5 questions and records answers.

- **Q:** Example question.
  **A:** What do you do to save water at home?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Combining information
*Student:* Combine notes from 3 sources into a one-page fact sheet on protecting a resource.

*How to teach:* Combine notes from 3 sources into a fact sheet. Your child groups facts by topic and credits each source.

- **Q:** How do you combine sources?
  **A:** Group facts by topic and credit each source.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share the fact sheet and take a quiz on conservation strategies.

*How to teach:* Share the fact sheet; take a quiz. Your child shares the fact sheet aloud; give the quiz.

- **Q:** Name 2 conservation strategies.
  **A:** Drip irrigation, recycling, protected areas.

**Assessment:** Fact sheet from multiple sources; quiz.

**Answer key:** Fact sheet rubric: 4 = 3 sources credited, accurate facts, organized; 3 = 2 sources; 2 = 1 source; 1 = incomplete. Quiz key: reduce, reuse, recycle; water-wise plants; protected marine areas; species recovery.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Victory at Yorktown and new governments

**Prepare:** Map of 1783 U.S.; Northwest Ordinance excerpt. Write the Unit 7 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the war ended at Yorktown officially.
- Thinking the Northwest Ordinance was fair to Native peoples.
- Thinking the U.S. extended to the Pacific.

### Day 1 (Monday): Yorktown
*Student:* Map the Yorktown campaign showing the French fleet and armies trapping Cornwallis.

*How to teach:* Map Yorktown. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Who trapped Cornwallis?
  **A:** Washington, French troops and the French fleet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Treaty of Paris 1783
*Student:* Compare maps before and after the Treaty of Paris and list the treaty's terms.

*How to teach:* Compare maps; list treaty terms. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Western border?
  **A:** The Mississippi River.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): State constitutions
*Student:* Read excerpts of the 1780 Massachusetts Constitution and identify ideas taken from the Declaration.

*How to teach:* Read the Massachusetts Constitution excerpt; identify ideas from the Declaration. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Idea from the Declaration?
  **A:** All men are born free and equal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Land ordinances
*Student:* Draw a township grid from the Land Ordinance of 1785 and read how the Northwest Ordinance banned slavery yet took Native lands.

*How to teach:* Draw a township grid; read about the Northwest Ordinance. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** How many sections?
  **A:** 36.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on the course and consequences of the Revolution.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** When was the Treaty of Paris?
  **A:** 1783.

**Assessment:** Township grid and Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Township grid: 36 sections. Unit 7 test key: battles, allies, home front, Yorktown, Treaty of Paris, ordinances.

## Art & Music 5: Finishing and presenting sculpture

**Prepare:** Make sure the sculpture is completely dry (air-dry clay usually takes 24–72 hours). Gather fine sandpaper or a damp sponge, acrylic or tempera paint, brushes, clear glue or Mod Podge, and newspaper.

**Watch for:**
- Painting before the sculpture is fully dry. Paint can crack or the piece can mold; check it's dry and room temperature.
- Skipping cleanup. Craftsmanship includes caring for tools and workspace.

### Day 1 (Monday): Smoothing
*Student:* Smooth rough edges of the dry sculpture carefully with fine sandpaper or a damp sponge.

*How to teach:* Smooth rough edges gently with sandpaper or a damp sponge, supporting thin parts. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why support thin parts while sanding?
  **A:** Dry clay is fragile and can break.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Painting
*Student:* Paint the sculpture with a planned color scheme.

*How to teach:* Paint with a planned color scheme, base colors first, then details. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Name a color scheme you could use.
  **A:** Complementary, analogous or monochromatic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sealing
*Student:* Seal the sculpture and clean all tools and work space.

*How to teach:* Seal with Mod Podge or clear glue; clean brushes and workspace. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why seal a painted sculpture?
  **A:** To protect the paint and give a finished look.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Display plan
*Student:* Write how the sculpture should be displayed safely (pedestal, label, lighting) and why.

*How to teach:* Write a display plan (pedestal, label, lighting). Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why does lighting matter in display?
  **A:** It shows form and texture with highlights and shadows.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the sculpture to family and explain revisions made during the process.

*How to teach:* Present the sculpture and explain revisions. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is craftsmanship?
  **A:** Care and skill in making the artwork—smooth surfaces, neat painting, secure joins.

**Assessment:** Sculpture craftsmanship rubric and display plan.

**Answer key:** Craftsmanship rubric (1–4 each): secure joins, smooth surfaces, neat painting with planned color scheme, sealed finish. Display plan proficient if it names pedestal/surface, label content and lighting with reasons.

## PE 5: Net games and strategy

**Prepare:** Gather the rope net, beach ball, paddles and balls; chalk a four-square court (four squares about 2 × 2 m). Review the net skills checklist for Friday. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Hitting right back to the opponent. Aim for open space, away from the player.
- Arguing line calls. Agree that the receiver calls their side honestly; when in doubt, replay.

### Day 1 (Monday): Placement
*Student:* Play a 1-v-1 paddle game scoring only when the ball lands in open space.

*How to teach:* Play 1-v-1 paddle; points count only when the ball lands in open space. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** Where should you aim to win points?
  **A:** Into open space, away from your opponent, especially deep or to the sides.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Modified volleyball
*Student:* Play 2-v-2 modified volleyball allowing one catch per side.

*How to teach:* Play 2-v-2 volleyball allowing one catch per side. Walk through the rules and boundaries first, then play; pause once to ask a quick strategy question ('Where is the open space?') and praise fair play and effort as much as skill.

- **Q:** How should two teammates share the court?
  **A:** Split responsibility (e.g., left/right or front/back) and call 'mine.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Four square
*Student:* Play four square with rules about fair calls.

*How to teach:* Play four square; review fair calls. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** In four square, what should you do on a close line call?
  **A:** Make an honest call; if unsure, replay the point.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Strategy talk
*Student:* Discuss and write two strategies that won points this week.

*How to teach:* Discuss and write two strategies that won points. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Give one net-game strategy.
  **A:** Hit to open space; vary placement deep and short; return to ready position in the middle.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Complete a net-game skills test (bump, set, serve, forehand) and a fair-play self-check.

*How to teach:* Skills test: 10 bumps, 10 sets, 10 serves, 10 forehands; your child completes a fair-play self-check.

- **Q:** What is a 'fair play' behavior in net games?
  **A:** Making honest calls, rotating fairly, encouraging others, shaking hands.

**Assessment:** Net skills test.

**Answer key:** Net skills test, each out of 10: bump to target, set to target, underhand serve over net, forehand over the rope. Proficient = 5+/10 on each with correct cues. Fair-play self-check completed honestly with one example.

## Health 5: Alcohol and the body

**Prepare:** Gather a body diagram; optionally clear a safe space for the spinning coordination demo. Read a NIDA/KidsHealth article on alcohol's effects.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking alcohol is a stimulant because people get loud. Alcohol is a depressant: it slows the brain and nervous system.
- Thinking only heavy drinking is harmful for kids. Any alcohol is harmful to a developing brain, and it's illegal under 21.

### Day 1 (Monday): Alcohol pathway
*Student:* Trace alcohol's path through the body on a diagram and note effects on the brain, liver and stomach.

*How to teach:* Trace alcohol's path: mouth → stomach and small intestine → bloodstream → brain (slows thinking, coordination) and liver (breaks it down).

- **Q:** Which organ breaks down alcohol?
  **A:** The liver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Coordination demo
*Student:* Try simple tasks (walking a line, tossing a ball) while spinning first to model impaired coordination, then discuss.

*How to teach:* Spin 5 times then try to walk a line (with a spotter, on soft ground). Discuss how alcohol similarly impairs balance and coordination.

- **Q:** How does alcohol affect coordination and reaction time?
  **A:** It slows the brain's signals, so balance, coordination and reaction time get worse.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Developing brain
*Student:* Read about why alcohol is especially harmful to the developing brain and why the legal age is 21.

*How to teach:* Read why alcohol harms the developing brain (memory, learning, decision-making); the brain develops into the mid-20s; legal age is 21.

- **Q:** Why is the legal drinking age 21?
  **A:** The brain is still developing, alcohol can damage it, and young drinkers are at higher risk of accidents and addiction.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Short vs. long term
*Student:* Make a chart of short- and long-term effects of alcohol.

*How to teach:* Make a short- vs. long-term chart. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Give one short-term and one long-term effect.
  **A:** Short: slowed reactions, poor judgment, vomiting. Long: liver disease, brain damage, addiction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete an alcohol facts quiz.

*How to teach:* Alcohol facts quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Is alcohol a stimulant or depressant?
  **A:** A depressant.

**Assessment:** Alcohol quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) depressant 2) liver breaks it down 3) bloodstream carries it to the brain 4) impairs coordination, judgment, reaction time 5) legal age 21 6) brain develops until mid-20s 7) long-term: liver disease, addiction, brain damage. Proficient = 80%.
