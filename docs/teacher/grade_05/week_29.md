# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 29

## ELA 5: The Secret Garden chapters 16–22: Colin's change

**Prepare:** Preview chapters 16–22. Optional: find an illustrated edition or the 1949/1993 film for visual comparison (preview first).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'Magic' is literal. The children call the healing power of nature and positive thinking 'Magic.'
- Thinking films must match books. Adaptations change things for the medium.
- Missing Colin's turning point.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 16–18
*Student:* Read chapters 16–18 and track Colin's attitude in a 'before and after' chart.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 16–18. Your child records Colin's attitude early (tantrums, fear of dying) and later, with quotations.

- **Q:** What changes Colin?
  **A:** Mary's honesty, Dickon's stories, the idea of the garden and fresh air.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 19–20
*Student:* Read chapters 19–20 and explain what the children mean by 'Magic.'

*How to teach:* Read chapters 19–20. Ask: What is Magic to the children? Your child explains with two quotations.

- **Q:** What do they mean by Magic?
  **A:** The force that makes things grow and that heals Colin — nature and positive thinking.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Visual elements
*Student:* Compare an illustrated edition's pictures (or a film scene) with the text and explain how they add to mood.

*How to teach:* Show an illustration or a short film scene (previewed). Your child writes how colors, lighting or music create mood compared with the words.

- **Q:** How can an illustration change mood?
  **A:** Colors, light and details can make a scene feel gloomy or joyful.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapters 21–22
*Student:* Read chapters 21–22 and discuss how Colin's standing up is a turning point.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 21–22. Discuss why standing up matters so much to Colin and how it changes the story.

- **Q:** Why does Colin stand?
  **A:** Ben Weatherstaff calls him a cripple with crooked legs, and Colin angrily stands to prove him wrong.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a theme paragraph about healing in nature with evidence from three chapters.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a theme paragraph about nature healing, with evidence from three different chapters, and checks it against the rubric.

- **Q:** Theme?
  **A:** Nature, friendship and hope can heal both body and spirit.

**Assessment:** Theme paragraph; visual analysis notes.

**Answer key:** Theme paragraph rubric: 4 = clear theme statement, evidence from three chapters, explained; 3 = two chapters; 2 = summary; 1 = none. Visual analysis: names one visual and explains its effect on mood.

## Math 5: The coordinate plane

**Prepare:** Masking tape to make a floor grid (about 6 × 6), graph paper, a mystery-picture list of ordered pairs.

**Watch for:**
- Plotting (3, 5) as 3 up, 5 over. x first: 'walk along the hall, then climb the stairs.'
- Starting at 1 instead of the origin (0, 0).
- Counting the starting point when finding distance. Subtract coordinates: (2, 7) to (2, 3) is 7 − 3 = 4.

### Day 1 (Monday): Building the plane
*Student:* Tape a floor grid with x- and y-axes and walk to ordered pairs called out, noting that x comes first.

*How to teach:* Tape x- and y-axes on the floor and number them. Call out pairs; the child walks x first, then y. Name origin, axes, coordinates.

- **Q:** From the origin, how do you get to (3, 5)?
  **A:** Move 3 right along the x-axis, then 5 up.
- **Q:** What are the coordinates of the origin?
  **A:** (0, 0)

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plotting points
*Student:* Plot 20 points on graph paper and name the coordinates of 10 given points.

*How to teach:* The child plots 20 points and names 10 given points on graph paper. Model plotting by tracing with your finger: over on the x-axis, then up. Your child plots 20 points and names 10 points you mark.

- **Q:** A point is 4 right and 0 up. Name it.
  **A:** (4, 0) — on the x-axis.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coordinate pictures
*Student:* Plot and connect a list of ordered pairs to reveal a mystery picture, then design your own for someone else.

*How to teach:* Plot and connect a mystery list in order, then the child designs a picture and writes its list. Give a short mystery list (a house or star). Your child plots in order, then designs a picture and writes the coordinate list for someone else.

- **Q:** Plot (1,1), (5,1), (5,4), (1,4). What shape?
  **A:** A rectangle 4 units by 3 units.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Distances on lines
*Student:* Find the distance between points that share an x- or y-coordinate on 10 problems.

*How to teach:* Find distances between points with the same x or y by subtracting the other coordinate. Model with (2, 7) and (2, 3): same x, so subtract y-values: 4. Your child solves 10 problems, checking by counting grid units.

- **Q:** Distance from (2, 7) to (2, 3)?
  **A:** 4 units
- **Q:** Distance from (1, 6) to (9, 6)?
  **A:** 8 units

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play coordinate 'Battleship' and take a quiz on plotting and naming points.

*How to teach:* Play coordinate Battleship, then the quiz. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Which axis is horizontal?
  **A:** The x-axis.

**Assessment:** Quiz on coordinate plane vocabulary and plotting.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) origin = (0, 0) 2) first number = x-coordinate (horizontal distance) 3) x-axis is horizontal, y-axis vertical 4) points plotted correctly (check each) 5) point A at 3 right, 6 up = (3, 6) 6) (0, 5) lies on the y-axis 7) distance (4, 2) to (4, 9) = 7 8) distance (3, 5) to (10, 5) = 7.

## Science 5: Gravity pulls down toward Earth's center

**Prepare:** String, washer (plumb line), balls of different masses, globe, small figurines; PhET 'Gravity and Orbits.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking heavier objects fall faster. Without air resistance, they fall at the same rate.
- Thinking people in Australia would fall off. 'Down' is toward Earth's center everywhere.
- Thinking gravity only works on Earth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drop tests
*Student:* Drop balls of different masses from the same height and record which lands first.

*How to teach:* Drop balls of different masses from the same height; record which lands first. Drop pairs from the same height at the same moment. Your child records which lands first over 3 trials.

- **Q:** Which lands first?
  **A:** They land at about the same time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plumb line
*Student:* Make a plumb line and test whether it always points the same direction in different places.

*How to teach:* Make a plumb line and test it in different places. Tie a washer to string; hold it still in several locations. Your child notes the direction each time.

- **Q:** Does the plumb line always point the same direction?
  **A:** Yes — straight down toward Earth's center.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Round Earth
*Student:* Place figurines around a globe and draw arrows showing 'down' for people in California, Australia and Antarctica.

*How to teach:* Place figurines around a globe and draw 'down' arrows. Tape figurines on the globe. Your child draws 'down' arrows pointing to the center.

- **Q:** Which way is down in Australia?
  **A:** Toward Earth's center.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gravity simulation
*Student:* Explore the PhET 'Gravity and Orbits' simulation and record how gravity affects motion.

*How to teach:* Explore the simulation; record how gravity affects motion. Explore the simulation together. Your child records what happens when gravity is turned off.

- **Q:** What keeps the Moon in orbit?
  **A:** Earth's gravity pulls it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an argument that gravity on Earth is directed toward its center using evidence from the investigations.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an argument that gravity on Earth is directed toward its center. Your child writes claim, evidence from three investigations, and reasoning.

- **Q:** Give a piece of evidence.
  **A:** Plumb lines point straight down everywhere; people everywhere stand upright.

**Assessment:** Written argument about gravity's direction.

**Answer key:** Argument rubric: 4 = claim, evidence from plumb line, drop test and globe model, reasoning; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = claim only; 1 = incorrect.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: The Constitutional Convention

**Prepare:** Constitution transcript; Preamble text.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all states attended.
- Thinking the Constitution ended slavery.
- Thinking Senate seats are by population.

### Day 1 (Monday): Philadelphia 1787
*Student:* Read about the delegates and James Madison's role and list who was not represented.

*How to teach:* Read about delegates and Madison; list who was not represented. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Who wasn't represented?
  **A:** Women, enslaved people, Native Americans; Rhode Island didn't attend.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Great Compromise
*Student:* Compare the Virginia and New Jersey Plans and explain the Great Compromise with a diagram.

*How to teach:* Compare plans; diagram the Great Compromise. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Great Compromise?
  **A:** House by population, Senate two per state.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Slavery and the Constitution
*Student:* Read about the Three-Fifths Compromise and the slave trade clause and discuss why they were controversial.

*How to teach:* Read about the Three-Fifths Compromise and slave trade clause; discuss. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** What was it?
  **A:** Counted three-fifths of enslaved people for representation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Primary source: Preamble
*Student:* Paraphrase each phrase of the Preamble and illustrate one goal.

*How to teach:* Paraphrase each Preamble phrase; illustrate one. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** 'Insure domestic tranquility'?
  **A:** Keep peace at home.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Recite the Preamble and take a quiz on the Convention's compromises.

*How to teach:* Your child recites the Preamble; give the quiz. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** First three words?
  **A:** We the People.

**Assessment:** Preamble recitation and quiz.

**Answer key:** Recitation: accurate. Quiz key: Virginia Plan, New Jersey Plan, Great Compromise, Three-Fifths, slave trade 1808.

## Art & Music 5: Instruments of the orchestra

**Prepare:** Find Britten's 'The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra' (1946, based on a theme by Henry Purcell) and the Classics for Kids instrument game. Print 16 instrument pictures. Gather supplies for a homemade instrument (shoebox and rubber bands; straws; glass bottles and water).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the saxophone is brass. It's metal but a woodwind (uses a reed).
- Thinking the piano is only a string instrument. It has strings but they're struck by hammers; it's often grouped with percussion or keyboards.

### Day 1 (Monday): Orchestra families
*Student:* Learn the four instrument families and sort 16 instrument pictures by family.

*How to teach:* Teach the four families: strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion; sort 16 pictures. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Which family does the flute belong to?
  **A:** Woodwinds.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Young Person's Guide
*Student:* Listen to Britten's 'Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra' and list instruments as they are featured.

*How to teach:* Listen to Britten; list instruments as featured. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** In what order does Britten present the families?
  **A:** Woodwinds, strings, brass, percussion (then a fugue with all).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timbre
*Student:* Play the Classics for Kids instrument game and describe timbres with adjectives (bright, mellow, brassy).

*How to teach:* Play the instrument game; describe timbres with adjectives. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Describe the timbre of a trumpet.
  **A:** Bright, brassy, bold.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Making an instrument
*Student:* Build a homemade instrument (rubber-band guitar, straw oboe or bottle xylophone) and explain how it makes sound.

*How to teach:* Build a homemade instrument; explain how it makes sound. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How does a rubber-band guitar make sound?
  **A:** The bands vibrate; tighter or thinner bands vibrate faster and make higher pitches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a listening quiz identifying 8 instruments by sound.

*How to teach:* Listening quiz identifying 8 instruments. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Which family makes sound by buzzing lips?
  **A:** Brass.

**Assessment:** Instrument listening quiz.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz: identifies 8 instruments (e.g., violin, cello, flute, clarinet, trumpet, trombone, timpani, snare drum) and their families. Proficient = 6/8.

## PE 5: Fielding and throwing

**Prepare:** Gather a glove (or use bare hands with a soft ball), bases (cones or paper plates 30 ft/about 9 m apart), and soft balls. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Fielding grounders standing up. Get low, glove on the ground, 'alligator hands' (top hand covers).
- Catching fly balls at the belly. Get under the ball and catch above the head with two hands.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ground balls
*Student:* Field 20 rolled ground balls with 'alligator hands' and get in front of the ball.

*How to teach:* Roll 20 grounders; your child moves in front, gets low, glove down, top hand covers like an alligator's jaws.

- **Q:** Why get in front of the ball?
  **A:** Your body acts as a backstop if the ball gets past the glove.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fly balls
*Student:* Catch 20 tossed fly balls using two hands above the head.

*How to teach:* Toss 20 fly balls; your child calls 'I got it,' gets under, catches above the head with two hands.

- **Q:** Why call 'I got it'?
  **A:** So teammates know who is catching and nobody collides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Throwing to bases
*Student:* Field and throw to a base target 30 ft away, recording accuracy.

*How to teach:* Field and throw to a base 30 ft away; record accurate throws. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What should you do with your feet before throwing to a base?
  **A:** Step toward the base target with the opposite foot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relay throws
*Student:* Practice a relay throw from outfield to cutoff to base.

*How to teach:* Practice relay: outfielder throws to cutoff, cutoff turns and throws to a base. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why use a cutoff throw?
  **A:** Two shorter, accurate throws are faster and more accurate than one long throw.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a fielding test of 10 grounders and 10 flies.

*How to teach:* Fielding test: 10 grounders and 10 flies. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** What does 'alligator hands' mean?
  **A:** Glove open on the ground with the top hand above it ready to close over the ball.

**Assessment:** Fielding test score.

**Answer key:** Fielding test: grounders /10 and flies /10. Proficient = 7+ each with correct form (low, in front, two hands).

## Health 5: Using medicines safely

**Prepare:** Get an empty OTC medicine box with a Drug Facts label (e.g., children's acetaminophen) and a prescription label (or an example). Check your home medicine storage beforehand. Write down Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking OTC medicines are always safe because no prescription is needed. Wrong doses can be dangerous.
- Sharing prescriptions. A prescription is for one person only.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rx vs. OTC
*Student:* Compare a prescription label and an OTC Drug Facts label and identify each part.

*How to teach:* Compare a prescription label (name, dose, doctor, pharmacy, refills) and Drug Facts label (active ingredient, uses, warnings, directions).

- **Q:** What does 'active ingredient' mean?
  **A:** The part of the medicine that works on the body.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dosage math
*Student:* Use a Drug Facts chart to find the correct dose by age and weight in 3 practice problems.

*How to teach:* Use a dose chart for 3 problems by age/weight. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** A chart says ages 6–11: 1 tablet every 4–6 hours, max 5 in 24 hours. What's the most you can take in a day?
  **A:** 5 tablets.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Misuse
*Student:* Discuss why sharing prescriptions or taking extra doses is dangerous.

*How to teach:* Discuss why sharing prescriptions or extra doses is dangerous. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why is taking double doses dangerous?
  **A:** Too much can cause overdose and organ damage.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safe storage
*Student:* Inspect your home's medicine storage with a parent and learn the Poison Control number (1-800-222-1222).

*How to teach:* Inspect home medicine storage; learn Poison Control number. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What's the Poison Control number?
  **A:** 1-800-222-1222.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a medicine-safety scenario quiz.

*How to teach:* Medicine-safety scenario quiz. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Who should give you medicine?
  **A:** A parent, guardian or trusted adult, following directions.

**Assessment:** Scenario quiz.

**Answer key:** Scenario quiz key: never take medicine without a trusted adult; follow directions; don't share prescriptions; store safely; call Poison Control. Proficient = 80%.
