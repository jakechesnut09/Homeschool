# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 34

## ELA 5: Inquiry: researching a California national park or natural wonder

**Prepare:** Bookmark nps.gov park pages; library books; note-taking template.

**Watch for:**
- Trusting any website. Check author, date, purpose.
- Copying sentences. Paraphrase.
- A synthesis that lists sources separately.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing and questioning
*Student:* Choose a CA national park (Yosemite, Joshua Tree, Redwood, Death Valley) and write 5 inquiry questions.

*How to teach:* Look at nps.gov pages together. Your child picks a park and writes 5 inquiry questions starting with how or why.

- **Q:** Example question.
  **A:** How did glaciers shape Yosemite Valley?

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Searching efficiently
*Student:* Practice keyword searching and evaluating credibility of 4 websites, then find answers on nps.gov.

*How to teach:* Show keyword searching (e.g., 'Yosemite glaciers formation'). Your child rates 4 websites on author, date, purpose and accuracy, then finds answers on nps.gov.

- **Q:** Why is nps.gov credible?
  **A:** It's the official National Park Service site with expert, current information.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Note-taking
*Student:* Take paraphrased notes from 3 sources organized by question.

*How to teach:* Your child takes paraphrased notes from 3 sources, organizing them by question and labeling each source.

- **Q:** What is paraphrasing?
  **A:** Restating in your own words.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Synthesis
*Student:* Write a synthesis paragraph combining information from at least 2 sources on one question.

*How to teach:* Model blending two sources in one sentence ('Both the NPS and…'). Your child writes a paragraph combining at least 2 sources on one question.

- **Q:** What is synthesis?
  **A:** Combining information from several sources into one explanation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Conference on notes and source list; complete a source credibility checklist.

*How to teach:* Conference on the notes and source list. Your child completes a credibility checklist for each source.

- **Q:** Checklist items?
  **A:** Author/organization, date, purpose, accuracy, .gov/.edu.

**Assessment:** Notes and source list; synthesis paragraph.

**Answer key:** Notes: organized by question, 3 sources, paraphrased. Synthesis paragraph: 4 = combines 2+ sources smoothly with credit; 3 = 2 sources separately; 2 = one source; 1 = copied.

## Math 5: Cumulative review: whole numbers, decimals and expressions

**Prepare:** Review task cards (write 20 decimal cards to hide around the house), timer.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing a timed set and skipping the estimate check. Estimate first.
- Forgetting how to interpret remainders. Reread the question.
- Mixing conversion directions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Multiplication fluency
*Student:* Complete a timed set of 10 multi-digit multiplication problems and track accuracy.

*How to teach:* Time 10 multi-digit multiplication problems; record accuracy and time. Have your child estimate before each problem. Record time and accuracy; accuracy matters more than speed.

- **Q:** 426 × 37 = ?
  **A:** 15,762

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Division review
*Student:* Solve 8 division problems with two-digit divisors and interpret remainders in 3 word problems.

*How to teach:* Solve 8 division problems and interpret remainders in 3 word problems. Your child solves 8 problems with checks and decides for 3 word problems whether to round up, drop or use the remainder.

- **Q:** 1,750 ÷ 42 = ?
  **A:** 41 R28
- **Q:** 100 cookies in boxes of 12: how many boxes to hold all?
  **A:** 9 boxes (8 R4, round up).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decimal operations
*Student:* Complete a 20-problem decimal operation scavenger hunt with task cards around the house.

*How to teach:* Hide 20 decimal task cards; the child finds and solves each. Hide cards around the house. Your child records each answer on a sheet and you check with the key.

- **Q:** 6.4 × 0.5 = ?
  **A:** 3.2
- **Q:** 9.1 − 4.75 = ?
  **A:** 4.35

### Day 4 (Thursday): Expressions and conversions
*Student:* Evaluate 8 expressions with grouping symbols and solve 5 measurement conversion problems.

*How to teach:* Evaluate 8 grouping-symbol expressions and 5 conversion problems. Your child evaluates expressions inside out and solves conversions, labeling all units.

- **Q:** 3 × [8 + (12 ÷ 4)] = ?
  **A:** 33
- **Q:** 4.2 km = ? m
  **A:** 4,200 m

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a cumulative quiz on number and operations in base ten.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative quiz. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Round 8.456 to the nearest tenth.
  **A:** 8.5

**Assessment:** Cumulative quiz on NBT, OA and conversions.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) value of 7 in 3.072 = 0.07 2) 4.5 × 10^3 = 4,500 3) 0.67 > 0.607 4) 532 × 48 = 25,536 5) 3,276 ÷ 36 = 91 6) 7.3 + 2.85 = 10.15 7) 2.4 × 0.3 = 0.72 8) 4.2 ÷ 0.6 = 7 9) (5 + 7) × 3 − 6 = 30 10) 3 ft 4 in = 40 in 11) 2.5 L = 2,500 mL 12) round 4.375 to hundredth = 4.38.

## Science 5: Seasonal star patterns

**Prepare:** Star chart or free planetarium app, red flashlight, lamp, constellation pictures. Check the weather for a clear night; supervise outdoor observation.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stars move on their own each night. Their apparent motion is due to Earth's rotation.
- Thinking the same stars are visible all year.
- Thinking constellations are close together in space.

### Day 1 (Monday): Night observation
*Student:* Go outside after dark with a star chart and sketch 2 constellations and their positions.

*How to teach:* Go outside after dark; sketch 2 constellations and positions. Choose a clear night; let eyes adjust 10–15 minutes. Your child sketches constellations with landmarks for position.

- **Q:** Why use a red flashlight?
  **A:** It preserves night vision.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Seasonal sky model
*Student:* Walk around a lamp (sun) with constellation pictures on the walls to model why we see different stars each season.

*How to teach:* Walk around a lamp with constellations on the walls. Tape constellation pictures on four walls. Your child walks around the lamp, noting which pictures face the 'night' side.

- **Q:** Why do we see different stars in different seasons?
  **A:** As Earth orbits the sun, the night side faces different directions in space.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data table
*Student:* Use a planetarium app to record which month Orion, Scorpius and the Big Dipper are visible at 9 p.m. and organize the data.

*How to teach:* Record when Orion, Scorpius and the Big Dipper are visible at 9 p.m. Use a planetarium app and set the time to 9 p.m. for different months. Your child records in a table.

- **Q:** When is Orion visible in the evening?
  **A:** Winter (December–March).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Second observation
*Student:* Observe the sky again 2 hours later and record how constellations moved, explaining rotation.

*How to teach:* Observe the sky again 2 hours later. Observe again 2 hours later. Your child notes the change in position.

- **Q:** How did constellations move?
  **A:** East to west, because Earth rotates.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 10 assessment on shadows, day length and seasonal stars.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 10 assessment. Give the test without help.

- **Q:** When is Scorpius visible?
  **A:** Summer evenings.

**Assessment:** Star observation sketches and Unit 10 test.

**Answer key:** Observation sketches: two constellations with positions and times. Unit 10 test key: shadow patterns; day length; tilt; seasonal stars; Earth's rotation.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Native nations and removal

**Prepare:** Cherokee syllabary chart; John Ross's 1836 letter excerpt; Tecumseh speech excerpt. Preview Trail of Tears content.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking removal was voluntary.
- Thinking the Supreme Court supported removal.
- Thinking Native nations didn't resist.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tecumseh
*Student:* Read about Tecumseh's confederacy and an excerpt of his speech urging unity, and explain his goals.

*How to teach:* Read about Tecumseh and his speech. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Goal?
  **A:** Unite nations to stop American expansion.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sequoyah
*Student:* Examine the Cherokee syllabary and explain how Sequoyah's invention and the Cherokee Phoenix newspaper strengthened the nation.

*How to teach:* Examine the syllabary; discuss the Cherokee Phoenix. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Why important?
  **A:** Cherokee could read and write in their language.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marshall vs. Jackson
*Student:* Learn about Worcester v. Georgia (John Marshall) and Andrew Jackson's refusal to enforce it.

*How to teach:* Learn about Worcester v. Georgia and Jackson's response.

- **Q:** Ruling?
  **A:** Georgia had no authority over the Cherokee.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trail of Tears
*Student:* Read John Ross's 1836 letter to Congress and a map of removal routes; discuss the human cost.

*How to teach:* Read Ross's letter; view removal routes. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** How many died?
  **A:** About 4,000 Cherokee.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on how one leader responded to the loss of Native lands, using a primary source.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a primary-source paragraph. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Ross's response?
  **A:** Legal and political protest.

**Assessment:** Primary-source paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear topic sentence, three or more accurate, specific details or quotations from sources, explanation of how they support the point, conclusion; 3 = accurate with two details; 2 = general or partly inaccurate; 1 = off topic or incomplete.

## Art & Music 5: Preparing the exhibition

**Prepare:** Gather mats or construction paper, painter's tape, label cards, and a wall or tables with good light.

**Watch for:**
- Hanging art at random heights. Hang centers at about eye level and space evenly.
- Writing long labels. Museum labels are short: title, artist, medium, date, and one or two sentences.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mounting
*Student:* Mount or mat each 2D artwork neatly.

*How to teach:* Mount or mat each 2D artwork neatly. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why mat artwork?
  **A:** It frames and protects the piece and looks professional.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Labels
*Student:* Write a museum label for each work (title, medium, date, short description).

*How to teach:* Write museum labels (title, medium, date, description). Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Write a sample museum label.
  **A:** 'Yosemite Dreams, watercolor, March 2027. Layers of cool washes create depth.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Installation
*Student:* Install the exhibition on a wall or tables with even spacing and good lighting.

*How to teach:* Install with even spacing and good lighting. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** How high should art hang?
  **A:** Center at about adult eye level (around 57–60 inches).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gallery talk prep
*Student:* Prepare a 2-minute gallery talk about the exhibition's theme.

*How to teach:* Prepare a 2-minute gallery talk. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What belongs in a gallery talk?
  **A:** The theme, highlights of a few pieces, and what you learned.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Walk through the exhibition and evaluate how well it presents your ideas, adjusting as needed.

*How to teach:* Walk through and adjust. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What would you adjust after a walk-through?
  **A:** Spacing, lighting, crooked art or missing labels.

**Assessment:** Installed exhibition and labels.

**Answer key:** Exhibition rubric: 4 = all work matted, labeled, evenly spaced at eye level, well lit, clear theme; 3 = minor issues; 2 = several problems; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 5: Hydration, nutrition and recovery

**Prepare:** Gather water bottle, food labels, mat. Know dehydration signs: thirst, dark urine, headache, dizziness, tiredness. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Only drinking when thirsty. Drink before, during and after activity.
- Thinking sports drinks are always needed. For under an hour of activity, water is enough.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hydration
*Student:* Learn signs of dehydration and track water intake around a 30-minute workout.

*How to teach:* Track water before, during and after a 30-minute workout; discuss dehydration signs. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Name two signs of dehydration.
  **A:** Thirst, dark urine, headache, dizziness, tiredness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fuel for activity
*Student:* Plan a pre-activity snack and post-activity snack and explain the choices.

*How to teach:* Plan pre- and post-activity snacks. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Give a good pre-activity snack and why.
  **A:** Banana or toast—easy-to-digest carbohydrates for energy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recovery
*Student:* Practice a full cool-down and foam-roll or stretching routine.

*How to teach:* Full cool-down and stretching routine. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why is recovery important?
  **A:** Rest lets muscles repair and get stronger and prevents injury.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice test
*Student:* Do a practice round of the PACER and curl-ups to check progress.

*How to teach:* Practice PACER and curl-ups. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why do a practice test?
  **A:** To check progress and adjust the plan before the post-test.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write how hydration and food affected your energy this week.

*How to teach:* Write how hydration and food affected energy. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** How might skipping breakfast affect a workout?
  **A:** Less energy, tiredness, or dizziness.

**Assessment:** Hydration log and written reflection.

**Answer key:** Hydration log completed for each day. Reflection rubric: 4 = connects specific food/water choices to energy with examples; 3 = general connection; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## Health 5: Health advocacy campaign planning

**Prepare:** Gather poster paper, markers and computer access. Bookmark valid sources (CDC.gov, KidsHealth.org, MyPlate.gov, NIDA for Teens, FDA). Print an advocacy checklist: clear health-enhancing message, accurate facts with sources, targeted to the audience, persuasive, call to action.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking advocacy is just stating facts. Advocacy persuades others to take a healthy action and gives them a way to do it.
- Using unreliable sources. Every fact should come from a valid source and be cited.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a topic
*Student:* Pick a topic (drug-free, vaping myths, healthy drinks, daily activity) and state a clear health-enhancing message.

*How to teach:* Your child picks a topic and writes a one-sentence health-enhancing message (e.g., 'Choose water over sugary drinks').

- **Q:** What is health advocacy?
  **A:** Speaking up or acting to encourage others to make healthy choices.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Gather 3 facts from valid sources to support the message.

*How to teach:* Your child gathers 3 facts from valid sources and records each source. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Is a fact from a vape company's website a valid source for a vaping campaign? Why?
  **A:** No — the company profits from selling vapes, so it is biased; use CDC or FDA instead.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Audience
*Student:* Identify the audience (younger siblings, family, community group) and plan how to reach them.

*How to teach:* Your child identifies the audience and how to reach them (poster on the fridge for siblings, brochure for a co-op).

- **Q:** Why does the audience matter?
  **A:** The message, words and format should fit who you're trying to persuade (e.g., simpler words and pictures for younger kids).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft materials
*Student:* Draft a poster, brochure or short video script.

*How to teach:* Draft the poster, brochure or video script. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What should every draft include?
  **A:** A clear message, supporting facts, and a call to action.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Peer- or parent-review the draft with an advocacy checklist.

*How to teach:* Parent reviews the draft with the checklist; your child marks what needs revising. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name one item on the advocacy checklist.
  **A:** Clear message, accurate facts with sources, fits the audience, persuasive, call to action.

**Assessment:** Draft and checklist.

**Answer key:** Draft and checklist: proficient draft has a clear health-enhancing message, at least 3 accurate facts with valid sources, an identified audience, and a call to action; checklist completed honestly with at least one planned revision.
