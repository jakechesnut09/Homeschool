# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 30

## ELA 5: The Secret Garden chapters 23–27 and readers' theater

**Prepare:** Preview chapters 23–27 and a script template. Plan family roles for readers' theater.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a script as prose. Scripts use character names, dialogue and stage directions.
- Forgetting the ending resolves Mr. Craven's story too.
- Reading lines flatly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finishing the novel
*Student:* Read chapters 23–27 and summarize how the ending resolves Mary's, Colin's and Mr. Craven's stories.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 23–27; summarize how the ending resolves Mary's, Colin's and Mr. Craven's stories.

- **Q:** How does the novel end?
  **A:** Mr. Craven returns home, finds Colin healthy in the garden, and walks with him across the lawn back to the house.
- **Q:** How has Mary changed?
  **A:** She is healthy, kind and caring.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Story vs. drama
*Student:* Study a sample script's scenes, cast list and stage directions, and compare its structure to chapters.

*How to teach:* Read a sample script together. Your child notes how scenes replace chapters, a cast list replaces descriptions, and stage directions show action.

- **Q:** What are stage directions?
  **A:** Instructions in parentheses or italics telling actors how to move or speak.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Writing a script
*Student:* Adapt one key chapter (finding the garden or Colin's first visit) into a 2-scene readers' theater script.

*How to teach:* Your child picks a key chapter and writes 2 scenes: a cast list, dialogue from the book, and stage directions in parentheses.

- **Q:** Format a line.
  **A:** MARY: (whispering) It's the key to the garden!

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearsal
*Student:* Rehearse the script with family members, focusing on expression, pacing and dialect.

*How to teach:* Assign roles and read through twice. Coach on expression, pacing and Yorkshire dialect.

- **Q:** How do you show emotion in readers' theater?
  **A:** With voice: tone, volume and pace.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Perform the readers' theater and take an assessment on the novel's structure, characters and theme.

*How to teach:* Perform the readers' theater for an audience. Then give the novel assessment independently and score it with the key.

- **Q:** Who is Mrs. Medlock?
  **A:** The housekeeper at Misselthwaite.

**Assessment:** Script and performance rubric; Unit 7 novel assessment.

**Answer key:** Script rubric: cast list, 2 scenes, accurate dialogue, stage directions. Performance: expressive, fluent. Novel assessment key: Mary changes from contrary to kind; Colin from invalid to healthy; theme of nature healing; garden symbolizes renewal; dialect adds setting.

## Math 5: Graphing real-world problems

**Prepare:** Graph paper, outdoor thermometer, a plant or a stick to measure shadows. Choose a day to record hourly temperature.

**Watch for:**
- Uneven scales (1, 2, 5, 10 spaced equally). Each grid step must represent the same amount.
- Starting a scale far from the data so points squash. Choose a scale that fits the range.
- Misreading a point's meaning. Say both parts: 'After 2 hours, 18 miles.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Graphing data
*Student:* Record outdoor temperature every hour for 6 hours and graph (hour, temperature) points.

*How to teach:* Record temperature each hour for 6 hours and graph (hour, temperature). Your child reads the thermometer at the same spot each hour and records it in a table. After the last reading they choose a scale and plot the points.

- **Q:** What does the point (3, 68) mean?
  **A:** At hour 3 the temperature was 68 degrees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choosing scales
*Student:* Graph 3 data sets (savings over weeks, plant height over days) choosing appropriate axis scales.

*How to teach:* Choose axis scales that fit each data set (by 1s, 5s, 10s). Graph savings over weeks and plant height over days.

- **Q:** Savings range from $0 to $80. What scale for the y-axis?
  **A:** Count by 10s (0–80) — 8 equal intervals.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interpreting graphs
*Student:* Answer 10 questions about what specific points mean on a graph of a bike trip's time and distance.

*How to teach:* Use a bike-trip graph (time, distance) to answer questions about points and changes. Draw or print a simple time–distance graph. Your child answers questions about what each point means, including flat sections.

- **Q:** (2, 18) on a bike-trip graph means?
  **A:** After 2 hours the rider had traveled 18 miles.
- **Q:** If the graph is flat from hour 2 to 3, what happened?
  **A:** The rider stopped; distance did not change.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Map coordinates
*Student:* Draw a neighborhood map on a coordinate grid and write directions between locations using coordinates.

*How to teach:* Draw a neighborhood map on a grid with labeled locations; write directions using coordinates. Your child places home, park, store, school at grid points, then writes directions like 'from (2, 3) go 4 right to (6, 3).'

- **Q:** Home (2, 3) to park (6, 3): how far and which direction?
  **A:** 4 units right (east).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz graphing and interpreting a real-world data set.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz graphing and interpreting a real-world data set. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** Hours 0–4, pages read 0, 15, 30, 45, 60. How many pages per hour?
  **A:** 15 pages per hour.

**Assessment:** Quiz on graphing and interpreting real-world points.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: given data hours 0–5 and savings $0, $5, $10, $15, $20, $25: 1) scale by 5s 2) points (0,0),(1,5)…(5,25) plotted 3) (3, 15) means after 3 weeks, $15 saved 4) saved $5 each week 5) predicted week 8 = $40. Rubric: title, labeled axes, even scale, accurate points, correct interpretations.

## Science 5: The sun is a star

**Prepare:** Two identical flashlights, measuring tape, dark room, optional light meter app; NASA Space Place 'What is a Star?'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the sun is a different kind of object than stars. It's a medium-sized star.
- Thinking brighter means bigger.
- Thinking distance doesn't affect brightness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sun as a star
*Student:* Read NASA Space Place 'What is a Star?' and compare the sun to other stars in a chart.

*How to teach:* Read 'What is a Star?'; compare the sun to other stars. Read together; your child compares the sun to other stars in a chart (size, temperature, distance).

- **Q:** Is the sun a star?
  **A:** Yes — a medium-sized star.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flashlight investigation
*Student:* In a dark room, compare the brightness of identical flashlights at 1 m, 3 m and 6 m and record observations.

*How to teach:* Compare flashlights at 1 m, 3 m, 6 m. In a dark room, compare flashlights at different distances. Your child records observations.

- **Q:** Which looks brightest?
  **A:** The closest one.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Measuring brightness
*Student:* Use a light meter app or a paper-layer test to measure brightness at different distances and graph the data.

*How to teach:* Measure brightness with a light meter or paper layers; graph. Use a light meter app or count paper layers needed to block light. Your child graphs brightness vs. distance.

- **Q:** What pattern do you see?
  **A:** Brightness decreases as distance increases.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Star distances
*Student:* Compare the distances of the sun, Sirius and Betelgeuse with a scale model using a long hallway or sidewalk.

*How to teach:* Compare distances of the sun, Sirius and Betelgeuse with a scale model. Use a hallway or sidewalk scale. Your child marks the sun, Sirius and Betelgeuse distances.

- **Q:** How far is Sirius?
  **A:** About 8.6 light-years; the sun is about 8 light-minutes away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a CER explaining why the sun looks brighter than other stars.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a CER on why the sun looks brighter. Your child writes the CER using the flashlight data as evidence.

- **Q:** Claim.
  **A:** The sun looks brighter because it is much closer than other stars.

**Assessment:** Brightness graph and CER.

**Answer key:** CER rubric: 4 = clear, accurate claim; specific evidence with numbers from the data; reasoning that links evidence to the science idea (particles, conservation, etc.); 3 = accurate claim and evidence, reasoning partial; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or inaccurate. Graph: distance vs brightness with labeled axes.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Three branches, checks and balances, and federalism

**Prepare:** Ben's Guide; iCivics; three-branch diagram.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the president makes laws.
- Thinking states have no powers.
- Thinking the Supreme Court writes laws.

### Day 1 (Monday): Legislative branch
*Student:* Read Article I basics and diagram how a bill becomes a law.

*How to teach:* Read Article I basics; diagram how a bill becomes law. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Steps?
  **A:** Introduced, passed both houses, signed by president.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Executive and judicial
*Student:* Read about the president's and Supreme Court's powers and complete a three-branch tree.

*How to teach:* Read about the executive and judicial branches; complete the tree. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Judicial role?
  **A:** Interprets laws.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Checks and balances
*Student:* Play an iCivics checks-and-balances game and list 4 checks between branches.

*How to teach:* Play iCivics; list 4 checks. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Veto; override; judicial review; Senate confirmation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Federalism
*Student:* Sort 15 powers into federal, state or shared (e.g., printing money, schools, taxes).

*How to teach:* Sort 15 powers into federal, state or shared. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Schools?
  **A:** State.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on branches, checks and balances and federalism.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Federal power?
  **A:** Printing money.

**Assessment:** Three-branch diagram and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: branches, checks, federalism examples.

## Art & Music 5: Program music: telling stories with sound

**Prepare:** Find Prokofiev's 'Peter and the Wolf' (1936) and Grieg's 'In the Hall of the Mountain King' (from Peer Gynt). Gather household sound makers. Choose a short story from ELA.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking program music needs words. It tells stories with instruments, themes, tempo and dynamics.
- Thinking louder means faster. Tempo (speed) and dynamics (volume) are separate.

### Day 1 (Monday): Peter and the Wolf
*Student:* Listen to 'Peter and the Wolf' and match each character to its instrument and theme.

*How to teach:* Listen and match characters to instruments. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Which instrument plays Peter? The wolf?
  **A:** Peter = strings; wolf = French horns (bird = flute, duck = oboe, cat = clarinet, grandfather = bassoon).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mountain King
*Student:* Listen to 'In the Hall of the Mountain King' and graph how tempo and dynamics build.

*How to teach:* Graph tempo and dynamics building in 'Mountain King.' Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What happens to tempo and dynamics in 'In the Hall of the Mountain King'?
  **A:** Both gradually increase—accelerando and crescendo—building to a wild ending.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sound story plan
*Student:* Choose a short story (from ELA) and assign sounds and instruments to characters and events.

*How to teach:* Plan a sound story assigning sounds to characters/events. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a leitmotif?
  **A:** A short musical theme representing a character or idea.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improvising
*Student:* Improvise and record the sound story with household instruments.

*How to teach:* Improvise and record the sound story. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** How could you show a chase in sound?
  **A:** Faster tempo and louder dynamics.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play the recording for family and explain how your choices of dynamics and tempo show the story.

*How to teach:* Play for family; explain choices. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is a crescendo?
  **A:** Gradually getting louder.

**Assessment:** Sound story recording and explanation.

**Answer key:** Sound story rubric: 4 = each character has a distinct sound/motif, tempo and dynamics change with events, clear explanation; 3 = most; 2 = few; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 5: Kickball and base running

**Prepare:** Gather a playground ball and four bases (cones). Draw a diamond diagram for Friday. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Slowing down before first base. Run through first base; you can overrun it.
- Running on a caught fly ball. Runners must tag up (return to their base) before advancing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kicking
*Student:* Kick rolled balls to left, center and right field on purpose.

*How to teach:* Roll balls; your child kicks to left, center and right by turning the kicking foot and hips.

- **Q:** To kick to the right side (for a right-footed kicker), what changes?
  **A:** Contact the ball later/with the inside of the foot angled right and point the hips toward right field.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Base running
*Student:* Practice running through first base and rounding bases efficiently.

*How to teach:* Practice running through first base, and rounding bases with a small curve. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why can you run past first base?
  **A:** The rules allow overrunning first base, so you don't slow down.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rules
*Student:* Learn force outs and tagging up and practice decisions in scenarios.

*How to teach:* Explain force outs and tagging up; quiz with scenarios. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** Runner on first, ball kicked on the ground. Where is the force out?
  **A:** At second base (and first for the kicker).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kickball game
*Student:* Play a family or neighborhood kickball game.

*How to teach:* Play a kickball game. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** If a fly ball is caught, what must runners do?
  **A:** Return to (tag) their base before advancing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Diagram where to kick the ball when fielders are positioned in a given way.

*How to teach:* Your child diagrams where to kick based on fielder positions. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** If all fielders are on the left side, where should you kick?
  **A:** To the right side/open space.

**Assessment:** Kick placement accuracy and strategy diagram.

**Answer key:** Kick placement: aims to left, center and right on request (proficient 2/3 each). Strategy diagram correctly places the kick in open space, explains why.

## Health 5: Getting help and community resources

**Prepare:** Make a wallet-card template. Look up your local resources (pediatrician, school or co-op counselor). Key numbers: 911 (emergency), Poison Control 1-800-222-1222, 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text). Al-Anon/Alateen supports young people affected by someone else's drinking. Find a short story or KidsHealth article about a child whose parent drinks too much. PARENT PREVIEW: if your family has experienced addiction, read the story first and be ready for questions.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a family member's drinking or drug use is the child's fault, or that the child can fix it. It is never the child's fault; adults are responsible for getting help.
- Thinking telling an adult about a vape or pills will get you or a friend 'in trouble.' Telling keeps people safe; trusted adults want to know.

### Day 1 (Monday): Who can help?
*Student:* List trusted adults and community resources (doctor, school counselor, Poison Control, faith leader) for drug concerns.

*How to teach:* Your child lists trusted adults and community resources for drug concerns (parent, doctor, school counselor, Poison Control, faith leader, relative).

- **Q:** Who would you call if a younger sibling swallowed pills from a cabinet?
  **A:** Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 right away (911 if the child is unconscious, having trouble breathing, or having a seizure), and tell a parent.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family affected
*Student:* Read a story about a family member's alcohol problem and discuss feelings and where kids can get support.

*How to teach:* Read the story; ask how the child feels (confused, embarrassed, worried) and where they could get support.

- **Q:** Name two places a kid can get support when a family member has an alcohol problem.
  **A:** Any two: a trusted adult (relative, teacher), school counselor, doctor, Alateen, faith leader.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Help script
*Student:* Write a script for telling a trusted adult you found a vape or pills.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a short script: 'I need to tell you something. I found ___ at ___. I'm worried. Can you help?' Practice saying it.

- **Q:** What three things should your script include?
  **A:** What you found or saw, where/when, and a clear request for help.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Resource card
*Student:* Design a wallet-size resource card with key numbers.

*How to teach:* Design a wallet-size card with 911, Poison Control, 988, and two trusted adults' numbers. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** When do you call 911 instead of Poison Control?
  **A:** When someone is unconscious, not breathing, having a seizure, or in immediate danger.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Alcohol and Other Drugs unit test.

*How to teach:* Give the Alcohol and Other Drugs unit test. Give it without notes, then review each missed item together using the answer key below and have your child correct it in their own words.

- **Q:** What is the first thing to do if you find pills or a vape?
  **A:** Don't touch or use it; tell a trusted adult right away.

**Assessment:** Unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: 1) alcohol is a depressant 2) liver breaks it down 3) legal age for alcohol and cannabis in CA = 21 4) THC is the active chemical in cannabis 5) edibles may look like candy—don't eat, tell an adult 6) inhalants can cause sudden death even first time 7) OTC vs. prescription differences 8) never share prescriptions 9) Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 10) tell a trusted adult. Proficient = 80% or better.
