# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 21

## ELA 5: Esperanza Rising: Los Espárragos, Los Duraznos, Las Uvas

**Prepare:** Preview 'Los Espárragos,' 'Los Duraznos' and 'Las Uvas.' Prepare a theme organizer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the novel ends where it began with no change. The grapes return but Esperanza is changed.
- Thinking a symbol is just an object. A symbol stands for an idea.
- Believing Miguel betrayed her. He used the money orders to bring Abuelita.

### Day 1 (Monday): Los Espárragos
*Student:* Read 'Los Espárragos' and discuss the betrayal and Esperanza's decision about Abuelita's money.

*How to teach:* Read 'Los Espárragos'; discuss the discovery that the money orders are gone and Esperanza's feeling of betrayal. Read together and stop at the discovery. Ask your child to predict what happened to the money orders and why.

- **Q:** Why does Esperanza think Miguel betrayed her?
  **A:** Her saved money orders for bringing Abuelita are missing and Miguel has left.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Los Duraznos
*Student:* Read 'Los Duraznos' and track the symbol of the phoenix and the blanket through the book.

*How to teach:* Read 'Los Duraznos'; track the phoenix and blanket symbols. Your child finds every mention of the phoenix and the blanket they can and explains what each moment shows.

- **Q:** What does the phoenix symbolize?
  **A:** Rising from the ashes — starting over and being reborn after disaster.
- **Q:** What does the blanket symbolize?
  **A:** Life's ups and downs (zigzag mountains and valleys) and Abuelita's love.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Las Uvas
*Student:* Read the final chapter and explain why the novel ends with the same fruit it begins with.

*How to teach:* Read 'Las Uvas'; explain why the novel ends with grapes. Compare the first and last chapters side by side: what is the same, and what is different about Esperanza?

- **Q:** Why end with grapes?
  **A:** It shows a full circle: Esperanza is now on her birthday again but changed — humble and hopeful, with Abuelita back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme
*Student:* Write 2 theme statements and support each with 3 pieces of evidence from across the novel.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 2 theme statements with 3 pieces of evidence each. Model one theme as a complete sentence. Evidence should come from the beginning, middle and end of the novel.

- **Q:** Theme example.
  **A:** Do not be afraid to start over; or, true wealth is family and love, not possessions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a whole-novel reading assessment on plot, character change and symbolism.

*How to teach:* Give the whole-novel reading assessment. Give it without help; afterward review missed questions by returning to the text.

- **Q:** How does Esperanza change?
  **A:** From a spoiled, proud girl to a hardworking, compassionate young woman who helps support her family.

**Assessment:** Theme organizer; novel assessment.

**Answer key:** Theme organizer: 2 statements, 3 pieces of evidence each from different parts of the novel. Novel assessment (suggested): 1) Papa's death 2) escape to California 3) Mama's Valley Fever 4) strike conflict 5) money orders and Miguel bringing Abuelita 6) phoenix = rebirth 7) blanket = ups and downs 8) Esperanza's change.

## Math 5: Multiplication as scaling and real-world problems

**Prepare:** A simple picture drawn on grid paper (e.g., a house 8 squares wide, 6 tall), blank grid paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any multiplication makes a number bigger. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 shrinks; by more than 1 enlarges; by 1 keeps it the same.
- Scaling only one dimension of a picture. Both width and height change by the scale factor.
- Forgetting to convert mixed numbers before multiplying.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scaling predictions
*Student:* Without calculating, decide whether 15 products like 3/4 x 12 or 1 1/2 x 12 are greater than, less than or equal to 12, and justify.

*How to teach:* Ask: is 3/4 × 12 more or less than 12? Less, since you take only part of 12. Then 1 1/2 × 12: more. The child decides 15 cases with a reason.

- **Q:** Is 3/4 × 12 greater than, less than or equal to 12?
  **A:** Less (it's 9), because 3/4 < 1.
- **Q:** Is 1 1/2 × 12 greater or less than 12?
  **A:** Greater (18), because 1 1/2 > 1.
- **Q:** 5/5 × 12?
  **A:** Equal to 12, because 5/5 = 1.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Resizing pictures
*Student:* Redraw a simple grid picture at 1/2 size and 1 1/2 size and explain how the scale factor changed it.

*How to teach:* Redraw the grid picture at 1/2 size and 1 1/2 size, scaling every length. The child explains how the factor changed it.

- **Q:** An 8-square-wide house at 1 1/2 size is how wide?
  **A:** 12 squares.
- **Q:** At 1/2 size?
  **A:** 4 squares wide.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Real-world problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems such as scaling a recipe by 2/3 or finding 3/8 of a 2 1/2 mile trail.

*How to teach:* Solve real-world scaling problems with an estimate first. Model: before solving 3/8 of 2 1/2, predict 'less than 2 1/2 because 3/8 < 1.' Your child writes an estimate sentence for each problem.

- **Q:** 3/8 of a 2 1/2 mile trail?
  **A:** 3/8 × 5/2 = 15/16 mile.
- **Q:** Scale 1 1/2 cups of oats by 2/3.
  **A:** 1 cup.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mixed practice
*Student:* Complete 15 mixed fraction multiplication problems and simplify answers.

*How to teach:* The child completes 15 mixed fraction multiplication problems and simplifies every answer. Remind your child to simplify before or after multiplying. Spot-check 5 answers and have them redo any not fully simplified.

- **Q:** 4/9 × 3/8 = ?
  **A:** 12/72 = 1/6

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz with scaling questions and multi-step fraction multiplication word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz with scaling questions and multi-step word problems. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** A 24-inch plant grows to 1 1/4 times its height. How tall now?
  **A:** 30 inches.

**Assessment:** Quiz on scaling and real-world fraction multiplication.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 2/3 × 15 < 15 2) 7/4 × 15 > 15 3) 1 × 15 = 15 4) Without computing: 5 × 3/4 < 5 5) 5/6 × 3/5 = 1/2 6) 2 1/4 × 4 = 9 7) Recipe 2 1/4 cups × 2/3 = 1 1/2 cups 8) Multi-step: a 3/4 acre field, 2/3 planted with corn, of which 1/2 is harvested: 3/4 × 2/3 × 1/2 = 1/4 acre. Scaling explanations must name factor > 1, < 1 or = 1.

## Science 5: Weathering, erosion and deposition

**Prepare:** Baking pan, sand and soil, watering can, rocks, jar with lid, sugar cubes, hair dryer. Work outside or on a tray.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up weathering and erosion. Weathering breaks rock; erosion moves pieces.
- Thinking deposition is erosion. Deposition is where sediment is dropped.
- Thinking plants have no effect on erosion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shake test
*Student:* Shake sugar cubes or small rocks in a jar with water for 3 minutes and observe physical weathering.

*How to teach:* Shake sugar cubes in a jar with water for 3 minutes and observe. Shake for 3 full minutes and observe the sugar cubes or rocks. Ask what real-world process this models (rocks tumbling in a river).

- **Q:** What happened to the cubes and what does it model?
  **A:** Corners broke and rounded — physical weathering, like rocks in a river.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stream table
*Student:* Build a sloped sand-and-soil pan and pour water slowly to observe erosion and deposition; sketch the channel and delta.

*How to teach:* Build a sloped pan and pour water slowly; sketch the channel and delta. Prop the pan on a book; pour slowly from one end. Your child sketches where sand was removed and where it collected.

- **Q:** Where is sand deposited?
  **A:** At the bottom where water slows, forming a delta.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Variables
*Student:* Repeat the stream table test with a steeper slope and with plants (grass clumps) and compare erosion.

*How to teach:* Repeat with a steeper slope and with grass clumps; compare. Change only one thing each time (slope, then plants). Your child compares sketches and states which caused more erosion.

- **Q:** How did a steeper slope and plants change erosion?
  **A:** Steeper = more erosion; plants = less erosion because roots hold soil.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Wind erosion
*Student:* Use a hair dryer on dry sand to model wind erosion and compare to wet sand.

*How to teach:* Blow a hair dryer on dry sand vs. wet sand.

- **Q:** Which erodes more?
  **A:** Dry sand; water holds grains together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write about how the sphere interactions changed the land in the models and connect to a CA example (Yosemite Valley or coastal cliffs).

*How to teach:* Your child writes about sphere interactions in the models and a CA example. Your child connects at least two models to spheres and to a California landform (Yosemite or coastal cliffs).

- **Q:** How was Yosemite Valley shaped?
  **A:** Glaciers (hydrosphere) carved the valley in granite (geosphere), and rivers continue to erode it.

**Assessment:** Stream table sketches and written explanation.

**Answer key:** Written explanation rubric: 4 = defines weathering, erosion, deposition, explains model results and connects to a CA example with spheres; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = definitions only; 1 = incomplete. Sketches: channel and delta labeled.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Boston Massacre, Boston Tea Party and the Coercive Acts

**Prepare:** Paul Revere's 1770 engraving; Captain Preston's account excerpt.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the engraving is accurate.
- Thinking the Tea Party was spontaneous.
- Mixing up Coercive Acts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Boston Massacre engraving
*Student:* Analyze Paul Revere's engraving, listing details that make the British look guilty.

*How to teach:* Look closely at Revere's engraving. Your child lists details that make the British look guilty.

- **Q:** Details making British look guilty?
  **A:** Soldiers firing in line, an officer ordering them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Another account
*Student:* Compare the engraving with Captain Preston's account and decide why the stories differ.

*How to teach:* Compare the engraving with Preston's account. Discuss why stories differ.

- **Q:** Why differ?
  **A:** Different perspectives and purposes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Boston Tea Party
*Student:* Read about the Tea Act and Tea Party and write a newspaper headline from both Patriot and Loyalist views.

*How to teach:* Read about the Tea Act and Tea Party. Your child writes Patriot and Loyalist headlines.

- **Q:** Patriot headline?
  **A:** Patriots defend liberty!

### Day 4 (Thursday): Coercive Acts and Committees
*Student:* List the Coercive Acts and explain how Committees of Correspondence helped colonies unite.

*How to teach:* List the Coercive Acts. Explain how Committees of Correspondence helped colonies unite.

- **Q:** What did the Coercive Acts do?
  **A:** Closed Boston Harbor and limited Massachusetts self-government.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz and create a cause-and-effect chain from the Stamp Act to the Coercive Acts.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; your child creates a cause-and-effect chain from 1765 to 1774. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Chain?
  **A:** Stamp Act → protests → Townshend Acts → Boston Massacre → Tea Act → Tea Party → Coercive Acts.

**Assessment:** Engraving analysis and cause-effect chain.

**Answer key:** Engraving analysis and chain: accurate order.

## Art & Music 5: Melody: steps, skips and leaps

**Prepare:** Bookmark musictheory.net note-identification exercise (treble clef). Print 'Ode to Joy' and 'Twinkle, Twinkle' notation and an 8-measure recorder melody. Recorder low D = thumb + all three left fingers + three right fingers (only the pinky hole open); check your fingering chart for F-sharp, which differs slightly between baroque and German recorders.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a skip means skipping a line on the staff. A skip moves from a line to the next line (or space to next space), skipping one note name.
- Mixing up lines and spaces. Treble lines (bottom to top) are E G B D F; spaces are F A C E.

### Day 1 (Monday): Melodic contour
*Student:* Listen to 4 melodies and draw their contour shapes with lines.

*How to teach:* Play 4 melodies; your child draws their contour (up, down, wavy). Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** What is melodic contour?
  **A:** The shape of a melody as it moves up and down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Steps, skips and leaps
*Student:* Identify steps, skips and leaps in 'Ode to Joy' and 'Twinkle, Twinkle' notation.

*How to teach:* Identify steps (next note), skips (skip one note) and leaps (bigger) in 'Ode to Joy' and 'Twinkle.'

- **Q:** In 'Twinkle, Twinkle,' the notes go C C G G. Is C to G a step, skip or leap?
  **A:** A leap (a fifth).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Note naming
*Student:* Practice naming notes on the treble staff with musictheory.net exercises until scoring 90%.

*How to teach:* Your child practices on musictheory.net until scoring 90%. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Name the lines of the treble staff from bottom to top.
  **A:** E, G, B, D, F.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recorder: F-sharp and low D
*Student:* Learn low D and F-sharp on recorder and play a D-major warm-up.

*How to teach:* Teach low D and F-sharp; play a warm-up D-E-F#-G-A and back. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why does low D need gentle breath?
  **A:** Low notes squeak if you blow too hard.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a note-naming and interval quiz and play an 8-measure melody.

*How to teach:* Take the note-naming and interval quiz; play an 8-measure melody. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Name the spaces of the treble staff from bottom to top.
  **A:** F, A, C, E.

**Assessment:** Note-naming quiz and melody performance.

**Answer key:** Quiz: name 10 notes on the treble staff (lines E G B D F, spaces F A C E) and label 5 intervals as step, skip or leap. Proficient = 90% on notes, 4/5 intervals. Melody performance: correct notes and steady rhythm (4 = no errors; 3 = 1–2; 2 = several; 1 = incomplete).

## PE 5: Square dance and line dance basics

**Prepare:** Find music: a Virginia Reel recording (fiddle tune) and a beginner line dance (e.g., Electric Slide-style). Learn the reel order from a short video. You need at least 2 couples—recruit family or play the extra role yourself. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Do-si-do and swing are the same. Do-si-do: pass right shoulders, go back-to-back, return without turning. Swing: link elbows (or hold) and turn around together.
- Ignoring the music. Steps should land on the beat; listen for 8-count phrases.

### Day 1 (Monday): Basic figures
*Student:* Learn do-si-do, promenade, swing and allemande with family partners.

*How to teach:* Teach do-si-do, promenade (side by side, walking counterclockwise holding hands), swing, and allemande left (left forearms, turn around and back).

- **Q:** Describe a do-si-do.
  **A:** Partners walk forward, pass right shoulders, step back-to-back, then walk backward to place without turning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Virginia Reel
*Student:* Learn and perform the Virginia Reel (a colonial-era dance tied to HSS).

*How to teach:* Teach the Virginia Reel figures in order: forward and back, right-hand turn, left-hand turn, two-hand turn, do-si-do, head couple sashays down and back, reel, cast off, arch. Connect it to colonial America.

- **Q:** Where does the Virginia Reel come from?
  **A:** It's an Anglo-American country dance popular in colonial and early American times, related to English and Scottish reels.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Line dance
*Student:* Learn grapevine, step-touch and pivot turns in a beginner line dance.

*How to teach:* Teach grapevine (side, behind, side, touch), step-touch, and a quarter pivot turn; dance a simple line dance.

- **Q:** What are the steps of a grapevine to the right?
  **A:** Step right, cross left behind, step right, touch left (or close).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Calling
*Student:* Take turns being the caller and following calls on the beat.

*How to teach:* Take turns as caller, calling moves a few beats early so dancers move on the beat. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why does a caller call moves before the beat they start on?
  **A:** So dancers have time to hear it and begin the move right on the beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the Virginia Reel and line dance for a family audience.

*How to teach:* Perform both dances for family. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** Name two dance figures from this week.
  **A:** Any two: do-si-do, promenade, swing, allemande, sashay, grapevine, pivot turn.

**Assessment:** Dance performance checklist.

**Answer key:** Dance checklist (✓ each): performs figures in correct order, stays on the beat, knows partner/position, uses correct footwork for grapevine and pivot, shows courtesy to partners. Proficient = 4 of 5.

## Health 5: Emotional and social changes

**Prepare:** Print a feelings wheel and write scenario cards (two friendship scenarios, stereotype statements such as 'Boys don't cry' and 'Girls are bad at math'). Have the health journal ready. PARENT PREVIEW: watch the videos and read materials before your child does; decide how you'll answer personal or values questions, and keep the anonymous question box open.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking strong or changing emotions mean something is wrong with you. Hormones and a developing brain make feelings more intense; this is normal and manageable.
- Thinking a crush means you have to act on it or tell everyone. Crushes are normal feelings; you can keep them private and always treat others respectfully.
- Treating stereotypes as facts. Statements about what all boys or all girls 'should' do are stereotypes; people vary.

### Day 1 (Monday): Feelings wheel
*Student:* Use a feelings wheel to name emotions common in puberty and write about one.

*How to teach:* Show the feelings wheel; your child points to emotions they've felt lately (embarrassed, frustrated, excited, anxious). They write a paragraph about one: what caused it and what helped.

- **Q:** Why might emotions feel stronger during puberty?
  **A:** Changing hormone levels and brain development make feelings more intense and can change quickly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Changing friendships
*Student:* Read two scenarios about friends drifting apart and use the decision steps to solve them.

*How to teach:* Read two friendship-drift scenarios; your child applies the five decision steps to each. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Your best friend starts sitting with a new group. Name two healthy options.
  **A:** Talk to your friend honestly about how you feel; join the group or invite them to do something; also make new friends or join a club.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crushes and respect
*Student:* Discuss crushes as normal and list ways to treat others respectfully, including respecting 'no'.

*How to teach:* Talk calmly about crushes as normal feelings. List respectful behaviors: kind words, no pressure, no teasing, and accepting 'no' immediately.

- **Q:** If someone says 'no' or 'stop,' what should you do?
  **A:** Stop right away and respect their answer, without arguing or pressuring.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Gender stereotypes
*Student:* Sort statements about how boys and girls 'should' act into stereotypes vs. facts and discuss respect for all.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts statements into stereotype vs. fact and rewrites each stereotype respectfully.

- **Q:** Is 'Boys don't cry' a fact or a stereotype? Rewrite it.
  **A:** A stereotype. Respectful version: 'Everyone has feelings, and crying is a normal way to express them.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a journal entry on a coping strategy for emotional changes and complete a check.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a journal entry about a coping strategy, then answers the check orally. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Name three healthy coping strategies for strong emotions.
  **A:** Any three: talk to a trusted adult or friend, exercise, deep breathing, journaling, drawing/music, taking a break, getting enough sleep.

**Assessment:** Journal entry and check.

**Answer key:** Check key: 1) Emotions get stronger due to hormones/brain development — normal 2) a respectful response to a friendship change 3) 'no' means stop 4) identifies stereotypes vs. facts 5) names a trusted adult. Journal rubric: 4 = names a specific emotion, a realistic coping strategy and a trusted adult, with reflection on how it would help; 3 = strategy and emotion; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.
