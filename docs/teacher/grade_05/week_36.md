# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 36

## ELA 5: Presentation and year-end portfolio

**Prepare:** Slideshow; writing portfolio folder; list of books read; fluency passage from week 1 level; end-of-year cold read and essay prompt.

**Watch for:**
- Reading from slides.
- Summaries with too many details.
- Reflections that just say 'I liked it.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Presentation day
*Student:* Deliver the multimedia presentation and answer audience questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents to family and answers questions. Score with the presentation rubric.

- **Q:** What makes a strong answer to an audience question?
  **A:** A direct answer with a fact from research.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Listening and summarizing
*Student:* Watch a family member's or online presentation and summarize its main points and supporting details.

*How to teach:* Watch a family member's or online presentation together. Your child writes the main points and supporting details in a short summary.

- **Q:** Summary structure?
  **A:** Main idea + 2–3 supporting details.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Portfolio selection
*Student:* Choose the best narrative, informative and opinion pieces from the year and write a reflection card for each.

*How to teach:* Spread out the portfolio. Your child chooses the best narrative, informative and opinion pieces and writes a reflection card for each.

- **Q:** What goes on a reflection card?
  **A:** Why you chose it, what you learned, what you'd improve.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reading reflection
*Student:* List all books read this year, rate them, and write a letter to next year's self about reading goals; do a final fluency check.

*How to teach:* Your child lists and rates books read this year and writes a letter to next year's self about reading goals. Do a 1-minute fluency read and compare with week 1.

- **Q:** How to measure growth?
  **A:** Compare this WPM with week 1.

### Day 5 (Friday): End-of-year assessment
*Student:* Take an end-of-year reading and writing assessment (cold read with evidence questions and an on-demand essay).

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year cold read and on-demand essay without help. Score with the rubrics and celebrate growth.

- **Q:** What makes a strong evidence answer?
  **A:** Claim, exact quotation, explanation.

**Assessment:** Presentation rubric; portfolio reflections; end-of-year assessment and fluency WPM compared to week 1.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = clear, organized, well-paced with media and sources, confident answers; 3 = minor issues; 2 = disorganized; 1 = incomplete. Portfolio: 3 pieces with reflections. End-of-year: Cold-read rubric (choose an unfamiliar grade-5 story from CommonLit): 4 = states a precise theme/central idea, explains character with 2+ exact quotations correctly punctuated and explained; 3 = accurate answer with at least one relevant quotation and explanation; 2 = accurate but general answer, evidence paraphrased or loosely connected; 1 = inaccurate or no evidence. Essay: 4-level rubric for claim, evidence, organization, conventions. Fluency: compare to week 1 WPM.

## Math 5: Capstone: Plan a backyard garden

**Prepare:** Graph paper, a garden store flyer or website prices for seeds, soil and boards, calculator for checking only. Set a $150 budget.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing units (inches and feet) in volume. Convert all dimensions to feet before computing cubic feet.
- Confusing area (square feet for planting) with volume (cubic feet of soil).
- Rounding prices too early in the budget.

### Day 1 (Monday): Design the layout
*Student:* Draw a garden plan on a coordinate grid, labeling the corners of each bed with ordered pairs.

*How to teach:* The child draws the garden on a coordinate grid, labeling each bed's corners with ordered pairs. Give the grid size (e.g., 20 × 20 feet). Your child labels each bed's corners and checks dimensions by subtracting coordinates.

- **Q:** A bed has corners (2,2), (8,2), (8,5), (2,5). What are its dimensions?
  **A:** 6 units by 3 units.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planter volume
*Student:* Calculate the soil volume for 2 raised planter boxes (including one composite shape) and convert to cubic feet.

*How to teach:* Compute soil volume for two planters (one composite), converting to cubic feet. Help convert inches to feet first (12 in = 1 ft). Your child shows V = l × w × h for each planter and adds the parts of the composite one.

- **Q:** A planter is 6 ft × 2 ft × 1 1/2 ft. Volume?
  **A:** 18 cubic feet.
- **Q:** A planter is 24 in × 36 in × 12 in. Volume in cubic feet?
  **A:** 2 ft × 3 ft × 1 ft = 6 cubic feet.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fractions of space
*Student:* Decide what fraction of each bed gets each plant and compute the area for each crop with fraction multiplication.

*How to teach:* Decide what fraction of each bed gets each plant; compute area with fraction multiplication. Your child decides on fractions (e.g., 1/2 tomatoes, 1/4 herbs) and multiplies each by the bed's area.

- **Q:** A 6 ft × 3 ft bed; 2/3 is tomatoes. How many square feet?
  **A:** 18 × 2/3 = 12 sq ft.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Budget
*Student:* Use decimal prices to total seeds, soil and boards, staying under a $150 budget.

*How to teach:* Total seeds, soil and boards using decimal prices; stay under $150. Your child lists items with prices in a table, totals them and subtracts from $150.

- **Q:** Soil $7.49 × 4 bags + seeds $12.75 = ?
  **A:** $29.96 + $12.75 = $42.71

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present the garden plan to family, explaining each calculation, and complete a year-end math reflection.

*How to teach:* The child presents the plan to family, explaining each calculation, and writes a year-end reflection. Your child presents each calculation step by step. Then they write a reflection on their favorite math topic and biggest growth.

- **Q:** Why did you multiply to find the soil needed?
  **A:** Volume = length × width × height tells how many cubic feet of soil fill the box.

**Assessment:** Capstone project rubric: accuracy of calculations, use of grid and units, clarity of explanation.

**Answer key:** Capstone rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning). Accuracy of calculations: proficient = all volume, area and budget computations correct (minor slip at most). Use of grid and units: proficient = every bed corner labeled with correct ordered pairs; square feet for area, cubic feet for volume, dollars for cost. Clarity: proficient = explains each step and why that operation was used; total cost under $150.

## Science 5: Capstone: analyzing and presenting

**Prepare:** Tri-fold board or slides, graphs, photos.

**Watch for:**
- Hypothesis 'wrong' means failure.
- Conclusions without data.
- Ignoring sources of error.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analysis
*Student:* Analyze data, describe patterns and decide whether the hypothesis was supported.

*How to teach:* Analyze data; decide if hypothesis was supported. Your child describes patterns in the data and decides if the hypothesis was supported.

- **Q:** What if the data didn't support the hypothesis?
  **A:** That is still a valid result; explain what you learned.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Conclusion
*Student:* Write a conclusion with sources of error and a new question for further investigation.

*How to teach:* Write a conclusion with error sources and a new question. Your child writes a conclusion, error sources and a new question.

- **Q:** Example error source.
  **A:** Inconsistent measuring.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Display
*Student:* Create a science fair board or slideshow with question, hypothesis, procedure, data, graph and conclusion.

*How to teach:* Create a board or slideshow. Your child creates the display with all sections.

- **Q:** What sections?
  **A:** Question, hypothesis, procedure, data, graph, conclusion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Science fair
*Student:* Present the investigation to family or a co-op science fair and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present to family or a co-op science fair. Your child presents and answers questions.

- **Q:** How to answer a question you don't know?
  **A:** Say so and suggest how to find out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Year reflection
*Student:* Review the science notebook and write about the three most important ideas learned this year.

*How to teach:* Your child reviews the notebook and writes about the three most important ideas. Your child reviews the notebook and writes about three big ideas.

- **Q:** Example big idea.
  **A:** Matter is made of particles and is conserved.

**Assessment:** Science fair rubric and year-end reflection.

**Answer key:** Science fair rubric (4 levels): question and hypothesis; controlled procedure; data and graph; conclusion with error; presentation. Reflection: 3 big ideas explained.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: 50 states and capitals; year review

**Prepare:** Blank U.S. map; flashcards; timeline from all units.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing similar capitals.
- Timeline events out of order.
- Rights without responsibilities.

### Day 1 (Monday): Western states
*Student:* Learn the remaining Mountain and Pacific states and capitals, including Alaska and Hawaii.

*How to teach:* Learn Mountain and Pacific states and capitals. Pause after the reading to ask your child to retell the key facts in their own words before writing anything.

- **Q:** Capital of Nevada?
  **A:** Carson City.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): All 50 practice
*Student:* Practice all 50 states and capitals with a blank map game and online puzzle.

*How to teach:* Practice all 50 with a map game. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Capital of Alaska?
  **A:** Juneau.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timeline review
*Student:* Build a class timeline from first peoples to 1850 with 20 key events from the year.

*How to teach:* Build a timeline of 20 key events. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Order: Declaration, Constitution, Jamestown.
  **A:** Jamestown 1607, Declaration 1776, Constitution 1787.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Being a citizen
*Student:* Write a reflection on the American creed: one right you value and one responsibility you will practice.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on a right and a responsibility. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Example responsibility?
  **A:** Voting, obeying laws.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take the 50 states and capitals map test and a year-end history review.

*How to teach:* Give the 50-states test and year-end review. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Capital of California?
  **A:** Sacramento.

**Assessment:** 50 states and capitals test and year-end review.

**Answer key:** 50 states test: Northeast capitals: Maine–Augusta, New Hampshire–Concord, Vermont–Montpelier, Massachusetts–Boston, Rhode Island–Providence, Connecticut–Hartford, New York–Albany, New Jersey–Trenton, Pennsylvania–Harrisburg, Delaware–Dover, Maryland–Annapolis. Southeast capitals: Virginia–Richmond, West Virginia–Charleston, Kentucky–Frankfort, Tennessee–Nashville, North Carolina–Raleigh, South Carolina–Columbia, Georgia–Atlanta, Florida–Tallahassee, Alabama–Montgomery, Mississippi–Jackson. Midwest capitals: Ohio–Columbus, Indiana–Indianapolis, Illinois–Springfield, Michigan–Lansing, Wisconsin–Madison, Minnesota–Saint Paul, Iowa–Des Moines, Missouri–Jefferson City, North Dakota–Bismarck, South Dakota–Pierre, Nebraska–Lincoln, Kansas–Topeka. South Central capitals: Texas–Austin, Oklahoma–Oklahoma City, Arkansas–Little Rock, Louisiana–Baton Rouge. West capitals: Montana–Helena, Idaho–Boise, Wyoming–Cheyenne, Nevada–Carson City, Utah–Salt Lake City, Colorado–Denver, Arizona–Phoenix, New Mexico–Santa Fe, Washington–Olympia, Oregon–Salem, California–Sacramento, Alaska–Juneau, Hawaii–Honolulu.

## Art & Music 5: Exhibition and concert night

**Prepare:** Make invitations and set a date. Gather the exhibition, concert program and a recording device. Review audience etiquette (quiet during pieces, applause at the end).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking mistakes ruin a performance. Keep going; most audiences won't notice.
- Skipping evaluation after the event. Reflecting helps you grow.

### Day 1 (Monday): Invitations
*Student:* Make and send invitations to family or friends for the exhibition and concert.

*How to teach:* Make and send invitations. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What information goes on an invitation?
  **A:** Event, date, time, place, and RSVP.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final rehearsal
*Student:* Run the full concert once and practice bows and introductions.

*How to teach:* Run the full concert; practice bows and introductions. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** When should you bow?
  **A:** After each piece or at the end when the audience applauds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Exhibition and concert
*Student:* Host the exhibition with a gallery talk and perform the concert.

*How to teach:* Host the exhibition with a gallery talk and perform the concert. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What should you do if you make a mistake while performing?
  **A:** Keep going without stopping or reacting.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluating
*Student:* Watch a recording of the concert and evaluate it using established criteria.

*How to teach:* Watch the recording; evaluate with criteria. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name one strength and one improvement from your recording.
  **A:** Answer varies; should be specific.

### Day 5 (Friday): Year reflection
*Student:* Write a final reflection on your growth in art and music and set a goal for next year.

*How to teach:* Write a final reflection and a goal for next year. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is one way you grew in art or music this year?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name a specific skill.

**Assessment:** Exhibition and concert performance; year-end reflection.

**Answer key:** Exhibition and concert: 4 = confident gallery talk, accurate expressive performance, good etiquette; 3 = minor issues; 2 = several issues; 1 = incomplete. Year reflection proficient if it names specific growth in both art and music and a goal.

## PE 5: Summer activity plan and field day

**Prepare:** Gather field-day equipment (ropes, balls, cones, sacks or pillowcases, disc), a planning template, and invite family or co-op friends for Wednesday. Check local options for a lifetime activity (trail, bike path, pool) for Thursday. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking exercise only happens in PE. Lifetime activities like hiking, biking, swimming and dancing keep you healthy all year and as an adult.
- Thinking leading means bossing. Good leaders explain clearly, demonstrate, keep people safe and encourage everyone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Summer plan
*Student:* Create a summer activity plan with weekly goals and favorite activities.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a summer calendar: at least 60 minutes of activity most days, weekly goals, and favorite activities from this year.

- **Q:** Make this summer goal specific: 'Be active.'
  **A:** Example: 'I will bike or swim 30 minutes and jump rope 10 minutes, 5 days each week in June.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Field day planning
*Student:* Plan 6 field day stations using skills from this year.

*How to teach:* Plan 6 stations that use this year's skills (e.g., jump rope, dribble course, disc toss, basketball shooting, sack race, bump-to-target) with rules and equipment for each.

- **Q:** Choose one station and name the critical cue you'll teach there.
  **A:** Example: disc toss — 'keep the disc flat and snap your wrist'; basketball — BEEF; bump — 'flat platform, legs lift.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Field day
*Student:* Host a family or co-op field day, leading the stations and encouraging others.

*How to teach:* Your child leads field day: explains each station, demonstrates, keeps time, and encourages participants.

- **Q:** Name two ways to encourage someone who is struggling at a station.
  **A:** Praise their effort specifically, give one simple tip, let them try an easier version, or cheer them on.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lifetime activities
*Student:* Try a new lifetime activity (hiking, biking, swimming) for 30 minutes.

*How to teach:* Try a new lifetime activity for 30 minutes and take a pulse afterward. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What makes an activity a 'lifetime activity'?
  **A:** You can keep doing it throughout life, often alone or with a few people and little equipment (hiking, biking, swimming, walking, dancing).

### Day 5 (Friday): Year reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on your favorite unit, biggest improvement and how you will stay active.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a year reflection: favorite unit, biggest improvement (with data), and a plan to stay active.

- **Q:** Use your data: what was your biggest improvement this year?
  **A:** Answer varies; a proficient answer cites baseline vs. post-test numbers (e.g., 'push-ups rose from 6 to 12').

**Assessment:** Summer plan and field day leadership observation.

**Answer key:** Summer plan rubric: 4 = weekly schedule meeting ~60 minutes/day, specific goals, variety of activities including one lifetime activity; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = vague; 1 = missing. Field-day leadership observation (✓ each): explains rules clearly, demonstrates a skill cue, keeps stations safe, encourages others, includes everyone. Proficient = 4 of 5.

## Health 5: Presentation and year review

**Prepare:** Arrange an audience (family, co-op or community group). Prepare a review game (e.g., Jeopardy categories: Nutrition, Fitness, Hygiene, Puberty, ATOD). Print the end-of-year assessment.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking health learning ends when the class does. Health skills are used every day.
- Thinking summer means no routines. Sleep, activity and hygiene habits matter all year.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Present the campaign to family, a co-op or community group.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the campaign. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What was your campaign's call to action?
  **A:** Answer varies; should be specific and clear.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Year review game
*Student:* Play a review game covering nutrition, fitness, hygiene, puberty and ATOD.

*How to teach:* Play the review game. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Name the six nutrients.
  **A:** Carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year health assessment.

*How to teach:* Take the end-of-year assessment. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What are the five components of fitness?
  **A:** Cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Summer plan
*Student:* Write a summer health plan with activity, sleep and hygiene goals.

*How to teach:* Write a summer health plan with activity, sleep and hygiene goals. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Write a SMART summer goal.
  **A:** Example: 'I will be active 60 minutes daily and go to bed by 9:00 p.m. on weeknights all summer.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on how you have grown this year and what health skill helps you most.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on growth this year and the most helpful health skill. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Which health skill helps you most, and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name a skill (decision making, refusal, accessing information) with an example.

**Assessment:** Campaign presentation and final assessment.

**Answer key:** Final assessment key: six nutrients; MyPlate; 5/20 rule; 60 minutes activity; five fitness components; hygiene (deodorant vs antiperspirant, brushing 2×2); 9–12 hours sleep; puberty starts ~8–14, hormones, pituitary; nicotine addictive, alcohol depressant; refusal steps; Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Campaign rubric: 4 = clear message, accurate cited facts, persuasive, confident delivery and call to action; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = major gaps; 1 = incomplete.
