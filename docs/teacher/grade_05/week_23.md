# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 23

## ELA 5: Analyzing arguments: reasons and evidence

**Prepare:** Choose paired opinion articles (CommonLit/ReadWorks) on zoos or homework and a short student speech/news clip. Argument map sheets.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking an opinion is evidence. Evidence is facts, statistics, examples or expert quotes.
- Confusing the claim with a reason.
- Assuming more evidence is better than relevant evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is an argument?
*Student:* Read a short opinion article and map its claim, reasons and evidence on a graphic organizer.

*How to teach:* Read the article aloud together. Model finding the claim (often in the first or last paragraph), then your child fills the organizer with each reason and the evidence under it.

- **Q:** What is a claim?
  **A:** The writer's main position.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluating evidence
*Student:* Rate 10 pieces of evidence as fact, statistic, expert quote or opinion and decide which are strongest.

*How to teach:* Define each type with an example. Your child sorts 10 evidence cards, then ranks the three strongest and explains why facts and expert quotes beat opinions.

- **Q:** Classify: '80% of students said…'
  **A:** Statistic.
- **Q:** Classify: 'Zoos are boring.'
  **A:** Opinion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two sides
*Student:* Read paired articles on whether zoos help animals and compare which reasons each author uses.

*How to teach:* Read both zoo articles, one per sitting if needed. Your child lists each author's reasons side by side and marks which are backed by evidence.

- **Q:** One pro and one con reason about zoos?
  **A:** Pro: zoos protect endangered species through breeding and research. Con: animals may suffer in small enclosures.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Listening for claims
*Student:* Watch a short student speech or news segment and summarize the speaker's claims and supporting reasons.

*How to teach:* Watch a short speech or news segment twice: first for the main claim, then for reasons. Your child writes a 3-sentence summary of the claim and two reasons.

- **Q:** What makes a speaker's reason convincing?
  **A:** It is supported by relevant evidence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz identifying claims and matching reasons to points in a new article.

*How to teach:* Give a new short opinion article and the quiz without help. Score it, then reread together any reason your child mismatched.

- **Q:** Which is a reason: 'Recess helps kids focus' or 'Recess should be longer'?
  **A:** 'Recess helps kids focus' is a reason; the other is the claim.

**Assessment:** Argument maps; reasons-and-evidence quiz.

**Answer key:** Argument maps: claim, 3 reasons, evidence matched. Quiz: proficient = correctly identifies claim and matches 4 of 5 reasons to evidence.

## Math 5: Fraction division word problems and unit assessment

**Prepare:** Measuring cups (1/3, 1/4, 1/2 cup), 2 cups of dry rice or water, bowls. Write the Unit 5 test from the key.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying when the question asks 'how many servings'. 'How many 1/3s fit in 2?' is division.
- Skipping a step in multi-step problems. Underline each question and answer it in order.
- Forgetting units in answers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Serving sizes
*Student:* Use a 1/3 cup measure to find how many servings are in 2 cups of rice, then solve 5 similar problems.

*How to teach:* Scoop 2 cups of rice with a 1/3 cup measure and count: 6 servings. Write 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6. The child solves 5 similar problems.

- **Q:** How many 1/3-cup servings in 2 cups?
  **A:** 6
- **Q:** How many 1/4-cup servings in 3 cups?
  **A:** 12

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiply or divide?
*Student:* Sort 10 fraction word problems as multiplication or division and solve them with diagrams.

*How to teach:* For each problem ask 'Am I finding a part of an amount (multiply) or how many groups / size of each share (divide)?' The child sorts 10 and solves with diagrams.

- **Q:** Ana has 4 yards of yarn and uses 3/4 of it. Multiply or divide?
  **A:** Multiply: 3/4 × 4 = 3 yards.
- **Q:** 4 yards cut into 1/2-yard pieces — how many?
  **A:** Divide: 4 ÷ 1/2 = 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Multi-step problems
*Student:* Solve 4 multi-step problems combining fraction multiplication, division and addition.

*How to teach:* Solve multi-step problems step by step, writing each step's equation. Your child underlines each question in the problem, numbers the steps, and labels each intermediate answer with units.

- **Q:** A 3-cup bag of trail mix: 2/3 of it is eaten, then the rest is split into 1/4-cup bags. How many bags?
  **A:** Eaten 2 cups; 1 cup left; 1 ÷ 1/4 = 4 bags.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a cumulative review of fractions as division, multiplication, scaling and division.

*How to teach:* Cumulative review of fractions as division, multiplication, scaling and division. Mix one problem of each type; for each, have your child say first whether the answer should be bigger or smaller than the starting number.

- **Q:** 7 ÷ 2 as a fraction?
  **A:** 7/2 = 3 1/2

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test on multiplying and dividing fractions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test independently. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** 1/2 ÷ 5 = ?
  **A:** 1/10

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test covering 5.NF.B.3–B.7.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test key (suggested): 1) 5 ÷ 6 = 5/6 2) 17 ÷ 4 = 4 1/4 3) 3/4 × 20 = 15 4) 2/3 × 5/8 = 5/12 5) 1 1/2 × 2 2/3 = 4 6) 4/5 × 9 is less than 9 7) area of 1/2 m × 3/4 m = 3/8 sq m 8) 1/4 ÷ 3 = 1/12 9) 6 ÷ 1/4 = 24 10) 3 friends share 1/2 lb of grapes = 1/6 lb each 11) 8 cups of soup in 1/2-cup servings: 8 ÷ 1/2 = 16 servings 12) 12 ft board, 1/3 is cut off; rest split in 4 equal pieces → 8 ÷ 4 = 2 ft each.

## Science 5: Sphere interactions in a California event

**Prepare:** Articles on a California wildfire followed by mudslides (e.g., the 2017 Thomas Fire and 2018 Montecito debris flows), poster board. Note: real disasters involved loss of life — preview for sensitive children. Write the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking events have one cause.
- Forgetting humans are in the biosphere.
- Thinking burned land absorbs more water. Burned soil can repel water, increasing runoff.

### Day 1 (Monday): Case study
*Student:* Read about a California wildfire followed by winter mudslides and list each sphere involved.

*How to teach:* Read the case study; list spheres involved. Read the article aloud; preview for sensitive details. Your child lists each sphere with an example from the event.

- **Q:** Which spheres were involved?
  **A:** Biosphere (vegetation burned), atmosphere (smoke, rain), geosphere (soil, mud), hydrosphere (rain, runoff).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cause-effect chain
*Student:* Build a cause-and-effect chain showing how the fire changed the biosphere and how rain then affected the geosphere.

*How to teach:* Build a cause-effect chain. Model the first link. Your child builds a chain on paper strips linking fire to mudslides.

- **Q:** Why did mudslides follow the fire?
  **A:** Fire removed plants whose roots held soil; heavy rain washed loose soil and debris downhill.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Model poster
*Student:* Create a poster model of the event with arrows labeling each sphere interaction.

*How to teach:* Create a poster model with labeled arrows. Your child sketches the hillside before and after, with arrows labeled for each interaction.

- **Q:** How did the atmosphere affect the fire?
  **A:** Dry, hot winds (Santa Anas) spread the fire.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Presentation
*Student:* Present the model to family and answer questions about how humans are part of the biosphere.

*How to teach:* Present the model; answer questions on humans in the biosphere. Your child presents for 2–3 minutes. Ask how people changed the area and how they might reduce future risk.

- **Q:** How are humans part of this?
  **A:** Humans live in the area, can start fires, fight fires and rebuild.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 assessment on Earth's spheres and their interactions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test. Give the test independently and review missed items with the poster.

- **Q:** Name an interaction between biosphere and geosphere.
  **A:** Roots hold soil in place.

**Assessment:** Model poster rubric and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric: 4 = all four spheres with labeled interactions and a clear cause-effect chain; 3 = three spheres; 2 = two; 1 = incomplete. Unit 6 test key (suggested): four spheres defined; weathering vs erosion; deposition; plants reduce erosion; clouds from condensation; ocean moderates climate.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Common Sense and the Declaration of Independence

**Prepare:** Declaration transcript (National Archives); Common Sense excerpt. Write the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Declaration created a government.
- Thinking Jefferson wrote alone.
- Thinking 'all men' included everyone then.

### Day 1 (Monday): Common Sense
*Student:* Read a short excerpt of Common Sense and explain how Paine persuaded ordinary colonists.

*How to teach:* Read a Common Sense excerpt. Your child explains how Paine persuaded ordinary people.

- **Q:** How did Paine persuade?
  **A:** Plain language and common sense.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drafting the Declaration
*Student:* Learn about the Committee of Five (Jefferson, Adams, Franklin, Sherman, Livingston) and how the Declaration was written.

*How to teach:* Learn about the Committee of Five and Jefferson's draft. Model the first item yourself, then let your child finish while you check accuracy against the source.

- **Q:** Who wrote the first draft?
  **A:** Thomas Jefferson.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Preamble
*Student:* Paraphrase the Declaration's second paragraph and explain unalienable rights and consent of the governed.

*How to teach:* Your child paraphrases the second paragraph and explains unalienable rights and consent of the governed. Ask 'How do you know?' and have your child point to the source or map for each answer.

- **Q:** Unalienable rights?
  **A:** Life, liberty, pursuit of happiness.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grievances
*Student:* Match 5 grievances against King George III to events studied earlier.

*How to teach:* Your child matches 5 grievances to earlier events. Before finishing, have your child explain their work aloud in 2–3 sentences; correct any errors then.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Taxes without consent → Stamp Act.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on the causes of the Revolution and the Declaration.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** When adopted?
  **A:** July 4, 1776.

**Assessment:** Declaration paraphrase and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Paraphrase: rights, consent. Unit 6 test key: taxes, Massacre, Tea Party, Coercive Acts, Continental Congresses, Lexington and Concord, Declaration.

## Art & Music 5: Harmony: rounds, partner songs and chords

**Prepare:** Print lyrics for 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat,' 'Frère Jacques,' 'This Old Man,' 'Skip to My Lou,' and 'Clementine.' Open Chrome Music Lab Chords. C major I chord = C-E-G; V chord = G-B-D.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking harmony means singing the same notes. Harmony is two or more different pitches sounding together.
- Losing your part in a round. Cover one ear and focus on your own melody.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rounds
*Student:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' and 'Frère Jacques' as rounds with family members.

*How to teach:* Sing rounds; voice 2 enters when voice 1 reaches the second phrase. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** When does the second voice enter in 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat'?
  **A:** When the first voice begins 'Gently down the stream.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Partner songs
*Student:* Sing two partner songs together ('This Old Man' and 'Skip to My Lou') to create harmony.

*How to teach:* Sing two partner songs at the same time. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What is a partner song?
  **A:** Two different songs that fit together harmonically when sung at the same time.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chords
*Student:* Use Chrome Music Lab Chords to build I and V chords in C and listen for when they change in a song.

*How to teach:* Build I and V in C; listen for changes in a song. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What notes make the I chord in C major?
  **A:** C, E, G.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Two-chord song
*Student:* Accompany a familiar two-chord song (e.g., 'Clementine') by playing I and V on a virtual keyboard or ukulele.

*How to teach:* Accompany 'Clementine' with I and V on keyboard or ukulele. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What notes make the V chord in C?
  **A:** G, B, D.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write the chord changes (I or V) above lyrics of a song and perform it.

*How to teach:* Write I or V above lyrics; perform. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Why does a song often end on the I chord?
  **A:** It sounds finished and resolved—home.

**Assessment:** Round performance and chord-change analysis.

**Answer key:** Round performance: holds own part through the round (4 = no errors; 3 = minor; 2 = loses part; 1 = cannot). Chord analysis: I and V marked correctly above lyrics at chord changes (proficient = 80%).

## PE 5: Creating a dance

**Prepare:** Choose music with a clear beat (about 2 minutes). Have paper to write counts and a recording device. Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking canon and unison are the same. Unison = everyone does the same move at the same time; canon = the same move starts at different times, like a round.
- Only using one level. Mix low (floor), middle and high levels for interest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choreography tools
*Student:* Learn to vary levels, formations, unison and canon and try each in short phrases.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate levels (low, middle, high), formations (line, circle, diagonal), unison and canon with an 8-count phrase. Your child tries each.

- **Q:** What is the difference between canon and unison?
  **A:** Unison: everyone moves the same at the same time. Canon: dancers start the same moves at different times, one after another.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planning
*Student:* Plan a 64-count dance with a partner or family member and write the counts.

*How to teach:* Plan 64 counts (eight 8-counts) on paper, noting moves, levels and formations. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** How many 8-count phrases are in 64 counts?
  **A:** Eight.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearsing
*Student:* Rehearse the dance, cleaning up timing and transitions.

*How to teach:* Rehearse; fix timing and smooth transitions. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is a transition in a dance?
  **A:** The movement that connects one section or move to the next.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recording
*Student:* Record the dance and revise one section.

*How to teach:* Record, watch, and revise one section. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What did you change and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; strong answers name a specific section and a reason (e.g., 'counts 17–24 were rushed, so I simplified the turn').

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Perform the original dance and explain how it uses rhythm and patterns.

*How to teach:* Perform; your child explains how the dance uses rhythm and patterns. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** How does your dance show a pattern?
  **A:** Moves repeat or follow a structure in 8-count phrases (e.g., AB AB), timed to the beat.

**Assessment:** Original dance rubric.

**Answer key:** Original dance rubric (1–4 each): 64 counts on beat; uses at least two levels, one formation change, and both unison and canon; smooth transitions; explanation of rhythm/pattern. Proficient = 3+ in each.

## Health 5: Puberty review and respect for differences

**Prepare:** Gather poster paper and markers. Write three fictional advice-column letters (e.g., 'I'm the shortest in my class,' 'I sweat a lot,' 'My friend teases me about my voice'). PARENT PREVIEW: watch the videos and read materials before your child does; decide how you'll answer personal or values questions, and keep the anonymous question box open.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking teasing about development is harmless. It hurts and is a form of bullying.
- Thinking a self-care kit is only for girls. Everyone benefits from deodorant, a spare shirt and tissues.

### Day 1 (Monday): Concept map
*Student:* Build a concept map linking hormones, physical changes, emotions, hygiene and trusted adults.

*How to teach:* Build a concept map linking hormones → physical changes → emotions → hygiene → trusted adults.

- **Q:** How are hormones connected to hygiene needs?
  **A:** Hormones make sweat and oil glands more active, so you need to shower, use deodorant and wash your face more often.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stop teasing
*Student:* Role-play responding to teasing about early or late development.

*How to teach:* Role-play responding to teasing about early or late development. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What's a good response if someone teases you about developing late?
  **A:** Stay calm, say 'Everyone grows at their own pace,' walk away, and tell an adult if it continues.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Self-care kit
*Student:* Assemble or list a personal self-care kit for school or travel.

*How to teach:* List or assemble a self-care kit: deodorant, tissues, wipes, spare shirt/underwear, pads if needed, lip balm, comb.

- **Q:** Name four items for a self-care kit.
  **A:** Deodorant, tissues, spare underwear/shirt, wipes, pads, comb, water bottle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Advice column
*Student:* Write answers to three advice-column questions from fictional 5th graders about puberty.

*How to teach:* Answer three advice-column letters with kind, accurate advice. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Answer: 'I'm the shortest in my class—is something wrong?'
  **A:** Probably not—puberty and growth spurts start at different ages; many people catch up. Talk to your doctor if worried.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the Growth and Development unit test and discuss results with a parent.

*How to teach:* Take the unit test and discuss results. Have your child work independently; afterward, go over any errors together and let them explain the correct answer back to you.

- **Q:** Which gland signals puberty to begin?
  **A:** The pituitary gland.

**Assessment:** Growth and Development unit test.

**Answer key:** Unit test key: 1) puberty starts ~8–14 2) pituitary gland 3) estrogen and testosterone 4) ovaries make eggs, testes make sperm 5) menstruation = shedding uterine lining 6) nocturnal emissions are normal 7) hygiene: shower, deodorant 8) emotions change, coping strategies 9) trusted adults and valid sources 10) respect and no teasing. Proficient = 80%.
