# Teacher's Guide — 5th Grade, Week 18

## ELA 5: Esperanza Rising: 'Aguascalientes, Mexico 1924' – Las Uvas through Las Guayabas

**Prepare:** Borrow Esperanza Rising by Pam Muñoz Ryan; preview the first four chapters. Parental note: Papa is killed by bandits and the family's house is burned. Find maps of Mexico and California.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking third-person limited means the narrator knows everyone's thoughts. It follows mainly Esperanza's thoughts and feelings.
- Skipping Spanish words. Use context; many are explained in the next sentence.
- Listing traits without evidence. Each trait needs an example from the text.

### Day 1 (Monday): Las Uvas
*Student:* Read 'Las Uvas' and describe the setting of El Rancho de las Rosas with 5 details; locate Aguascalientes on a map.

*How to teach:* Read 'Las Uvas'; your child lists 5 details about El Rancho de las Rosas and locates Aguascalientes. Read aloud together; your child jots setting details as you go. Find Aguascalientes in central Mexico on the map.

- **Q:** Describe Esperanza's life at the start.
  **A:** She lives a wealthy, privileged life on a large ranch with servants, beautiful clothes and a loving father.
- **Q:** What tradition does Papa share at the harvest?
  **A:** Esperanza cuts the first bunch of grapes, and Papa teaches her to listen to the heartbeat of the land.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Las Papayas
*Student:* Read 'Las Papayas' and discuss how Papa's death changes everything; mark Spanish words and use context to define them.

*How to teach:* Read 'Las Papayas'; discuss how Papa's death changes everything; define Spanish words in context. Your child makes a list of Spanish words with the context clue and a guessed meaning, checking later in a dictionary.

- **Q:** How does Papa's death change things?
  **A:** Papa's stepbrothers, Tío Luis and Tío Marco, take control of the ranch's future; Tío Luis wants to marry Mama, and when she refuses, the house is burned.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Point of view
*Student:* Explain how the third-person limited narrator shows events through Esperanza's eyes, using 2 quotations.

*How to teach:* Your child explains third-person limited point of view with 2 quotations. Explain: the narrator says 'she' but shows only Esperanza's thoughts. Your child finds 2 quotations showing her feelings.

- **Q:** Why tell the story from Esperanza's view?
  **A:** Readers experience her loss and see her pride and later her growth as she learns.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Los Higos and Las Guayabas
*Student:* Read 'Los Higos' and 'Las Guayabas' and create a character trait web for Esperanza with evidence.

*How to teach:* Read 'Los Higos' and 'Las Guayabas'; build a character trait web with evidence. Your child adds at least 4 traits, each with a quotation and page number. Discuss which traits might change.

- **Q:** How do they escape?
  **A:** Hidden in a wagon under guavas with Alfonso, Hortensia and Miguel; Abuelita stays behind to recover.
- **Q:** One trait of Esperanza?
  **A:** Proud or spoiled — she thinks she is above the servants.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on the first four chapters and write a journal entry as Esperanza leaving Mexico.

*How to teach:* Give a chapter quiz; your child writes a journal entry as Esperanza leaving Mexico. Grade the quiz; the journal entry should mention real details (the wagon, Abuelita, the blanket).

- **Q:** What does Abuelita give Esperanza before she leaves?
  **A:** The blanket she has been crocheting, to finish.

**Assessment:** Character trait web; journal entry; chapter quiz.

**Answer key:** Chapter quiz (suggested): 1) Papa was killed by bandits 2) Tío Luis wanted to marry Mama 3) the house was burned 4) they escape hidden in a wagon under guavas 5) Abuelita stays behind 6) the family goes to California. Trait web: 4 traits with evidence. Journal: first-person, accurate feelings and events.

## Math 5: Fractions as division

**Prepare:** Paper circles ('pizzas') or tortillas and scissors. Plan 5 sharing situations (3 among 4, 2 among 3, 5 among 2, 4 among 6, 7 among 3).

**Watch for:**
- Writing the fraction upside down (3 pizzas ÷ 4 people = 4/3). The thing being shared is the numerator.
- Thinking a fraction can't be a division answer. a ÷ b = a/b every time.
- Ignoring the remainder in context (50 lb ÷ 8 = 6 R2). Express it as a fraction: 6 2/8 = 6 1/4 lb.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sharing wholes
*Student:* Cut paper circles to share 3 pizzas among 4 people and record 3 / 4 = 3/4; repeat for 4 other sharing situations.

*How to teach:* Cut each of 3 pizzas into 4 pieces and deal them out: each person gets 3/4. Record 3 ÷ 4 = 3/4. The child repeats 4 sharing situations.

- **Q:** 3 pizzas shared by 4 people — how much each?
  **A:** 3/4 of a pizza.
- **Q:** 2 pizzas shared by 3 people?
  **A:** 2/3 of a pizza.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Division as fraction
*Student:* Write 15 division expressions as fractions and fractions as division (7 / 3 = 7/3 = 2 1/3).

*How to teach:* Write division as fraction and fraction as division; change improper fractions to mixed numbers. The child completes 15.

- **Q:** 7 ÷ 3 = ?
  **A:** 7/3 = 2 1/3
- **Q:** Write 5/8 as a division expression.
  **A:** 5 ÷ 8

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Between which wholes?
*Student:* Decide between which two whole numbers each of 10 quotients like 47 / 6 lies, and write each as a mixed number.

*How to teach:* Use multiplication facts to find which wholes a quotient falls between: 47 ÷ 6 → 6 × 7 = 42, 6 × 8 = 48. The child does 10.

- **Q:** 47 ÷ 6 is between which two whole numbers? As a mixed number?
  **A:** Between 7 and 8; 7 5/6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 sharing problems such as 50 pounds of rice shared among 8 families.

*How to teach:* Solve sharing problems with a picture or equation, stating answers as fractions or mixed numbers with units. Model the rice problem: 50 ÷ 8 = 6 R2, and the 2 leftover pounds are split 8 ways, giving 2/8 = 1/4. Your child draws a picture for at least two problems.

- **Q:** 50 pounds of rice shared among 8 families?
  **A:** 50/8 = 6 2/8 = 6 1/4 pounds each.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on fractions as division and explain one problem with a drawing.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and have the child explain one problem with a drawing. Score it with the assessment key, then have your child rework each missed problem aloud, naming the error, before you move on.

- **Q:** 5 friends share 2 sandwiches equally. How much each?
  **A:** 2/5 of a sandwich.

**Assessment:** Quiz on interpreting fractions as division.

**Answer key:** Suggested key: 1) 4 ÷ 5 = 4/5 2) 9 ÷ 4 = 2 1/4 3) 11/3 = 11 ÷ 3 = 3 2/3 4) 23 ÷ 5 is between 4 and 5 = 4 3/5 5) 6 people share 4 pies = 2/3 pie each 6) 15 yd of ribbon for 4 bows = 3 3/4 yd each 7) 3 L of juice in 8 cups = 3/8 L each. Drawing explanation: shows wholes split into equal parts and dealt out.

## Science 5: Modeling matter cycling in an ecosystem

**Prepare:** Large jar with lid, gravel, soil, small plants or moss, optional pill bugs, spray bottle. Place the jar in bright, indirect light.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a closed jar runs out of water. Water cycles through evaporation and condensation.
- Thinking plants only produce oxygen. They also use it.
- Thinking nutrients come from outside.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build a terrarium
*Student:* Build a closed terrarium with gravel, soil, plants and (optionally) pill bugs, and diagram its parts.

*How to teach:* Build the terrarium: gravel, soil, plants, optional pill bugs. Your child diagrams its parts.

- **Q:** Why put gravel at the bottom?
  **A:** For drainage so roots don't sit in water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Predicting cycles
*Student:* Predict how water, oxygen, carbon dioxide and nutrients will move in the sealed jar.

*How to teach:* Your child predicts how water, oxygen, carbon dioxide and nutrients will move. Ask about each substance: 'Where does it come from, where does it go?' Your child writes predictions with arrows.

- **Q:** How will carbon dioxide move?
  **A:** Animals and decomposers release it; plants take it in for photosynthesis.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Observing
*Student:* Observe condensation on the jar walls and plant health and record observations.

*How to teach:* Observe condensation and plant health. Look closely morning and afternoon. Your child records drops, fog and plant color in the notebook.

- **Q:** Why does water form on the jar walls?
  **A:** Water evaporates from soil and leaves, then condenses on the cooler glass.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mold and compost update
*Student:* Record final observations of mold bags and compost column and compare to day 1.

*How to teach:* Record final observations of mold bags and compost; compare to day 1. Your child compares final sketches with day 1 sketches and lists changes. Dispose of mold bags sealed in the trash.

- **Q:** How did the compost change?
  **A:** Fruit and leaves broke down and shrank, becoming darker and more soil-like.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw a matter-cycle model of the terrarium showing arrows for water, gases and nutrients.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a matter-cycle model with arrows for water, gases and nutrients. Your child draws the jar with color-coded arrows: blue for water, green for gases, brown for nutrients. Check with the rubric.

- **Q:** Where do nutrients come from in the sealed jar?
  **A:** Decomposers break down dead leaves and waste, returning nutrients to the soil.

**Assessment:** Terrarium matter-cycle model.

**Answer key:** Terrarium model rubric: 4 = arrows showing water (evaporation, condensation, uptake), gases (O2 and CO2 exchange), and nutrients (decomposition to soil to plants) labeled; 3 = two cycles; 2 = one; 1 = incomplete.

## United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation: Conflict: Powhatan Wars, Pequot War and King Philip's War

**Prepare:** Excerpt of Mary Rowlandson's narrative or Metacom's grievances; timeline template. Preview violent content.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Native nations were united.
- Thinking wars were one-sided.
- Confusing the wars.

### Day 1 (Monday): Powhatan Wars
*Student:* Read about the Powhatan Wars in Virginia and list causes, including land and tobacco expansion.

*How to teach:* Read about the Powhatan Wars. Your child lists causes, especially expanding tobacco farms on Powhatan land.

- **Q:** Cause?
  **A:** Colonists took land for tobacco.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pequot War
*Student:* Read about the Pequot War and the Mystic massacre and discuss how colonists and Native allies fought.

*How to teach:* Read about the Pequot War (1636–38) and the 1637 Mystic massacre. Discuss how colonists and Native allies fought together.

- **Q:** What happened at Mystic (1637)?
  **A:** English and allied Narragansett and Mohegan warriors burned a Pequot fort, killing hundreds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): King Philip's War
*Student:* Read Metacom's reported grievances (1675) and explain why he resisted colonial expansion.

*How to teach:* Read Metacom's grievances (1675). Your child explains why he resisted colonial expansion.

- **Q:** Why did Metacom resist?
  **A:** Colonists took land and imposed laws.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Native rivalries
*Student:* Read about rivalries among Native nations (Haudenosaunee and Huron) and how Europeans used them.

*How to teach:* Read about rivalries (Haudenosaunee vs. Huron). Discuss how Europeans used them to gain allies.

- **Q:** Example?
  **A:** Haudenosaunee vs. Huron over fur trade.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a cause-and-effect timeline of the colonial wars and take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Your child builds a timeline of the colonial wars with causes and effects, then takes the quiz. Use the assessment key to score it, then reteach any missed idea with the week's sources.

- **Q:** Order?
  **A:** Powhatan Wars (1610s–40s), Pequot War (1636–38), King Philip's War (1675–76).

**Assessment:** Timeline and quiz.

**Answer key:** Timeline: causes and effects. Quiz key: dates, leaders, causes.

## Art & Music 5: Colonial portraits and folk art

**Prepare:** Find John Singleton Copley's 'Paul Revere' (1768, Museum of Fine Arts Boston) and an Ammi Phillips folk portrait (e.g., 'Girl in Red Dress with Cat and Dog'). Set up a mirror, pencils and paper. Facial proportions: eyes halfway down the head; nose bottom halfway between eyes and chin; mouth one-third of the way from nose to chin; ears between eye and nose lines.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing eyes near the top of the head. Eyes are about halfway down the head.
- Thinking folk art is 'bad' art. It follows different goals (bold patterns, flat shapes) and is judged by different criteria.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading a portrait
*Student:* Analyze Copley's 'Paul Revere' portrait and list clues about his job and status.

*How to teach:* Analyze Copley's portrait; list clues about Revere's job and status. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** What clues show Paul Revere's job?
  **A:** He holds a silver teapot and engraving tools lie on the table—he was a silversmith; he's in work clothes (shirtsleeves, vest).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Folk art
*Student:* Compare a professional portrait with a folk-art portrait by Ammi Phillips and discuss different criteria for judging them.

*How to teach:* Compare professional and folk portraits; discuss different criteria. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How does Ammi Phillips's style differ from Copley's?
  **A:** Phillips used flatter shapes, bold colors and simplified forms; Copley used realistic detail, light and shadow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Facial proportions
*Student:* Learn basic facial proportions and draw a guided self-portrait from a mirror.

*How to teach:* Teach facial proportions; draw a self-portrait from a mirror. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Where are the eyes on a face?
  **A:** About halfway between the top of the head and the chin.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Symbolic objects
*Student:* Add objects to your self-portrait that show your interests, as colonial portraitists did.

*How to teach:* Add symbolic objects showing interests. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why did colonial portraitists include objects?
  **A:** To show the sitter's job, status, interests or values.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare your interpretation of the Copley portrait with a family member's and write about the differences.

*How to teach:* Compare interpretation of Copley's portrait with a family member's; write about differences.

- **Q:** Why might two people interpret the same portrait differently?
  **A:** They notice different details and bring different experiences and knowledge.

**Assessment:** Self-portrait with symbolic objects and interpretation comparison.

**Answer key:** Self-portrait rubric: 4 = accurate proportions (eyes halfway, nose, mouth, ears placed correctly), value shading, 2+ symbolic objects explained; 3 = minor errors; 2 = proportions off or no symbols; 1 = incomplete. Comparison: proficient if it explains specific differences and reasons.

## PE 5: Basketball passing and shooting

**Prepare:** Gather a basketball, a hoop (or a bucket/laundry basket target), and a wall. Learn BEEF: Balance, Eyes on target, Elbow under the ball, Follow-through ('reach into the cookie jar'). Before each session: check the space for hazards, have water ready, wear athletic shoes, and stop if your child shows signs of heat illness (dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps).

**Watch for:**
- Shooting with two hands pushing. The shooting hand does the work; the other hand only guides.
- Jumping off the wrong foot in a layup. A right-hand layup goes right foot, left foot, jump off the left and lift the right knee.

### Day 1 (Monday): Passing
*Student:* Practice chest, bounce and overhead passes against a wall, 20 each.

*How to teach:* Practice 20 chest passes (thumbs down on release), 20 bounce passes (bounce about 2/3 of the way to the target), 20 overhead passes against the wall.

- **Q:** Where should a bounce pass hit the ground?
  **A:** About two-thirds of the way toward the receiver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Form shooting
*Student:* Shoot close to the hoop using BEEF cues, 30 shots.

*How to teach:* Form shooting close to the basket using BEEF, 30 shots, one hand on the ball first. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does BEEF stand for?
  **A:** Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Layups
*Student:* Practice the right-hand layup footwork (right-left-up) slowly, then at speed.

*How to teach:* Walk through right-hand layup footwork: step right, step left, jump off the left and lift the right knee, shoot off the backboard. Slowly first, then at speed.

- **Q:** On a right-handed layup, which foot do you jump off?
  **A:** The left foot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shooting game
*Student:* Play 'Around the World' at 5 spots.

*How to teach:* Play Around the World at 5 spots; you must make a shot to move to the next spot. Set clear boundaries and a safety reminder before starting, keep rounds short so everyone stays active, and finish by asking what strategy worked best.

- **Q:** What part of BEEF helps aim the ball?
  **A:** Eyes on the target and elbow pointing at the basket (under the ball).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Evaluate your shooting form on video against BEEF cues and set one fix.

*How to teach:* Video a few shots and check each BEEF cue; your child writes one fix to practice. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Your shot keeps falling short. Which cue might be missing?
  **A:** Balance/using the legs or a full follow-through—bend the knees and extend fully.

**Assessment:** Shooting form self-assessment.

**Answer key:** Self-assessment: for each BEEF cue mark yes/sometimes/no from video, plus one specific fix. Proficient = honest marks that match the video and a fix tied to a cue (e.g., 'snap my wrist and hold the follow-through').

## Health 5: Hormones and the endocrine system

**Prepare:** Have the body map from week 2. Prepare a simple 'message-passing' game (folded notes passed from room to room). Preview the KidsHealth/AMAZE explanation of hormones in puberty. PARENT PREVIEW: watch the videos and read materials before your child does; decide how you'll answer personal or values questions, and keep the anonymous question box open.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking hormones are only about moods. Hormones control growth, body changes and many body processes; moods are just one effect.
- Thinking boys only have testosterone and girls only estrogen. Everyone has both; the amounts differ.

### Day 1 (Monday): Messengers
*Student:* Learn how hormones act as chemical messengers and model it with a message-passing game.

*How to teach:* Explain hormones as chemical messengers released by glands into the blood. Play a message game: you (the 'gland') send a note that travels by 'bloodstream' (hallway) to the target room.

- **Q:** How do hormones travel through the body?
  **A:** Through the bloodstream to target organs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pituitary gland
*Student:* Add the pituitary gland to your body map and explain how it signals the ovaries or testes.

*How to teach:* Add the pituitary gland to the body map. Explain it releases hormones that signal the ovaries or testes to make sex hormones.

- **Q:** Why is the pituitary called the 'master gland'?
  **A:** It sends signals that control many other glands, including the ovaries and testes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hormone effects
*Student:* Make a cause-and-effect chart linking estrogen and testosterone to body changes.

*How to teach:* Make a cause-and-effect chart: estrogen → breast development, wider hips, menstruation; testosterone → deeper voice, facial hair, muscle growth.

- **Q:** Which hormone causes the voice to deepen?
  **A:** Testosterone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mood and hormones
*Student:* Read about how hormones can affect moods and brainstorm healthy ways to handle mood swings.

*How to teach:* Read about mood changes; brainstorm coping strategies (exercise, talking, sleep, journaling).

- **Q:** Give two healthy ways to deal with mood swings.
  **A:** Exercise, talking to a trusted adult, journaling, deep breathing, getting enough sleep.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw a flowchart of the endocrine system's role in puberty and take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Draw a flowchart: brain/pituitary → hormones → ovaries/testes → estrogen/testosterone → body changes. Take the quiz.

- **Q:** What are the glands that make sex hormones?
  **A:** Ovaries (estrogen) and testes (testosterone).

**Assessment:** Endocrine flowchart and quiz.

**Answer key:** Flowchart: pituitary (in brain) → signals → ovaries or testes → estrogen or testosterone → physical changes. Quiz: 1) hormones = chemical messengers 2) travel in blood 3) pituitary = master gland 4) ovaries → estrogen 5) testes → testosterone 6) coping strategy. Proficient = 5 of 6.
