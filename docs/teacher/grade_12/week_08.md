# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 8

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Macbeth Act I – prophecy and temptation

**Prepare:** Download Macbeth from the Folger Shakespeare Library (free). Read Act I. Find a short source on James I, witchcraft, and the Gunpowder Plot. Parental note: the play contains murder and violence.

**Watch for:**
- Reading line by line as prose and getting lost. Read to punctuation, not to line endings.
- Thinking the witches control Macbeth. They prophesy; Macbeth and Lady Macbeth choose to act.
- Iambic pentameter = 10 syllables, 5 unstressed–stressed feet; students may count syllables but ignore stress.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shakespeare's stage
*Student:* Read background on the Globe, James I, the Gunpowder Plot, and witchcraft beliefs. Learn to scan a line of iambic pentameter and mark stresses in three lines.

*How to teach:* Present background on the Globe and James I. Model marking stresses in 'So foul and fair a day I have not seen.' The student scans three lines.

- **Q:** Scan 'So foul and fair a day I have not seen.'
  **A:** so FOUL / and FAIR / a DAY / i HAVE / not SEEN — five iambs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Act I, scenes 1–3
*Student:* Read I.1–I.3 aloud with a parent taking parts. Analyze 'Fair is foul, and foul is fair' as the play's motif of equivocation.

*How to teach:* Read I.1–I.3 aloud with parts. Discuss 'Fair is foul' as equivocation. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What do the witches prophesy to Macbeth and Banquo?
  **A:** Macbeth will be Thane of Cawdor and king; Banquo will father kings though not be one.
- **Q:** What does 'Fair is foul, and foul is fair' suggest?
  **A:** Appearances deceive; good and evil are confused — the play's theme of equivocation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Act I, scenes 4–5
*Student:* Read I.4–I.5. Paraphrase Lady Macbeth's 'unsex me here' soliloquy line by line and identify her strategy.

*How to teach:* Read I.4–I.5. Paraphrase Lady Macbeth's soliloquy line by line. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What does Lady Macbeth fear about her husband?
  **A:** He is 'too full o' the milk of human kindness' to take the quickest way to the crown.
- **Q:** What does she ask the spirits?
  **A:** To 'unsex' her and fill her with cruelty so no remorse stops her.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Act I, scenes 6–7
*Student:* Read I.6–I.7. Annotate Macbeth's 'If it were done' soliloquy for imagery and reasoning; chart his arguments for and against the murder.

*How to teach:* Read I.6–I.7. The student charts the arguments in 'If it were done.' Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What are Macbeth's reasons not to kill Duncan?
  **A:** He is Duncan's kinsman, subject, and host; Duncan is a virtuous king; and only 'vaulting ambition' drives him.
- **Q:** How does Lady Macbeth change his mind?
  **A:** She questions his manhood and courage and gives him a plan to drug the guards and blame them.

### Day 5 (Friday): Act I check
*Student:* Memorize and perform 10 lines from a soliloquy; take an Act I quiz on plot, character, and figurative language.

*How to teach:* The student recites 10 lines; give the Act I quiz. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Who is named Prince of Cumberland?
  **A:** Malcolm.

**Assessment:** Recitation and Act I quiz.

**Answer key:** Act I quiz: witches' prophecies; Macbeth made Thane of Cawdor; Malcolm named heir; Lady Macbeth's 'unsex me' soliloquy; 'vaulting ambition'; manhood argument. Recitation rubric: 4 = 10 lines word-perfect with meaningful pauses and emphasis; 3 = 1–2 slips; 2 = several prompts needed; 1 = not memorized.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Exponential functions and logarithms

**Prepare:** Calculator with ln and log keys. Preview OpenStax 6.1–6.6. Pre-make a table for $1000 at 5% for 10 years with columns for annual, monthly, daily, continuous compounding.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking log(a + b) = log a + log b. The product rule is log(ab) = log a + log b; there is no rule for a sum.
- Confusing growth and decay: y = a·bˣ decays when 0 < b < 1 (or k < 0 in e^(kx)).
- Mis-reading log_b(x) = y. Say it aloud: 'b to the y equals x.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Exponential graphs
*Student:* Graph growth and decay functions including base e; identify asymptotes and transformations.

*How to teach:* Graph y = 2ˣ, (1/2)ˣ, eˣ and transformations like eˣ − 3. The student identifies growth/decay, y-intercept, and horizontal asymptote for each.

- **Q:** Give the y-intercept and asymptote of y = 3(0.5)ˣ + 2.
  **A:** y-intercept 3 + 2 = 5; horizontal asymptote y = 2; decay.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compound interest and e
*Student:* Compute compound interest for annual through continuous compounding and watch the values approach e.

*How to teach:* Fill in the compounding table using A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) and A = Pe^(rt). Then compute (1 + 1/n)^n for n = 1, 10, 100, 10000 to see it approach e ≈ 2.71828.

- **Q:** $1000 at 5% for 10 years: annual vs. monthly vs. continuous?
  **A:** Annual $1628.89; monthly $1647.01; continuous 1000e^0.5 ≈ $1648.72.
- **Q:** What does (1 + 1/n)^n approach as n grows?
  **A:** e ≈ 2.71828.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Logarithms
*Student:* Convert between exponential and log form; graph y = log_b(x) as the inverse of y = b^x.

*How to teach:* Teach 'log_b x = y means b^y = x' and convert both directions. Graph y = 2ˣ and y = log₂ x together with y = x to show they are inverses.

- **Q:** Evaluate log₂ 8, log 1000, ln e⁵, log₃(1/9).
  **A:** 3, 3, 5, −2.
- **Q:** Rewrite 5² = 25 in log form.
  **A:** log₅ 25 = 2

### Day 4 (Thursday): Log properties
*Student:* Expand and condense 15 expressions using product, quotient, and power rules; use change of base.

*How to teach:* Model expanding and condensing, connecting each rule to an exponent rule. Teach change of base: log_b x = ln x / ln b. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Expand log(x³y/z).
  **A:** 3 log x + log y − log z
- **Q:** Condense 2 ln x − ln(x + 1).
  **A:** ln(x²/(x + 1))
- **Q:** Evaluate log₅ 20 to 3 decimals.
  **A:** ln 20 / ln 5 ≈ 1.861

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a quiz on exponential/log graphs and properties.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz below (calculator allowed for the last two items). Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Is ln 0 defined?
  **A:** No. eʸ is always positive, so no y gives eʸ = 0; the domain of ln x is x > 0.

**Assessment:** Exponent/log quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) y = 3(0.5)ˣ + 2: y-int 5, HA y = 2, decay. 2) log₂ 8 = 3. 3) log₃(1/9) = −2. 4) Expand log(x³y/z) = 3log x + log y − log z. 5) Condense 2 ln x − ln(x + 1) = ln(x²/(x + 1)). 6) log₅ 20 ≈ 1.861. 7) $1000 at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years ≈ $1647.01. 8) Continuous ≈ $1648.72. Proficient: 6 of 8.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Earth's interior and mantle convection

**Prepare:** Slinky, glass pan, water, food coloring, candle or low stovetop (adult supervision), seismic shadow zone diagram, Hawaiian island age data (Kauai ~5.1 Ma, Oahu ~3 Ma, Maui ~1.3 Ma, Big Island 0–0.4 Ma; Kauai ~520 km from Kilauea).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the mantle is liquid magma. It is mostly solid rock that flows very slowly (plastic) over geologic time.
- Believing the core is cold or that the inner core is liquid. The outer core is liquid; the inner core is solid due to immense pressure.
- Assuming the hot spot moves. The plate moves over a (relatively) fixed mantle plume.

### Day 1 (Monday): Seismic waves
*Student:* Use a Slinky to model P-waves and S-waves; explain why S-waves cannot pass through liquid.

*How to teach:* Slinky: push/pull for P-waves (compressional, particles move parallel to wave), shake sideways for S-waves (shear, perpendicular). Explain liquids can't transmit shear.

- **Q:** Why can't S-waves travel through liquids?
  **A:** S-waves require the material to resist shearing and spring back; liquids have no shear strength.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shadow zones
*Student:* Analyze a diagram of seismic shadow zones and infer the liquid outer core; draw Earth's layers to scale.

*How to teach:* Analyze shadow zones: no direct S-waves beyond ~104° from the epicenter; no direct P-waves between ~104° and 140° (refraction). Infer a liquid outer core. Draw layers to scale (crust 5–70 km, mantle to 2,900 km, outer core to 5,150 km, inner core to 6,371 km).

- **Q:** What evidence shows Earth's outer core is liquid?
  **A:** S-waves don't pass through it, creating a large S-wave shadow zone on the side of Earth opposite an earthquake.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Convection lab
*Student:* Heat water in a glass pan over a low flame and add drops of food coloring to observe convection cells; sketch and relate to the mantle.

*How to teach:* Heat water in a glass pan over a low flame; add food coloring near the heat and observe rising, spreading, sinking loops. Relate to mantle convection.

- **Q:** Why does heated fluid rise?
  **A:** It expands and becomes less dense than surrounding cooler fluid, so buoyancy lifts it; cooler, denser fluid sinks.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hot spots
*Student:* Use Hawaiian island ages to calculate Pacific Plate speed and direction over the Hawaii hot spot.

*How to teach:* Calculate Pacific Plate speed: Kauai is ~520 km from Kilauea and ~5.1 million years old.

- **Q:** Calculate the Pacific Plate's speed over the Hawaii hot spot.
  **A:** 520 km ÷ 5.1 Myr ≈ 102 km/Myr = 10.2 cm/yr, moving northwest (islands get older to the northwest).

### Day 5 (Friday): Model
*Student:* Draw a labeled model of Earth's interior showing how convection cycles matter and drives plates; take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Student draws a labeled interior model showing convection, ridge push, and slab pull; give a quiz.

- **Q:** What is slab pull?
  **A:** The pull of a cold, dense subducting plate sinking into the mantle, dragging the rest of the plate — considered a major plate-driving force.

**Assessment:** Interior/convection model and quiz.

**Answer key:** Interior/convection model rubric. 4 – Layers to scale with depths and states (solid inner core, liquid outer core, plastic mantle, rigid lithosphere), seismic evidence, convection cells linked to ridges and subduction, ridge push and slab pull, mantle plume. 3 (proficient) – Accurate layers, states, convection-plate connection. 2 – Errors in states or mechanism. 1 – Incomplete. Quiz key: 1) P-waves compressional, S-waves shear. 2) S-wave shadow → liquid outer core. 3) Mantle is solid but flows slowly. 4) Hawaii rate ~10 cm/yr NW. 5) Convection driven by density differences from heat.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): The judicial branch

**Prepare:** Print Article III. Find a short confirmation-hearing clip (C-SPAN). Pick a recent Supreme Court opinion syllabus (supremecourt.gov). Prepare Unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Supreme Court hears all appeals. It chooses a small share through certiorari.
- Confusing the holding with the reasoning.
- Assuming justices serve fixed terms. They serve during 'good behaviour' (life tenure).

### Day 1 (Monday): Article III
*Student:* Read Article III; diagram district courts, circuit courts, and the Supreme Court; locate California in the Ninth Circuit.

*How to teach:* Diagram the federal courts; locate California in the Ninth Circuit. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How many levels are in the federal court system?
  **A:** Three: district courts, courts of appeals (13 circuits), Supreme Court.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): How cases reach the Court
*Student:* Learn the certiorari process and the 'rule of four'; analyze how few petitions are granted.

*How to teach:* Explain certiorari and the rule of four. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is the rule of four?
  **A:** Four justices must vote to grant certiorari for the Court to hear a case.
- **Q:** About what share of petitions are granted?
  **A:** Around 1% — roughly 60–80 cases from several thousand petitions per year.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Selecting justices
*Student:* Watch a segment of a recent confirmation hearing and evaluate the questions senators asked.

*How to teach:* Watch a hearing segment; evaluate senators' questions. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is the Senate's role?
  **A:** Advice and consent — confirms nominees by majority vote.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reading an opinion
*Student:* Read the syllabus of a recent Supreme Court opinion and identify the question, holding, and reasoning.

*How to teach:* Read a syllabus; identify question, holding, reasoning. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is a 'holding'?
  **A:** The Court's answer to the legal question.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on the three branches.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name one check on the judiciary.
  **A:** Senate confirmation, impeachment, constitutional amendments, or Congress setting jurisdiction.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key: 1) House 25/7/2; Senate 30/9/6. 2) Veto override two-thirds of both houses. 3) Amendment: two-thirds proposal, three-fourths ratification. 4) Commander in chief, treaties, appointments, veto, pardon. 5) Executive orders need constitutional or statutory basis. 6) 15 departments. 7) District courts → 13 courts of appeals → Supreme Court; California in the Ninth Circuit. 8) Rule of four; roughly 1% of petitions granted. 9) Senate confirms justices by majority. 10) One check on each branch. Scoring: 90%+ mastery, 75–89% proficient, 60–74% developing.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Color theory in paint

**Prepare:** Acrylic or gouache: a warm and cool version of each primary if possible (or one red, yellow, blue) plus white; for Day 4 a Zorn palette (yellow ochre, cadmium red light or vermilion, ivory black, titanium white). Brushes, palette, canvas paper, water containers.

**Watch for:**
- Making colors darker with black only, which can dull them. Also use complements or darker hues.
- Confusing value and saturation. Value is light/dark; saturation is intensity/purity.
- Thinking a limited palette is limiting. It forces color harmony.

### Day 1 (Monday): Color wheel
*Student:* Mix a 12-step color wheel from three primaries plus white.

*How to teach:* Mix a 12-step wheel: 3 primaries, 3 secondaries, 6 tertiaries.

- **Q:** What are the complementary pairs on the traditional (RYB) color wheel?
  **A:** Red–green, blue–orange, yellow–violet.
- **Q:** Name the tertiary colors.
  **A:** Red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet, red-violet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Value in color
*Student:* Paint a grayscale and then a full-color version of the same simple object.

*How to teach:* Paint a grayscale version then a full-color version of one object; compare values.

- **Q:** Why paint in grayscale first?
  **A:** It separates value from hue so you can see whether the form reads; good value structure makes color work.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Temperature
*Student:* Paint the same landscape twice: once warm-dominant, once cool-dominant.

*How to teach:* Paint the same landscape warm-dominant and cool-dominant.

- **Q:** How does color temperature affect space?
  **A:** Warm colors tend to advance; cool colors recede — useful for depth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Limited palette
*Student:* Paint a still life with a Zorn palette (yellow ochre, red, black, white).

*How to teach:* Still life with a Zorn palette.

- **Q:** What colors are in a Zorn palette, and how can you get a 'blue'?
  **A:** Yellow ochre, red (cadmium/vermilion), ivory black, white; black mixed with white reads as a cool blue-gray next to warm colors.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Self-critique color studies and note what you'll apply going forward.

*How to teach:* Self-critique the studies.

- **Q:** How do you lower saturation without changing value much?
  **A:** Mix in a small amount of the complement or a gray of the same value.

**Assessment:** Color study sheets.

**Answer key:** Color study sheets rubric. 4 – Accurate 12-step wheel with clean mixtures, grayscale/color pair with matching values, convincing warm vs. cool versions, harmonious Zorn still life, written self-critique applying terms (hue, value, saturation, temperature). 3 (proficient) – All studies complete and accurate. 2 – Muddy mixtures or missing studies. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Strength retest and program evaluation

**Prepare:** Weights/bands, fitness log with week 1 and week 5 data. Prepare the training-principles quiz. Ask a family member to be the 'student' on Day 4.

**Watch for:**
- Retesting on a fatigued day. Retest after a deload.
- Calculating percent change from the new value. Always divide by the original (baseline).
- Teaching with too many cues. Give one or two key cues at a time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Deload
*Student:* Do a light deload session at 60% of usual load.

*How to teach:* Deload: same exercises at about 60% of usual load, fewer sets.

- **Q:** Why deload before retesting?
  **A:** To reduce fatigue so the retest shows true fitness gains.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Retest strength
*Student:* Retest 10-rep maxes and push-up/curl-up scores.

*How to teach:* Retest 10RMs and push-up/curl-up scores.

- **Q:** Why must retest conditions match the original test?
  **A:** So differences reflect real change, not changes in protocol.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Analyze
*Student:* Calculate percent improvement and write what worked.

*How to teach:* Calculate percent improvement for each test and write what worked.

- **Q:** Goblet squat 10RM rose from 30 to 40 lb. Percent improvement?
  **A:** (40 − 30) ÷ 30 ≈ 33%.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teach a lift
*Student:* Teach a family member one lift with correct cues.

*How to teach:* Student teaches a family member one lift using a demo, 2–3 cues, and feedback.

- **Q:** What are the steps for teaching a lift?
  **A:** Demonstrate, explain 2–3 key cues, have the learner try with light load, give specific feedback, repeat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit check
*Student:* Take a written quiz on training principles and safety.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** State the principle of specificity.
  **A:** The body adapts specifically to the type of training done (SAID: specific adaptations to imposed demands).

**Assessment:** Program evaluation and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Progressive overload = gradually increasing demand. 2) Specificity (SAID). 3) Reversibility = gains lost without training. 4) Strength: ~1–6 reps, heavy, 2–5 min rest; endurance: 12–20+, light, 30–60 s rest. 5) Volume = sets × reps × load. 6) Neutral spine and controlled tempo. 7) Deload purpose. 8) DOMS peaks 24–72 h. 9) Push/pull balance. 10) Never hold breath through many reps; exhale on exertion. Program evaluation (proficient): percent changes computed correctly, analysis of what worked, next-step plan.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Credit cards, loans & the cost of borrowing

**Prepare:** Find three real credit card offers' Schumer boxes (student, secured, rewards). Open the CFPB credit card calculator or a spreadsheet. Build or download a simple amortization spreadsheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the minimum payment is a good plan. Minimum payments mostly cover interest and stretch debt for years.
- Thinking carrying a balance builds credit. Paying in full each month builds credit just as well and costs no interest.
- Ignoring APR on 'small fees' like payday loans. A $15 fee per $100 for two weeks is about a 390% APR.

### Day 1 (Monday): Card terms
*Student:* Compare the Schumer boxes of three real credit card offers (student, secured, rewards) for APR, fees, and penalties.

*How to teach:* Compare three Schumer boxes: purchase APR, penalty APR, annual fee, late fee, foreign transaction fee, grace period.

- **Q:** What is a secured credit card?
  **A:** A card backed by a cash deposit that usually equals the credit limit — a way to build credit with no or limited history.
- **Q:** What is a grace period?
  **A:** The time between the end of a billing cycle and the due date when no interest is charged on purchases if you pay the full balance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Minimum payment trap
*Student:* Calculate how long a $2,000 balance at 24% APR takes to repay with minimum payments vs. $150/month.

*How to teach:* Calculate repaying $2,000 at 24% APR with minimum payments vs. $150/month using the CFPB calculator or spreadsheet.

- **Q:** How long does $150/month take, and about how much interest?
  **A:** About 16 months and roughly $350 in interest.
- **Q:** With minimum payments of interest + 1% of the balance?
  **A:** About 12 years and nearly $2,900 in interest.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Auto and personal loans
*Student:* Use an amortization spreadsheet to compare a 36- vs. 72-month auto loan for total interest paid.

*How to teach:* Use the amortization spreadsheet to compare a 36- vs. 72-month auto loan for $20,000 at 7%.

- **Q:** Monthly payment and total interest for 36 vs. 72 months?
  **A:** 36 months: $617.54/month, about $2,232 interest. 72 months: $340.98/month, about $4,551 interest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Predatory products
*Student:* Research payday loans, rent-to-own, and buy-now-pay-later; compute the APR of a $15-per-$100 two-week payday loan.

*How to teach:* Research payday loans, rent-to-own and buy-now-pay-later; compute payday APR.

- **Q:** APR of a $15 fee per $100 borrowed for 2 weeks?
  **A:** 15% per 2 weeks × 26 periods = 390% APR.
- **Q:** What is a risk of buy-now-pay-later?
  **A:** Easy to overspend and juggle multiple plans; missed payments bring late fees, and some providers now report to credit bureaus.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 'first credit card' plan (which card, limits, autopay) and take a borrowing-costs quiz.

*How to teach:* The student writes a 'first credit card' plan and takes the quiz.

- **Q:** What's the best way to use a credit card to build credit at no cost?
  **A:** Use it for small planned purchases and pay the full statement balance automatically every month.

**Assessment:** Borrowing-cost calculations and first credit card plan.

**Answer key:** Borrowing-cost calculations key: 1) $2,000 at 24% APR, $150/month → about 16 months, about $350 interest (total ≈ $2,350). 2) Same balance with a minimum payment of interest + 1% of balance (minimum $25) → about 143 months (≈ 12 years), total paid ≈ $4,887 (≈ $2,887 interest). 3) $20,000 auto loan at 7%: 36 months → $617.54/month, total interest ≈ $2,232; 72 months → $340.98/month, total interest ≈ $4,551. 4) Payday loan $15 per $100 for 2 weeks → 15% × 26 = 390% APR. 5) A grace period means no interest on purchases if the full statement balance is paid by the due date. First credit card plan rubric: 4 = chooses a specific card type (student or secured, no annual fee), low limit, autopay for the full statement balance, utilization target under 30% (ideally under 10%), and a plan for monitoring; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
