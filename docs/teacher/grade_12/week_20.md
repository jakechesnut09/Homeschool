# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 20

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: A Doll's House

**Prepare:** Download A Doll's House (Project Gutenberg). Read the play. Prepare seminar norms and the question.

**Watch for:**
- Seeing Torvald's pet names as simple affection. List them and ask what kind of creature each names — a pet or a child — to show how he diminishes Nora.
- Calling Nora's forgery selfish. Point out she borrowed secretly to pay for the trip that saved Torvald's life, and has been repaying it herself.
- Judging the ending from personal feelings alone. Require a line from each act to support any claim in seminar.

### Day 1 (Monday): Act I
*Student:* Read Act I. List every pet name Torvald uses for Nora and what each suggests about their marriage.

*How to teach:* Read Act I. The student lists every pet name Torvald uses and what each suggests. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name pet names Torvald uses.
  **A:** 'Little lark,' 'squirrel,' 'little spendthrift,' 'little featherhead' — diminishing names for a pet or child.
- **Q:** What is Nora's secret?
  **A:** She borrowed money from Krogstad to fund a trip to Italy that saved Torvald's health, forging her father's signature.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Act II
*Student:* Read Act II. Analyze the tarantella as a symbol of Nora's desperation.

*How to teach:* Read Act II. Analyze the tarantella. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why does Nora dance the tarantella so wildly?
  **A:** To distract Torvald from the letterbox where Krogstad's letter waits; it expresses her desperation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Act III
*Student:* Read Act III. Annotate Nora's final conversation for shifts in power and analyze the door slam.

*How to teach:* Read Act III. Annotate shifts in power. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How does Torvald react to the letter, then to the returned bond?
  **A:** He calls her a liar and criminal; when the bond is returned he forgives her — showing his love was about his reputation.
- **Q:** What does Nora realize?
  **A:** She has been a 'doll-wife,' as she was her father's 'doll-child,' and must leave to understand herself.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Seminar
*Student:* Hold a seminar: Is Nora's leaving heroic, selfish, or both? Support claims with lines from all three acts.

*How to teach:* Hold the seminar: heroic, selfish, or both? Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give a line supporting Nora's choice.
  **A:** She says she has duties to herself that are as sacred as those to her husband and children.

### Day 5 (Friday): Response essay
*Student:* Write a 600-word response tracing how Ibsen develops Nora's transformation across the three acts.

*How to teach:* The student writes a 600-word essay on Nora's transformation. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Trace Nora's transformation in one sentence.
  **A:** Nora moves from a playful doll-wife to a woman who recognizes she must stand alone.

**Assessment:** Seminar rubric and character-development essay.

**Answer key:** Seminar rubric (4 levels): 4 = cites all three acts, builds on others, poses questions. Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = traces change across all three acts with quotations, explains causes (Krogstad's threat, Torvald's reaction), analyzes the door slam.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Sequences and series

**Prepare:** Preview OpenStax 13.1–13.4. Have a rubber ball and tape measure if you want to measure bounce heights.

**Watch for:**
- Using n instead of n − 1 in aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d or aₙ = a₁rⁿ⁻¹.
- Applying the infinite-series formula when |r| ≥ 1. The sum a/(1 − r) exists only if |r| < 1.
- Miscounting terms in sigma notation. Σ from k = 1 to 5 has 5 terms; from k = 0 to 5 has 6.

### Day 1 (Monday): Arithmetic sequences
*Student:* Write arithmetic sequences recursively and explicitly and find sums with the arithmetic series formula.

*How to teach:* Model 5, 8, 11, …: recursive a₁ = 5, aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + 3; explicit aₙ = 3n + 2. Derive Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2 with the Gauss pairing trick.

- **Q:** Find a₂₀ and S₂₀ for 5, 8, 11, ….
  **A:** a₂₀ = 5 + 19·3 = 62; S₂₀ = 20(5 + 62)/2 = 670.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Geometric sequences
*Student:* Write geometric sequences both ways; model a bouncing ball's rebound heights.

*How to teach:* Model 3, 6, 12, …: a₁ = 3, aₙ = 2aₙ₋₁; aₙ = 3·2ⁿ⁻¹. Bouncing ball dropped from 10 ft rebounding 80%. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Write the rebound height after the nth bounce for a ball dropped from 10 ft with 80% rebound.
  **A:** hₙ = 10(0.8)ⁿ; after the 3rd bounce, 10(0.512) = 5.12 ft.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geometric series
*Student:* Derive the finite geometric series formula and use it to compute a loan or annuity total.

*How to teach:* Derive Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) by writing S − rS. Apply to an annuity. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Find 2 + 6 + 18 + … + 2·3⁹.
  **A:** S₁₀ = 2(3¹⁰ − 1)/(3 − 1) = 59,048.
- **Q:** $200 is deposited each month at 6%/yr compounded monthly for 10 years. Value?
  **A:** 200[(1.005)¹²⁰ − 1]/0.005 ≈ $32,776.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Infinite series
*Student:* Determine convergence of infinite geometric series and find sums; write repeating decimals as fractions.

*How to teach:* Show partial sums of 12 + 6 + 3 + … approaching 24. Convert repeating decimals using a/(1 − r). Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Sum 12 + 6 + 3 + ….
  **A:** 12/(1 − 1/2) = 24.
- **Q:** Write 0.272727… as a fraction.
  **A:** 0.27/(1 − 0.01) = 27/99 = 3/11.
- **Q:** Does 1 + 1.5 + 2.25 + … converge?
  **A:** No, r = 1.5 > 1, so it diverges.

### Day 5 (Friday): Quiz
*Student:* Take a sequences and series quiz using sigma notation.

*How to teach:* Give the sequences and series quiz. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Evaluate Σ_{k=1}^{5} (2k + 1).
  **A:** 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + 11 = 35

**Assessment:** Sequences and series quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) aₙ for 5, 8, 11: 3n + 2. 2) S₂₀ = 670. 3) aₙ for 3, 6, 12: 3·2ⁿ⁻¹. 4) Σ_{k=1}^{5}(2k + 1) = 35. 5) Σ_{k=0}^{9} 2·3ᵏ = 59,048. 6) 12 + 6 + 3 + … = 24. 7) 0.2727… = 3/11. 8) Total distance of a ball dropped 10 ft with 80% rebound (forever): 10 + 2·(8/(1 − 0.8)) = 10 + 80 = 90 ft. Proficient: 6 of 8.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Human impacts on biodiversity

**Prepare:** California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) species pages, map of global biodiversity hotspots, condor population data (22 individuals in 1982; last wild bird captured 1987; releases began 1992; now several hundred, more than half in the wild).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking extinction is only from hunting. Habitat loss is the leading cause globally.
- Believing invasive species are any non-native species. Invasives are non-natives that spread and cause harm.
- Assuming recovered species are 'saved' forever. Condors still need management (lead poisoning remains a major threat).

### Day 1 (Monday): HIPPO
*Student:* Learn the HIPPO threats (Habitat loss, Invasive species, Pollution, Population, Overharvesting) and sort 10 case examples.

*How to teach:* Teach HIPPO and sort 10 case examples.

- **Q:** Classify: quagga mussels in Lake Mead; draining Central Valley wetlands; overfishing sardines.
  **A:** Invasive species; habitat loss; overharvesting.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California hotspot
*Student:* Explain why the California Floristic Province is a biodiversity hotspot and list five endemic species.

*How to teach:* Explain why the California Floristic Province is a hotspot (thousands of native plant species, more than half endemic, with much original habitat lost). List five endemics.

- **Q:** Name five species endemic to California.
  **A:** Examples: giant sequoia, valley oak, Monterey cypress, island fox, California tiger salamander.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Invasives
*Student:* Investigate one California invasive (quagga mussel, Argentine ant, ice plant) and its ecological and economic effects.

*How to teach:* Investigate one invasive: quagga mussel (clogs water pipes, filters plankton from food webs), Argentine ant (displaces native ants; coast horned lizards decline), or ice plant (smothers native dune plants).

- **Q:** How do Argentine ants harm coast horned lizards?
  **A:** They displace native harvester ants, the lizards' main food, and the lizards don't eat Argentine ants.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Condor recovery
*Student:* Analyze California condor population data from 1982 to present and identify which interventions worked.

*How to teach:* Graph condor data and identify interventions: captive breeding, releases, lead-ammunition ban (fully in effect 2019), monitoring and chelation treatment.

- **Q:** What is the biggest ongoing threat to wild condors?
  **A:** Lead poisoning from eating carcasses containing lead ammunition fragments.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a quiz and write a paragraph on which threat is most serious in your region.

*How to teach:* Quiz plus a paragraph on the most serious threat in the region.

- **Q:** What does HIPPO stand for?
  **A:** Habitat loss, Invasive species, Pollution, Population (human), Overharvesting.

**Assessment:** Quiz and regional-threat paragraph.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) HIPPO. 2) Hotspot = high endemism + major habitat loss. 3) Endemic = found only in one region. 4) Invasive example and effect. 5) Condor 22 in 1982; captive breeding; lead. 6) Keystone species. Paragraph rubric: proficient = names a regional threat, supports with specific local evidence, explains mechanism, suggests action.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Incentives and economic systems

**Prepare:** Print excerpts from The Wealth of Nations (Book I ch. 1 pin factory; Book IV ch. 2 'invisible hand') and a short Marx excerpt (Communist Manifesto). Find GDP-per-capita data for North/South Korea or East/West Germany (Maddison Project, World Bank).

**Watch for:**
- Equating 'invisible hand' with 'no rules.' Smith assumed laws, property rights, and competition.
- Thinking all economies are pure types. Real economies are mixed.
- Ignoring nonmonetary incentives.

### Day 1 (Monday): Incentives
*Student:* Classify 10 incentives (bottle deposits, grades, fines, praise) as monetary or nonmonetary and predict behavior changes.

*How to teach:* Define incentives. The student classifies 10 and predicts behavior; check predictions are specific. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Is California's bottle deposit (CRV) monetary or nonmonetary? Predicted effect?
  **A:** Monetary — people return more bottles and cans for the refund.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adam Smith
*Student:* Read the 'invisible hand' and pin-factory excerpts from The Wealth of Nations and summarize Smith's argument.

*How to teach:* Read the pin-factory and invisible-hand excerpts. The student summarizes Smith's argument in three sentences. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did the pin factory show?
  **A:** Division of labor greatly increases productivity — ten workers specializing made thousands of pins a day.
- **Q:** Explain the 'invisible hand.'
  **A:** Individuals pursuing their own gain in competitive markets are led to promote society's interest without intending it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Command economies
*Student:* Compare East and West Germany or North and South Korea using GDP-per-capita data.

*How to teach:* Compare North and South Korea with data. The student graphs GDP per capita over time. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What does the Korea comparison suggest?
  **A:** Similar starting points in the 1950s, but South Korea's market economy grew far richer than North Korea's command economy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Property and conservation
*Student:* Analyze how property rights affect conservation (e.g., California groundwater rights, fishing quotas).

*How to teach:* Discuss property rights and conservation (groundwater, fishing quotas). Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why do individual transferable fishing quotas help conservation?
  **A:** They give fishers a property stake in the long-run fishery, reducing the race to overfish.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test with a short essay comparing economic systems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test with an essay comparing systems. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Define a mixed economy.
  **A:** One combining markets with government involvement — like the U.S.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test: scarcity, opportunity cost, PPF, marginal analysis, incentives, Smith, systems. Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = compares market, command, mixed with real evidence (Korea/Germany), addresses property rights, balanced conclusion.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Investigation: piece 7

**Prepare:** Very large paper or canvas and small cards for scale studies; chosen media.

**Watch for:**
- Enlarging without changing approach. Scale changes mark-making, viewing distance, and impact.
- Thinking small work is less important. Intimate scale draws viewers close.
- Not documenting dimensions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scale studies
*Student:* Make one very small and one very large study of the same idea.

*How to teach:* Make a very small and a very large study of one idea.

- **Q:** How does scale change a viewer's experience?
  **A:** Large works can overwhelm or immerse; small works invite close, intimate viewing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Piece 7
*Student:* Begin piece 7 at a scale new to you.

*How to teach:* Begin piece 7 at a new scale.

- **Q:** What does 'monumental' mean in art?
  **A:** Large in scale or impression, often commanding public space.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Continue
*Student:* Continue piece 7.

*How to teach:* Continue piece 7.

- **Q:** Name a technical challenge of working large.
  **A:** Maintaining consistent proportions and value across the surface; need to step back often.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish
*Student:* Finish piece 7.

*How to teach:* Finish piece 7.

- **Q:** What does 'intimate' scale mean?
  **A:** Small work meant to be viewed up close.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Document and write how scale changed the meaning.

*How to teach:* Document and reflect on how scale changed meaning.

- **Q:** Why include a scale reference when photographing?
  **A:** To show viewers the actual size, which affects interpretation.

**Assessment:** Piece 7 and reflection.

**Answer key:** Piece 7 and reflection: proficient = completed piece at a new scale, scale studies, documentation with dimensions, reflection explaining how scale changed meaning. 4 = scale clearly strengthens the inquiry; 2 = little reflection; 1 = incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Pickleball strategy and play

**Prepare:** Pickleball gear; agility ladder (or chalk lines). Look up open-play times at local parks/recreation centers.

**Watch for:**
- Partners both chasing the same ball. Communicate ('mine,' 'yours'); the forehand usually takes middle balls.
- Standing in the transition zone ('no-man's land'). Move up to the kitchen line or stay back; reset if caught.
- Poaching without communication, which leaves gaps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Positioning
*Student:* Learn doubles positioning and communication; drill transition-zone movement.

*How to teach:* Doubles positioning: move up together, side by side; transition zone drill (split-step, reset shot).

- **Q:** What is the transition zone?
  **A:** The area between the baseline and the kitchen line, where players are vulnerable to shots at their feet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Game play
*Student:* Play doubles games, tracking unforced errors.

*How to teach:* Play doubles, tracking unforced errors.

- **Q:** What is an unforced error?
  **A:** A mistake not caused by the opponent's good shot (e.g., hitting an easy ball into the net).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Community play
*Student:* Attend open play at a local park or recreation center.

*How to teach:* Attend open play at a local court.

- **Q:** What is paddle-stacking at open play?
  **A:** Placing paddles in a rack to wait your turn for the next game.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fitness
*Student:* Do agility ladder and lateral shuffle drills for court movement.

*How to teach:* Agility ladder and lateral shuffles.

- **Q:** What is a split-step?
  **A:** A small hop landing on both feet as the opponent hits, so you can move quickly in any direction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Tournament
*Student:* Play a family round-robin tournament demonstrating etiquette and scoring.

*How to teach:* Family round-robin with etiquette and correct scoring.

- **Q:** What is 'stacking' in doubles strategy?
  **A:** Arranging partner positions so each player stays on their preferred side (e.g., forehand in the middle) regardless of score.

**Assessment:** Game-play rubric.

**Answer key:** Game-play rubric (1–4 each): positioning (moves to kitchen line together), shot selection (dinks/drops/drives appropriate), communication, scoring and rules, etiquette. Proficient = 3 in each: calls score correctly, follows kitchen and two-bounce rules, communicates, respectful play.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): California postsecondary pathways

**Prepare:** Open CaliforniaColleges.edu college search, College Scorecard (collegescorecard.ed.gov), and three colleges' net price calculators. Have family income information available for the calculators (estimates are fine).

**Watch for:**
- Judging colleges by sticker price. Net price (cost after grants and scholarships) can make a private school cheaper than a public one for some families.
- Applying only to reach schools. A balanced list has likely, match and reach options, including at least one affordable likely option.
- Thinking a 4-year college is the only path. Community college, apprenticeships, CTE certificates and the military lead to good careers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Systems compared
*Student:* Make a chart comparing the UC, CSU, California Community Colleges, and private colleges by mission, cost, size, and admission requirements.

*How to teach:* Make a chart comparing UC, CSU, California Community Colleges and private colleges by mission, cost, size and admission.

- **Q:** How many campuses do the UC, CSU and California Community College systems have?
  **A:** UC: 10 campuses (9 with undergraduates); CSU: 23; Community Colleges: 116.
- **Q:** What are UC and CSU's basic subject requirements called?
  **A:** The a–g requirements (15 year-long college-prep courses).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Beyond college
*Student:* Research a California registered apprenticeship, a CTE certificate, and military enlistment requirements.

*How to teach:* Research a California registered apprenticeship, a CTE certificate and military enlistment requirements.

- **Q:** What is a registered apprenticeship?
  **A:** A paid job with structured on-the-job training plus classroom instruction, leading to a nationally recognized credential (e.g. electrician, plumber).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): College search
*Student:* Use CaliforniaColleges.edu and College Scorecard to find eight schools and record graduation rate, median debt, and earnings.

*How to teach:* Find eight schools on CaliforniaColleges.edu and College Scorecard; record graduation rate, median debt and earnings.

- **Q:** What does College Scorecard's 'median earnings' show?
  **A:** The typical earnings of former students several years after entering the school.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Net price
*Student:* Run three college net price calculators with your family and compare estimated costs.

*How to teach:* Run three net price calculators with your family.

- **Q:** What is net price?
  **A:** Cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships — what the family actually pays (through savings, income, work or loans).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit a balanced list of likely, match, and reach options with a pathway rationale.

*How to teach:* The student submits the balanced list with a rationale.

- **Q:** What's an 'impacted' major?
  **A:** A major with more qualified applicants than space, so it has higher admission standards.

**Assessment:** Balanced college/pathway list with data.

**Answer key:** Balanced list rubric: 4 = eight or more options with a mix of likely, match and reach (and/or apprenticeship/CTE/military), each with graduation rate, median debt and median earnings from College Scorecard, net price estimates for three schools, and a rationale connecting to career goals; 3 = balanced but missing some data; 2 = unbalanced or missing data; 1 = incomplete. Systems chart key: UC — 10 campuses (9 enroll undergraduates), research universities, most selective, higher tuition; CSU — 23 campuses, teaching and career-focused, moderate tuition; California Community Colleges — 116 colleges, open access, lowest cost, certificates, associate degrees and transfer; private colleges — varied missions and cost, often significant institutional aid.
