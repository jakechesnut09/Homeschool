# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 4

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Beowulf: the heroic code

**Prepare:** Obtain Beowulf (Gummere on Project Gutenberg or Heaney). Read the first half yourself. Find a short article on the Sutton Hoo ship burial (British Museum site). Parental note: battle violence is frequent but not graphic by modern standards. Line numbers differ by translation — use your edition's.

**Watch for:**
- Reading Beowulf as a fantasy adventure only. Point out the poem's mix of pagan heroic values (fame, gold, revenge) and Christian commentary (God, Cain).
- Kennings misread as metaphors in general. A kenning is a compressed two-word metaphorical name: 'whale-road' for the sea.
- Assuming the boast is arrogance. In the heroic code a boast is a public promise the hero must fulfill.

### Day 1 (Monday): Anglo-Saxon world
*Student:* Read a short overview of Anglo-Saxon England and the Sutton Hoo ship burial; list five values a warrior culture would prize. Read 'The Seafarer' and mark where Christian and pagan ideas collide.

*How to teach:* Read a Sutton Hoo overview and list warrior values (loyalty, courage, generosity, fame, revenge). Then read 'The Seafarer' and mark where Christian ideas take over.

- **Q:** Where do pagan and Christian ideas meet in 'The Seafarer'?
  **A:** The speaker describes the hardships and loneliness of sea life and fleeting earthly glory (an older heroic/elegiac view), then turns to praising God and seeking a lasting home in heaven.
- **Q:** Name three values of the Anglo-Saxon warrior culture.
  **A:** Loyalty to one's lord, courage in battle, and a lord's generosity (ring-giving); also fame and revenge.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Grendel attacks
*Student:* Read Beowulf lines 1–700 (Prologue through Beowulf's arrival). Collect 8 kennings and translate each into plain English.

*How to teach:* Read the opening through Beowulf's arrival at Heorot. The student collects 8 kennings and translates them. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Translate: whale-road, ring-giver, bone-house.
  **A:** The sea; a king or lord; the body.
- **Q:** Why does Grendel attack Heorot?
  **A:** He is enraged by the joyful sounds of feasting and songs of creation in the hall; the poem says he descends from Cain.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The boast and the fight
*Student:* Read the Unferth exchange and the fight with Grendel. Write a paragraph analyzing what Beowulf's boast reveals about the heroic code, citing two quotations.

*How to teach:* Read the Unferth exchange and the fight. Model one paragraph structure: claim, quotation, explanation. The student writes the paragraph citing two quotations.

- **Q:** What does Unferth accuse Beowulf of, and how does Beowulf respond?
  **A:** Of losing a swimming contest with Breca. Beowulf says he won, killed sea-monsters, and taunts Unferth for killing his own brothers.
- **Q:** How does Beowulf defeat Grendel?
  **A:** Bare-handed — he tears off Grendel's arm, and Grendel flees to die.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grendel's mother
*Student:* Read the episode of Grendel's mother. Chart parallels and differences between the first two battles and discuss why the poet includes revenge as a motive.

*How to teach:* Read Grendel's mother. The student charts the two battles side by side and discusses revenge. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why does Grendel's mother attack and whom does she kill?
  **A:** To avenge her son; she kills Aeschere, Hrothgar's trusted counselor.
- **Q:** What happens to Hrunting?
  **A:** Unferth's sword fails against her; Beowulf kills her with a giant's sword he finds in her lair.

### Day 5 (Friday): Weekly check
*Student:* Take a 20-question reading quiz on Beowulf so far and write a timed 20-minute response: 'Is Beowulf motivated more by glory or by duty?'

*How to teach:* Give a 20-question quiz, then a timed 20-minute response on glory vs. duty. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Who is king of the Danes, and what is his hall?
  **A:** Hrothgar; Heorot.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz plus timed analytical paragraph scored for evidence.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz sample answers: Hrothgar, Danes; Heorot; Grendel descended from Cain; Beowulf is a Geat, Hygelac's nephew; Unferth and the Breca contest; arm torn off; Aeschere killed; Hrunting fails; giant's sword. Timed paragraph rubric (4 levels): 4 = clear claim (glory, duty, or both), two well-chosen pieces of evidence, explanation linking evidence to the heroic code; 3 = clear claim with one well-explained piece; 2 = summary; 1 = off-topic.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Inverse functions and Unit 1 test

**Prepare:** Get patty paper (or tracing paper). Preview OpenStax 1.7 (Inverse Functions). Prepare the Unit 1 review set and an answer key (OpenStax chapter 1 review exercises work well) and the test below.

**Watch for:**
- Writing f⁻¹(x) as 1/f(x). Emphasize that the −1 means 'undo,' not a reciprocal: if f(x) = 2x, f⁻¹(x) = x/2, not 1/(2x).
- Forgetting to restrict the domain of a non-one-to-one function before inverting, e.g. claiming √x is the inverse of x² on all reals. It is only the inverse on x ≥ 0.
- Swapping x and y but not solving for y. The procedure is: write y = f(x), swap x and y, solve for y.

### Day 1 (Monday): Inverses graphically
*Student:* Reflect graphs over y = x on patty paper and in Desmos; apply the horizontal-line test to 10 functions.

*How to teach:* Trace a graph on patty paper, fold along y = x, and see the reflected inverse; repeat in Desmos. The student applies the horizontal-line test to 10 functions; check that they connect 'passes' to one-to-one.

- **Q:** Does f(x) = x² have an inverse function on all reals? Why?
  **A:** No. The horizontal line y = 4 hits the graph at x = 2 and x = −2, so it is not one-to-one.
- **Q:** If (3, 7) is on the graph of f, what point is on f⁻¹?
  **A:** (7, 3)

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Inverses algebraically
*Student:* Find inverses of 10 functions algebraically and verify each by showing f(f⁻¹(x)) = x.

*How to teach:* Model: y = (2x + 1)/3 → x = (2y + 1)/3 → y = (3x − 1)/2, then verify f(f⁻¹(x)) = x. The student finds inverses of 10 functions and verifies each by composition.

- **Q:** Find the inverse of f(x) = x³ − 4 and verify.
  **A:** f⁻¹(x) = ∛(x + 4). Check: f(f⁻¹(x)) = (∛(x + 4))³ − 4 = x.
- **Q:** Find the inverse of f(x) = 1/(x − 1).
  **A:** Swap: x = 1/(y − 1) → y − 1 = 1/x → f⁻¹(x) = 1/x + 1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Restricting domains
*Student:* Restrict domains of quadratics and absolute value functions so they are invertible; find each inverse.

*How to teach:* Graph y = (x − 2)² and show that keeping only x ≥ 2 passes the horizontal-line test. The student restricts and inverts quadratics and absolute-value functions, stating the inverse's domain and range.

- **Q:** Restrict f(x) = (x − 2)² to make it invertible, then find f⁻¹.
  **A:** Restrict to x ≥ 2; f⁻¹(x) = √x + 2 with domain x ≥ 0 and range y ≥ 2.
- **Q:** Restrict f(x) = |x| + 1 and give the inverse.
  **A:** For x ≥ 0, f(x) = x + 1 so f⁻¹(x) = x − 1 for x ≥ 1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review set on all Unit 1 topics and correct errors using an answer key.

*How to teach:* The student works the mixed review independently, then self-checks with the answer key, writing a corrected solution next to each miss. Spend the last 15 minutes reteaching the most-missed topic.

- **Q:** Describe y = −(x + 3)² − 1 and give its range.
  **A:** Reflect y = x² over the x-axis, shift left 3 and down 1; vertex (−3, −1); range (−∞, −1].

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on functions, transformations, composition, and inverses.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test (about 50 minutes, Desmos allowed only on marked items). Grade with the key below and review errors the next day.

- **Q:** What is the composition check that f and g are inverses?
  **A:** f(g(x)) = x and g(f(x)) = x for all x in the appropriate domains.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test sample key (write the test with these items): 1) Domain of √(5 − x): (−∞, 5]. 2) Domain of (x + 1)/(x² − 9): x ≠ ±3. 3) y = −2|x − 1| + 3: reflect, stretch 2, right 1, up 3, vertex (1, 3), range (−∞, 3]. 4) f(x) = x² − x is neither even nor odd. 5) f(x) = 3x − 2, g(x) = x²: f(g(x)) = 3x² − 2, g(f(x)) = 9x² − 12x + 4. 6) ARC of x² on [2, 5] = 7. 7) Difference quotient of 3x² = 6x + 3h. 8) Inverse of (2x + 1)/3 = (3x − 1)/2. 9) Inverse of (x − 2)², x ≥ 2: √x + 2. 10) Piecewise graph with correct open/closed endpoints. Scoring: 90%+ mastery, 75–89% proficient, 60–74% developing, below 60% reteach before Unit 2.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Orbits and Kepler's laws

**Prepare:** String, two pushpins, cardboard, paper; PhET 'Gravity and Orbits' (free online); NASA Planetary Fact Sheet. Constants: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg², Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, radius 6,371 km. Prepare the Unit 1 test (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking seasons come from Earth's elliptical orbit. Earth's orbit is nearly circular (e = 0.017); seasons come from axial tilt.
- Believing satellites stay up because there's no gravity in space. They are in constant free fall, moving sideways fast enough to keep missing Earth.
- Using a in km and T in days in T² = a³. That simple form works only with years and AU for objects orbiting the Sun.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kepler's first law
*Student:* Draw ellipses with string and pins at different focus spacings; calculate eccentricity and compare with planetary orbits.

*How to teach:* Draw ellipses with the string loop around two pins at 2, 5, and 8 cm apart. Measure major axis and focal distance; e = (distance between foci) ÷ (major axis). Compare to Earth (0.017), Mars (0.093), Mercury (0.206).

- **Q:** Foci are 6 cm apart and the major axis is 20 cm. What is the eccentricity?
  **A:** e = 6/20 = 0.30.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Kepler's second law
*Student:* Use the PhET Gravity and Orbits simulation to show equal areas in equal times; explain why planets speed up near perihelion.

*How to teach:* In PhET, show equal areas swept in equal times; observe speed changes.

- **Q:** Why does a planet move fastest at perihelion?
  **A:** It's closest to the Sun, where gravity is strongest; conservation of angular momentum means it must move faster to sweep equal areas in equal times.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kepler's third law
*Student:* Graph T² vs. a³ for planets using NASA fact-sheet data and confirm the linear relationship; predict a moon's period.

*How to teach:* Graph T² (years²) vs. a³ (AU³) for 8 planets — slope = 1. Predict periods.

- **Q:** Mars's semi-major axis is 1.52 AU. Find its period.
  **A:** T = √(1.52³) = √3.51 ≈ 1.87 years (actual 1.88).
- **Q:** Using T² = 4π²a³/(GM), predict the Moon's period (a = 3.84 × 10⁸ m, GM_Earth = 3.99 × 10¹⁴).
  **A:** T = 2π√(a³/GM) ≈ 2π√(1.42 × 10¹¹) ≈ 2.37 × 10⁶ s ≈ 27.4 days (actual sidereal 27.3 days).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Satellites
*Student:* Use Newton's law of gravitation to calculate the orbital speed of the ISS and a geostationary satellite.

*How to teach:* Derive v = √(GM/r) from setting gravity equal to centripetal force. Calculate the ISS (altitude ~420 km) and a geostationary satellite (r = 42,164 km).

- **Q:** Calculate the ISS orbital speed and period.
  **A:** r = 6.79 × 10⁶ m; v = √(3.99 × 10¹⁴ / 6.79 × 10⁶) ≈ 7.66 km/s; T = 2πr/v ≈ 5,570 s ≈ 93 minutes.
- **Q:** Calculate the geostationary orbital speed.
  **A:** v = √(3.99 × 10¹⁴ / 4.22 × 10⁷) ≈ 3.07 km/s (period 24 h, matching Earth's rotation).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on the Sun, the Big Bang, stars, and orbits.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** State Kepler's three laws.
  **A:** 1) Planets orbit in ellipses with the Sun at one focus. 2) A line from Sun to planet sweeps equal areas in equal times. 3) T² ∝ a³.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test key (sample items): 1) Fusion of H→He in the core; mass converted via E = mc². 2) Layers: core, radiative, convective, photosphere, chromosphere, corona. 3) Big Bang evidence: redshift/Hubble's law, CMB, H/He abundances. 4) v = H₀d; 7,000 km/s ÷ 70 = 100 Mpc. 5) H-R diagram regions. 6) Low-mass vs. high-mass life cycles. 7) Fe endpoint; heavy elements by neutron capture. 8) Kepler's laws. 9) Jupiter a = 5.2 AU → T ≈ 11.9 yr. 10) e = c/a concept; Earth's orbit nearly circular. 11) v = √(GM/r). Scoring: 90%+ advanced, 75–89% proficient.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Citizenship and civil society

**Prepare:** Print or open the USCIS civics test (uscis.gov). Note: the 2008 version has 100 questions; a 2025 revision expanded the bank — use whichever USCIS currently posts. Find California jury information (courts.ca.gov) and sss.gov. Line up an interview with a volunteer or nonprofit leader.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking citizenship only means rights. Discuss obligations: jury service, obeying laws, paying taxes, Selective Service for men 18–25.
- Believing civil society means government. Civil society is voluntary associations outside government.
- Position essays that ignore the other side. Address the strongest counterargument.

*How to teach:* The student takes the civics test and researches naturalization requirements. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Obligations

*How to teach:* Research jury duty and Selective Service. The student writes about why each matters. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Who must register with Selective Service?
  **A:** Nearly all men ages 18 through 25 living in the U.S.
- **Q:** Name two California juror qualifications.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Civil society
*Student:* Read Tocqueville on Americans' associations; map 10 civic organizations in your community and their purposes.

- **Q:** Why did Tocqueville admire American associations?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview

*How to teach:* The student interviews a volunteer/nonprofit leader and writes notes. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give two good interview questions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test with a position essay on the balance between rights and responsibilities.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test and the position essay. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give a thesis on rights vs. responsibilities.
  **A:** Rights depend on citizens accepting responsibilities like jury service, since rights like a fair trial require them.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and position essay.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test: citizenship requirements, obligations, civil society. Position essay (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): 4 = clear thesis, 2+ examples, counterargument.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Value and form

**Prepare:** White eggs (hard-boiled) or white objects, a single desk lamp in a darkened room, vine and compressed charcoal, toned paper, white charcoal or chalk, kneaded eraser, blending stump. Set up the still life before Day 3.

**Watch for:**
- Outlining shadows with lines. Value is shown with tone; edges are where values meet.
- Making reflected light as bright as the highlight. Reflected light is always darker than the lit side.
- Keeping everything mid-gray. Push the darkest darks and preserve the white of the paper for highlights to create a full value range.

### Day 1 (Monday): Value scale
*Student:* Make a 10-step value scale in graphite and in charcoal.

*How to teach:* Student makes a 10-step value scale in graphite and in charcoal, from white to black, with even steps.

- **Q:** Why make a value scale?
  **A:** To practice control of pressure and layering and to have a reference for matching values across the full range.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg study
*Student:* Draw white eggs under a single lamp, identifying highlight, core shadow, reflected light, and cast shadow.

*How to teach:* Draw eggs under one lamp; label highlight, light, halftone, core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow, and occlusion shadow.

- **Q:** Where is the darkest part of the cast shadow?
  **A:** Right where the object touches the surface (the occlusion shadow).
- **Q:** What is the core shadow?
  **A:** The darkest band on the object itself, where the form turns away from the light, just before reflected light.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Still life
*Student:* Set up a white still life (cups, fabric) and start a 3-hour chiaroscuro drawing.

*How to teach:* Set up a white still life with cups and fabric; begin a 3-hour chiaroscuro drawing — block in big shapes and shadow shapes first.

- **Q:** What is chiaroscuro?
  **A:** Strong contrast of light and dark to model three-dimensional form (associated with Renaissance and Baroque artists like Leonardo and Caravaggio).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Continue
*Student:* Continue the still life, pushing the darkest darks and preserving highlights.

*How to teach:* Continue: push darkest darks, keep highlights clean, soften and sharpen edges deliberately.

- **Q:** Why squint at a still life?
  **A:** Squinting simplifies detail and reveals the big value shapes and relationships.

### Day 5 (Friday): Critique
*Student:* Finish and self-critique using a value checklist; photograph for your journal.

*How to teach:* Finish; self-critique with a value checklist; photograph.

- **Q:** Give one item for a value checklist.
  **A:** Full range from white to black; consistent light direction; reflected light darker than the lit side; cast shadows darkest near the object.

**Assessment:** Chiaroscuro still-life drawing.

**Answer key:** Chiaroscuro still-life rubric. 4 – Full value range, consistent single light source, convincing form with core shadow and reflected light, varied edges, accurate proportions, strong composition. 3 (proficient) – Clear light source, wide value range, believable form. 2 – Limited values or inconsistent light. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Resistance training safety and technique

**Prepare:** Gather dumbbells or water jugs, resistance bands, a broomstick, and a phone to film form. Clear a safe space.

**Watch for:**
- Rounding the spine when lifting from the floor. Hinge at the hips with a neutral spine.
- Using momentum (swinging). Control both lifting and lowering phases.
- Thinking spotting is only for bench press. Spotters help with any lift where a failed rep could trap or injure the lifter; with home equipment, choose loads you can control.

### Day 1 (Monday): Movement patterns
*Student:* Learn the six patterns (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, lunge) with a broomstick; film form.

*How to teach:* Teach six patterns with a broomstick: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, lunge. Film each.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a squat and a hinge?
  **A:** A squat bends knees and hips roughly equally with a more upright torso; a hinge moves mainly at the hips with a slight knee bend and the torso leaning forward.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lower body
*Student:* Practice goblet squats and Romanian deadlifts, 3×10, focusing on neutral spine.

*How to teach:* Goblet squats and Romanian deadlifts 3×10 with neutral spine cues (broomstick touching head, upper back, tailbone during hinge practice).

- **Q:** Name two cues for a goblet squat.
  **A:** Elbows inside knees at the bottom, chest up, knees track over toes, heels down.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Upper body
*Student:* Practice push-ups, band rows, and overhead press, 3×10 each.

*How to teach:* Push-ups, band rows, overhead press 3×10 each.

- **Q:** Which pattern balances push-ups to protect shoulder health?
  **A:** Pulling (rows) — balancing push and pull keeps the shoulders healthy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Leverage
*Student:* Learn how lever length and joint angle change difficulty; test incline vs. decline push-ups.

*How to teach:* Explain levers: longer lever arm or less favorable angle = harder. Compare incline (hands elevated, easier) vs. decline (feet elevated, harder) push-ups.

- **Q:** Why is a decline push-up harder than an incline push-up?
  **A:** More of the body weight is shifted onto the arms and the angle increases the load on the chest and shoulders.

### Day 5 (Friday): Form check
*Student:* Review form video with a checklist and correct two faults.

*How to teach:* Review video with a checklist; identify and correct two faults.

- **Q:** What does 'neutral spine' mean during lifting?
  **A:** Maintaining the spine's natural curves — not rounding or overarching — to distribute load safely.

**Assessment:** Form-check video rubric.

**Answer key:** Form-check video rubric (each pattern 1–4). Proficient (3): squat—depth with heels down and knees tracking; hinge—neutral spine, hips back; push—straight body line, controlled; pull—shoulder blades squeeze, no torso swing; carry—tall posture; lunge—front knee over ankle. Student identifies two faults and shows corrected reps.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Smart spending & big purchases

**Prepare:** Gather grocery ads or two store websites, Kelley Blue Book or Edmunds, and a calculator. Look up California DMV registration fee estimates (dmv.ca.gov fee calculator) and current local gas prices. Prepare the Unit 1 test from the key below.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing package prices instead of unit prices. Divide price by quantity to compare.
- Considering only a car's purchase price. Total cost of ownership includes depreciation, insurance, registration, fuel, maintenance and financing.
- Thinking small purchases don't matter. Small, frequent spending adds up — $5 a day is about $1,825 a year.

### Day 1 (Monday): Opportunity cost
*Student:* Analyze three of your own recent purchases for opportunity cost and compute what each would be worth saved at 5% for 10 years.

*How to teach:* Analyze three of the student's purchases for opportunity cost; compute each saved at 5% for 10 years (amount × 1.05^10 ≈ amount × 1.629).

- **Q:** What would $100 grow to at 5% for 10 years?
  **A:** $100 × 1.05^10 ≈ $162.89.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparison shopping
*Student:* Compare unit prices for 10 grocery items across two stores or websites and calculate monthly savings.

*How to teach:* Compare unit prices for 10 grocery items at two stores; compute monthly savings.

- **Q:** Which is cheaper: 18 oz cereal for $4.50 or 24 oz for $5.52?
  **A:** 18 oz = $0.25/oz; 24 oz = $0.23/oz → the 24 oz box.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Marketing tactics
*Student:* Identify five persuasive techniques in online ads (scarcity, social proof, anchoring, free trials, influencer posts) using real examples.

*How to teach:* Find real ads showing scarcity, social proof, anchoring, free trials and influencer posts.

- **Q:** What is anchoring in pricing?
  **A:** Showing a high 'original' price first so the sale price seems like a bargain.
- **Q:** Why are free trials risky?
  **A:** They often auto-renew into paid subscriptions you forget to cancel.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cost of a car
*Student:* Calculate the 5-year total cost of ownership for a used vs. new car including California registration, insurance, gas, and maintenance.

*How to teach:* Calculate 5-year total cost of ownership for a used and a new car: price/depreciation, California registration, insurance, gas (miles ÷ mpg × price per gallon), maintenance, loan interest.

- **Q:** You drive 12,000 miles a year in a 30-mpg car with gas at $5.00/gallon. Yearly fuel cost?
  **A:** 12,000 ÷ 30 = 400 gallons × $5.00 = $2,000.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Unit 1 test: paychecks, budgeting, and consumer choice scenarios, plus final 7-day spending reflection.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test in the key, then the student writes a final reflection on the 7-day spending log.

- **Q:** What did your 7-day log reveal?
  **A:** Answers vary; strong answers cite totals by category and name a specific change.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test and car-cost comparison project.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test answers: 1) Gross vs. net pay. 2) FICA 7.65%. 3) California daily overtime after 8 hours. 4) 10 hours at $16.90 in one day → 8 × 16.90 + 2 × 25.35 = $135.20 + $50.70 = $185.90. 5) Fixed vs. variable expenses. 6) 50/30/20 of $3,000 → $1,500/$900/$600. 7) Opportunity cost of a $60 purchase saved at 5% for 10 years → $60 × 1.05^10 ≈ $97.73. 8) Unit price: 24 oz for $4.80 = $0.20/oz vs. 32 oz for $5.76 = $0.18/oz → larger is cheaper. 9) Scarcity marketing = 'only 2 left!' to create urgency. 10) Total cost of ownership definition. Car project rubric: 4 = realistic 5-year totals for both cars with purchase price/depreciation, registration, insurance quote estimate, fuel (miles ÷ mpg × price), maintenance, and loan interest if financed; clear recommendation; 3 = one category missing; 2 = several missing; 1 = incomplete.
