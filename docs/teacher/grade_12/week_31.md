# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 31

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: War poets and Modernist poetry

**Prepare:** Print Brooke 'The Soldier,' Owen 'Dulce et Decorum Est' and 'Anthem for Doomed Youth,' Sassoon 'Suicide in the Trenches,' and Eliot 'Prufrock.' Parental preview: Owen depicts a gas death graphically; Sassoon's poem depicts a soldier's suicide — check in with the student emotionally.

**Watch for:**
- Reading all WWI poems as anti-war. Brooke's 'The Soldier' (1914) is idealistic and patriotic; Owen and Sassoon write from later trench experience.
- Assuming Prufrock is literally going to a party and proposing. The 'overwhelming question' is never asked; the poem dramatizes paralysis and self-consciousness.
- Missing form as meaning: 'Anthem for Doomed Youth' is a sonnet — a love-poem form — used for mass death.

### Day 1 (Monday): Before and after
*Student:* Compare Rupert Brooke's 'The Soldier' with Wilfred Owen's 'Dulce et Decorum Est'; chart contrasts in imagery and attitude.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Soldier' and 'Dulce et Decorum Est' aloud. The student charts imagery and attitude; check that they quote both poems.

- **Q:** How does Brooke imagine a soldier's death?
  **A:** As a noble gift: 'some corner of a foreign field / That is for ever England.'
- **Q:** What is 'the old Lie' in Owen's poem?
  **A:** 'Dulce et decorum est / Pro patria mori' — it is sweet and fitting to die for one's country (from Horace).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sassoon and Owen
*Student:* Read Sassoon's 'Suicide in the Trenches' and Owen's 'Anthem for Doomed Youth'; explain how the sonnet form is subverted.

*How to teach:* Read 'Suicide in the Trenches' and 'Anthem for Doomed Youth.' Explain how Owen subverts the sonnet; ask what Sassoon blames.

- **Q:** How does 'Anthem for Doomed Youth' subvert the sonnet?
  **A:** It uses the sonnet's lyrical form for a funeral without rites: 'What passing-bells for these who die as cattle? / Only the monstrous anger of the guns.'
- **Q:** Whom does Sassoon's final stanza attack?
  **A:** The 'smug-faced crowds' at home who cheer soldiers marching off but never face 'the hell where youth and laughter go.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prufrock I
*Student:* Read 'The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock' aloud; annotate fragmented images and the speaker's paralysis.

*How to teach:* Read 'Prufrock' aloud. The student annotates fragmented images and signs of paralysis. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What simile opens the poem and what does it suggest?
  **A:** The evening 'spread out against the sky / Like a patient etherised upon a table' — numbness and passivity.
- **Q:** Quote a line showing Prufrock's paralysis.
  **A:** 'Do I dare / Disturb the universe?' or 'I have measured out my life with coffee spoons.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prufrock II
*Student:* Discuss how Prufrock's allusions (Hamlet, Lazarus, Michelangelo) show alienation; compare to Victorian dramatic monologue.

*How to teach:* Discuss allusions; compare with Browning's monologue. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What does Prufrock say about Hamlet?
  **A:** 'No! I am not Prince Hamlet, nor was meant to be; / Am an attendant lord' — he sees himself as a minor character.
- **Q:** How is Prufrock unlike the Duke?
  **A:** The Duke is confident and controlling; Prufrock is anxious and indecisive, and his monologue is internal and fragmented.

### Day 5 (Friday): Timed analysis
*Student:* Write a 40-minute timed poetry analysis comparing Owen and Brooke on war and sacrifice.

*How to teach:* Give the 40-minute timed comparison of Owen and Brooke. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** State a comparative thesis.
  **A:** Brooke presents death as a noble gift to England, while Owen's graphic gas attack exposes that idea as 'the old Lie.'

**Assessment:** Timed comparative poetry essay.

**Answer key:** Timed essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = thesis contrasting attitudes toward war and sacrifice, close analysis of imagery and form in both poems (Brooke's sonnet of idealized England; Owen's gas imagery 'As under a green sea' and the Horace allusion), context; 3 = clear comparison; 2 = summary; 1 = one poem only.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Simulation, margin of error, and comparing treatments

**Prepare:** CODAP (use its 'Sampler' plugin), 40+ index cards, a timer, and the Week 30 memory-experiment design. Recruit at least 10 participants (family, co-op). Preview Intro Stats 8.3 (confidence interval for a proportion).

**Watch for:**
- Saying '95% confident' means a 95% chance the true value is in THIS interval. It means the method captures the true value in about 95% of samples.
- Thinking margin of error covers bias. It only accounts for random sampling variability.
- Calling any difference between groups 'significant.' Compare it to the shuffle distribution: if differences that large happen often by chance alone, it is not convincing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sampling distributions
*Student:* Simulate 500 samples in CODAP and observe how sample proportions vary; relate spread to sample size.

*How to teach:* In CODAP, draw 500 samples of size 25 and then 100 from a population with p = 0.5 and plot the sample proportions. Ask the student to describe center, shape, and spread and how spread changes with n.

- **Q:** What happens to the spread of sample proportions as n increases from 25 to 100?
  **A:** It gets smaller — the standard deviation is √(p(1 − p)/n), so quadrupling n halves it (0.10 to 0.05 when p = 0.5).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Margin of error
*Student:* Compute a 95% margin of error for a poll proportion and interpret the confidence interval.

*How to teach:* Teach ME = 1.96√(p̂(1 − p̂)/n) and the interval p̂ ± ME. Model with a poll and have the student interpret in a full sentence.

- **Q:** A poll of 1,000 finds 54% support. Find the 95% margin of error and interval.
  **A:** ME = 1.96√(0.54·0.46/1000) ≈ 0.031. Interval ≈ 0.509 to 0.571: we're 95% confident the true support is between about 51% and 57%.
- **Q:** How large a sample cuts the margin of error in half?
  **A:** About 4 times as large.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Run the experiment
*Student:* Carry out your Week 30 memory experiment with at least 10 participants randomly assigned to two groups.

*How to teach:* Randomly assign participants (draw names from a hat) to music vs. silence; each studies a 20-word list for 2 minutes and recalls for 2 minutes. Record recall counts in a table.

- **Q:** Why must assignment be random and not by choice?
  **A:** People who choose music may differ (habits, ability); random assignment balances those differences between groups.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Randomization test
*Student:* Use index cards or CODAP to shuffle group labels 100+ times and decide whether your observed difference is significant.

*How to teach:* Write every score on an index card, shuffle, deal into two groups of the original sizes, and record the difference in means; repeat 100+ times (or use CODAP). Locate the observed difference in the shuffle distribution.

- **Q:** If 3 of 200 shuffled differences were as large as the observed one, what do you conclude?
  **A:** Only 1.5% by chance, so the observed difference is statistically significant — evidence the treatment matters.

### Day 5 (Friday): Lab report
*Student:* Write a lab report with design, results, and a justified conclusion.

*How to teach:* The student writes the lab report: question, design, data table, graph, shuffle result, conclusion, limitations. Check that the conclusion matches the evidence.

- **Q:** Name one limitation of a 10-person experiment.
  **A:** Small sample — chance has a big effect; results may not generalize beyond the volunteers.

**Assessment:** Experiment lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = clear question and hypothesis; random assignment described; control of other variables; table and comparative graph; randomization test with 100+ shuffles and the proportion as extreme stated; conclusion that matches the evidence and avoids overgeneralizing; 2+ limitations. 3 = all parts present with a small error. 2 = conclusion not supported by the simulation or randomization missing. 1 = incomplete.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Designing and building a prototype

**Prepare:** Cardboard box, aluminum foil, plastic wrap, black paper, tape, oven thermometer (or other materials for a cool-roof model or water filter). Choose a sunny day for testing. SAFETY: solar ovens can exceed 90 °C; use oven mitts.

**Watch for:**
- Changing multiple variables between versions.
- Testing at different times of day or weather and comparing directly. Control conditions or test side by side.
- Stopping after one version. Iteration is the heart of engineering.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose design
*Student:* Use a decision matrix to choose the best design (example: a solar oven for clean cooking, a cool-roof model, or a water filter).

*How to teach:* Decision matrix: score options against weighted criteria; choose the best.

- **Q:** Option A scores 4 (weight 3) and 2 (weight 1); Option B scores 3 and 5. Which wins?
  **A:** A: 4×3 + 2×1 = 14. B: 3×3 + 5×1 = 14. A tie — use another criterion or refine weights.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build v1
*Student:* Build version 1 of your prototype from household materials.

*How to teach:* Build version 1.

- **Q:** Why does a solar oven use black paper inside and foil flaps?
  **A:** Foil reflects sunlight into the box; black surfaces absorb light and convert it to heat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test v1
*Student:* Test the prototype with a data protocol (e.g., solar oven temperature every 5 minutes for an hour).

*How to teach:* Test v1: temperature every 5 minutes for an hour.

- **Q:** Why does plastic wrap help a solar oven?
  **A:** It lets sunlight in but traps warm air (reduces convection heat loss).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Iterate
*Student:* Modify one variable and build version 2.

*How to teach:* Modify one variable (e.g., add insulation or a second reflector) to make v2.

- **Q:** Why change only one variable?
  **A:** So any performance change can be attributed to that change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Test v2
*Student:* Retest version 2 and compare performance with a graph.

*How to teach:* Retest v2 side by side or under similar conditions; graph both.

- **Q:** v1 peaked at 65 °C, v2 at 82 °C. Percent improvement?
  **A:** (82 − 65) ÷ 65 ≈ 26%.

**Assessment:** Prototype test data and graphs.

**Answer key:** Prototype test data rubric. 4 – Decision matrix with weighted criteria, v1 and v2 built, controlled testing with data tables every 5 min, graph comparing versions, analysis of the one changed variable, ideas for v3. 3 (proficient) – Two versions tested with data and graph. 2 – One version or uncontrolled tests. 1 – Incomplete.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Money, the Fed, and fiscal vs. monetary policy

**Prepare:** Read the latest FOMC statement (federalreserve.gov). Bookmark FRED DGS3MO and DGS10 (or T10Y3M). Prepare 10 policy scenarios and the Unit 12 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Fed prints money to pay government bills. The Fed conducts monetary policy independently; Congress and the president handle fiscal policy.
- Confusing fiscal (taxes/spending) and monetary (interest rates/money supply) policy.
- Thinking banks lend out deposits only once. Lending creates new deposits throughout the system.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is money?
*Student:* Explain the functions of money and how banks create money through fractional reserve lending with a worked example.

*How to teach:* Teach money's functions: medium of exchange, store of value, unit of account. Model fractional reserve lending with a T-account.

- **Q:** $1,000 is deposited; the reserve requirement is 10%. What is the maximum total new money the system can create?
  **A:** Money multiplier 1/0.10 = 10, so up to $10,000 in total deposits — $9,000 of new money from loans.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Fed
*Student:* Read the most recent FOMC statement and identify the policy decision and the reasons given.

*How to teach:* Read the FOMC statement. The student identifies the decision and reasons. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is the Fed's dual mandate?
  **A:** Maximum employment and stable prices (about 2% inflation).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interest rates
*Student:* Graph 3-month vs. 10-year Treasury yields on FRED and explain the yield curve and why inversions matter.

*How to teach:* Graph 3-month vs. 10-year yields. Explain the yield curve. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is an inverted yield curve and why does it matter?
  **A:** Short-term yields exceed long-term yields; it has preceded most recent U.S. recessions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Policy match
*Student:* Match 10 scenarios to appropriate fiscal or monetary responses and explain trade-offs.

*How to teach:* The student matches 10 scenarios to fiscal or monetary responses and explains trade-offs. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Recession with high unemployment: give one fiscal and one monetary response.
  **A:** Fiscal: cut taxes or raise spending; monetary: lower the federal funds rate.
- **Q:** Inflation is 6%: what would the Fed do?
  **A:** Raise interest rates to slow borrowing and spending.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 12 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 12 test on macroeconomics.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 12 test. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name the Fed's main policy rate.
  **A:** The federal funds rate.

**Assessment:** Unit 12 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 12 test key: GDP and components; unemployment types and rate; CPI and inflation; money's functions; multiplier; dual mandate; fiscal vs. monetary tools; yield curve. Scoring: 90%+ mastery, 75–89% proficient.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Artist statement and online portfolio

**Prepare:** Free portfolio site (Google Sites, Adobe Portfolio, Cargo). Final images and captions. Parental note: use a professional email and avoid posting home address or personal details.

**Watch for:**
- Statements full of jargon ('juxtaposes liminal dichotomies'). Use clear, specific language.
- Writing about technique only. Include ideas and why the work matters.
- Publishing without testing on a phone.

### Day 1 (Monday): Statement draft
*Student:* Draft a 200-word artist statement about your inquiry and process.

*How to teach:* Draft a 200-word statement: inquiry, process, why it matters.

- **Q:** What three things should an artist statement address?
  **A:** What you make (subject/media), how you make it (process), and why (ideas and meaning).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise the statement for clarity, removing jargon; read it aloud.

*How to teach:* Revise; remove jargon; read aloud.

- **Q:** Why read a statement aloud?
  **A:** To catch awkward phrasing and ensure it sounds like you.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Captions
*Student:* Write captions for each piece (title, media, size, year, short note).

*How to teach:* Write captions: title, media, size, year, short note.

- **Q:** Write a correct caption format.
  **A:** Example: Kitchen Memory, 2025. Graphite and gel transfer on wood, 18 × 24 in. — Note: recipe cards from my grandmother.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build site
*Student:* Build an online portfolio site with gallery, statement, and contact info.

*How to teach:* Build site: gallery, statement, contact.

- **Q:** What is a CV for an artist?
  **A:** A list of exhibitions, education, awards, and publications.

### Day 5 (Friday): Publish
*Student:* Publish the site and test it on phone and computer.

*How to teach:* Publish; test on phone and computer.

- **Q:** Why test on mobile?
  **A:** Many viewers, including admissions readers, view portfolios on phones; layouts and image sizes must work.

**Assessment:** Artist statement and published portfolio.

**Answer key:** Artist statement and published portfolio rubric. 4 – Clear, jargon-free 200-word statement addressing what/how/why; complete captions; site with organized gallery, statement, bio, safe contact info; tested on two devices. 3 (proficient) – Statement, captions, and functioning site. 2 – Unclear statement or missing captions. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Evaluating fitness resources and claims

**Prepare:** Collect gym brochures or websites (3 gyms, community center), app store pages, and FDA and NIH Office of Dietary Supplements pages. Trainer certification registry (USREPS or certification bodies).

**Watch for:**
- Signing long gym contracts without reading cancellation terms.
- Assuming supplements are tested or approved by the FDA before sale. They are not.
- Trusting any 'certified' trainer. Look for NCCA-accredited certifications (e.g., ACE, ACSM, NASM, NSCA).

### Day 1 (Monday): Gym options
*Student:* Compare costs and contracts of three gyms, a community center, and free options; do a workout.

*How to teach:* Compare cost (initiation, monthly, annual fees), contract length, cancellation terms, and free options; do a workout.

- **Q:** Gym A: $50 sign-up + $30/month. Gym B: no sign-up, $35/month. Which is cheaper over 12 months?
  **A:** Gym A: 50 + 360 = $410. Gym B: $420. Gym A is $10 cheaper over a year (B is cheaper for shorter periods).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Apps and wearables
*Student:* Evaluate two fitness apps and a wearable for usefulness and privacy.

*How to teach:* Evaluate two apps and a wearable for usefulness, accuracy, cost, and privacy (what data is collected/shared).

- **Q:** What privacy question should you ask about a fitness app?
  **A:** What data (location, health) does it collect, who is it shared with or sold to, and can you delete it?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Supplements
*Student:* Evaluate three supplement claims using FDA and NIH sources.

*How to teach:* Evaluate three supplement claims using FDA and NIH ODS sources.

- **Q:** Does FDA approve dietary supplements before sale?
  **A:** No — manufacturers are responsible for safety and truthful labels; FDA acts after problems arise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trainers
*Student:* Learn how to check a personal trainer's certification (NCCA-accredited).

*How to teach:* Learn how to check trainer certification (NCCA accreditation; verify on the certifying body's site).

- **Q:** Name two NCCA-accredited trainer certifications.
  **A:** ACE, ACSM, NASM, NSCA (CSCS or CPT).

### Day 5 (Friday): Consumer report
*Student:* Write a consumer guide to adult fitness options in your area.

*How to teach:* Write a consumer guide to adult fitness options in your area.

- **Q:** What are red flags in a fitness product ad?
  **A:** Quick-fix promises, spot reduction, 'no effort,' testimonials only, 'secret' formulas.

**Assessment:** Consumer guide.

**Answer key:** Consumer guide rubric. 4 – Compares at least four options with accurate costs over 12 months, contract terms, free alternatives, app/wearable evaluation including privacy, accurate supplement regulation, how to verify trainers. 3 (proficient) – Accurate cost comparison, supplement facts, trainer verification. 2 – Inaccuracies. 1 – Incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Resumes & cover letters

**Prepare:** Have a resume template, a real local job posting, and a word processor. Line up a parent/mentor to review and two potential references (former supervisors, coaches, volunteer coordinators — not relatives).

**Watch for:**
- Listing duties instead of accomplishments. Use action verb + task + result/number.
- A generic cover letter. Tailor each letter to the posting and include its keywords (applicant tracking systems scan for them).
- Fancy formatting that confuses ATS software. Use a simple, clean layout with standard headings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Resume formats
*Student:* Compare chronological, skills-based, and student resume samples and pick a format.

*How to teach:* Compare chronological, skills-based and student resume samples; pick a format.

- **Q:** Which resume format suits a student with little work history?
  **A:** A student or skills-based (functional/hybrid) resume that highlights education, projects, volunteer work and skills.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Accomplishments
*Student:* Rewrite 10 duties into quantified accomplishment statements using strong action verbs.

*How to teach:* Rewrite 10 duties into quantified accomplishment statements with action verbs.

- **Q:** Rewrite: 'Responsible for helping customers.'
  **A:** 'Assisted 50+ customers per shift, resolving questions quickly and earning a 'staff pick' recognition' (any action verb + task + measurable result).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Full resume
*Student:* Draft a one-page resume including homeschool education, volunteer work, skills, and certifications.

*How to teach:* Draft a one-page resume including homeschool education, volunteer work, skills and certifications.

- **Q:** How should a homeschool student list education?
  **A:** List the homeschool's name (the private school name on the affidavit) or 'Homeschool', city, expected graduation date, GPA if strong, and notable courses, AP exams or college classes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cover letter
*Student:* Write a tailored cover letter for a real local job posting, matching keywords from the posting.

*How to teach:* Write a tailored cover letter for a real local posting, matching keywords.

- **Q:** What is an ATS?
  **A:** An applicant tracking system — software that scans and ranks applications, often by keywords from the job posting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Revise resume and cover letter after a parent or mentor review and request two references.

*How to teach:* Revise after review and request two references.

- **Q:** Who makes a good reference?
  **A:** An adult who has supervised or worked with you (employer, coach, volunteer coordinator, teacher) and isn't a relative; ask permission first.

**Assessment:** Final resume and cover letter scored with a rubric.

**Answer key:** Resume and cover letter rubric (4 levels): 4 = one page; contact info; education (homeschool program name, expected graduation, relevant courses/honors); experience and volunteer work with quantified accomplishment statements starting with action verbs; skills and certifications; no errors; cover letter addressed to a person if possible, names the job, connects two or three qualifications to the posting's keywords, ends with a call to action; references confirmed; 3 = minor errors; 2 = duty lists or generic letter; 1 = incomplete.
