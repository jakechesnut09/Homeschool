# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 11

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Macbeth argument essay and the sonnets

**Prepare:** Print Sonnets 18, 29, 73, 116, 130. Gather Macbeth notes and motif tracker. Prepare an argument-essay rubric.

**Watch for:**
- A counterclaim that is a straw man. Present the strongest opposing view fairly before rebutting.
- Switching to past tense when discussing literature. Use the literary present: 'Macbeth kills Duncan.'
- Assuming the volta in a Shakespearean sonnet always comes at line 9. It often comes at the couplet (line 13).

### Day 1 (Monday): Claim and counterclaim
*Student:* Choose a prompt (e.g., 'Who is more responsible for Duncan's murder?') and draft a thesis plus a counterclaim you will rebut. Build an evidence outline.

*How to teach:* Model a thesis and counterclaim for 'Who is more responsible for Duncan's murder?' The student drafts a thesis, counterclaim, and evidence outline.

- **Q:** Give a sample thesis and counterclaim.
  **A:** Thesis: Macbeth bears more responsibility because he imagines the murder before Lady Macbeth speaks and commits further murders on his own. Counterclaim: Lady Macbeth manipulates him by attacking his manhood in I.7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft body
*Student:* Draft the full argument essay (900–1,200 words) with embedded quotations and act/scene/line citations.

*How to teach:* The student drafts the 900–1,200-word essay with (Act.Scene.Line) citations. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How should a Macbeth quotation be cited in MLA?
  **A:** By act, scene, and line: (1.7.25–28).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sonnets
*Student:* Read Sonnets 18, 73, and 130. Label the quatrains, volta, and couplet; explain how 130 parodies the Petrarchan blazon.

*How to teach:* Read Sonnets 18, 73, 130; label quatrains, volta, couplet. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How does Sonnet 130 parody the Petrarchan blazon?
  **A:** It denies each conventional comparison ('My mistress' eyes are nothing like the sun') and then insists in the couplet that his love is as rare as any woman 'belied with false compare.'
- **Q:** What three images of aging does Sonnet 73 use?
  **A:** Autumn (yellow leaves), twilight, and a dying fire.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise essay
*Student:* Revise the argument essay for transitions between claims and to strengthen the rebuttal paragraph; edit for verb tense consistency (literary present).

*How to teach:* The student revises for transitions and literary present. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Macbeth was haunted by Banquo's ghost.'
  **A:** 'Macbeth is haunted by Banquo's ghost.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Submit final Macbeth essay and write a 14-line Shakespearean sonnet of your own in iambic pentameter.

*How to teach:* The student submits the essay and an original sonnet. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is the rhyme scheme of a Shakespearean sonnet?
  **A:** ABAB CDCD EFEF GG

**Assessment:** Argument essay (W.11-12.1 rubric) and original sonnet.

**Answer key:** Argument essay (W.11-12.1) rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): Claim — precise, arguable. Evidence — 5+ cited quotations. Counterclaim — strongest opposing view fairly stated and rebutted. Organization/style — transitions, literary present, MLA citations. Sonnet: 14 lines, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, mostly iambic pentameter, a turn at line 9 or 13.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Graphs of sine and cosine

**Prepare:** Desmos; find the Desmos 'unit circle to sine graph' animation or build one with a point (cos t, sin t). Look up daylight hours by month for your California city on timeanddate.com (sunrise/sunset table).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing period with b. The period is 2π/b, so y = sin(2x) has period π, not 2.
- Reading the amplitude as the max value. Amplitude = (max − min)/2; midline = (max + min)/2.
- Sign of phase shift: y = sin(x − π/4) shifts RIGHT π/4.

### Day 1 (Monday): Unwrapping the circle
*Student:* Use a Desmos animation to unwrap the unit circle into sine and cosine graphs; explain even/odd symmetry.

*How to teach:* Animate the point moving around the circle and trace its y-value (sine) and x-value (cosine) against angle. Discuss why cos is even (symmetric about the y-axis) and sin is odd.

- **Q:** Why is cos(−x) = cos x?
  **A:** Angles x and −x reflect across the x-axis on the unit circle, so they have the same x-coordinate. Cosine is even.
- **Q:** What are the period and range of y = sin x?
  **A:** Period 2π; range [−1, 1].

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Amplitude & period
*Student:* Graph y = a sin(bx) and y = a cos(bx) for 8 cases by hand.

*How to teach:* Graph y = 3 sin(2x) by finding the period, dividing it into four quarters, and plotting max/zero/min points. The student graphs 8 by hand.

- **Q:** Amplitude and period of y = 3 sin(2x)?
  **A:** Amplitude 3; period π.
- **Q:** Amplitude, period, and midline of y = −2 cos(x/2) + 1?
  **A:** Amplitude 2; period 4π; midline y = 1; reflected so it starts at a minimum of −1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Phase shift & midline
*Student:* Graph fully transformed sinusoids y = a sin(b(x−h)) + k and write equations from graphs.

*How to teach:* Model writing an equation from a graph: midline, amplitude, period → b, then a starting point for h. The student graphs and writes several.

- **Q:** A sinusoid has max 5, min −1, period 12, and a max at x = 0. Write its equation.
  **A:** Midline 2, amplitude 3, b = 2π/12 = π/6: y = 3 cos(πx/6) + 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Modeling periodic data
*Student:* Model daylight hours for your California city over a year (data from timeanddate or NOAA) with a sinusoid.

*How to teach:* Enter monthly daylight data (day of year vs. hours) and fit y = a sin(b(x − h)) + k with b = 2π/365. Interpret each parameter.

- **Q:** For a California city with about 14.4 hours of daylight in late June and 9.9 hours in late December, give the amplitude and midline.
  **A:** Amplitude ≈ (14.4 − 9.9)/2 ≈ 2.25 hours; midline ≈ 12.15 hours.
- **Q:** Why is b = 2π/365?
  **A:** The pattern repeats every 365 days, so the period is 365.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a quiz on graphing and writing sinusoidal equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz below; collect the daylight model. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Write y = sin x shifted right π/4 and up 2.
  **A:** y = sin(x − π/4) + 2

**Assessment:** Sinusoid quiz and daylight model.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) y = 3 sin 2x: A = 3, P = π. 2) y = −2cos(x/2) + 1: A = 2, P = 4π, midline y = 1. 3) Max 5, min −1, period 12, max at 0: y = 3cos(πx/6) + 2. 4) y = sin(x − π/4) + 2: right π/4, up 2. 5) Graph of y = 2 sin(πx) + 1 with key points (0,1), (0.5,3), (1,1), (1.5,−1), (2,1). Daylight model rubric: 4 = data cited, b = 2π/365, amplitude/midline/shift match data, residuals discussed; 3 = good fit, minor errors; 2 = wrong period or midline; 1 = incomplete.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Feedbacks among Earth systems

**Prepare:** Black and white paper, ice cubes of equal size, lamp, 2 thermometers. Find global temperature data after the June 1991 Pinatubo eruption (NASA). Read about Matilija Dam (Ventura River, built 1947, now mostly filled with sediment; removal planned). Prepare the Unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'positive feedback' means good. It means a change is amplified; negative feedback dampens change.
- Assuming volcanoes only warm climate. Large explosive eruptions inject sulfur dioxide that forms reflective aerosols, cooling the planet for 1–3 years.
- Treating Earth's spheres separately. Events in one sphere ripple through the others.

### Day 1 (Monday): Four spheres
*Student:* Diagram interactions among geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, and biosphere for a wildfire event.

*How to teach:* Diagram a wildfire across spheres: atmosphere (smoke, CO₂), biosphere (vegetation loss), geosphere (soil hydrophobic, landslides/debris flows), hydrosphere (runoff, water quality).

- **Q:** How can a wildfire lead to a debris flow?
  **A:** Fire removes vegetation and creates water-repellent soil; heavy rain then runs off rapidly, carrying soil and rock downhill.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Albedo lab
*Student:* Melt ice cubes on black vs. white paper under a lamp and record melt time and temperature; relate to sea-ice feedback.

*How to teach:* Albedo lab: ice cubes on black vs. white paper under a lamp; record melt time and paper temperature.

- **Q:** Explain the ice-albedo feedback.
  **A:** Warming melts sea ice, exposing dark ocean (albedo ~0.06 vs. ~0.5–0.7 for ice), which absorbs more sunlight, causing more warming and melting — a positive feedback.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Volcanic feedback
*Student:* Analyze temperature data after the 1991 Mt. Pinatubo eruption and explain the cooling effect of aerosols.

*How to teach:* Analyze temperature data after Pinatubo: global average cooled about 0.5 °C in 1992.

- **Q:** Why did Pinatubo cool the planet?
  **A:** It injected ~15–20 million tons of SO₂ into the stratosphere, forming sulfate aerosols that reflected sunlight back to space.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dams and rivers
*Student:* Analyze how a dam changes sediment, beaches, and ecosystems downstream (e.g., Matilija Dam) as a feedback chain.

*How to teach:* Matilija Dam: traps sediment → less sand to the Ventura River mouth → beach erosion and loss of steelhead habitat. Draw the chain.

- **Q:** How can a dam cause beaches downstream to shrink?
  **A:** It traps sediment that would otherwise reach the coast to replenish beaches, so waves erode beaches faster than they're resupplied.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on Earth's dynamic systems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** Give an example of a negative feedback in the climate system.
  **A:** Example: warmer temperatures increase outgoing infrared radiation (Earth radiates more as it warms), or more plant growth with more CO₂ removes some CO₂.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key (sample): 1) P- and S-waves; liquid outer core evidence. 2) Mantle convection drives plates; slab pull. 3) Hot spot calculation. 4) Boundaries and California landforms. 5) San Andreas slip ~3.5 cm/yr. 6) Water's high heat capacity; frost wedging; acid weathering. 7) Weathering vs. erosion. 8) Positive vs. negative feedback with examples (ice-albedo positive). 9) Pinatubo aerosols cooled ~0.5 °C. 10) Dams trap sediment → beach erosion. 90%+ advanced, 75–89% proficient.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Parties, campaigns, and money

**Prepare:** Print Washington's Farewell Address excerpt. Bookmark FEC.gov and the Living Room Candidate archive. Find two recent polls with methodology.

**Watch for:**
- Believing the Constitution creates parties. Parties developed despite founders' warnings.
- Thinking Citizens United allowed unlimited direct contributions to candidates. It allowed unlimited independent expenditures; direct contributions remain capped.
- Treating any poll as accurate. Check sample, margin of error, wording.

### Day 1 (Monday): Origin of parties
*Student:* Read Washington's warning about factions in his Farewell Address and trace the development of the two-party system.

*How to teach:* Read Washington's warning. Trace party systems. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did Washington warn about?
  **A:** The 'baneful effects of the spirit of party.'
- **Q:** Name the first two parties.
  **A:** Federalists (Hamilton) and Democratic-Republicans (Jefferson, Madison).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Nominations
*Student:* Explain how presidential primaries and conventions evolved and how California's top-two primary works.

*How to teach:* Explain primaries, caucuses, conventions, and California's top-two primary. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How does California's top-two primary work?
  **A:** All candidates appear on one ballot; the top two vote-getters advance to November regardless of party (Prop 14, 2010).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Campaign ads
*Student:* Analyze three historical campaign ads for persuasive techniques and target audiences.

*How to teach:* Analyze three campaign ads (e.g., 'Daisy' 1964, 'Morning in America' 1984). Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What technique does 'Daisy' (1964) use?
  **A:** Fear appeal — nuclear war imagery.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Money in politics
*Student:* Use FEC.gov to compare fundraising for two candidates and analyze Citizens United v. FEC.

*How to teach:* Use FEC.gov; analyze Citizens United. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did Citizens United v. FEC (2010) decide?
  **A:** Government can't limit independent political spending by corporations and unions; it led to super PACs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Polls
*Student:* Evaluate two recent polls for sample size, margin of error, and question wording; take a quiz.

*How to teach:* Evaluate two polls; take the quiz. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** A poll shows 48% vs 45% with ±3.5% margin. Is it a clear lead?
  **A:** No — within the margin of error.

**Assessment:** Campaign ad analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Washington warned of the 'baneful effects of the spirit of party.' 2) First parties: Federalists and Democratic-Republicans. 3) Top-two primary: top two vote-getters advance regardless of party (Prop 14, 2010). 4) Citizens United (2010): no limits on independent expenditures by corporations/unions; direct contributions still capped. 5) 48% vs. 45% with ±3.5% is within the margin of error. Ad analysis rubric (4 levels): 4 = identifies technique (fear, bandwagon, testimonial, etc.), target audience, message, and evaluates effectiveness with historical context for all three ads.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Abstraction and expressive painting

**Prepare:** Large paper or canvas, acrylics, palette knives, wide brushes; music for Day 2. Images: Kandinsky (Composition VII, 1913), Frankenthaler (Mountains and Sea, 1952), Rothko (color field paintings, 1950s).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking abstraction is random. Strong abstraction uses deliberate composition, color, and contrast.
- Believing abstract art has no meaning. Artists express emotion, music, or ideas through non-representational form.
- Overworking an abstract piece until colors turn muddy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Abstraction research
*Student:* Compare works by Kandinsky, Helen Frankenthaler, and Mark Rothko; note how each conveys emotion without imagery.

*How to teach:* Compare Kandinsky (linked color and form to music; 'Concerning the Spiritual in Art,' 1911), Frankenthaler (soak-stain technique, thinned paint on raw canvas), Rothko (large soft-edged rectangles of color meant to evoke deep emotion).

- **Q:** What technique did Helen Frankenthaler pioneer?
  **A:** Soak-stain: pouring thinned paint onto unprimed canvas so it soaks into the fibers.
- **Q:** How did Kandinsky connect art and music?
  **A:** He believed colors and forms could act like musical tones and chords, evoking emotion without depicting objects.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Studies
*Student:* Make four small abstract studies based on a piece of music or an emotion.

*How to teach:* Four small abstract studies responding to a piece of music or an emotion.

- **Q:** Name two visual choices that can express calm vs. anxiety.
  **A:** Calm: horizontal lines, cool harmonious colors, soft edges. Anxiety: jagged diagonals, clashing saturated colors, crowded composition.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final abstraction
*Student:* Begin a larger abstract painting developed from your best study.

*How to teach:* Begin a larger abstraction from the best study.

- **Q:** What is color field painting?
  **A:** A style of Abstract Expressionism using large, flat areas of color to create an immersive, contemplative experience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish
*Student:* Complete the painting and finish your studio landscape from Week 10.

*How to teach:* Finish the abstraction and the Week 10 studio landscape.

- **Q:** How do you know when an abstract painting is finished?
  **A:** When the composition feels resolved — balance, focal areas, and color relationships work — and further marks would weaken it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit critique
*Student:* Hold a painting critique and select one painting for the portfolio.

*How to teach:* Painting critique (describe, analyze, interpret, evaluate); select one painting for the portfolio.

- **Q:** What is Abstract Expressionism?
  **A:** A post-WWII American movement (New York School) emphasizing spontaneous, gestural, or color-field abstraction to express emotion.

**Assessment:** Abstract painting and unit critique.

**Answer key:** Abstract painting and unit critique rubric. 4 – Abstraction shows clear intent (music/emotion), deliberate composition and color, developed from studies; critique uses all four steps with specific evidence; justified portfolio selection. 3 (proficient) – Intentional abstraction and complete critique. 2 – Little intention or partial critique. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Swimming or cycling skills

**Prepare:** Arrange public pool access with a lifeguard OR a bicycle, helmet, and safe empty parking lot/path. Review California bicycle laws (CVC): helmets required under 18; at night a white front light and red rear reflector (plus pedal/side reflectors); ride as far right as practicable in the same direction as traffic; drivers must give 3 feet when passing.

**Watch for:**
- (Swim) Lifting the head forward to breathe. Rotate to the side with the body roll.
- (Bike) Braking hard with only the front brake, which can pitch you over the bars. Use both brakes, rear slightly first, weight back.
- (Bike) Riding against traffic. California law requires riding with traffic.

### Day 1 (Monday): Technique
*Student:* Swim: practice freestyle breathing and kicks for 30 minutes; or Bike: practice shifting and braking drills in a safe lot.

*How to teach:* Swim: freestyle breathing drills (side breathing, kick with board). Bike: shifting (shift before hills, while pedaling lightly) and emergency braking drills.

- **Q:** When should a cyclist shift to an easier gear?
  **A:** Before the hill, while pedaling with light pressure — not mid-climb under heavy load.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Endurance
*Student:* Swim 20 continuous minutes or ride 45 minutes on a bike path.

*How to teach:* Swim 20 continuous minutes or ride 45 minutes on a path.

- **Q:** What cadence range is efficient for most cyclists?
  **A:** About 70–90 rpm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Run
*Student:* Do a 30-minute run with 4 hill repeats.

*How to teach:* 30-minute run with 4 hill repeats.

- **Q:** Cue for running uphill?
  **A:** Shorter steps, drive the arms, lean slightly from the ankles, keep effort (not pace) steady.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safety
*Student:* Learn California bike laws (helmet under 18, lights, lane use) or water-safety rescue skills; do a 30-minute session.

*How to teach:* Learn bike laws or water-rescue skills (reach-throw-don't-go); 30-minute session.

- **Q:** Is a 17-year-old required to wear a bicycle helmet in California?
  **A:** Yes — California requires helmets for riders under 18.
- **Q:** What lighting is required to ride at night in California?
  **A:** A white front light visible from 300 ft, a red rear reflector (or light), white/yellow pedal or shoe reflectors, and side reflectors.

### Day 5 (Friday): Intervals
*Student:* Swim 8 × 50 m or bike 6 × 2-minute hard efforts.

*How to teach:* Swim 8 × 50 m or bike 6 × 2-minute hard efforts with equal rest.

- **Q:** What is the work-to-rest ratio of 2 minutes hard and 2 minutes easy?
  **A:** 1:1.

**Assessment:** Technique self-check.

**Answer key:** Technique self-check: proficient (swim) = horizontal body, flutter kick from hips, rotation breathing without lifting head, 20 minutes continuous; (bike) = correct shifting before load, controlled stop using both brakes, shoulder check without swerving, correct hand signals, knowledge of CA helmet/light laws. 4 = all items plus self-analysis from video; 2 = several faults; 1 = unsafe.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Filing a return: W-2, 1040 & CA 540

**Prepare:** Download a sample W-2 and practice copies of IRS Form 1040 and CA Form 540 for the most recent tax year. Look up that year's standard deduction (single) and CalEITC income limits. Find IRS Free File, VITA locator and CalFile pages.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a refund is a bonus. A refund is your own over-withheld money returned without interest.
- Copying the wrong W-2 box. Box 1 (wages) goes on the 1040; Box 2 (federal withholding) is a payment; Boxes 16–17 are California wages and withholding.
- Not filing because income was low. You may still want to file to get back withholding and claim refundable credits like the EITC and CalEITC.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading a W-2
*Student:* Label each box on a sample W-2 and match boxes to the lines where they go on the 1040 and 540.

*How to teach:* Label each W-2 box and match it to 1040/540 lines.

- **Q:** Which W-2 box shows federal income tax withheld?
  **A:** Box 2.
- **Q:** Which boxes show Social Security and Medicare wages and tax?
  **A:** Boxes 3–4 (Social Security) and 5–6 (Medicare).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Form 1040
*Student:* Complete a practice Form 1040 for a single filer with one W-2 and the standard deduction; compute the refund or amount owed.

*How to teach:* Complete a practice 1040 for a single filer with one W-2 and the standard deduction.

- **Q:** Wages $30,000, standard deduction $15,000 (example). Taxable income?
  **A:** $15,000.
- **Q:** If tax is $1,600 and withholding is $2,000, what happens?
  **A:** A $400 refund.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): CA Form 540
*Student:* Complete a practice CA Form 540 using the same W-2 and check eligibility for the California Earned Income Tax Credit.

*How to teach:* Complete a practice CA 540 with the same W-2 and check CalEITC eligibility.

- **Q:** What is the CalEITC?
  **A:** A refundable California tax credit for low-income workers; you can receive it even if you owe no tax.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Filing options
*Student:* Compare IRS Free File, VITA sites, and the FTB's CalFile, and list documents to keep and how long to keep them.

*How to teach:* Compare IRS Free File, VITA sites and CalFile; list documents to keep and for how long.

- **Q:** How long should you keep tax records?
  **A:** At least 3 years for the IRS (longer in some cases); California's FTB generally can review for 4 years, so keep records at least 4 years.
- **Q:** What is VITA?
  **A:** Volunteer Income Tax Assistance — free tax preparation for people with low to moderate income.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Unit 4 assessment: file a complete practice federal and California return from a new W-2.

*How to teach:* Unit 4 assessment: complete a full practice federal and California return from a new W-2; check with the key.

- **Q:** What two numbers determine whether you get a refund?
  **A:** Your total tax liability and the total tax already paid/withheld (plus refundable credits).

**Assessment:** Accuracy of practice 1040 and 540 returns.

**Answer key:** Return accuracy check (new W-2): 1040 — Box 1 wages on line 1a; subtract the standard deduction for that year to get taxable income (not below $0); compute tax from the table; Box 2 withholding on line 25a; refund = withholding − tax (or amount owed if negative). Score 4 = every line correct; 3 = one arithmetic error; 2 = wrong deduction or wrong box; 1 = incomplete. Example (use your year's figures): wages $14,000, federal withholding $600, standard deduction larger than $14,000 → taxable income $0, tax $0, refund $600. CA 540 — California wages (Box 16), CA standard deduction, tax from the FTB table, minus personal exemption credit, withholding from Box 17 → refund or owed; check CalEITC eligibility (earned income under the year's limit, age 18+ or qualifying child).
