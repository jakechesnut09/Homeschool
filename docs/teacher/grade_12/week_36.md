# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 36

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Senior portfolio and final reflection

**Prepare:** Gather all writing from the year. Choose Google Sites or a binder. Schedule the final conference.

**Watch for:**
- Treating the portfolio as a collection only. Each piece needs a short note on why it was chosen.
- Cosmetic revision. Revise argument, structure, or evidence — not just word choice.
- Vague goals ('read more'). Make goals specific and measurable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Select work
*Student:* Choose five pieces for your senior portfolio: a PIQ, a literary analysis, a timed write, the research paper, and a creative piece.

*How to teach:* The student selects five pieces and writes a sentence for each explaining the choice. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What five pieces go in the portfolio?
  **A:** A PIQ, a literary analysis, a timed write, the research paper, and a creative piece.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise one piece
*Student:* Revise one earlier piece using everything you've learned this year and note changes.

*How to teach:* The student revises one earlier piece and lists the changes. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What makes a revision meaningful?
  **A:** Changes to thesis, structure, or evidence that improve the argument.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Letter to self
*Student:* Write a 'letter to my future self' narrative about the year's reading, using a quotation from a text that mattered to you.

*How to teach:* The student writes a 'letter to my future self' using a quotation from a text that mattered. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How should the quotation be integrated?
  **A:** Introduce it, quote it accurately with citation, and explain why it matters to them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish
*Student:* Publish the portfolio as a Google Site or bound binder with a reflective introduction.

*How to teach:* The student publishes the portfolio with a reflective introduction. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What belongs in the reflective introduction?
  **A:** An overview of growth with specific examples from the pieces.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final conference
*Student:* Present the portfolio in a final conference and set three reading/writing goals for college or career.

*How to teach:* Hold the final conference; the student sets three goals. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give a specific reading goal.
  **A:** 'Read one classic novel per month this summer and keep a one-page response for each.'

**Assessment:** Portfolio and reflective introduction scored with portfolio rubric.

**Answer key:** Portfolio rubric (each 1–4): Selection (five required pieces with rationales); Revision (substantive, documented); Reflection (specific evidence of growth); Presentation (organized, polished). Proficient: 12/16.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Final review and exam

**Prepare:** Assemble a cumulative review packet (OpenStax chapter reviews) and the final exam. Find a calculus-readiness diagnostic (e.g., MAA-style placement practice online).

**Watch for:**
- Studying only the most recent units. Use the practice final to target all.
- Not showing work on technology items.
- Treating the calculus diagnostic as a grade. It is a summer study plan.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: functions & trig
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of functions, logs, trig, vectors, and matrices.

*How to teach:* The student completes the functions/trig/vectors/matrices review; check and reteach top misses. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Find the inverse of [[2, 1], [5, 3]].
  **A:** [[3, −1], [−5, 2]]
- **Q:** Solve 2 sin x − 1 = 0 on [0, 2π).
  **A:** π/6, 5π/6

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: statistics
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of distributions, regression, probability, and inference.

*How to teach:* The student completes the statistics review. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Find P(disease | positive) for 1% prevalence, 90% sensitivity, 9% false positives.
  **A:** ≈ 0.092

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice final
*Student:* Take a timed practice final and correct mistakes.

*How to teach:* Give the timed practice final; the student corrects every error. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Interpret r² = 0.64.
  **A:** 64% of the variation in y is explained by the linear model.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Calculus readiness
*Student:* Take a calculus-readiness diagnostic and list topics to review this summer.

*How to teach:* Give the calculus-readiness diagnostic; the student writes a summer review list. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + x − 6).
  **A:** (x − 3)/(x − 2), x ≠ −3

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the cumulative final exam.

*How to teach:* Administer the cumulative final exam. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** State the 68-95-99.7 rule.
  **A:** In a normal distribution, about 68%, 95%, 99.7% of data lie within 1, 2, 3 SDs of the mean.

**Assessment:** Cumulative final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam sample key: 1) f(g(x)) = 4x² − 12x + 10. 2) Zeros of x³ − x² + 4x − 4: 1, ±2i. 3) 5e^(2x) = 40 → x ≈ 1.04. 4) sin 75° = (√6 + √2)/4. 5) SAS c ≈ 6.24. 6) Inverse of [[2, 1], [5, 3]]. 7) Σ(2k + 1), k = 1..5 = 35. 8) Ellipse foci (±4, 0). 9) Mean/median/IQR of 2, 4, 4, 5, 7, 9, 20. 10) z-scores 1.25 vs 1.4. 11) P(heart or king) = 4/13. 12) 10C3 = 120. 13) ME ≈ 3.1%. 14) lim_{x→3}(x² − 9)/(x − 3) = 6. Scoring: 90%+ A, 80–89% B, 70–79% C.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Capstone presentation and final

**Prepare:** Poster, review notes, concept map paper. Prepare the cumulative final (key below). Arrange the audience for the capstone presentation.

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing facts without connections. Use the concept map to link units.
- Skipping review of calculations (half-life, Kepler, logistic, footprint).
- Reflections without concrete actions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Present your capstone to family, co-op, or a local group and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present capstone and answer questions.

- **Q:** How do you handle a question about a limitation?
  **A:** Acknowledge it, explain its effect on conclusions, and suggest how future work could address it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review S2
*Student:* Review Units 6–9 with a concept map linking biodiversity, pollution, climate, and engineering.

*How to teach:* Concept map linking biodiversity, pollution, climate, engineering.

- **Q:** Link climate change to biodiversity with an example.
  **A:** Warming oceans and disease (e.g., sea-star wasting) contributed to kelp forest loss, reducing habitat for many species.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice test
*Student:* Take a practice final exam and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Practice final; correct errors.

- **Q:** What is the MSY in a logistic model with r = 0.4, K = 2,000?
  **A:** rK/4 = 200 per year.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the cumulative final exam.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative final.

- **Q:** State the main cause of current global warming.
  **A:** Increased greenhouse gases (mainly CO₂ from burning fossil fuels) enhancing the greenhouse effect.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on the most important environmental action you will take as an adult.

*How to teach:* Reflection on the most important environmental action as an adult.

- **Q:** What makes an action plan strong?
  **A:** It is specific, measurable, and based on evidence of impact.

**Assessment:** Capstone presentation and final exam.

**Answer key:** Capstone presentation rubric: proficient = clear question, methods, data graphs, conclusion with uncertainty, recommendation, answers questions accurately. Cumulative final key (sample): 1) Fusion/E = mc². 2) Big Bang evidence. 3) Star life cycles/nucleosynthesis. 4) Kepler's laws. 5) Earth formation/half-life. 6) Plate tectonics evidence and boundaries. 7) Seismic evidence for interior. 8) Feedbacks. 9) Energy budget/greenhouse/Milankovitch. 10) Carbon cycle. 11) Coevolution (GOE). 12) Hazards and resources. 13) Biodiversity, keystone, HIPPO. 14) Logistic/MSY. 15) Water and air pollution. 16) Climate evidence, models, California impacts, mitigation policy. 17) Engineering design process. 90%+ advanced, 75–89% proficient.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Economics review and final

**Prepare:** Write three review sets (micro, macro, trade) and the Economics final (about 40 items plus a graph-based short essay). Use AP Macro/Micro free-response questions (College Board, free) for practice.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up micro and macro tools. Micro studies individual markets; macro studies the whole economy (GDP, inflation, unemployment, policy).
- Shifting the wrong curve. Ask: does this change buyers' willingness (demand) or sellers' costs (supply)?
- Reversing exchange-rate effects. Re-derive: a stronger dollar makes U.S. exports more expensive abroad.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review micro
*Student:* Complete a review set on opportunity cost, supply and demand, and market structures.

*How to teach:* The student completes the micro review; check graphs immediately. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Demand for electric cars rises while battery costs fall. Effect on price and quantity?
  **A:** Quantity rises; price change is indeterminate (demand pushes up, supply pushes down).
- **Q:** Define opportunity cost.
  **A:** The value of the next-best alternative given up.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review macro
*Student:* Complete a review set on GDP, unemployment, inflation, fiscal and monetary policy.

*How to teach:* The student completes the macro review. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Nominal GDP $26T, deflator 130. Real GDP?
  **A:** $20 trillion.
- **Q:** What would the Fed do during a recession?
  **A:** Lower the federal funds rate (and possibly buy securities) to encourage borrowing and spending.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review trade
*Student:* Complete a review set on comparative advantage, tariffs, and exchange rates.

*How to teach:* The student completes the trade review. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Define comparative advantage.
  **A:** The ability to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another producer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a timed practice final and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Give a timed practice final; the student corrects every error with an explanation. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Fiscal or monetary: Congress cuts income taxes.
  **A:** Fiscal policy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the Economics final exam.

*How to teach:* Administer the Economics final. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** A $2 tariff is placed on a $10 imported good. What happens to the domestic price and imports?
  **A:** The price rises toward $12 and imports fall.

**Assessment:** Economics final exam.

**Answer key:** Economics final key: micro — opportunity cost, PPF, marginal analysis, demand/supply shifts, elasticity (midpoint), equilibrium, ceilings/floors, market structures, externalities; macro — GDP (C+I+G+NX), real vs. nominal, unemployment rate and types, CPI inflation, fiscal vs. monetary policy, money multiplier, yield curve; trade — comparative advantage, tariffs, exchange rates. Scoring: 90%+ A, 80–89% B, 70–79% C; graph essay scored 0–4 for correct shift, labels, and explanation.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Art beyond high school

**Prepare:** Internet access; local community college catalog; list of local open studios or life-drawing groups. Portfolio for the conference.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking art careers mean only fine artist. Many paths use art skills (design, illustration, therapy, conservation, teaching).
- Stopping art after high school without a plan.
- Summer plans without goals or schedules.

### Day 1 (Monday): Art careers
*Student:* Research five art careers (illustrator, art therapist, UX designer, conservator, teacher) and their pathways.

*How to teach:* Research five careers and pathways.

- **Q:** What training does an art therapist need?
  **A:** Typically a master's degree in art therapy plus supervised clinical hours and credentialing (e.g., ATR through the Art Therapy Credentials Board).
- **Q:** What does a conservator do?
  **A:** Examines, preserves, and restores artworks using scientific and art-historical knowledge; usually requires a graduate degree.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Community
*Student:* Find local open studios, life-drawing groups, or community-college art classes.

*How to teach:* Find local open studios, life-drawing groups, or community-college classes.

- **Q:** Why join a community art group?
  **A:** Regular practice, feedback, models/resources, and connection with other artists.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Summer project
*Student:* Plan a summer art project with goals and a schedule.

*How to teach:* Plan a summer project with goals and schedule.

- **Q:** What makes a summer art plan realistic?
  **A:** Specific goals, weekly schedule, materials budget, and a finish date.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final piece
*Student:* Create a final sketchbook spread summarizing your year.

*How to teach:* Create a final sketchbook spread summarizing the year.

- **Q:** What could a summary spread include?
  **A:** Key images, inquiry question, quotes from reflections, and goals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Portfolio conference
*Student:* Present your final portfolio and plan in a conference.

*How to teach:* Portfolio conference: present final portfolio and plan.

- **Q:** What is an artist residency?
  **A:** A program providing artists time, space, and sometimes funding to create work, often away from home.

**Assessment:** Final portfolio conference.

**Answer key:** Final portfolio conference rubric. 4 – Polished portfolio (online and physical), confident presentation of inquiry and growth, researched career/education plan, community resources, specific summer project. 3 (proficient) – Complete portfolio and clear plan. 2 – Incomplete materials or vague plan. 1 – Not prepared.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Launch plan & course capstone

**Prepare:** Gather the Unit 7 capstone budget, aid or job offers, and a presentation tool. Check deadlines: UC and CSU freshman Statements of Intent to Register are typically due May 1. Invite a guest for the final presentation.

**Watch for:**
- Missing post-acceptance steps. Accepting an offer requires the Statement of Intent to Register (and deposit/waiver), housing applications, orientation and final transcripts.
- Forgetting adult obligations. Men must register with Selective Service within 30 days of turning 18; voter registration, health insurance and important documents also need attention.
- Treating the budget as finished. Update it with real aid, wages and costs and revisit it every few months.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final decisions
*Student:* Complete enrollment or job acceptance steps (Statement of Intent to Register, housing, orientation) or a gap-year plan with milestones.

*How to teach:* Complete enrollment or job acceptance steps (or a gap-year plan with milestones).

- **Q:** What is a Statement of Intent to Register?
  **A:** The UC/CSU form (with deposit or waiver) a student submits to accept an admission offer; typically due May 1 for freshmen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): First-year budget
*Student:* Update the Unit 7 life budget with actual aid, wages, and costs for your first year after graduation.

*How to teach:* Update the Unit 7 life budget with actual aid, wages and costs.

- **Q:** Your aid covers $8,000 of a $12,000 housing cost. How much per month over 9 months must you cover?
  **A:** $4,000 ÷ 9 ≈ $444 per month.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adulting checklist
*Student:* Complete a checklist: voter registration, Selective Service if applicable, health insurance, bank accounts, credit freeze, and important documents.

*How to teach:* Complete the adulting checklist.

- **Q:** Who must register with Selective Service, and when?
  **A:** Most men ages 18–25 living in the U.S., within 30 days of turning 18.
- **Q:** At what age can Californians pre-register to vote?
  **A:** 16 (registration becomes active at 18).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Portfolio
*Student:* Assemble a digital portfolio with resume, transcript, PIQs, budget, and career report.

*How to teach:* Assemble the digital portfolio.

- **Q:** What documents belong in the portfolio?
  **A:** Resume, transcript, PIQs, budget and career report (plus work samples).

### Day 5 (Friday): Final presentation
*Student:* Present your launch plan and portfolio to family and a guest; take the course final reflection.

*How to teach:* Present the launch plan and portfolio; complete the final reflection.

- **Q:** Name one financial habit you'll keep from this course and why.
  **A:** Answers vary; strong answers are specific (e.g. autopay full credit card balance to avoid interest and build credit).

**Assessment:** Capstone presentation and portfolio.

**Answer key:** Capstone presentation and portfolio rubric (4 levels): 4 = clear launch plan (college/job/gap year) with completed enrollment or acceptance steps and dates; first-year budget updated with actual aid and wages and balanced; adulting checklist complete (voter registration, Selective Service if applicable, health insurance plan, bank accounts, credit freeze, secure copies of birth certificate, Social Security card, ID); digital portfolio with resume, transcript, PIQs, budget and career report; confident 10-minute presentation and thoughtful answers; final reflection names specific growth; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing major components; 1 = incomplete.
