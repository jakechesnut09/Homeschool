# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 18

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Semester 1 synthesis and final

**Prepare:** Gather notes, vocabulary logs, and the writing portfolio. Write the semester final: 3 short passages (Beowulf, Macbeth, Frankenstein) with multiple-choice and short-answer items, plus a synthesis essay prompt.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up literary periods. Anchor each text to a date and one trait (e.g., Beowulf — Anglo-Saxon, oral heroic code).
- Defining 'overreacher' too broadly. An overreacher pursues power or knowledge beyond human limits and is destroyed by it.
- Writing a portfolio reflection as a list. Compare a specific early sentence or paragraph with a later one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Timeline review
*Student:* Build a literary timeline from Anglo-Saxon to Romantic periods, placing each text read with one defining trait of its period.

*How to teach:* The student builds a timeline placing each text with one defining trait of its period. Check order and traits orally. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Put in order with periods: Frankenstein, Beowulf, Macbeth, The Canterbury Tales, A Modest Proposal.
  **A:** Beowulf (Anglo-Saxon), Canterbury Tales (Middle English/Medieval), Macbeth (Renaissance/Elizabethan–Jacobean), A Modest Proposal (Enlightenment/Neoclassical, 1729), Frankenstein (Romantic, 1818).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Archetype seminar
*Student:* Discuss the 'overreacher' archetype across Beowulf, Macbeth, and Victor Frankenstein; take notes for the final.

*How to teach:* Discuss the overreacher archetype across Beowulf, Macbeth, and Victor. The student takes notes with one quotation per character.

- **Q:** How is Macbeth an overreacher?
  **A:** He seizes the crown beyond his rightful place, and his ambition destroys him.
- **Q:** Is Beowulf an overreacher?
  **A:** Arguably in his final fight: as an old king he insists on fighting the dragon largely alone, dies, and leaves his people exposed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Vocabulary & grammar review
*Student:* Review the semester's vocabulary logs and grammar lessons with a practice test on appositives, pronouns, and punctuation.

*How to teach:* The student takes a practice test on appositives, pronoun reference, and semicolons/colons; review errors immediately. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Punctuate: Victor a scientist from Geneva abandons his creation
  **A:** Victor, a scientist from Geneva, abandons his creation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the semester final: multiple choice on passages, short answers, and a 45-minute synthesis essay comparing two works.

*How to teach:* Administer the final (about 2 hours). Grade using the key. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name two traits of Romanticism.
  **A:** Emphasis on emotion and imagination, and on nature and the sublime (also the individual).

### Day 5 (Friday): Portfolio reflection
*Student:* Select your two best pieces of writing and write a 400-word reflection on how your writing changed this semester.

*How to teach:* The student selects two best pieces and writes a 400-word reflection comparing early and later writing. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What makes a strong portfolio reflection?
  **A:** Specific before/after examples and an explanation of what skill improved.

**Assessment:** Semester final exam and portfolio reflection.

**Answer key:** Final exam key: passages identified (Beowulf, Macbeth, Frankenstein) with period traits; short answers drawn from weekly quizzes (e.g., Hrothgar/Heorot; 'vaulting ambition'; Walton's frame). Synthesis essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = thesis comparing two works, balanced evidence, analysis of period/archetype; 3 = clear comparison; 2 = summary; 1 = one work. Portfolio reflection rubric: 4 = specific examples of growth; 2 = general statements.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Semester 1 review and final exam

**Prepare:** Assemble a review packet from OpenStax chapter reviews (chapters 1, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10) and set up Khan Academy precalculus unit tests. Write the semester final (sample key below).

**Watch for:**
- Rushing through review without error analysis. Have the student label each missed problem by topic and cause (concept, careless, or time).
- Memorizing procedures without checking. Require a check: plug back in, graph in Desmos, or estimate.
- Treating the practice exam score as the goal. Its purpose is to find the two weakest topics.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: functions
*Student:* Complete a review set on transformations, inverses, polynomials, rationals, and logs.

*How to teach:* The student completes the function review; check answers together and reteach the most-missed type. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Find the inverse of f(x) = 2ˣ − 3.
  **A:** y + 3 = 2ˣ → f⁻¹(x) = log₂(x + 3).
- **Q:** Find the horizontal asymptote of (3x² + 1)/(x² − 4).
  **A:** y = 3

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: trig
*Student:* Complete a review set on the unit circle, sinusoids, identities, and triangle laws.

*How to teach:* The student completes the trig review; ask them to explain one unit-circle value and one triangle problem aloud. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Evaluate cos(11π/6).
  **A:** √3/2
- **Q:** Write a cosine model with amplitude 2, period π, midline y = 3.
  **A:** y = 2 cos(2x) + 3

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a timed practice exam and analyze errors by topic.

*How to teach:* Give a timed practice exam (90 minutes). Grade it and have the student tally errors by topic. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Solve e^(x) = 20.
  **A:** x = ln 20 ≈ 3.00

### Day 4 (Thursday): Targeted practice
*Student:* Do Khan Academy practice on your two weakest topics from the practice exam.

*How to teach:* Assign Khan Academy practice on the two weakest topics until 'Proficient.' Sit with the student for the first few problems.

- **Q:** What does 'error analysis' tell you about a missed problem?
  **A:** Whether the miss was a concept gap, a careless slip, or a time issue — which tells you what to practice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester final
*Student:* Take the cumulative Semester 1 final exam.

*How to teach:* Administer the cumulative Semester 1 final (2 hours, scientific calculator; Desmos for marked items). Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** State the Law of Cosines.
  **A:** c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

**Assessment:** Semester 1 final exam.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 final sample key: 1) Domain of √(x − 3)/(x − 5): [3, 5) ∪ (5, ∞). 2) f(g(x)) for f = x² + 1, g = 2x − 3: 4x² − 12x + 10. 3) Inverse of (2x + 1)/3: (3x − 1)/2. 4) Zeros of x³ − x² + 4x − 4: 1, ±2i. 5) Asymptotes of (x² − 1)/(x² − x − 2): VA x = 2, HA y = 1, hole x = −1. 6) 5e^(2x) = 40 → x ≈ 1.04. 7) C-14 30% → ≈ 9950 yr. 8) sin(5π/6) = 1/2. 9) y = 3cos(πx/6) + 2 from max 5, min −1, period 12. 10) sin 75° = (√6 + √2)/4. 11) SSS 7, 8, 9: largest angle ≈ 73.4°. 12) (1 + i)⁸ = 16. 13) Plane problem: ≈ 202.2 mph. Scoring: 90%+ A, 80–89% B, 70–79% C; below 70% schedule targeted review.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Semester 1 review and final

**Prepare:** Notes, lab notebook, large paper for concept map, CK-12 Earth Science readings for weak areas. Prepare a data-analysis review set (graphs from each unit) and the semester final.

**Watch for:**
- Studying by rereading only. Use practice questions and explain answers aloud.
- Concept maps without linking words. Every arrow needs a verb phrase.
- Ignoring units in calculations.

### Day 1 (Monday): Concept map
*Student:* Build a semester concept map linking stars, Earth formation, plate tectonics, climate, and resources.

*How to teach:* Build a concept map linking stars → elements → Earth formation → plate tectonics → climate → resources.

- **Q:** Give one linking statement between stars and plate tectonics.
  **A:** Example: Supernovae produced radioactive elements (U, Th, K) whose decay heats Earth's interior, driving mantle convection and plate motion.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Data practice
*Student:* Complete a data-analysis review set using graphs from each unit.

*How to teach:* Complete a data-analysis set (H-R diagram, Hubble plot, seafloor age, ice cores, Keeling Curve).

- **Q:** A graph of velocity vs. distance for galaxies is a straight line with slope 70 km/s/Mpc. What does the slope represent?
  **A:** The Hubble constant — the expansion rate of the universe.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice test
*Student:* Take a practice exam and target weak areas with CK-12 readings.

*How to teach:* Practice exam; target weak areas with CK-12 readings.

- **Q:** What is the half-life if 1/16 of a parent remains after 20,000 years?
  **A:** 4 half-lives in 20,000 years → 5,000 years per half-life.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lab review
*Student:* Review your lab notebook and summarize the claim and evidence from five investigations.

*How to teach:* Summarize claim and evidence from five investigations in the lab notebook.

- **Q:** What made your best lab conclusion strong?
  **A:** Answers vary; it should cite specific data, explain the scientific reasoning, and note error sources.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester final
*Student:* Take the Semester 1 final exam.

*How to teach:* Give the Semester 1 final.

- **Q:** What evidence shows the outer core is liquid?
  **A:** The S-wave shadow zone — S-waves can't pass through liquid.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 final exam.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 final key (sample): 1) Fusion and E = mc². 2) Big Bang evidence (3). 3) H-R diagram/star life cycles. 4) Element origins. 5) Kepler's laws and T² = a³. 6) v = √(GM/r). 7) Solar nebula; meteorite age 4.57 Ga; half-life. 8) Plate tectonics evidence; spreading rate. 9) Relative dating laws. 10) Seismic waves and interior. 11) Boundaries; San Andreas transform. 12) Water's properties; weathering. 13) Feedbacks; albedo. 14) Energy budget; greenhouse; Milankovitch. 15) Carbon cycle numbers. 16) GOE. 17) Hazards vs. risk; liquefaction. 18) Resources; subsidence. 19) Capacity factor calc. 90%+ advanced, 75–89% proficient.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Government semester review and final

**Prepare:** Write a review guide and the Government final: 30 multiple-choice/short-answer items plus a DBQ on an enduring constitutional tension (e.g., liberty vs. security) with 5–6 documents (Federalist 51, Bill of Rights, Tinker, King's Letter, a modern case).

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing case names without holdings. Make cards with name, year, question, holding, significance.
- Mixing up branch powers. Sort powers into a three-column chart from memory.
- Treating a DBQ as document summary. Each document must be used to support the thesis, with outside knowledge.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: foundations & rights
*Student:* Create a one-page study guide on founding principles and the Bill of Rights.

*How to teach:* The student creates a one-page study guide on founding principles and the Bill of Rights. Quiz them orally from it. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name the five First Amendment freedoms.
  **A:** Religion, speech, press, assembly, petition.
- **Q:** Which thinker's ideas appear in the Declaration's second paragraph?
  **A:** John Locke's.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: branches & courts
*Student:* Complete a review set matching powers to branches and cases to holdings.

*How to teach:* The student completes a matching set of powers to branches and cases to holdings. Correct together. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Match: Marbury v. Madison, McCulloch v. Maryland, Gideon v. Wainwright.
  **A:** Judicial review; implied powers and federal supremacy; right to counsel in state felony cases.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Civic action presentation
*Student:* Present your civic action project in 5 minutes.

*How to teach:* The student presents the civic action project in 5 minutes. Ask two follow-up questions. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What makes a strong civic action presentation?
  **A:** Clear issue, action taken with evidence, results, and lessons learned.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice exam
*Student:* Retake the USCIS civics test and complete a practice exam.

*How to teach:* The student retakes the USCIS civics test and completes a practice exam; review misses. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is the supreme law of the land?
  **A:** The Constitution.
- **Q:** How many amendments does the Constitution have?
  **A:** 27.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester final
*Student:* Take the Government semester final with a DBQ essay on an enduring constitutional tension.

*How to teach:* Administer the final with DBQ. Grade with the key. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give an enduring constitutional tension.
  **A:** Liberty vs. order/security, majority rule vs. minority rights, or federal vs. state power.

**Assessment:** Government final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam key: foundations (Locke, Magna Carta, Federalist 10/51/78); Bill of Rights and incorporation; Congress (terms, powers, lawmaking, veto override 2/3); president (powers, executive orders); courts (certiorari, Marbury, McCulloch, Nixon, Brown, Tinker, Miranda, Gideon); elections (top-two, Electoral College 538/270); federalism and California government. DBQ rubric (4 levels): 4 = clear thesis on the tension, uses 4+ documents accurately, outside knowledge, analysis of how the tension has been balanced over time; 3 = solid with 3 documents; 2 = summary; 1 = minimal.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Mixed media and semester review

**Prepare:** Gel medium (matte), laser prints or photocopies of own photos (inkjet doesn't transfer well), paper and wood scraps, brushes, sponge, found objects, paint. Space to lay out all semester work.

**Watch for:**
- Using inkjet prints for gel transfers. Laser/toner prints transfer best.
- Peeling paper before the gel dries. Let it dry fully (several hours or overnight) before rubbing off the paper.
- Mixed media as random collage. Each material should carry meaning for the inquiry.

### Day 1 (Monday): Image transfer
*Student:* Practice gel-medium image transfers of your own photos onto paper and wood.

*How to teach:* Practice transfers: coat surface and print face with gel, press face-down, dry, dampen and rub off paper. Remember the image reverses.

- **Q:** Why is a gel-medium transfer reversed?
  **A:** The print is placed face-down, so the image appears as a mirror image.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mixed-media piece
*Student:* Create a mixed-media work for your investigation combining transfer, paint, and drawing.

*How to teach:* Create a mixed-media work combining transfer, paint, and drawing.

- **Q:** What is assemblage?
  **A:** Three-dimensional art made by combining found objects.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish
*Student:* Complete the mixed-media piece.

*How to teach:* Complete the piece.

- **Q:** How can materials carry meaning?
  **A:** Their history and associations (e.g., old letters suggest memory) add content beyond the image.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review
*Student:* Lay out all semester work and select your top 8 pieces.

*How to teach:* Lay out all work; select top 8.

- **Q:** What criteria help select top pieces?
  **A:** Technical skill, strength of idea, connection to inquiry, and variety of approaches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester critique
*Student:* Write a semester self-evaluation and present your top pieces.

*How to teach:* Semester self-evaluation and presentation.

- **Q:** What is the difference between collage and mixed media?
  **A:** Collage glues flat materials onto a surface; mixed media combines any different media (paint, drawing, collage, objects) in one work.

**Assessment:** Semester portfolio review.

**Answer key:** Semester portfolio review rubric. 4 – Top 8 selected with written justification, mixed-media piece integrates transfer/paint/drawing meaningfully, self-evaluation cites specific growth and goals, at least 5 investigation pieces documented. 3 (proficient) – Top 8 selected, self-evaluation with evidence, investigation on track. 2 – Selection without reasoning. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Semester reflection and new plan

**Prepare:** Fitness log, semester data. Prepare the semester exam (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Reflections that list workouts without analysis.
- New goals that are identical to old ones.
- Plans without a lifetime sport or enjoyment component.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on your semester training and what motivated you; do a 30-minute workout.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on training and motivation; 30-minute workout.

- **Q:** What motivates long-term activity best?
  **A:** Intrinsic motivation — enjoyment, competence, and personal meaning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): New goals
*Student:* Write new SMART goals for semester 2.

*How to teach:* Write new SMART goals for semester 2.

- **Q:** Give a semester-2 SMART goal involving a lifetime sport.
  **A:** Example: 'Play pickleball at open play twice a week and reach a 15-dink rally by March 1.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise plan
*Student:* Revise your weekly plan to include strength, cardio, and a lifetime sport.

*How to teach:* Revise weekly plan: strength 2–3 days, cardio 3+ days, a lifetime sport.

- **Q:** Does a plan with 3 × 30 min jogging and 2 strength days meet adult aerobic guidelines?
  **A:** Not quite at moderate intensity (90 min < 150); at vigorous intensity, 90 min exceeds the 75-minute vigorous minimum.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Choice workout
*Student:* Lead a family member through a workout you designed.

*How to teach:* Lead a family member through a workout.

- **Q:** What should every led workout include?
  **A:** Warm-up, main set with modifications, cool-down, and clear cues.

### Day 5 (Friday): Written exam
*Student:* Take the semester written exam on fitness concepts.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam.

- **Q:** Karvonen: age 18, rest 60, 70%?
  **A:** HRmax 202, HRR 142; 0.7 × 142 + 60 = 159.4 ≈ 159 bpm.

**Assessment:** Semester exam and revised plan.

**Answer key:** Semester exam key: 1) Five components of health-related fitness. 2) FITT. 3) Overload, progression, specificity, reversibility. 4) Karvonen calculation. 5) Volume = sets × reps × load. 6) Strength vs. endurance rep ranges. 7) Aerobic vs. anaerobic; VO2 max; lactate threshold. 8) 10% rule, taper. 9) Static vs. dynamic vs. PNF. 10) Macronutrients and kcal/g; sweat replacement 16–24 oz per lb. 11) Teen sleep 8–10 h. 12) BMI limits. Revised plan rubric: proficient = new SMART goals plus weekly plan with strength, cardio meeting guidelines, flexibility, and a lifetime sport.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Renting your first apartment & semester capstone

**Prepare:** Gather rental listings near a chosen college or job, a sample California lease, and CA Courts Self-Help tenant pages. Know current rules: since July 1, 2024 (AB 12), most landlords may charge a security deposit of no more than one month's rent (small landlords owning no more than two properties with four total units may charge up to two months); deposits must be returned with an itemized statement within 21 days after move-out. Prepare or assign the NGPF post-assessment/semester final.

**Watch for:**
- Applying the 30% rule to gross vs. net pay inconsistently. The 30% guideline usually uses gross income; on net pay rent at 30% is even tighter.
- Forgetting move-in costs. First month's rent, security deposit, application fees, utilities setup and furniture add up quickly.
- Signing a lease without reading it. Note late fees, repair responsibilities, guest rules, and termination terms.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rental search
*Student:* Find three rentals near a chosen college or job, apply the 30% rule, and calculate total move-in costs.

*How to teach:* Find three rentals, apply the 30% rule and compute move-in costs.

- **Q:** Gross monthly income $2,800. Max rent under the 30% rule?
  **A:** $840.
- **Q:** Rent $1,100, deposit one month, $50 application fee. Move-in cost?
  **A:** $1,100 + $1,100 + $50 = $2,250.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading a lease
*Student:* Annotate a sample California lease for rent, deposit, late fees, repairs, and termination; list your tenant rights and the legal security deposit return timeline.

*How to teach:* Annotate a sample California lease for rent, deposit, late fees, repairs, termination; list tenant rights and the 21-day deposit return rule.

- **Q:** How long does a California landlord have to return a security deposit?
  **A:** 21 days after move-out, with an itemized statement of any deductions.
- **Q:** What is the maximum security deposit for most California rentals?
  **A:** One month's rent (since July 1, 2024), with an exception allowing two months for certain small landlords.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Roommates
*Student:* Write a roommate agreement covering rent split, utilities, guests, and chores.

*How to teach:* Write a roommate agreement: rent split, utilities, guests, chores, quiet hours, what happens if someone moves out.

- **Q:** Why put a roommate agreement in writing?
  **A:** It sets clear expectations and gives a reference if disagreements arise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Capstone budget
*Student:* Build a complete first-year independent life budget combining a job's net pay, rent, insurance, transportation, savings, and debt payments.

*How to teach:* Build the complete first-year independent life budget.

- **Q:** What categories must the life budget include?
  **A:** Net pay, rent, utilities, food, transportation, insurance, phone, savings, debt payments and some fun money.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester final
*Student:* Take the NGPF post-assessment/semester final and present your capstone budget to a parent.

*How to teach:* Give the semester final and have the student present the capstone budget.

- **Q:** What is one change you'd make to your budget if rent rose by $150?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g. find a roommate, cut wants, or increase income.

**Assessment:** Semester final exam and life-budget capstone.

**Answer key:** Semester final: score with the NGPF post-assessment key (target 80%+). Capstone budget rubric: 4 = realistic net pay from a researched job; rent at or near 30% of gross income (or with roommates); utilities, food, transportation, insurance (health, renters, auto), phone, savings (emergency fund and retirement), debt payments; balanced; brief presentation answering parent questions; 3 = minor omissions; 2 = unrealistic or unbalanced; 1 = incomplete. Rental check example: gross pay $3,000/month → 30% rule max rent $900; move-in for $1,200 rent with one-month deposit = $1,200 + $1,200 = $2,400 plus fees.
