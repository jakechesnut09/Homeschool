# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 35

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Senior research defense

**Prepare:** Invite at least two adults for the panel and print scoring sheets (content, delivery, visuals, Q&A). Choose a TED talk to evaluate. Find a local paper's op-ed guidelines.

**Watch for:**
- Defending rather than engaging during Q&A. Acknowledge good points and explain with evidence.
- Evaluating a speaker only on style. Assess reasoning and evidence too.
- Writing the op-ed like an academic paper. Use a hook, short paragraphs, plain language, and one clear argument.

### Day 1 (Monday): Defense day
*Student:* Present your research to a panel of at least two adults (family, co-op, or community mentor) and answer 5 minutes of questions.

*How to teach:* The student presents to the panel and answers 5 minutes of questions. Panelists score with the rubric. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How should the student answer a question they can't fully answer?
  **A:** Acknowledge the limit and explain how they would investigate it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluate a speaker
*Student:* Watch a TED talk or classmate presentation and write an evaluation of its reasoning, evidence, and rhetoric.

*How to teach:* Watch the talk. The student evaluates reasoning, evidence, and rhetoric in a page. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What three features should the evaluation address?
  **A:** Reasoning (logic), evidence (quality and sources), and rhetoric (ethos, pathos, logos).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Panel feedback
*Student:* Review panel scoring sheets and write a response to the toughest question you received.

*How to teach:* Review the panel sheets together. The student writes a full response to the toughest question. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why write a response after the defense?
  **A:** To give a fuller, evidence-based answer and show reflection.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Op-ed
*Student:* Turn your research into a 650-word op-ed for a general audience and submit it to a local paper or blog.

*How to teach:* The student writes a 650-word op-ed for a general audience and submits it. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How does an op-ed differ from a research paper?
  **A:** It is short, opens with a hook, uses plain language, and makes one clear argument with a call to action.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on the full research process from question to defense.

*How to teach:* The student writes a reflection on the research process from question to defense. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What should the reflection include?
  **A:** Key decisions, challenges, growth, and what they'd do differently.

**Assessment:** Defense scored by panel rubric; op-ed draft.

**Answer key:** Defense panel rubric (each 1–4): Content (clear thesis, evidence); Delivery (pacing, eye contact); Visuals (clear, sourced); Q&A (thoughtful, evidence-based). Op-ed rubric (4 levels): 4 = about 650 words, hook, clear claim, 2+ pieces of evidence, plain style, call to action.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Statistics capstone: analysis and presentation

**Prepare:** Slides tool (Google Slides or similar), CODAP/Desmos. Arrange an audience for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Overstating conclusions (claiming cause from observational data).
- Too many graphs in a presentation. Choose the three that answer the question.
- Listing limitations generically. Tie each to the study.

### Day 1 (Monday): Model
*Student:* Fit a regression or run a simulation-based comparison appropriate to your question.

*How to teach:* The student chooses a regression or simulation comparison that fits the question and runs it; check residuals or shuffle distribution.

- **Q:** When is a randomization test better than regression?
  **A:** When comparing two groups on a quantitative response rather than relating two quantitative variables.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interpret
*Student:* Interpret results in context and list at least three limitations.

*How to teach:* The student writes interpretations in context and lists at least three limitations. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give an example of a well-worded conclusion from observational data.
  **A:** 'Counties with higher median income tended to have lower PM2.5 (r = −0.4), but this association doesn't show income causes cleaner air.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Write report
*Student:* Write a 4–6 page statistical report with graphs, methods, and conclusions.

*How to teach:* The student drafts the 4–6 page report (intro, methods, results with graphs, conclusion, limitations). Review a draft section.

- **Q:** What belongs in Methods?
  **A:** Data source, sampling, variables, cleaning, and the analysis used.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build presentation
*Student:* Create a 7-minute presentation with your three most important visuals.

*How to teach:* Build a 7-minute presentation with three key visuals; rehearse once with a timer. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What makes a good presentation graph?
  **A:** Titled, labeled axes with units, readable fonts, one clear message.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present the capstone to family or co-op and answer questions.

*How to teach:* The student presents and answers questions. Use the rubric below. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What should the student do if they can't answer a question?
  **A:** Acknowledge it and explain how they could find out.

**Assessment:** Capstone report and presentation.

**Answer key:** Rubric (4 levels): Report — 4 = clear question, documented methods, correct analysis with appropriate graphs, conclusions in context matching study type, 3+ specific limitations; 3 = minor errors; 2 = analysis inappropriate or conclusions overstated; 1 = incomplete. Presentation — 4 = within 7 min, 3 clear visuals, confident answers; 3 = slightly over time or one weak visual; 2 = disorganized; 1 = not delivered.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Capstone: analysis and solution

**Prepare:** Data, graphs, poster board or slide software; contact information for a local official, school, or water district.

**Watch for:**
- Claiming the data 'prove' the hypothesis. Data support or fail to support it.
- Recommendations not tied to findings.
- Letters that are too technical or too long for stakeholders.

### Day 1 (Monday): Analyze
*Student:* Interpret patterns in the data and state whether evidence supports the hypothesis.

*How to teach:* Interpret patterns; state whether evidence supports the hypothesis.

- **Q:** Shaded sites averaged 31 °C and unshaded 41 °C surface temperature. What do you conclude?
  **A:** Data support the hypothesis that tree cover lowers surface temperature (by ~10 °C in this sample), with limits from sample size and conditions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Propose
*Student:* Propose a local solution with trade-offs evaluated against criteria.

*How to teach:* Propose a local solution and evaluate trade-offs.

- **Q:** Give a trade-off of planting more street trees.
  **A:** Benefits: shade, cooling, habitat. Costs: water use, maintenance, root damage to sidewalks, leaf litter.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Write
*Student:* Write the capstone report (introduction, methods, results, discussion, recommendation).

*How to teach:* Write the report: introduction, methods, results, discussion, recommendation.

- **Q:** What belongs in the discussion section?
  **A:** Interpretation of results, comparison to sources, limitations and errors, and implications.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Stakeholder letter
*Student:* Write a letter to a city council member, school, or water district sharing your findings.

*How to teach:* Write a stakeholder letter.

- **Q:** What makes a stakeholder letter effective?
  **A:** Brief, respectful, clear finding with one key graph or number, specific request, and contact information.

### Day 5 (Friday): Poster
*Student:* Create a science-fair-style poster or slide deck.

*How to teach:* Create poster or slides.

- **Q:** What is the most important element of a science poster?
  **A:** A clear main finding (graph and conclusion) readable at a glance.

**Assessment:** Capstone report and stakeholder letter.

**Answer key:** Capstone report and stakeholder letter rubric. 4 – Full report with all sections, data-based conclusion with uncertainty, solution with trade-offs, polished letter with a specific ask, poster communicates key finding. 3 (proficient) – Complete report and letter with data support. 2 – Missing sections or weak links to data. 1 – Incomplete.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Economics capstone: policy analysis

**Prepare:** Browse the Legislative Analyst's Office (lao.ca.gov) and CBO (cbo.gov) for reports on housing, minimum wage, gas tax, or tariffs. Bookmark FRED and BLS. Arrange an audience for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a policy too broad ('fix the economy'). Pick one specific policy with an LAO or CBO analysis.
- Graphs that shift the wrong curve. A tax on sellers shifts supply left; a subsidy to buyers shifts demand right.
- Recommendations without trade-offs. Name who gains and who loses.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a policy
*Student:* Choose a current California or federal economic policy (housing, minimum wage, gas tax, tariffs) and read an LAO or CBO analysis.

*How to teach:* The student chooses a policy and reads an LAO or CBO analysis, taking notes on goals, costs, and predicted effects. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why use LAO or CBO reports?
  **A:** They are nonpartisan analyses of fiscal and economic effects, written for lawmakers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Analyze
*Student:* Use supply-and-demand graphs to predict effects on prices, quantities, and different groups.

*How to teach:* Model the supply-and-demand graph for one policy. The student graphs their policy and predicts effects on price, quantity, and different groups.

- **Q:** Graph a per-gallon gas tax. What happens?
  **A:** Supply shifts left (or a wedge appears); price paid by consumers rises, price received by sellers falls, quantity falls; the government collects revenue; the burden falls more on consumers because demand is inelastic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data
*Student:* Gather at least two data series from FRED or BLS supporting your analysis.

*How to teach:* The student gathers at least two FRED or BLS data series and makes graphs with sources. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why use at least two data series?
  **A:** To corroborate the analysis and show the problem from more than one angle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write
*Student:* Write a 1,000-word policy analysis with a recommendation and acknowledgment of trade-offs.

*How to teach:* The student writes the 1,000-word analysis: background, model, data, effects on groups, recommendation, trade-offs. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What should the recommendation section include?
  **A:** A clear recommendation, the evidence for it, and an honest statement of who bears the costs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present your analysis in 7 minutes to family or co-op.

*How to teach:* The student presents for 7 minutes and answers questions. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What three visuals belong in the presentation?
  **A:** The supply-and-demand graph, a data graph, and a summary of the recommendation and trade-offs.

**Assessment:** Policy analysis paper and presentation.

**Answer key:** Policy analysis rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): Economic reasoning — correct graph and predictions; Evidence — LAO/CBO source plus two data series cited; Trade-offs — effects on at least three groups; Recommendation — clear and justified; Presentation — 7 minutes, clear visuals, answers questions. Proficient: 3 in each.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Reflection on growth

**Prepare:** Baseline self-portrait from Week 1, mirror, same pencils and lighting. Arrange a mentor conversation (art teacher, local artist).

**Watch for:**
- Growth analysis that only says 'I got better.' Cite specific visual evidence.
- Judging only technique. Also assess ideas and process.
- Comparing to others rather than to your past work.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final self-portrait
*Student:* Draw a new 45-minute self-portrait and compare it with Week 1.

*How to teach:* 45-minute self-portrait under similar conditions; compare with Week 1.

- **Q:** Why use the same conditions?
  **A:** So differences reflect growth in skill, not changes in setup.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Growth analysis
*Student:* Write a growth analysis comparing early and late work with specific evidence.

*How to teach:* Growth analysis with specific evidence.

- **Q:** Give an example of specific evidence of growth.
  **A:** 'In September my shadows were outlined; now the core shadow and reflected light model the cheek.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Evaluate with two lenses
*Student:* Evaluate your investigation with two different criteria sets (technical and conceptual).

*How to teach:* Evaluate the investigation with technical and conceptual criteria.

- **Q:** What is the difference between technical and conceptual criteria?
  **A:** Technical: skill with materials (drawing, value, color, craft). Conceptual: strength and development of ideas and meaning.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peer/mentor talk
*Student:* Discuss your year with an artist mentor or teacher.

*How to teach:* Discuss the year with a mentor.

- **Q:** What questions might you ask a mentor?
  **A:** What's the strongest work and why? What should I develop next? How did you build your practice?

### Day 5 (Friday): Final reflection
*Student:* Write a final reflection on your artistic identity.

*How to teach:* Final reflection on artistic identity.

- **Q:** What is metacognition?
  **A:** Thinking about your own thinking and learning processes.

**Assessment:** Growth analysis.

**Answer key:** Growth analysis rubric. 4 – Side-by-side self-portraits, specific visual evidence of growth in at least four areas, evaluation through technical and conceptual lenses, mentor insights, thoughtful identity reflection. 3 (proficient) – Comparison with specific evidence and both lenses. 2 – General statements. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Presenting the plan

**Prepare:** Slides or poster materials. Schedule the family presentation.

**Watch for:**
- Presentations that read slides word for word.
- Plans with no evidence for choices.
- Ignoring feedback.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finalize
*Student:* Finalize the plan and do a workout from it.

*How to teach:* Finalize the plan; do a workout from it.

- **Q:** Why finalize with a real workout?
  **A:** To verify the plan is practical and enjoyable.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prepare
*Student:* Prepare a 5-minute presentation of your plan.

*How to teach:* Prepare a 5-minute presentation: goals, plan, data, resources.

- **Q:** What are the key parts of the presentation?
  **A:** Your data and goals, weekly plan meeting guidelines, resources/budget, strategies for barriers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Present
*Student:* Present the plan to family and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present to family; answer questions.

- **Q:** How do you answer a question you don't know?
  **A:** Acknowledge it, explain how you'd find out, and follow up.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lead
*Student:* Lead family through one session from your plan.

*How to teach:* Lead family through one session.

- **Q:** How do you adapt a session for an older family member?
  **A:** Lower impact, reduce load, use modifications, and monitor RPE.

### Day 5 (Friday): Revise
*Student:* Make final revisions based on feedback.

*How to teach:* Final revisions based on feedback.

- **Q:** Why revise after feedback?
  **A:** Feedback shows gaps in clarity or practicality.

**Assessment:** Plan presentation.

**Answer key:** Plan presentation rubric. 4 – Clear 5-minute talk with data visuals, plan meets guidelines, addresses barriers and resources, confident answers, leads a safe session, revisions documented. 3 (proficient) – Clear presentation and session, plan meets guidelines. 2 – Unclear or missing elements. 1 – Incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Homeschool transcript & diploma

**Prepare:** Gather the course list from Unit 8, a transcript template and a copy of each year's filed Private School Affidavit (filed with the CDE each October 1–15). Have the school's name and CDS code (if assigned) ready.

**Watch for:**
- Calculating GPA with credits ignored. Weight each grade by its credits if course lengths differ.
- Thinking the state issues homeschool diplomas. In a private (PSA) homeschool, the school administrator (usually the parent) issues the diploma.
- Losing records. Keep permanent digital and paper copies — colleges, employers and the military may ask years later.

### Day 1 (Monday): GPA and credits
*Student:* Calculate cumulative unweighted GPA and total credits (10 credits per year-long course) from your four-year record.

*How to teach:* Calculate cumulative unweighted GPA and total credits.

- **Q:** Grades (equal credits): A, B, B, A, C, A, B, A. Unweighted GPA?
  **A:** (4 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 4 + 3 + 4) ÷ 8 = 27 ÷ 8 = 3.375 ≈ 3.38.
- **Q:** Six year-long courses per year for four years at 10 credits each. Total credits?
  **A:** 240 credits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final transcript
*Student:* Finalize the transcript with school name, CDS/affidavit info, courses by year, a–g letters, grades, GPA, and graduation date, signed by the administrator.

*How to teach:* Finalize the transcript: school name, affidavit info, courses by year, a–g letters, grades, GPA, graduation date, signature.

- **Q:** Why list a–g letters on the transcript?
  **A:** It helps UC/CSU and counselors quickly see which courses meet a–g subject requirements.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diploma and graduation
*Student:* Learn that the private school (PSA) administrator issues the diploma, review graduation requirements, and compare with the CHSPE as an alternative.

*How to teach:* Learn that the PSA administrator issues the diploma; review graduation requirements; compare with the CHSPE.

- **Q:** What is the CHSPE?
  **A:** The California High School Proficiency Examination; passing it earns a Certificate of Proficiency, legally equivalent to a California high school diploma (eligible at age 16 or after completing 10th grade, among other criteria).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Records packet
*Student:* Assemble a permanent records packet: transcript, course descriptions, reading lists, test scores, work samples, and copies of affidavits.

*How to teach:* Assemble the permanent records packet.

- **Q:** Why keep copies of Private School Affidavits?
  **A:** They document that the homeschool was a legally operating private school in California each year.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Parent signs and seals the final transcript; student creates digital and paper copies.

*How to teach:* Parent signs and seals the final transcript; the student makes digital and paper copies.

- **Q:** What makes a transcript 'official'?
  **A:** It is signed and dated by the school administrator and sent directly (often in a sealed envelope or securely electronically) to the receiving institution.

**Assessment:** Signed final transcript and records packet.

**Answer key:** Signed final transcript and records packet (pass when complete): transcript shows school name, address and affidavit/CDS info; student info; courses by year with a–g letters, grades and credits (10 per year-long course); cumulative GPA (check: sum of grade points × credits ÷ total credits); total credits; graduation date; administrator signature and date; matches the self-reported application records. Records packet contains transcript, course descriptions, reading lists, test scores, work samples and copies of affidavits. GPA example: year grades A, A, B, C, A, B (equal credits) → (4 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 4 + 3) ÷ 6 = 3.33.
