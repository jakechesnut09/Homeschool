# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 33

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Orwell: language and power

**Prepare:** Obtain 'Shooting an Elephant,' 'Politics and the English Language,' and the 1984 Appendix ('The Principles of Newspeak'). Collect two current euphemism examples from news or ads. Prepare the Modernism unit test.

**Watch for:**
- Reading 'Shooting an Elephant' as a hunting story. It is about imperialism's effect on the oppressor.
- Treating Orwell's rules as absolute. Rule 6 says to break them rather than write something barbarous.
- Thinking euphemism is always dishonest. It becomes political when it hides wrongdoing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shooting an Elephant
*Student:* Read 'Shooting an Elephant' and analyze how Orwell's personal narrative indicts imperialism.

*How to teach:* Read 'Shooting an Elephant.' Ask why Orwell shoots the elephant; the student explains the indictment of imperialism. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why does Orwell shoot the elephant?
  **A:** Because a crowd of Burmese expects it; he admits he did it 'solely to avoid looking a fool.'
- **Q:** What realization does he state?
  **A:** 'When the white man turns tyrant it is his own freedom that he destroys.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Politics and the English Language
*Student:* Read the essay; outline its structure (problem, examples, causes, rules) and evaluate its effectiveness.

*How to teach:* Read 'Politics and the English Language'; the student outlines structure and evaluates. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** List Orwell's six rules.
  **A:** 1) No stale figures of speech; 2) short word over long; 3) cut words when possible; 4) active over passive; 5) no foreign/jargon word if an everyday one exists; 6) break any rule sooner than say anything barbarous.
- **Q:** Give an example of political euphemism from the essay.
  **A:** 'Pacification' for bombing villages and driving out inhabitants.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Newspeak
*Student:* Read the Appendix on Newspeak from 1984; connect it to euphemisms found in modern news or advertising.

*How to teach:* Read the Newspeak Appendix; connect to modern euphemisms. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is Newspeak's purpose?
  **A:** To narrow the range of thought so that dissent becomes literally unthinkable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Apply the rules
*Student:* Revise a paragraph from your research paper using Orwell's six rules; annotate each change.

*How to teach:* The student revises a research-paper paragraph using Orwell's rules. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Revise: 'It is believed by many that utilization of phones is impactful.'
  **A:** 'Many people believe phones affect learning.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Modernism test
*Student:* Take a unit test on Modernist poetry, Kafka, and Orwell with passage analysis and short essays.

*How to teach:* Give the Modernism unit test. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name two Modernist techniques.
  **A:** Fragmentation and stream of consciousness/interior monologue.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test key: Owen's 'old Lie'; Brooke's 'foreign field'; Prufrock's paralysis; Metamorphosis family response; Orwell's reason for shooting; six rules; Newspeak's purpose. Short essays rubric (4 levels): thesis, evidence, analysis.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Limits preview: rates of change

**Prepare:** Desmos; a ball and a phone that records video (slow-motion helps) near a wall with a measuring tape. Preview OpenStax Precalculus 12.1–12.4 (limits preview).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a limit is just f(a). A limit describes what f approaches near a, even when f(a) is undefined.
- Stopping at 0/0. That form means 'simplify more' (factor, rationalize).
- Confusing average velocity over an interval with instantaneous velocity at a moment.

### Day 1 (Monday): Approaching a value
*Student:* Estimate limits from tables and graphs, including functions with holes.

*How to teach:* Make tables approaching x = 2 from both sides for f(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2) and graph it showing the hole. Explain the notation lim_{x→2}.

- **Q:** Estimate lim_{x→2} (x² − 4)/(x − 2).
  **A:** 4 — values near 2 approach 4, though f(2) is undefined.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Limits algebraically
*Student:* Evaluate limits by direct substitution, factoring, and rationalizing for 10 problems.

*How to teach:* Model direct substitution, then factoring, then rationalizing. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** lim_{x→3} (x² − 9)/(x − 3)
  **A:** 6
- **Q:** lim_{x→0} (√(x + 9) − 3)/x
  **A:** Multiply by the conjugate: 1/(√(x + 9) + 3) → 1/6.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Secant to tangent
*Student:* Use a Desmos slider to shrink the interval of a secant line and estimate instantaneous rate of change.

*How to teach:* In Desmos draw f(x) = x² and a secant from (1, 1) to (1 + h, (1 + h)²) with an h slider; shrink h and watch the slope approach 2.

- **Q:** Slope of the secant on [1, 1.1]?
  **A:** (1.21 − 1)/0.1 = 2.1; as h → 0 the slope approaches 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Falling ball
*Student:* Video a dropped ball, model height with a quadratic, and estimate its velocity at a moment using shrinking intervals.

*How to teach:* Video a dropped ball against a measuring tape, record height at several frames, and fit h = h₀ − 4.9t². Estimate velocity at t = 0.5 s using shrinking intervals.

- **Q:** For h = 2 − 4.9t², estimate the velocity at t = 0.5 s.
  **A:** Average velocity over [0.5, 0.5 + h] = −4.9(1 + h) → −4.9 m/s as h → 0.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a limits preview quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the limits preview quiz. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** lim_{x→4} (3x + 1)?
  **A:** 13

**Assessment:** Limits quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) lim_{x→2}(x² − 4)/(x − 2) = 4. 2) lim_{x→3}(x² − 9)/(x − 3) = 6. 3) lim_{x→0}(√(x + 9) − 3)/x = 1/6. 4) Secant slope of x² on [1, 1.1] = 2.1; tangent slope 2. 5) Velocity of h = 2 − 4.9t² at t = 0.5: −4.9 m/s. 6) lim_{x→4}(3x + 1) = 13. Proficient: 5 of 6.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Capstone: research question and plan

**Prepare:** Field notebook, test kits (water strips, thermometers, particle cards) from earlier units. Help the student choose a feasible local question; review safety (traffic, water, private property, parent accompanies).

**Watch for:**
- Questions too broad to answer in three field days.
- No replicates. Multiple samples per site increase reliability.
- Changing methods midway without documenting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Question
*Student:* Choose a local question (e.g., Does street tree cover lower surface temperatures? How clean is the creek after rain?).

*How to teach:* Choose a local, testable question.

- **Q:** Rewrite 'Is pollution bad here?' as a testable question.
  **A:** Example: 'Is nitrate concentration higher downstream of the golf course than upstream after rain?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Background
*Student:* Find three credible sources on the issue and summarize what is known.

*How to teach:* Find three credible sources; summarize.

- **Q:** How do you judge a source's credibility?
  **A:** Author expertise, peer review or government/academic origin, evidence cited, date, and purpose/bias.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan
*Student:* Write a procedure with variables, sampling locations, replicates, and safety plan.

*How to teach:* Write procedure: variables, sampling locations, replicates, safety plan.

- **Q:** Why include replicates?
  **A:** To check consistency, reduce the effect of random error, and calculate averages.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pilot
*Student:* Collect pilot data and adjust methods as needed.

*How to teach:* Collect pilot data; adjust methods.

- **Q:** What is a pilot study?
  **A:** A small trial run to test methods before full data collection.

### Day 5 (Friday): Approval
*Student:* Present the plan in a conference for approval.

*How to teach:* Approval conference.

- **Q:** What is a control in a field study?
  **A:** A comparison site or condition without the factor being tested (e.g., upstream site or shaded vs. unshaded).

**Assessment:** Approved capstone plan.

**Answer key:** Approved capstone plan rubric. 4 – Testable question and hypothesis, three credible sources summarized, clear independent/dependent/controlled variables, at least 3 sites with 3 replicates, data sheet, safety plan, pilot results and adjustments. 3 (proficient) – All plan elements present. 2 – Missing replicates or safety plan. 1 – Not approvable.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Trade policy: tariffs and agreements

**Prepare:** Print the 1930 economists' statement against Smoot-Hawley and a USMCA summary (ustr.gov). Find a current tariff debate article.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking foreign countries pay tariffs. Importers pay them, and costs are usually passed largely to consumers.
- Blaming the Depression on Smoot-Hawley alone. It worsened the collapse in trade but didn't cause the Depression.
- Ignoring the winners from protection. Protected domestic producers and their workers gain; consumers and import-using industries lose.

### Day 1 (Monday): Smoot-Hawley
*Student:* Read the 1930 economists' petition against Smoot-Hawley and analyze its effects on trade during the Depression.

*How to teach:* Read the economists' petition. Discuss effects. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did over 1,000 economists warn in 1930?
  **A:** That higher tariffs would raise prices, invite retaliation, and hurt exports and farmers.
- **Q:** What happened to world trade?
  **A:** It fell sharply in the early 1930s as other countries retaliated.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tariff graph
*Student:* Graph the effect of a tariff on domestic price, imports, and deadweight loss.

*How to teach:* Graph a tariff: world price $10, tariff $2. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What happens to domestic price, production, imports?
  **A:** Price rises to $12; domestic production rises; consumption falls; imports fall; government collects revenue; deadweight loss appears.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): NAFTA to USMCA
*Student:* Evaluate winners and losers of NAFTA and changes in USMCA from the perspectives of workers, farmers, and consumers.

*How to teach:* Evaluate NAFTA and USMCA. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name one USMCA change.
  **A:** Higher auto rules of origin (75% North American content) and labor value content requirements.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Current debate
*Student:* Analyze a current tariff debate and identify the arguments of labor, business, and political leaders.

*How to teach:* Analyze a current debate. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What argument do domestic producers make?
  **A:** Tariffs protect jobs from unfair competition.

### Day 5 (Friday): Debate
*Student:* Hold a structured debate on free trade vs. protection and write a reflection.

*How to teach:* Hold the debate and write a reflection. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What makes a strong rebuttal?
  **A:** Directly addressing opposing evidence.

**Assessment:** Trade policy debate.

**Answer key:** Debate rubric (4 = exemplary … 1 = beginning): Position — clear claim on free trade vs. protection; Evidence — uses Smoot-Hawley history (economists' warning, retaliation, trade collapse), the tariff graph (higher price, fewer imports, deadweight loss, revenue), and NAFTA/USMCA winners and losers; Rebuttal — answers the other side's strongest point; Reflection — explains whether and why their view changed.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Preparing and protecting work

**Prepare:** Acid-free mat board (pre-cut mats are fine), mat cutter if available, acid-free hinging tape, frames, D-rings and hanging wire, screwdriver, level, pedestals or boxes painted white for 3-D work.

**Watch for:**
- Using masking tape or regular tape on artwork. It yellows and damages paper; use acid-free hinging tape.
- Hanging work in direct sunlight. UV light fades media.
- Using sawtooth hangers on heavy frames. Use D-rings and wire.

### Day 1 (Monday): Preservation
*Student:* Research archival practices (acid-free materials, UV light, humidity) and compare methods.

*How to teach:* Research archival practices: acid- and lignin-free materials, UV-filtering glazing, stable humidity (~40–50%), avoid direct sun.

- **Q:** Why use acid-free materials?
  **A:** Acidic paper and board yellow and become brittle over time, damaging the artwork they touch.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mat and frame
*Student:* Mat and/or frame 2-D works.

*How to teach:* Mat and/or frame 2-D works (hinge at the top with a T-hinge).

- **Q:** How should artwork be attached to a mat?
  **A:** With acid-free hinges (e.g., T-hinges) at the top so the paper hangs freely and can expand/contract.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hardware
*Student:* Attach hanging hardware and prepare pedestals for 3-D works.

*How to teach:* Attach hanging hardware; prepare pedestals.

- **Q:** Where should D-rings go on a frame?
  **A:** About one-quarter to one-third down from the top on each side, with wire strung between them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mock hang
*Student:* Do a mock hang at home to check spacing and sightlines.

*How to teach:* Mock hang at home; check spacing and sightlines.

- **Q:** Why do a mock hang?
  **A:** To test spacing, order, and visual flow before installation day.

### Day 5 (Friday): Checklist
*Student:* Complete an exhibition checklist and transport plan.

*How to teach:* Complete exhibition checklist and transport plan.

- **Q:** How should framed work be transported?
  **A:** Wrapped with corner protectors and padding, glass taped or protected, stored upright and secured.

**Assessment:** Prepared works.

**Answer key:** Prepared works rubric. 4 – All works matted/framed or mounted with archival materials, secure hardware, pedestals ready, mock hang photographed, complete checklist and transport plan. 3 (proficient) – Works prepared safely with hardware and checklist. 2 – Some works unprepared or non-archival methods. 1 – Not ready.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Writing the adult fitness plan

**Prepare:** Plan template (12 weeks), adult activity guidelines (Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans). Budget sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Plans that exceed realistic time. Adherence first.
- Omitting strength training. Adults need 2+ days.
- Not planning for progression or maintenance phases.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft
*Student:* Draft a 12-week plan meeting adult guidelines (150+ min aerobic, 2 strength days).

*How to teach:* Draft a 12-week plan meeting guidelines: 150–300 min moderate (or 75–150 vigorous) aerobic, strength 2+ days, flexibility.

- **Q:** State the adult aerobic guideline.
  **A:** 150–300 minutes moderate or 75–150 minutes vigorous aerobic activity per week (or an equivalent mix), plus muscle-strengthening on 2+ days.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test week 1
*Student:* Complete day 1 of your plan.

*How to teach:* Complete day 1 of the plan.

- **Q:** Why test the plan before finalizing?
  **A:** To check that time, intensity, and logistics are realistic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Budget
*Student:* Add a budget and resource list to the plan.

*How to teach:* Add a budget and resource list (gear, membership, free options).

- **Q:** Give a low-cost way to meet strength guidelines.
  **A:** Bodyweight or resistance band workouts at home or in a park.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test week 1
*Student:* Complete day 2 of your plan.

*How to teach:* Complete day 2.

- **Q:** What is adherence?
  **A:** Consistently following the plan over time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Revise
*Student:* Revise the plan based on how the first days felt.

*How to teach:* Revise based on how the days felt.

- **Q:** If day 2 felt too hard (RPE 9), what should change?
  **A:** Reduce intensity or volume so most sessions are RPE 6–8, then progress gradually.

**Assessment:** Draft adult fitness plan.

**Answer key:** Draft adult fitness plan rubric. 4 – 12 weeks with phases (build, progress, maintain), meets aerobic and strength guidelines, flexibility, lifetime activities, budget and resources, tested and revised. 3 (proficient) – Meets guidelines, budget, revisions. 2 – Missing a component. 1 – Incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Interviews

**Prepare:** Have a phone or webcam for recording and a list of common interview questions. Arrange an adult (ideally not a parent — a family friend or mentor) for a 20-minute mock interview.

**Watch for:**
- Unstructured, rambling answers. Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Not researching the company. Know what it does and why you want to work there.
- Forgetting the thank-you. Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interview types
*Student:* Compare phone, video, group, and behavioral interviews and research the company for a target job.

*How to teach:* Compare phone, video, group and behavioral interviews; research a target company.

- **Q:** What is a behavioral interview question?
  **A:** A question about past behavior to predict future performance, e.g. 'Tell me about a time you solved a problem.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): STAR stories
*Student:* Write five STAR stories from your experience (teamwork, conflict, failure, leadership, initiative).

*How to teach:* Write five STAR stories (teamwork, conflict, failure, leadership, initiative).

- **Q:** What does STAR stand for?
  **A:** Situation, Task, Action, Result.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Elevator pitch
*Student:* Write and record a 30-second elevator pitch, then revise after watching it back.

*How to teach:* Write and record a 30-second elevator pitch; revise.

- **Q:** What belongs in a 30-second pitch?
  **A:** Who you are, relevant skills/experience, and what you're looking for or can offer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mock interview
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute recorded mock interview with an adult using 10 common questions.

*How to teach:* Run the 20-minute recorded mock interview with 10 common questions.

- **Q:** How should you answer 'What's your greatest weakness?'
  **A:** Name a real but manageable weakness and the specific steps you're taking to improve it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-score the recording with a rubric and send a professional thank-you email.

*How to teach:* Self-score with the rubric and send a professional thank-you email.

- **Q:** What should a thank-you email include?
  **A:** Thanks for their time, a specific point from the conversation, a brief restatement of interest, and a professional closing.

**Assessment:** Mock interview rubric score and thank-you email.

**Answer key:** Mock interview rubric (4 levels): 4 = professional greeting and appearance; confident 30-second introduction; answers to behavioral questions use STAR with specific results; shows knowledge of the company; asks two thoughtful questions; good eye contact and pace; thank-you email sent within 24 hours that references something specific from the interview; 3 = solid with a few weak answers; 2 = vague answers, little preparation; 1 = unprepared.
