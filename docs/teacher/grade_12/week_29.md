# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 29

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Revising and editing the research paper

**Prepare:** Print a research-paper rubric for the reader. Arrange a reader (parent, mentor, tutor) for Day 2. Have the source notes available for the plagiarism self-check.

**Watch for:**
- Treating revision as proofreading. Revise argument and organization first; edit sentences last.
- Faulty parallelism in lists. Make every list item the same grammatical form.
- Unintentional plagiarism from notes. Compare every paraphrase with the original source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reverse outline
*Student:* Create a reverse outline of your draft (one sentence per paragraph) and reorder or cut paragraphs that don't advance the thesis.

*How to teach:* Model a reverse outline on one page. The student writes one sentence per paragraph and cuts or moves paragraphs that don't advance the thesis.

- **Q:** What is a reverse outline used for?
  **A:** To see the draft's actual structure and find paragraphs that repeat, drift, or are out of order.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Feedback
*Student:* Get written feedback from a reader (parent, mentor, or tutor) using a research-paper rubric; prioritize three revisions.

*How to teach:* The reader scores the draft with the rubric and writes comments. The student picks the three highest-impact revisions. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How should feedback be prioritized?
  **A:** Argument and structure first, then evidence and commentary, then style and mechanics.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise
*Student:* Make the three priority revisions and strengthen transitions between sections.

*How to teach:* The student makes the three revisions and adds transitions between major sections. Read the transitions aloud together. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Write a transition between sections.
  **A:** 'While these studies show phones reduce attention, they leave open whether restrictions actually improve grades.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit
*Student:* Edit sentence by sentence for parallelism, hyphenation, and comma usage; run a plagiarism self-check against notes.

*How to teach:* The student edits sentence by sentence for parallelism, hyphenation, and commas, then checks every paraphrase against notes.

- **Q:** Fix: 'Students like reading, to write, and math.'
  **A:** 'Students like reading, writing, and doing math.'
- **Q:** Hyphenate correctly: 'a well known study' vs. 'the study is well known.'
  **A:** 'a well-known study' (before the noun); 'the study is well known' (after).

### Day 5 (Friday): MLA polish
*Student:* Format the paper in MLA (header, title, page numbers, hanging-indent Works Cited) and submit the revised draft.

*How to teach:* The student formats in MLA and submits the revised draft with tracked changes. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What goes in the MLA heading?
  **A:** Student name, instructor, course, and date, flush left and double-spaced; last name and page number top right.

**Assessment:** Revised draft with tracked changes.

**Answer key:** Revised draft rubric (4 levels): 4 = tracked changes show substantive revision (structure, evidence, counterargument), improved transitions, clean mechanics, correct MLA format; 3 = real revision plus editing; 2 = mostly surface edits; 1 = little change.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Random variables and expected value

**Prepare:** Dice, coins, a spreadsheet. Find a census table of household size (data.census.gov, table on household size) or use the illustrative distribution in Day 4. Prepare the Unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the expected value must be a possible outcome. E(X) = 2.5 for a die is a long-run average.
- Using the binomial formula when conditions fail. Check BINS: Binary outcomes, Independent trials, fixed Number, Same probability.
- Probabilities in a distribution that don't sum to 1. Always check the total.

### Day 1 (Monday): Distributions
*Student:* Define a random variable for the number of heads in 4 flips; build and graph its probability distribution.

*How to teach:* Define X = number of heads in 4 flips. List all 16 outcomes, count heads, and build the table; graph it as a histogram. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give the probability distribution of X.
  **A:** P(0) = 1/16, P(1) = 4/16, P(2) = 6/16, P(3) = 4/16, P(4) = 1/16.
- **Q:** What is E(X)?
  **A:** 2 heads.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Expected value
*Student:* Compute expected values for games of chance and decide whether each game is fair.

*How to teach:* Teach E(X) = Σ x·P(x) and model a game. The student decides whether each game is fair (E = 0). Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Pay $1 to roll a die; win $5 (net +$4) on a 6, otherwise lose $1. Expected value?
  **A:** (4)(1/6) + (−1)(5/6) = −1/6 ≈ −$0.17 per play; not fair.
- **Q:** What prize on a 6 would make it fair?
  **A:** Net win w with w/6 − 5/6 = 0 → w = $5 net (a $6 prize including the $1 back).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Binomial
*Student:* Use the binomial formula and technology to compute probabilities for free-throw and quality-control scenarios.

*How to teach:* Introduce P(X = k) = C(n, k)pᵏ(1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ and binompdf/binomcdf. Model a 70% free-throw shooter taking 10 shots. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** P(exactly 7 of 10)?
  **A:** C(10, 7)(0.7)⁷(0.3)³ ≈ 0.267.
- **Q:** P(at least 8 of 10)?
  **A:** ≈ 0.383.
- **Q:** 2% of items are defective. P(no defects in 20)?
  **A:** 0.98²⁰ ≈ 0.668.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Empirical distribution
*Student:* Build an empirical distribution from real data (e.g., household size from census tables) and find its mean.

*How to teach:* Build an empirical distribution from census data and compute its mean. Illustrative distribution: size 1: 0.28, 2: 0.35, 3: 0.15, 4: 0.13, 5: 0.06, 6: 0.03.

- **Q:** Mean household size from the illustrative table?
  **A:** 0.28 + 0.70 + 0.45 + 0.52 + 0.30 + 0.18 = 2.43 people.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on probability.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Mean of a binomial with n = 10, p = 0.7?
  **A:** np = 7.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test sample key: 1) P(heart or king) = 4/13. 2) At least one six in 4 rolls ≈ 0.518. 3) P(11–12 | evening) ≈ 0.571. 4) Medical test ≈ 0.092. 5) 10C3 = 120; 8P3 = 336. 6) Distribution of heads in 4 flips: 1/16, 4/16, 6/16, 4/16, 1/16; E = 2. 7) Game EV = −$0.17. 8) Binomial P(7 of 10) ≈ 0.267. Scoring: 90%+ mastery, 75–89% proficient, 60–74% developing.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Mitigation and your carbon footprint

**Prepare:** EPA household carbon footprint calculator, spreadsheet, utility bills and mileage estimates. Read summaries of AB 32 (2006), SB 32 (2016), cap-and-trade (began 2012), SB 100 (100% clean electricity by 2045), and AB 1279 (2022, carbon neutrality by 2045). Prepare the Unit 8 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking individual action and policy are opposites. Both matter; policy shapes choices at scale.
- Believing tree planting alone can offset emissions. Land is limited and forests can burn; deep emission cuts are still required.
- Confusing mitigation (reducing causes) with adaptation (adjusting to impacts).

### Day 1 (Monday): Footprint
*Student:* Calculate your household carbon footprint with the EPA calculator and identify the three largest sources.

*How to teach:* Calculate household footprint; identify three largest sources.

- **Q:** A car gets 25 mpg and drives 12,000 miles/year. Each gallon of gasoline emits about 8.9 kg CO₂. Annual emissions?
  **A:** 480 gallons × 8.9 ≈ 4,270 kg ≈ 4.3 metric tons CO₂.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reduction plan
*Student:* Model in a spreadsheet how three changes would reduce your footprint over 5 years.

*How to teach:* Model three changes over 5 years in a spreadsheet.

- **Q:** Switching that car to 50 mpg cuts emissions by how much per year?
  **A:** From 480 to 240 gallons → saves ~2,140 kg (≈2.1 t) CO₂ per year.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): California policy
*Student:* Read about AB 32, cap-and-trade, and the 2045 carbon-neutrality goal; summarize each.

*How to teach:* Summarize California policy.

- **Q:** What did AB 32 require?
  **A:** Reduce California's greenhouse gas emissions to 1990 levels by 2020 (met in 2016); SB 32 extended the target to 40% below 1990 by 2030.
- **Q:** How does cap-and-trade work?
  **A:** The state sets a declining cap on total emissions from large sources and issues allowances; companies must hold allowances for their emissions and can trade them, putting a price on carbon.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sequestration
*Student:* Compare natural (forests, wetlands, soils) and technological (direct air capture) sequestration costs and capacities.

*How to teach:* Compare sequestration: forests/wetlands/soils (low cost, ~$10–100/t, but limited and reversible) vs. direct air capture (currently hundreds of dollars per ton, small scale, durable storage).

- **Q:** Why is direct air capture expensive?
  **A:** CO₂ is very dilute in air (~0.04%), so capturing it takes large amounts of energy and equipment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on climate change.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** Define net zero.
  **A:** Balancing remaining greenhouse gas emissions with an equal amount of removals so net emissions are zero.

**Assessment:** Footprint reduction model and Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Footprint reduction model: proficient = baseline footprint by category, three changes with correct calculations, 5-year cumulative savings graph. Unit 8 test key: 1) Weather vs. climate. 2) Temperature, sea-level, sea-ice trends. 3) CO₂ vs. ice-core range. 4) Models and hindcasting. 5) Scenarios and ranges (~1.8 °C low vs. ~4.4 °C very high by 2100). 6) Snowpack and atmospheric rivers. 7) Thermal expansion and land ice. 8) Mitigation vs. adaptation. 9) AB 32/SB 32/cap-and-trade/2045 neutrality. 10) Sequestration comparison.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): GDP and economic growth

**Prepare:** Bookmark FRED series GDPC1 (real GDP) and the latest BEA 'Gross Domestic Product' news release (bea.gov). Prepare 10 transactions to classify.

**Watch for:**
- Counting intermediate goods (flour sold to a bakery) and the final good (bread) — double counting. GDP counts only final goods and services.
- Counting transfer payments, used goods, or stock purchases. These don't represent new production.
- Comparing nominal GDP across years without adjusting for inflation. Use real GDP for growth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Measuring GDP
*Student:* Learn the expenditure approach (C+I+G+NX) and classify 10 transactions as counted or not.

*How to teach:* Explain GDP = C + I + G + NX with an example of each. The student classifies 10 transactions as counted (and which component) or not counted; check reasons.

- **Q:** Is a used car sale counted? A Social Security check?
  **A:** Neither — the car was counted when new; Social Security is a transfer payment, not payment for production.
- **Q:** A California winery exports wine to Japan. Which component?
  **A:** Net exports (exports add to GDP).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Real vs. nominal
*Student:* Convert nominal GDP to real GDP with a price index and compute growth rates.

*How to teach:* Teach real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ (price index/100). The student converts and computes growth rates. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Nominal GDP $24 trillion, GDP deflator 120. Real GDP?
  **A:** $24T ÷ 1.20 = $20 trillion (in base-year dollars).
- **Q:** Real GDP grows from $20.0T to $20.6T. Growth rate?
  **A:** 3%.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Business cycle
*Student:* Graph real GDP from FRED since 2000 and label expansions and recessions (2001, 2008, 2020).

*How to teach:* Graph real GDP since 2000 on FRED with recession shading. The student labels expansions and the 2001, 2008, 2020 recessions.

- **Q:** Give the dates of the Great Recession and the COVID recession.
  **A:** December 2007–June 2009; February–April 2020.
- **Q:** What is the common rule of thumb for a recession and who officially dates them?
  **A:** Two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP is a rule of thumb; the NBER's Business Cycle Dating Committee officially dates recessions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Latest release
*Student:* Read the latest BEA GDP news release and summarize what drove growth.

*How to teach:* Read the BEA release headline and summary paragraph together. The student summarizes the growth rate and which components drove it.

- **Q:** Which GDP component is largest?
  **A:** Personal consumption expenditures — about two-thirds of U.S. GDP.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a GDP quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the GDP quiz. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Define GDP.
  **A:** The market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period.

**Assessment:** GDP quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) GDP definition. 2) C + I + G + NX. 3) Not counted: used goods, transfers, intermediate goods, stocks, household work. 4) Real GDP = $20T. 5) Growth 3%. 6) Recessions 2001, 2007–09, 2020. 7) Consumption ≈ 2/3. Proficient: 6 of 7.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Photographing artwork

**Prepare:** Phone or camera on a tripod (or stack of books), two identical lamps (same bulb type) at 45° or indirect window light, neutral gray/white backdrop, gray card or white paper for white balance, editing app.

**Watch for:**
- Shooting at an angle, causing keystoning. Keep the camera centered and parallel to the artwork.
- Using mixed light sources (lamp + window), which causes color casts.
- Over-editing color so the photo no longer matches the work.

### Day 1 (Monday): Setup
*Student:* Set up even lighting with two lamps at 45° and a neutral backdrop.

*How to teach:* Set up two lamps at 45° on either side, equal distance, neutral backdrop.

- **Q:** Why place lights at 45° on both sides?
  **A:** It gives even illumination and reduces glare and hot spots.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shoot 2-D
*Student:* Photograph all 2-D works square to the camera, correcting glare and keystoning.

*How to teach:* Photograph 2-D works square to the camera; correct glare (adjust angle/polarizer) and keystoning.

- **Q:** What is keystoning?
  **A:** Distortion where a rectangle appears as a trapezoid because the camera isn't parallel to the artwork.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoot 3-D
*Student:* Photograph 3-D works with multiple views and a detail shot.

*How to teach:* Photograph 3-D works: multiple views plus a detail.

- **Q:** What views should a 3-D work have?
  **A:** Front, side(s), back or three-quarter, and a detail.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit
*Student:* Crop, color-correct, and export images at portfolio specs (e.g., 1200–2000 px).

*How to teach:* Crop, color-correct (white balance), export at 1200–2000 px on the long side.

- **Q:** What does white balance correct?
  **A:** Color casts from the light source so whites appear neutral.

### Day 5 (Friday): Archive
*Student:* Create an organized digital archive with titles, media, dimensions, and dates.

*How to teach:* Create an organized archive: title, media, dimensions (H × W × D), date.

- **Q:** What order are dimensions listed in?
  **A:** Height × width × depth.

**Assessment:** Digital archive.

**Answer key:** Digital archive rubric. 4 – All works photographed square, evenly lit, color-accurate, cropped, consistent sizes; 3-D works with multiple views; organized folders with complete metadata (title, media, H × W × D, year). 3 (proficient) – Accurate photos and complete metadata. 2 – Glare, keystoning, or missing info. 1 – Incomplete.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Social and cultural dance

**Prepare:** Music player and open floor. Choose cumbia songs, an East Coast swing song (~120–150 BPM), and two line dances (e.g., Cupid Shuffle, Electric Slide). Find a reputable short video on cumbia's origins.

**Watch for:**
- Treating cultural dance as a costume or joke. Learn origins and dance with respect.
- Stiff partner frame or pulling hard. Use gentle, connected frame — lead with the body.
- Counting swing in 8s. East Coast swing basic is 6 counts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cumbia
*Student:* Learn basic cumbia steps and their cultural origins; dance for 30 minutes.

*How to teach:* Learn cumbia basic (step back and return, alternating sides, relaxed upper body) and its origins on Colombia's Caribbean coast, blending Indigenous, African, and Spanish influences; spread across Latin America (strong traditions in Mexico and among Mexican-American communities). Dance 30 minutes.

- **Q:** Where did cumbia originate?
  **A:** On the Caribbean coast of Colombia, blending Indigenous, African, and Spanish musical and dance traditions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Swing
*Student:* Learn East Coast swing basic and a turn with a partner.

*How to teach:* East Coast swing basic: triple step (1&2), triple step (3&4), rock step (5, 6) — and an underarm turn with a partner.

- **Q:** How many counts is the East Coast swing basic?
  **A:** 6 counts: triple step, triple step, rock step.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Line dance
*Student:* Learn two line dances.

*How to teach:* Learn two line dances.

- **Q:** What is a 'wall' in line dancing?
  **A:** The direction dancers face; many line dances rotate 90° to a new wall each repetition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heart rate
*Student:* Dance continuously for 30 minutes, tracking heart rate.

*How to teach:* Dance continuously for 30 minutes tracking HR.

- **Q:** Can social dancing meet aerobic guidelines?
  **A:** Yes — sustained dancing typically reaches moderate (sometimes vigorous) intensity.

### Day 5 (Friday): Showcase
*Student:* Perform a dance for family and explain its cultural background.

*How to teach:* Perform a dance for family and explain its cultural background.

- **Q:** What is partner frame?
  **A:** The connected posture of arms and upper body that lets partners communicate leads and follows.

**Assessment:** Dance performance.

**Answer key:** Dance performance rubric. 4 – On-beat, correct basic steps and a figure, smooth partner connection (if partnered), accurate cultural explanation with origins and significance. 3 (proficient) – Mostly on-beat, correct steps, accurate cultural background. 2 – Frequently off-beat or inaccurate background. 1 – Incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): California Community Colleges

**Prepare:** Open the local community college's website and catalog and CCCApply (cccapply.org). Find the college's special admission (dual enrollment) form for high school students and its counseling/orientation pages.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking community college is only for transfer. It also offers career certificates, associate degrees and workforce training.
- Skipping counseling. A counselor-built education plan prevents wasted units and keeps students on track for transfer or a credential.
- Assuming remedial classes are required. Under AB 705/AB 1705, most students start directly in transfer-level English and math, placed using high school records and guided self-placement.

### Day 1 (Monday): CCC programs
*Student:* Explore a local college catalog and list three certificates, three AA/AS degrees, and their unit requirements.

*How to teach:* Explore the catalog; list three certificates, three AA/AS degrees and unit requirements.

- **Q:** How many semester units is a typical associate degree?
  **A:** 60 semester units (90 quarter units).
- **Q:** What's the difference between a certificate and an associate degree?
  **A:** A certificate is a shorter, career-focused program; an associate degree includes general education plus a major, about 60 units.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): CCCApply
*Student:* Walk through CCCApply and record residency, education, and enrollment information requirements.

*How to teach:* Walk through CCCApply; note residency, education and enrollment questions.

- **Q:** Why does CCCApply ask about residency?
  **A:** California residents pay the low resident fee ($46/unit); nonresidents pay much higher tuition unless exempt (e.g. AB 540).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Enrollment steps
*Student:* Map orientation, placement (AB 705 guided self-placement), counseling, and the education plan.

*How to teach:* Map orientation, placement (guided self-placement), counseling and the education plan.

- **Q:** What does AB 705 (and AB 1705) require of community colleges?
  **A:** That students be placed into transfer-level English and math whenever they are likely to succeed, using high school records and guided self-placement rather than placement tests.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dual enrollment
*Student:* Research how homeschool students take community college classes now (special admit) and what transfers.

*How to teach:* Research how homeschool students take community college classes now (special admit) and what transfers.

- **Q:** How can a homeschool student take community college classes before graduating?
  **A:** Through special admission (dual/concurrent enrollment), usually with a parent/school administrator approval form; units are often limited per term.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a semester-by-semester community college plan for one goal.

*How to teach:* Create a semester-by-semester community college plan for one goal.

- **Q:** What is priority registration?
  **A:** Earlier enrollment for students who complete orientation, assessment/placement and an education plan (and certain groups), so they can get needed classes.

**Assessment:** Community college education plan.

**Answer key:** Community college education plan rubric: 4 = names one goal (certificate, associate degree or transfer); semester-by-semester courses with units that total the requirement (e.g. 60 semester units for an associate degree); includes orientation, placement, counseling and priority registration steps; notes financial aid (CCPG) and deadlines; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = units don't add up or missing steps; 1 = incomplete.
