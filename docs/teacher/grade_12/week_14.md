# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 14

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: Frankenstein: frame and creation

**Prepare:** Obtain Frankenstein (1818 text, Project Gutenberg). Read the 1831 introduction and Letters 1–4 and chapters 1–5. Make a vocabulary log template.

**Watch for:**
- Calling the creature 'Frankenstein.' Victor Frankenstein is the creator; the creature is unnamed.
- Skipping the frame. Walton's letters mirror Victor's ambition.
- Assuming the novel is a horror story only. It explores responsibility and ambition.

### Day 1 (Monday): Origins
*Student:* Read Shelley's 1831 introduction about the ghost-story contest at Lake Geneva. Read Walton's letters 1–4 and note how Walton mirrors Victor.

*How to teach:* Read the 1831 introduction and Walton's letters. The student notes parallels. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What prompted Mary Shelley's novel?
  **A:** A ghost-story contest with Byron, Percy Shelley, and Polidori at Lake Geneva in 1816.
- **Q:** How does Walton mirror Victor?
  **A:** Both pursue glory through discovery, ignoring danger and isolation.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Victor's education
*Student:* Read chapters 1–3. Track Victor's influences (Agrippa, Paracelsus, Waldman) and his attitude toward knowledge.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–3. Track influences. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Which professor inspires Victor at Ingolstadt?
  **A:** M. Waldman.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The creation
*Student:* Read chapters 4–5. Annotate the creation scene for Gothic diction and analyze Victor's immediate reaction.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 4–5. Annotate Gothic diction. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How does Victor react when the creature opens its eyes?
  **A:** Horror and disgust — he flees and abandons the creature.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Vocabulary in context
*Student:* Use context clues and Greek/Latin roots to define 15 words from chapters 1–5 (e.g., 'ardour,' 'countenance,' 'lassitude'); build a vocabulary log.

*How to teach:* Use context and roots to define 15 words. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Define 'lassitude.'
  **A:** Weariness; lack of energy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reading check
*Student:* Take a reading quiz and write a paragraph on why Shelley subtitled the novel 'The Modern Prometheus.'

*How to teach:* Give the reading quiz and Prometheus paragraph. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Why 'The Modern Prometheus'?
  **A:** Prometheus stole fire for humanity and was punished; Victor seizes the power of creating life and suffers.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and analytical paragraph.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Walton writes to sister Margaret; Victor from Geneva; mother dies of scarlet fever; Ingolstadt; Waldman; creation in November. Paragraph rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): 4 = explains the myth and connects to Victor with quotations.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Trigonometric identities

**Prepare:** Make an identity reference sheet (reciprocal, quotient, Pythagorean, sum/difference, double-angle). Preview OpenStax 9.1–9.3.

**Watch for:**
- Working both sides of an identity at once as if solving an equation. Work one side (usually the more complicated) until it matches the other.
- Thinking sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B. Test with A = B = 30°: sin 60° ≈ 0.866 ≠ 1.
- Wrong sign when finding a trig value from the Pythagorean identity. Use the quadrant to choose the sign of the square root.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pythagorean identity
*Student:* Prove sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 from the unit circle and use it to find trig values given one ratio and the quadrant.

*How to teach:* On the unit circle the point (cos θ, sin θ) is distance 1 from the origin, so cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 by Pythagoras. Use it to find missing ratios given a quadrant.

- **Q:** sin θ = 3/5 and θ is in Quadrant II. Find cos θ and tan θ.
  **A:** cos θ = −4/5; tan θ = −3/4.
- **Q:** Derive 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ.
  **A:** Divide sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 by cos²θ: tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Verifying identities
*Student:* Verify 10 identities using reciprocal, quotient, and Pythagorean identities.

*How to teach:* Model a verification by rewriting in sines and cosines and simplifying one side. The student verifies 10 identities. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Verify sec²θ − tan²θ = 1.
  **A:** Rearrange 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ (shown above) — subtract tan²θ.
- **Q:** Verify sin θ·cot θ = cos θ.
  **A:** sin θ·(cos θ/sin θ) = cos θ.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sum and difference
*Student:* Follow a proof of cos(A−B) and use sum/difference formulas to find exact values like sin 75°.

*How to teach:* Walk through the distance-formula proof of cos(A − B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B, then use the formulas for exact values.

- **Q:** Find sin 75° exactly.
  **A:** sin(45° + 30°) = (√2/2)(√3/2) + (√2/2)(1/2) = (√6 + √2)/4.
- **Q:** Find cos 15° exactly.
  **A:** cos(45° − 30°) = (√2/2)(√3/2) + (√2/2)(1/2) = (√6 + √2)/4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Double angle
*Student:* Derive double-angle formulas and use them to solve equations such as sin 2x = cos x.

*How to teach:* Set A = B in the sum formulas to get sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x and cos 2x = cos²x − sin²x. Solve sin 2x = cos x by factoring.

- **Q:** Solve sin 2x = cos x on [0, 2π).
  **A:** 2 sin x cos x − cos x = 0 → cos x(2 sin x − 1) = 0 → x = π/2, 3π/2, π/6, 5π/6.

### Day 5 (Friday): Quiz
*Student:* Take an identities quiz with two proofs and four exact-value problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz below: two proofs and four exact values. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Give the three forms of cos 2x.
  **A:** cos²x − sin²x = 2cos²x − 1 = 1 − 2sin²x.

**Assessment:** Identities quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz: Proof 1) sec²θ − tan²θ = 1 (from Pythagorean identity ÷ cos²θ). Proof 2) (1 − cos²x)/sin x = sin x (numerator = sin²x). Exact: 3) sin 75° = (√6 + √2)/4. 4) cos 15° = (√6 + √2)/4. 5) tan 15° = 2 − √3. 6) sin 2θ if sin θ = 3/5 in QII: 2(3/5)(−4/5) = −24/25. Proofs scored 0–3 (3 = every step justified, one side transformed). Proficient: 14/18.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Coevolution of Earth and life

**Prepare:** Steel wool, vinegar, jars with lids (or a balloon over the mouth), thermometer; the Week 7 timeline; images of stromatolites (Shark Bay) and banded iron formations. Prepare the Unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Earth always had oxygen. Free O₂ accumulated only after the Great Oxidation Event (~2.4–2.1 Ga).
- Believing life just adapted to Earth. Life also changed Earth (atmosphere, soils, rocks) — coevolution.
- Assuming mass extinctions all had the same cause.

### Day 1 (Monday): Oxygen revolution
*Student:* Read about cyanobacteria and the Great Oxidation Event; examine images of stromatolites and banded iron formations.

*How to teach:* Read about cyanobacteria producing O₂ by photosynthesis; stromatolites (layered microbial mats) date back ~3.5 Ga; the Great Oxidation Event ~2.4 Ga.

- **Q:** What organisms caused the Great Oxidation Event?
  **A:** Cyanobacteria, through oxygenic photosynthesis.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rusting model
*Student:* Place vinegar-soaked steel wool in a sealed jar to show iron consuming oxygen; relate to banded iron formation.

*How to teach:* Seal vinegar-soaked steel wool in a jar with a thermometer or balloon; observe rust and oxygen consumption (balloon pulled in, temperature rises slightly).

- **Q:** How does the steel wool model banded iron formations?
  **A:** Dissolved iron in early oceans reacted with oxygen from cyanobacteria to form iron oxides that settled as red layers — just as iron in the jar consumes oxygen and rusts.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Land plants and soils
*Student:* Trace how land plants changed weathering, soil formation, and atmospheric CO₂ during the Devonian.

*How to teach:* Trace Devonian land plants (419–359 Ma): roots deepened weathering and built soils, pulling down CO₂ and cooling climate.

- **Q:** How did land plants lower atmospheric CO₂?
  **A:** Roots accelerated chemical weathering of silicate rocks (which consumes CO₂) and plants buried organic carbon in soils and sediments (later coal).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Extinctions
*Student:* Analyze the end-Permian and end-Cretaceous extinctions as Earth-system events affecting life.

*How to teach:* Analyze end-Permian (252 Ma; Siberian Traps volcanism, ~90%+ of marine species lost) and end-Cretaceous (66 Ma; Chicxulub asteroid impact plus Deccan Traps; ~75% of species lost including non-avian dinosaurs).

- **Q:** What evidence supports an asteroid impact at the end of the Cretaceous?
  **A:** A worldwide iridium-rich clay layer, shocked quartz, tektites, and the Chicxulub crater (~180 km) in Mexico's Yucatán dated to 66 Ma.

### Day 5 (Friday): Argument & test
*Student:* Write an evidence-based argument on coevolution and take the Unit 4 test.

*How to teach:* Student writes an argument on coevolution; give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** Give an example of life changing the geosphere.
  **A:** Cyanobacteria oxidized the atmosphere/ocean producing banded iron formations; plants built soils; corals and plankton built limestone.

**Assessment:** Coevolution argument and Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Coevolution argument rubric. 4 – Claim that Earth's systems and life coevolved; at least three examples with dates (GOE ~2.4 Ga and BIFs, Devonian plants and CO₂/soils, mass extinctions from Earth-system changes); explains feedback both ways. 3 (proficient) – Two or three accurate examples with reasoning. 2 – One example. 1 – Unsupported. Unit 4 test key: 1) Energy budget numbers. 2) Greenhouse mechanism. 3) Milankovitch periods. 4) Carbon reservoirs/fluxes; ~half of emissions stay airborne. 5) Ocean acidification chemistry. 6) Keeling Curve seasonality. 7) GOE and cyanobacteria. 8) BIFs. 9) Devonian plants. 10) End-Permian vs. end-Cretaceous causes.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): Local and tribal government, budgets, and courts

**Prepare:** Get your city/county budget and a council agenda. Visit California Courts website. Research a California tribe's government.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the city provides everything. Special districts, counties, and school districts provide many services.
- Misunderstanding Prop 13 as a cap on all taxes. It limits property tax rates (1%) and assessment increases (2%/yr until sale), which shifted local finance toward other sources.
- Thinking tribes are subject to state government like counties. Tribes are sovereign nations with government-to-government relationships.

### Day 1 (Monday): Local government
*Student:* Map who provides your water, schools, police, fire, roads, and parks (city, county, special district).

*How to teach:* Map who provides water, schools, police, fire, roads, parks. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is a special district?
  **A:** A local government for one service, like water or fire.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Budgets
*Student:* Analyze your city or county budget: identify the top revenue sources and top spending categories; explain Proposition 13's effect.

*How to teach:* Analyze the budget; explain Prop 13. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did Prop 13 (1978) do?
  **A:** Capped property taxes at 1% of assessed value and limited annual assessment increases to 2% until sale.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tribal governments
*Student:* Research a California tribal government's structure and its government-to-government relationship with the state.

*How to teach:* Research a tribal government. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** About how many federally recognized tribes are in California?
  **A:** More than 100 (about 109).

### Day 4 (Thursday): California courts
*Student:* Diagram superior courts, courts of appeal, and the California Supreme Court; compare to federal courts.

*How to teach:* Diagram California courts. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name the three levels.
  **A:** Superior Courts (58, one per county), Courts of Appeal, Supreme Court (7 justices).

### Day 5 (Friday): Attend a meeting
*Student:* Attend or watch a recorded city council or school board meeting; write a summary and a public comment you could give. Take the Unit 6 test.

*How to teach:* Watch a meeting; write summary and public comment; take Unit 6 test. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How long is a typical public comment?
  **A:** Usually 1–3 minutes.

**Assessment:** Meeting report and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test key: 1) Special districts provide single services (water, fire). 2) Prop 13 (1978): 1% cap on property tax rate, 2%/yr assessment growth until sale, two-thirds vote for special taxes. 3) Tribes are sovereign with government-to-government relations; CA has about 109 federally recognized tribes. 4) CA courts: 58 superior courts, Courts of Appeal (6 districts), Supreme Court of 7 justices. Meeting report rubric (4 levels): 4 = accurate summary of agenda items and decisions, identifies who decided and how public input was used, a specific 1–3 minute public comment with evidence.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Flexibility and mobility

**Prepare:** Yoga mat, strap or towel, a partner for PNF. Week 1 flexibility data.

**Watch for:**
- Static stretching as the warm-up. Dynamic movement before, static after.
- Bouncing in stretches (ballistic) without training. It can trigger the stretch reflex and strain muscles.
- Partners pushing hard in PNF. Gentle pressure, stretcher controls.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dynamic warm-up
*Student:* Learn and perform a 10-minute dynamic warm-up, then jog 20 minutes.

*How to teach:* 10-minute dynamic warm-up (leg swings, walking lunges with twist, arm circles, high knees, carioca), then 20-minute jog.

- **Q:** Name three dynamic warm-up movements.
  **A:** Leg swings, walking lunges, high knees, butt kicks, arm circles, carioca.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Static stretching
*Student:* Perform a 30-minute static stretching routine holding each stretch 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* 30-minute static routine, each stretch held 30 s, major groups.

- **Q:** How long should static stretches be held?
  **A:** About 15–30 seconds (to mild tension), repeated 2–4 times.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): PNF
*Student:* Practice PNF hamstring and chest stretches with a partner or strap.

*How to teach:* PNF contract-relax: stretch, contract against resistance 6 s at light-moderate effort, relax, stretch deeper 20–30 s. Hamstrings with strap; chest with doorway.

- **Q:** Why does PNF often increase range more than static stretching?
  **A:** The contraction is followed by relaxation (autogenic inhibition), allowing the muscle to stretch further.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mobility
*Student:* Do a hip and shoulder mobility routine.

*How to teach:* Hip and shoulder mobility: 90/90, hip circles, thoracic rotations, wall slides.

- **Q:** What is the difference between flexibility and mobility?
  **A:** Flexibility is the passive length of a muscle; mobility is the ability to actively move a joint through its range with control.

### Day 5 (Friday): Retest
*Student:* Retest sit-and-reach and shoulder stretch; compare with baseline.

*How to teach:* Retest sit-and-reach and shoulder stretch; compare.

- **Q:** Sit-and-reach improved from 9 to 11 inches. Percent change?
  **A:** About 22% ((11 − 9) ÷ 9).

**Assessment:** Flexibility retest.

**Answer key:** Flexibility retest: proficient = sit-and-reach (both sides) and shoulder stretch retested with correct protocol, compared with baseline, change calculated, and a written explanation plus a plan (frequency and type of stretching) to continue. 4 adds HFZ comparison; 2 = data only; 1 = incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Compound growth & investment basics

**Prepare:** Open a spreadsheet and the Investor.gov compound interest calculator. Find Khan Academy's stocks and bonds videos. Look up current I bond and CD rates (TreasuryDirect, bank sites) and an S&P 500 index fund's expense ratio.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking investing is gambling. Diversified, long-term investing in productive companies differs from short-term speculation.
- Ignoring time. Starting early often matters more than the amount invested, because of compounding.
- Thinking bonds are risk-free. Bonds carry interest-rate risk and (except U.S. Treasuries) default risk.

### Day 1 (Monday): Time is money
*Student:* Model two savers in a spreadsheet: one invests $200/month from 22–32, the other from 32–65, at 7%; graph the results.

*How to teach:* Model Saver A and Saver B in a spreadsheet (=FV) and graph the results.

- **Q:** Who ends with more at 65, and why?
  **A:** Saver A (about $346,000 vs. $309,000), because the early money had 33 more years to compound.
- **Q:** How much did each contribute?
  **A:** Saver A $24,000; Saver B $79,200.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): What is a stock
*Student:* Watch the Khan Academy stocks video and explain ownership, dividends, and capital gains with an example company.

*How to teach:* Watch the stocks video; explain ownership, dividends and capital gains with an example company.

- **Q:** You buy a share at $50, receive $2 in dividends, and sell at $65. Total return?
  **A:** Capital gain $15 + dividends $2 = $17, which is 34% of $50.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bonds and cash
*Student:* Compare Treasury bonds, I bonds, CDs, and corporate bonds for risk and return.

*How to teach:* Compare Treasury bonds, I bonds, CDs and corporate bonds for risk and return.

- **Q:** Why do corporate bonds usually pay more than Treasury bonds?
  **A:** They carry more default risk, so investors demand higher interest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Funds
*Student:* Compare an S&P 500 index fund and an actively managed fund using their expense ratios and 10-year returns.

*How to teach:* Compare an S&P 500 index fund and an actively managed fund using expense ratios and 10-year returns.

- **Q:** What is an expense ratio?
  **A:** The annual fee a fund charges, as a percent of your investment (e.g. 0.03% or 1.00%).
- **Q:** Why do index funds often outperform active funds over time?
  **A:** Lower fees, and most active managers fail to beat the index consistently after costs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on asset classes and compound-growth calculations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on asset classes and compound growth in the key.

- **Q:** What's the difference between a stock and a bond?
  **A:** A stock is ownership in a company; a bond is a loan to a company or government that pays interest.

**Assessment:** Compound growth spreadsheet and asset-class quiz.

**Answer key:** Compound growth spreadsheet (monthly compounding, 7%): Saver A invests $200/month ages 22–32 ($24,000 total) → about $34,600 at 32, then grows untouched to about $346,000 at 65. Saver B invests $200/month ages 32–65 ($79,200 total) → about $309,000 at 65. Saver A ends with more despite contributing less than a third as much. Accept answers within a few percent depending on compounding assumptions. Asset-class quiz: 1) Stock = ownership share in a company. 2) Bond = a loan to a company or government that pays interest. 3) Mutual fund = pooled money invested in many securities. 4) Index fund = fund that tracks a market index. 5) ETF = fund that trades like a stock. 6) Risk-return tradeoff = higher potential return comes with higher risk. 7) Dividends vs. capital gains.
