# Teacher's Guide — 12th Grade, Week 2

## English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research: UC Personal Insight Questions – drafting

**Prepare:** Read the UC PIQ page and tips with the student. Set up a word processor with word count visible. Note: each PIQ response is limited to 350 words and students choose 4 of 8.

**Watch for:**
- Writing about the prompt topic instead of about themselves. Every paragraph should reveal the student's actions, thinking, or growth.
- Two PIQs that show the same trait. Plan four responses that together show different sides.
- Treating cutting as losing content. Cutting filler usually makes the voice stronger.

### Day 1 (Monday): Decoding the prompts
*Student:* Read all eight UC Personal Insight Questions and the UC tips page. Chart which of your starred stories fits each prompt and choose your four.

*How to teach:* Read all 8 prompts aloud and chart which starred stories fit which prompts. The student chooses four and explains why each shows something different.

- **Q:** How many UC PIQs must a student answer and what is the word limit?
  **A:** Four of the eight, each up to 350 words.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft PIQ #1
*Student:* Draft the first PIQ response (350-word limit) using a 'moment → action → result → meaning' structure.

*How to teach:* Model the moment → action → result → meaning structure by outlining one story together. The student drafts PIQ #1. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** In 'moment → action → result → meaning,' what goes in 'meaning'?
  **A:** What the experience shows about the student — what they learned, value, or will bring to college.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft PIQ #2
*Student:* Draft a second PIQ on a different prompt, making sure it reveals a different side of you than PIQ #1.

*How to teach:* The student drafts PIQ #2 on a different prompt. Check: does it show a different quality than #1? Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** If PIQ #1 shows leadership, what might PIQ #2 show?
  **A:** A different side, e.g., intellectual curiosity, creativity, or overcoming a challenge.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Concision workshop
*Student:* Cut each draft by 15% by removing filler words (very, really, in order to), passive constructions, and repeated ideas. Track word counts before and after.

*How to teach:* Model cutting 15%: delete 'very,' 'really,' 'in order to,' change passive to active, remove repeated ideas. The student records word counts before and after.

- **Q:** Tighten: 'In order to really improve, the project was redone by me.'
  **A:** 'To improve, I redid the project.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Peer-style review
*Student:* Exchange drafts with a parent or mentor using a checklist (specific? answers the prompt? shows impact?). Write a revision plan for each response.

*How to teach:* Review drafts with the checklist (specific? answers the prompt? shows impact?). The student writes a revision plan for each.

- **Q:** What makes a revision plan useful?
  **A:** It names specific changes (e.g., 'replace paragraph 2 summary with the moment in the lab').

**Assessment:** Two complete PIQ drafts under 350 words with a written revision plan.

**Answer key:** Two PIQ drafts under 350 words plus revision plan. Rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): 4 = each draft answers its prompt directly, uses a specific moment, shows the student's action and impact, ends with insight; revision plan lists concrete changes. 3 = clear and specific with minor generalities. 2 = summary-heavy or off-prompt. 1 = incomplete or over the limit.

## Precalculus with Statistics: Parent functions and transformations

**Prepare:** Open Desmos and practice making a slider (type y = a(x − h)² + k and click 'add slider'). Print or draw a blank parent-function reference sheet with 8 small grids. Preview OpenStax 1.5 (Transformation of Functions).

**Watch for:**
- Moving the wrong way for horizontal shifts: f(x − 3) moves RIGHT 3. Have the student solve x − 3 = 0 to see the new 'starting' x is 3.
- Confusing k·f(x) (vertical stretch) with f(kx) (horizontal compression by 1/k). Test a point: for f(2x), the point (4, 2) on √x moves to (2, 2).
- Thinking 'even/odd' refers to the exponent only. Use the test f(−x) = f(x) (even) or f(−x) = −f(x) (odd); e.g. x² + x is neither.

### Day 1 (Monday): Parent library
*Student:* Sketch and memorize key points of 8 parent functions (linear, quadratic, cubic, absolute value, square root, cube root, reciprocal, exponential).

*How to teach:* Sketch each parent function together, plotting 3–5 key points (for √x: (0,0), (1,1), (4,2), (9,3)). The student draws all 8 from memory on the reference sheet and states domain and range for each. Check by covering the sheet and asking for key points aloud.

- **Q:** Give three key points of y = ∛x and its domain.
  **A:** (−8, −2), (−1, −1), (0, 0), (1, 1), (8, 2) — any three; domain all real numbers.
- **Q:** What is the horizontal asymptote of y = 2ˣ and of y = 1/x?
  **A:** y = 2ˣ: y = 0. y = 1/x: y = 0 (it also has vertical asymptote x = 0).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shifts
*Student:* Use Desmos sliders to discover the effects of f(x)+k and f(x+k); graph 10 shifted functions by hand.

*How to teach:* Use sliders on f(x) = (x − h)² + k and ask the student to predict before dragging. Then they graph the 10 shifted functions by hand, moving key points; verify in Desmos.

- **Q:** Describe y = (x − 2)² + 3 relative to y = x².
  **A:** Shift right 2 and up 3; vertex (2, 3).
- **Q:** Write the equation of y = |x| shifted left 4 and down 1.
  **A:** y = |x + 4| − 1

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stretches & reflections
*Student:* Explore k·f(x) and f(kx) with sliders; write equations for 8 transformed graphs.

*How to teach:* Explore y = a·f(x) and y = f(bx) with sliders, noting that a negative a reflects over the x-axis and negative b over the y-axis. The student writes equations for the 8 graphs by identifying the reflection, stretch, then shift. Check by graphing their equation over the target.

- **Q:** Describe y = −√(x + 1).
  **A:** Reflect √x over the x-axis and shift left 1; starts at (−1, 0) and goes down-right.
- **Q:** What does y = (2x)² do to the point (2, 4) on y = x²?
  **A:** It compresses horizontally by 1/2, so the point moves to (1, 4).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Even and odd
*Student:* Test 10 functions algebraically (f(−x)) and graphically for even/odd symmetry.

*How to teach:* Model the algebraic test: substitute −x and simplify, then compare to f(x) and −f(x). Show the graph symmetry (y-axis for even, origin for odd). The student tests the 10 functions both ways; check that the algebra matches the picture.

- **Q:** Is f(x) = x³ − x even, odd, or neither?
  **A:** Odd: f(−x) = −x³ + x = −(x³ − x) = −f(x).
- **Q:** Is g(x) = x² + x even, odd, or neither?
  **A:** Neither: g(−x) = x² − x, which equals neither g(x) nor −g(x).

### Day 5 (Friday): Transformation challenge
*Student:* Complete a Desmos 'match the graph' set of 12 multi-step transformations and take a short check.

*How to teach:* Run the Desmos match-the-graph set; the student writes equations and checks them by overlaying. Then give the 6-item check below on paper without technology.

- **Q:** Write the equation: y = x² reflected over the x-axis, stretched by 2, shifted right 1 and up 5.
  **A:** y = −2(x − 1)² + 5

**Assessment:** Transformation matching challenge.

**Answer key:** Check (6 items): 1) y = (x − 2)² + 3 → right 2, up 3, vertex (2, 3). 2) y = −√(x + 1) → reflect over x-axis, left 1, starts at (−1, 0). 3) y = 2|x| − 4 → vertical stretch 2, down 4, vertex (0, −4). 4) y = 1/(x − 3) + 1 → asymptotes x = 3, y = 1. 5) y = 2ˣ − 5 → down 5, asymptote y = −5. 6) y = −2(x − 1)² + 5. Matching challenge: 12/12 = mastery, 10–11 proficient, 7–9 developing (reteach horizontal shifts/stretches), below 7 reteach.

## Earth & Space / Environmental Science: Light, spectra, and the Big Bang

**Prepare:** Old CD/DVD, cardboard tube (paper towel roll), tape, foil with a thin slit; incandescent, LED, and fluorescent (CFL) light sources. Balloon and marker, ruler. Print element emission spectra (H, He, Na, Hg) from a NASA or university site.

**Watch for:**
- Picturing the Big Bang as an explosion into empty space from a center. Space itself expands everywhere; there is no center.
- Thinking redshift means objects are red. It means wavelengths are shifted toward longer (redder) values.
- Assuming individual galaxies or bound objects expand. Gravity holds galaxies together; expansion acts on large scales between galaxy groups.

### Day 1 (Monday): DIY spectroscope
*Student:* Build a spectroscope from a CD and cardboard tube; compare spectra of an incandescent bulb, LED, and fluorescent light and sketch the lines.

*How to teach:* Build the spectroscope (slit at one end, CD piece at an angle at the other). View an incandescent bulb (continuous rainbow), an LED (smooth with a strong blue peak), and a CFL (bright lines: violet-blue, green, yellow-orange from mercury and phosphors). Sketch each.

- **Q:** Why does an incandescent bulb give a continuous spectrum while a fluorescent lamp shows bright lines?
  **A:** A hot filament emits all wavelengths (thermal radiation); a fluorescent tube's excited mercury atoms (and phosphors) emit only specific wavelengths matching electron energy-level transitions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Spectral fingerprints
*Student:* Match unknown spectra to element spectra and explain how astronomers determine star composition.

*How to teach:* Match unknown spectra to element spectra; explain that each element's absorption/emission lines are a fingerprint, so star spectra reveal composition.

- **Q:** How do astronomers know what stars are made of?
  **A:** Dark absorption lines in a star's spectrum occur at wavelengths unique to each element; matching them to lab spectra identifies the elements present.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Expanding balloon
*Student:* Draw dots on a balloon, inflate it in stages, and measure distances between dots to model Hubble's law; graph velocity vs. distance.

*How to teach:* Mark 5 dots on a balloon; measure distances from one 'home' dot at three inflation stages. Compute speeds (change in distance ÷ time step) and graph velocity vs. distance — a straight line (Hubble's law).

- **Q:** In the balloon model, dots 2 cm and 4 cm from home move to 3 cm and 6 cm in one step. What are their speeds, and what does this show?
  **A:** 1 cm/step and 2 cm/step — twice as far moves twice as fast, so v ∝ d (Hubble's law).
- **Q:** A galaxy's hydrogen line (656.3 nm in the lab) is observed at 662.9 nm. Find its recession speed and distance (H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc).
  **A:** z = 6.6/656.3 ≈ 0.0101; v ≈ cz ≈ 3,000 km/s; d = v/H₀ ≈ 43 Mpc.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evidence for the Big Bang
*Student:* Read about redshifted galaxies, the cosmic microwave background (COBE/Planck), and the H/He ratio; organize the three lines of evidence in a table.

*How to teach:* Read about three lines of evidence and organize them in a table: redshift/expansion (Hubble, 1929), cosmic microwave background (discovered 1965; mapped by COBE, WMAP, Planck; ~2.7 K), and the abundance of light elements (~75% H, ~25% He by mass).

- **Q:** What is the cosmic microwave background?
  **A:** The leftover radiation from about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, when the universe cooled enough for atoms to form; now redshifted to microwaves at about 2.7 K, nearly uniform in every direction.

### Day 5 (Friday): Explanation
*Student:* Write a 1-page scientific explanation of the Big Bang theory citing all three lines of evidence.

*How to teach:* Student writes a 1-page scientific explanation of the Big Bang citing all three lines of evidence.

- **Q:** About how old is the universe?
  **A:** About 13.8 billion years.

**Assessment:** Big Bang explanation scored on evidence and reasoning.

**Answer key:** Big Bang explanation rubric. 4 – Clearly states the theory (universe expanded from a hot, dense state ~13.8 billion years ago); explains all three evidence lines with specifics (redshift proportional to distance/Hubble's law, CMB ~2.7 K uniform radiation, ~3:1 H:He mass ratio predicted by Big Bang nucleosynthesis); reasoning links each to the model; corrects the 'explosion from a center' misconception. 3 (proficient) – Three lines of evidence accurately explained. 2 – One or two lines or vague reasoning. 1 – Inaccurate.

## American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2): The Constitution and the Federalist Papers

**Prepare:** Use the National Constitution Center's Interactive Constitution. Print Federalist 10, 51 (excerpts), 78 (excerpts), Brutus No. 1 (excerpt), and Tocqueville on 'tyranny of the majority.' Prepare the Unit 1 test and DBQ documents.

**Watch for:**
- Reading Madison as anti-faction in the sense of banning parties. He argues you can't remove the causes of faction without destroying liberty, so you control its effects.
- Thinking the Anti-Federalists opposed any national government. They feared a distant, consolidated government and demanded a bill of rights.
- Confusing separation of powers with checks and balances. Separation divides functions; checks let each branch limit the others.

### Day 1 (Monday): Articles to Constitution
*Student:* List weaknesses of the Articles of Confederation and match each to a fix in the Constitution.

*How to teach:* List the Articles' weaknesses together. The student matches each to the Constitution's fix. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name three weaknesses of the Articles and the Constitution's fix.
  **A:** No power to tax (Congress may tax); no regulation of interstate commerce (Commerce Clause); no national executive or courts (Articles II and III); amendments required unanimity (Article V three-fourths).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Federalist 10
*Student:* Read Federalist 10 and explain Madison's argument that a large republic controls the effects of faction.

*How to teach:* Read Federalist 10 in chunks. The student explains Madison's argument in steps: definition, causes, cure. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What is a faction and why can't its causes be removed?
  **A:** A group united by an interest adverse to others' rights or the common good; its causes are sown in human nature and liberty, so removing them would destroy liberty.
- **Q:** How does a large republic control faction?
  **A:** It includes many interests, making it harder for any one faction to form a majority and oppress others; representatives filter public views.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Federalist 51 and 78
*Student:* Read excerpts of Federalist 51 ('ambition must be made to counteract ambition') and 78 (independent judiciary); diagram checks and balances.

*How to teach:* Read 51 and 78 excerpts. The student diagrams checks and balances with an example for each arrow. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Explain 'ambition must be made to counteract ambition.'
  **A:** Each branch is given the means and motive to resist encroachment by the others; 'If men were angels, no government would be necessary.'
- **Q:** Why does Hamilton call the judiciary the 'least dangerous' branch?
  **A:** It has 'neither force nor will, but merely judgment' — no army or purse.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Anti-Federalists and Tocqueville
*Student:* Read Brutus No. 1 and a Tocqueville excerpt on 'tyranny of the majority'; explain how the Bill of Rights answered these fears.

*How to teach:* Read Brutus No. 1 and the Tocqueville excerpt. The student explains how the Bill of Rights answered these fears. Before moving on, have the student answer the check questions below without notes, then compare with the answers and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** What did Brutus fear?
  **A:** That a large republic couldn't work and the Necessary and Proper and Supremacy Clauses would let the national government swallow the states.
- **Q:** What is 'tyranny of the majority'?
  **A:** Tocqueville's warning that a democratic majority can oppress minorities through law and public opinion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test with a DBQ-style essay on how the Constitution limits government power.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test with a DBQ essay on how the Constitution limits government power. Before moving on, have the student answer the check question below without notes, then compare with the answer and reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** Name three ways the Constitution limits government power.
  **A:** Separation of powers, checks and balances, federalism (also enumerated powers and the Bill of Rights).

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test with document-based essay.

**Answer key:** Test key: Articles weaknesses/fixes; Fed 10 faction; Fed 51 checks; Fed 78 judiciary; Brutus fears; Tocqueville. DBQ rubric (4 = exemplary, 3 = proficient, 2 = developing, 1 = beginning): 4 = thesis naming specific limits (separation of powers, checks, federalism, Bill of Rights), uses 4+ documents with accurate interpretation, outside knowledge, explains why limits matter.

## Advanced Art & Portfolio: Idea generation strategies

**Prepare:** Sketchbook, magazines, scissors, glue stick. Bookmark Smithsonian Learning Lab and the National Gallery of Art open-access images (NGA Images).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing the first idea. Strong work usually comes from pushing past obvious ideas.
- Thumbnails that are too detailed. Thumbnails are small, fast composition plans (value and placement), not finished drawings.
- Using images found online as if they were free to copy. Use open-access or your own photos and cite sources.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mind map
*Student:* Create a mind map around a single word (e.g., 'home') and branch to 30 visual ideas.

*How to teach:* Student writes one word in the center and branches to 30 visual ideas (objects, places, memories, metaphors).

- **Q:** Why use a mind map before drawing?
  **A:** It generates many associations quickly, helping move past obvious ideas to more personal, original ones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): 20 thumbnails
*Student:* Draw 20 two-inch thumbnails exploring compositions from your mind map.

*How to teach:* Student draws 20 two-inch thumbnails varying viewpoint, cropping, and value.

- **Q:** What should a thumbnail sketch decide?
  **A:** Composition: placement of main shapes, viewpoint, cropping, and basic value pattern.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Image research
*Student:* Collect 10 reference images from Smithsonian Learning Lab or NGA open access that connect to your ideas.

*How to teach:* Collect 10 references from open-access collections, noting source and credit line.

- **Q:** What does 'open access' mean for museum images?
  **A:** The museum has released the images (often public domain) for free use, including reuse and adaptation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Collage brainstorm
*Student:* Make a quick magazine collage that combines unexpected images into a new idea.

*How to teach:* Make a quick magazine collage combining unexpected images into a new idea.

- **Q:** How can collage help generate ideas?
  **A:** Juxtaposing unrelated images creates surprising combinations and new meanings.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share & check
*Student:* Present your three strongest ideas to a family member and record their feedback.

*How to teach:* Present three strongest ideas to a family member; record feedback.

- **Q:** What makes feedback useful?
  **A:** It is specific — what the viewer noticed, what they felt or understood, and questions it raised.

**Assessment:** Sketchbook idea pages.

**Answer key:** Sketchbook idea pages rubric. 4 – Mind map with 30+ ideas, 20 varied thumbnails, 10 credited references, collage, feedback recorded and three ideas developed with notes. 3 (proficient) – All components complete with variety. 2 – Missing components or repetitive thumbnails. 1 – Minimal.

## Lifetime Fitness II: Heart rate and intensity

**Prepare:** Stopwatch or watch, jump rope, graph paper or spreadsheet. Compute the student's Karvonen zones in advance from age and week 1 resting HR.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying intensity percent by max HR in the Karvonen formula. Use heart rate reserve: (HRmax − HRrest) × % + HRrest.
- Assuming 220 − age is exact. It's an estimate with a ±10–12 bpm error; use RPE to cross-check.
- Thinking higher HR is always better training. Different zones train different qualities.

### Day 1 (Monday): Calculate zones
*Student:* Calculate target heart rate zones with the Karvonen formula; do a 20-minute brisk walk checking pulse every 5 minutes.

*How to teach:* Compute Karvonen zones: HRmax = 220 − age; HRR = HRmax − HRrest; THR = HRR × % + HRrest. Then a 20-minute brisk walk with pulse checks every 5 minutes.

- **Q:** Age 17, resting HR 60. Calculate 60% and 80% of HRR.
  **A:** HRmax 203, HRR 143. 60%: 85.8 + 60 ≈ 146 bpm. 80%: 114.4 + 60 ≈ 174 bpm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Intervals
*Student:* Do jump-rope intervals (30 s on / 30 s off × 10) and record heart rate and RPE.

*How to teach:* Jump-rope intervals (30 s on/30 s off ×10), recording HR and RPE after each work bout.

- **Q:** On a 0–10 RPE scale, about where is vigorous intensity?
  **A:** About 7–8 (moderate ≈ 5–6).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Steady state
*Student:* Jog or cycle 25 minutes holding 60–70% heart rate reserve.

*How to teach:* Jog or cycle 25 minutes holding 60–70% HRR; check HR every 5 minutes and adjust.

- **Q:** Your HR drifts upward late in a steady run at constant pace. What is this called?
  **A:** Cardiovascular drift — caused by rising body temperature and fluid loss; slow down slightly to stay in zone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Circuit
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute bodyweight circuit (squats, lunges, push-ups, planks) and track RPE.

*How to teach:* Bodyweight circuit for 20 minutes; track RPE.

- **Q:** Why might a circuit raise HR even though it's 'strength' work?
  **A:** Moving continuously between exercises with little rest keeps large muscle groups working, raising cardiovascular demand.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Graph the week's heart-rate data and explain which workouts hit your zone.

*How to teach:* Graph HR for each workout vs. the target zone band; student explains which workouts hit the zone.

- **Q:** What does a fast drop in HR after exercise indicate?
  **A:** Good cardiovascular fitness (quick heart rate recovery).

**Assessment:** Heart rate data analysis.

**Answer key:** Heart rate data analysis rubric. 4 – Correct Karvonen calculations shown, graph with zone band, all five workouts plotted, accurate explanation of which hit the zone and why, with an adjustment plan. 3 (proficient) – Correct calculations, graph, explanation. 2 – Calculation errors or missing graph. 1 – Incomplete.

## Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2): Paychecks, pay stubs & the W-4

**Prepare:** Download an NGPF sample pay stub, a blank IRS Form W-4 (irs.gov) and a blank CA EDD Form DE 4 (edd.ca.gov). Look up the current California minimum wage (dir.ca.gov; $16.90/hour statewide in 2026 — some cities are higher) and current FICA rates (Social Security 6.2%, Medicare 1.45%) and the current CA SDI rate (edd.ca.gov).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking gross pay is what lands in the bank. Net pay is gross pay minus taxes and deductions.
- Missing California daily overtime. California requires 1.5× pay for hours over 8 in a day or over 40 in a week (and 2× over 12 in a day), not only over 40 per week.
- Thinking withholding is the final tax bill. Withholding is a prepayment; the tax return settles up with a refund or amount owed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gross vs. net
*Student:* Calculate gross pay for hourly jobs at California minimum wage with overtime (over 8 hours/day or 40/week) for three sample schedules.

*How to teach:* Teach California overtime. Compute gross pay at $16.90 for three schedules: 5 × 8 hours; 4 × 10 hours; 6 × 8 hours.

- **Q:** 5 days × 8 hours at $16.90?
  **A:** 40 regular hours × $16.90 = $676.00.
- **Q:** 4 days × 10 hours?
  **A:** Each day has 2 overtime hours: 32 × $16.90 = $540.80 + 8 × $25.35 = $202.80 → $743.60.
- **Q:** 6 days × 8 hours (48 hours, no day over 8)?
  **A:** 40 × $16.90 = $676.00 + 8 weekly-overtime hours × $25.35 = $202.80 → $878.80.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading a pay stub
*Student:* Label every line on an NGPF sample pay stub (federal and state withholding, Social Security, Medicare, CA SDI, YTD) and find net pay.

*How to teach:* Label every line on the sample pay stub: gross pay, federal income tax withholding, state (CA PIT) withholding, Social Security (OASDI), Medicare, CA SDI, year-to-date (YTD) totals, net pay.

- **Q:** What does FICA fund?
  **A:** Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) — a total of 7.65% of wages, matched by the employer.
- **Q:** What does CA SDI fund?
  **A:** California State Disability Insurance and Paid Family Leave — partial wage replacement if you can't work due to illness, injury, pregnancy or caring for family.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Form W-4
*Student:* Fill out a practice IRS Form W-4 for a single teen with one job and explain each step in writing.

*How to teach:* Walk through the W-4: Step 1 (personal info and filing status), Step 2 (multiple jobs), Step 3 (dependents), Step 4 (other adjustments), Step 5 (sign). A single teen with one job fills Steps 1 and 5 only.

- **Q:** Which W-4 steps does a single teen with one job and no dependents complete?
  **A:** Step 1 and Step 5 (sign); Steps 2–4 are left blank unless needed.
- **Q:** What happens if too little tax is withheld?
  **A:** You may owe money (and possibly a penalty) when you file your return.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Form DE 4 and benefits
*Student:* Complete a practice CA DE 4 and compare two job offers that include health insurance and 401(k) match to see total compensation.

*How to teach:* Complete the DE 4 (California withholding). Then compare two offers including health insurance and 401(k) match to find total compensation.

- **Q:** Offer A: $45,000 + employer pays $3,000 toward health insurance + 4% 401(k) match. Total compensation?
  **A:** $45,000 + $3,000 + $1,800 = $49,800 (if you contribute at least 4%).
- **Q:** Why does California have its own withholding form?
  **A:** California's income tax rules and allowances differ from federal; the DE 4 sets state withholding.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play NGPF 'Paycheck' scenarios and solve five net-pay problems on a short quiz.

*How to teach:* Play NGPF paycheck scenarios, then give the 5-problem net-pay quiz in the key.

- **Q:** Gross pay is $800. What is the total FICA withheld?
  **A:** $800 × 7.65% = $61.20.

**Assessment:** Net-pay quiz and accuracy check of practice W-4/DE 4.

**Answer key:** Net-pay quiz (use $16.90/hour; Social Security 6.2%, Medicare 1.45%): 1) 30 hours → gross $507.00. 2) Social Security on $507 = $31.43; Medicare = $7.35. 3) 5 days × 9 hours → 40 regular + 5 daily-OT hours: 40 × 16.90 = $676.00 + 5 × 25.35 = $126.75 → $803.25. 4) Net pay if gross $600 and total deductions $92 → $508. 5) FICA total rate = 7.65%; on $1,000 = $76.50. Practice W-4 check: single, one job, no dependents → Step 1 completed, Steps 2–4 left blank, Step 5 signed and dated. DE 4: single, allowances claimed consistent with the worksheet (often 1 for a single filer with one job). Benefits comparison: Offer A $45,000 + $3,000 health contribution + 4% match ($1,800) = $49,800 total compensation; compare similarly for B.
