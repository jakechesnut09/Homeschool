# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 2

## English 9: Point of view and mood: Poe's "The Cask of Amontillado"

**Prepare:** Print "The Cask of Amontillado" (Project Gutenberg). Read it first; look up 'catacombs', 'nitre', 'motley', 'trowel'. Prepare a phrases reference sheet (prepositional, appositive, participial).

**Watch for:**
- Taking Montresor at his word: he never names a single specific injury, so readers should question his reliability.
- Mixing up verbal and dramatic irony: verbal = a character says the opposite of what he means; dramatic = the reader knows something a character doesn't.
- Thinking every -ing word starts a participial phrase: in 'He was walking,' walking is a verb; a participial phrase acts as an adjective ('Walking deeper, Fortunato coughed').

### Day 1 (Monday): Narrator and point of view
*Student:* Read "The Cask of Amontillado" aloud; list every claim Montresor makes about Fortunato's "thousand injuries" and judge whether he is reliable.

*How to teach:* Read the story aloud together (about 20 minutes). Your child lists every claim Montresor makes about Fortunato and the 'thousand injuries,' then decides whether he is reliable.

- **Q:** Does Montresor ever tell us what Fortunato's 'insult' was?
  **A:** No. He mentions a 'thousand injuries' and an insult but never gives specifics, which makes him an unreliable narrator.
- **Q:** Who is the narrator and what point of view is used?
  **A:** Montresor, in first person, telling the story 'half of a century' later.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Irony hunt
*Student:* Find five examples of verbal or dramatic irony (the trowel, "I drink to your long life") and explain the effect of each in a two-column chart.

*How to teach:* Define verbal, situational, and dramatic irony. Model one example, then your child completes a five-example chart with effects.

- **Q:** What is ironic about Montresor showing a trowel when Fortunato asks if he is a mason?
  **A:** Fortunato means the secret society (Freemasons); Montresor means a literal mason — he will use the trowel to wall Fortunato in. Dramatic/verbal irony.
- **Q:** Why is 'I drink to your long life' ironic?
  **A:** Montresor plans to kill Fortunato that night — verbal irony.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diction and mood
*Student:* Highlight words in the catacomb scenes that create dread; rewrite one paragraph in a cheerful tone to see how diction controls mood.

*How to teach:* Highlight dread-building words in the catacombs (damp, nitre, bones, darkness). Your child rewrites one paragraph in a cheerful tone and notes what changed.

- **Q:** Name three words or images in the catacomb scenes that create dread.
  **A:** Examples: piles of human bones, damp nitre 'hanging like moss', flambeaux that barely glow, the 'foulness of the air', the recess walled with stones.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: phrases
*Student:* Learn prepositional, appositive, and participial phrases; combine 10 choppy sentences from your own notebook using phrases.

*How to teach:* Teach prepositional (in the vault), appositive (Fortunato, a wine expert,) and participial (Laughing loudly, Fortunato...) phrases. Your child combines 10 sentences from their notebook and labels phrase types.

- **Q:** Identify the phrase type: 'Fortunato, a proud man, followed Montresor into the vaults.'
  **A:** 'a proud man' — appositive phrase; 'into the vaults' — prepositional phrase.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a timed paragraph (25 min) answering: How does Poe make Montresor's revenge chilling? Take a phrases mini-quiz.

*How to teach:* Timed paragraph (25 minutes), then the phrases mini-quiz. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What are Fortunato's last words, and how does Montresor answer?
  **A:** 'For the love of God, Montresor!' Montresor replies, 'Yes, for the love of God!' — mocking his plea.

**Assessment:** Timed analytical paragraph scored on claim, two cited quotations, and commentary; phrase identification quiz.

**Answer key:** Timed paragraph proficient answer: claims Poe makes the revenge chilling through Montresor's calm, unreliable narration and irony; cites e.g. 'I drink to your long life' and 'Yes, for the love of God!' or 'My heart grew sick — on account of the dampness of the catacombs'; explains how his cold control and lack of remorse after fifty years ('In pace requiescat!') horrify the reader. Paragraph rubric: 4 = arguable claim, two well-chosen quotations embedded and cited, commentary that explains HOW the evidence proves the claim; 3 = claim and evidence with thinner commentary; 2 = summary instead of analysis or only one piece of evidence; 1 = off-topic or no evidence. Phrase quiz sample: 1) 'beneath the palazzo' — prepositional. 2) 'my friend' (in 'Fortunato, my friend,') — appositive. 3) 'Coughing violently' — participial. 4) 'during the carnival season' — prepositional. 5) 'wearing a jester's cap' — participial.

## Algebra I: Interpreting algebraic expressions

**Prepare:** Make expression cards (index cards with expressions such as 3(x+2) − 5x², 4x² − 7x + 9, 1.08p, 50 + 12h). Open Desmos to check equivalence by graphing both expressions.

**Watch for:**
- Saying the coefficient of −5x² is 5: the sign belongs to the coefficient, so it is −5.
- Distributing only to the first term: 3(x + 2) is 3x + 6, not 3x + 2. Draw arrows from the 3 to each term.
- Combining unlike terms (x² + x = x³): only terms with the same variable and exponent combine.

### Day 1 (Monday): Parts of expressions
*Student:* Label terms, coefficients, factors, and constants in 15 expressions such as 3(x+2)−5x².

*How to teach:* Label one expression together: in 3(x+2) − 5x², the terms are 3(x+2) and −5x²; 3 and (x+2) are factors of the first term; −5 is the coefficient of x². Your child labels the remaining 14, then you spot-check signs.

- **Q:** In 4x² − 7x + 9, name the terms, the coefficients, and the constant.
  **A:** Terms: 4x², −7x, 9. Coefficients: 4 and −7. Constant: 9.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Expressions in context
*Student:* Interpret expressions like 1.08p and 50+12h in shopping and phone-plan contexts.

*How to teach:* Discuss how an expression tells a story: 1.08p = p + 0.08p, the price plus 8% tax. Your child interprets each contextual expression in a full sentence, naming what each number means.

- **Q:** What does 50 + 12h mean for a phone repair service that charges by the hour?
  **A:** A $50 flat fee plus $12 for each hour h of work.
- **Q:** Write an expression for a price p after a 15% discount.
  **A:** 0.85p (p − 0.15p).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Order of operations and evaluating
*Student:* Evaluate 20 expressions with integers, fractions, and exponents for given values.

*How to teach:* Review order of operations (parentheses, exponents, multiply/divide left to right, add/subtract left to right). Model substituting with parentheses: for x = −2, 2x² − 3x + 1 = 2(−2)² − 3(−2) + 1. Your child evaluates the 20 expressions.

- **Q:** Evaluate 2x² − 3x + 1 for x = −2.
  **A:** 2(4) + 6 + 1 = 15.
- **Q:** Evaluate (a + b)/2 · a for a = 1/2, b = 3/2.
  **A:** (2)/2 · 1/2 = 1 · 1/2 = 1/2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Simplifying
*Student:* Combine like terms and use the distributive property to simplify 20 expressions; verify equivalence by substitution.

*How to teach:* Model simplifying 3(2x − 4) − (x − 5): distribute (6x − 12 − x + 5), then combine like terms to 5x − 7. Then check equivalence by substituting x = 1 into both forms. Your child simplifies 20 and checks at least 5 by substitution.

- **Q:** Simplify 3(2x − 4) − (x − 5) and check with x = 1.
  **A:** 5x − 7. Check: original 3(−2) − (−4) = −2; simplified 5 − 7 = −2. ✓

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a quiz on interpreting and simplifying expressions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz from the key without notes. Score it together and have your child redo any missed problem with a substitution check.

- **Q:** Simplify 2(3a − b) + 4b.
  **A:** 6a + 2b.

**Assessment:** Expression quiz (85%+).

**Answer key:** Quiz answers (85% = proficient): 1) 5x³ − 2x + 7 has 3 terms, leading coefficient 5, constant 7. 2) 0.85p = price after a 15% discount. 3) In 20 + 0.15m, 20 is a fixed monthly fee and 0.15 is the cost per minute. 4) x² − 4x at x = −3 → 21. 5) (2x + 1)/3 at x = 4 → 3. 6) 4(x + 3) − 2(x − 1) → 2x + 14. 7) 2(3a − b) + 4b → 6a + 2b. 8) 5 − 2(x − 1) → 7 − 2x. 9) Are 3(x + 1) and 3x + 1 equivalent? No — at x = 0 they give 3 and 1.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Population growth and carrying capacity

**Prepare:** Three clean plastic bottles, three balloons, active dry yeast, sugar, warm (not hot, about 40°C/105°F) water, string and ruler for circumference, timer. Dried beans and graph paper. Find the St. Matthew Island reindeer data (29 reindeer introduced in 1944; about 6,000 by 1963; 42 by 1966).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking populations grow forever: resources limit growth; logistic growth levels off at carrying capacity (K).
- Confusing density-dependent and density-independent factors: density-dependent effects get stronger as the population gets crowded (food, disease); density-independent factors affect populations regardless of size (fire, freeze).
- Believing carrying capacity is fixed: overuse can damage the habitat and lower K.

### Day 1 (Monday): Growth models
*Student:* Graph exponential vs. logistic growth from a sample data set and identify carrying capacity.

*How to teach:* Graph sample data for exponential (J-curve) and logistic (S-curve) growth; mark K.

- **Q:** Describe the shape of a logistic growth curve and where carrying capacity is.
  **A:** S-shaped: slow start, rapid growth, then leveling off; K is the level where the curve flattens.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Yeast balloon lab
*Student:* Mix yeast, warm water, and 0/1/2 tsp sugar in three bottles with balloons; measure balloon circumference every 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Mix 1 tsp yeast + 1 cup warm water in each bottle with 0, 1, or 2 tsp sugar; attach balloons; measure circumference every 10 minutes.

- **Q:** Predict which balloon inflates most and why.
  **A:** The 2-tsp bottle — yeast use sugar for respiration/fermentation and release CO2; more food supports more activity. The 0-sugar bottle barely inflates.
- **Q:** What is the independent variable?
  **A:** Amount of sugar; the dependent variable is balloon circumference.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Limiting factors
*Student:* Analyze the yeast data: which factor limited growth? Classify density-dependent and independent factors.

*How to teach:* Analyze the yeast data; classify limiting factors.

- **Q:** Classify: disease, wildfire, food supply, drought.
  **A:** Density-dependent: disease, food supply. Density-independent: wildfire, drought (weather).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Case study
*Student:* Analyze the St. Matthew Island reindeer data and explain the crash using carrying capacity.

*How to teach:* Analyze the reindeer data; explain the crash.

- **Q:** Why did the St. Matthew Island reindeer crash?
  **A:** With no predators, the population overshot carrying capacity, overgrazed the slow-growing lichen, and then starved during a harsh winter (1963–64), dropping from about 6,000 to 42.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on population growth with a graph interpretation.

*How to teach:* Quiz with a graph interpretation.

- **Q:** On a logistic curve, when is the population growing fastest?
  **A:** In the middle (about half of K), where the curve is steepest.

**Assessment:** Lab report on yeast growth and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Quiz answers: 1) J-curve = exponential, unlimited resources. 2) S-curve = logistic. 3) K = carrying capacity — the maximum population the environment can support. 4) Limiting factor examples (food, space, water). 5) Density-dependent example: disease. 6) Density-independent: hurricane. 7) Graph interpretation: identify K as the plateau value and the fastest growth at the inflection point.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Maps, projections, and geospatial technology

**Prepare:** An orange and a marker; printouts of Mercator, Robinson, and Gall-Peters maps; a Library of Congress historical map (loc.gov/maps); Google Earth or ArcGIS Online (free).

**Watch for:**
- Believing Greenland is as big as Africa: Mercator inflates areas near the poles; Africa is about 14 times larger.
- Thinking any map is 'correct': every flat map distorts shape, area, distance, or direction.
- Misusing scale: 1:100,000 means 1 unit on the map = 100,000 of the same units on the ground.

### Day 1 (Monday): Projections
*Student:* Peel an orange to see why flat maps distort; compare Mercator, Robinson, and Peters projections.

*How to teach:* Draw continents on an orange, peel it, and flatten; compare projections.

- **Q:** What does the Mercator projection preserve and distort?
  **A:** It preserves shapes locally and compass direction (useful for navigation) but greatly distorts area near the poles.
- **Q:** What does the Gall-Peters projection preserve?
  **A:** Relative area (equal-area), while distorting shapes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map types
*Student:* Analyze a historical map from the Library of Congress and identify its purpose and point of view.

*How to teach:* Analyze a historical map's purpose and point of view.

- **Q:** What questions should you ask about a historical map?
  **A:** Who made it, when, for whom, for what purpose, and what it emphasizes or leaves out.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): GIS
*Student:* Use an online GIS map (ArcGIS or Google Earth) to layer population and rivers.

*How to teach:* Layer population and rivers in GIS.

- **Q:** What pattern often appears when population and rivers are layered?
  **A:** Dense populations cluster along rivers (water, farmland, transport) — e.g., the Nile, Ganges, Yangtze.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scale
*Student:* Calculate real distances using map scales for three trips.

*How to teach:* Calculate real distances with scales.

- **Q:** On a 1:100,000 map, two towns are 7.5 cm apart. Real distance?
  **A:** 7.5 × 100,000 cm = 750,000 cm = 7.5 km.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on projections and map types.

*How to teach:* Quiz.

- **Q:** Which projection is a compromise that distorts everything a little?
  **A:** The Robinson projection.

**Assessment:** Projection quiz and GIS screenshot analysis.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Mercator — conformal, area distortion at high latitudes. 2) Gall-Peters — equal area. 3) Robinson — compromise. 4) Thematic map shows a topic (e.g., population density); reference map shows locations. 5) GIS layers data on maps. 6) Scale problem: 1 inch = 50 miles, 3.5 inches → 175 miles. GIS screenshot analysis: proficient = identifies layers and describes a spatial pattern with an explanation.

## Spanish II: Mi rutina diaria: reflexive verbs

**Prepare:** Make or print picture cards for the daily routine (waking, showering, brushing teeth, dressing, going to bed) and set out a clock with movable hands. Find a short Spanish 'mi rutina' video (search 'mi rutina diaria' on YouTube) and preview it.

**Watch for:**
- Dropping the reflexive pronoun (Despierto a las seis): with reflexive verbs the pronoun matches the subject — Me despierto a las seis.
- Attaching the pronoun wrongly (Me voy a ducharme): either 'Me voy a duchar' or 'Voy a ducharme', not both.
- Forgetting stem changes inside reflexives: despertarse (e→ie) me despierto; acostarse (o→ue) me acuesto; vestirse (e→i) me visto.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reflexive verbs
*Student:* Learn reflexive pronouns and conjugate 10 routine verbs; label pictures of a routine.

*How to teach:* Show the pronoun chart (me, te, se, nos, os, se) and act out 'me lavo las manos' vs. 'lavo el carro' to show the action returns to the subject. Your child conjugates 10 routine verbs and labels the picture cards; check the pronouns and stem changes.

- **Q:** Conjugate levantarse for yo, tú, and ellos.
  **A:** me levanto, te levantas, se levantan
- **Q:** Why is it 'Me lavo las manos' and not 'Me lavo mis manos'?
  **A:** With reflexive verbs, Spanish uses the definite article for body parts because the pronoun already shows whose hands they are.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mi horario
*Student:* Write your weekday routine with times (A las seis y media me despierto...).

*How to teach:* Model telling time with a routine: 'A las seis y media me despierto. A las siete menos cuarto me ducho.' Your child writes the full weekday routine with times; check 'a la una' (singular) vs. 'a las dos'.

- **Q:** Write 'At 7:15 I get dressed.'
  **A:** A las siete y cuarto me visto.
- **Q:** Write 'At 1:00 I eat lunch.'
  **A:** A la una almuerzo.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening
*Student:* Listen to a Spanish speaker describing a routine (SpanishDict or a Spanish YouTube vlog) and fill a timeline.

*How to teach:* Play the routine video once just to listen, then again pausing to fill a timeline (time + activity). Your child compares the timeline with the speaker's words and you check two items by replaying.

- **Q:** If the speaker says 'Me acuesto a las once de la noche,' what time does he go to bed?
  **A:** 11:00 p.m.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing routines
*Student:* Compare your routine with a teen in Spain (late dinner, siesta) in a Venn diagram.

*How to teach:* Explain that in Spain dinner is often around 9–10 p.m., the main meal (la comida) is around 2–3 p.m., and the long midday break is more common than a daily nap. Your child fills a Venn diagram comparing their routine to a Spanish teen's and writes 3 comparison sentences.

- **Q:** Write a comparison: 'I eat dinner at 6; a teen in Spain eats at 9.'
  **A:** Yo ceno a las seis, pero un joven en España cena a las nueve.
- **Q:** What is 'la comida' in Spain?
  **A:** The main midday meal, usually eaten around 2–3 p.m.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Reflexive verb quiz and oral description of your routine.

*How to teach:* Give the reflexive quiz, then have your child describe the routine aloud from memory using times and transition words (primero, luego, después, finalmente). Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Say 'First I wake up, then I shower, and finally I get dressed.'
  **A:** Primero me despierto, luego me ducho y finalmente me visto.

**Assessment:** Reflexive quiz and oral routine description.

**Answer key:** Reflexive quiz sample key: 1) yo/levantarse — me levanto 2) tú/ducharse — te duchas 3) ella/vestirse — se viste 4) nosotros/acostarse — nos acostamos 5) ellos/despertarse — se despiertan 6) yo/cepillarse los dientes — me cepillo los dientes. Oral routine rubric: 4 = 8+ routine actions in order with times and correct pronouns; 3 = 6–7 actions, minor errors; 2 = under 6 actions or frequent pronoun errors; 1 = isolated words.

## Visual Art I: Shape, gesture, and proportion

**Prepare:** Sketchbook, pencil, timer, three household objects, a chair and plant. Teach sighting: arm straight, close one eye, pencil as a measuring stick.

**Watch for:**
- Bending the elbow while sighting. Correct: lock the arm straight so measurements stay consistent.
- Focusing on details too early. Correct: block in big shapes and proportions first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shapes underneath
*Student:* Block in a still life of three household objects using basic geometric shapes before refining edges.

*How to teach:* Block in a still life with basic shapes, then refine.

- **Q:** Why start with geometric shapes?
  **A:** They establish proportion, placement and structure before details.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sighting and measuring
*Student:* Learn pencil sighting to measure proportions; draw a chair accurately using sighting.

*How to teach:* Sight-measure a chair.

- **Q:** How does sighting work?
  **A:** Hold the pencil at arm's length, align the tip with one edge and mark with the thumb to measure a unit, then compare other lengths and angles to it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Gesture
*Student:* Do 20 thirty-second gesture drawings of a family member doing household tasks or of figures in a sports video.

*How to teach:* Twenty 30-second gesture drawings.

- **Q:** What should a gesture drawing capture?
  **A:** The action, movement and overall pose — the 'line of action' — not details.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Negative space
*Student:* Draw only the negative spaces around a chair or plant to correct shape errors.

*How to teach:* Draw only the negative spaces around a chair or plant.

- **Q:** What is negative space?
  **A:** The empty space around and between objects.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a 45-minute proportion drawing of a still life and self-check with sighting.

*How to teach:* 45-minute proportion drawing; self-check with sighting.

- **Q:** Why does drawing negative space fix errors?
  **A:** Negative shapes are unfamiliar, so you draw what you see instead of what you assume.

**Assessment:** Proportion still life evaluated for accurate shapes and proportions.

**Answer key:** Proportion still life: 4 = accurate relative sizes and placement verified by sighting, correct ellipses/angles, refined edges, composition fills the page; 3 = minor proportion errors; 2 = noticeable distortions; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Heart rate and the FITT principle

**Prepare:** Stopwatch or phone; optional HR app. Teach the radial or carotid pulse (count 15 s × 4). RPE (0–10) chart.

**Watch for:**
- Measuring pulse with the thumb. Correct: use index and middle fingers; the thumb has its own pulse.
- Thinking 220 − age is exact. Correct: it's an estimate; individuals vary by ±10–12 bpm, so also use RPE.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heart rate
*Student:* Measure resting heart rate for three mornings; calculate maximum (220 − age) and a 60–85% target zone.

*How to teach:* Measure resting HR three mornings; compute max and 60–85% zone.

- **Q:** For a 15-year-old, what is the estimated max HR and 60–85% target zone?
  **A:** Max = 220 − 15 = 205 bpm; 60% = 123, 85% ≈ 174, so about 123–174 bpm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Intensity test
*Student:* Jog, walk, and sprint intervals for 20 minutes, checking heart rate after each and recording RPE.

*How to teach:* 20 minutes of jog, walk and sprint intervals; check HR and RPE after each.

- **Q:** Which interval should produce the highest HR and RPE?
  **A:** The sprint interval.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): FITT
*Student:* Design a 30-minute cardio workout labeling frequency, intensity, time, and type, then complete it.

*How to teach:* Design a FITT cardio workout and complete it.

- **Q:** Write FITT for a cardio workout.
  **A:** Example: F 4 days/week; I 70–80% max HR (~145–165 bpm); T 30 minutes; T jogging.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interval training
*Student:* Complete a 25-minute interval workout (1 min hard, 2 min easy) staying in your target zone.

*How to teach:* 25-minute interval workout: 1 min hard, 2 min easy, staying in zone.

- **Q:** Why does interval training work?
  **A:** Alternating hard and easy efforts allows more total time at high intensity, improving aerobic and anaerobic fitness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on heart rate calculations and FITT; turn in your activity log.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; collect the log.

- **Q:** Your 15-second pulse count is 38. What's your HR?
  **A:** 38 × 4 = 152 bpm.

**Assessment:** Heart rate calculations and FITT workout plan.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) max HR = 220 − age 2) target zone 60–85% 3) 16-year-old max 204, zone ≈ 122–173 4) 15-s count × 4 5) FITT = Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type 6) lower resting HR generally reflects better cardio fitness 7) RPE = rating of perceived exertion 8) interval training alternates hard and easy. FITT plan: all four elements labeled and realistic.

## Health & Wellness: Decision making and goal setting

**Prepare:** Print a list of 15 values, a DECIDE worksheet and a 6-week goal tracker. Write 4 scenarios (staying up late before a test, skipping breakfast, party invite, sports injury).

**Watch for:**
- Making decisions only on what feels good now. Correct: consider consequences for health, family, goals and values.
- Writing goals that are too vague to track. Correct: SMART goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Values inventory
*Student:* Rank 15 personal values (family, honesty, fitness, faith, achievement…) and explain how your top three guide health choices.

*How to teach:* The child ranks values and explains how the top three affect health choices.

- **Q:** How could valuing 'achievement' guide a health choice?
  **A:** Example: choosing to sleep 8–10 hours rather than staying up gaming because rest improves performance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): DECIDE model
*Student:* Learn the DECIDE steps (Define, Explore, Consider, Identify, Decide, Evaluate) and apply them to a scenario about staying up late before a test.

*How to teach:* Teach DECIDE: Define the problem, Explore options, Consider consequences, Identify values, Decide and act, Evaluate. Apply to the late-night scenario.

- **Q:** What does the 'E' at the end of DECIDE mean?
  **A:** Evaluate — look back at the result and ask whether it was a good decision and what you learned.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Decision practice
*Student:* Work through three more scenarios (skipping breakfast, a party invite, a sports injury) with the DECIDE model, writing pros and cons for each option.

*How to teach:* The child completes DECIDE worksheets for three scenarios with pros and cons.

- **Q:** For a sports injury that still hurts, what's a healthy decision?
  **A:** Stop playing, tell the coach/parent and see a medical provider before returning.

### Day 4 (Thursday): SMART goals
*Student:* Rewrite five vague goals as SMART goals, then write one SMART health goal for yourself with a 6-week tracker.

*How to teach:* Rewrite five vague goals; then the child writes a SMART health goal and a 6-week tracker.

- **Q:** Make 'get fit' SMART.
  **A:** Example: 'I will jog 20 minutes 4 days a week for 6 weeks and record each run on my tracker.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present your SMART goal and action plan to a parent and complete a short quiz on decision-making steps.

*How to teach:* The child presents the goal; give the quiz.

- **Q:** List the six DECIDE steps.
  **A:** Define, Explore, Consider, Identify values, Decide, Evaluate.

**Assessment:** Completed DECIDE worksheet and SMART goal action plan.

**Answer key:** DECIDE worksheet: proficient if all six steps are completed with at least three options and realistic consequences. SMART goal: 1 pt per letter + tracker = 6. Quiz key: 1) Define the problem 2) Explore options 3) Consider consequences 4) Identify values 5) Decide and act 6) Evaluate 7) SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound 8) values guide decisions.
