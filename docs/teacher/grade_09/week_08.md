# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 8

## English 9: Writing the literary analysis essay

**Prepare:** Outline from Week 7, the Purdue OWL MLA page on in-text citations, and three model introductions (from CommonLit or online writing samples).

**Watch for:**
- Dropping quotations in without introduction: embed them in your own sentence with a signal phrase.
- Commentary that just repeats the quote: explain HOW the words prove the claim (focus on specific word choices).
- Conclusions that only restate: extend — why does this theme matter?

### Day 1 (Monday): Introductions
*Student:* Study three model introductions; write a hook, context, and thesis for your literary analysis essay.

*How to teach:* Compare three model introductions. Your child writes hook, context (title, author, situation), and thesis.

- **Q:** What three parts should a literary analysis introduction contain?
  **A:** A hook, brief context (title, author, situation of the story), and an arguable thesis.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body paragraphs
*Student:* Draft two body paragraphs using the claim–evidence–commentary pattern with embedded quotations and MLA citations.

*How to teach:* Model a claim–evidence–commentary paragraph with an embedded quotation and MLA citation (Author page). Your child drafts two body paragraphs.

- **Q:** Fix the citation: As Poe says 'I drink to your long life'. (pg. 4)
  **A:** Montresor toasts, "I drink to your long life" (Poe 4).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish and conclude
*Student:* Draft the final body paragraph and a conclusion that extends the idea; aim for 900–1,100 words.

*How to teach:* Draft the last body paragraph and a conclusion (900–1,100 words total).

- **Q:** What should a conclusion do beyond restating the thesis?
  **A:** Extend the idea — explain why the theme matters or how it connects beyond the story.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise for transitions and precise verbs ("reveals," "underscores"); edit punctuation of quotations.

*How to teach:* Revise transitions and verbs; edit quotation punctuation.

- **Q:** Replace 'shows' with three more precise analytical verbs.
  **A:** Reveals, underscores, emphasizes (also suggests, exposes, highlights).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit the final essay and complete a 45-minute timed write on an unseen short poem-length story excerpt.

*How to teach:* Collect the final essay. Give a 45-minute timed write on an unseen excerpt.

- **Q:** In a timed write, what should you do in the first 5 minutes?
  **A:** Read the prompt and passage, annotate, and jot a thesis and two supporting points.

**Assessment:** Literary analysis essay (W.9-10.2 rubric) and timed write.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = precise, arguable thesis; logically ordered body paragraphs each with a topic sentence, embedded quotations cited correctly, and commentary that explains significance; effective transitions; conclusion extends the idea; few errors. 3 = clear thesis, adequate evidence and explanation, some summary, minor errors. 2 = vague thesis, evidence thin or poorly explained, organization uneven. 1 = no clear thesis, little evidence, errors interfere. Timed write: same rubric scaled for a draft; proficient = clear claim about the excerpt, at least two quoted details, explanation of their effect.

## Algebra I: What is a function?

**Prepare:** Make 16 cards of mappings, tables, and graphs (include a circle, a vertical line, a parabola, and repeated-x tables). Graph paper; Desmos open.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a function can't repeat outputs: repeated y-values are fine; each INPUT must have exactly one output.
- Reading f(x) as 'f times x': it is the output of f at input x.
- Listing domain as all reals in contexts: ticket counts, for example, must be whole numbers from 0 up to capacity.

### Day 1 (Monday): Relations and functions
*Student:* Sort 16 mappings, tables, and graphs into functions/non-functions and explain the rule.

*How to teach:* Define a function: each input gives exactly one output. Model the vertical line test on a graph and a repeated-input check on a table. Your child sorts the 16 cards and writes the reason for each.

- **Q:** Is {(1, 2), (2, 2), (3, 5)} a function? Is {(1, 2), (1, 3)}?
  **A:** First: yes (each input has one output; repeated output 2 is fine). Second: no — input 1 has two outputs.
- **Q:** Is a circle the graph of a function of x?
  **A:** No — a vertical line crosses it twice.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Function notation
*Student:* Evaluate f(x)=3x²−2x for 10 inputs and interpret f(5)=120 in a context.

*How to teach:* Model evaluating f(x) = 3x² − 2x at x = −2 using parentheses. Then interpret notation in context: if C(h) is the cost of h hours of rental, C(5) = 120 means 5 hours costs $120. Your child evaluates 10 inputs and writes the interpretation.

- **Q:** For f(x) = 3x² − 2x, find f(−2) and f(5).
  **A:** f(−2) = 12 + 4 = 16; f(5) = 75 − 10 = 65.
- **Q:** If H(t) is a plant's height in cm after t weeks, what does H(4) = 12 mean?
  **A:** After 4 weeks the plant is 12 cm tall.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Graphs as solution sets
*Student:* Plot points from an equation and explain why every point on the graph makes it true.

*How to teach:* Plot points for y = 2x + 1 from a table and connect them. Test points on and off the line in the equation. Your child explains in writing why every point on the graph is a solution.

- **Q:** Is (3, 7) on the graph of y = 2x + 1? Is (1, 4)?
  **A:** (3, 7): yes, 2·3 + 1 = 7. (1, 4): no, 2·1 + 1 = 3 ≠ 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Domain and range
*Student:* Find domain and range from graphs, tables, and context (e.g., tickets sold can't be negative).

*How to teach:* Show domain (inputs) and range (outputs) on a graph using left–right and up–down sweeps. Discuss context limits. Your child finds domain and range for each graph, table, and context.

- **Q:** Domain and range of f(x) = x²?
  **A:** Domain: all real numbers. Range: y ≥ 0.
- **Q:** Tickets cost $8 and a theater holds 200. What is the domain of revenue R(n) = 8n?
  **A:** Whole numbers 0, 1, 2, …, 200.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on function definition, notation, and domain/range.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key; then review any missed items.

- **Q:** If g(x) = x² + 1, what is g(−3)?
  **A:** 10.

**Assessment:** Function quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) {(2,5),(3,5),(4,6)} — function (yes). 2) A circle — not a function (fails vertical line test). 3) f(x) = 2x − 7: f(4) = 1. 4) g(x) = x² + 1: g(−3) = 10. 5) Solve f(x) = 2x − 7 = 5 → x = 6. 6) Domain/range of y = |x|: all reals; y ≥ 0. 7) If P(t) is population in thousands t years after 2000, P(10) = 52 means in 2010 the population was 52,000. 8) Domain for the number of cars washed at a fundraiser: whole numbers 0 up to the maximum possible.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Cellular respiration and fermentation

**Prepare:** Red cabbage indicator (boil chopped red cabbage 10 minutes, strain, cool) or bromothymol blue; straws; yeast and sugar; two bottles with balloons. Safety: blow OUT only through the straw — never inhale indicator.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking respiration = breathing: cellular respiration is the chemical process in cells that releases energy from glucose.
- Believing fermentation produces lots of energy: it yields only 2 ATP per glucose versus about 30–38 in aerobic respiration.
- Thinking ATP is stored long-term: cells constantly regenerate ATP; it's an energy 'currency,' not storage.

### Day 1 (Monday): Respiration model
*Student:* Model C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy with molecule cards, showing bonds broken and formed.

*How to teach:* Model with molecule cards: bonds broken, bonds formed, energy released.

- **Q:** Write the equation for cellular respiration.
  **A:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP).
- **Q:** How are photosynthesis and respiration related?
  **A:** They are reverse processes; the products of one are the reactants of the other.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): CO2 indicator lab
*Student:* Blow through a straw into red-cabbage indicator before and after exercise and compare color change.

*How to teach:* Blow through a straw into indicator at rest and after exercise; compare color.

- **Q:** Predict the color change after exercise and explain.
  **A:** Red cabbage turns from purple toward pink/red (bromothymol blue turns green/yellow) faster after exercise, because more CO₂ is exhaled, forming carbonic acid.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fermentation
*Student:* Compare yeast fermentation with and without oxygen and connect to bread and yogurt.

*How to teach:* Compare yeast with and without oxygen; connect to bread and yogurt.

- **Q:** What does yeast produce during fermentation?
  **A:** Ethanol and CO₂ (the CO₂ makes bread rise).
- **Q:** What type of fermentation makes yogurt?
  **A:** Lactic acid fermentation by bacteria.

### Day 4 (Thursday): ATP
*Student:* Explain how ATP transfers energy in cells with an analogy and diagram.

*How to teach:* Explain ATP with an analogy (a rechargeable battery).

- **Q:** How does ATP release energy?
  **A:** Breaking the bond to the third phosphate (ATP → ADP + P) releases energy for cell work.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz comparing photosynthesis and respiration.

*How to teach:* Quiz comparing photosynthesis and respiration.

- **Q:** Which organisms do cellular respiration?
  **A:** Nearly all organisms — plants, animals, fungi, and many microbes.

**Assessment:** Indicator lab write-up and quiz.

**Answer key:** Indicator lab write-up: 4 = prediction, data (color and time) before/after exercise, explanation linking exercise → more respiration → more CO₂ → acid → color change. Quiz answers: 1) Photosynthesis: chloroplast, stores energy, uses CO₂ + H₂O. 2) Respiration: mitochondria, releases energy, uses glucose + O₂. 3) Aerobic ~30–38 ATP vs. fermentation 2 ATP. 4) Yeast fermentation products. 5) ATP → ADP + P releases energy.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Migration

**Prepare:** Migration maps (Great Migration, Dust Bowl); family interview questions. Be sensitive: family migration stories can include trauma, and some children may not know details — any family story (even recent moves) works.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking migration is only about poverty: people move for safety, family, education, environment, and opportunity.
- Confusing immigrant, emigrant, and refugee: a refugee flees persecution/conflict and is recognized under international law.
- Viewing push and pull as separate: most migrations combine both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Push and pull
*Student:* Classify push and pull factors in 10 migration cases.

*How to teach:* Classify push and pull factors in 10 cases.

- **Q:** Classify: war, job opportunities, drought, family reunification.
  **A:** Push: war, drought. Pull: job opportunities, family reunification.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family interview
*Student:* Interview a family member about your family's migration history.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about migration history.

- **Q:** Give two good interview questions.
  **A:** 'Why did our family move to where we live now?' and 'What was hardest about moving?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Migration flows
*Student:* Analyze maps of the Great Migration and Dust Bowl migration to California.

*How to teach:* Analyze maps of the Great Migration and Dust Bowl.

- **Q:** What pushed African Americans to leave the South during the Great Migration?
  **A:** Jim Crow segregation, racial violence and lynching, limited economic opportunity (sharecropping, the boll weevil).
- **Q:** What pulled Dust Bowl migrants to California in the 1930s?
  **A:** Farm work, a mild climate, and hope of a fresh start after drought, dust storms, and farm foreclosures in Oklahoma, Texas, and Arkansas.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Refugees
*Student:* Read about a current refugee crisis and its causes.

*How to teach:* Read about a current refugee crisis.

- **Q:** What is the legal definition of a refugee?
  **A:** A person outside their country who cannot return because of a well-founded fear of persecution (race, religion, nationality, social group, political opinion) — 1951 Refugee Convention.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a migration case study paragraph on the Dust Bowl or Great Migration explaining two push and two pull factors with map evidence.

*How to teach:* Write a migration case-study paragraph.

- **Q:** About how many African Americans moved in the Great Migration (1910s–1970)?
  **A:** About 6 million.

**Assessment:** Migration case study.

**Answer key:** Case study paragraph: Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate. Proficient content (Great Migration): pushes — Jim Crow, violence; pulls — industrial jobs in Northern and Western cities (e.g., wartime shipyards in Richmond and Oakland), less legal segregation; map evidence of routes from the South to Chicago, Detroit, New York, Los Angeles, Oakland. (Dust Bowl): pushes — drought, dust storms, foreclosures, Depression; pulls — farm jobs, Route 66 access to California.

## Spanish II: Unit 2 project: Mi niñez

**Prepare:** Gather photos or drawings, construction paper or a slide template, and glue. Print an editing checklist: imperfect endings and accents, ser/ir/ver forms, noun–adjective agreement. Write the Unit 2 test (imperfect forms, childhood vocabulary, games, describing the past).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing present tense into captions (Vivo en...): captions about childhood need the imperfect (Vivía en...).
- Reading the presentation word-for-word: use photos as cues and speak from memory.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a 6-page memory book: choose photos or drawings for home, family, school, games, food, and holidays.

*How to teach:* Help your child choose a photo or drawing for each page topic: home, family, school, games, food, holidays. List key vocabulary for each page in the notebook.

- **Q:** Give a Spanish title for the food page.
  **A:** Example: La comida de mi niñez / Lo que comía de niño/a

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write
*Student:* Write 3–4 imperfect-tense captions for each page (Vivíamos en..., Siempre jugaba...).

*How to teach:* Your child writes 3–4 imperfect captions per page (Vivíamos en una casa amarilla. Siempre jugaba con mi perro.). Circulate and point out good examples.

- **Q:** Write a caption: 'On holidays we always ate tamales.'
  **A:** En los días festivos siempre comíamos tamales.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Edit captions with a checklist for imperfect endings, ser/ir/ver forms, and noun-adjective agreement.

*How to teach:* Your child edits with the checklist, underlining every verb and checking its ending and accent. You spot-check one page and mark errors with a symbol (not the correction).

- **Q:** Find and fix: 'Mis abuelos vivia en Chicago.'
  **A:** Mis abuelos vivían en Chicago.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the memory book orally for 2 minutes without reading word-for-word, then answer two questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the book for 2 minutes using pictures as cues, then answers two questions you ask (¿Cuál era tu juguete favorito? ¿Qué te gustaba comer?).

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Cuál era tu juguete favorito?'
  **A:** Example: Mi juguete favorito era un oso de peluche.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test. Grade and review.

- **Q:** Conjugate ver in the imperfect for all forms.
  **A:** veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veíais, veían

**Assessment:** Memory book rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Memory book rubric: Content — 6 pages with 3–4 captions each; Language — accurate imperfect forms and agreement; Presentation — 2 minutes, not read, answers 2 questions; Visuals — clear and labeled. 4 = all criteria met with few errors; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing pages or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Unit test sample: 1) nosotros/ir — íbamos 2) yo/ver — veía 3) ellos/ser — eran 4) 'jugábamos a las canicas' = we used to play marbles 5) 'Había' = there was/were.

## Visual Art I: Atmospheric perspective and landscape

**Prepare:** Pencils, landscape photos; images of a Song dynasty landscape (e.g., Fan Kuan's 'Travelers among Mountains and Streams,' c. 1000, or Guo Xi's 'Early Spring,' 1072) and Hudson River School paintings (Thomas Cole, Albert Bierstadt, Frederic Church).

**Watch for:**
- Drawing distant objects as dark and detailed as near ones. Correct: distant objects are lighter, bluer, lower contrast and less detailed.
- Thinking linear perspective is the only way to show depth. Correct: Chinese painters used mist, overlapping and vertical placement.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chinese landscape painting
*Student:* Study a Song dynasty landscape and identify how mist shows depth.

*How to teach:* Study a Song landscape; identify mist and depth.

- **Q:** How do Song landscape painters show depth?
  **A:** Mist separating foreground, middle and background; overlapping forms; placing distant elements higher; fading ink tones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plein air
*Student:* Draw a landscape outdoors with foreground, middle ground, and background.

*How to teach:* Draw a landscape outdoors with three grounds.

- **Q:** Define foreground, middle ground, background.
  **A:** Areas closest, in the middle and farthest from the viewer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Value layers
*Student:* Redo the landscape with value fading in the distance.

*How to teach:* Redo the landscape with value fading in distance.

- **Q:** What is atmospheric perspective?
  **A:** Distant objects appear lighter, cooler (bluer), blurrier and lower in contrast because of air and particles.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare Chinese and Hudson River School landscapes in writing.

*How to teach:* Compare Chinese and Hudson River School landscapes in writing.

- **Q:** Name one difference.
  **A:** Song paintings use ink, vertical scrolls, mist and a moving viewpoint; Hudson River School uses oil paint, dramatic light and realistic detail celebrating the American wilderness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Unit 2 review of perspective drawings.

*How to teach:* Unit 2 review.

- **Q:** Which perspective techniques have you used?
  **A:** One-point, two-point, three-point and atmospheric perspective.

**Assessment:** Landscape drawing and comparison.

**Answer key:** Landscape drawing and comparison: drawing 4 = clear foreground/middle/background with atmospheric value change; comparison 4 = accurate details of both traditions (medium, format, depth techniques, purpose), with visual evidence and a conclusion; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = superficial; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Cardio endurance challenge

**Prepare:** Stopwatch, route map with a hill; option to swim, bike or hike.

**Watch for:**
- Running every run hard. Correct: most runs should be easy; hard days need recovery.
- Walking down hills too fast. Correct: walk-down is recovery.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fartlek
*Student:* Complete a 25-minute fartlek run with surges.

*How to teach:* 25-minute fartlek with surges (e.g., sprint to next tree, jog to recover).

- **Q:** What is fartlek training?
  **A:** 'Speed play' — continuous running with unstructured surges of faster running.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hill repeats
*Student:* Do 6 hill repeats with walk-down recovery.

*How to teach:* 6 hill repeats with walk-down.

- **Q:** What do hill repeats build?
  **A:** Leg strength, power and cardio fitness with less impact than flat sprinting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cross-training
*Student:* Swim, bike, or hike for 40 minutes at target heart rate.

*How to teach:* 40 minutes swim/bike/hike at target HR.

- **Q:** Why cross-train?
  **A:** Builds fitness while reducing repetitive stress injuries and boredom.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Easy run
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute recovery run and stretching.

*How to teach:* 20-minute recovery run and stretching.

- **Q:** What is the purpose of a recovery run?
  **A:** Light activity that promotes blood flow and adaptation without added stress.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Retake the mile or PACER and compare to week 3 baseline.

*How to teach:* Retake mile or PACER; compare to week 3.

- **Q:** Your PACER went from 42 to 50 laps. What percent improvement?
  **A:** (50 − 42) ÷ 42 ≈ 19%.

**Assessment:** Mile/PACER retest compared to baseline.

**Answer key:** Retest comparison: proficient = retest recorded, change calculated (difference and percent), connects change to training types done in weeks 5–8, updates HFZ status.

## Health & Wellness: Physical activity, sleep and a personal nutrition plan

**Prepare:** Gather an activity log and grocery flyer. Plan to cook together one day. Write the Unit 2 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking teens need less sleep than kids. Correct: teens need 8–10 hours; their body clock shifts later.
- Believing only sports count as activity. Correct: any moderate-to-vigorous movement (walking fast, biking, dancing) counts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Activity guidelines
*Student:* Learn the 60-minutes-per-day guideline for teens and log your activity minutes and type for today.

*How to teach:* Teach 60 minutes daily of moderate-to-vigorous activity with muscle- and bone-strengthening 3 days/week. Log today.

- **Q:** What are the activity guidelines for teens?
  **A:** At least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily, mostly aerobic, with vigorous, muscle-strengthening and bone-strengthening activity at least 3 days a week each.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sleep and health
*Student:* Read about teen sleep needs (8–10 hours) and design a sleep-hygiene routine for school nights.

*How to teach:* Read about teen sleep (8–10 hours) and design a sleep-hygiene routine.

- **Q:** Name three sleep-hygiene habits.
  **A:** Consistent bedtime/wake time, no screens 30–60 minutes before bed, cool dark room, limit caffeine after noon, avoid long late naps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Meal planning
*Student:* Plan three days of balanced meals and snacks on a budget using a grocery flyer and MyPlate targets.

*How to teach:* Plan three days of balanced meals on a budget.

- **Q:** How can you lower cost while staying balanced?
  **A:** Use beans, eggs, frozen produce, store brands and compare unit prices.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cook a meal
*Student:* Prepare one meal from your plan with family, photograph it and label its food groups.

*How to teach:* Cook one meal; photograph and label food groups.

- **Q:** Does your meal include at least three food groups?
  **A:** Answers vary; check the labeled photo.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on nutrients, labels, energy balance, marketing and food safety, and submit your one-week plan.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test and collect the plan.

- **Q:** What is the 5/20 rule?
  **A:** 5% DV or less is low; 20% DV or more is high.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test and personal nutrition/activity plan.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test key: six nutrients and functions; calories per gram (4/4/9); MyPlate groups; serving size and %DV; 5/20 rule; grams sugar ÷ 4 = tsp; added sugar <10% calories; sodium <2,300 mg; energy balance; eating disorder types; marketing techniques; supplements not FDA-approved; Clean/Separate/Cook/Chill; temps 165/160/145; 60 min activity; 8–10 hours sleep. Plan: proficient = three balanced days, budget, activity and sleep components.
