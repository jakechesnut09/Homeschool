# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 18

## English 9: The Odyssey: the Underworld and the Sirens (Books 11–12); semester review

**Prepare:** Odyssey Books 11–12; a semester review packet (literary terms, rhetorical devices, short practice passages). Plan a 2-hour exam block for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Odysseus loses his last crew because of Scylla: the crew dies after eating Helios's sacred cattle against his warning.
- Reading the Sirens as only literal monsters: they also represent temptation and dangerous knowledge.
- Forgetting that Books 9–12 are Odysseus's story told at the Phaeacian court (a flashback).

### Day 1 (Monday): Book 11
*Student:* Read the Underworld episode; explain what Tiresias's prophecy and the meeting with Achilles reveal about glory.

*How to teach:* Read Book 11; explain Tiresias's prophecy and Achilles's words about glory.

- **Q:** What does Tiresias warn Odysseus about?
  **A:** Not to harm the cattle of the sun god Helios; if they do, his crew will be destroyed and he will return home late and alone to find trouble in his house.
- **Q:** What does Achilles say about death and glory?
  **A:** He would rather be a poor laborer/servant on earth than king over all the dead — challenging the value of glory.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Book 12
*Student:* Read the Sirens and Scylla and Charybdis; discuss what each hazard represents.

*How to teach:* Read Book 12; discuss what each hazard represents.

- **Q:** How does Odysseus get past the Sirens?
  **A:** He plugs his men's ears with beeswax and has himself tied to the mast so he can hear the song safely.
- **Q:** Why does Odysseus choose Scylla over Charybdis?
  **A:** Charybdis's whirlpool could destroy the whole ship; Scylla takes only six men.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Structure
*Student:* Explain how Homer's use of Odysseus's flashback (Books 9–12) builds suspense for the return.

*How to teach:* Explain how the flashback structure builds suspense for the return.

- **Q:** Why does Homer tell Books 9–12 as a flashback?
  **A:** We meet Odysseus near the end of his journey and learn his past through his own story, which deepens his character and builds anticipation for his return to Ithaca.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review
*Student:* Review terms, rhetorical devices, and essay skills; complete practice reading passages.

*How to teach:* Review terms and complete practice passages.

- **Q:** Define dramatic irony and give an example from this semester.
  **A:** When the audience knows something characters don't — e.g., the reader knows Montresor's plan while Fortunato doesn't.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam: unseen story and nonfiction passages plus a timed analytical essay.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam: unseen story and nonfiction passages plus a timed analytical essay.

- **Q:** What should a timed analytical essay thesis contain?
  **A:** A specific, arguable claim that answers the prompt and previews the main points.

**Assessment:** Semester exam (reading passages + timed essay).

**Answer key:** Semester exam: reading questions scored right/wrong (80%+ proficient); timed essay with the rubric. Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = precise, arguable thesis; logically ordered body paragraphs each with a topic sentence, embedded quotations cited correctly, and commentary that explains significance; effective transitions; conclusion extends the idea; few errors. 3 = clear thesis, adequate evidence and explanation, some summary, minor errors. 2 = vague thesis, evidence thin or poorly explained, organization uneven. 1 = no clear thesis, little evidence, errors interfere. Sample term answers: irony types, conflict types, theme statement, ethos/pathos/logos, antithesis, epic conventions, in medias res, xenia.

## Algebra I: Semester review and exam

**Prepare:** Assemble the semester review packet from Weeks 1–17 quizzes (mix of equations, inequalities, functions, slope, linear models, systems). Plan a quiet 90-minute exam block.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing review by redoing only easy problems: focus practice on topics missed on earlier quizzes.
- Not checking answers: substitute solutions back into equations and systems.
- Mixing up slope formula order or sign rules under time pressure: write the formula before using it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: equations and inequalities
*Student:* Complete mixed review on Units 1–2 and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Units 1–2 review; you mark it and they correct errors with a one-line explanation each.

- **Q:** Solve 2(x − 3) + 4 = 3x − 5.
  **A:** 2x − 2 = 3x − 5 → x = 3.
- **Q:** Solve |x − 2| ≤ 5.
  **A:** −3 ≤ x ≤ 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: functions and linear models
*Student:* Complete a mixed review on Units 3–4 (function notation, slope, writing linear equations) and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Units 3–4 review and corrects errors.

- **Q:** Write the equation of the line through (−1, 3) and (2, 9).
  **A:** m = 2; y = 2x + 5.
- **Q:** If f(x) = −x² + 4, find f(3).
  **A:** −5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: systems
*Student:* Complete mixed review on Unit 5, including a context problem.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Unit 5 review, including a context problem.

- **Q:** Solve: 2x + y = 7 and x − y = 2.
  **A:** 3x = 9 → x = 3, y = 1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a timed practice exam and analyze mistakes.

*How to teach:* Give a timed practice exam; analyze errors by topic and review the two weakest topics.

- **Q:** Solve 4 − 3x > 13.
  **A:** x < −3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam (about 90 minutes). Grade with the key.

- **Q:** Slope of 6x − 3y = 9?
  **A:** 2.

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam sample answers: 1) Classify √36 → integer/rational. 2) 3(2x − 1) = 4x + 7 → x = 5. 3) Solve A = P + Prt for r → r = (A − P)/(Pt). 4) −2 ≤ 3x + 1 < 10 → −1 ≤ x < 3. 5) |2x − 3| = 7 → x = 5 or −2. 6) f(x) = 2x² − x: f(−1) = 3. 7) 6, 11, 16, … → a_n = 5n + 1. 8) Slope through (1, −2), (5, 6) → 2. 9) Line through (2, 1) parallel to y = −3x + 4 → y = −3x + 7. 10) Inverse of f(x) = 4x − 8 → x/4 + 2. 11) x + y = 12, x − y = 2 → (7, 5). 12) 3x + 2y = 16, x − 2y = 0 → (4, 2). 13) Graph y < −x + 4 (dashed, shade below). Scoring: 90%+ A, 80–89 B, 70–79 C; below 70 reteach.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Semester review and exam

**Prepare:** Semester review guide covering ecosystems, energy/matter, and evolution. Index cards for flashcards. Plan a 90-minute exam block.

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing diagrams without understanding arrows: explain each arrow aloud.
- Mixing up photosynthesis and respiration locations and products.
- Using 'adapt' to mean individuals change on purpose.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review ecosystems
*Student:* Create a one-page summary of ecosystem concepts with diagrams.

*How to teach:* One-page summary of ecosystem concepts with diagrams.

- **Q:** Explain carrying capacity in one sentence.
  **A:** The maximum population size an environment can sustain given its resources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review energy and matter
*Student:* Redraw photosynthesis, respiration, and carbon cycle models from memory.

*How to teach:* Redraw photosynthesis, respiration, and carbon cycle models from memory.

- **Q:** Where does carbon in a tree's wood come from?
  **A:** CO₂ from the air, fixed during photosynthesis.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review evolution
*Student:* Make flashcards of evidence for evolution and natural selection.

*How to teach:* Make evolution flashcards.

- **Q:** Explain natural selection in two sentences.
  **A:** Individuals in a population vary in heritable traits. Those with traits better suited to the environment survive and reproduce more, so those traits become more common over generations.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a practice exam and correct mistakes.

*How to teach:* Practice exam; correct mistakes.

- **Q:** Using the 10% rule, how much energy reaches the third trophic level from 8,000 kcal of producers?
  **A:** 80 kcal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam.

- **Q:** What is a keystone species example from California?
  **A:** Sea otters (kelp forests).

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam: multiple choice and short answers on Units 1–3 scored with the unit keys; constructed responses scored with the CER rubric. CER rubric: 4 = claim answers the question; evidence uses specific data (numbers, units, observations); reasoning connects the evidence to a scientific principle; 3 = claim and evidence with partial reasoning; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or incorrect.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Country geography profile and semester exam

**Prepare:** Atlas, World Factbook, Gapminder, populationpyramid.net. Plan a 90-minute exam block. Semester vocabulary list.

**Watch for:**
- Profiles that list facts without connections: explain how physical geography shapes how people live.
- Thematic maps without legends or sources.
- Cramming: review over several days.

### Day 1 (Monday): Profile research
*Student:* Choose a country and gather physical geography, climate, population, culture, and economic data.

*How to teach:* Research a country's physical, climate, population, culture, and economic data.

- **Q:** Which World Factbook sections help with a population profile?
  **A:** People and Society (population, age structure, growth rate, urbanization, languages, religions).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Profile maps
*Student:* Create two maps (physical and population/thematic) and a population pyramid for your country.

*How to teach:* Create physical and thematic maps and a population pyramid.

- **Q:** What must every thematic map include?
  **A:** Title, legend/key, scale, orientation (north arrow), and data source.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Present
*Student:* Present a 5-minute country profile connecting physical geography to how people live there.

*How to teach:* Present a 5-minute country profile.

- **Q:** Give an example of connecting physical geography to daily life.
  **A:** Egypt: nearly all people live along the Nile because the rest is desert.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review semester vocabulary and map skills with a practice test.

*How to teach:* Review vocabulary and map skills.

- **Q:** Define 'situation'.
  **A:** A place's location relative to other places and features.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the World Geography semester exam (map skills, concepts, and a data-based essay).

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam.

- **Q:** Which model describes population change as countries industrialize?
  **A:** The demographic transition model.

**Assessment:** Country profile presentation and semester exam.

**Answer key:** Profile presentation rubric: 4 = accurate data, two maps and a pyramid, clear links between physical geography and human life, within time; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = facts without connections; 1 = incomplete. Semester exam: map skills, concepts from Units 1–5, and a data-based essay scored with the paragraph rubric expanded to essay length. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish II: Semester exam (integrated performance assessment)

**Prepare:** Find or prepare two short audio clips (a restaurant order and a childhood memory — you can record a Spanish-speaking friend or use the UT Austin Spanish Proficiency Exercises) and a reading about a Latin American festival (e.g., Inti Raymi in Peru or Carnaval de Barranquilla). Prepare interview questions on routines, childhood, and food.

**Watch for:**
- Panicking when not every word is understood: focus on main idea, key words, numbers, and cognates.
- Giving one-word answers in the interview: expand with a detail and a reason (porque...).

### Day 1 (Monday): Listening section
*Student:* Listen to two authentic audio clips (a restaurant order and a childhood memory) and answer comprehension questions.

*How to teach:* Play each clip twice. Your child answers comprehension questions; on the second listen, they confirm details.

- **Q:** In a restaurant clip, a customer says 'Para mí, el pescado y un agua sin gas.' What did she order?
  **A:** The fish and a still (non-carbonated) water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading section
*Student:* Read an authentic article about a Latin American festival and answer main-idea and detail questions.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the festival article and answers main-idea and detail questions, underlining evidence.

- **Q:** What is Inti Raymi?
  **A:** The Inca 'Festival of the Sun,' celebrated every June 24 in Cusco, Peru, around the winter solstice.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speaking interview
*Student:* Hold a 5-minute interview with the teacher on routines, childhood, and food.

*How to teach:* Conduct the 5-minute interview: ¿Cómo es tu rutina? ¿Cómo eras de niño/a? ¿Qué comiste ayer? ¿Cuál es tu comida favorita? Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Answer with detail: '¿Cómo eras de niño/a?'
  **A:** Example: Era muy activo y un poco travieso. Siempre jugaba afuera con mis vecinos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing section
*Student:* Write a 250-word email to a host family describing yourself, your childhood, and a recent celebration.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the 250-word email to a host family. Allow 40 minutes and no notes.

- **Q:** Write a sentence about a recent celebration.
  **A:** Example: El mes pasado celebramos el cumpleaños de mi abuelo; comimos mole y bailamos toda la noche.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection & goals
*Student:* Review your exam results and write three Spanish goals for semester 2 (in Spanish).

*How to teach:* Review results together. Your child writes three goals in Spanish for semester 2.

- **Q:** Write a goal: 'I want to speak more in class.'
  **A:** Quiero hablar más en la clase.

**Assessment:** Semester exam across all modes.

**Answer key:** Semester exam rubric across modes (4 levels each): Listening/Reading — 4 = main idea + most details correct; Speaking — 4 = 5 minutes sustained, accurate present/past tenses, asks a question back; Writing (250-word email) — 4 = greeting/closing, describes self (present), childhood (imperfect), recent celebration (preterite) with few errors; 3 = complete with some errors; 2 = missing a section or many errors; 1 = fragments. Sample reading answer for Inti Raymi: an Inca festival of the sun held June 24 in Cusco, Peru, at the winter solstice.

## Visual Art I: Semester portfolio review and critique

**Prepare:** All semester work, phone camera, neutral backdrop (white sheet or wall), natural light.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing only one medium. Correct: show range.
- Vague reflections. Correct: state goal, process and specific changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Select
*Student:* Lay out all semester work and select six pieces that show range (drawing, perspective, painting, print, design).

*How to teach:* Select six pieces showing range.

- **Q:** Why show range?
  **A:** It demonstrates skills across media and techniques.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Document
*Student:* Photograph each piece squarely in natural light with a neutral backdrop and crop/label the files.

*How to teach:* Photograph squarely in natural light; crop/label.

- **Q:** How do you avoid distortion when photographing art?
  **A:** Hold the camera parallel to the artwork, centered, and fill the frame.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a 100-word reflection for each piece: goal, process, what you'd change.

*How to teach:* 100-word reflections.

- **Q:** What three questions should each reflection answer?
  **A:** What was the goal? What was the process? What would you change?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the portfolio to family, explaining one piece in depth with a Feldman-style critique.

*How to teach:* Present with an in-depth Feldman critique.

- **Q:** What are the four Feldman steps?
  **A:** Describe, analyze, interpret, judge.

### Day 5 (Friday): Goal setting
*Student:* Compare your week 1 contour drawing to a recent drawing and write three goals for semester 2.

*How to teach:* Compare week 1 to recent drawing; write three goals.

- **Q:** Give an example of a strong art goal.
  **A:** 'Improve proportion accuracy by doing three sighting drawings per week.'

**Assessment:** Semester portfolio and presentation.

**Answer key:** Semester portfolio: 4 = six well-documented pieces across 4+ media, 100-word reflections with goal/process/change, confident presentation with complete Feldman critique, three specific goals; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = thin reflections; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Tumbling basics and semester fitness check

**Prepare:** Thick mat; spotting plan for the cartwheel; push-up and curl-up test materials; week 3 data.

**Watch for:**
- Rolling on the head/neck. Correct: tuck chin to chest and roll across the shoulders and back.
- Cartwheel with bent arms and legs. Correct: straight arms, hand-hand-foot-foot in a line.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rolls
*Student:* Practice log rolls, forward rolls, and backward shoulder rolls on a mat.

*How to teach:* Log rolls, forward rolls (chin tucked, push with hands) and backward shoulder rolls (over one shoulder).

- **Q:** What is the key safety cue for a forward roll?
  **A:** Tuck your chin to your chest so you roll on your upper back, not your head/neck.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cartwheel
*Student:* Progress to a cartwheel with spotting.

*How to teach:* Cartwheel progressions: lunge start, hands down in a line, kick legs over, land facing start; parent spots at the hips.

- **Q:** What is the order of contact in a cartwheel?
  **A:** Hand, hand, foot, foot — in a straight line.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fitness check
*Student:* Retake push-up and curl-up tests and compare to baseline.

*How to teach:* Retest push-ups and curl-ups; compare.

- **Q:** Your curl-ups went from 30 to 41. What's the change?
  **A:** +11 curl-ups, about 37% improvement.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sequence
*Student:* Link rolls and balances into a short routine.

*How to teach:* Link rolls and balances into a routine.

- **Q:** Why plan transitions?
  **A:** Smooth links show control and keep the routine flowing safely.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform routine and update goal sheet.

*How to teach:* Perform the routine and update goals.

- **Q:** If you met your goal early, what next?
  **A:** Set a new, higher target with a date.

**Assessment:** Tumbling routine and updated goals.

**Answer key:** Tumbling routine: 4 = includes at least two rolls, a cartwheel (or progression), two balances, smooth transitions, safe technique; goal sheet updated with retest data. 3 = minor technique errors; 2 = unsafe or incomplete elements; 1 = not attempted.

## Health & Wellness: Influences, refusal skills and semester review

**Prepare:** Semester review packet with practice questions on units 1–4; role-play cards; semester exam; refusal-skills checklist (clear 'no,' reason, alternative, firm body language, leave if needed).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you need a complicated excuse to refuse. Correct: 'No thanks' is enough; repeat it and leave if needed.
- Believing 'everyone is doing it.' Correct: most teens don't use substances (look up current Monitoring the Future data).

### Day 1 (Monday): Influences
*Student:* Map the influences on teen substance use (peers, family, media, community) and identify protective factors.

*How to teach:* Map influences and protective factors.

- **Q:** Name one protective factor against teen substance use.
  **A:** Parental monitoring and communication, involvement in activities, clear family rules, strong school connection, friends who don't use.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Refusal skills
*Student:* Practice five refusal strategies (say no, give a reason, suggest an alternative, leave, use humor) in role-plays.

*How to teach:* Role-play five refusal strategies.

- **Q:** Demonstrate 'suggest an alternative.'
  **A:** 'Nah, let's go shoot hoops instead.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Advocacy
*Student:* Create a short PSA promoting a substance-free lifestyle.

*How to teach:* Create a PSA promoting a substance-free lifestyle.

- **Q:** What makes a PSA persuasive?
  **A:** One clear message, accurate fact, target audience and call to action.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review
*Student:* Review units 1–4 using a study guide and practice questions.

*How to teach:* Review units 1–4.

- **Q:** What's the 5/20 rule?
  **A:** 5% DV or less is low; 20% DV or more is high.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam covering wellness, nutrition, mental health and substance use.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam and refusal role-play check.

- **Q:** Name the DECIDE steps.
  **A:** Define, Explore, Consider, Identify values, Decide, Evaluate.

**Assessment:** Semester exam and refusal-skills role-play checklist.

**Answer key:** Semester exam key draws on Unit tests 1–3 keys plus: dopamine/reward; tolerance/dependence; nicotine effects; Tobacco 21; alcohol poisoning signs; BAC zero tolerance; fentanyl; naloxone steps. Refusal checklist (1 pt each): clear no, reason, alternative, firm voice/body language, exits if needed — 4–5 = proficient.
