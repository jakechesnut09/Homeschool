# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 3

## English 9: Irony and craft in "The Gift of the Magi"; planning a personal narrative

**Prepare:** Print "The Gift of the Magi". Gather two short mentor narratives (a memoir excerpt from CommonLit works). Prepare a clause reference sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Calling the ending a 'twist' without explaining the irony: the gifts are now useless, yet the sacrifice proves their love — the irony carries the theme.
- Choosing a narrative topic that is a whole summer or a list of events: a strong narrative zooms in on one turning point.
- Confusing phrases with clauses: a clause has a subject and a verb; a phrase does not.

### Day 1 (Monday): Situational irony
*Student:* Read "The Gift of the Magi"; explain in writing how the ending reverses expectations and what O. Henry suggests about love and sacrifice.

*How to teach:* Read the story together. Your child explains the situational irony and what O. Henry suggests about love and sacrifice, citing the closing comparison to the Magi.

- **Q:** What does Della sell and what does she buy? What does Jim sell and buy?
  **A:** Della sells her hair (for $20) to buy a platinum fob chain for Jim's watch; Jim sells his watch to buy combs for Della's hair.
- **Q:** Why does the narrator call them 'the wisest' givers?
  **A:** Because they sacrificed their greatest treasures for each other; their love, not the gifts, is the true gift.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Narrative techniques
*Student:* Study how O. Henry uses dialogue, a narrator's asides, and pacing; imitate one technique in a 150-word scene from your own life.

*How to teach:* Point out the narrator's asides (e.g., the opening counting of $1.87, commentary on the Magi) and dialogue. Your child imitates one technique in a 150-word scene from life.

- **Q:** How much money does Della have at the start and why repeat it?
  **A:** One dollar and eighty-seven cents; repeating it stresses her poverty and desperation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Brainstorm a turning point
*Student:* Make a life map of 8 memorable moments; choose one turning point and draft a story arc (setup, conflict, climax, reflection).

*How to teach:* Your child maps 8 memorable moments, chooses a turning point, and drafts a story arc: setup, conflict, climax, reflection. Ask 'What changed in you?' to test the choice.

- **Q:** What makes a moment a good 'turning point' for a personal narrative?
  **A:** It has a conflict and leads to a change in the writer — a new understanding, decision, or feeling that can be reflected on.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grammar: clauses
*Student:* Learn independent, dependent, noun, adjective, and adverb clauses; write 8 sentences about your memory using each clause type.

*How to teach:* Teach independent vs. dependent clauses and noun, adjective, and adverb clause types with examples. Your child writes 8 sentences about their memory using each type.

- **Q:** Identify the clause type: 'The day that I broke my arm changed my summer.'
  **A:** 'that I broke my arm' — adjective clause (modifies 'day').

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share your narrative plan in a conference and revise it from feedback; take a clauses quiz.

*How to teach:* Conference: your child presents their plan; you ask about the conflict and the reflection. They revise. Give the clause quiz.

- **Q:** Identify: 'Because the bus was late, we missed the game.'
  **A:** 'Because the bus was late' — dependent adverb clause; 'we missed the game' — independent clause.

**Assessment:** Narrative plan conference checklist; clause quiz (80%+).

**Answer key:** Conference checklist (each yes/no): a single focused turning point; a clear conflict; a planned climax; a planned reflection about change; an opening image or line chosen; revision made from feedback. Clause quiz answers (sample): 1) 'what she said' in 'I believed what she said' — noun clause. 2) 'who lives next door' — adjective clause. 3) 'when the storm hit' — adverb clause. 4) 'The lights flickered' — independent clause. 5) 'although I was nervous' — dependent adverb clause. 80%+ proficient.

## Algebra I: Units, quantities, and measurement accuracy

**Prepare:** Gather a measuring tape (with 1/16-inch marks), kitchen scale, and calculator. Print a conversion sheet (1 mi = 5280 ft, 1 h = 3600 s, 1 gal ≈ 3.785 L, 1 in = 2.54 cm).

**Watch for:**
- Flipping a conversion factor the wrong way: write units on every factor and cancel them; the factor is right only if unwanted units cancel.
- Reporting false precision (e.g., 47.3125 in from a tape marked in sixteenths when the reading is uncertain): report to the smallest mark you can actually read.
- Ignoring units in answers: every answer needs a unit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dimensional analysis
*Student:* Convert 12 rates (mph to ft/s, gallons/day to L/year) using conversion-factor chains.

*How to teach:* Model 60 mph → ft/s as a chain: 60 mi/h × 5280 ft/mi × 1 h/3600 s, crossing out units. Your child converts the 12 rates, writing units on every factor.

- **Q:** Convert 60 mph to feet per second.
  **A:** 60 × 5280 / 3600 = 88 ft/s.
- **Q:** Convert 10 gallons/day to liters/year (1 gal ≈ 3.785 L).
  **A:** 10 × 3.785 × 365 ≈ 13,815 L/year.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measuring lab
*Student:* Measure a room, a book, and a mug with a tape; report each measurement with appropriate precision.

*How to teach:* Show how to read the tape to the nearest 1/16 inch (or mm). Your child measures the room, book, and mug twice each and records values with units and the precision used (e.g., 9 3/16 in).

- **Q:** Your tape is marked in sixteenths of an inch. Is it reasonable to report a book as 9.1873 in?
  **A:** No — report to the nearest 1/16 in (about 9 3/16 in); extra digits claim precision the tool doesn't have.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Defining quantities
*Student:* For a road-trip problem, define the quantities needed (distance, fuel efficiency, cost) and build an expression for total cost.

*How to teach:* Ask: what do we need to know to find the cost of a road trip? List quantities with units (distance d mi, efficiency m mi/gal, price p $/gal). Your child builds the expression cost = (d/m)·p and evaluates it for a real trip.

- **Q:** A 300-mile trip in a car getting 25 mpg with gas at $4.80/gal: what is the fuel cost?
  **A:** 300/25 = 12 gal; 12 × $4.80 = $57.60.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scales on graphs
*Student:* Choose axis scales and units for three data sets and explain your choices.

*How to teach:* Show a graph with a poorly chosen scale and one with a good one. Your child picks axis scales so data fill most of the grid, labels units, and writes why.

- **Q:** Data range from 0 to 950 and your graph has 10 gridlines up the y-axis. What scale should each gridline be?
  **A:** 100 per gridline (0 to 1000) — fits all data and uses easy numbers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on real numbers, expressions, and units.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test (see key) under quiet, timed conditions (about 45 minutes, calculator allowed for the units section). Grade together and log missed topics for review.

- **Q:** Before the test: which number type is −√16?
  **A:** −√16 = −4: an integer, rational, real.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test sample answers: 1) −√16 = −4: integer, rational, real. 2) 3 + √5 is irrational. 3) Name the property: 2(x + 7) = 2x + 14 → distributive. 4) Evaluate 2x² − 5 at x = −3 → 13. 5) Simplify 2(x − 4) + 3x → 5x − 8. 6) Interpret 25 + 0.10m → $25 base plus $0.10 per minute. 7) Convert 5 m/s to km/h → 18 km/h. 8) Convert 3 ft² to in² → 432 in². 9) A tape reads to the nearest mm; report a 23.4 cm length as 234 mm (not 234.00 mm). 10) Explain why rational + irrational is irrational (see Week 1 rubric). Scoring: 90%+ excellent, 80–89% proficient, below 70% reteach.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Biodiversity and species interactions

**Prepare:** Quadrat data from Week 1; calculator. Print the lynx–hare (Hudson's Bay Company pelt) graph and a sea otter–urchin–kelp diagram. Simpson's index formula: D = 1 − Σn(n − 1)/[N(N − 1)].

**Watch for:**
- Calling all close relationships 'symbiosis = helpful': symbiosis includes mutualism (+/+), commensalism (+/0), and parasitism (+/−).
- Thinking predators always 'win': predator and prey populations cycle together.
- Equating diversity with number of species only: diversity also depends on evenness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interactions
*Student:* Classify 15 species pairs by interaction type with examples from California ecosystems.

*How to teach:* Your child classifies 15 species pairs: predation, competition, mutualism, commensalism, parasitism.

- **Q:** Classify: bees and California poppies; ticks on deer; barnacles on a whale.
  **A:** Mutualism (+/+); parasitism (+/−); commensalism (+/0).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Predator–prey
*Student:* Graph the lynx–hare data and explain the cycles.

*How to teach:* Graph lynx–hare data; explain cycles.

- **Q:** Why do lynx population peaks come slightly after hare peaks?
  **A:** More hares means more food, so lynx populations rise; then lynx overeat hares, hares decline, and lynx decline afterward — a lag of a year or two in roughly 10-year cycles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Keystone species
*Student:* Read about sea otters and kelp forests and draw the trophic cascade.

*How to teach:* Read about otters and kelp; draw the trophic cascade.

- **Q:** What happens to kelp forests if sea otters disappear?
  **A:** Sea urchins (otter prey) increase and overgraze kelp, creating 'urchin barrens'; otters are a keystone species.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Diversity index
*Student:* Calculate Simpson's diversity index for your two quadrats and interpret.

*How to teach:* Calculate Simpson's index for both quadrats.

- **Q:** Quadrat counts: 5 clover, 3 grass, 2 dandelions. Find Simpson's diversity index (1 − D).
  **A:** N = 10; Σn(n−1) = 20 + 6 + 2 = 28; 28/90 = 0.311; index = 1 − 0.311 = 0.69.
- **Q:** Which is more diverse: 8, 1, 1 or 5, 3, 2?
  **A:** 5, 3, 2 (index 0.69 vs. 0.38) — more even.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on interactions and diversity calculations.

*How to teach:* Quiz on interactions and diversity.

- **Q:** What is a keystone species?
  **A:** A species with a disproportionately large effect on its ecosystem relative to its abundance (e.g., sea otters).

**Assessment:** Quiz and diversity index calculation.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Mutualism +/+, commensalism +/0, parasitism +/−, predation +/−, competition −/−. 2) Lynx–hare cycle explanation (lag). 3) Otters → urchins ↓ → kelp ↑. 4) Simpson's index for 4, 4, 2 → N = 10, Σ = 12 + 12 + 2 = 26, 26/90 = 0.289, index 0.71. 5) Higher index = more diverse. Diversity calculation scored for correct N, Σn(n−1), and interpretation.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Regions

**Prepare:** Colored pencils, U.S. map, blank world regions map. Survey 5+ family members/friends.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking regions have exact natural boundaries: many regions (perceptual) have fuzzy, debated boundaries.
- Mixing up formal and functional regions: formal = shared characteristic (Corn Belt); functional = organized around a central node (a TV market, a delivery area).
- Lumping 'Africa' as one region: North Africa is often grouped with Southwest Asia; Sub-Saharan Africa is a separate cultural region.

### Day 1 (Monday): Region types
*Student:* Classify 12 regions (Bay Area, Sun Belt, Corn Belt) by type.

*How to teach:* Classify 12 regions as formal, functional, or perceptual.

- **Q:** Classify the Corn Belt, a pizza delivery area, and 'the South.'
  **A:** Formal; functional; perceptual (vernacular).
- **Q:** What kind of region is the Sun Belt?
  **A:** Usually perceptual (vernacular), though it is loosely tied to warm climate (formal traits).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Perceptual regions
*Student:* Survey family members on where "Southern California" begins and map the results.

*How to teach:* Survey people on where 'Southern California' begins; map the results.

- **Q:** Why do survey answers about where Southern California begins differ?
  **A:** It is a perceptual region — boundaries depend on people's experiences and identities.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): World regions
*Student:* Label the world's major cultural regions (Latin America, Sub-Saharan Africa, East Asia, etc.) on a blank map and note one trait that unifies each.

*How to teach:* Label world cultural regions and a unifying trait for each.

- **Q:** Give one unifying trait of Latin America.
  **A:** Predominance of Spanish and Portuguese languages and Roman Catholic heritage from Iberian colonization.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Region essay
*Student:* Write a paragraph defining a region you belong to and its characteristics.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph defining a region you belong to.

- **Q:** What makes a good region definition?
  **A:** It names the criteria (physical or human traits), describes boundaries, and gives examples.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** What theme of geography groups places with shared traits?
  **A:** Region.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test sample answers: five themes; coordinates of given cities; absolute vs. relative location; projection distortions; scale calculation; region types (formal, functional, perceptual) with examples; GIS definition. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish II: La escuela y las actividades: stem-changing verbs and time expressions

**Prepare:** Copy the child's class schedule onto the template; prepare stem-changer playing cards (verb on one side, subject on the other). Find a short reading on a school day in Mexico or Argentina (e.g., a textbook reading or a Spanish blog about 'la escuela secundaria').

**Watch for:**
- Changing the stem in nosotros (*empiezamos): nosotros and vosotros keep the original stem — empezamos, almorzamos, pedimos.
- Confusing e→ie and e→i: preferir becomes prefiero (ie), but pedir becomes pido (i). Memorize each with its pattern.
- Saying 'Me gusta la matemática' for school subjects: the subject is usually plural — Me gustan las matemáticas.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mis clases
*Student:* Describe your class schedule and opinions of each subject in 10 sentences.

*How to teach:* Model: 'Tengo matemáticas a las nueve. Me gustan porque son interesantes, pero son difíciles.' Your child writes 10 sentences about classes and opinions; check that adjectives agree with the subject (las ciencias son fascinantes).

- **Q:** Write 'History is boring but art is fun.'
  **A:** La historia es aburrida, pero el arte es divertido.
- **Q:** How do you say 'school subject' and 'schedule'?
  **A:** la asignatura (or la materia); el horario

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stem-changers
*Student:* Practice e→ie, o→ue, e→i verbs with a card game and written drill.

*How to teach:* Sort verbs into three 'boots': e→ie (empezar, querer, preferir), o→ue (almorzar, poder, dormir), e→i (pedir, servir, repetir). Play the card game: flip a verb and a subject and say the form; then complete the written drill.

- **Q:** Conjugate almorzar for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** almuerzo; almorzamos
- **Q:** Conjugate pedir for ella.
  **A:** pide

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Schools abroad
*Student:* Read about a school day in Mexico or Argentina and list five differences.

*How to teach:* Read the article together; your child lists five differences (e.g., uniforms, morning or afternoon shifts called turnos in Mexico, students staying in one classroom while teachers move, calling teachers 'maestro/profe'). Check that each difference comes from the reading.

- **Q:** Write a sentence about a difference: 'In Mexico, many students wear uniforms.'
  **A:** En México, muchos estudiantes llevan uniforme.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conversation
*Student:* Hold a role-play conversation about after-school activities using preferir and querer.

*How to teach:* Act out a conversation: '¿Qué prefieres hacer después de las clases?' — 'Prefiero jugar al fútbol. ¿Y tú?' Your child must use preferir and querer at least three times each and ask follow-up questions.

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Qué quieres hacer esta tarde?' (You want to swim.)
  **A:** Quiero nadar esta tarde.
- **Q:** Ask a friend 'Do you prefer soccer or basketball?'
  **A:** ¿Prefieres el fútbol o el básquetbol?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Stem-changer quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the stem-changer quiz and score the role-play from day 4 with the rubric. Have your child re-conjugate any missed verb in all six forms.

- **Q:** Conjugate dormir in all six present forms.
  **A:** duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormís, duermen

**Assessment:** Stem-changer quiz and role-play rubric.

**Answer key:** Stem-changer quiz sample key: empezar/yo — empiezo; almorzar/tú — almuerzas; preferir/ella — prefiere; pedir/nosotros — pedimos; querer/ellos — quieren; dormir/yo — duermo; servir/tú — sirves; poder/ustedes — pueden; volver/él — vuelve; repetir/yo — repito. Role-play rubric: 4 = asks and answers 5+ questions with correct stem changes and natural follow-ups; 3 = 4 exchanges, few errors; 2 = short answers, several errors; 1 = cannot sustain.

## Visual Art I: Value: from light to dark

**Prepare:** Pencils 2H–6B, blending stump, fine-tip pen, white egg, lamp, phone camera, sphere/cube/cylinder objects (ball, box, can).

**Watch for:**
- Blending everything with a finger. Correct: use a stump or tissue; fingers leave oils and smudges.
- Making the cast shadow lighter than the form shadow. Correct: the cast shadow's core (near the object) is usually the darkest area.

### Day 1 (Monday): Value scales
*Student:* Create 9-step value scales with pencil blending, hatching, cross-hatching, and stippling.

*How to teach:* 9-step value scales in four techniques.

- **Q:** Name four shading techniques.
  **A:** Blending, hatching, cross-hatching, stippling.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shading spheres
*Student:* Render a sphere, cube, and cylinder with highlight, midtone, core shadow, reflected light, and cast shadow.

*How to teach:* Render sphere, cube, cylinder with five lighting zones.

- **Q:** List the five parts of light on a form.
  **A:** Highlight, midtone (light), core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Light source lab
*Student:* Photograph an egg under a single lamp from three angles and draw the best lighting.

*How to teach:* Photograph an egg from three angles; draw the best.

- **Q:** Why use a single light source?
  **A:** It creates clear, consistent shadows that reveal form.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pen techniques
*Student:* Shade the same egg in pen with hatching only.

*How to teach:* Shade the egg in pen with hatching only.

- **Q:** How do you create darker values with pen?
  **A:** Lines closer together, layered cross-hatching or thicker lines.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Peer/parent critique comparing pencil and pen versions using the elements vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Critique comparing pencil and pen versions.

- **Q:** What vocabulary describes the difference?
  **A:** Value range, contrast, texture, line quality.

**Assessment:** Value scales and shaded forms checked against a rubric.

**Answer key:** Rubric: value scales smooth with 9 distinct steps in each technique (4); forms show all five light zones (4); egg drawings accurate with consistent light (4); critique uses element vocabulary (4). Proficient = 3+ in each.

## PE 9 (Course 1): FITNESSGRAM pre-assessment, part 1

**Prepare:** Cones/tape for a 20 m PACER lane, PACER audio, measuring tape, curl-up measuring strip (FITNESSGRAM uses a 4.5-inch strip for ages 10 and up; 3-inch for ages 5–9), mat, cadence audio. Fitness portfolio folder.

**Watch for:**
- Treating the PACER as a sprint. Correct: start slowly; speed increases each level.
- Bouncing off the floor on curl-ups or sagging on push-ups. Correct: controlled form; stop at the second form correction.

### Day 1 (Monday): PACER or mile
*Student:* Complete the 20-meter PACER (or timed 1-mile run) after a full warm-up and record results.

*How to teach:* Full warm-up; PACER (or mile). Record laps or time.

- **Q:** How do you score the PACER?
  **A:** Total laps completed; the test ends on the second missed beep.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Curl-ups
*Student:* Learn proper curl-up form and complete the cadence-based curl-up test.

*How to teach:* Curl-up form: knees ~140°, feet flat, arms straight, fingertips slide across the strip, head returns to mat; one every 3 s; max 75.

- **Q:** What is the cadence for curl-ups?
  **A:** One curl-up every 3 seconds (20 per minute).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Push-ups
*Student:* Learn 90-degree push-up form and complete the push-up test.

*How to teach:* 90° push-ups: body straight, lower until upper arms parallel to floor (elbows at 90°), one every 3 s.

- **Q:** What ends the push-up test?
  **A:** The second form correction (or stopping/failing to keep cadence).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Active recovery
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute easy walk and 15 minutes of mobility work.

*How to teach:* 30-minute easy walk and 15 minutes mobility (hip circles, thoracic rotation, ankle mobility).

- **Q:** Why include active recovery days?
  **A:** Light movement increases blood flow and aids recovery without adding stress.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare results to Healthy Fitness Zone charts and record them in your fitness portfolio.

*How to teach:* Compare results to Healthy Fitness Zone (HFZ) charts and record.

- **Q:** What is the Healthy Fitness Zone?
  **A:** FITNESSGRAM's criterion-referenced standard showing the level of fitness associated with good health for age and sex.

**Assessment:** Baseline FITNESSGRAM results recorded.

**Answer key:** Baseline results recorded: proficient = PACER/mile, curl-ups and push-ups all recorded with date, units and HFZ status (Healthy Fitness Zone or Needs Improvement) in the portfolio.

## Health & Wellness: Accessing valid health information and services

**Prepare:** Print a CRAAP checklist; pick four websites (.gov, .org, .com product). Find three viral health claims. Look up local clinic, pharmacy, urgent care and Covered California/Medi-Cal info. Write the Unit 1 test.

**Watch for:**
- Trusting a source because it's popular or has many shares. Correct: check authority, evidence and purpose.
- Using the ER for minor problems. Correct: ER is for emergencies; urgent care for non-life-threatening problems that need same-day care; primary care for routine and ongoing needs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Credible or not?
*Student:* Use a CRAAP checklist (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose) to evaluate four health websites, including a .gov, a .org and a product site.

*How to teach:* The child scores each site with CRAAP and explains the most and least reliable.

- **Q:** Why might a product site be less reliable?
  **A:** Its purpose is to sell, so information may be biased or exaggerated.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Spotting misinformation
*Student:* Analyze three viral social-media health claims and trace each back to (or fail to find) its original research source.

*How to teach:* The child traces three claims to their origins and judges each.

- **Q:** What should you do with a health claim that has no traceable source?
  **A:** Treat it as unverified; don't share it, and check reputable sources such as CDC or NIH.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Health care providers
*Student:* Make a chart of health care providers (primary care, specialists, urgent care, ER, school nurse, telehealth) and when to use each.

*How to teach:* Make a provider chart: primary care (checkups, ongoing care), specialists, urgent care, ER, school nurse, telehealth.

- **Q:** Where should you go for a sprained ankle on a Saturday? For chest pain and trouble breathing?
  **A:** Sprained ankle – urgent care (or primary care next week if mild). Chest pain/trouble breathing – call 911/ER.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Local services map
*Student:* Research and list your local clinic, pharmacy, urgent care, Medi-Cal/Covered California options and teen confidential services with phone numbers.

*How to teach:* Research and list local services with phone numbers.

- **Q:** What is Covered California?
  **A:** California's health insurance marketplace where people buy plans, often with financial help; Medi-Cal is California's Medicaid program for low-income residents.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on wellness, decision making, goal setting and evaluating health information.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** What does the 'A' for Authority ask?
  **A:** Who wrote or published it and are they qualified/credible?

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test and completed local health services directory.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test key: 1) WHO health definition 2) health triangle 3) wellness continuum 4) risk vs protective factors 5) DECIDE steps 6) SMART goal definition 7) rewrite vague goal correctly 8) CRAAP = Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose 9) .gov/.edu and medical organizations generally more reliable 10) when to use ER vs urgent care vs primary care 11) Medi-Cal = CA Medicaid; Covered California = marketplace. Directory: 4 = at least 6 services with addresses/phones including teen confidential services.
