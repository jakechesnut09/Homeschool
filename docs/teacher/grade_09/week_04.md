# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 4

## English 9: Drafting, revising, and publishing a personal narrative

**Prepare:** Have the narrative plan, a laptop or notebook, and a revision checklist (show-don't-tell, varied openings, dialogue punctuation, commas after introductory elements, consistent tense). Prepare the Unit 1 test.

**Watch for:**
- Ending with a moral tacked on ('And that's why you should never give up'): reflection should grow from the story and show specific change.
- Dialogue punctuation errors: punctuation goes inside the quotation marks, and a new speaker starts a new paragraph.
- Shifting verb tense mid-story: pick past or present and keep it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft the opening
*Student:* Write the first half of your personal narrative, opening in the middle of the action with dialogue or a vivid image.

*How to teach:* Read two strong openings that start in action. Your child drafts the first half beginning with dialogue or a vivid image.

- **Q:** Rewrite 'I was nervous before the race' as a showing opening.
  **A:** Sample: 'My shoelace had come untied three times, and each time my fingers shook harder.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finish the draft
*Student:* Complete the narrative through the climax and a reflective ending that shows what you learned (800–1,200 words).

*How to teach:* Your child finishes the draft (800–1,200 words) through the climax and a reflective ending.

- **Q:** What should a reflective ending do?
  **A:** Explain what the writer learned or how they changed because of the event, connected to specific moments.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise for craft
*Student:* Highlight telling sentences and rewrite five as showing details; vary sentence openings using phrases and clauses from weeks 2–3.

*How to teach:* Highlight 'telling' sentences together; your child rewrites five as showing details and varies sentence openings with phrases and clauses.

- **Q:** Revise 'She was angry' to show instead of tell.
  **A:** Sample: 'She slammed the cabinet so hard the plates rattled.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit and publish
*Student:* Edit with a checklist (commas after introductory elements, dialogue punctuation, verb tense) and type a final copy.

*How to teach:* Edit with the checklist; type the final copy.

- **Q:** Punctuate: I can't believe it she said
  **A:** "I can't believe it," she said.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read your narrative aloud to family; take the Unit 1 test on plot, conflict, irony, point of view, and phrases/clauses.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the narrative aloud to family; then give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** Name the four types of conflict.
  **A:** Person vs. person, person vs. nature, person vs. society, person vs. self.

**Assessment:** Personal narrative scored with W.9-10.3 rubric; Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (W.9-10.3): 4 = engaging opening, clear sequence building to a climax, vivid sensory details and dialogue, varied sentences, meaningful reflection, nearly error-free; 3 = clear story and reflection, some telling, minor errors; 2 = list-like events, little detail or reflection; 1 = incomplete. Unit 1 test sample answers: 1) Climax of 'The Most Dangerous Game' — Rainsford confronts Zaroff in the bedroom. 2) Montresor is unreliable because he never specifies the insult. 3) 'I drink to your long life' — verbal irony. 4) Gift of the Magi ending — situational irony. 5) First-person point of view = narrator uses 'I'. 6) Identify a participial phrase and an adjective clause in given sentences. 7) Explain how foreshadowing builds suspense with one example.

## Algebra I: Solving multi-step linear equations

**Prepare:** Draw a balance scale template (or use algebra tiles). Write the properties of equality on a card: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division properties of equality; distributive property.

**Watch for:**
- Doing an operation to only one side: the balance must stay level — whatever is done to one side is done to the other.
- Sign errors when distributing a negative: −2(x − 4) = −2x + 8, not −2x − 8.
- Thinking 0 = 0 means x = 0: a true statement with no variable means infinitely many solutions; a false one (like 1 = −4) means no solution.

### Day 1 (Monday): Balance model
*Student:* Use balance-scale drawings to solve 10 two-step equations and write the property justifying each step.

*How to teach:* Draw 3x + 5 = 20 on a balance: remove 5 from both pans, then split into 3 equal groups. Your child solves the 10 equations, naming the property for each step.

- **Q:** Solve 3x + 5 = 20 and justify each step.
  **A:** Subtract 5 from both sides (subtraction property of equality): 3x = 15. Divide both sides by 3 (division property): x = 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Distributive equations
*Student:* Solve 15 equations involving parentheses and fractions (e.g., ⅔(x−6)=4).

*How to teach:* Model ⅔(x − 6) = 4: multiply both sides by 3/2 to get x − 6 = 6, so x = 12; or distribute first. Your child solves 15 problems, checking each by substitution.

- **Q:** Solve ⅔(x − 6) = 4.
  **A:** x − 6 = 6, so x = 12. Check: ⅔(6) = 4 ✓.
- **Q:** Solve 4(2x − 3) = 28.
  **A:** 8x − 12 = 28 → 8x = 40 → x = 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Variables on both sides
*Student:* Solve 15 equations with variables on both sides, including ones with no solution or infinitely many.

*How to teach:* Model collecting variables on one side: 5x − 3 = 2x + 12 → 3x = 15 → x = 5. Show special cases: 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 gives 6 = 6 (all real numbers); 3x + 1 = 3x − 4 gives 1 = −4 (no solution). Your child solves the 15.

- **Q:** Solve 5x − 3 = 2x + 12.
  **A:** x = 5.
- **Q:** How many solutions does 3x + 1 = 3x − 4 have?
  **A:** None — subtracting 3x gives 1 = −4, which is false.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Find and correct the error in 8 worked solutions, explaining each mistake in writing.

*How to teach:* Read one worked solution aloud and find the error together. Your child finds and corrects the error in the other 7, writing a sentence explaining each mistake.

- **Q:** A student solved 3x − 2 = 7 by writing 3x = 5, x = 5/3. Find the error.
  **A:** They subtracted 2 instead of adding 2. Correct: 3x = 9, x = 3.
- **Q:** A student wrote −2(x − 4) = −2x − 8. What's wrong?
  **A:** −2 times −4 is +8, so it should be −2x + 8.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on multi-step equations with step justifications.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key; your child must write a justification for each step. Award partial credit for correct reasoning with arithmetic slips.

- **Q:** Solve and justify: x/4 + 3 = 7.
  **A:** Subtract 3: x/4 = 4. Multiply both sides by 4: x = 16.

**Assessment:** Equation quiz with justifications.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) 4x − 7 = 13 → x = 5. 2) 3(x + 2) = −9 → x = −5. 3) ¾x + 2 = 11 → x = 12. 4) 6x − 4 = 2x + 16 → x = 5. 5) 2(x − 1) = 2x + 3 → no solution. 6) 5(x + 1) − x = 4x + 5 → all real numbers. 7) 0.5(4x − 8) = x + 2 → x = 6. 8) −3(x − 2) = 15 → x = −3. Each step must be justified (e.g., 'add 7 to both sides — addition property of equality'). Rubric: full credit = correct answer + correct justifications; half credit = correct answer with missing/wrong justifications.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Energy flow and matter cycling in food webs

**Prepare:** Purchase a sterilized owl pellet kit (heat-treated) or use a virtual pellet (e.g., KidWings). Tweezers, gloves, paper plate, bone chart. Wash hands after dissection.

**Watch for:**
- Arrows pointing from predator to prey: food-web arrows show energy flow — from the organism eaten to the eater.
- Thinking 90% of energy 'disappears': it is used for life processes and lost as heat, not destroyed.
- Assuming top predators are most numerous: fewer organisms can be supported at higher trophic levels.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food webs
*Student:* Build a food web for a California chaparral ecosystem with at least 12 organisms.

*How to teach:* Your child builds a chaparral food web with 12+ organisms.

- **Q:** Give a 4-link chaparral food chain.
  **A:** Chamise/manzanita → brush rabbit or deer mouse → gopher snake → red-tailed hawk (other valid chains accepted).
- **Q:** Which direction should arrows point?
  **A:** From food to consumer — the direction energy flows.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Owl pellet dissection
*Student:* Dissect an owl pellet (or virtual pellet), identify bones, and estimate prey eaten.

*How to teach:* Dissect the pellet; sort bones; estimate prey eaten (count skulls).

- **Q:** How can you tell how many animals an owl ate from a pellet?
  **A:** Count skulls or pairs of jaws — each skull is one prey animal.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Energy pyramids
*Student:* Use the 10% rule to calculate energy at each trophic level from 10,000 kcal of producers.

*How to teach:* Apply the 10% rule from 10,000 kcal.

- **Q:** Starting with 10,000 kcal in producers, how much reaches tertiary consumers?
  **A:** Primary 1,000; secondary 100; tertiary 10 kcal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Biomass
*Student:* Explain why biomass pyramids narrow and where energy is lost as heat.

*How to teach:* Explain why biomass pyramids narrow and where energy goes.

- **Q:** Where does the 'missing' 90% of energy go at each level?
  **A:** Used for cellular respiration (movement, growth, body heat) and lost as heat, or not eaten/digested (waste).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on energy flow calculations.

*How to teach:* Energy-flow quiz.

- **Q:** If a hawk gets 5 kcal, how much producer energy supported it in a 4-level chain?
  **A:** 5 × 10 × 10 × 10 = 5,000 kcal.

**Assessment:** Energy calculations quiz and dissection data sheet.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) 20,000 kcal producers → 2,000 → 200 → 20 kcal. 2) Arrow direction: toward the consumer. 3) Trophic level of a snake eating mice that eat seeds: third (secondary consumer). 4) Why fewer top predators: less energy available. 5) Decomposers recycle matter, not energy. Dissection data sheet: bones sorted by type, prey identified by skull, count of prey individuals, sketch.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Plate tectonics and landforms

**Prepare:** Graham crackers, frosting (mantle), wax paper; USGS Latest Earthquakes map (earthquake.usgs.gov). Photos of landforms (canyon, delta, dunes, glacial valley like Yosemite).

**Watch for:**
- Believing California will 'fall into the ocean': the San Andreas is a transform fault — plates slide horizontally past each other.
- Thinking all earthquakes occur on plate boundaries: most do, but some are intraplate.
- Confusing weathering and erosion: weathering breaks rock down; erosion moves it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plate boundaries
*Student:* Model convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries with crackers and frosting.

*How to teach:* Model convergent, divergent, transform boundaries.

- **Q:** What happens at each boundary type?
  **A:** Convergent: plates collide (mountains, subduction, volcanoes). Divergent: plates separate (rifts, mid-ocean ridges). Transform: plates slide past (earthquakes).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California faults
*Student:* Map the San Andreas Fault and recent earthquakes using the USGS live earthquake map.

*How to teach:* Map the San Andreas Fault and recent earthquakes.

- **Q:** Which plates meet along the San Andreas Fault and how do they move?
  **A:** The Pacific Plate moves northwest relative to the North American Plate — a transform boundary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Landforms
*Student:* Identify landforms formed by plates, water, wind, and ice in photos.

*How to teach:* Identify landforms by agent.

- **Q:** What formed Yosemite Valley's U shape?
  **A:** Glaciers carved it (with granite formed underground and uplifted).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hazards
*Student:* Analyze how earthquakes and volcanoes affect human settlement (e.g., 1906 San Francisco).

*How to teach:* Analyze hazards' effects on settlement.

- **Q:** What happened in San Francisco in 1906?
  **A:** A magnitude ~7.9 earthquake on April 18 and the fires that followed destroyed much of the city and killed an estimated 3,000 people; it led to rebuilding and later stronger building codes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on tectonics and landforms.

*How to teach:* Tectonics quiz.

- **Q:** What landform forms where an ocean plate subducts under a continental plate?
  **A:** A volcanic mountain range (e.g., the Cascades) and an ocean trench.

**Assessment:** Tectonics quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Three boundary types. 2) San Andreas = transform. 3) Himalayas = continental–continental convergence. 4) Mid-Atlantic Ridge = divergent. 5) Ring of Fire = zone of volcanoes and earthquakes around the Pacific. 6) Weathering vs. erosion. 7) 1906 earthquake impacts.

## Spanish II: Unit 1 integrated performance assessment

**Prepare:** Print the IPA task sheet and find an authentic school-schedule or school-life article (e.g., from a Spanish-language school website or a teen magazine). Plan the 3-minute conversation prompts and gather Unit 1 flashcards. Write the unit test covering present tense, reflexives, stem-changers, routine, and school vocabulary.

**Watch for:**
- Translating word-for-word from English in the email: encourage short, known structures (Me levanto a las siete) rather than invented phrases.
- Forgetting greetings and closings in an email: use 'Hola, ...' / 'Querido/a ...' and 'Un abrazo' or 'Saludos'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interpretive task
*Student:* Read an authentic school-schedule article from a Spanish-language site and answer comprehension questions.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the article independently, highlighting cognates first, then answers the comprehension questions in English or simple Spanish. Afterward, discuss which clues (cognates, numbers, times) helped.

- **Q:** What strategy helps when you meet an unknown word in an authentic text?
  **A:** Look for cognates (e.g., 'horario' near times and class names), use context, and skip words not needed for the question.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interpersonal task
*Student:* Hold a 3-minute conversation about routines and school with a parent or tutor.

*How to teach:* Hold the 3-minute conversation using questions like ¿A qué hora te levantas? ¿Cuál es tu clase favorita? ¿Qué haces después de las clases? Keep it natural; note errors but don't interrupt. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** How would you answer '¿A qué hora te acuestas?' (11:00 p.m.)
  **A:** Me acuesto a las once de la noche.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentational task
*Student:* Write a 150-word email to a new exchange student describing your routine.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the 150-word email to a new exchange student, including a greeting, routine with times, classes, and a question for the student. Check reflexive pronouns and word count.

- **Q:** Write a good closing sentence asking a question back.
  **A:** Example: ¿Y tú? ¿Cómo es tu rutina? ¡Escríbeme pronto! Saludos, (nombre)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Vocabulary review
*Student:* Review Unit 1 vocabulary with Quizlet or flashcards and correct errors.

*How to teach:* Review all Unit 1 flashcards; sort into 'know' and 'don't know' piles and redo the second pile until it is empty. Your child corrects errors from the IPA tasks and writes the rule beside each.

- **Q:** Translate: 'to brush one's teeth,' 'to get up,' 'to go to bed.'
  **A:** cepillarse los dientes, levantarse, acostarse

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 written test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test without notes. Grade, go over errors together, and log scores.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Nosotros nos acuestamos tarde.'
  **A:** Nosotros nos acostamos tarde (no stem change in nosotros).

**Assessment:** Integrated performance assessment and unit test.

**Answer key:** IPA rubric (each mode, 4 levels): Interpretive — 4 = identifies main idea and 5+ details accurately; 3 = main idea and 3–4 details; 2 = some details; 1 = isolated words. Interpersonal — 4 = sustains 3 minutes, asks and answers questions, mostly accurate present/reflexive verbs; 3 = sustains with some pauses; 2 = mostly short answers; 1 = cannot sustain. Presentational — 4 = ~150 words, greeting/closing, routine in sequence with times, accurate reflexives; 3 = complete with minor errors; 2 = under 100 words or many errors; 1 = fragments. Unit test sample items: me ducho; nosotros almorzamos; ella se viste; 'A las ocho y media empiezo las clases.'

## Visual Art I: Still life value drawing

**Prepare:** 3–5 white objects (eggs, mugs, bowls, crumpled paper), one strong lamp, good drawing paper.

**Watch for:**
- Using too little contrast. Correct: commit to the darkest darks early to set the range.
- Outlining shadows. Correct: shadows have soft or hard edges, not outlines.

### Day 1 (Monday): Set up
*Student:* Arrange 3–5 white objects under a strong lamp; make three thumbnail compositions and pick one.

*How to teach:* Arrange objects; three thumbnails; pick one.

- **Q:** What makes a strong still-life composition?
  **A:** Overlapping objects, varied heights, an odd number of objects, asymmetrical balance and a clear focal point.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Block-in
*Student:* Lightly block in the composition with sighting.

*How to teach:* Block in with sighting.

- **Q:** Why block in lightly?
  **A:** So proportions can be corrected easily.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Value
*Student:* Lay in the darkest darks and lightest lights, then build midtones.

*How to teach:* Lay in darks and lights, then midtones.

- **Q:** Why establish darks first?
  **A:** It sets the full value range so midtones can be judged against it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Refine
*Student:* Refine edges, textures, and cast shadows.

*How to teach:* Refine edges, textures, cast shadows.

- **Q:** What is edge quality?
  **A:** How sharp or soft a transition is; varying edges creates depth and focus.

### Day 5 (Friday): Critique
*Student:* Write criteria for a successful still life and use them to self-evaluate your drawing.

*How to teach:* Write criteria and self-evaluate.

- **Q:** Give an example of a strong success criterion.
  **A:** 'The drawing includes at least 7 distinct values from white to near black.'

**Assessment:** Still life drawing scored against self-written and teacher criteria.

**Answer key:** Still life rubric: Composition (thumbnail-based, balanced), Proportion (accurate), Value (full range, form zones on each object), Edges/Texture (varied, believable), Craftsmanship. 4 levels each; proficient = 3+. Self-written criteria scored for specificity.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Pre-assessment, part 2, and goal setting

**Prepare:** Yardstick and 12-inch box for sit-and-reach; ruler for trunk lift; SMART goal sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Hyperextending on the trunk lift. Correct: lift slowly to a maximum of 12 inches.
- Focusing only on weight. Correct: body composition is one part of health; fitness, habits and how you feel matter too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trunk lift and flexibility
*Student:* Complete the trunk lift and back-saver sit-and-reach tests.

*How to teach:* Trunk lift (max 12 in) and back-saver sit-and-reach (each leg).

- **Q:** What does the trunk lift measure?
  **A:** Trunk extensor strength and flexibility (lower back).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body composition
*Student:* Learn what body composition measures and why it's one part of health, then complete a 30-minute workout of choice.

*How to teach:* Explain body composition (percent body fat; methods include skinfolds, bioelectrical impedance, BMI as a screening estimate) and its limits; 30-minute workout.

- **Q:** Why is BMI an imperfect measure for athletes?
  **A:** It uses only height and weight, so muscular people can be classified as 'overweight' despite low body fat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): SMART goals
*Student:* Write two SMART fitness goals based on baseline results.

*How to teach:* Write two SMART goals from baseline.

- **Q:** Make 'do more push-ups' SMART.
  **A:** 'Increase my 90° push-ups from 12 to 18 by week 18 by doing 3 sets of push-ups 3 days a week.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Goal workout
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute workout targeting your weakest component.

*How to teach:* 30-minute workout targeting the weakest component.

- **Q:** If flexibility is weakest, what would you do?
  **A:** Stretch major muscle groups 3+ days a week, holding 15–30 s, plus yoga.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit a goal sheet with baseline data, goals, and a plan to reach them.

*How to teach:* Submit the goal sheet.

- **Q:** What does 'Time-bound' mean?
  **A:** The goal has a specific deadline.

**Assessment:** SMART goal sheet.

**Answer key:** SMART goal sheet: proficient = baseline data for all tests, two goals each meeting all five SMART criteria tied to a specific weak area, and a weekly action plan (FITT) for each.

## Health & Wellness: Nutrients and MyPlate

**Prepare:** Find a short nutrition video (e.g., USDA MyPlate). Open the MyPlate Plan tool. Gather measuring cups, cereal, rice, juice and nuts. Prepare a 3-day food log sheet and a 15-question quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking carbohydrates or fats are 'bad.' Correct: both are essential; choose whole grains and unsaturated fats.
- Underestimating portions. Correct: measured servings are usually smaller than what people pour.

### Day 1 (Monday): Six nutrients
*Student:* Watch a short nutrition video and make a chart of the six nutrient classes with functions and three food sources each.

*How to teach:* Chart the six nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water with functions and sources.

- **Q:** Which three nutrients provide energy and how many calories per gram?
  **A:** Carbohydrates (4 cal/g), protein (4 cal/g), fat (9 cal/g).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): MyPlate
*Student:* Use the MyPlate Plan tool to find your personal daily food-group targets and record them.

*How to teach:* Use MyPlate Plan with the child's info; record daily targets.

- **Q:** Name the five MyPlate food groups.
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables, grains, protein foods, dairy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Portion lab
*Student:* Measure out recommended portions of cereal, rice, juice and nuts with measuring cups and compare them to the amounts you usually serve.

*How to teach:* Measure recommended portions (1 cup cereal, ½ cup cooked rice = 1 oz-eq, ½ oz nuts = 1 oz-eq protein, 1 cup juice) and compare to usual servings.

- **Q:** How much cooked rice counts as 1 ounce-equivalent of grains?
  **A:** ½ cup.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Food log
*Student:* Begin a 3-day food log, recording everything you eat and drink with food groups and approximate portions.

*How to teach:* Start the 3-day food log with food groups and portions.

- **Q:** Why log drinks too?
  **A:** Beverages add calories and sugar (soda, juice) and count toward dairy/fruit groups.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Compare your food log to your MyPlate targets and take a 15-question nutrient quiz.

*How to teach:* Compare log to targets; give the quiz.

- **Q:** What are the main functions of protein?
  **A:** Building and repairing tissues, making enzymes and hormones, supporting immune function.

**Assessment:** Nutrient quiz and food log comparison.

**Answer key:** Nutrient quiz key (15): carbs – main energy; protein – growth/repair; fats – energy storage, vitamin absorption (A, D, E, K), cell membranes; vitamins – regulate body processes; minerals – e.g., calcium for bones, iron for blood; water – temperature regulation, transport; calories: carbs 4, protein 4, fat 9; fiber is a carbohydrate; MyPlate groups (5); half of grains whole; half plate fruits and vegetables. 12/15 proficient. Food-log comparison: compares each group to targets and names one change.
