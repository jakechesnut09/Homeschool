# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 29

## English 9: Courage and conscience: Chapters 7–11

**Prepare:** TKAM Chapters 7–11. Theme tracker (empathy, courage, justice, innocence).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the mockingbird quote is only about birds: it's about harming innocents.
- Defining courage as 'a man with a gun': Atticus explicitly rejects that definition.
- Seeing Mrs. Dubose as simply mean: her fight against morphine addiction is Atticus's example of real courage.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gifts in the tree
*Student:* Read Chapters 7–8; analyze the knot-hole gifts and the fire as signs of Boo's character.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 7–8; analyze the gifts and the fire.

- **Q:** What gifts appear in the tree in Chapter 7?
  **A:** Soap figures carved to look like Jem and Scout, a pocket watch and chain, a spelling medal, and gum.
- **Q:** What happens during the fire at Miss Maudie's?
  **A:** Someone puts a blanket around Scout's shoulders without her noticing — it was Boo Radley.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Atticus takes the case
*Student:* Read Chapter 9; explain Atticus's reasons for defending Tom Robinson.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 9; explain why Atticus defends Tom.

- **Q:** Why does Atticus take Tom Robinson's case?
  **A:** Because it's right; if he didn't, he 'couldn't hold up my head in town' or tell his children what to do.
- **Q:** What does Atticus say about trying even when you will lose?
  **A:** 'Simply because we were licked a hundred years before we started is no reason for us not to try to win.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The mad dog
*Student:* Read Chapter 10; explain the mockingbird quote and what Atticus's shooting reveals.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 10; explain the mockingbird quote.

- **Q:** Why is it 'a sin to kill a mockingbird'?
  **A:** Miss Maudie explains that mockingbirds only make music for us; they do no harm — so harming them (innocents) is wrong.
- **Q:** What does the mad-dog scene reveal about Atticus?
  **A:** He's an excellent shot ('One-Shot Finch') but doesn't boast — showing humility and restraint.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mrs. Dubose
*Student:* Read Chapter 11; write about Atticus's definition of real courage.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 11; write about Atticus's definition of courage.

- **Q:** How does Atticus define real courage?
  **A:** 'It's when you know you're licked before you begin but you begin anyway and you see it through no matter what.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Chapters 7–11; theme tracker update with six quotations.

*How to teach:* Quiz on Chapters 7–11; update theme tracker with six quotations.

- **Q:** What did Mrs. Dubose struggle against before she died?
  **A:** Morphine addiction — she was determined to die free of it.

**Assessment:** Quiz and theme tracker.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Nathan Radley fills the knot-hole with cement. 2) Boo puts the blanket on Scout. 3) Atticus defends Tom Robinson. 4) Mockingbirds harm no one. 5) Atticus shoots the mad dog (Tim Johnson). 6) Jem destroys Mrs. Dubose's camellias. 7) His punishment: reading to her. 8) She was overcoming morphine addiction. Theme tracker: proficient = six accurate quotes with page numbers and theme connections.

## Algebra I: Forms of quadratic functions

**Prepare:** Desmos. A card listing the three forms: standard y = ax² + bx + c, factored y = a(x − p)(x − q), vertex y = a(x − h)² + k.

**Watch for:**
- Reading zeros of (x − 2)(x + 4) as −2 and 4: set each factor to 0 → x = 2 and x = −4.
- When completing the square, forgetting to subtract what you added: x² + 6x + 5 = (x² + 6x + 9) − 9 + 5.
- Expanding (x − 3)² as x² + 9.

### Day 1 (Monday): Factored form
*Student:* Find zeros from factored form and sketch the graph.

*How to teach:* Find zeros from factored form, the axis halfway between, then the vertex.

- **Q:** Sketch y = (x − 2)(x + 4): zeros and vertex?
  **A:** Zeros 2 and −4; axis x = −1; vertex (−1, −9).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Vertex form
*Student:* Graph from vertex form and convert to standard form.

*How to teach:* Graph from vertex form, then expand to standard form.

- **Q:** Convert y = (x − 3)² − 4 to standard form.
  **A:** x² − 6x + 9 − 4 = x² − 6x + 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Completing the square to vertex form
*Student:* Rewrite x²+6x+5 in vertex form by completing the square.

*How to teach:* Complete the square: take half of b, square it, add and subtract.

- **Q:** Write x² + 6x + 5 in vertex form.
  **A:** (x + 3)² − 4; vertex (−3, −4).
- **Q:** Write x² − 10x + 7 in vertex form.
  **A:** (x − 5)² − 18.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Choosing a form
*Student:* Decide which form best answers a question (max height vs. landing point).

*How to teach:* Discuss: vertex form gives max height directly; factored form gives landing points (zeros); standard form gives the starting height (y-intercept).

- **Q:** Which form best shows when a ball hits the ground?
  **A:** Factored form — the zeros.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on quadratic forms.

*How to teach:* Give the forms quiz.

- **Q:** Zeros of y = 2(x + 1)(x − 5)?
  **A:** x = −1 and x = 5.

**Assessment:** Forms quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) y = (x + 1)(x − 3): zeros −1 and 3; vertex (1, −4). 2) y = 2(x − 1)² + 3 → standard: 2x² − 4x + 5. 3) x² + 8x + 10 → (x + 4)² − 6. 4) x² − 4x − 5 → (x − 5)(x + 1) and (x − 2)² − 9. 5) Best form for max height → vertex; for x-intercepts → factored; for y-intercept → standard.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Growth, differentiation, and stem cells

**Prepare:** Pothos cutting with at least one node; jar of water; window light. Readings on stem cells and cancer (OpenStax Biology).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking differentiated cells lose genes: they keep all genes but switch some on and off.
- Believing cancer is 'catching': cancer results from mutations that disrupt cell-cycle control.
- Assuming all stem cells are embryonic: adult stem cells (e.g., bone marrow) and induced pluripotent stem cells exist.

### Day 1 (Monday): Differentiation
*Student:* Explain how cells with the same DNA become different through gene expression.

*How to teach:* Explain gene expression and differentiation.

- **Q:** How can a muscle cell and a nerve cell have the same DNA but look different?
  **A:** They express different genes, making different proteins.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plant cuttings
*Student:* Root a pothos cutting in water to observe growth from undifferentiated cells.

*How to teach:* Root a pothos cutting at a node; observe over weeks.

- **Q:** Where do new roots grow from on a pothos cutting and why?
  **A:** From the nodes, where meristem-like cells can divide and differentiate into roots.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stem cells
*Student:* Read about stem cell types and their medical uses.

*How to teach:* Read about stem cell types.

- **Q:** What is the difference between embryonic and adult stem cells?
  **A:** Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent (can become almost any cell); adult stem cells are more limited (e.g., blood stem cells make blood cells).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cancer
*Student:* Explain cancer as uncontrolled cell division and checkpoint failure.

*How to teach:* Explain cancer as checkpoint failure.

- **Q:** How does cancer develop?
  **A:** Mutations in genes controlling the cell cycle (e.g., checkpoints, tumor suppressors like p53) let cells divide uncontrollably, forming tumors.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on growth and differentiation.

*How to teach:* Differentiation quiz.

- **Q:** What is a medical use of stem cells today?
  **A:** Bone marrow (hematopoietic stem cell) transplants for leukemia.

**Assessment:** Differentiation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Differentiation = gene expression. 2) Stem cell definition. 3) Embryonic vs. adult vs. iPS. 4) Cell cycle checkpoints. 5) Cancer = uncontrolled division. 6) Plant meristems.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Labor and the farmworker movement

**Prepare:** Plan de Delano (1966); bracero contract and photos (Bracero History Archive, braceroarchive.org); Delano strike photos; readings on Larry Itliong, Cesar Chavez, and Dolores Huerta.

**Watch for:**
- Crediting the 1965 strike only to Cesar Chavez: Filipino workers of the AWOC, led by Larry Itliong, walked out first on September 8, 1965; Chavez's NFWA joined about a week later.
- Thinking the Bracero Program was purely beneficial: workers often faced poor housing, withheld wages, and abuse.
- Seeing boycotts as passive: the grape boycott required national organizing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bracero Program
*Student:* Read about the Bracero Program (1942–1964) and analyze a bracero contract and photographs for working conditions.

*How to teach:* Read about the Bracero Program; analyze a contract and photos.

- **Q:** What was the Bracero Program?
  **A:** A U.S.–Mexico agreement (1942–1964) bringing millions of Mexican men on temporary contracts to work in agriculture and railroads.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Delano grape strike
*Student:* Learn how Filipino workers (Larry Itliong) began the 1965 strike and joined with Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta.

*How to teach:* Learn about the Delano grape strike.

- **Q:** Who began the Delano grape strike and when?
  **A:** Filipino farmworkers of the Agricultural Workers Organizing Committee, led by Larry Itliong, on September 8, 1965.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan de Delano
*Student:* Read the 1966 Plan de Delano and identify its demands, religious imagery, and intended audiences.

*How to teach:* Read the Plan de Delano.

- **Q:** What religious imagery does the Plan de Delano use, and why?
  **A:** It describes the 1966 march to Sacramento as a pilgrimage and invokes the Virgin of Guadalupe, appealing to farmworkers' Catholic faith and framing the cause as moral.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Boycott
*Student:* Analyze the 1965–1970 grape boycott: how did consumers across the country pressure growers to sign contracts?

*How to teach:* Analyze the grape boycott.

- **Q:** How did the grape boycott pressure growers?
  **A:** Millions of consumers stopped buying table grapes, and stores were pressured to stop selling them, cutting growers' profits until they signed union contracts in 1970.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how Filipino and Mexican farmworkers built a coalition and what made it effective.

*How to teach:* Paragraph on the Filipino–Mexican coalition.

- **Q:** What union formed when the AWOC and NFWA merged?
  **A:** The United Farm Workers Organizing Committee (later the United Farm Workers, UFW).

**Assessment:** Coalition paragraph.

**Answer key:** Coalition paragraph: Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate. Proficient content: AWOC (Itliong, Filipino) and NFWA (Chavez, Huerta, Mexican American) joined forces in 1965 and merged in 1966; shared goals (wages, conditions, union recognition); strategies — strikes, the march to Sacramento, nonviolence, and the national boycott — produced 1970 contracts.

## Spanish II: Introduction to the present subjunctive: recommendations and wishes

**Prepare:** Make a subjunctive chart: yo present → drop -o → opposite endings (-ar: -e, -es, -e, -emos, -éis, -en; -er/-ir: -a, -as, -a, -amos, -áis, -an). Irregulars: dar (dé), ir (vaya), ser (sea), estar (esté), saber (sepa), haber (haya).

**Watch for:**
- Using the subjunctive without a change of subject: 'Quiero ir' (same subject, infinitive) vs. 'Quiero que tú vayas' (different subjects, subjunctive).
- Forgetting 'que' after the trigger: Es importante que reciclemos.
- Using the subjunctive after ojalá in a different way: ojalá is always followed by the subjunctive (Ojalá que llueva).

### Day 1 (Monday): Forms
*Student:* Learn the "opposite vowel" rule from the yo form and conjugate 20 verbs, including irregulars (sea, vaya, tenga).

*How to teach:* Teach the 'opposite vowel' rule from the yo form (tengo → tenga, hago → haga, digo → diga). Your child conjugates 20 verbs.

- **Q:** Give the subjunctive yo form of tener, salir, and conocer.
  **A:** tenga, salga, conozca
- **Q:** Give the subjunctive of ir for nosotros.
  **A:** vayamos

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Recommendations
*Student:* Write 10 recommendations for your family about saving water (Recomiendo que tomemos duchas cortas).

*How to teach:* Model: 'Recomiendo que tomemos duchas cortas.' Your child writes 10 water-saving recommendations to family members.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I suggest that my brother turn off the water.'
  **A:** Sugiero que mi hermano cierre el agua (or apague la llave).
- **Q:** Translate: 'I recommend that we use less water.'
  **A:** Recomiendo que usemos menos agua.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Impersonal expressions
*Student:* Complete 15 sentences using es importante/necesario/mejor que + subjunctive.

*How to teach:* Your child completes 15 sentences with es importante/necesario/mejor que + subjunctive.

- **Q:** Complete: 'Es necesario que nosotros ___ (proteger) el planeta.'
  **A:** protejamos (g → j spelling change)

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ojalá
*Student:* Write five wishes for your community's future with ojalá and share them aloud.

*How to teach:* Your child writes five wishes with ojalá and reads them aloud.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I hope there are more parks.'
  **A:** Ojalá (que) haya más parques.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Subjunctive forms and usage quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the subjunctive quiz.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Es importante que nosotros reciclamos.'
  **A:** Es importante que nosotros reciclemos.

**Assessment:** Subjunctive quiz.

**Answer key:** Subjunctive quiz sample key: hablar/yo — hable; comer/tú — comas; vivir/nosotros — vivamos; tener/ella — tenga; hacer/ellos — hagan; ir/yo — vaya; ser/tú — seas; dar/él — dé; 'It's important that we recycle' — Es importante que reciclemos; 'I recommend that you (tú) save water' — Te recomiendo que ahorres agua.

## Visual Art I: Photo essay: my community

**Prepare:** Camera, notebook. Gordon Parks examples (e.g., 'American Gothic, Washington, D.C.,' 1942; LIFE photo essays like 'Flavio,' 1961). Permission script for photographing people and businesses.

**Watch for:**
- Shooting without a plan. Correct: a shot list ensures variety (establishing, detail, portrait, action).
- Photographing people without consent. Correct: ask permission, especially for close portraits and minors.

### Day 1 (Monday): Documentary photography
*Student:* Study Gordon Parks's photo essays and how sequence creates narrative.

*How to teach:* Study Parks's photo essays and sequencing.

- **Q:** Who was Gordon Parks?
  **A:** An African American photographer, writer and filmmaker who documented civil rights, poverty and Black American life for the FSA and LIFE magazine.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Choose a subject in your community (a market, a family recipe, a local business) and write a shot list.

*How to teach:* Choose a subject; write a shot list.

- **Q:** What shots belong on a shot list?
  **A:** Establishing/wide shot, medium action shots, close-up details, portraits, a closing image.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoot
*Student:* Shoot 50+ photos following the shot list and permission guidelines.

*How to teach:* Shoot 50+ photos with permissions.

- **Q:** Why take many more photos than you need?
  **A:** To have choices for the strongest images and sequence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit and sequence
*Student:* Select and sequence 8 photos and write captions.

*How to teach:* Select and sequence 8; write captions.

- **Q:** What do good captions do?
  **A:** Give who/what/where/when and context without repeating what's obvious.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the photo essay and explain your sequencing choices.

*How to teach:* Present; explain sequencing.

- **Q:** Why does sequence matter?
  **A:** It creates a narrative arc — opening, development, closing — guiding the viewer's understanding.

**Assessment:** Photo essay.

**Answer key:** Photo essay: 4 = 8 strong, varied photos in a narrative sequence with informative captions, permissions obtained, clear explanation of sequencing choices; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = weak narrative; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Jump rope skills and conditioning

**Prepare:** Jump rope per student; music for routine. Rope sizing: stand on the middle; handles should reach the armpits (beginners) or chest.

**Watch for:**
- Jumping too high. Correct: small jumps (1–2 inches) on the balls of the feet.
- Turning with the arms/shoulders. Correct: turn with the wrists, elbows close to the body.

### Day 1 (Monday): Basics
*Student:* Size the rope and practice basic bounce and alternate step for 10 × 1 minute.

*How to teach:* Size rope; basic bounce and alternate step 10×1 min.

- **Q:** How do you size a jump rope?
  **A:** Stand on the middle with one foot; handles should reach about armpit height.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tricks
*Student:* Learn criss-cross, side swing, and boxer step.

*How to teach:* Criss-cross, side swing, boxer step.

- **Q:** Describe a criss-cross.
  **A:** Cross arms at the waist on the downswing so you jump through the crossed loop, then uncross.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Intervals
*Student:* Complete a 20-minute jump rope interval workout and track heart rate.

*How to teach:* 20-minute interval workout; track HR.

- **Q:** Is jumping rope moderate or vigorous?
  **A:** Vigorous for most people at a steady pace.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Routine
*Student:* Create a 1-minute jump rope routine to music with 5 tricks.

*How to teach:* Create a 1-minute routine with 5 tricks.

- **Q:** How do you link tricks smoothly?
  **A:** Use a basic bounce between tricks and plan them on the beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the routine and a 1-minute speed jump test.

*How to teach:* Perform the routine and 1-minute speed test.

- **Q:** How is a speed test counted?
  **A:** Count each time the right foot touches (or every rotation) in 1 minute.

**Assessment:** Jump rope routine and speed test.

**Answer key:** Routine: 4 = 5 tricks, on the beat, smooth transitions, ~1 minute, few misses; speed jump recorded. 3 = 4 tricks or several misses; 2 = 2–3 tricks; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Emergency preparedness: earthquakes, wildfires and smoke

**Prepare:** Household items for a go-bag (water, flashlight, batteries, N95s, first aid, medications, document copies). Ready.gov kit list; AirNow.gov; MyShake app (California earthquake alerts).

**Watch for:**
- Running outside during an earthquake. Correct: Drop, Cover and Hold On where you are.
- Thinking cloth masks protect from wildfire smoke. Correct: a well-fitted N95 filters fine particles; cloth masks don't.

### Day 1 (Monday): Earthquakes
*Student:* Practice Drop, Cover and Hold On and identify safe spots at home.

*How to teach:* Practice Drop, Cover, Hold On; identify safe spots (under sturdy tables) and hazards (windows, tall furniture).

- **Q:** What should you do if there's no table?
  **A:** Drop near an interior wall, cover head and neck with your arms, hold on; stay away from windows.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wildfire and smoke
*Student:* Learn evacuation steps and how to read the Air Quality Index.

*How to teach:* Learn Ready-Set-Go evacuation and AQI categories.

- **Q:** What AQI level is unhealthy for everyone?
  **A:** 151–200 (red, Unhealthy); 101–150 is unhealthy for sensitive groups.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Go-bag
*Student:* Using the Ready.gov list, write a go-bag checklist for your household and gather water, flashlight, medications, N95 masks and copies of documents.

*How to teach:* Write the go-bag checklist and gather items.

- **Q:** How much water per person?
  **A:** At least 1 gallon per person per day for several days (Ready.gov recommends a 3-day supply for evacuation, 2 weeks at home).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family plan
*Student:* Create a family emergency plan with meeting points and contacts.

*How to teach:* Create the family plan with meeting points and contacts.

- **Q:** Why have an out-of-area contact?
  **A:** Long-distance calls/texts often work when local lines are jammed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present your family emergency plan, run a timed earthquake and evacuation drill, and record what to improve.

*How to teach:* Present the plan; run a timed drill; note improvements.

- **Q:** What should you do after shaking stops?
  **A:** Check for injuries, look for hazards (gas, fire), evacuate if unsafe, expect aftershocks and follow the family plan.

**Assessment:** Family emergency plan and drill.

**Answer key:** Family plan rubric: 4 = contacts incl. out-of-area, two meeting places, evacuation routes, go-bag checklist complete, roles assigned, drill run with improvements recorded; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = partial; 1 = incomplete.
