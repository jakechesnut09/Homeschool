# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 32

## English 9: Resolution and theme essay: Chapters 27–31

**Prepare:** TKAM Chapters 27–31. Essay planner. Prepare the Unit 7 test. Note: Chapter 28 includes an attack on the children.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming Jem killed Bob Ewell: Boo Radley killed him while defending the children; Heck Tate reports Ewell 'fell on his knife.'
- Missing the structure: the novel ends where it began — with Boo — completing Scout's growth in empathy.
- Thinking Heck Tate's decision is simply lying: he protects a shy, innocent man — 'a sin' like killing a mockingbird.

### Day 1 (Monday): Halloween
*Student:* Read Chapters 27–28; analyze the attack and how the author builds suspense.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 27–28; analyze suspense in the attack.

- **Q:** How does Lee build suspense on Halloween?
  **A:** Darkness, Scout's limited view from inside the ham costume, strange sounds of someone following, and the shortcut through the dark.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Boo Radley
*Student:* Read Chapters 29–31; explain how Scout standing on the Radley porch completes the empathy theme.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 29–31; explain the porch scene.

- **Q:** What does Scout say when she recognizes Boo?
  **A:** 'Hey, Boo.'
- **Q:** How does standing on the Radley porch complete the empathy theme?
  **A:** Scout sees the neighborhood from Boo's point of view — 'Atticus was right… you never really know a man until you stand in his shoes and walk around in them.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Essay plan
*Student:* Plan an essay tracing one theme (empathy, courage, or justice) across the novel.

*How to teach:* Plan a theme essay (empathy, courage, or justice).

- **Q:** Give three moments that trace the empathy theme.
  **A:** Atticus's 'climb into his skin' lesson (Ch. 3), Scout talking down the mob (Ch. 15), standing on Boo's porch (Ch. 31).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft and revise
*Student:* Draft and revise the 900-word theme essay with at least six quotations.

*How to teach:* Draft and revise the 900-word essay with at least six quotations.

- **Q:** Why does Scout agree with Heck Tate's decision?
  **A:** She says exposing Boo would be 'sort of like shootin' a mockingbird.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit the essay and take the Unit 7 test.

*How to teach:* Submit essay; Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Who carries Jem home after the attack?
  **A:** Boo Radley.

**Assessment:** Theme essay and Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = precise, arguable thesis; logically ordered body paragraphs each with a topic sentence, embedded quotations cited correctly, and commentary that explains significance; effective transitions; conclusion extends the idea; few errors. 3 = clear thesis, adequate evidence and explanation, some summary, minor errors. 2 = vague thesis, evidence thin or poorly explained, organization uneven. 1 = no clear thesis, little evidence, errors interfere. Unit 7 test sample answers: narrator perspective; Scottsboro connection; knot-hole gifts; Atticus's definition of courage; mockingbird meaning; Scout and the mob; Tom's arm and the left-handed evidence; verdict; Underwood's editorial; Miss Gates; Boo saves the children; 'Hey, Boo'; porch scene.

## Algebra I: Completing the square and the quadratic formula

**Prepare:** Algebra tiles for completing the square. A derivation sheet outline.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to divide by a before completing the square.
- Writing the quadratic formula with −b only over 2a or ± only on b: the whole numerator −b ± √(b² − 4ac) is over 2a.
- Sign errors when b or c is negative: substitute with parentheses.

### Day 1 (Monday): Completing the square
*Student:* Solve 10 equations by completing the square with tiles, then symbolically.

*How to teach:* With tiles, arrange x² + 6x into a square missing a corner; add 9 to complete it. Then symbolically.

- **Q:** Solve x² + 6x = 7 by completing the square.
  **A:** (x + 3)² = 16 → x = 1 or −7.
- **Q:** Solve x² − 4x − 1 = 0 by completing the square.
  **A:** (x − 2)² = 5 → x = 2 ± √5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Deriving the formula
*Student:* Complete the square on ax²+bx+c=0 to derive the quadratic formula.

*How to teach:* Walk through deriving x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/(2a): divide by a, move c/a, add (b/2a)², factor, square-root, isolate x. Your child writes each step with a reason.

- **Q:** In the derivation, what term is added to both sides of x² + (b/a)x = −c/a?
  **A:** (b/2a)² = b²/(4a²).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Using the formula
*Student:* Solve 15 equations with the quadratic formula, giving exact and decimal answers.

*How to teach:* Model identifying a, b, c then substituting. Give exact and decimal answers.

- **Q:** Solve 2x² − 4x − 3 = 0.
  **A:** x = (4 ± √40)/4 = (2 ± √10)/2 ≈ 2.58 or −0.58.
- **Q:** Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0 using the formula.
  **A:** x = (−5 ± 1)/2 → −2 or −3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Discriminant
*Student:* Use b²−4ac to predict the number of real solutions and explain why some solutions are irrational.

*How to teach:* Discriminant: positive → 2 real solutions; zero → 1; negative → none. If it's not a perfect square, solutions are irrational.

- **Q:** How many real solutions for x² + 2x + 5 = 0?
  **A:** b² − 4ac = 4 − 20 = −16 < 0 → none.
- **Q:** Why are solutions of x² − 3x + 1 = 0 irrational?
  **A:** The discriminant is 5, not a perfect square, so √5 stays irrational.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on completing the square and the quadratic formula.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Discriminant of x² − 6x + 9?
  **A:** 0 → one real solution (x = 3).

**Assessment:** Quadratic formula quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Complete the square: x² + 8x = 9 → x = 1 or −9. 2) x² − 2x − 4 = 0 → x = 1 ± √5. 3) Formula: 3x² + 2x − 1 = 0 → x = 1/3 or −1. 4) x² − 4x + 1 = 0 → x = 2 ± √3 ≈ 3.73, 0.27. 5) Discriminant of 2x² + 3x + 5 → −31, no real solutions. 6) 4x² − 4x + 1 → 0, one solution x = 1/2.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Climate change and Earth systems

**Prepare:** Two identical jars, two thermometers, plastic wrap, a lamp (or sunlight), timer. En-ROADS climate simulator (en-roads.climateinteractive.org). Reading on coral bleaching and Sierra snowpack.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing weather and climate: weather is short-term; climate is long-term averages.
- Thinking the greenhouse effect is bad in itself: the natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth habitable; the problem is its enhancement by added greenhouse gases.
- Mixing up the ozone hole with climate change: they are different problems.

### Day 1 (Monday): Greenhouse jar lab
*Student:* Compare temperature in covered and uncovered jars under a lamp.

*How to teach:* Measure temperature in covered vs. uncovered jars every 2 minutes for 20 minutes.

- **Q:** Predict which jar warms more and why.
  **A:** The covered jar — the plastic traps warm air (a partial model of greenhouse gases trapping heat).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Climate models
*Student:* Use an online climate simulator (e.g., En-ROADS) to test emissions scenarios.

*How to teach:* Use En-ROADS to test scenarios.

- **Q:** Which En-ROADS changes reduce projected warming most?
  **A:** Large changes such as carbon pricing and reducing coal/oil use, along with energy efficiency and renewables (answers vary; the key is reducing emissions).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ecosystem effects
*Student:* Analyze effects of warming on coral reefs and Sierra Nevada snowpack.

*How to teach:* Analyze coral reefs and Sierra snowpack.

- **Q:** What causes coral bleaching?
  **A:** Warm water stresses corals, which expel their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae), turning white and starving.
- **Q:** Why does Sierra snowpack matter to California?
  **A:** It acts as natural water storage, supplying roughly 30% of the state's water as it melts; warming causes less snow and earlier melt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ecosystem change
*Student:* Evaluate claims about ecosystems shifting to new states (e.g., kelp to urchin barrens).

*How to teach:* Evaluate claims about kelp forests shifting to urchin barrens.

- **Q:** What caused much of Northern California's bull kelp loss after 2014?
  **A:** A marine heatwave plus sea star wasting disease, which killed sunflower sea stars (urchin predators), letting purple urchins explode and graze kelp — more than 90% loss in some areas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on climate and ecosystems.

*How to teach:* Quiz on climate and ecosystems.

- **Q:** Name two major greenhouse gases.
  **A:** Carbon dioxide and methane (also water vapor, nitrous oxide).

**Assessment:** Greenhouse lab report and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Quiz answers: 1) Greenhouse effect. 2) CO₂ and methane sources. 3) Coral bleaching. 4) Snowpack and water supply. 5) Kelp-urchin shift. 6) Weather vs. climate.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Japanese American incarceration

**Prepare:** Executive Order 9066 (National Archives); Dorothea Lange's Manzanar photos (Library of Congress/National Archives); Densho (densho.org) for Korematsu and redress; Civil Liberties Act of 1988.

**Watch for:**
- Calling it 'internment' only: about two-thirds of the roughly 120,000 people removed were U.S. citizens; many historians use 'incarceration.'
- Believing there was evidence of disloyalty: government reports found none; the 1983 court ruling found evidence had been suppressed.
- Thinking Korematsu was overturned in 1983: his conviction was vacated in 1983; the Supreme Court repudiated the decision in 2018 (Trump v. Hawaii).

### Day 1 (Monday): Executive Order 9066
*Student:* Read Executive Order 9066 and identify words that hide its effect (e.g., "military areas"); note who it targeted without naming them.

*How to teach:* Read EO 9066; identify euphemisms.

- **Q:** When did President Roosevelt sign Executive Order 9066, and what did it authorize?
  **A:** February 19, 1942; it authorized the military to designate 'military areas' from which 'any or all persons may be excluded' — used to remove Japanese Americans from the West Coast.
- **Q:** Does EO 9066 name Japanese Americans?
  **A:** No — it uses neutral language, though it was used to target them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lange photos
*Student:* Analyze three Dorothea Lange photos of Manzanar and the government's decision to impound many of them.

*How to teach:* Analyze three Lange photos.

- **Q:** Why did the Army impound many of Lange's photos?
  **A:** They showed the dignity of Japanese Americans and the injustice and harsh conditions of removal, which contradicted government messaging.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Korematsu
*Student:* Read about Fred Korematsu's case and the 1983 reversal.

*How to teach:* Read about Korematsu.

- **Q:** What happened in Korematsu v. United States (1944) and in 1983?
  **A:** The Supreme Court upheld Fred Korematsu's conviction for defying removal (6–3); in 1983 a federal court vacated his conviction after finding the government suppressed evidence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Redress
*Student:* Analyze the Civil Liberties Act of 1988 and the apology.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Civil Liberties Act of 1988.

- **Q:** What did the Civil Liberties Act of 1988 provide?
  **A:** A formal apology and $20,000 to each surviving incarcerated person; it said the causes were 'race prejudice, war hysteria, and a failure of political leadership.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an argument on the lessons of incarceration.

*How to teach:* Argument on lessons of incarceration.

- **Q:** Where was Manzanar located?
  **A:** In the Owens Valley of eastern California.

**Assessment:** Argument paragraph.

**Answer key:** Argument paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim about a lesson (e.g., civil liberties must be protected in crises), evidence from EO 9066, Lange photos, Korematsu, and the 1988 Act, reasoning connecting to today; 3 = claim and two pieces of evidence; 2 = one; 1 = none.

## Spanish II: Comparing art: comparatives and superlatives

**Prepare:** Bookmark the Museo del Prado online collection: 'Las Meninas' (Velázquez, 1656), Goya's 'El 3 de mayo de 1808' and 'El quitasol'. Find Picasso's 'Las Meninas' series (1957, Museu Picasso, Barcelona). Chart comparatives and irregular forms.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'más bueno' or 'más malo' for quality: use mejor/peor.
- Using 'que' with tan (*tan alto que): equality uses tan + adjective + como.
- Using 'en' in superlatives (*el más alto en la familia): Spanish uses de — el más alto de la familia.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparatives
*Student:* Learn comparisons of inequality and equality and write 10 comparing family members.

*How to teach:* Teach más/menos + adjective + que and tan + adjective + como; your child writes 10 sentences comparing family members.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My sister is as tall as my mother.'
  **A:** Mi hermana es tan alta como mi mamá.
- **Q:** Translate: 'I am less patient than my father.'
  **A:** Soy menos paciente que mi papá.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Superlatives
*Student:* Learn superlatives and irregular forms (mejor, peor, mayor, menor).

*How to teach:* Teach el/la/los/las + más + adjective + de and irregular mejor, peor, mayor, menor.

- **Q:** Translate: 'She is the best student in the class.'
  **A:** Es la mejor estudiante de la clase.
- **Q:** Translate: 'This is the worst movie of the year.'
  **A:** Es la peor película del año.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prado tour
*Student:* Take the Museo del Prado's online tour and choose works by Velázquez and Goya.

*How to teach:* Take the Prado online tour; your child chooses one Velázquez and one Goya work and notes title, date, and description.

- **Q:** Who painted 'Las Meninas' and whom does it show?
  **A:** Diego Velázquez (1656); it shows the young Infanta Margarita with her maids of honor (las meninas), Velázquez himself painting, and the king and queen reflected in a mirror.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare art
*Student:* Compare "Las Meninas" with Picasso's versions in a written paragraph using comparatives.

*How to teach:* Your child compares Velázquez's 'Las Meninas' with one of Picasso's 1957 versions using at least six comparatives.

- **Q:** Write: 'Picasso's version is more abstract than Velázquez's.'
  **A:** La versión de Picasso es más abstracta que la de Velázquez.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Comparatives quiz and a partner conversation ranking three paintings.

*How to teach:* Give the comparatives quiz, then rank three paintings in conversation (Para mí, el mejor es... porque...).

- **Q:** Translate: 'It is the most famous painting in the museum.'
  **A:** Es el cuadro más famoso del museo.

**Assessment:** Comparatives quiz and conversation.

**Answer key:** Comparatives quiz sample key: 1) Mi hermano es más alto que yo 2) La pintura es tan bonita como el mural 3) Tengo tantos libros como tú 4) Este cuadro es el más famoso del museo 5) Goya es mejor que... (opinion; check structure) 6) mi hermana mayor = my older sister. Conversation rubric: 4 = ranks three paintings with 5+ correct comparatives/superlatives and reasons; 3 = minor errors; 2 = few comparisons; 1 = minimal.

## Visual Art I: Contemporary art and finding your theme

**Prepare:** Smithsonian Learning Lab access; sketchbook. Artists: Kara Walker (cut-paper silhouettes on slavery and race), El Anatsui (metal bottle-cap tapestries), Ai Weiwei (art and activism), Judy Baca ('The Great Wall of Los Angeles' mural).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a theme too broad ('life'). Correct: narrow to a specific idea you can show in three pieces.
- A profile that only lists biography. Correct: analyze themes, media and specific works.

### Day 1 (Monday): Contemporary artists
*Student:* Explore work by Kara Walker, El Anatsui, Ai Weiwei, and Judy Baca and note their themes.

*How to teach:* Explore the four artists; note themes.

- **Q:** What is El Anatsui known for?
  **A:** Shimmering, flexible wall hangings made of flattened bottle caps and aluminum joined with copper wire, addressing consumption, trade and history.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Artist research
*Student:* Research one artist in depth and write a 400-word profile with images.

*How to teach:* Write a 400-word profile with images.

- **Q:** What is Judy Baca's 'Great Wall of Los Angeles'?
  **A:** A half-mile-long mural in the Tujunga Wash painted with hundreds of youth beginning in 1976, depicting California history from the perspective of diverse communities.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mind map
*Student:* Make a mind map of personal themes (family, place, nature, technology).

*How to teach:* Mind map of personal themes.

- **Q:** Why make a mind map?
  **A:** It generates connected ideas and helps narrow a theme.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Proposal
*Student:* Write a proposal for a 3-piece body of work with media and sketches.

*How to teach:* Write a 3-piece proposal with media and sketches.

- **Q:** What makes a 'body of work'?
  **A:** A series of related pieces exploring one theme with consistent and varied approaches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Conference on the proposal and revise.

*How to teach:* Conference; revise.

- **Q:** What questions should the conference ask?

**Assessment:** Artist profile and approved proposal.

**Answer key:** Artist profile and proposal: profile 4 = ~400 words, analyzes themes, media and at least two specific works with images and sources; proposal 4 = focused theme, three distinct planned pieces, media, sketches and timeline, revised after conference; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Chronic diseases and personal health care

**Prepare:** CDC fact sheets on heart disease, type 2 diabetes and cancer; family health history form; screening chart template (USPSTF/ACS guidance).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking screenings are only for when you feel sick. Correct: screenings find problems early before symptoms.
- Believing sunscreen is only needed on sunny days. Correct: UV passes through clouds; use SPF 30+ daily outdoors.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chronic diseases
*Student:* Research heart disease, type 2 diabetes and cancer risk factors.

*How to teach:* Research risk factors: heart disease (smoking, high BP, high cholesterol, inactivity), diabetes (excess weight, inactivity, family history), cancer (tobacco, UV, alcohol, HPV).

- **Q:** Name two controllable risk factors for heart disease.
  **A:** Smoking, inactivity, unhealthy diet high in saturated fat and sodium, excess weight, uncontrolled blood pressure.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family history
*Student:* Interview a family member to build a family health history.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member and record the history.

- **Q:** Why does family history matter?
  **A:** Some conditions have genetic risk; knowing it guides earlier screening and prevention.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prevention
*Student:* Make a chart of recommended screenings (blood pressure, cholesterol, skin checks, Pap/HPV, testicular self-exam) and the lifestyle habits that lower chronic-disease risk.

*How to teach:* Chart screenings and habits: blood pressure (yearly checkups), cholesterol (once between ages 9–11 and again 17–21 per AAP), skin checks, cervical screening starting at 21, testicular self-awareness.

- **Q:** At what age does routine cervical cancer screening begin?
  **A:** Age 21 (Pap test every 3 years for ages 21–29).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Personal care
*Student:* Learn about dental care, vision, hearing protection and sun safety.

*How to teach:* Learn dental care (brush twice, floss daily, checkups), vision checks, hearing protection (sounds above 85 dB), sun safety.

- **Q:** What volume can damage hearing over time?
  **A:** Sounds at or above about 85 decibels with long exposure; keep earbuds below 60% volume.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a personal chronic-disease prevention plan using your family history, with three habits to start now and screenings to ask about later.

*How to teach:* Child writes the prevention plan.

- **Q:** What three habits lower chronic disease risk?
  **A:** Regular activity, a balanced diet, no tobacco (also sleep, limiting alcohol, sun protection).

**Assessment:** Personal prevention plan.

**Answer key:** Prevention plan: 4 = uses family history, names specific risks, three habits to start now with how to track, and age-appropriate future screenings; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = generic; 1 = incomplete.
