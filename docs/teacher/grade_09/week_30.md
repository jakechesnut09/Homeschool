# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 30

## English 9: The trial: Chapters 12–19

**Prepare:** TKAM Chapters 12–19. Evidence chart for the trial (witness, claim, evidence against). Preview Chapters 17–19 for mature trial content.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the trial evidence is ambiguous: the text shows Mayella's injuries were on her right side, likely from a left-handed attacker; Bob Ewell is left-handed and Tom's left arm is crippled.
- Missing why Tom's 'I felt sorry for her' hurts him: in 1930s Maycomb, a Black man pitying a white woman was seen as overstepping.
- Seeing the mob scene as luck: Scout's innocent talk reminds Mr. Cunningham of his humanity — empathy works.

### Day 1 (Monday): Calpurnia's church
*Student:* Read Chapters 12–13; compare Calpurnia's two worlds and Aunt Alexandra's ideas of family.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 12–13; compare Calpurnia's two worlds and Aunt Alexandra.

- **Q:** Why does Calpurnia speak differently at First Purchase church?
  **A:** She talks like her community there; she says it's not necessary to show off to people who would think she was 'puttin' on airs.'
- **Q:** What are Aunt Alexandra's ideas of family?
  **A:** She stresses 'fine folks' and family heritage/background, judging people by lineage.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The mob
*Student:* Read Chapters 14–15; analyze how Scout disperses the mob outside the jail.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 14–15; analyze how Scout disperses the mob.

- **Q:** How does Scout break up the mob at the jail?
  **A:** She innocently talks to Mr. Cunningham about his entailment and his son Walter, making him see the situation as a father and neighbor; he tells the men to leave.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Testimony
*Student:* Read Chapters 16–18; record each witness's claims and the evidence against them.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 16–18; record witness claims and contradicting evidence.

- **Q:** What does Atticus establish about Bob Ewell's handedness and why does it matter?
  **A:** Ewell is left-handed; Mayella's injuries were mostly on the right side of her face, consistent with a left-handed attacker.
- **Q:** What important fact about medical care comes out?
  **A:** No one called a doctor for Mayella.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tom's testimony
*Student:* Read Chapter 19; analyze the moment Tom says he felt sorry for Mayella.

*How to teach:* Read Chapter 19; analyze 'I felt sorry for her.'

- **Q:** Why can't Tom have hit Mayella as described?
  **A:** His left arm was crippled in a cotton gin accident; he couldn't have choked and beaten her the way she claimed.
- **Q:** Why does Tom's statement 'I felt right sorry for her' damage him with the jury?
  **A:** The white jury sees a Black man pitying a white woman as insulting his 'place' in the social order.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a mock closing argument for the defense using trial evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a mock closing argument for the defense.

- **Q:** What is the strongest physical evidence for the defense?
  **A:** Tom's useless left arm versus injuries on the right side of Mayella's face, plus no doctor called.

**Assessment:** Mock closing argument and evidence chart.

**Answer key:** Mock closing rubric: 4 = cites at least three pieces of trial evidence (left-handedness, right-side injuries, Tom's arm, no doctor, inconsistencies in testimony), addresses the jury persuasively with appeals, logically organized; 3 = two pieces of evidence; 2 = mostly emotional; 1 = inaccurate. Evidence chart: Heck Tate — right eye injury, no doctor; Bob Ewell — left-handed; Mayella — inconsistent, avoids questions; Tom — crippled left arm, helped Mayella with chores.

## Algebra I: Quadratic modeling: projectiles and area

**Prepare:** A soft ball, stopwatch or phone video (slow-motion helps), open outdoor space. Desmos. Model: h(t) = −16t² + v₀t + h₀ (feet, seconds).

**Watch for:**
- Treating the parabola as the ball's path sideways: h(t) graphs height versus TIME for a straight-up toss.
- Using the whole parabola as the domain: time starts at 0 and ends when the ball lands.
- Confusing when the max occurs (t-value) with the max height (h-value).

### Day 1 (Monday): Projectile lab
*Student:* Toss a ball straight up, time the flight, and use h(t)=−16t²+v₀t+h₀ to estimate initial velocity.

*How to teach:* Toss the ball straight up and catch it at the same height; time several flights. If thrown and caught at the same height, 0 = −16T² + v₀T, so v₀ = 16T. Your child estimates v₀.

- **Q:** A ball is in the air 2.5 s before being caught at release height. Estimate v₀.
  **A:** v₀ = 16 × 2.5 = 40 ft/s.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Max height
*Student:* Find maximum height and when it occurs for several launch scenarios.

*How to teach:* Find the vertex: t = −b/(2a). Your child does several scenarios.

- **Q:** For h = −16t² + 64t + 5, when is the max and what is it?
  **A:** t = 64/32 = 2 s; h(2) = −64 + 128 + 5 = 69 ft.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Area models
*Student:* Maximize the area of a garden with 40 ft of fencing using a quadratic function.

*How to teach:* Model fencing: 40 ft perimeter, width x, length 20 − x, A = x(20 − x). Find the vertex.

- **Q:** What dimensions maximize area with 40 ft of fencing? Max area?
  **A:** 10 ft by 10 ft; 100 ft².

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpreting
*Student:* Answer context questions about domain, range, and intercepts for projectile models.

*How to teach:* Interpret intercepts and reasonable domain/range in context.

- **Q:** For h = −16t² + 64t + 5, what does 5 mean and when does the ball land?
  **A:** 5 ft = launch height. Lands when h = 0: t = (64 + √4416)/32 ≈ 4.08 s.
- **Q:** Reasonable domain and range for that model?
  **A:** Domain 0 ≤ t ≤ about 4.08 s; range 0 ≤ h ≤ 69 ft.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on quadratic functions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test (about 50 minutes) and collect the lab write-up.

- **Q:** What does the vertex of a projectile model represent?
  **A:** The maximum height and the time it occurs.

**Assessment:** Lab write-up and Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Lab rubric: 4 = several timed trials, average flight time, correct v₀ = 16T, max height computed (v₀²/64 above release), sources of error; 3 = minor errors; 2 = incomplete; 1 = missing. Unit 9 test sample answers: 1) y = x² − 4x − 5: vertex (2, −9), zeros −1 and 5. 2) y = −(x + 2)² + 3 → standard −x² − 4x − 1. 3) x² + 4x + 1 = (x + 2)² − 3. 4) h = −16t² + 32t + 6: max at t = 1, height 22 ft. 5) Max area with 60 ft of fence → 15 × 15 = 225 ft².

## Biology – The Living Earth: Nutrition, enzymes, and Unit 5 test

**Prepare:** 3% hydrogen peroxide, raw potato cubes (or liver), test tubes or small cups, ruler, thermometer; ice water, room temperature, and boiled potato. Goggles. Prepare the Unit 5 review and test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking enzymes are used up: enzymes are reusable catalysts.
- Believing 'hotter is always faster': past the optimum, enzymes denature and stop working.
- Confusing denaturing with killing: denaturing is a change in a protein's shape that destroys its active site.

### Day 1 (Monday): Enzymes
*Student:* Model enzyme-substrate specificity with a lock-and-key drawing.

*How to teach:* Draw lock-and-key and induced fit.

- **Q:** Why is each enzyme specific to its substrate?
  **A:** The active site's shape fits only certain substrates.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Catalase lab
*Student:* Test catalase in potato with hydrogen peroxide at three temperatures and measure foam height.

*How to teach:* Test catalase at three temperatures; measure foam height.

- **Q:** What reaction does catalase speed up?
  **A:** 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (the bubbles are oxygen).
- **Q:** Predict foam heights for cold, room-temperature, and boiled potato.
  **A:** Cold: moderate/less; room/warm: most; boiled: little or none.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Enzyme data
*Student:* Graph foam height vs. temperature for the catalase lab and explain the drop at high temperature as denaturation.

*How to teach:* Graph and explain.

- **Q:** Why does boiled potato produce almost no foam?
  **A:** High heat denatured the catalase, changing the active site's shape so it can't bind H₂O₂.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit review
*Student:* Complete a Unit 5 review guide covering cells, transport, body systems, homeostasis, and enzymes.

*How to teach:* Unit 5 review guide.

- **Q:** Name the four levels of organization from cell to system.
  **A:** Cell, tissue, organ, organ system.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** What does a catalyst do?
  **A:** It speeds up a reaction by lowering activation energy without being used up.

**Assessment:** Catalase lab and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Unit 5 test sample answers: cell theory; organelle functions; prokaryote vs. eukaryote; osmosis predictions; active transport; heart blood path; feedback loops (insulin/glucagon); positive vs. negative feedback; differentiation; enzymes and denaturation. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Education rights and Chicano movement

**Prepare:** Mendez v. Westminster ruling excerpt (1946); 1968 East LA walkout demands; images of Judith Baca's Great Wall of Los Angeles (SPARC website); data on Latino diversity in California. Prepare the Unit 10 assessment.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Brown v. Board was the first school desegregation case: Mendez v. Westminster (1946) ended segregation of Mexican American students in Orange County schools years earlier.
- Treating 'Latino' as one culture: Latinos in California include people of Mexican, Salvadoran, Guatemalan, Puerto Rican, Cuban, and many other origins, with Indigenous and Afro-Latino identities.
- Seeing murals only as decoration: Chicano murals tell community histories and make political statements.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mendez v. Westminster
*Student:* Read the 1946 ruling and connect to Brown v. Board.

*How to teach:* Read the Mendez ruling; connect to Brown.

- **Q:** What did Mendez v. Westminster (1946) decide?
  **A:** A federal court ruled that segregating Mexican American children into separate schools violated the 14th Amendment; it was upheld on appeal in 1947.
- **Q:** How is Mendez connected to Brown v. Board?
  **A:** Thurgood Marshall filed a brief in the Mendez appeal, and Governor Earl Warren signed a 1947 law ending school segregation in California — Warren later wrote the Brown decision.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Walkouts
*Student:* Analyze the 1968 East LA walkouts and students' demands.

*How to teach:* Analyze the 1968 East LA walkouts.

- **Q:** What did students demand in the 1968 East LA walkouts?
  **A:** Bilingual/bicultural education, Mexican American history, more Mexican American teachers and administrators, smaller classes, better facilities, and an end to punishment for speaking Spanish.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chicano art
*Student:* Analyze Chicano murals, such as Judith Baca's Great Wall of Los Angeles.

*How to teach:* Analyze Chicano murals.

- **Q:** What does Judith Baca's Great Wall of Los Angeles depict?
  **A:** A half-mile-long mural in the Tujunga Wash showing California history from the perspectives of people of color, women, and immigrants.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Latino diversity
*Student:* Research the diversity of Latino communities in California.

*How to teach:* Research Latino diversity in California.

- **Q:** What is the difference between Hispanic and Latino?
  **A:** Hispanic refers to Spanish-speaking origin (includes Spain); Latino refers to Latin American origin (includes Brazil, not Spain).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 assessment
*Student:* Complete a unit assessment: key terms, a source analysis of Mendez v. Westminster, and a short essay on student activism.

*How to teach:* Unit 10 assessment.

- **Q:** Who was Sal Castro?
  **A:** A Lincoln High School teacher who supported the 1968 walkouts.

**Assessment:** Unit 10 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 10 assessment answers: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo; Land Act of 1851; Bracero Program; Delano strike (Itliong, Chavez, Huerta); Plan de Delano; grape boycott; Mendez v. Westminster; East LA walkouts; Great Wall of LA. Source analysis of Mendez scored with the source rubric; short essay on student activism scored with the paragraph rubric. Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.

## Spanish II: Unit 7 project: environmental awareness campaign

**Prepare:** Gather poster board or a video app. Help your child pick a local problem (plastic on beaches, water waste, litter in parks). Write the Unit 7 test (community, environment, subjunctive with recommendations and ojalá).

**Watch for:**
- Writing slogans as literal translations: keep them short and catchy in Spanish (¡Cuida el agua, cuida la vida!).
- Mixing tú and usted commands on one poster: choose one register for the campaign.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a problem
*Student:* Choose a local environmental problem (e.g., plastic on beaches, water waste) and list facts in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a problem and lists 5–8 facts in Spanish from reliable sources.

- **Q:** Write a fact: 'Every year, millions of tons of plastic end up in the ocean.'
  **A:** Cada año, millones de toneladas de plástico terminan en el océano.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slogans and recommendations
*Student:* Write three slogans and eight recommendations using formal commands and the subjunctive.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 3 slogans and 8 recommendations (Recoja su basura. Es necesario que usemos botellas reutilizables.).

- **Q:** Write a usted command: 'Use reusable bags.'
  **A:** Use bolsas reutilizables.
- **Q:** Write a subjunctive recommendation: 'It's better that people walk.'
  **A:** Es mejor que la gente camine.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Design a bilingual poster or 60-second video campaign.

*How to teach:* Your child designs the bilingual poster or 60-second video; check Spanish accuracy before finalizing.

- **Q:** Create a slogan about water.
  **A:** Example: ¡Cada gota cuenta! / ¡Cuida el agua, cuida la vida!

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the campaign and answer two questions in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the campaign and answers two questions in Spanish (¿Qué puede hacer una familia? ¿Por qué es importante?).

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Qué puede hacer una familia?'
  **A:** Example: Una familia puede reciclar, usar menos plástico y tomar duchas más cortas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on community, environment, and the subjunctive.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Fill in: Ojalá que mi comunidad ___ (tener) más árboles.
  **A:** tenga

**Assessment:** Campaign rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** Campaign rubric: 4 = accurate facts, 3 slogans, 8 recommendations using formal commands and subjunctive correctly, clear visuals, answers 2 questions; 3 = minor errors; 2 = fewer recommendations or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Unit test sample: 1) Es importante que (nosotros) apaguemos las luces 2) Ojalá que no haya contaminación 3) la sequía = drought 4) Recicle las botellas (usted command).

## Visual Art I: Collage and mixed media

**Prepare:** Magazines, photocopies of old photos (not originals), glue, acrylic paint. Images: Romare Bearden's collages of Harlem life (e.g., 'The Dove,' 1964) and Hannah Höch's photomontage ('Cut with the Kitchen Knife,' 1919–20).

**Watch for:**
- Gluing too early. Correct: experiment with arrangements and photograph options before gluing.
- Collages that feel random. Correct: use a theme and unify with color, paint or repeated shapes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Romare Bearden
*Student:* Study Bearden's collages of Harlem life and Hannah Höch's photomontage.

*How to teach:* Study Bearden and Höch.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gather
*Student:* Gather images, textures, and papers around a theme (home, memory, the future).

*How to teach:* Gather images around a theme.

- **Q:** What is photomontage?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compose
*Student:* Experiment with arrangements without gluing; photograph three options.

*How to teach:* Experiment; photograph three options.

- **Q:** Why photograph options?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build
*Student:* Glue down and add paint and drawing to unify the collage.

*How to teach:* Glue down; unify with paint and drawing.

- **Q:** How can you unify a collage?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check

*How to teach:* Interpret a sibling's collage, then your own.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Disc golf fundamentals

**Prepare:** Discs, a local disc golf course or targets (hoop/trash can). Note: hyzer = outer edge tilted down; anhyzer = outer edge tilted up.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing with only the arm. Correct: reach back, pull across the chest with the body, snap the wrist.
- Confusing hyzer and anhyzer. Correct: for a right-hand backhand throw, hyzer curves left and anhyzer curves right.

### Day 1 (Monday): Backhand throw
*Student:* Learn backhand grip, reach-back, and follow-through; throw 30 discs at a target.

*How to teach:* Backhand grip, reach-back, follow-through; 30 throws at a target.

- **Q:** Describe a power grip for backhand.
  **A:** Four fingers curled under the rim, thumb on top of the flight plate.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forehand throw
*Student:* Learn the forehand (sidearm) throw; throw 30 discs.

*How to teach:* Forehand (sidearm) throw; 30 throws.

- **Q:** Describe the forehand grip.
  **A:** Index and middle fingers under the rim against the inside edge, thumb on top, like a sidearm throw.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Putting
*Student:* Practice putting from 10, 15, and 20 feet into a basket or target.

*How to teach:* Putting from 10, 15, 20 ft.

- **Q:** Why is putting important?
  **A:** Most strokes are saved or lost on short throws to the basket.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Flight angles
*Student:* Experiment with hyzer and anhyzer release angles and record flight paths.

*How to teach:* Experiment with hyzer and anhyzer.

- **Q:** For a right-handed backhand, which way does a hyzer release curve?
  **A:** To the left.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Skills test: accuracy throws and putting percentage.

*How to teach:* Skills test: accuracy throws and putting percentage.

- **Q:** You made 14 of 20 putts. Percentage?
  **A:** 14 ÷ 20 = 70%.

**Assessment:** Disc golf skills test.

**Answer key:** Skills test: accuracy throws (e.g., 10 throws to land within 10 ft of a target at 50 ft: 5+ proficient), putting percentage from 15 ft (60%+ proficient), plus correct description of hyzer/anhyzer flight.

## Health & Wellness: Basic first aid and Hands-Only CPR

**Prepare:** First aid kit, pillow or manikin, AED demo video, CPR checklist and the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Removing a cloth soaked with blood. Correct: add more layers on top and keep pressure.
- Putting ice or butter on burns. Correct: cool with cool running water for 10–20 minutes; cover loosely.

### Day 1 (Monday): Emergency steps
*Student:* Learn the Check–Call–Care steps and practice a mock 911 call giving location, situation and number of victims.

*How to teach:* Teach Check–Call–Care; practice a 911 call.

- **Q:** What should you give a dispatcher first?
  **A:** Your exact location, then what happened and the number of victims; stay on the line.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bleeding and burns
*Student:* Practice treating cuts and burns using a first aid kit.

*How to teach:* Practice cuts (gloves, direct pressure, bandage) and burns (cool running water, cover, don't pop blisters).

- **Q:** How do you stop severe bleeding?
  **A:** Gloves if possible, firm direct pressure with gauze, add more layers if soaked, call 911; a tourniquet for life-threatening limb bleeding.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hands-Only CPR
*Student:* Practice Hands-Only CPR on a pillow to the beat of 'Stayin' Alive'.

*How to teach:* Practice Hands-Only CPR to 'Stayin' Alive' (100–120/min, at least 2 inches).

- **Q:** Where do you place your hands?
  **A:** Heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), other hand on top.

### Day 4 (Thursday): AED
*Student:* Watch an AED demonstration video, then sequence the AED steps on cards and talk through pad placement on a diagram.

*How to teach:* Watch AED video; sequence cards; pad placement: upper right chest below collarbone, lower left side below armpit.

- **Q:** What is the first AED step?
  **A:** Turn it on and follow the voice prompts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test and perform a 2-minute Hands-Only CPR skills check for a parent with a checklist.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test and 2-minute CPR check.

- **Q:** When should you stop CPR?
  **A:** When EMS or an AED takes over, the person shows signs of life, the scene becomes unsafe, or you are too exhausted.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test and CPR skills check.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test key: leading teen causes of death; firearm storage; drowning signs; concussion law AB 2127; cycle of abuse; de-escalation; digital footprint; sextortion steps; Drop-Cover-Hold On; AQI levels; go-bag; Check-Call-Care; bleeding control; burn care (cool water 10–20 min); CPR rate/depth; AED pad placement. CPR checklist: scene safety, responsiveness, 911/AED, hand placement, 100–120/min, 2 inches, full recoil, minimal pauses (7–8 of 8 proficient).
