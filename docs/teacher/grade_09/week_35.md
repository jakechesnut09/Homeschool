# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 35

## English 9: Revision and multimedia presentation

**Prepare:** Research draft; slide software; a revision checklist (thesis, synthesis, transitions, citations, MLA format).

**Watch for:**
- Revision = proofreading: revise ideas, structure, and evidence first; edit sentences last.
- Slides crowded with text: use images, a chart, and key words; say the details aloud.
- Colon after a verb or semicolon before a list (see Weeks 5–6).

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise
*Student:* Revise the paper using a teacher conference and a checklist for synthesis and transitions.

*How to teach:* Conference on the draft; your child revises for synthesis and transitions.

- **Q:** Name two transitions that show contrast.
  **A:** However, on the other hand (also nevertheless, in contrast).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Edit
*Student:* Edit for MLA format, parallel structure, and semicolon/colon use.

*How to teach:* Edit for MLA format, parallel structure, and semicolons/colons.

- **Q:** Correct: 'The study found three causes; poor sleep, stress, and diet.'
  **A:** Use a colon: 'The study found three causes: poor sleep, stress, and diet.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentation design
*Student:* Build a 6–8 slide presentation with images, a chart, and minimal text.

*How to teach:* Build a 6–8 slide presentation with images, a chart, and minimal text.

- **Q:** What is a good rule for text on slides?
  **A:** A few key words or a short phrase per point — the speaker explains the rest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Speaker notes
*Student:* Write speaker notes and rehearse a 6-minute talk.

*How to teach:* Write speaker notes; rehearse a 6-minute talk.

- **Q:** What should speaker notes contain?
  **A:** Key points, transitions, and data to mention — not a full script to read word for word.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit the final research paper with Works Cited and a short reflection on your revision choices.

*How to teach:* Submit the final paper and a reflection on revision choices.

- **Q:** What is one thing a revision reflection should explain?
  **A:** A specific change made and why it improved the paper.

**Assessment:** Final research paper (W.9-10.7 rubric).

**Answer key:** Final research paper rubric (W.9-10.7): 4 = focused question answered with a clear thesis; synthesis of 6+ credible sources; accurate paraphrase/quotation with MLA citations; logical organization and transitions; correct Works Cited; few errors. 3 = adequate with minor issues; 2 = summary-heavy, weak citation; 1 = incomplete or plagiarized content (rewrite required).

## Algebra I: Absolute value, piecewise, and square root functions

**Prepare:** Graph paper, Desmos. Shipping chart example: $5 for up to 2 lb, $8 for over 2 up to 5 lb, $12 over 5 lb. Function-family cards for the sort (linear, quadratic, exponential, absolute value, square root, piecewise).

**Watch for:**
- Shifting y = |x − 3| left: like parabolas, it shifts RIGHT 3.
- Overlapping pieces in piecewise functions: each input belongs to exactly one piece — watch ≤ vs <.
- Stating domain of √(x − 2) as all reals: the radicand must be ≥ 0, so x ≥ 2.

### Day 1 (Monday): Absolute value functions
*Student:* Graph 8 functions of the form y=a|x−h|+k and describe each transformation from y=|x|.

*How to teach:* Graph y = |x|, then use a, h, k as with parabolas. Your child graphs 8 and describes transformations.

- **Q:** Describe y = 2|x − 3| + 1.
  **A:** V-shape opening up, vertex (3, 1), twice as steep as y = |x|.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Piecewise functions
*Student:* Graph piecewise functions for tax brackets and shipping costs.

*How to teach:* Graph each piece only on its interval, using open/closed dots. Evaluate inputs using the right piece.

- **Q:** Using the shipping chart, what does a 2-lb package cost? A 4.5-lb package?
  **A:** $5 (2 ≤ 2); $8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Square root functions
*Student:* Graph 6 functions of the form y=√(x−h)+k and state domain and range for each.

*How to teach:* Graph y = √x from a table (0, 1, 4, 9), then shift.

- **Q:** Domain and range of y = √(x − 2) + 3?
  **A:** Domain x ≥ 2; range y ≥ 3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing functions
*Student:* Compare function families from tables, graphs, and equations in a card sort.

*How to teach:* Sort cards by family using table patterns (constant difference, constant ratio, second differences, V shape, half-parabola).

- **Q:** A table has first differences 3, 5, 7, 9. What family?
  **A:** Quadratic (constant second differences of 2).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on other function families.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Vertex of y = −|x + 1| + 4?
  **A:** (−1, 4), opens down.

**Assessment:** Function family quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) y = |x + 2| − 3 → vertex (−2, −3), opens up. 2) y = −3|x| → opens down, steeper. 3) f(x) = {x + 1 if x < 0; 2x if x ≥ 0}: f(−3) = −2, f(0) = 0, f(4) = 8. 4) y = √(x + 4) − 1: domain x ≥ −4, range y ≥ −1. 5) Table 2, 6, 18, 54 → exponential. 6) Table 1, 4, 9, 16 → quadratic.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Evaluating solutions and presenting

**Prepare:** Presentation software; timer; invite family or co-op audience for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Presenting only benefits: credible engineering presentations discuss trade-offs and limitations.
- Ignoring stakeholders (neighbors, landowners, wildlife agencies).
- Reading slides instead of explaining data.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trade-offs
*Student:* Evaluate your solution's costs, benefits, and trade-offs.

*How to teach:* Evaluate costs, benefits, trade-offs.

- **Q:** Give a trade-off for a pollinator garden.
  **A:** Higher water use or initial cost vs. long-term biodiversity benefit; native plants may need establishment watering but less water later.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stakeholders
*Student:* Consider perspectives of stakeholders and revise.

*How to teach:* Consider stakeholders; revise.

- **Q:** Name three stakeholders for a neighborhood habitat project.
  **A:** Homeowners/neighbors, local government or HOA, wildlife agencies or conservation groups (also gardeners, beekeepers).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prepare presentation
*Student:* Prepare a 6-minute presentation with the problem, data, design, simulation results, and trade-offs.

*How to teach:* Prepare a 6-minute presentation.

- **Q:** What sections should the presentation include?
  **A:** Problem, data, design, simulation results, trade-offs, and next steps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the presentation twice with a timer and revise slides after feedback.

*How to teach:* Rehearse twice with a timer; revise.

- **Q:** How do you make data clear in a presentation?
  **A:** Use simple labeled graphs and state the key takeaway aloud.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present the solution to family or a co-op group.

*How to teach:* Present to an audience.

- **Q:** How should you respond to a question you can't answer?
  **A:** Acknowledge it, explain how you could find out, and note it as future work.

**Assessment:** Project presentation rubric.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = clearly defined problem with data, design and simulation explained, trade-offs and stakeholders addressed, visuals effective, within 6 minutes, answers questions; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing trade-offs or data; 1 = incomplete.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Community action project: research and planning

**Prepare:** Local newspaper archives; city council or school board agendas/minutes; interview questions; rubric for evidence and respectful tone.

**Watch for:**
- Treating all sources as equally credible.
- Writing an action that only complains: include evidence and a clear, specific request.
- Ignoring the other side: summarize the strongest opposing arguments fairly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Local sources
*Student:* Find at least four sources on your issue (news article, city/county data, an agency report, a meeting agenda) and annotate each for credibility and bias.

*How to teach:* Find and annotate four sources.

- **Q:** How do you check a local news source for bias?
  **A:** Check author and outlet, distinguish news from opinion, see whose voices are quoted, and compare with other sources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stakeholder interview
*Student:* Interview a stakeholder (librarian, community organizer, council staffer, or neighbor) using prepared questions.

*How to teach:* Interview a stakeholder.

- **Q:** Write one strong stakeholder interview question.
  **A:** 'What do you see as the biggest obstacle to solving this issue, and what would help?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Evaluate arguments
*Student:* Summarize the strongest arguments on each side and flag any fallacious reasoning you found.

*How to teach:* Summarize both sides; flag fallacies.

- **Q:** Name a fallacy common in public debates.
  **A:** Slippery slope, straw man, or ad hominem.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Action plan
*Student:* Choose an action (letter to an official, public comment, informational flyer, short video) and draft it with evidence and a clear request.

*How to teach:* Draft the action product.

- **Q:** What are the parts of an effective letter to an official?
  **A:** Introduction of yourself, the issue with evidence, a clear specific request, and a respectful closing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Revise the draft action after parent/teacher feedback against a rubric for evidence and respectful tone.

*How to teach:* Revise after feedback.

- **Q:** Why does respectful tone matter in civic action?
  **A:** Officials and community members are more likely to listen and act when arguments are respectful and evidence-based.

**Assessment:** Research notes and draft action product.

**Answer key:** Research notes rubric: 4 = four credible sources annotated for credibility/bias, interview notes, both sides summarized fairly. Draft action rubric: 4 = clear issue, specific evidence, specific request to the right decision-maker, respectful tone, correct format; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = vague request; 1 = incomplete.

## Spanish II: Final project: "Mi vida en español" video

**Prepare:** Print a script template with four sections (childhood, memorable event, routine, future) and a checklist of tenses and agreement. Have a phone/camera and simple video editor ready. Schedule a 20-minute script conference on day 3.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing tenses across sections: imperfect for childhood description, preterite for the event, present for routine, future for plans.
- Writing too complex sentences from English: keep to structures you know and use connectors (además, sin embargo, por eso).

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Outline a 4-minute video: your childhood (imperfect), a memorable event (preterite), your routine (present), and your future plans (future).

*How to teach:* Your child outlines the 4-minute video with key sentences and visuals for each section.

- **Q:** Write one sentence for each tense about yourself.
  **A:** Example: De niño vivía en Fresno (imperfect). En 2020 fui a México (preterite). Ahora me levanto a las siete (present). En el futuro estudiaré medicina (future).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Script
*Student:* Write the script (500+ words) and self-edit with a checklist of tenses and agreement.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 500+ word script and self-edits with the checklist, underlining each verb and labeling its tense.

- **Q:** Fix: 'El año pasado iba a Guatemala por una semana.'
  **A:** El año pasado fui a Guatemala por una semana (completed trip = preterite).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conference
*Student:* Revise the script after a teacher conference focusing on accuracy and connectors (además, sin embargo).

*How to teach:* Hold a conference: read the script aloud together, focusing on accuracy and adding connectors. Your child revises.

- **Q:** Use 'sin embargo' in a sentence.
  **A:** Example: Me gusta mi ciudad; sin embargo, quiero vivir en otro país algún día.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Record
*Student:* Record the video with visuals and subtitles in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child records the video with visuals and Spanish subtitles, re-recording any section with errors.

- **Q:** Translate: 'In ten years, I will live in a big city.'
  **A:** En diez años viviré en una ciudad grande.

### Day 5 (Friday): Premiere
*Student:* Show the video to family and answer their questions in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Hold a family premiere; family members ask questions in Spanish, and your child answers.

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Qué harás después de graduarte?'
  **A:** Example: Después de graduarme, iré a la universidad y estudiaré biología.

**Assessment:** Final video project rubric.

**Answer key:** Final video rubric (4 levels per row): Content — all four sections, ~4 minutes; Language — accurate imperfect, preterite, present, and future with 4+ connectors; Delivery — clear pronunciation, not reading; Production — visuals and Spanish subtitles. 4 = excellent on all rows; 3 = minor issues; 2 = missing a section or many errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Visual Art I: Curating an exhibition

**Prepare:** Artwork, tape or hooks, label cards; a virtual museum exhibition (Google Arts & Culture or a museum website).

**Watch for:**
- Hanging art too high or crowded. Correct: center works at about 57–60 inches from the floor with even spacing.
- Labels without information. Correct: title, medium, date and a one-sentence description.

### Day 1 (Monday): Exhibitions
*Student:* Take a virtual tour of a museum exhibition and analyze how works are grouped, labeled, and lit.

*How to teach:* Virtual tour; analyze grouping, labels, lighting.

- **Q:** What does a curator do?
  **A:** Selects, organizes, interprets and presents artworks for an exhibition.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Select
*Student:* Select 10–12 works from the year for your exhibition, including the series.

*How to teach:* Select 10–12 works including the series.

- **Q:** How should works be grouped?
  **A:** By theme, medium or chronology so the show tells a clear story.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Labels
*Student:* Write wall labels with title, medium, date, and a one-sentence description.

*How to teach:* Write wall labels.

- **Q:** What goes on a wall label?
  **A:** Artist, title, date, medium and a short description.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Install
*Student:* Install the exhibition in your home or a community space, considering flow and lighting.

*How to teach:* Install considering flow and lighting.

- **Q:** What is the standard hanging height?
  **A:** Center of artwork about 57–60 inches from the floor.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Walk through the show and adjust placement.

*How to teach:* Walk through; adjust.

- **Q:** Why walk through the show?
  **A:** To check flow, spacing, lighting and readability like a visitor.

**Assessment:** Installed exhibition.

**Answer key:** Installed exhibition: 4 = 10–12 works including the series, logically grouped, consistent hanging height and spacing, complete labels, good lighting and flow; 3 = minor issues; 2 = disorganized; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Monitoring, retesting, and adjusting the plan

**Prepare:** Activity log, stopwatch, PACER audio; earlier results from weeks 3, 8 and 28; article on intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation.

**Watch for:**
- Only using extrinsic motivators. Correct: build intrinsic motivation through enjoyment, mastery and choice.
- Leading a workout without a warm-up. Correct: always warm up and cool down.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cardio retest
*Student:* Retest the mile or PACER after a full warm-up and compare to weeks 3, 8, and 28.

*How to teach:* Retest mile or PACER; compare to weeks 3, 8 and 28.

- **Q:** What trend do your four results show?
  **A:** Answers vary; describe direction and size of change.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Overload session
*Student:* Complete your strength session with one added set or heavier resistance and log it.

*How to teach:* Strength session with one added set or heavier resistance; log.

- **Q:** What principle does adding a set demonstrate?
  **A:** Progressive overload.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Motivation
*Student:* Read about intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation and write which strategies (music, partner, tracking app) kept you active this year; then do a 30-minute choice workout.

*How to teach:* Read about motivation; write strategies; 30-minute workout.

- **Q:** Give one intrinsic and one extrinsic motivator.
  **A:** Intrinsic: enjoying how running feels. Extrinsic: earning a reward or praise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lead a family workout
*Student:* Plan and lead a 30-minute workout for your family, including warm-up and cool-down.

*How to teach:* Plan and lead a 30-minute family workout.

- **Q:** What three parts must your workout have?
  **A:** Warm-up, main workout, cool-down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a one-page evaluation of your plan: adherence percentage, results, and two adjustments.

*How to teach:* Write one-page evaluation with adherence and two adjustments.

- **Q:** You completed 14 of 16 planned sessions. Adherence?
  **A:** 14 ÷ 16 = 87.5%.

**Assessment:** Plan evaluation with data.

**Answer key:** Plan evaluation: 4 = adherence % calculated, retest results compared across four dates with trend, evidence-based assessment of what worked, two specific adjustments; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = little data; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Health advocacy project

**Prepare:** Computer, poster board; advocacy rubric (accuracy, audience, persuasive appeal, call to action); citation help.

**Watch for:**
- Picking a topic too broad. Correct: narrow to a specific problem and audience.
- Forgetting the call to action. Correct: tell the audience exactly what to do.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a topic
*Student:* Choose a teen health issue from the course (e.g., vaping, sleep, teen mental health access, wildfire smoke) and write a one-paragraph problem statement with a target audience.

*How to teach:* Child chooses a topic and writes a problem statement with audience.

- **Q:** What makes a strong problem statement?
  **A:** It names a specific issue, who it affects, evidence of the problem and why it matters.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Gather three credible sources (.gov, .edu, peer-reviewed) and record two statistics and one expert recommendation on a note sheet with citations.

*How to teach:* Gather three credible sources, two stats and one expert recommendation with citations.

- **Q:** Is a TikTok video a credible source?
  **A:** Not by itself; trace claims to .gov, .edu or peer-reviewed sources.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Build an advocacy product: a 60-second PSA video, an infographic poster, or a letter to a school board member or legislator with a clear call to action.

*How to teach:* Build the product.

- **Q:** What is a call to action?
  **A:** A specific request telling the audience what to do (e.g., 'Ask the school board to start school after 8:30').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Get feedback from a parent using the advocacy rubric (accuracy, audience, persuasive appeal, call to action) and revise the product.

*How to teach:* Parent scores with rubric; child revises.

- **Q:** Which rubric category was weakest and how did you fix it?
  **A:** Answers vary; look for a specific revision.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present
*Student:* Present the advocacy product to family or a co-op group, answer questions, and submit it with a works-cited list.

*How to teach:* Child presents and submits with works cited.

- **Q:** How do you answer a question you don't know?
  **A:** Say you'll look it up and cite a credible source later.

**Assessment:** Advocacy project rubric.

**Answer key:** Advocacy rubric (4 levels each): Accuracy — 3+ credible cited sources, correct statistics; Audience — message and format fit the audience; Persuasive appeal — uses evidence and emotion effectively; Call to action — specific, doable. Proficient = 3s across.
