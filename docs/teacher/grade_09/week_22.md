# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 22

## English 9: Shakespeare's world; Romeo and Juliet, Prologue and Act 1

**Prepare:** Bookmark the Folger free online Romeo and Juliet. Watch a short Globe Theatre video. Make a glossary card: wherefore = why; anon = soon; hie = hurry; ere = before; thou/thee = you; art = are; hath = has; marry = indeed; 'tis = it is.

**Watch for:**
- 'Wherefore art thou Romeo' means 'why are you Romeo' (a Montague), not 'where are you.'
- Expecting suspense from 'what happens': the Prologue reveals the ending; suspense comes from how and why.
- Reading every line literally: Shakespeare's characters pun constantly (Mercutio, Sampson and Gregory).

### Day 1 (Monday): Elizabethan theater
*Student:* Learn about the Globe, Elizabethan audiences, and Shakespeare's language; translate 15 common words (wherefore, anon, hie).

*How to teach:* Introduce the Globe, groundlings, and Shakespeare's language; your child translates 15 words.

- **Q:** What does 'wherefore' mean?
  **A:** Why.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Prologue
*Student:* Read and paraphrase the Prologue sonnet; predict how knowing the ending affects suspense.

*How to teach:* Read and paraphrase the Prologue sonnet; predict how knowing the ending affects suspense.

- **Q:** What does the Prologue reveal about the lovers?
  **A:** They are 'star-crossed' lovers from two feuding households in Verona whose deaths will end the feud.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Act 1 scenes 1–3
*Student:* Read the street brawl and meet Romeo and Juliet; mark puns and insults.

*How to teach:* Read 1.1–1.3; mark puns and insults.

- **Q:** How does the brawl start in 1.1?
  **A:** Capulet servants Sampson and Gregory provoke Montague servants (biting the thumb); a fight breaks out, Tybalt joins, and the Prince threatens death for further fighting.
- **Q:** Whom does Romeo love at the start?
  **A:** Rosaline, who doesn't return his love.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Act 1 scenes 4–5
*Student:* Read Mercutio's Queen Mab speech and the shared sonnet at the party; analyze the first meeting.

*How to teach:* Read 1.4–1.5; analyze Queen Mab and the shared sonnet.

- **Q:** What image system do Romeo and Juliet use in their first conversation?
  **A:** Religious imagery — pilgrims, saints, holy shrine, prayer — forming a shared sonnet.
- **Q:** What does Juliet say on learning Romeo is a Montague?
  **A:** 'My only love sprung from my only hate!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Act 1 quiz and a paraphrase of 20 lines of your choice into modern English.

*How to teach:* Act 1 quiz; paraphrase 20 lines.

- **Q:** Who recognizes Romeo at the party and wants to fight?
  **A:** Tybalt; Lord Capulet stops him.

**Assessment:** Act 1 quiz and paraphrase.

**Answer key:** Act 1 quiz answers: 1) Setting: Verona. 2) Prince's decree: death for future street fighting. 3) Romeo loves Rosaline at first. 4) Paris wants to marry Juliet. 5) Juliet is not yet 14. 6) Mercutio gives the Queen Mab speech. 7) Romeo and Juliet meet at the Capulets' feast. 8) Tybalt recognizes Romeo's voice. 9) Shared sonnet uses pilgrim/saint imagery. 10) 'My only love sprung from my only hate.' Paraphrase rubric: accurate meaning, modern English, no lines skipped.

## Algebra I: Properties of exponents and radicals

**Prepare:** Make exponent rule cards: product a^m·a^n = a^(m+n), quotient a^m/a^n = a^(m−n), power (a^m)^n = a^(mn), zero a⁰ = 1, negative a^(−n) = 1/a^n, rational a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying bases: 2³·2⁴ = 2⁷, not 4⁷.
- Thinking a negative exponent makes a number negative: 2⁻³ = 1/8.
- (x + y)² = x² + y²: exponents don't distribute over addition.

### Day 1 (Monday): Exponent rules
*Student:* Derive product, quotient, and power rules with expanded form; simplify 20 expressions.

*How to teach:* Write x³·x⁵ in expanded form (xxx·xxxxx) to see 8 factors; derive the other rules similarly. Your child simplifies 20 expressions.

- **Q:** Simplify (2x³)(4x²).
  **A:** 8x⁵.
- **Q:** Simplify (x²)⁴ and x⁷/x³.
  **A:** x⁸ and x⁴.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Zero and negative exponents
*Student:* Explain patterns that define a⁰=1 and a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ; simplify 15 expressions.

*How to teach:* Show the pattern 2³ = 8, 2² = 4, 2¹ = 2, 2⁰ = 1, 2⁻¹ = 1/2 (divide by 2 each step). Your child simplifies 15 expressions with zero and negative exponents.

- **Q:** Simplify 2⁻³ and 5⁰.
  **A:** 1/8 and 1.
- **Q:** Write x⁻²y³ with positive exponents.
  **A:** y³/x².

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rational exponents
*Student:* Show why 8^(1/3)=∛8 using the power rule; convert between forms.

*How to teach:* Show (8^(1/3))³ = 8¹ = 8, so 8^(1/3) is the cube root of 8. Your child converts between radical and rational-exponent forms.

- **Q:** Evaluate 27^(2/3).
  **A:** (∛27)² = 3² = 9.
- **Q:** Evaluate 16^(3/4).
  **A:** (⁴√16)³ = 2³ = 8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Simplifying radicals
*Student:* Simplify radicals like √72 and expressions with rational exponents.

*How to teach:* Simplify √72 by pulling out perfect-square factors: √(36·2) = 6√2. Your child simplifies radicals and rational-exponent expressions.

- **Q:** Simplify √72 and √50.
  **A:** 6√2 and 5√2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on exponents and radicals.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key.

- **Q:** Simplify (a²b⁻³)/(a⁻¹b).
  **A:** a³/b⁴.

**Assessment:** Exponent quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) (2x³)(4x²) = 8x⁵. 2) (x⁴)³ = x¹². 3) 4⁻² = 1/16. 4) 125^(1/3) = 5. 5) √48 = 4√3. 6) x^(3/2) = √(x³) = (√x)³. 7) (a²b⁻³)/(a⁻¹b) = a³/b⁴. 8) (3x)⁰ = 1. 9) 32^(2/5) = 4. 10) √(18x²) = 3x√2 (x ≥ 0).

## Biology – The Living Earth: Mendelian genetics and probability

**Prepare:** Coins (2 per pair), Punnett square worksheets.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing genotype and phenotype: genotype = alleles (Pp); phenotype = observable trait (purple).
- Thinking a 3:1 ratio guarantees 3 of 4 offspring: it gives probabilities, not exact outcomes.
- Assuming dominant means more common: dominance is about expression, not frequency.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mendel's peas
*Student:* Read about Mendel's experiments and define key terms.

*How to teach:* Read about Mendel; define allele, homozygous, heterozygous, dominant, recessive, genotype, phenotype.

- **Q:** What did Mendel find when he crossed two hybrid (Pp) purple-flowered pea plants?
  **A:** About 3 purple : 1 white in the offspring.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Monohybrid crosses
*Student:* Solve 10 monohybrid Punnett squares for pea and human traits and state genotype and phenotype ratios.

*How to teach:* Solve 10 monohybrid crosses.

- **Q:** Cross Pp × Pp. Give genotype and phenotype ratios.
  **A:** Genotypes 1 PP : 2 Pp : 1 pp; phenotypes 3 purple : 1 white.
- **Q:** Cross Tt × tt (T = tall).
  **A:** 1 Tt : 1 tt; 1 tall : 1 short.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coin-toss genetics
*Student:* Flip coins to simulate allele inheritance and compare to expected ratios.

*How to teach:* Flip two coins (heads = A, tails = a) 40 times; compare to 1:2:1.

- **Q:** Expected counts for 40 tosses of Aa × Aa?
  **A:** 10 AA, 20 Aa, 10 aa.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dihybrid crosses
*Student:* Solve dihybrid crosses and predict 9:3:3:1 ratios.

*How to teach:* Solve dihybrid crosses.

- **Q:** In RrYy × RrYy, what fraction are round and yellow (both dominant)?
  **A:** 9/16.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Mendelian genetics.

*How to teach:* Genetics quiz.

- **Q:** What is the probability of aa from Aa × Aa?
  **A:** 1/4 (25%).

**Assessment:** Genetics quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Definitions. 2) Bb × bb → 50% brown, 50% blue (if B = brown). 3) Hh × Hh → 1:2:1, 3:1. 4) Test cross purpose. 5) RrYy × RrYy → 9:3:3:1. 6) Probability of rryy → 1/16.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): California Native peoples before and during the Mission era

**Prepare:** Map of California Native territories and languages (Native-land.ca or the California Indian Heritage Center); readings on cultural burning and acorn processing; Pablo Tac excerpt; Toypurina reading. Note: mission history includes forced labor and high death rates — handle with care and accuracy.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking California Native peoples were one group: there were many distinct nations speaking more than 100 languages/dialects.
- Viewing missions only as peaceful or only as prisons: sources reveal coercion, disease, and resistance, as well as survival and adaptation; weigh perspectives.
- Speaking of Native peoples only in the past tense: California has over 100 federally recognized tribes today.

### Day 1 (Monday): Native California
*Student:* Map California Native nations (100+ languages) and identify the people whose land you live on.

*How to teach:* Map Native nations; identify whose land you live on.

- **Q:** Roughly how many languages were spoken in Native California before European contact?
  **A:** More than 100 (languages and dialects), from many language families.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Traditional ecological knowledge
*Student:* Read about Native land management such as cultural burning and acorn processing.

*How to teach:* Read about cultural burning and acorns.

- **Q:** What was the purpose of cultural burning?
  **A:** Controlled fires managed landscapes — reducing fuel, encouraging food and basketry plants, and improving habitat for game.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Missions
*Student:* Analyze mission-era primary sources, including Pablo Tac's writings, and identify perspectives.

*How to teach:* Analyze mission primary sources, including Pablo Tac.

- **Q:** Who was Pablo Tac?
  **A:** A Luiseño (Quechnajuichom) man raised at Mission San Luis Rey who wrote in the 1830s about his people and mission life — a rare Native-authored mission source.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Resistance
*Student:* Read about the 1785 Toypurina-led uprising at Mission San Gabriel and explain its causes.

*How to teach:* Read about the 1785 Toypurina-led uprising.

- **Q:** What caused the 1785 uprising at Mission San Gabriel?
  **A:** Tongva resentment over loss of land, forced labor, and Spanish bans on traditional dances and ceremonies; Toypurina helped organize it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph evaluating the Mission system's impacts using two sources.

*How to teach:* Write a source-based paragraph evaluating mission impacts.

- **Q:** Name one major impact of the missions on Native peoples.
  **A:** Severe population decline from disease, poor conditions, and violence; loss of land and suppression of cultural practices.

**Assessment:** Source-based paragraph.

**Answer key:** Source-based paragraph: Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate. Proficient content: uses two sources (e.g., Pablo Tac and a padre's report or population data) to evaluate impacts such as disease, forced labor, cultural suppression, and resistance (Toypurina).

## Spanish II: Unit 5 project: travel itinerary

**Prepare:** Help your child pick a Spanish-speaking city and bookmark Spanish-language tourism, museum, and hotel sites. Prepare a slide template and look up the local currency (euro, peso mexicano, sol peruano, peso colombiano, etc.) with a current exchange rate. Write the Unit 5 test (travel vocabulary, future, geography, directions, por/para).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing currencies or forgetting conversion: state the exchange rate on the budget slide and convert all prices consistently.
- Writing the itinerary in the present: plans should use the future (Visitaremos...) or ir a + infinitive.

### Day 1 (Monday): Research
*Student:* Choose a Spanish-speaking city and research sites, food, and lodging on Spanish-language websites.

*How to teach:* Your child researches sites, typical foods, and lodging on Spanish-language pages, taking notes in Spanish with prices.

- **Q:** What is the currency of Spain and Mexico?
  **A:** Spain: el euro; Mexico: el peso mexicano.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Itinerary
*Student:* Write a 5-day itinerary in the future tense with a budget in local currency.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 5-day itinerary in the future tense with a budget table (lodging, food, transport, entry fees).

- **Q:** Write: 'On the first day we will visit the cathedral.'
  **A:** El primer día visitaremos la catedral.
- **Q:** If a hotel costs 80 euros per night for 4 nights, what is the total?
  **A:** 320 euros (convert to dollars with the current rate).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Slides
*Student:* Create 8 slides with images and Spanish captions for each day of the itinerary.

*How to teach:* Your child creates 8 slides with images and Spanish captions; check captions for future forms and accents.

- **Q:** Write a caption: 'We will eat paella in Valencia.'
  **A:** Comeremos paella en Valencia.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the itinerary in Spanish for 3 minutes and answer questions about budget and plans.

*How to teach:* Your child presents for 3 minutes. Ask: ¿Cuánto costará el hotel? ¿Qué harás el último día?

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Cuánto dinero necesitarás en total?'
  **A:** Example: Necesitaré unos mil doscientos euros en total.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I will leave for Lima on Friday.'
  **A:** Saldré para Lima el viernes.

**Assessment:** Itinerary presentation and unit test.

**Answer key:** Itinerary presentation rubric: 4 = 3 minutes in Spanish, 5 days with sites/food/lodging, budget in local currency with U.S. conversion, accurate future tense, answers questions; 3 = minor errors or slight gaps; 2 = missing days/budget or frequent errors; 1 = incomplete. Unit test sample: 1) iremos/haremos/tendré 2) Dobla a la derecha 3) por/para items with reasons 4) capital of Colombia — Bogotá 5) el boleto de ida y vuelta = round-trip ticket.

## Visual Art I: Installation and site-specific art

**Prepare:** Shoebox, found materials, camera, measuring tape. Images of Christo and Jeanne-Claude's 'Running Fence' (1976) and Yayoi Kusama's Infinity Mirror Rooms.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking installation is just a big sculpture. Correct: installation transforms a space and the viewer's experience inside it.
- Ignoring scale in the model. Correct: choose a scale (e.g., 1 inch = 1 foot) and apply it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Installation art
*Student:* Study Christo and Jeanne-Claude's "Running Fence" (Sonoma/Marin, 1976) and Yayoi Kusama's infinity rooms.

*How to teach:* Study 'Running Fence' and Kusama.

- **Q:** Describe 'Running Fence.'
  **A:** A temporary 24.5-mile, 18-foot-tall white fabric fence across Sonoma and Marin County hills to the Pacific, installed in 1976 for two weeks.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Site study
*Student:* Choose a space (yard, hallway, park) and photograph and measure it.

*How to teach:* Photograph and measure a space.

- **Q:** Why measure the site?
  **A:** To scale the model and plan materials accurately.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Proposal
*Student:* Collaborate with a sibling or friend to sketch a proposal that transforms the space.

*How to teach:* Collaborate to sketch a proposal.

- **Q:** What is site-specific art?
  **A:** Art created for a particular location, responding to its features and meaning.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Model
*Student:* Build a scale model in a shoebox.

*How to teach:* Build a scale model in a shoebox.

- **Q:** If the scale is 1 inch = 1 foot, how big is a 6-foot wall in the model?
  **A:** 6 inches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the proposal and model and explain how it changes a viewer's experience.

*How to teach:* Present proposal and model.

- **Q:** How do Kusama's rooms change the viewer's experience?
  **A:** Mirrors and repeated lights or dots create an illusion of infinite space that surrounds the viewer.

**Assessment:** Installation proposal and model.

**Answer key:** Proposal and model: 4 = clear concept transforming a specific site, measured and correctly scaled model, materials list, explanation of viewer experience and collaboration; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = unscaled or unclear concept; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Swimming fundamentals: floating, treading, freestyle

**Prepare:** Lifeguarded community pool (or land-based alternative: bench for arm motion and breathing), goggles, kickboard. Stay within arm's reach of a weak swimmer.

**Watch for:**
- Lifting the head forward to breathe in freestyle. Correct: rotate the head to the side with one goggle in the water.
- Kicking from the knees. Correct: kick from the hips with nearly straight legs and loose ankles.

### Day 1 (Monday): Floating
*Student:* At a community pool, practice back and front floats for 30 seconds each and push-off glides. (No pool: practice freestyle arm motion and breathing on a bench.)

*How to teach:* Back and front floats (30 s), push-off glides.

- **Q:** What helps you float on your back?
  **A:** Relax, head back with ears in water, chest up, arms out, slow breathing — a full breath increases buoyancy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Treading water
*Student:* Practice eggbeater and scissor kicks while treading water for 3 × 1 minute.

*How to teach:* Eggbeater and scissor kicks; tread 3×1 minute.

- **Q:** Why learn to tread water?
  **A:** To stay at the surface with your head up to breathe and call for help.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flutter kick
*Student:* Kick 8 lengths with a kickboard, keeping legs straight and kicks from the hip.

*How to teach:* Flutter kick 8 lengths with kickboard.

- **Q:** Where should a flutter kick come from?
  **A:** The hips, with long legs, slight knee bend and pointed toes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Freestyle
*Student:* Swim freestyle with bilateral breathing every three strokes for 8 × 25 yards with rest.

*How to teach:* Freestyle with bilateral breathing every three strokes, 8×25 yd.

- **Q:** Why breathe every three strokes?
  **A:** It alternates sides, balancing the stroke and building symmetry.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Skills check: float 1 minute, tread 2 minutes, and swim 50 yards freestyle.

*How to teach:* Skills check: float 1 min, tread 2 min, 50 yd freestyle.

- **Q:** What's the correct freestyle arm entry?
  **A:** Hand enters in front of the shoulder, fingertips first, then extends forward before the pull.

**Assessment:** Swim skills check (or land-based technique check).

**Answer key:** Swim skills check: float 1 minute (pass/fail), tread 2 minutes (pass/fail), 50 yd freestyle continuous with side breathing (pass/fail); land-based alternative: demonstrates arm stroke, side breathing and kick on a bench with correct technique. All three = proficient.

## Health & Wellness: Conception, pregnancy and parenting

**Prepare:** Fertilization and fetal development diagrams; sources on prenatal care (March of Dimes, ACOG). Read California's Safely Surrendered Baby Law (infant up to 72 hours old, at a hospital or designated safe-surrender site). Prepare a budget worksheet and quiz. Discuss the topic of pregnancy options with your values in mind; the curriculum presents legal options factually.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking prenatal care can wait until late pregnancy. Correct: major organs form in the first trimester, often before a person knows they are pregnant.
- Underestimating the cost of a baby. Correct: first-year costs typically run many thousands of dollars.

### Day 1 (Monday): Conception
*Student:* Describe fertilization and implantation with a diagram.

*How to teach:* Describe fertilization in the fallopian tube and implantation in the uterine lining about 6–10 days later.

- **Q:** What is a zygote?
  **A:** The single cell formed when a sperm fertilizes an egg.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fetal development
*Student:* Create an illustrated timeline of embryonic and fetal development by trimester, noting when major organs form and why early prenatal care matters.

*How to teach:* Make the timeline: embryo weeks 3–8 (heart, brain, spinal cord form; heartbeat around week 5–6), fetus from week 9; 2nd trimester movement, hearing; 3rd trimester weight gain and lung maturation.

- **Q:** When do most major organs form?
  **A:** During the embryonic period, about weeks 3–8 of development (first trimester).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prenatal care
*Student:* Research prenatal care and how substances affect fetal development.

*How to teach:* Research prenatal care and effects of alcohol (FASD), tobacco (low birth weight), drugs.

- **Q:** What is FASD?
  **A:** Fetal alcohol spectrum disorders — lifelong physical, behavioral and learning problems caused by alcohol exposure before birth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Options and laws
*Student:* Learn about parenting, adoption and abortion as legal options, and California's Safely Surrendered Baby Law.

*How to teach:* Present parenting, adoption and abortion as legal options in California; explain the Safely Surrendered Baby Law.

- **Q:** What does California's Safely Surrendered Baby Law allow?
  **A:** A parent or person with lawful custody can confidentially surrender a baby up to 72 hours old at a hospital or designated safe-surrender site without fear of arrest for abandonment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a budget activity estimating first-year costs of raising a baby, and a quiz.

*How to teach:* Complete the budget and quiz.

- **Q:** List four first-year baby costs.
  **A:** Diapers, formula/food, clothing, car seat, crib, medical care, childcare, housing.

**Assessment:** Budget activity and quiz.

**Answer key:** Budget activity: proficient if at least 8 categories are estimated with realistic monthly costs and a first-year total. Quiz key: fertilization in fallopian tube; implantation in uterus; organs form weeks 3–8; trimesters (~13/27/40 weeks); prenatal care and folic acid; FASD; Safely Surrendered Baby Law (72 hours).
