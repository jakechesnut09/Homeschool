# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 36

## English 9: Presentations, portfolio reflection, and final exam

**Prepare:** Presentation slides; portfolio folder (narrative, literary analysis, argument, research). Choose a TED-Ed talk for evaluation. Prepare the final exam (unseen literary and informational passages plus a timed argument essay).

**Watch for:**
- Reading slides aloud: look at the audience and speak from notes.
- Portfolio reflections that only praise: name specific growth with evidence and a goal.
- Arguing in a timed essay without a counterclaim.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Deliver the research presentation to family or a co-op group and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents to family or a co-op and answers questions.

- **Q:** What should you do if you don't know the answer to an audience question?
  **A:** Admit it honestly, offer what you do know, and say how you would find out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Listen and evaluate
*Student:* Evaluate another speaker (or a TED-Ed talk) for reasoning and evidence using a rubric.

*How to teach:* Your child evaluates another speaker with a rubric.

- **Q:** What makes a speaker's reasoning strong?
  **A:** Clear claims supported by relevant, credible evidence and logical connections; no fallacies.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Portfolio
*Student:* Assemble a portfolio of your best narrative, analysis, argument, and research writing.

*How to teach:* Assemble the portfolio of best work.

- **Q:** Why include drafts as well as finals?
  **A:** They show growth and revision choices.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection letter
*Student:* Write a reflection letter describing your growth and goals for English 10.

*How to teach:* Write the reflection letter on growth and goals.

- **Q:** What should the reflection letter include?
  **A:** Specific strengths and growth with examples from the portfolio, a challenge overcome, and goals for English 10.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the final exam: unseen literary and informational passages and a timed argument essay.

*How to teach:* Final exam.

- **Q:** What are the parts of a strong argument essay?
  **A:** Precise claim, reasons with evidence, counterclaim and rebuttal, conclusion.

**Assessment:** Presentation rubric, portfolio reflection, final exam.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = clear organization, eye contact, appropriate pace, visuals that support, handles questions, within time; 3 = minor issues; 2 = reads from slides; 1 = incomplete. Reflection letter: 4 = specific evidence of growth across genres and concrete goals. Final exam: reading items 80%+ proficient; timed argument essay scored with the W.9-10.1 argument rubric (Week 12).

## Algebra I: Final review and exam

**Prepare:** Assemble a cumulative review packet from unit tests. Schedule a quiet 2-hour final exam block.

**Watch for:**
- Treating the final as only recent material: review early units (equations, inequalities) too.
- Not showing work, which loses partial credit and makes errors hard to find.
- Panicking on unfamiliar-looking problems: identify the function family or equation type first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: linear and systems
*Student:* Complete a cumulative review on Units 1–5 (equations, functions, linear models, systems).

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Units 1–5 review; correct errors together.

- **Q:** Solve the system x + 2y = 7, 3x − y = 7.
  **A:** From elimination: x = 3, y = 2.
- **Q:** Write the line through (0, −3) with slope 4.
  **A:** y = 4x − 3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: statistics and exponentials
*Student:* Complete a cumulative review on Units 6–7 (statistics and exponential functions).

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Units 6–7 review.

- **Q:** Median and IQR of 2, 5, 6, 8, 10, 13, 15, 20?
  **A:** Median 9; Q1 5.5, Q3 14, IQR 8.5.
- **Q:** $500 grows 6% per year. Value after 3 years?
  **A:** 500(1.06)³ ≈ $595.51.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review: polynomials and quadratics
*Student:* Complete a cumulative review on Units 8–11 (polynomials, factoring, quadratics, other functions).

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Units 8–11 review.

- **Q:** Factor 2x² − x − 6.
  **A:** (2x + 3)(x − 2).
- **Q:** Solve x² − 6x + 4 = 0.
  **A:** x = 3 ± √5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice final
*Student:* Take a timed practice final and analyze errors.

*How to teach:* Give a timed practice final; review errors by topic.

- **Q:** Domain of y = √(x + 5)?
  **A:** x ≥ −5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the final exam.

*How to teach:* Give the final exam (about 2 hours). Grade with the key and celebrate completing Algebra I.

- **Q:** Which grows fastest eventually: 1000x, 50x², or 1.01^x?
  **A:** 1.01^x.

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam sample answers: 1) 5 − 2(x − 3) = 3x + 1 → x = 2. 2) −3 < 2x + 5 ≤ 11 → −4 < x ≤ 3. 3) f(x) = x² − 2x: f(−3) = 15. 4) Line through (2, 7), (4, 11) → y = 2x + 3. 5) 2x + y = 9, x − y = 3 → (4, 1). 6) Data 4, 7, 7, 9, 13 → mean 8, median 7. 7) Regression interpretation with units. 8) 1000(1.04)^5 ≈ 1216.65. 9) (x − 5)(x + 2) = x² − 3x − 10. 10) Factor 9x² − 16 = (3x − 4)(3x + 4). 11) Vertex of y = x² − 8x + 12 → (4, −4), zeros 2 and 6. 12) 2x² + 3x − 2 = 0 → 1/2, −2. 13) x² + 2x − 7 = 0 → −1 ± 2√2. 14) Domain of y = √(x − 1) → x ≥ 1. Scoring: 90%+ A, 80–89 B, 70–79 C.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Final review and exam

**Prepare:** Final review guide; flashcards; practice exam. Plan a 2-hour exam block.

**Watch for:**
- Studying only by re-reading: test yourself by redrawing models and solving problems.
- Skipping Unit 1–2 content: the final is cumulative.
- Leaving out units in calculations.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review Units 1–2
*Student:* Redraw the food web, energy pyramid, photosynthesis, respiration, and carbon cycle models from memory and check them.

*How to teach:* Redraw food web, energy pyramid, photosynthesis, respiration, and carbon cycle models; check them.

- **Q:** Which process removes CO₂ from the atmosphere?
  **A:** Photosynthesis (also ocean absorption).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review Units 3–4
*Student:* Practice 20 genetics and evolution problems (Punnett squares, selection data, cladograms).

*How to teach:* Practice 20 genetics and evolution problems.

- **Q:** Cross XᴴXʰ × XᴴY. Chance a son has hemophilia?
  **A:** 50%.
- **Q:** If 36 of 100 mice show a recessive phenotype, is the trait likely to increase if it's camouflaging?
  **A:** Yes — natural selection would increase a trait that improves survival.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review Units 5–6
*Student:* Review homeostasis feedback loops and human-impact case studies with flashcards and a concept map.

*How to teach:* Review homeostasis feedback loops and human-impact cases.

- **Q:** What hormone raises blood glucose?
  **A:** Glucagon.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a practice exam.

*How to teach:* Practice exam; review errors.

- **Q:** Percent change from 50 g to 45 g?
  **A:** −10%.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the final exam.

*How to teach:* Final exam.

- **Q:** What is the main source of new genetic variation?
  **A:** Mutation (with recombination in sexual reproduction).

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam: cumulative items scored with unit keys (energy pyramids, photosynthesis/respiration, carbon cycle, evidence for evolution, natural selection, Punnett squares, protein synthesis, membrane transport, homeostasis, human impacts). Constructed responses scored with the CER rubric. 90%+ A, 80–89 B, 70–79 C.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Community action presentation and final reflection

**Prepare:** Presentation slides; final action product; plan an audience for Day 2. Prepare the Ethnic Studies final.

**Watch for:**
- Presenting only the problem: include history, evidence, and your proposal.
- Reflection essays without sources: cite two primary sources.
- Expecting immediate results: civic change takes time; record any response.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finalize
*Student:* Finalize the action product and build 5 slides summarizing the issue, history, evidence, and your proposal.

*How to teach:* Finalize the product; build 5 slides.

- **Q:** What should the 5 slides cover?
  **A:** Issue, history, evidence, your proposal, and how others can help.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Present
*Student:* Present the project (8 minutes) to family or a co-op group and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Present for 8 minutes; answer questions.

- **Q:** How should you answer a challenging question?
  **A:** Listen fully, acknowledge the point, respond with evidence, and stay respectful.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Take action
*Student:* Send the letter, submit the public comment, or share the flyer/video, and record any response.

*How to teach:* Send the letter or submit the comment; record responses.

- **Q:** Where can you submit a public comment locally?
  **A:** At a city council or school board meeting (in person or written comment per the agenda instructions).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection essay
*Student:* Write a 600-word reflection on how your understanding of identity and community changed this semester, citing two primary sources.

*How to teach:* Write a 600-word reflection citing two primary sources.

- **Q:** Give an example of citing a primary source in a reflection.
  **A:** 'As Executive Order 9066 (1942) shows, neutral-sounding language can hide targeted injustice…'

### Day 5 (Friday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the Ethnic Studies final: key terms, primary source analysis, and a short essay on a movement's strategies.

*How to teach:* Final exam.

- **Q:** Name the four levels of oppression.
  **A:** Ideological, institutional, interpersonal, internalized.

**Assessment:** Project presentation rubric, reflection essay, and final exam.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = clear issue and history, strong evidence, specific proposal, engaging delivery within time, thoughtful answers; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = thin evidence; 1 = incomplete. Reflection essay: 4 = specific growth in understanding of identity/community, two accurately cited primary sources, personal insight. Final exam: key terms, primary source analysis (Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.), short essay on a movement's strategies (proficient = names movement, strategies, outcomes, evidence).

## Spanish II: Final exam and reflection

**Prepare:** Prepare a mixed grammar drill and a vocabulary game (e.g., Pictionary or Quizlet Live) covering all units. Find a short travel podcast clip (e.g., from a Spanish learner podcast) and a short newspaper article. Plan the 6-minute interview with a pharmacy or hotel role-play.

**Watch for:**
- Using the indicative after recommendations (*Te recomiendo que estudias): use the subjunctive — estudies.
- Forgetting usted forms in the role-play: speak to the pharmacist or receptionist with usted.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review
*Student:* Review with a mixed grammar drill and a vocabulary game covering all units.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the mixed grammar drill and plays the vocabulary game. Review any weak area.

- **Q:** Give: preterite yo of ir, imperfect yo of ver, future yo of tener, subjunctive yo of hacer.
  **A:** fui, veía, tendré, haga

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Listening and reading
*Student:* Answer questions on an authentic travel podcast clip and a newspaper article.

*How to teach:* Play the podcast clip twice and give the article; your child answers comprehension questions.

- **Q:** What strategy helps with a fast podcast?
  **A:** Listen first for the main idea, then listen again for key details, numbers, and names; use cognates and context.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speaking interview
*Student:* Hold a 6-minute interview with the teacher, including a role-play at a pharmacy or hotel.

*How to teach:* Hold the 6-minute interview with a hotel or pharmacy role-play (Quisiera una habitación doble por tres noches / ¿Tiene algo para la tos?).

- **Q:** Ask a hotel clerk for a double room for two nights.
  **A:** Quisiera una habitación doble por dos noches, por favor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing
*Student:* Write a 300-word letter to a future Spanish III classmate giving advice with commands and the subjunctive.

*How to teach:* Your child writes the 300-word letter to a future Spanish III student with commands and subjunctive advice.

- **Q:** Write two pieces of advice.
  **A:** Example: Estudia el vocabulario cada día. Te recomiendo que hables con hispanohablantes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Complete can-do self-assessment and write Spanish III goals.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the can-do self-assessment and writes Spanish III goals in Spanish.

- **Q:** Write a goal: 'Next year I want to read a novel in Spanish.'
  **A:** El próximo año quiero leer una novela en español.

**Assessment:** Final exam across all modes.

**Answer key:** Final exam rubric (4 levels per mode): Interpretive — main idea and most details; Interpersonal — 6 minutes, handles the role-play, asks and answers questions with mostly accurate tenses; Presentational (300-word letter) — advice with 4+ tú commands and 4+ subjunctive recommendations (Te recomiendo que estudies...; Es importante que practiques...), clear organization. Sample correct forms: Practica todos los días; No tengas miedo; Es importante que hables mucho; Te recomiendo que uses Conjuguemos.

**Watch for:**

*How to teach:* Create a captioned digital portfolio.

*How to teach:* Final reflection on growth and goals.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Lifelong fitness portfolio and reflection

**Prepare:** Fitness portfolio, calendar, all data, graphs, goal sheets, logs and skill videos. Summer plan template.

**Watch for:**
- Planning outdoor workouts at midday in summer heat. Correct: exercise early or late, hydrate and have indoor backups.
- Reflecting only on fitness scores. Correct: include skills, sportsmanship and habits.

### Day 1 (Monday): Favorite activity
*Student:* Spend 45 minutes on the activity you enjoyed most this year and record heart rate and RPE.

*How to teach:* 45 minutes of favorite activity; record HR and RPE.

- **Q:** Why might enjoyment matter most for lifelong fitness?
  **A:** People continue activities they enjoy, which leads to long-term health.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Portfolio
*Student:* Assemble your portfolio: baseline and spring PFT data, graphs, goal sheets, logs, and best skill videos.

*How to teach:* Assemble portfolio.

- **Q:** What belongs in the portfolio?
  **A:** Baseline and spring PFT data, graphs, goal sheets, logs and skill videos.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a 1-page reflection on your growth in skill, fitness, and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Write a 1-page reflection.

- **Q:** What is one area of growth besides fitness?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g., sportsmanship, leadership, a new skill.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Summer plan
*Student:* Write an 8-week summer activity plan with at least 4 active days per week and a backup for hot days.

*How to teach:* Write an 8-week summer plan with 4+ active days/week and hot-day backups.

- **Q:** Give a hot-day backup.
  **A:** Swim, indoor workout, exercise early morning or evening, mall walking.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final activity day
*Student:* Play a family game day (pickleball, disc golf, or dance) and self-assess sportsmanship one last time.

*How to teach:* Family game day; self-assess sportsmanship.

- **Q:** Name a sign of good sportsmanship.
  **A:** Shaking hands, fair calls, encouraging others, winning and losing gracefully.

**Assessment:** Fitness portfolio and reflection.

**Answer key:** Portfolio and reflection: 4 = complete portfolio (all data, graphs, goals, logs, videos), reflection with specific evidence of growth in fitness, skill and sportsmanship, and an 8-week summer plan with 4+ active days/week and backups; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing sections; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Final review and personal wellness plan

**Prepare:** Review packet; 30 unit flashcards; 25 practice questions; final exam; week-1 wellness inventory for retake; wellness plan template.

**Watch for:**
- Cramming instead of retrieval practice. Correct: quiz yourself and fix errors.
- Vague wellness goals. Correct: write SMART goals with tracking.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review: units 1–4
*Student:* Complete a review grid for wellness, nutrition, mental health and substance use, then quiz yourself with 30 flashcards built from unit vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Complete the units 1–4 review grid and flashcards.

- **Q:** What's the difference between tolerance and dependence?
  **A:** Tolerance: need more for the same effect. Dependence: body relies on the drug and withdrawal occurs without it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review: units 5–7
*Student:* Answer 25 practice questions on relationships, injury prevention, disease prevention and consumer health, and correct missed items with notes.

*How to teach:* Answer 25 practice questions on units 5–7; correct misses.

- **Q:** What does AB 2127 require?
  **A:** Removal for the day after a suspected concussion, written clearance and at least a 7-day graduated return-to-play protocol.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Wellness plan
*Student:* Write a personal wellness plan with one SMART goal each for physical, mental/emotional and social health, including supports, obstacles and a tracking method.

*How to teach:* Write a wellness plan with SMART goals for physical, mental/emotional and social health.

- **Q:** Give a SMART social goal.
  **A:** 'I'll join one club this semester and attend at least 3 meetings a month, tracked on my calendar.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the cumulative final exam (multiple choice, scenarios and a short decision-making essay).

*How to teach:* Give the final exam.

- **Q:** Name the steps of DECIDE.
  **A:** Define, Explore, Consider, Identify values, Decide, Evaluate.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Compare your week-1 wellness self-inventory to a retake today and write a one-page reflection on how your health knowledge and habits changed.

*How to teach:* Retake the inventory; child writes a reflection comparing results.

- **Q:** What changed most since week 1?
  **A:** Answers vary; should cite score changes and specific habits.

**Assessment:** Final exam and wellness plan.

**Answer key:** Final exam key draws on all unit keys: WHO health definition; DECIDE; SMART; CRAAP; six nutrients; 5/20 rule; stress hormones; ACT and 988; dopamine and addiction; Tobacco 21; BAC laws; fentanyl/naloxone; affirmative consent; contraception effectiveness; STI curable vs treatable; Title IX; drowning signs; concussion law; Drop-Cover-Hold On; CPR 100–120/min; vaccines; insurance terms. Essay: 4 = applies all DECIDE steps with realistic consequences and values. Wellness plan: 4 = three SMART goals with supports, obstacles and tracking.
