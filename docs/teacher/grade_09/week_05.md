# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 5

## English 9: Character and theme in "The Necklace"

**Prepare:** Print "The Necklace" (translation). Read it first. Prepare 8 sentences for semicolon practice.

**Watch for:**
- Stating a theme as one word ('pride'): a theme is a full statement, e.g., 'Valuing appearances over honesty can cost a person everything.'
- Putting opinions in an objective summary ('Mathilde was selfish'): stick to events.
- Using a semicolon before a dependent clause or a list introduction: a semicolon joins two independent clauses.

### Day 1 (Monday): Meet Mathilde
*Student:* Read "The Necklace" through the ball; list Mathilde's desires and the details Maupassant uses to show her dissatisfaction.

*How to teach:* Read through the ball. Your child lists Mathilde's desires and the details that show her dissatisfaction.

- **Q:** What is Mathilde unhappy about at the start?
  **A:** She feels she was born for luxury but married a minor clerk; she dreams of fine furniture, dresses, and jewels and is unhappy with her plain home.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finish and summarize
*Student:* Finish the story and write an objective 6-sentence summary with no opinions.

*How to teach:* Finish the story; your child writes a 6-sentence objective summary.

- **Q:** What happens after the ball?
  **A:** Mathilde loses the borrowed necklace; she and her husband replace it with one costing 36,000 francs, spend ten years in poverty repaying the debt, and then learn the original was fake (worth at most 500 francs).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): From topic to theme
*Student:* Turn topics (pride, appearance, class) into full theme statements and support the strongest with three quotations.

*How to teach:* Model turning a topic into a theme statement; your child writes several, chooses the strongest, and supports it with three quotations.

- **Q:** Turn the topic 'appearances' into a theme statement for the story.
  **A:** Sample: 'When people value how they appear to others over honesty, they may suffer far more than the truth would have cost.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cultural lens & punctuation
*Student:* Discuss how 19th-century French class expectations shape Mathilde's choices; practice semicolons joining related clauses in 8 sentences.

*How to teach:* Discuss 19th-century French class expectations (rank shown by dress and possessions). Teach semicolons joining related independent clauses; your child writes 8.

- **Q:** Why didn't Mathilde simply tell Madame Forestier she lost the necklace?
  **A:** Pride and fear of shame — admitting it would hurt her reputation and status.
- **Q:** Punctuate: Mathilde borrowed the necklace she lost it at the ball
  **A:** Mathilde borrowed the necklace; she lost it at the ball.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a theme paragraph and take a reading/semicolon quiz.

*How to teach:* Theme paragraph and quiz.

- **Q:** What is the final ironic revelation?
  **A:** Madame Forestier's necklace was imitation (paste), so their ten years of hardship were unnecessary.

**Assessment:** Theme paragraph (claim + 2 quotations + commentary) and quiz.

**Answer key:** Theme paragraph proficient: a full theme statement (e.g., pride and concern for appearances lead to ruin) supported by two quotations (her dissatisfaction at the start; the years of labor; the final reveal) with commentary. Paragraph rubric: 4 = arguable claim, two well-chosen quotations embedded and cited, commentary that explains HOW the evidence proves the claim; 3 = claim and evidence with thinner commentary; 2 = summary instead of analysis or only one piece of evidence; 1 = off-topic or no evidence. Quiz answers: 1) Mathilde's husband is a clerk in the Ministry of Education/Public Instruction. 2) He gets an invitation to a ball. 3) She borrows a diamond necklace from Madame Forestier. 4) Replacement cost: 36,000 francs. 5) Ten years. 6) The necklace was fake. 7) Correct semicolon use in two sentences.

## Algebra I: Writing equations to solve problems; literal equations

**Prepare:** Print a formula sheet (A = ½bh, d = rt, C = 5/9(F − 32), I = Prt, P = 2l + 2w). Make a blank 'rate × time = distance' table template.

**Watch for:**
- Defining the variable vaguely ('x = tickets'): define exactly ('a = number of adult tickets sold').
- In consecutive-integer problems, writing n, n + 2, n + 3: consecutive integers are n, n + 1, n + 2; consecutive even (or odd) integers are n, n + 2, n + 4.
- Solving a literal equation by plugging in numbers: keep letters as letters and undo operations in reverse order.

### Day 1 (Monday): Modeling with equations
*Student:* Write and solve equations for 8 word problems (consecutive integers, perimeter, ticket sales).

*How to teach:* Model a four-step routine: define the variable, write the equation, solve, answer in a sentence with units. Your child solves the 8 word problems with the routine.

- **Q:** The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. Find them.
  **A:** n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 72 → 3n + 3 = 72 → n = 23: 23, 24, 25.
- **Q:** Adult tickets cost $8, student tickets $5. 100 tickets brought in $650. How many adult tickets?
  **A:** 8a + 5(100 − a) = 650 → 3a + 500 = 650 → a = 50 adult tickets.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rate and mixture problems
*Student:* Solve distance-rate-time and mixture problems with tables to organize information.

*How to teach:* Set up a table with rows for each object/solution and columns rate × time = distance (or concentration × amount = pure amount). Your child fills the table first, then writes the equation.

- **Q:** Two cars leave the same point in opposite directions at 50 mph and 60 mph. When are they 330 miles apart?
  **A:** 50t + 60t = 330 → 110t = 330 → t = 3 hours.
- **Q:** How many liters of 20% acid must be added to 10 L of 50% acid to make a 30% solution?
  **A:** 0.20x + 0.50(10) = 0.30(x + 10) → 0.2x + 5 = 0.3x + 3 → x = 20 L.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Literal equations
*Student:* Solve formulas like A=½bh, d=rt, and C=5/9(F−32) for a specified variable.

*How to teach:* Model solving C = 5/9(F − 32) for F: multiply by 9/5, then add 32 → F = 9/5·C + 32. Your child solves each formula for the named variable and checks with a numeric example.

- **Q:** Solve A = ½bh for h.
  **A:** h = 2A/b.
- **Q:** Solve C = 5/9(F − 32) for F.
  **A:** F = (9/5)C + 32.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Formulas in context
*Student:* Rearrange the BMI and simple-interest formulas to answer questions about real data.

*How to teach:* Use I = Prt: rearrange to r = I/(Pt), then answer real questions. Your child rearranges the interest and BMI formulas and answers the posed questions with units.

- **Q:** A $1,500 deposit earned $90 simple interest in 2 years. What was the annual rate?
  **A:** r = I/(Pt) = 90/(1500·2) = 0.03 = 3% per year.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on modeling and literal equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key. Require a defined variable and a sentence answer on each word problem.

- **Q:** Solve P = 2l + 2w for w.
  **A:** w = (P − 2l)/2 = P/2 − l.

**Assessment:** Modeling/literal equations quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Rectangle with perimeter 54 and length 3 more than twice the width: 2w + 2(2w + 3) = 54 → w = 8, l = 19. 2) Two consecutive even integers sum to 86 → 42 and 44. 3) Solve d = rt for t → t = d/r. 4) Solve P = 2l + 2w for w → w = (P − 2l)/2. 5) Solve y = mx + b for m → m = (y − b)/x. 6) A cyclist rides 45 mi in 3 h → 15 mph. 7) I = Prt with I = $60, P = $1000, t = 3 → r = 2%. 8) 68°F in Celsius → 20°C. Rubric for word problems: variable defined, correct equation, correct solution, sentence with units.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Group behavior and ecosystem stability

**Prepare:** Sugar water (1:1) in a bottle cap; ant trail (outdoors); timer. Find a wildfire-recovery case study (e.g., chaparral regrowth after a fire — many chaparral plants resprout or have fire-triggered seeds). Prepare the Unit 1 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking animals cooperate 'to help the species': behaviors persist because they increase the survival and reproduction of individuals and relatives.
- Believing ecosystems return exactly to how they were: they may recover, or shift to a new state if disturbance is extreme or frequent.
- Counting only one observation as evidence: repeat counts at intervals.

### Day 1 (Monday): Group behavior
*Student:* Read about bee swarming, fish schooling, and wolf pack hunting; list the survival advantage of each.

*How to teach:* Read about bee swarming, fish schooling, and wolf packs; list survival advantages.

- **Q:** How does schooling help fish survive?
  **A:** It confuses predators (hard to target one fish), and many eyes detect danger sooner.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ant observation
*Student:* Place sugar water near an ant trail and record recruitment over 30 minutes.

*How to teach:* Place sugar water near an ant trail; count ants every 5 minutes for 30 minutes.

- **Q:** Predict how ant numbers at the sugar will change, and why.
  **A:** They increase over time because scouts lay pheromone trails that recruit nestmates (recruitment), then may level off.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stability
*Student:* Analyze a case study of ecosystem recovery after a California wildfire.

*How to teach:* Analyze a chaparral wildfire recovery case study.

- **Q:** How do many chaparral plants recover after fire?
  **A:** Some resprout from burls/roots; others have seeds that germinate after heat or smoke; but fires that are too frequent can convert chaparral to non-native grassland.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evidence evaluation
*Student:* Write a CER evaluating whether group behavior increases survival using two data sources.

*How to teach:* Write a CER on whether group behavior increases survival using two data sources.

- **Q:** What makes evidence strong in a CER?
  **A:** Specific, relevant data from reliable sources, with numbers and comparisons.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on ecosystems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** What does the 10% rule predict for 50,000 kcal of producers at the secondary consumer level?
  **A:** 500 kcal.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test sample answers: 1) Levels of organization order. 2) Logistic curve and K. 3) Density-dependent vs. independent examples. 4) Interaction types. 5) Keystone species (sea otter). 6) Simpson's index for 6, 2, 2 → Σ = 30 + 2 + 2 = 34, 34/90 = 0.378, index 0.62. 7) 10% rule calculation. 8) Group behavior advantage example. 9) Ecosystem resilience after disturbance. Scoring: 80%+ proficient. CER rubric: 4 = claim answers the question; evidence uses specific data (numbers, units, observations); reasoning connects the evidence to a scientific principle; 3 = claim and evidence with partial reasoning; 2 = claim with vague evidence; 1 = claim only or incorrect.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Weather, climate, and climate regions

**Prepare:** Monthly temperature and precipitation data for Sacramento and Singapore (e.g., NOAA or climate-data.org); graph paper; Köppen climate map.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing weather and climate.
- Thinking elevation doesn't matter: temperature drops about 6.5°C per 1,000 m (3.5°F per 1,000 ft).
- Believing deserts are always hot: deserts are defined by low precipitation (e.g., Antarctica, Gobi).

### Day 1 (Monday): Climate factors
*Student:* Learn latitude, elevation, ocean currents, and prevailing winds as climate controls.

*How to teach:* Teach climate controls: latitude, elevation, ocean currents, prevailing winds.

- **Q:** Why is San Francisco's summer cooler than Sacramento's?
  **A:** The cold California Current and ocean fog cool coastal San Francisco; inland Sacramento lacks that marine influence.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Climographs
*Student:* Build climographs for Sacramento and Singapore and compare.

*How to teach:* Build and compare climographs.

- **Q:** How do Sacramento's and Singapore's climographs differ?
  **A:** Sacramento: hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters (Mediterranean). Singapore: warm (~27°C) all year with heavy rain every month (tropical rainforest).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rain shadow
*Student:* Model a rain shadow with the Sierra Nevada and explain Death Valley's aridity.

*How to teach:* Model a rain shadow.

- **Q:** Explain the rain shadow east of the Sierra Nevada.
  **A:** Moist Pacific air rises up the western slopes, cools, and drops rain/snow; descending air on the east side warms and dries, leaving deserts like the Owens Valley and Death Valley.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Climate regions
*Student:* Map Köppen climate regions and explain human adaptations.

*How to teach:* Map Köppen regions and adaptations.

- **Q:** What Köppen climate type is most of coastal California?
  **A:** Mediterranean (Csa/Csb) — dry summers, wet winters.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on climate.

*How to teach:* Climate quiz.

- **Q:** Why does latitude affect temperature?
  **A:** Near the equator, sunlight strikes more directly year-round; at high latitudes it strikes at a low angle, spreading energy out.

**Assessment:** Climograph analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Climograph analysis: proficient = correct bars (precipitation) and line (temperature), identifies climate type for each city with reasons. Quiz answers: four climate controls; rain shadow; Mediterranean climate; ocean currents (warm Gulf Stream, cold California Current); lapse rate; weather vs. climate.

## Spanish II: Cuando era niño/a: the imperfect tense

**Prepare:** Gather 3–5 childhood photos. Make an imperfect chart: -ar: -aba, -abas, -aba, -ábamos, -abais, -aban; -er/-ir: -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían; irregulars ser (era), ir (iba), ver (veía).

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the accent on -ía (*comia): every -er/-ir imperfect form has an accent on the í (comía, vivíamos).
- Missing the accent on nosotros -ar (*hablabamos): it is hablábamos.
- Using the preterite for habitual past actions: 'used to / would' habits take the imperfect (Siempre jugaba en el parque).

### Day 1 (Monday): Imperfect forms
*Student:* Learn -aba and -ía endings and conjugate 20 verbs.

*How to teach:* Explain that the imperfect describes how things were or used to be. Model 'De niña, yo jugaba mucho y comía helado.' Your child conjugates the 20 verbs; check accents.

- **Q:** Conjugate estudiar in the imperfect for yo and nosotros.
  **A:** estudiaba; estudiábamos
- **Q:** Conjugate escribir for ellos.
  **A:** escribían

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Childhood habits
*Student:* Write 10 sentences about what you used to do as a child using frequency words.

*How to teach:* List frequency words (siempre, a menudo, todos los días, cada verano, a veces, nunca). Your child writes 10 'used to' sentences about childhood, each with a frequency word.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Every summer we used to go to the beach.'
  **A:** Cada verano íbamos a la playa.
- **Q:** Translate: 'I often watched cartoons.'
  **A:** A menudo veía dibujos animados.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Irregulars
*Student:* Learn ser, ir, and ver in the imperfect and use each in sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach the only three irregulars: ser (era, eras, era, éramos, erais, eran), ir (iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, ibais, iban), ver (veía, veías...). Your child writes two sentences for each.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My grandmother was very kind.'
  **A:** Mi abuela era muy amable.
- **Q:** Translate: 'We used to go to the park.'
  **A:** Íbamos al parque.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Photo description
*Student:* Describe a childhood photo orally using the imperfect.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a photo and describes it aloud: age (Tenía cinco años), place (Vivíamos en...), people, and what they used to do. Ask two follow-up questions.

- **Q:** How do you say 'I was five years old'?
  **A:** Tenía cinco años (age in the past uses tener in the imperfect).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Imperfect quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the imperfect quiz and score the photo description with the rubric. Review any missed accents.

- **Q:** Fill in: Cuando yo ___ (ser) niño, ___ (vivir) en Texas.
  **A:** era; vivía

**Assessment:** Imperfect quiz and photo description.

**Answer key:** Imperfect quiz sample key: hablar/yo — hablaba; comer/nosotros — comíamos; vivir/ellos — vivían; jugar/tú — jugabas; ser/yo — era; ir/nosotros — íbamos; ver/ella — veía; tener/ustedes — tenían; 'When I was little, I used to play soccer' — Cuando era pequeño/a, jugaba al fútbol. Photo description rubric: 4 = 6+ imperfect sentences describing age, place, people, and activities, accurate forms; 3 = 4–5 sentences; 2 = few verbs or mixed tenses; 1 = isolated words.

## Visual Art I: Texture and drawing media exploration

**Prepare:** Crayons and thin paper for rubbings; vine and compressed charcoal; India ink, brush, three cups for dilutions; spray fixative or hairspray (use outdoors).

**Watch for:**
- Pressing hard with compressed charcoal at the start. Correct: start with vine charcoal (soft, erasable), then add compressed for darks.
- Ink washes too dark. Correct: test dilutions on scrap first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Frottage
*Student:* Make crayon rubbings of 10 textures around the house and label them.

*How to teach:* Ten labeled crayon rubbings (frottage).

- **Q:** What is frottage?

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Texture study
*Student:* Draw close-up studies of tree bark, fabric, and metal.

*How to teach:* Close-up texture studies of bark, fabric, metal.

- **Q:** How can you draw metal's texture?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Charcoal
*Student:* Draw a crumpled paper bag in charcoal using subtractive eraser drawing.

*How to teach:* Charcoal paper bag using subtractive eraser drawing.

- **Q:** What is subtractive drawing?
  **A:** Covering the paper with a tone, then erasing to create lights.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ink wash
*Student:* Paint a quick ink-wash study of a houseplant with three dilutions.

*How to teach:* Ink-wash houseplant with three dilutions.

- **Q:** How do you make a light ink wash?

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 portfolio review
*Student:* Assemble your best three drawings and document your growth with photos and captions.

*How to teach:* Assemble best three drawings; document growth.

- **Q:** Why document with photos and captions?
  **A:** It records growth and process for the portfolio.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 portfolio review.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 portfolio review: 4 = three strong drawings showing contour, proportion, value and texture, clear photos with captions describing technique and growth; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = weak documentation; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Running form and pacing

**Prepare:** Phone for video, stopwatch, a measured 400 m (track or measured route). Running-form checklist (tall posture, slight forward lean, relaxed shoulders, 90° arm swing front-to-back, midfoot strike under hips, quick cadence).

**Watch for:**
- Overstriding (landing on heel far ahead). Correct: shorter, quicker steps landing under the hips.
- Arms crossing the body. Correct: swing forward and back, hands relaxed, 'cheek to pocket.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Running form
*Student:* Learn posture, arm swing, and midfoot strike; do 6 form drills (A-skips, B-skips) and a 15-minute run.

*How to teach:* Teach form; do 6 drills (A-skips, B-skips, high knees, butt kicks, carioca, strides) and a 15-minute run.

- **Q:** What is an A-skip?
  **A:** A skipping drill driving one knee up to hip height with the opposite arm, then pawing the foot down under the hip.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Video analysis
*Student:* Film yourself running and compare to a form checklist; note two corrections.

*How to teach:* Film running; note two corrections.

- **Q:** Why is heel-striking far in front of the body inefficient?
  **A:** It acts as a brake and increases impact forces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pacing
*Student:* Run 4 × 400 m (or 2-minute repeats) trying to keep each within 3 seconds.

*How to teach:* 4×400 m (or 2-min repeats) within 3 seconds.

- **Q:** If your 400s are 2:00, 1:52, 2:05, 1:58, were they within 3 seconds?
  **A:** No — the range is 1:52 to 2:05 (13 seconds).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Long easy run
*Student:* Complete a 25–30 minute continuous easy run or run/walk.

*How to teach:* 25–30 minute easy run or run/walk.

- **Q:** How should an easy run feel?
  **A:** Conversational pace (RPE ~3–4).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Time trial: 1 mile, recording splits and comparing to your baseline.

*How to teach:* 1-mile time trial with splits; compare to baseline.

- **Q:** Your quarter splits were 2:10, 2:15, 2:20, 2:05. Total?
  **A:** 8:50.

**Assessment:** Mile time trial and form video analysis.

**Answer key:** Mile time trial: recorded with four splits and comparison to baseline. Video analysis: proficient = identifies two specific form issues against the checklist with cues/drills to fix them.

## Health & Wellness: Food labels and the Dietary Guidelines

**Prepare:** Collect five packaged food labels (two cereals, two drinks, one snack), a sugar bowl, teaspoon, a can of soda and a sports drink. Read the Dietary Guidelines for Americans executive summary. Prepare a 10-question label practical with two unfamiliar labels and write your own answers from those labels.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking %DV is the percentage of the food that is that nutrient. Correct: %DV shows how much one serving contributes to a day's recommended amount on a 2,000-calorie diet.
- Ignoring serving size. Correct: all numbers are per serving; eating two servings doubles everything.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading a label
*Student:* Annotate a Nutrition Facts label for serving size, calories, %DV, added sugar and sodium using the 5/20 rule.

*How to teach:* Annotate a label: serving size and servings per container, calories, %DV, added sugars, sodium. Teach 5/20: 5% DV or less is low, 20% or more is high.

- **Q:** A cereal has 25% DV sodium per serving. Is that high or low?
  **A:** High (20% or more).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Label comparison
*Student:* Compare five pantry items in pairs (two cereals, two drinks, etc.) and choose the healthier option with evidence from the labels.

*How to teach:* Compare pairs and choose the healthier option with label evidence (fiber, added sugar, sodium, saturated fat).

- **Q:** Cereal A: 3 g fiber, 12 g added sugar; Cereal B: 6 g fiber, 4 g added sugar. Which is healthier?
  **A:** Cereal B — more fiber and less added sugar.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sugar and sodium
*Student:* Measure the teaspoons of sugar in a can of soda and a sports drink using a sugar bowl and the 4 g = 1 tsp conversion.

*How to teach:* Measure sugar: grams ÷ 4 = teaspoons. A 12-oz cola with about 39 g sugar ≈ 10 tsp; a 20-oz sports drink with about 34 g ≈ 8.5 tsp.

- **Q:** A drink has 52 g sugar. How many teaspoons is that?
  **A:** 52 ÷ 4 = 13 teaspoons.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dietary Guidelines
*Student:* Read the Dietary Guidelines for Americans key recommendations and summarize four in your own words.

*How to teach:* Read the key recommendations and the child summarizes four in their own words.

- **Q:** What do the Dietary Guidelines say about added sugars and sodium?
  **A:** Limit added sugars to less than 10% of calories per day starting at age 2, and sodium to less than 2,300 mg per day for ages 14+ (lower for younger children).

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a label-reading practical: answer 10 questions using two unfamiliar food labels.

*How to teach:* Give the label practical.

- **Q:** If a serving is 1 cup with 150 calories and you eat 2½ cups, how many calories?
  **A:** 375 calories.

**Assessment:** Food-label practical with at least 8/10 correct.

**Answer key:** Label practical: score against your answer sheet for the two labels; typical items: serving size, servings per container, calories per serving and for the whole package (calories × servings), %DV of sodium (high/low using 5/20), added sugar in teaspoons (g ÷ 4), fiber, which label is healthier and why. 8/10 = proficient.
