# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 16

## English 9: Introduction to the epic: The Odyssey, Books 1 and 5

**Prepare:** Get The Odyssey (Butler translation free, or Fagles/Fitzgerald from the library) and a Mediterranean map. Read Books 1 and 5 first and a short background article (oral tradition, Trojan War, Homer). Note: translations differ in wording — let your child cite their edition.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the poem begins at the beginning of the journey: it starts in medias res, ten years after Troy, with Odysseus stranded.
- Seeing xenia as simple politeness: guest-friendship was a sacred obligation protected by Zeus.
- Assuming Odysseus is purely heroic: Homer presents a clever but flawed hero.

### Day 1 (Monday): Greek world
*Student:* Read a background article on Homer, oral tradition, and the Trojan War; map Odysseus's journey on a Mediterranean map.

*How to teach:* Read the background article and map key stops (Troy, Ithaca, the Cyclops' island, Circe's island, Calypso's Ogygia, Phaeacia).

- **Q:** What war is Odysseus returning from, and how long has he been gone?
  **A:** The Trojan War; the war took ten years and his journey home another ten — twenty years in all.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Book 1
*Student:* Read Book 1; identify the invocation, in medias res, and how Telemachus and the suitors are introduced.

*How to teach:* Read Book 1; identify the invocation, in medias res, Telemachus, and the suitors.

- **Q:** What is the invocation?
  **A:** The opening prayer asking the Muse to sing the story of the man 'of many ways/twists and turns.'
- **Q:** What problem do Penelope and Telemachus face in Ithaca?
  **A:** Suitors are living in Odysseus's house, eating his wealth, and pressuring Penelope to remarry.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hospitality
*Student:* Track examples of xenia (guest-friendship) in Book 1 and explain why it matters to Greek culture.

*How to teach:* Track xenia in Book 1.

- **Q:** How does Telemachus show xenia to Athena (disguised as Mentes)?
  **A:** He welcomes the stranger, takes the spear, seats and feeds the guest before asking who he is.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Book 5
*Student:* Read Book 5 (Calypso); analyze Odysseus's choice to leave immortality behind.

*How to teach:* Read Book 5; analyze Odysseus's choice to leave Calypso.

- **Q:** Why does Odysseus leave Calypso even though she offers immortality?
  **A:** He longs for home, Ithaca, and Penelope; he chooses mortal life and identity over endless life with her.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on epic conventions and Books 1 and 5; write an entry on what Greek culture values so far.

*How to teach:* Quiz on epic conventions and Books 1 and 5; notebook entry on Greek values.

- **Q:** Who orders Calypso to release Odysseus?
  **A:** Zeus, through the messenger god Hermes (at Athena's request).

**Assessment:** Epic conventions quiz; reader's notebook entry.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Epic = long narrative poem about a hero's deeds. 2) Invocation = appeal to the Muse. 3) In medias res = beginning in the middle. 4) Epithet example: 'grey-eyed Athena' / 'rosy-fingered Dawn'. 5) Athena disguises herself as Mentes. 6) Suitors are consuming Odysseus's estate. 7) Calypso holds Odysseus on Ogygia for seven years. 8) Hermes delivers Zeus's command. 9) Poseidon wrecks Odysseus's raft. 10) He washes ashore in Phaeacia. Notebook entry proficient: names values such as hospitality (xenia), loyalty, cleverness, honor, and respect for the gods with examples.

## Algebra I: Solving systems by elimination

**Prepare:** Prepare 10 'adding' systems, 10 'multiply first' systems, and 8 mixed systems. Optional algebra tiles.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying only one side of an equation: multiply EVERY term on both sides.
- Adding when you should subtract (or vice versa): look for opposite coefficients; if they're equal, subtract (or multiply one equation by −1).
- Forgetting to find the second variable after eliminating.

### Day 1 (Monday): Elimination by adding
*Student:* Solve 10 systems where adding eliminates a variable.

*How to teach:* Model x + y = 10, x − y = 4: adding cancels y, 2x = 14, x = 7, y = 3. Your child solves 10 systems and checks each.

- **Q:** Solve: x + y = 10, x − y = 4.
  **A:** (7, 3).
- **Q:** Solve: 3x + y = 11, 2x − y = 4.
  **A:** 5x = 15 → x = 3, y = 2.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multiplying first
*Student:* Solve 10 systems that require multiplying one or both equations.

*How to teach:* Model 2x + 3y = 12, x − y = 1: multiply the second by 3 so y terms are opposites, add → 5x = 15, x = 3, y = 2. Your child solves 10 such systems.

- **Q:** Solve: 2x + 3y = 12 and x − y = 1.
  **A:** (3, 2).
- **Q:** Solve: 2x + 5y = 1 and 3x − 2y = 11.
  **A:** Multiply by 2 and 5: 4x + 10y = 2, 15x − 10y = 55 → 19x = 57, x = 3, y = −1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Why it works
*Student:* Write an argument explaining why replacing an equation with a sum of equations keeps the same solutions.

*How to teach:* Discuss: if (x, y) makes both equations true, it makes their sum true (equals added to equals are equal); and you can recover the original equation by subtracting, so no solutions are gained or lost. Your child writes this argument in a paragraph.

- **Q:** Why does adding two equations not change the system's solutions?
  **A:** Any pair satisfying both equations also satisfies their sum (adding equal amounts to equal amounts), and the original can be recovered by subtracting, so the new system has exactly the same solutions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Choosing a method
*Student:* Solve 8 mixed systems choosing the most efficient method; justify choices.

*How to teach:* Discuss when each method is best (substitution when a variable is already isolated; elimination when coefficients line up). Your child solves 8 mixed systems and justifies the choice.

- **Q:** Which method for y = x − 2 and 2x + y = 10? Solve it.
  **A:** Substitution (y is isolated): 2x + x − 2 = 10 → x = 4, y = 2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on elimination and method choice.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key with one written justification of method choice.

- **Q:** Solve: 4x + 2y = 14, 3x − 2y = 7.
  **A:** 7x = 21 → x = 3, y = 1.

**Assessment:** Elimination quiz with written justification.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) 3x + y = 11, 2x − y = 4 → (3, 2). 2) 4x + 2y = 14, 3x − 2y = 7 → (3, 1). 3) 2x + 5y = 1, 3x − 2y = 11 → (3, −1). 4) y = x − 2, 2x + y = 10 → (4, 2). 5) 5x − 2y = 4, 3x + 4y = 18 → (2, 3). 6) Justification (proficient): names the method and why (e.g., 'elimination, because the y-coefficients are opposites').

## Biology – The Living Earth: Speciation

**Prepare:** Map of Ensatina salamander subspecies around California's Central Valley (search 'Ensatina ring species map', e.g., UC Berkeley Understanding Evolution). Hawaiian honeycreeper beak images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking species are always clearly separate: ring species and hybrids show speciation is a gradual process.
- Confusing prezygotic and postzygotic barriers: prezygotic prevent mating/fertilization; postzygotic affect the hybrid after fertilization.
- Believing new species appear in one generation (usually they accumulate differences over many).

### Day 1 (Monday): Defining species
*Student:* Discuss the biological species concept and its limits.

*How to teach:* Discuss the biological species concept and its limits.

- **Q:** What is the biological species concept?
  **A:** Populations whose members can interbreed in nature and produce fertile offspring.
- **Q:** Name one limitation.
  **A:** It can't be applied to asexual organisms or fossils, and some species hybridize.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California ring species
*Student:* Map Ensatina salamander subspecies around the Central Valley and explain ring species.

*How to teach:* Map Ensatina subspecies.

- **Q:** Why are Ensatina salamanders called a ring species?
  **A:** Neighboring populations around the Central Valley interbreed, but where the two ends of the ring meet in Southern California, the forms rarely interbreed and act like separate species.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Isolation mechanisms
*Student:* Classify 10 examples of prezygotic and postzygotic barriers (mating seasons, mule sterility, bird songs).

*How to teach:* Classify 10 barriers.

- **Q:** Classify: two frog species breed in different months; a mule (horse × donkey) is sterile.
  **A:** Temporal isolation (prezygotic); hybrid sterility (postzygotic).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Adaptive radiation
*Student:* Analyze Hawaiian honeycreepers as adaptive radiation.

*How to teach:* Analyze Hawaiian honeycreepers.

- **Q:** How do Hawaiian honeycreepers show adaptive radiation?
  **A:** A single finch-like ancestor colonized the islands and diversified into many species with beaks adapted to different foods (nectar, seeds, insects).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on speciation.

*How to teach:* Speciation quiz.

- **Q:** Which type of speciation results from a geographic barrier?
  **A:** Allopatric speciation.

**Assessment:** Speciation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Biological species concept. 2) Allopatric vs. sympatric speciation. 3) Prezygotic examples: temporal, behavioral (bird songs), mechanical, habitat, gametic. 4) Postzygotic: hybrid sterility (mule), hybrid inviability. 5) Ensatina ring species. 6) Honeycreeper adaptive radiation.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Urbanization and urban challenges

**Prepare:** UN World Urbanization Prospects summary (percent urban: about 30% in 1950, over 50% by about 2007–2008, about 55% in 2018, projected ~68% by 2050). Megacity readings. Historic and modern LA maps.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming all megacities are in rich countries: most of the fastest-growing are in Asia and Africa.
- Viewing informal settlements as only problems: they also house dynamic economies and communities.
- Thinking freeways were neutral: their routes displaced many communities, often low-income communities of color.

### Day 1 (Monday): Urbanization trends
*Student:* Graph the percent of world population living in cities from 1950 to 2050 (UN projections) and identify the fastest-urbanizing regions.

*How to teach:* Graph percent urban 1950–2050.

- **Q:** About when did more than half the world's people begin living in cities?
  **A:** Around 2007–2008.
- **Q:** Which regions are urbanizing fastest?
  **A:** Africa and Asia.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Megacity profile
*Student:* Research Lagos, Mumbai, or Mexico City: growth rate, housing, transit, and informal settlements.

*How to teach:* Profile Lagos, Mumbai, or Mexico City.

- **Q:** What is an informal settlement?
  **A:** Housing built without legal title or permits, often lacking services — e.g., Mumbai's Dharavi.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Los Angeles sprawl
*Student:* Compare 1920s and today's maps of Los Angeles and analyze freeway building's effect on neighborhoods.

*How to teach:* Compare 1920s and today's LA maps; analyze freeways.

- **Q:** How did freeway building affect neighborhoods like Boyle Heights?
  **A:** Multiple freeways (including the East LA Interchange) cut through the neighborhood, displacing residents and dividing the community.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Planning solutions
*Student:* Design a one-page plan for a walkable neighborhood with housing, transit, and parks; justify choices.

*How to teach:* Design a walkable neighborhood plan.

- **Q:** Name two features of a walkable neighborhood.
  **A:** Mixed land use (homes near shops/schools), sidewalks and safe crossings, transit stops, parks within a short walk.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on urbanization concepts.

*How to teach:* Urbanization quiz.

- **Q:** What is urban sprawl?
  **A:** Low-density development spreading outward from a city, dependent on cars.

**Assessment:** Urban planning page and quiz.

**Answer key:** Urban plan rubric: 4 = labeled map with housing, transit, parks, shops; justification using concepts (density, mixed use, access); 3 = minor gaps; 2 = drawing without justification; 1 = incomplete. Quiz answers: urbanization definition; percent urban trend; megacity (10+ million); informal settlements; sprawl; gentrification definition.

## Spanish II: Regional cuisines of the Spanish-speaking world

**Prepare:** Print a map of Latin America and Spain. Bookmark sources on paella (Valencia, Spain), arepas (Venezuela/Colombia), ceviche (Peru), mole (Puebla/Oaxaca, Mexico), pupusas (El Salvador), empanadas, asado (Argentina), gallo pinto (Costa Rica/Nicaragua), sancocho, and tortilla española.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming all Spanish-speaking countries eat 'Mexican food': cuisines differ widely by region and ingredients (e.g., ceviche in Peru, asado in Argentina).
- Misplacing dishes: paella is from Valencia, Spain; arepas are from Venezuela and Colombia; pupusas from El Salvador.

### Day 1 (Monday): Regional dishes
*Student:* Research dishes like paella, arepas, ceviche, and mole.

*How to teach:* Your child researches 4–6 dishes and records country, main ingredients, and one fact for each in Spanish.

- **Q:** What is ceviche and where is it a national dish?
  **A:** Raw fish 'cooked' (marinated) in lime juice with onion and chile — Peru's national dish.
- **Q:** Where is paella from and what grain is it made with?
  **A:** Valencia, Spain — rice (arroz), cooked in a wide pan.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map
*Student:* Mark 10 dishes on a map of Latin America and Spain and write one ingredient sentence for each.

*How to teach:* Your child marks 10 dishes on the map and writes one ingredient sentence for each (Las arepas se hacen con harina de maíz).

- **Q:** Translate: 'Mole has chocolate and chiles.'
  **A:** El mole tiene chocolate y chiles.
- **Q:** Where are pupusas from and what are they?
  **A:** El Salvador — thick corn tortillas stuffed with cheese, beans, or pork.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentation
*Student:* Prepare a 2-minute presentation on one dish's history.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses one dish and outlines a 2-minute talk: origin, ingredients, history, when it is eaten, and a personal opinion. Practice once with notes.

- **Q:** Write a sentence about when a dish is eaten: 'Tamales are eaten at Christmas.'
  **A:** Los tamales se comen en Navidad.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the dish for 2 minutes in Spanish (bring a sample if possible) and answer two questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the dish (with a sample if possible). Ask two questions such as ¿Es picante? ¿Cuáles son los ingredientes principales?

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Es difícil preparar la paella?'
  **A:** Example: Sí, es un poco difícil porque el arroz necesita mucho tiempo y atención.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read a short article about a regional cuisine and answer 8 comprehension questions in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child reads a short article about a regional cuisine and answers 8 questions in Spanish. Check answers against the text.

- **Q:** What does 'plato típico' mean?
  **A:** A traditional or signature dish of a region.

**Assessment:** Dish presentation.

**Answer key:** Dish presentation rubric: 4 = 2 minutes in Spanish, covers origin, main ingredients, history, and how/when it is eaten, accurate facts, answers 2 questions; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = under 1.5 minutes or inaccurate facts; 1 = incomplete. Reading comprehension key depends on the chosen article — the answers must quote or paraphrase the text in Spanish (e.g., for a ceviche article: El ceviche es de Perú; se prepara con pescado crudo marinado en jugo de limón).

## Visual Art I: Relief printmaking: carving and printing

**Prepare:** Carving tools (or pencil/ballpoint for foam), bench hook, block printing ink (water-based), brayer, inking plate (glass or tray), paper, spoon or baren.

**Watch for:**
- Carving toward the hand. Correct: always carve away from the body and hold the block behind the tool (use a bench hook).
- Over-inking. Correct: roll a thin, even layer that 'hisses' like velvet.

### Day 1 (Monday): Carving safety
*Student:* Learn tool safety (carve away from hands).

*How to teach:* Tool safety demonstration.

- **Q:** What is the most important carving safety rule?
  **A:** Always carve away from your hands and body.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Carve
*Student:* Carve or press the plate.

*How to teach:* Carve or press the plate.

- **Q:** Why carve shallow first?
  **A:** You can always remove more, but can't add material back.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Proof
*Student:* Pull a proof print and adjust.

*How to teach:* Pull a proof; adjust.

- **Q:** What is a proof?
  **A:** A test print to check the image before printing the edition.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edition
*Student:* Print an edition of 5 prints, numbering and signing correctly.

*How to teach:* Print an edition of 5; number and sign.

- **Q:** How is an edition numbered and signed?
  **A:** In pencil below the image: edition number like 1/5 at left, title in center, signature (and date) at right.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Select the best print and critique.

*How to teach:* Select the best print; critique.

- **Q:** What makes a 'good' print in an edition?
  **A:** Even ink coverage, clean margins, consistent across copies.

**Assessment:** Print edition.

**Answer key:** Print edition: 4 = 5 consistent, clean prints with even inking and margins, correctly numbered (1/5–5/5), titled and signed in pencil; critique evaluates the best print; 3 = minor inconsistencies; 2 = uneven or unsigned; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Alcohol

**Prepare:** Computer; BAC factor chart; DMV handbook excerpt. Party scenario. Quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Believing you can 'sleep it off' safely after heavy drinking. Correct: BAC can keep rising; a person with alcohol poisoning may stop breathing or choke on vomit.
- Thinking cold showers or coffee sober you up. Correct: only time lowers BAC.

### Day 1 (Monday): Alcohol and the body
*Student:* Trace alcohol's path through the body and describe effects on each organ system.

*How to teach:* Trace: mouth → stomach (~20% absorbed) → small intestine (~80%) → bloodstream → brain, liver (metabolizes), heart.

- **Q:** Which organ metabolizes most alcohol?
  **A:** The liver.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): BAC factors
*Student:* Learn what affects blood alcohol concentration and why 'sobering up' cannot be sped up.

*How to teach:* Factors: amount, time, body size, sex, food, carbonation; liver processes about one standard drink per hour.

- **Q:** What is a standard drink?
  **A:** About 14 g of pure alcohol: 12 oz beer (5%), 5 oz wine (12%), 1.5 oz spirits (40%).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Alcohol poisoning
*Student:* Learn the signs of alcohol poisoning and the steps to take, including calling 911.

*How to teach:* Signs of alcohol poisoning: confusion, vomiting, seizures, slow/irregular breathing, blue-tinged or pale skin, low body temp, can't wake. Steps: call 911, don't leave alone, turn on side, don't give coffee or a cold shower.

- **Q:** Name the acronym for alcohol poisoning signs.
  **A:** One common one is PUBS (or CUPS): Puking while passed out, Unresponsive, Breathing slow/irregular, Skin cold/blue/clammy. Any sign = call 911.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Laws and consequences
*Student:* Research California's zero-tolerance law for drivers under 21 and other legal consequences.

*How to teach:* Under-21 driver limit 0.01%; adults 0.08%; minor in possession penalties; license suspension; social host liability.

- **Q:** What happens to a CA driver under 21 with BAC of 0.01% or more?
  **A:** One-year license suspension (zero-tolerance law), and higher BACs bring DUI charges.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Use the decision-making model on a party scenario involving alcohol and take a short quiz.

*How to teach:* Use DECIDE on the party scenario; give the quiz.

- **Q:** A friend passed out at a party and is breathing irregularly. What do you do?
  **A:** Call 911, stay with them, roll them onto their side, don't let them 'sleep it off.'

**Assessment:** Decision-making scenario and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: liver metabolizes alcohol; only time lowers BAC; standard drink = 14 g alcohol; BAC factors; alcohol poisoning signs and 911; CA under-21 limit 0.01% with 1-year suspension; adult 0.08%. Scenario: uses all DECIDE steps and chooses a safe, legal option.
