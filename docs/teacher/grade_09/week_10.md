# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 10

## English 9: Argument structure: "Letter from Birmingham Jail"

**Prepare:** Print 'A Call for Unity' (1963) and King's 'Letter from Birmingham Jail' (King Institute at Stanford has the text). Read both first; the Letter is long (about 7,000 words), so plan reading in thirds. Brief background: the 1963 Birmingham campaign against segregation; King was jailed on April 12, 1963, for violating an injunction against demonstrating.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking King rejects all law: he argues one must obey just laws and openly, lovingly, and with willingness to accept the penalty, break unjust ones.
- Seeing the Letter as purely emotional: it is tightly reasoned (logos), with ethos and pathos layered in.
- Missing the audience: King addresses the clergymen (and white moderates) respectfully while refuting them.

### Day 1 (Monday): The occasion
*Student:* Read the clergymen's "A Call for Unity" and summarize their objections; note the date and context of the Birmingham campaign.

*How to teach:* Read 'A Call for Unity'; your child summarizes the clergymen's objections and notes the context.

- **Q:** What do the clergymen call the demonstrations?
  **A:** 'Unwise and untimely'; they urged people to rely on the courts and negotiation and criticized 'outsiders' for leading them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): King's response, part 1
*Student:* Read the first third of the Letter; chart each criticism King answers and the reasoning he uses.

*How to teach:* Read the first third; chart each criticism and King's reasoning.

- **Q:** How does King answer the charge that he is an outsider?
  **A:** He was invited by an affiliate organization, and 'I am in Birmingham because injustice is here'; 'Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere.'
- **Q:** What are the four basic steps of a nonviolent campaign King lists?
  **A:** Collection of the facts to determine whether injustices exist; negotiation; self-purification; and direct action.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Just and unjust laws
*Student:* Read the middle section; explain King's definition of just vs. unjust laws in your own words with two quotations.

*How to teach:* Read the middle section; your child explains just vs. unjust laws with two quotations.

- **Q:** How does King define a just law and an unjust law?
  **A:** A just law 'squares with the moral law or the law of God'; an unjust law is 'out of harmony with the moral law.' Any law that uplifts human personality is just; any law that degrades it is unjust.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tone and appeals
*Student:* Finish the Letter; analyze how King balances patience and urgency, then evaluate his strongest piece of evidence.

*How to teach:* Finish the Letter; analyze how King balances patience and urgency; evaluate his strongest evidence.

- **Q:** What does King say about the word 'Wait'?
  **A:** That 'Wait' has almost always meant 'Never,' and that 'justice too long delayed is justice denied.'
- **Q:** Why is King 'gravely disappointed with the white moderate'?
  **A:** Because the moderate prefers order ('the absence of tension') to justice and tells Black citizens to wait for a 'more convenient season.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 1-page analysis: How does King refute the charge that his actions are "untimely"?

*How to teach:* Your child writes a one-page analysis: How does King refute the charge of 'untimely'?

- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence King uses to show the action is not untimely.
  **A:** Examples: Black Americans have waited more than 340 years for rights; the long sentence describing lynch mobs, police brutality, and explaining segregation to a child shows why waiting is unbearable.

**Assessment:** One-page argument analysis scored for tracing claims and evaluating evidence.

**Answer key:** One-page analysis rubric: 4 = identifies the clergymen's 'untimely' charge, traces King's counterargument ('Wait' means 'Never'; 340 years of waiting; the long sentence of grievances; freedom is never voluntarily given by the oppressor), evaluates why his evidence is persuasive, and quotes accurately; 3 = traces the argument with less evaluation; 2 = mostly summary; 1 = inaccurate or no evidence.

## Algebra I: Sequences as functions

**Prepare:** Gather about 60 toothpicks or square tiles. Prepare sequence lists and the Unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Using a_n = a₁ + nd instead of a₁ + (n − 1)d: check with n = 1, which must give a₁.
- Confusing the term number n with the term value a_n.
- Connecting sequence points with a line: sequences are only defined at whole-number term numbers, so the graph is separate dots.

### Day 1 (Monday): Visual patterns
*Student:* Build toothpick patterns and write how many toothpicks are in step n.

*How to teach:* Build squares in a row from toothpicks: 1 square = 4, 2 squares = 7, 3 = 10. Ask what is added each time and what is 'left over'. Your child builds, tables, and writes a rule for step n.

- **Q:** How many toothpicks for n squares in a row? How many for 20 squares?
  **A:** 3n + 1; for 20 squares, 61.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Arithmetic sequences
*Student:* Find common differences and write explicit formulas aₙ=a₁+(n−1)d for 10 sequences.

*How to teach:* Find the common difference and model a_n = a₁ + (n − 1)d. Your child writes explicit formulas for 10 sequences and finds a later term in each.

- **Q:** Write the explicit formula for 5, 9, 13, 17, … and find a₂₀.
  **A:** d = 4: a_n = 5 + 4(n − 1) = 4n + 1; a₂₀ = 81.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recursive formulas
*Student:* Write recursive definitions and convert between recursive and explicit forms.

*How to teach:* Show a recursive rule (start value + 'what to do to the previous term'). Convert both ways. Your child writes recursive definitions and converts to explicit form.

- **Q:** Write 5, 9, 13, … recursively.
  **A:** a₁ = 5; a_n = a_(n−1) + 4.
- **Q:** Convert a₁ = −2, a_n = a_(n−1) − 3 to explicit form.
  **A:** a_n = −2 − 3(n − 1) = 1 − 3n.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sequences as functions
*Student:* Graph sequences as discrete points and compare to linear functions.

*How to teach:* Plot 4n + 1 for n = 1..5 as dots and graph y = 4x + 1 in Desmos. Discuss: the slope equals d, and the sequence is the line restricted to whole numbers.

- **Q:** How is a_n = 4n + 1 related to y = 4x + 1?
  **A:** Same rule: the sequence is the linear function with domain restricted to positive integers; the common difference 4 is the slope.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on functions and sequences.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test (about 50 minutes). Grade with the key.

- **Q:** Is 3, 6, 12, 24 arithmetic?
  **A:** No — it has a common ratio (2), not a common difference.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample answers: 1) {(1,3),(2,3),(3,4)} is a function. 2) f(x) = x² − 3x: f(−2) = 10. 3) Domain and range of y = √x: x ≥ 0; y ≥ 0. 4) For a graph of a ball's height, name the maximum and intercepts (read from graph). 5) 7, 11, 15, … → a_n = 4n + 3; a₁₀ = 43. 6) Recursive form: a₁ = 7, a_n = a_(n−1) + 4. 7) 20, 17, 14, … → a_n = 23 − 3n; a₁₅ = −22. 8) A table vs. graph rate comparison — the larger change per unit input grows faster. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Biology – The Living Earth: The carbon cycle and Earth's spheres

**Prepare:** Print the Keeling curve data (NOAA Global Monitoring Lab, Mauna Loa monthly CO₂). Carbonated water, red cabbage indicator, cups. Reference values: atmosphere holds about 870 GtC; 1 ppm CO₂ ≈ 2.13 GtC; fossil fuels emit about 10 GtC per year. Prepare the Unit 2 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the seasonal wiggle is from human activity: it's from Northern Hemisphere plant growth (photosynthesis) in summer and decay/respiration in winter.
- Believing all emitted CO₂ stays in the air: roughly half is absorbed by oceans and land plants.
- Thinking the ocean sink is harmless: absorbed CO₂ forms carbonic acid, lowering ocean pH.

### Day 1 (Monday): Carbon cycle model
*Student:* Draw a carbon cycle model across biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere with flux arrows.

*How to teach:* Draw the carbon cycle across spheres with flux arrows (photosynthesis, respiration, combustion, ocean exchange, sedimentation).

- **Q:** Name two processes that move carbon from the biosphere to the atmosphere.
  **A:** Cellular respiration and decomposition (also burning/combustion of biomass).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Keeling curve
*Student:* Graph Mauna Loa CO2 data and explain seasonal wiggles with photosynthesis and respiration.

*How to teach:* Graph Mauna Loa data; explain the seasonal pattern.

- **Q:** Why does CO₂ fall each Northern Hemisphere summer?
  **A:** Plants in the Northern Hemisphere (which has more land) photosynthesize more, removing CO₂.
- **Q:** About how much has CO₂ risen since 1958?
  **A:** From about 315 ppm to over 420 ppm in recent years.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Quantitative model
*Student:* Use reservoir values (gigatons C) to calculate how burning fossil fuels changes atmospheric carbon.

*How to teach:* Use reservoir values to compute fossil fuel impact.

- **Q:** If about 10 GtC are emitted per year and about 45% stays in the air, how much does CO₂ rise per year (1 ppm ≈ 2.13 GtC)?
  **A:** 4.5 GtC ÷ 2.13 ≈ 2.1 ppm per year — close to the observed rate.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ocean sink
*Student:* Model ocean acidification with carbonated water and red cabbage indicator.

*How to teach:* Add carbonated water to cabbage indicator; compare to plain water.

- **Q:** What happens to indicator when CO₂-rich water is added, and what does it model?
  **A:** It shifts toward pink/red (more acidic) — modeling ocean acidification as oceans absorb CO₂.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test.

- **Q:** Which organelle performs photosynthesis?
  **A:** Chloroplast.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test sample answers: 1) Great Oxidation Event ~2.4 bya by cyanobacteria. 2) Photosynthesis and respiration equations. 3) Leaf disks float due to O₂. 4) Light reactions/Calvin cycle locations. 5) Fermentation vs. aerobic ATP. 6) Macromolecule monomers. 7) Carbon cycle processes. 8) Keeling curve trend and seasonal cycle. 9) Ocean acidification. 10) Calculation: 6 GtC added to air ≈ 2.8 ppm. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Culture, language, and religion

**Prepare:** Blank world maps for language families and religions; arrows/colored pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing language family with language: Indo-European is a family containing English, Spanish, Hindi, Russian, and more.
- Thinking religions stayed in their hearths: universalizing religions (Christianity, Islam, Buddhism) spread widely; ethnic religions (Hinduism, Judaism) are more concentrated.
- Treating culture as fixed: cultures change through diffusion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Culture traits
*Student:* Define culture and identify traits of your own culture.

*How to teach:* Define culture and list traits of your own.

- **Q:** What is culture?
  **A:** The shared beliefs, practices, language, foods, arts, and values of a group, learned and passed on.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Languages
*Student:* Color a world map of the major language families (Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, Niger-Congo, Afro-Asiatic) and explain how Spanish spread to the Americas.

*How to teach:* Map language families; explain Spanish in the Americas.

- **Q:** Which language family has the most speakers?
  **A:** Indo-European.
- **Q:** How did Spanish spread to the Americas?
  **A:** Through Spanish conquest and colonization beginning in the late 1400s and 1500s (relocation diffusion).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Religions
*Student:* Map the hearths and spread of Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Christianity, and Islam with arrows showing diffusion routes.

*How to teach:* Map religious hearths and diffusion.

- **Q:** Where are the hearths of Islam and Buddhism?
  **A:** Islam: the Arabian Peninsula (Mecca and Medina), 7th century CE. Buddhism: northeastern India/Nepal region, around the 5th century BCE.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Diffusion
*Student:* Trace the diffusion of a food (e.g., chili peppers via the Columbian Exchange) or music genre (hip-hop) on a world map with dates.

*How to teach:* Trace diffusion of chili peppers or hip-hop.

- **Q:** How did chili peppers reach India and Thailand?
  **A:** Native to the Americas, they were carried by Portuguese and Spanish traders after 1492 (the Columbian Exchange) and spread across Asia in the 1500s.
- **Q:** Where and when did hip-hop begin?
  **A:** The Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on cultural geography.

*How to teach:* Cultural geography quiz.

- **Q:** What type of diffusion spreads through direct contact, like a wave?
  **A:** Contagious diffusion (a type of expansion diffusion).

**Assessment:** Cultural geography quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Culture definition. 2) Indo-European largest family. 3) Hearths of five religions (Hinduism — Indus/Ganges region; Judaism — Levant; Christianity — Levant; Islam — Arabia; Buddhism — NE India). 4) Universalizing vs. ethnic religions. 5) Relocation vs. expansion diffusion. 6) Columbian Exchange example.

## Spanish II: Día de los Muertos and fiestas: irregular preterite

**Prepare:** Gather ofrenda materials (photo, candles or LED candles, paper marigolds or cempasúchil images, papel picado, small foods) or a slide template. Preview a respectful reading on Día de los Muertos. Chart the irregular preterites: ser/ir (fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron), hacer (hice, hiciste, hizo...), tener (tuve...), estar (estuve...), poder (pude...).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Día de los Muertos is 'Mexican Halloween': it is a celebration (Nov. 1–2) honoring and remembering loved ones who have died, not a scary holiday.
- Adding accents to irregular preterites (*tuvó, *hizó): the irregular 'stem' preterites have no written accents — tuve, tuvo, hice, hizo.
- Confusing fui = 'I went' and 'I was': ser and ir share preterite forms; context decides.

### Day 1 (Monday): Irregular preterite
*Student:* Learn ser/ir, hacer, tener, estar, poder in the preterite.

*How to teach:* Teach the irregulars as patterns (hacer: hic/hiz-, tener: tuv-, estar: estuv-, poder: pud-, all with endings -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron). Drill with quick oral prompts.

- **Q:** Translate: 'We went to the cemetery.'
  **A:** Fuimos al cementerio.
- **Q:** Conjugate hacer for él.
  **A:** hizo (z keeps the soft sound before o)

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Día de los Muertos
*Student:* Read about Día de los Muertos traditions and their meanings.

*How to teach:* Read about traditions: ofrendas, cempasúchil (marigolds whose color and scent guide spirits), pan de muerto, calaveras, visits to graves. Your child lists five traditions and their meanings.

- **Q:** Why are marigolds (cempasúchil) used?
  **A:** Their bright color and scent are believed to guide the spirits of loved ones back to the ofrenda.
- **Q:** When is Día de los Muertos celebrated?
  **A:** November 1 and 2 (Nov. 1 often honors children, Nov. 2 adults).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ofrenda
*Student:* Build a small ofrenda (or digital one) honoring a family member or historical figure, labeled in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child builds a small or digital ofrenda for a relative or a historical figure (e.g., Frida Kahlo) and labels each item in Spanish with its meaning.

- **Q:** What does the water on an ofrenda represent?
  **A:** It quenches the thirst of the spirits after their long journey.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Narration
*Student:* Write a paragraph describing a holiday you celebrated using irregular preterite.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph about a holiday they celebrated (Thanksgiving, a birthday, New Year) using at least 6 irregular preterites (fui, hice, tuve, estuve, pude).

- **Q:** Translate: 'I was at my grandmother's house and we made tamales.'
  **A:** Estuve en la casa de mi abuela e hicimos tamales.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Irregular preterite quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the irregular preterite quiz and check the ofrenda labels with the rubric.

- **Q:** Fill in: Ellos no ___ (poder) venir porque ___ (tener) que trabajar.
  **A:** pudieron; tuvieron

**Assessment:** Irregular quiz and ofrenda labels.

**Answer key:** Irregular quiz sample key: ir/yo — fui; ser/ella — fue; hacer/él — hizo; hacer/nosotros — hicimos; tener/tú — tuviste; estar/ellos — estuvieron; poder/yo — pude; tener/nosotros — tuvimos. Ofrenda label rubric: 4 = 8+ items labeled correctly in Spanish (la vela, el cempasúchil, el pan de muerto, el papel picado, la foto, el agua, la calavera de azúcar) with a sentence explaining the meaning of each; 3 = 6–7; 2 = 4–5; 1 = fewer.

## Visual Art I: Watercolor techniques

**Prepare:** Watercolor set, watercolor paper (140 lb), salt, white crayon, paper towels, two water jars. Safety: don't pour pigment water down storm drains; dispose in a sink or let it evaporate and trash solids.

**Watch for:**
- Overworking wet paint. Correct: let layers dry before glazing; work quickly in washes.
- Using white paint for lights. Correct: in transparent watercolor, the paper is the white — plan and save it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Material safety
*Student:* Learn safe handling and cleanup of paints and brushes, including not pouring pigments down storm drains.

*How to teach:* Safe handling and cleanup.

- **Q:** Why not pour paint water into a storm drain?
  **A:** Storm drains flow untreated into creeks and the ocean; pigments can pollute water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Technique sampler
*Student:* Paint a sampler grid of wet-on-wet, gradient wash, glazing, salt, lifting, and wax resist.

*How to teach:* Paint the sampler grid of six techniques.

- **Q:** What is glazing?
  **A:** Painting a transparent layer over a dry layer to change color or value.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sky study
*Student:* Paint three sky studies at different times of day.

*How to teach:* Three sky studies.

- **Q:** What technique makes soft clouds?
  **A:** Wet-on-wet, or lifting color with a tissue while wet.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Botanical
*Student:* Paint a leaf or flower from observation with glazing.

*How to teach:* Botanical study with glazing.

- **Q:** How do you keep highlights white?
  **A:** Paint around them, use wax resist or lift.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label the sampler and choose the technique you will use in the landscape.

*How to teach:* Label the sampler; choose a technique.

- **Q:** What does salt do on wet watercolor?
  **A:** It absorbs pigment and water, creating starburst textures.

**Assessment:** Technique sampler.

**Answer key:** Technique sampler: 6 labeled techniques each clearly demonstrated (wet-on-wet soft bleeds, graded wash smooth, glazing visible layers, salt texture, lifting lights, wax resist). 6/6 proficient.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Badminton strategy and games

**Prepare:** Badminton set; court diagram showing singles (long and narrow) and doubles (short and wide service court; full width in play) boundaries.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only the server scores. Correct: badminton uses rally scoring — a point every rally.
- Both doubles partners chasing the same shuttle. Correct: side-by-side on defense, front-back on attack; call 'mine.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules and scoring
*Student:* Learn rally scoring and court boundaries; play practice games.

*How to teach:* Teach rally scoring: games to 21, win by 2, cap at 30; best of three games. Server serves from right court when score is even, left when odd.

- **Q:** Score is 20-20. How does the game end?
  **A:** A player must win by 2 (e.g., 22-20); if it reaches 29-29, the next point (30) wins.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Singles strategy
*Student:* Practice moving opponents with clears and drops.

*How to teach:* Move opponents with clears and drops.

- **Q:** Why alternate clears and drops?
  **A:** It forces the opponent to cover the full length of the court, tiring them and creating openings.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Smash
*Student:* Learn the smash and when to use it.

*How to teach:* Teach the smash (steep downward overhead).

- **Q:** When should you smash?
  **A:** When the shuttle is high and short (in the front-mid court), giving a steep angle downward.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Doubles
*Student:* Play doubles with family, practicing side-by-side and front-back formation.

*How to teach:* Doubles formations: side-by-side defending, front-back attacking.

- **Q:** When does a doubles team go front-back?
  **A:** When attacking (after a smash or drop), with the back player hitting down and the front player covering the net.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play a round-robin and self-evaluate sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Round-robin; self-evaluate sportsmanship.

- **Q:** Name two sportsmanship behaviors.
  **A:** Calling lines honestly, shaking hands, encouraging partners, accepting calls.

**Assessment:** Game play rubric including sportsmanship.

**Answer key:** Game play rubric: Skills (clears, drops, serve), Strategy (moves opponent, uses formations), Rules/scoring (keeps score correctly), Sportsmanship (fair calls, respect). Each 1–4; proficient = 3+ in every row.

## Health & Wellness: Stress and coping

**Prepare:** Timer, journal, and a fight-or-flight diagram. Find guided scripts for box breathing, progressive muscle relaxation and 5-4-3-2-1 grounding. Write a short quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all stress is bad. Correct: eustress (positive stress) motivates; chronic distress harms health.
- Trying to control everything. Correct: focus effort on controllable stressors; use coping for uncontrollable ones.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stress response
*Student:* Diagram the fight-or-flight response and label the hormones and body changes involved.

*How to teach:* Diagram: stressor → hypothalamus → adrenal glands release adrenaline (epinephrine) and cortisol → heart rate, breathing, blood pressure up, digestion slows, muscles tense.

- **Q:** Which two hormones are released in the stress response?
  **A:** Adrenaline (epinephrine) and cortisol.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stressor inventory
*Student:* List your current stressors, rate each 1–5 and sort them into things you can and cannot control.

*How to teach:* List and rate stressors; sort controllable vs. not.

- **Q:** Is 'a big test Friday' controllable?
  **A:** Partly — you can't change the test, but you can control studying, sleep and preparation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Relaxation practice
*Student:* Practice box breathing, progressive muscle relaxation and a 5-4-3-2-1 grounding exercise, rating each afterward.

*How to teach:* Practice each technique (box breathing 4-4-4-4) and rate them.

- **Q:** Describe box breathing.
  **A:** Inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4; repeat several times.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Time management
*Student:* Build a weekly schedule that balances school, activities, sleep and downtime.

*How to teach:* Build a balanced weekly schedule.

- **Q:** Why schedule downtime?
  **A:** Rest and fun reduce stress and prevent burnout.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Create a personal stress-management toolkit card with five strategies and take a short quiz.

*How to teach:* Make the toolkit card; give the quiz.

- **Q:** What's the difference between eustress and distress?
  **A:** Eustress is positive, motivating stress (e.g., before a game); distress is negative stress that feels overwhelming and can harm health.

**Assessment:** Stress toolkit card and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) stressor definition 2) adrenaline and cortisol 3) fight-or-flight body changes 4) eustress vs distress 5) chronic stress effects: sleep problems, weakened immunity, headaches 6) box breathing steps 7) 5-4-3-2-1 = 5 things you see, 4 touch, 3 hear, 2 smell, 1 taste. Toolkit: 5 healthy strategies with when to use each.
