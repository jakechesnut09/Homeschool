# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 28

## English 9: Historical context and Maycomb: To Kill a Mockingbird, Chapters 1–6

**Prepare:** Get To Kill a Mockingbird (library). Read the whole novel before starting or stay ahead. Parental note: the novel includes racial slurs, racism, and a rape accusation at its center; discuss why Lee depicts them and how you'll handle slurs when reading aloud. Gather Library of Congress Depression photos and a Scottsboro Boys overview (1931 case of nine Black teenagers falsely accused in Alabama).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing the adult narrator with child Scout: the narrator is adult Jean Louise looking back with understanding the child lacks.
- Treating Boo Radley rumors as facts: they are town gossip and children's imagination.
- Seeing setting as background only: Maycomb's poverty, racism, and class structure drive the plot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Historical context
*Student:* Study Library of Congress Depression-era photos and a Scottsboro trial overview; list three facts that will help you read Maycomb.

*How to teach:* Study photos and the Scottsboro overview; list three facts.

- **Q:** What happened in the Scottsboro case?
  **A:** In 1931, nine Black teenagers in Alabama were falsely accused of raping two white women; despite weak evidence, all-white juries convicted them — a case that exposed racial injustice in the South.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 1–3
*Student:* Read; analyze how adult Scout's narration differs from child Scout's perspective.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 1–3; analyze adult vs. child narration.

- **Q:** What lesson does Atticus teach Scout in Chapter 3?
  **A:** 'You never really understand a person until you consider things from his point of view… until you climb into his skin and walk around in it.'
- **Q:** What happens with Walter Cunningham at lunch?
  **A:** Scout criticizes him for pouring syrup on his food; Calpurnia scolds her that guests must be treated with respect.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chapters 4–6
*Student:* Read; track the children's changing view of Boo Radley.

*How to teach:* Read Chapters 4–6; track changing views of Boo.

- **Q:** What do the children find in the knot-hole of the tree?
  **A:** Gum, then two Indian-head pennies (more gifts follow later).
- **Q:** What happens to Jem's pants?
  **A:** He loses them on the Radley fence; when he returns, they've been mended and folded over the fence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Accounts in different media
*Student:* Compare a newspaper account and a documentary clip of the Scottsboro case; note what each emphasizes.

*How to teach:* Compare a newspaper account and a documentary clip of Scottsboro; note emphases.

- **Q:** Why might a 1931 Southern newspaper and a modern documentary tell the Scottsboro story differently?
  **A:** Different audiences, purposes, and time periods — the newspaper may reflect contemporary prejudices; the documentary has historical hindsight and testimony.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on Chapters 1–6 and a setting analysis paragraph.

*How to teach:* Quiz on Chapters 1–6; setting paragraph.

- **Q:** How does Scout describe Maycomb?
  **A:** As 'a tired old town' — hot, slow, and poor during the Depression.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and setting paragraph.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Narrator: Scout (Jean Louise Finch). 2) Dill visits each summer. 3) Boo (Arthur) Radley. 4) Miss Caroline is the new teacher. 5) Atticus's 'climb into his skin' lesson. 6) Gifts in the knot-hole. 7) Jem's pants mended. 8) Miss Maudie is the neighbor who explains Boo's family. Setting paragraph: Paragraph rubric: 4 = arguable claim, two well-chosen quotations embedded and cited, commentary that explains HOW the evidence proves the claim; 3 = claim and evidence with thinner commentary; 2 = summary instead of analysis or only one piece of evidence; 1 = off-topic or no evidence.

## Algebra I: Graphing quadratic functions

**Prepare:** Graph paper, Desmos (type y = a(x − h)² + k and add sliders).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking (x − 3)² shifts left: (x − h)² shifts RIGHT by h.
- Using x = b/2a instead of x = −b/(2a).
- Forgetting that a < 0 opens downward (a maximum, not a minimum).

### Day 1 (Monday): Exploring parabolas
*Student:* Graph y=x² and use Desmos sliders to describe the effects of a, h, and k.

*How to teach:* Graph y = x² from a table, then use sliders for a, h, k. Your child writes the effect of each.

- **Q:** Describe y = −2(x − 3)² + 1 compared to y = x².
  **A:** Opens down, narrower (stretch by 2), vertex shifted to (3, 1).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Key features
*Student:* Identify vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, and max/min for 10 parabolas.

*How to teach:* Model labeling vertex, axis, intercepts, max/min. Your child labels 10 parabolas.

- **Q:** For y = x² − 6x + 8, give the vertex, axis of symmetry, x-intercepts, and y-intercept.
  **A:** Vertex (3, −1); axis x = 3; x-intercepts 2 and 4; y-intercept 8; minimum −1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Standard form
*Student:* Graph y=ax²+bx+c by finding the vertex with x=−b/2a and building a symmetric table for 8 functions.

*How to teach:* Find the vertex with x = −b/(2a), then build a symmetric table around it.

- **Q:** Find the vertex of y = 2x² − 8x + 3.
  **A:** x = 8/4 = 2; y = 8 − 16 + 3 = −5 → (2, −5).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Transformations
*Student:* Write equations of transformed parabolas from graphs.

*How to teach:* Read the vertex from a graph, then use another point to find a.

- **Q:** A parabola has vertex (−1, 4) and passes through (0, 2). Write its equation.
  **A:** y = a(x + 1)² + 4; 2 = a + 4 → a = −2: y = −2(x + 1)² + 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on parabola features.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key.

- **Q:** Does y = −x² + 5 have a max or min? Value?
  **A:** Maximum of 5.

**Assessment:** Parabola quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) y = (x − 2)² − 3 → vertex (2, −3), opens up, min −3. 2) y = −x² + 4x → vertex (2, 4), x-intercepts 0 and 4. 3) y = x² + 2x − 3 → vertex (−1, −4), zeros −3 and 1, y-int −3. 4) Axis of symmetry of y = 3x² − 12x + 1 → x = 2. 5) Equation with vertex (1, −2) through (3, 6): y = 2(x − 1)² − 2.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Homeostasis and feedback

**Prepare:** Thermometer, stopwatch, safe exercise space (jumping jacks or stairs). Check with your child's doctor if any health condition limits exercise.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking homeostasis means nothing changes: values fluctuate around a set point.
- Confusing negative and positive feedback: negative feedback reverses change; positive feedback amplifies it.
- Believing insulin raises blood sugar: insulin lowers it; glucagon raises it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Feedback loops
*Student:* Diagram negative feedback for body temperature and blood glucose.

*How to teach:* Diagram negative feedback for temperature and glucose.

- **Q:** Describe how the body responds when blood glucose rises after a meal.
  **A:** The pancreas releases insulin; cells take up glucose and the liver stores it as glycogen, lowering blood glucose back to set point.
- **Q:** How does the body cool when temperature rises?
  **A:** Sweating and dilating skin blood vessels release heat.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Exercise investigation
*Student:* Measure heart rate and breathing rate before, during, and after exercise.

*How to teach:* Measure heart and breathing rates at rest, during, and after exercise (every minute for 5 minutes after).

- **Q:** Predict heart rate during and after exercise.
  **A:** It rises during exercise and gradually returns toward resting rate afterward.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data analysis
*Student:* Graph results and explain how feedback returns values toward set point.

*How to teach:* Graph results; explain return toward set point.

- **Q:** Why does heart rate return to normal after exercise?
  **A:** Negative feedback: as CO₂ and O₂ demand return to normal, the nervous system slows heart and breathing rates.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Positive feedback
*Student:* Compare positive feedback in childbirth and blood clotting with negative feedback in a diagram.

*How to teach:* Compare positive and negative feedback.

- **Q:** Give an example of positive feedback.
  **A:** Childbirth: contractions trigger more oxytocin, causing stronger contractions until birth (also blood clotting).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a lab report on the exercise investigation.

*How to teach:* Write the lab report.

- **Q:** What is the set point for human body temperature?
  **A:** About 37°C (98.6°F).

**Assessment:** Homeostasis lab report.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Proficient analysis: identifies independent variable (activity level/time), dependent variables (heart and breathing rates), graphs data, explains the rise as increased demand for O₂ and CO₂ removal, and the recovery as negative feedback restoring set points.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Borders and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

**Prepare:** Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo (National Archives; Articles VIII and IX); Land Act of 1851 summary; excerpt from María Amparo Ruiz de Burton's The Squatter and the Don (1885); historical border maps (1848, 1853 Gadsden Purchase, today).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Mexicans 'crossed the border' into California: as the saying goes, the border crossed them in 1848.
- Assuming treaty promises were kept: the Land Act's costly legal process led many Californios to lose land.
- Viewing Californios as one group: they ranged from elite rancheros to laborers.

### Day 1 (Monday): The Treaty
*Student:* Read Articles VIII and IX and explain promised rights.

*How to teach:* Read Articles VIII and IX.

- **Q:** What did Article VIII promise Mexicans living in the ceded territory?
  **A:** They could stay or leave, keep their property, and choose U.S. citizenship (automatically after one year if they didn't declare Mexican citizenship); property was to be 'inviolably respected.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Californios
*Student:* Analyze how the Land Act of 1851 led to loss of Californio land.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Land Act of 1851.

- **Q:** How did the Land Act of 1851 cause Californios to lose land?
  **A:** It required landowners to prove their titles before a federal commission; cases took years (often over a decade), legal fees and squatters forced many to sell or lose land.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Perspective
*Student:* Read an excerpt from María Amparo Ruiz de Burton's The Squatter and the Don.

*How to teach:* Read the Ruiz de Burton excerpt.

- **Q:** What perspective does The Squatter and the Don present?
  **A:** A Californio family's (the Alamars') view of losing their rancho to American squatters and unjust laws — written by a Mexican American woman in English.

### Day 4 (Thursday): The border
*Student:* Trace the border's history from 1848 to today on maps.

*How to teach:* Trace the border on maps 1848–today.

- **Q:** What changed the border in 1853?
  **A:** The Gadsden Purchase added southern Arizona and southwestern New Mexico.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a one-page analysis comparing the Treaty's promises of property rights with the outcomes of the Land Act of 1851.

*How to teach:* One-page analysis comparing promises and outcomes.

- **Q:** When was the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo signed?
  **A:** February 2, 1848.

**Assessment:** Treaty analysis.

**Answer key:** Treaty analysis rubric: 4 = quotes Articles VIII/IX accurately, explains the Land Act process with specifics, gives examples of outcomes (land loss, legal costs, squatters), evaluates the gap between promise and reality; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = summary; 1 = inaccurate. Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.

## Spanish II: El medio ambiente: problems and solutions

**Prepare:** Gather Spanish articles on Amazon deforestation and Mexico City air quality (BBC Mundo, El País) and set out household bins. Prepare a short Spanish news clip on drought or air quality. Choose an unseen environmental article for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Using 'reciclar' as a noun: el reciclaje is the noun; reciclar is the verb.
- Assuming Mexico City's smog comes only from cars: its high altitude basin surrounded by mountains traps pollution, combined with vehicles and industry.

### Day 1 (Monday): Environmental vocabulary
*Student:* Learn 25 environment terms with images and use each in a sentence about California.

*How to teach:* Teach 25 terms with images (la contaminación, la deforestación, la sequía, reciclar, proteger, los recursos naturales, la basura, el calentamiento global). Your child writes one sentence about California for each.

- **Q:** Translate: 'California has a lot of drought.'
  **A:** En California hay mucha sequía.
- **Q:** What does 'proteger los recursos naturales' mean?
  **A:** To protect natural resources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Amazon reading
*Student:* Read a Spanish article about Amazon deforestation and summarize causes and effects in a chart.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the Amazon article and fills a cause/effect chart.

- **Q:** Name two causes of Amazon deforestation.
  **A:** Cattle ranching (la ganadería), agriculture such as soy, logging, mining, and fires/clearing for farmland.
- **Q:** Name one effect.
  **A:** Loss of biodiversity and habitat; more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere; harm to Indigenous communities.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recycling audit
*Student:* Audit one day of household trash and recycling and write a short report in Spanish with quantities.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts one day's trash and recycling, counts items, and writes a short report in Spanish.

- **Q:** Translate: 'We threw away eight plastic bottles.'
  **A:** Tiramos ocho botellas de plástico.

### Day 4 (Thursday): City comparison
*Student:* Compare air quality and drought issues in Mexico City and Los Angeles using a Spanish news clip.

*How to teach:* Watch the news clip; your child compares Mexico City and Los Angeles (both basins surrounded by mountains, with car-related smog and water stress).

- **Q:** Why do Mexico City and Los Angeles both have smog problems?
  **A:** Both sit in basins surrounded by mountains that trap polluted air, and both have heavy vehicle traffic.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Reading comprehension check on an unseen environmental article.

*How to teach:* Your child reads an unseen article and answers comprehension questions.

- **Q:** What is 'la sequía'?
  **A:** Drought.

**Assessment:** Recycling report and reading check.

**Answer key:** Recycling report rubric: 4 = quantities for each category (Tiramos 12 botellas de plástico...), a conclusion, and 2 recommendations in Spanish; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = incomplete data; 1 = mostly English. Reading check: answers must cite the unseen article's main idea, one cause, one effect, and one solution.

## Visual Art I: Photography: composition and light

**Prepare:** Phone or digital camera; free editing app (Snapseed, built-in Photos). Images of Dorothea Lange's 'Migrant Mother' (1936) and Ansel Adams's Yosemite photographs.

**Watch for:**
- Always shooting at noon. Correct: golden hour (just after sunrise and before sunset) gives warm, soft, directional light.
- Over-editing. Correct: subtle adjustments; keep the image honest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Photo history
*Student:* Compare Dorothea Lange's "Migrant Mother" and Ansel Adams's Yosemite photographs; note composition and purpose.

*How to teach:* Compare Lange and Adams.

- **Q:** How did Adams's purpose differ from Lange's?
  **A:** Lange documented human suffering to prompt social aid; Adams celebrated the grandeur of wilderness and supported its preservation, using precise black-and-white tonal control (Zone System).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Composition challenge
*Student:* Shoot 10 photos each demonstrating rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, symmetry, and pattern.

*How to teach:* Shoot 10 photos each technique.

- **Q:** What is symmetry in photography?
  **A:** A balanced composition where both halves mirror each other.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Light study
*Student:* Photograph the same object at morning, noon, and golden hour and describe the differences.

*How to teach:* Photograph one object at three times; describe.

- **Q:** How does noon light look compared with golden hour?
  **A:** Noon: harsh, overhead, short dark shadows, high contrast. Golden hour: warm, soft, long shadows.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Edit
*Student:* Edit your best five photos for crop, exposure, and black-and-white conversion in a free editing app.

*How to teach:* Edit best five: crop, exposure, black-and-white.

- **Q:** Why convert to black and white?
  **A:** It emphasizes value, shape, texture and contrast over color.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present a 5-photo contact sheet labeled with the technique each shows.

*How to teach:* Present a 5-photo contact sheet.

- **Q:** What is a contact sheet?
  **A:** A sheet of small thumbnail images for reviewing and selecting photos.

**Assessment:** Composition contact sheet.

**Answer key:** Contact sheet: 4 = five photos each clearly labeled and demonstrating a different technique (rule of thirds, leading lines, framing, symmetry, pattern), thoughtful edits, light study description; 3 = 4 clear; 2 = techniques unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Online safety and digital wellness

**Prepare:** Phone and computer; NCMEC Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) and CyberTipline info; a grooming case study. Prepare a 3-scenario quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking privacy settings make posts private forever. Correct: anything posted can be screenshotted and shared.
- Believing sextortion victims should pay. Correct: don't pay; save evidence, stop contact, tell an adult and report.

### Day 1 (Monday): Digital footprint
*Student:* Search your own name and review what's public; adjust privacy settings.

*How to teach:* Search the child's name together; review and tighten privacy settings.

- **Q:** What is a digital footprint?
  **A:** The trail of data you leave online — posts, photos, comments, searches — which can be permanent and public.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sextortion
*Student:* Learn what sextortion is and how to get help (Take It Down, NCMEC).

*How to teach:* Explain sextortion and steps: stop responding, don't pay, save evidence, block, tell an adult, report to CyberTipline, use Take It Down.

- **Q:** What does Take It Down do?
  **A:** A free NCMEC service that helps remove or stop the online sharing of nude or explicit images taken of someone under 18.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Online predators
*Student:* Analyze a case study of online grooming, list the tactics used at each stage, and practice blocking and reporting on two apps you use.

*How to teach:* Analyze the case study stages (targeting, trust, filling a need, isolation, sexualization, control) and practice blocking/reporting.

- **Q:** What are early signs of online grooming?
  **A:** Excessive flattery, gifts, asking for secrecy, moving to private apps, asking personal questions, then requesting photos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Digital wellness
*Student:* Create a personal tech-use plan with phone-free times, notification settings and a bedtime device rule, and share it with a parent.

*How to teach:* Create a tech-use plan.

- **Q:** Name one notification setting that improves wellbeing.
  **A:** Turn off non-essential notifications or use Do Not Disturb/Focus at night.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a digital safety scenario quiz (phishing message, sextortion threat, oversharing post) explaining the safest response for each.

*How to teach:* Give the scenario quiz.

- **Q:** How can you spot a phishing message?
  **A:** Urgent threats, unfamiliar sender, misspellings, requests for passwords or money, suspicious links.

**Assessment:** Digital safety quiz.

**Answer key:** Scenario quiz key: phishing — don't click, verify sender, report/delete; sextortion — don't pay or respond, save evidence, block, tell adult, report to NCMEC/Take It Down; oversharing post — remove location/school info, adjust privacy. Proficient: safest response for all three with reasons.
