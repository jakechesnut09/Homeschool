# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 27

## English 9: Romeo and Juliet argument essay and scene performance

**Prepare:** Full R&J text; simple props. Prepare the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Citing Shakespeare by page: cite act.scene.line, e.g., (3.1.94).
- Ignoring counterclaims: acknowledge another character's responsibility.
- Memorizing without understanding: paraphrase lines before performing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Essay plan
*Student:* Choose who bears most responsibility for the deaths and outline a claim, reasons, and counterclaim.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses who bears most responsibility and outlines claim, reasons, counterclaim.

- **Q:** Give a reason the Friar bears responsibility.
  **A:** He secretly marries them and devises the risky potion plan, then flees the tomb, leaving Juliet alone.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the argument essay with at least five quotations cited by act, scene, and line.

*How to teach:* Draft with at least five quotations cited by act, scene, and line.

- **Q:** Cite 'These violent delights have violent ends' correctly.
  **A:** (2.6.9) — act 2, scene 6, line 9 (Folger line numbers may vary slightly).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for counterclaim strength and edit quotation punctuation.

*How to teach:* Revise counterclaims; edit quotation punctuation (slashes for line breaks).

- **Q:** How do you quote two lines of verse within a sentence?
  **A:** Use a slash with spaces: 'It is too rash, too unadvised, too sudden, / Too like the lightning' (2.2.125–126; line numbers vary slightly by edition).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse a scene
*Student:* Memorize 15–20 lines from a scene; plan blocking and vocal choices.

*How to teach:* Memorize 15–20 lines; plan blocking and vocal choices.

- **Q:** What is blocking?
  **A:** Planned movement and positions of actors on stage.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the scene (live or recorded), submit the essay, and take the Unit 6 test.

*How to teach:* Performance, essay submission, Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** Who says 'A plague o' both your houses!'?
  **A:** Mercutio.

**Assessment:** Argument essay, scene performance rubric, Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = precise, arguable thesis; logically ordered body paragraphs each with a topic sentence, embedded quotations cited correctly, and commentary that explains significance; effective transitions; conclusion extends the idea; few errors. 3 = clear thesis, adequate evidence and explanation, some summary, minor errors. 2 = vague thesis, evidence thin or poorly explained, organization uneven. 1 = no clear thesis, little evidence, errors interfere. Performance rubric: memorized (few prompts), clear delivery, vocal expression matches meaning, purposeful movement. Unit 6 test sample answers: Prologue info; Queen Mab speaker (Mercutio); 'Wisely and slow' (Friar); Mercutio's curse; Juliet's oxymorons; potion plan; Friar John's quarantine; dramatic irony in 4.5; ending reconciliation.

## Algebra I: Factoring practice and Unit 8 test

**Prepare:** Have your child draw a factoring flowchart (GCF? → number of terms → difference of squares / perfect square / trinomial / grouping → factor further?). Unit 8 review and test.

**Watch for:**
- Stopping too early: x⁴ − 16 = (x² + 4)(x² − 4) is not finished — x² − 4 factors further.
- Not seeing structure: (x + 2)² − 25 is a difference of squares with a = x + 2.
- Dropping the GCF from the final answer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Factoring flowchart
*Student:* Build a decision flowchart and factor 20 mixed polynomials completely.

*How to teach:* Your child builds the flowchart and factors 20 mixed polynomials completely, checking by multiplying.

- **Q:** Factor 2x³ − 18x completely.
  **A:** 2x(x + 3)(x − 3).
- **Q:** Factor 3x² + 12x + 12 completely.
  **A:** 3(x + 2)².

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Structure
*Student:* Factor expressions like 4x²−9y² and (x+2)²−25 by seeing them as patterns.

*How to teach:* Treat chunks as single quantities. Your child factors structural expressions.

- **Q:** Factor 4x² − 9y².
  **A:** (2x − 3y)(2x + 3y).
- **Q:** Factor (x + 2)² − 25.
  **A:** (x + 2 − 5)(x + 2 + 5) = (x − 3)(x + 7).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Area applications
*Student:* Write factored expressions for areas of rectangles and borders.

*How to teach:* Model: a rectangle's area is x² + 8x + 15, so its sides could be (x + 3) and (x + 5). Border problem: a 10 by 8 garden with a path of width x has total area (10 + 2x)(8 + 2x).

- **Q:** A rectangle has area x² + 8x + 15. Find expressions for its sides.
  **A:** x + 3 and x + 5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Complete a mixed Unit 8 review.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the Unit 8 review; correct together.

- **Q:** Multiply (x − 4)(2x + 3).
  **A:** 2x² − 5x − 12.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test (about 50 minutes).

- **Q:** Factor x² − 8x + 16.
  **A:** (x − 4)².

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test sample answers: 1) (3x² − x + 4) − (x² + 2x − 1) = 2x² − 3x + 5. 2) (x + 6)(x − 2) = x² + 4x − 12. 3) (2x − 3)² = 4x² − 12x + 9. 4) 12x²y − 18xy = 6xy(2x − 3). 5) x² + 2x − 24 = (x + 6)(x − 4). 6) 4x² + 4x − 3 = (2x − 1)(2x + 3). 7) 25x² − 1 = (5x − 1)(5x + 1). 8) x³ − 9x = x(x − 3)(x + 3). 9) (x − 1)² − 16 = (x − 5)(x + 3). Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Levels of organization and body systems

**Prepare:** Store-bought chicken or pig heart, dissection scissors, gloves, tray; wash surfaces and hands. Celery stalks with leaves, food coloring, cups.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking blood is blue in veins: deoxygenated blood is dark red.
- Believing body systems work alone: e.g., exercise needs muscular, circulatory, respiratory, and nervous systems together.
- Mixing up xylem and phloem: xylem carries water up from roots; phloem carries sugars.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hierarchy
*Student:* Build a model from cell to organism using the digestive system.

*How to teach:* Build a cell → tissue → organ → system → organism model using digestion.

- **Q:** Give the levels of organization for the stomach.
  **A:** Smooth muscle cell → muscle tissue → stomach (organ) → digestive system → organism.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Systems interaction
*Student:* Trace how circulatory, respiratory, and muscular systems interact during exercise.

*How to teach:* Trace interactions during exercise.

- **Q:** How do the respiratory and circulatory systems work together during exercise?
  **A:** Breathing rate increases to bring in more O₂ and remove CO₂; heart rate increases to deliver O₂ to muscles and carry CO₂ to lungs.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Heart dissection
*Student:* Dissect a store-bought chicken or pig heart and identify chambers.

*How to teach:* Dissect the heart; identify chambers.

- **Q:** Which chamber has the thickest wall and why?
  **A:** The left ventricle — it pumps blood to the entire body.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plant systems
*Student:* Compare plant transport systems with celery and food coloring.

*How to teach:* Put celery in colored water; observe after several hours.

- **Q:** What does the colored celery show?
  **A:** Xylem tubes carry water up the stem; the colored dots in a cross-section are xylem bundles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on organization and systems.

*How to teach:* Systems quiz.

- **Q:** Which side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs?
  **A:** The right side (right ventricle).

**Assessment:** Systems quiz and dissection sheet.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Levels of organization. 2) Blood path: body → right atrium → right ventricle → lungs → left atrium → left ventricle → body. 3) Exercise interactions. 4) Xylem vs. phloem. 5) Stomata exchange gases. Dissection sheet: proficient = labeled four chambers, aorta, valves (if visible), comparison of ventricle walls.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): African American culture and contemporary issues

**Prepare:** Langston Hughes poems ('I, Too'; 'The Negro Speaks of Rivers'); Duke Ellington recording (e.g., 'Take the "A" Train' — composed by Billy Strayhorn for Ellington's orchestra); research on a Black Californian leader; data source (e.g., Kidsdata.org, Census).

**Watch for:**
- Treating the Harlem Renaissance as only art: it was also a political assertion of Black identity and dignity.
- Using single data points to make sweeping claims: compare over time and across groups.
- Reflections without evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Harlem Renaissance
*Student:* Read Hughes and listen to Duke Ellington; analyze themes of identity.

*How to teach:* Read Hughes; listen to Ellington; analyze identity themes.

- **Q:** What is the message of Hughes's 'I, Too'?
  **A:** Black Americans are fully American ('I, too, sing America'); though sent to 'eat in the kitchen,' the speaker will one day sit at the table, and others will 'be ashamed.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Black Californians
*Student:* Research a Black Californian leader (e.g., Tom Bradley, Maya Angelou).

*How to teach:* Research a Black Californian leader.

- **Q:** Who was Tom Bradley?
  **A:** Los Angeles's first Black mayor, serving five terms (1973–1993).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Contemporary issues
*Student:* Analyze data on an equity issue (education, housing, health).

*How to teach:* Analyze data on an equity issue.

- **Q:** What questions should you ask about equity data?
  **A:** Source, date, how groups are defined, sample size, and what factors might explain differences.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection connecting history to contemporary issues.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection connecting history to today.

- **Q:** Give an example of a historical policy with present effects.
  **A:** Redlining in the 1930s and lower homeownership and wealth in those neighborhoods today.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 assessment
*Student:* Complete a unit assessment: identify key terms and people, then write a document-based paragraph using two Unit 9 sources.

*How to teach:* Unit 9 assessment.

- **Q:** Who wrote Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass?
  **A:** Frederick Douglass (1845).

**Assessment:** Unit 9 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 assessment answers: key terms (resistance, Jim Crow, redlining, Great Migration, Black Power, Harlem Renaissance); people (Douglass, Biddy Mason, Archy Lee, Huey Newton, Bobby Seale, Langston Hughes, Tom Bradley); document paragraph scored with the paragraph rubric. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish II: Mi comunidad: places, jobs, and volunteering

**Prepare:** Print a simple map of your town. Find an article on teen volunteers in Latin America — TECHO (founded in Chile in 1997) builds emergency housing with young volunteers. Prepare community vocabulary: el ayuntamiento/la alcaldía, la estación de bomberos, la comisaría, la biblioteca, el hospital, el comedor de beneficencia.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing 'el bombero' (firefighter) with 'la bomba' (bomb/pump).
- Mixing preterite and present perfect: preterite for a finished time (El verano pasado ayudé...), present perfect for life experience (He ayudado...).

### Day 1 (Monday): Community vocabulary
*Student:* Draw a map of your town and label 15 places and the jobs done there in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a town map with 15 places and labels the job done at each (En la estación de bomberos trabajan los bomberos).

- **Q:** Translate: 'The library is next to the park.'
  **A:** La biblioteca está al lado del parque.
- **Q:** Who works at a hospital?
  **A:** los médicos y los enfermeros (las enfermeras)

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading
*Student:* Read an article about teen volunteers in Latin America (e.g., Techo in Chile) and answer main-idea and detail questions.

*How to teach:* Read about TECHO or a similar group; your child answers main-idea and detail questions.

- **Q:** What does TECHO do?
  **A:** It mobilizes young volunteers to build emergency housing and work with families in poor communities across Latin America; it started in Chile.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a family member or neighbor about volunteer work they have done, using the present perfect and preterite.

*How to teach:* Your child interviews someone: ¿Has trabajado como voluntario? ¿Dónde ayudaste? ¿Qué hiciste? Notes use present perfect and preterite.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Have you ever volunteered?'
  **A:** ¿Alguna vez has trabajado como voluntario/a?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing
*Student:* Write a 150-word paragraph about a volunteer project you have done or could do, explaining who it helps.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 150-word paragraph about a real or possible volunteer project explaining who it helps.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I could help at a soup kitchen.'
  **A:** Podría ayudar en un comedor de beneficencia.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Community vocabulary quiz and a 2-minute oral description of your map.

*How to teach:* Give the vocabulary quiz and the 2-minute oral map description.

- **Q:** Translate: 'The fire station is across from the school.'
  **A:** La estación de bomberos está enfrente de la escuela.

**Assessment:** Vocabulary quiz and oral map description.

**Answer key:** Vocabulary quiz sample key: el bombero = firefighter; el ayuntamiento = city hall; el comedor de beneficencia = soup kitchen; el/la voluntario/a = volunteer; ayudar = to help; la biblioteca = library; el cartero = mail carrier; el/la enfermero/a = nurse. Oral map description rubric: 4 = 2 minutes, 10+ places with location phrases (al lado de, cerca de) and jobs; 3 = 7–9 places; 2 = under 7; 1 = list only.

## Visual Art I: Portrait project: someone who matters

**Prepare:** Painting or drawing materials, camera. Choose a subject (family member, mentor) and prepare interview questions.

**Watch for:**
- Copying a photo without setting meaning. Correct: include objects and setting that reveal who the person is.
- Rushing the face. Correct: build proportions with guidelines before details.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interview
*Student:* Interview your subject and photograph them in a meaningful setting.

*How to teach:* Interview the subject; photograph in a meaningful setting.

- **Q:** Give two good interview questions.
  **A:** 'What place feels most like you?' 'What object would you save in a fire and why?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sketch
*Student:* Make a composition sketch including setting objects.

*How to teach:* Composition sketch with setting objects.

- **Q:** How can composition emphasize the person?
  **A:** Place the face at a rule-of-thirds point, use contrast and lead lines toward the face.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Create
*Student:* Begin the final portrait.

*How to teach:* Begin the final portrait.

- **Q:** Which proportion rules apply?
  **A:** Eyes halfway, five eye-widths across, ears between brow and nose, etc.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish
*Student:* Finish the portrait.

*How to teach:* Finish.

- **Q:** How do you check likeness?
  **A:** Compare feature spacing and shapes to the photo; flip the image or view in a mirror to spot errors.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the portrait to the subject and record their reaction.

*How to teach:* Present to the subject; record reaction.

- **Q:** Why record the subject's reaction?
  **A:** It shows how the portrait communicated and gives feedback for reflection.

**Assessment:** Final portrait.

**Answer key:** Final portrait: 4 = accurate proportions and likeness, setting/objects reflect interview, strong composition, skilled media handling, reaction recorded; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = weak likeness or no setting meaning; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): California Physical Fitness Test preparation

**Prepare:** PACER audio, measuring tape, mat, fall baseline data. Explain the California PFT (FITNESSGRAM) is given in grades 5, 7 and 9.

**Watch for:**
- Not pacing the PACER. Correct: run just fast enough to beat each beep; turn efficiently.
- Practicing only on test day. Correct: train components weekly.

### Day 1 (Monday): PACER practice
*Student:* Practice the PACER with a pacing strategy and record laps.

*How to teach:* PACER practice with pacing strategy.

- **Q:** What is a good PACER pacing strategy?
  **A:** Arrive at the line just as the beep sounds, pivot quickly, and conserve energy in early levels.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Upper body
*Student:* Practice push-up cadence and complete a push-up improvement circuit.

*How to teach:* Push-up cadence practice; improvement circuit.

- **Q:** Give a push-up improvement exercise.
  **A:** Incline push-ups, negative (slow lowering) push-ups, plank holds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Core and flexibility
*Student:* Practice curl-ups, trunk lift, and sit-and-reach with flexibility work.

*How to teach:* Curl-ups, trunk lift, sit-and-reach with flexibility work.

- **Q:** Which stretches help sit-and-reach?
  **A:** Hamstring and lower back stretches (seated forward fold, PNF hamstring stretch).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Targeted workout
*Student:* Complete a 35-minute workout aimed at your lowest component.

*How to teach:* 35-minute workout targeting the lowest component.

- **Q:** If cardio is lowest, what workout?
  **A:** Intervals or a 30+ minute run/bike in the target zone.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Full PFT practice run and comparison with fall baseline.

*How to teach:* Full PFT practice; compare to fall.

- **Q:** Why compare to your own baseline?
  **A:** Progress against yourself shows improvement regardless of others.

**Assessment:** PFT practice results.

**Answer key:** PFT practice results: proficient = all tests completed with correct protocol, recorded in portfolio and compared to fall baseline with HFZ status noted.

## Health & Wellness: Violence prevention and dating violence

**Prepare:** Role-play scenarios for de-escalation; loveisrespect.org cycle-of-abuse page; dating violence safety-plan template. Know how to report threats (Say Something app, school, 911).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking abuse is only physical. Correct: it includes emotional, verbal, digital, sexual and financial abuse.
- Believing the abuser will change after apologizing. Correct: apologies and 'honeymoon' periods are part of the cycle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Causes of violence
*Student:* Identify risk factors and protective factors for violence.

*How to teach:* Identify risk (exposure to violence, substance use, access to weapons) and protective factors (connected adults, conflict skills).

- **Q:** Name one protective factor against violence.
  **A:** Strong family connection, positive adult mentors, involvement in activities, conflict-resolution skills.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): De-escalation
*Student:* Practice de-escalation techniques (calm voice, space, active listening, walking away) in three role-plays and rate which worked best.

*How to teach:* Role-play de-escalation and rate techniques.

- **Q:** Name two de-escalation techniques.
  **A:** Calm, low voice; give space; listen and acknowledge; avoid blaming; walk away and get help.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dating violence
*Student:* Learn the cycle of abuse and warning signs using loveisrespect.org.

*How to teach:* Teach the cycle: tension building → incident → reconciliation/honeymoon → calm, and warning signs.

- **Q:** Describe the cycle of abuse.
  **A:** Tension builds, an abusive incident occurs, the abuser apologizes or promises change (honeymoon), a calm period follows, and the cycle repeats.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Weapons and gangs
*Student:* Discuss the dangers of weapons and gang involvement and how to report threats.

*How to teach:* Discuss weapons and gang dangers and how to report threats.

- **Q:** If you hear someone plans to bring a weapon to school, what do you do?
  **A:** Tell a trusted adult immediately or use an anonymous tip line; call 911 if the danger is immediate.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a safety plan for a scenario involving dating violence.

*How to teach:* The child writes a safety plan for a scenario.

- **Q:** What goes into a dating violence safety plan?
  **A:** Trusted people to tell, safe places, code words, how to get help (911, loveisrespect: text LOVEIS to 22522 or call 1-866-331-9474), digital safety steps, documenting incidents.

**Assessment:** Safety plan scenario.

**Answer key:** Safety plan rubric: 4 = recognizes warning signs in the scenario, lists trusted adults and hotlines (loveisrespect 1-866-331-9474), safe places, digital safety steps, documentation, and 911 for danger; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.
