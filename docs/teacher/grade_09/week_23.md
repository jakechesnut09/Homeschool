# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 23

## English 9: Romeo and Juliet, Act 2: the balcony and the secret marriage

**Prepare:** R&J Act 2. A light/dark imagery chart. Recruit family for reading parts.

**Watch for:**
- 'What's in a name?' doesn't mean names are unimportant to the play: Juliet wishes the name Montague didn't matter — but the feud makes it matter fatally.
- Seeing the Friar as purely wise: he warns 'Wisely and slow' yet rushes the marriage for political hopes.
- Thinking Juliet is as impulsive as Romeo in 2.2: she voices caution ('too rash, too unadvised, too sudden').

### Day 1 (Monday): Balcony scene
*Student:* Read 2.2 aloud in parts; collect light/dark imagery in a chart.

*How to teach:* Read 2.2 aloud; chart light/dark imagery.

- **Q:** What metaphor does Romeo use when he sees Juliet at the window?
  **A:** 'It is the east, and Juliet is the sun' — she is light that outshines everything.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Juliet's caution
*Student:* Analyze how Juliet's speeches show more caution than Romeo's; cite two lines.

*How to teach:* Analyze Juliet's caution with two lines.

- **Q:** Quote a line where Juliet is cautious.
  **A:** 'It is too rash, too unadvised, too sudden, / Too like the lightning' — or 'O, swear not by the moon, th' inconstant moon.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Friar Laurence
*Student:* Read 2.3; explain the Friar's warning "Wisely and slow" and his reasons for helping.

*How to teach:* Read 2.3; explain 'Wisely and slow' and the Friar's reasons.

- **Q:** Why does Friar Laurence agree to marry them?
  **A:** He hopes the marriage will turn the households' 'rancor to pure love' — end the feud.
- **Q:** What does 'Wisely and slow; they stumble that run fast' warn?
  **A:** Rushing leads to mistakes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nurse and marriage
*Student:* Read 2.4–2.6; perform a short scene with family focusing on vocal expression.

*How to teach:* Read 2.4–2.6; perform a short scene with family.

- **Q:** What does the Friar warn in 2.6?
  **A:** 'These violent delights have violent ends' — intense passion can end in disaster.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Act 2 quiz and journal: Who is most responsible for the rush to marry?

*How to teach:* Act 2 quiz; journal: Who is most responsible for the rush to marry?

- **Q:** Who carries messages between Romeo and Juliet?
  **A:** The Nurse.

**Assessment:** Act 2 quiz and journal entry.

**Answer key:** Act 2 quiz answers: 1) Romeo overhears Juliet in the orchard/at the balcony. 2) 'Wherefore art thou Romeo' = why are you Romeo. 3) Juliet asks Romeo to send word about marriage. 4) The Friar was surprised Romeo forgot Rosaline. 5) Friar's reason: end the feud. 6) The Nurse delays giving Juliet the news. 7) They marry in the Friar's cell. Journal: proficient = names a character and supports with two quotations.

## Algebra I: Exponential growth and decay

**Prepare:** 100 coins or M&Ms (M side = 'heads') and a cup. Data table for rounds. Desmos for an exponential regression (y₁ ~ a·b^x₁).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing growth factor with growth rate: a 4% increase is a factor of 1.04; a 15% decrease is a factor of 0.85.
- Thinking exponential means 'grows fast' from the start: early values can be small; the key is the constant RATIO.
- Using a₁·r^n instead of a₁·r^(n−1) for the nth term of a geometric sequence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Linear vs. exponential
*Student:* Compare a $1/day plan and a doubling-penny plan in tables to see constant difference vs. constant ratio.

*How to teach:* Make tables for Plan A ($1 per day) and Plan B (1¢ on day 1, doubling daily). Look at differences (A constant) and ratios (B constant).

- **Q:** On which day does the doubling plan pay 2^29 cents, and how much is that?
  **A:** Day 30: 2^29 = 536,870,912 cents = $5,368,709.12.
- **Q:** What stays constant in linear vs. exponential tables?
  **A:** Linear: constant difference. Exponential: constant ratio.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decay simulation
*Student:* Shake 100 coins, remove heads each round, record the counts, and fit an exponential decay model.

*How to teach:* Shake 100 coins, remove heads, count the rest, repeat until none. Fit y = a·b^x in Desmos; b should be close to 0.5.

- **Q:** What model do you expect for the coins remaining after n shakes?
  **A:** y ≈ 100(0.5)^n.
- **Q:** About how many coins remain after 3 shakes?
  **A:** 100(0.5)³ = 12.5 → about 12 or 13.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Growth and decay factors
*Student:* Write functions like A=500(1.04)^t and identify percent rate of change.

*How to teach:* Read a·(1 ± r)^t: a = start value, 1 + r = growth factor. Your child writes functions and states percent rates.

- **Q:** A = 500(1.04)^t: initial value and percent rate?
  **A:** Start $500, growing 4% per period.
- **Q:** Write a model for a $20,000 car losing 15% of value per year.
  **A:** V = 20,000(0.85)^t.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Geometric sequences
*Student:* Write geometric sequences recursively and explicitly.

*How to teach:* Model a geometric sequence both ways: 3, 6, 12, 24 → a₁ = 3, a_n = 2·a_(n−1); a_n = 3·2^(n−1).

- **Q:** Find a₈ for 3, 6, 12, 24, …
  **A:** 3·2⁷ = 384.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on exponential models.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the simulation analysis (data, model, comparison to 0.5).

- **Q:** Is y = 4(0.9)^x growth or decay? Rate?
  **A:** Decay, 10% per unit.

**Assessment:** Exponential quiz and simulation analysis.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Table 3, 6, 12, 24 — exponential (ratio 2); table 3, 6, 9, 12 — linear (difference 3). 2) y = 800(1.06)^t: 6% growth; value at t = 2 → 898.88. 3) y = 50(0.75)^t: 25% decay. 4) Geometric 2, 6, 18, … → a_n = 2·3^(n−1); a₆ = 486. 5) Recursive: a₁ = 2, a_n = 3a_(n−1). Simulation rubric: data table, graph, regression equation with b near 0.5, explanation of why b ≈ 0.5 (each coin has a 1/2 chance to be removed).

## Biology – The Living Earth: Non-Mendelian inheritance and human traits

**Prepare:** Pedigree worksheets; tape measure for family heights.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking codominance and incomplete dominance are the same: incomplete = blend (pink); codominance = both shown (AB blood, roan).
- Believing females can't have sex-linked recessive traits: they can, if they inherit two recessive alleles, but it's rarer.
- Assuming every trait has 2 forms: many traits (height, skin color) are polygenic with continuous variation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Beyond Mendel
*Student:* Solve problems on incomplete dominance and codominance (blood types).

*How to teach:* Solve incomplete dominance and codominance problems.

- **Q:** Cross two pink snapdragons (RW × RW).
  **A:** 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white.
- **Q:** Parents with blood types Iᴬi and Iᴮi — possible children?
  **A:** AB, A, B, and O, each with probability 1/4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sex-linked traits
*Student:* Solve 8 sex-linked problems on color blindness and hemophilia, explaining why males are more often affected.

*How to teach:* Solve sex-linked problems.

- **Q:** A carrier mother (XᴮXᵇ) and a father with normal vision (XᴮY): chance a son is color-blind? A daughter?
  **A:** Sons: 50%. Daughters: 0% affected (50% carriers).
- **Q:** Why are males more often affected?
  **A:** Males have one X chromosome, so a single recessive allele is expressed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pedigrees
*Student:* Analyze pedigrees to determine inheritance patterns.

*How to teach:* Analyze pedigrees.

- **Q:** Two unaffected parents have an affected child. What inheritance pattern is likely?
  **A:** Recessive (both parents are carriers).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Polygenic traits
*Student:* Measure family heights and graph a distribution of polygenic traits.

*How to teach:* Measure and graph family heights.

- **Q:** What shape does a polygenic trait's distribution usually show?
  **A:** A bell-shaped (normal) curve.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on non-Mendelian inheritance.

*How to teach:* Quiz; pedigree analysis.

- **Q:** In a pedigree, what does a shaded square represent?
  **A:** An affected male.

**Assessment:** Non-Mendelian quiz and pedigree analysis.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Incomplete dominance ratio 1:2:1. 2) Codominance blood type cross. 3) Sex-linked cross (above). 4) Hemophilia father (XʰY) × normal mother (XᴴXᴴ) → all daughters carriers, all sons unaffected. 5) Pedigree symbols. Pedigree analysis: proficient = correctly identifies pattern with reasoning (skipped generations → recessive; mostly males → X-linked).

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Gold Rush, statehood, and Native survival

**Prepare:** Excerpts of the 1850 Act for the Government and Protection of Indians and Gov. Peter Burnett's 1851 annual message; a summary of the 18 unratified treaties; Gov. Newsom's 2019 executive order apology. Parental note: these documents describe violence and genocide; preview.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the 1850 Act protected Native people as its title says: it allowed the indenture of Native children and adults.
- Assuming treaties were honored: the U.S. Senate rejected all 18 treaties in 1852 and kept them secret until 1905.
- Treating apology as the end of the story: tribes continue to seek land, recognition, and repair.

### Day 1 (Monday): 1850 Act
*Student:* Read excerpts of the 1850 Act and explain how it allowed indenture of Native people.

*How to teach:* Read the 1850 Act excerpts.

- **Q:** How did the 1850 Act allow exploitation of Native people?
  **A:** It allowed whites to take custody of Native children as 'apprentices' and let Native adults arrested for 'vagrancy' be hired out to the highest bidder; it also barred convicting a white man based on Native testimony.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Burnett's address
*Student:* Analyze Gov. Burnett's 1851 message and identify its purpose and effects.

*How to teach:* Analyze Burnett's 1851 message.

- **Q:** What did Governor Burnett predict in 1851?
  **A:** That 'a war of extermination will continue to be waged between the races until the Indian race becomes extinct.'
- **Q:** What was the effect of such rhetoric?
  **A:** It encouraged state-funded militia campaigns and violence against Native Californians.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Unratified treaties
*Student:* Learn about the 18 unratified treaties of 1851–52 and their consequences for land.

*How to teach:* Learn about the 18 unratified treaties.

- **Q:** What happened to the 18 treaties signed in 1851–52?
  **A:** Tribes agreed to give up land in exchange for reservations (about 7.5 million acres), but the U.S. Senate refused to ratify them and sealed them; tribes lost land without receiving the promised reservations.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Governor's apology
*Student:* Read Gov. Newsom's 2019 apology to California Native peoples and compare it with 1851 sources.

*How to teach:* Read Newsom's 2019 apology; compare with 1851 sources.

- **Q:** What did Governor Newsom's 2019 apology acknowledge?
  **A:** That the state committed violence, exploitation, and genocide against California Native peoples; it created a Truth and Healing Council.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a source analysis comparing two documents.

*How to teach:* Source analysis comparing two documents.

- **Q:** Why is comparing an 1851 and a 2019 document useful?
  **A:** It shows how state perspectives and official language changed, and what has and hasn't been addressed.

**Assessment:** Document comparison.

**Answer key:** Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.

## Spanish II: El cuerpo y la salud

**Prepare:** Print a body diagram. Chart doler (me duele + singular, me duelen + plural) and illness vocabulary: la gripe, el resfriado, la tos, la fiebre, la alergia, el dolor de cabeza/garganta/estómago.

**Watch for:**
- Using a possessive with body parts (*Me duele mi cabeza): Spanish uses the article — Me duele la cabeza.
- Forgetting plural agreement: Me duelen los pies (plural subject).
- Confusing 'tener fiebre' with 'estar fiebre': tener fiebre, tener tos, but estar enfermo/a, estar resfriado/a.

### Day 1 (Monday): Body vocabulary
*Student:* Label a body diagram with 30 parts and play a Simón dice game with family using the vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Your child labels 30 body parts, then plays Simón dice (Simón dice: tócate la nariz) with family, taking turns as Simón.

- **Q:** Give the Spanish for: shoulder, knee, elbow, back.
  **A:** el hombro, la rodilla, el codo, la espalda

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Doler
*Student:* Use doler with indirect object pronouns (Me duele la cabeza).

*How to teach:* Model: 'Me duele la cabeza. A mi hermano le duelen los oídos.' Your child writes 10 sentences with different people and body parts.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Her throat hurts.'
  **A:** Le duele la garganta.
- **Q:** Translate: 'My eyes hurt.'
  **A:** Me duelen los ojos.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Symptoms
*Student:* Write symptom descriptions for 10 illnesses (la gripe, una alergia) using tener, doler, and estar.

*How to teach:* Your child writes symptom descriptions for 10 illnesses (Con la gripe, tengo fiebre, me duele el cuerpo y estoy cansado/a).

- **Q:** Describe allergy symptoms in Spanish.
  **A:** Con una alergia, estornudo mucho, tengo los ojos rojos y me pica la nariz.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Doctor role-play
*Student:* Role-play a doctor's visit with a family member.

*How to teach:* You are el médico: '¿Qué le pasa? ¿Desde cuándo? ¿Tiene fiebre?' Your child is the patient and describes symptoms using usted with you.

- **Q:** How does a doctor ask 'What's wrong?' formally?
  **A:** ¿Qué le pasa? / ¿Qué tiene?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Health vocabulary quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the vocabulary quiz and score the role-play.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Me duele mis pies.'
  **A:** Me duelen los pies.

**Assessment:** Vocabulary quiz and role-play.

**Answer key:** Health vocabulary quiz sample key: la cabeza = head; la garganta = throat; el estómago = stomach; la espalda = back; el brazo = arm; la rodilla = knee; toser = to cough; estornudar = to sneeze; tener fiebre = to have a fever; 'My feet hurt' = Me duelen los pies. Doctor role-play rubric: 4 = greets, describes 3+ symptoms with doler/tener/estar correctly, answers the doctor's questions; 3 = minor errors; 2 = limited; 1 = cannot complete.

## Visual Art I: Facial proportions

**Prepare:** Mirror, pencils, drawing paper. Front-view proportion guide: eyes halfway down the head; face width about five eye-widths; bottom of the nose about halfway between eyes and chin; mouth about one-third of the way from nose to chin; ears between brow line and nose bottom.

**Watch for:**
- Placing eyes too high. Correct: the eyes sit halfway — the forehead and hair are bigger than people think.
- Drawing a flat top of the head. Correct: the skull extends well above the hairline.

### Day 1 (Monday): Proportion guide
*Student:* Learn standard facial proportions (eyes halfway, etc.) and draw a front-view guide.

*How to teach:* Draw a front-view guide.

- **Q:** How many eye-widths wide is a face?
  **A:** About five, with one eye-width between the eyes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eyes and nose
*Student:* Draw 6 studies of eyes and noses from a mirror.

*How to teach:* Six eye and nose studies.

- **Q:** How do you show a nose without outlining it?
  **A:** Shade the sides and underside planes and the nostril shadows; leave the bridge light.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mouth and ears
*Student:* Draw studies of mouths and ears.

*How to teach:* Mouth and ear studies.

- **Q:** Where do the corners of the mouth usually line up?
  **A:** Roughly under the pupils.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Full face
*Student:* Draw a family member's face from life using the guide.

*How to teach:* Draw a family member's face from life with the guide.

- **Q:** Where do the ears sit?
  **A:** Between the eyebrow line and the bottom of the nose.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare to a photo and correct proportions.

*How to teach:* Compare to a photo; correct proportions.

- **Q:** What's a common proportion error?
  **A:** Eyes too high, too large, or the head too short above the eyes.

**Assessment:** Proportion studies.

**Answer key:** Proportion studies: 4 = guide drawn correctly with labels, 6+ feature studies with shading, life drawing follows the guide and shows corrections; 3 = minor errors; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Swimming strokes: backstroke and breaststroke

**Prepare:** Pool access, phone for video (with permission at public pools), technique checklists for backstroke and breaststroke.

**Watch for:**
- Sitting low in the water on backstroke. Correct: hips up, ears in the water, eyes up.
- Breaststroke flutter kick. Correct: whip kick — heels to buttocks, feet turn out, kick around and together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Streamline
*Student:* Practice streamline push-offs and measure how far you glide.

*How to teach:* Streamline push-offs; measure glide.

- **Q:** Describe a streamline position.
  **A:** Arms overhead, one hand on top of the other, biceps squeezing the ears, body tight and straight.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Backstroke
*Student:* Learn backstroke arm rotation and flutter kick; swim 6 × 25 yards.

*How to teach:* Backstroke arm rotation (pinky enters first) and flutter kick; 6×25.

- **Q:** Which part of the hand enters first in backstroke?
  **A:** The pinky finger.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Breaststroke kick
*Student:* Learn the whip kick holding the wall, then with a kickboard.

*How to teach:* Whip kick at the wall, then with a kickboard.

- **Q:** Describe the breaststroke kick.
  **A:** Bend knees bringing heels toward the buttocks, turn feet out, then kick out and around, snapping legs together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Breaststroke
*Student:* Combine pull, breathe, kick, glide; swim 6 × 25 yards and film one length.

*How to teach:* Combine pull, breathe, kick, glide; film one length.

- **Q:** What is the breaststroke timing?
  **A:** Pull and breathe, kick, then glide — 'pull, breathe, kick, glide.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare your video to a technique checklist and demonstrate both strokes.

*How to teach:* Compare video to checklist; demonstrate both strokes.

- **Q:** Which strokes are swum on the front?
  **A:** Freestyle, breaststroke and butterfly; backstroke is on the back.

**Assessment:** Stroke demonstration and video self-analysis.

**Answer key:** Stroke demonstration and video analysis: proficient = swims 25 yd of each stroke with checklist points (backstroke: hips up, straight-arm recovery, pinky entry; breaststroke: whip kick, glide, breath with pull) and identifies two corrections from the video.

## Health & Wellness: Abstinence and contraception

**Prepare:** CDC 'Contraceptive Effectiveness' chart (typical-use failure rates). No demonstrations needed. Write a matching quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking withdrawal or 'the first time' can't cause pregnancy. Correct: pregnancy can occur any time sperm meet an egg; withdrawal has about a 20% typical-use failure rate.
- Believing hormonal methods protect against STIs. Correct: only condoms reduce STI risk; abstinence eliminates it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Abstinence
*Student:* Discuss abstinence as the only 100% effective method and reasons people choose it.

*How to teach:* Discuss abstinence as the only 100% effective method and reasons people choose it.

- **Q:** Why is abstinence the only 100% method?
  **A:** No sexual contact means no exposure to sperm or sexually transmitted infections.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Contraceptive methods
*Student:* Learn about all FDA-approved methods using a CDC effectiveness chart.

*How to teach:* Review all FDA-approved methods on the CDC chart.

- **Q:** What are LARC methods?
  **A:** Long-acting reversible contraception: IUDs (hormonal and copper) and the implant, all over 99% effective.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing methods
*Student:* Compare methods by effectiveness, STI protection and access.

*How to teach:* Compare methods by effectiveness, STI protection and access (in CA, minors can obtain contraception confidentially).

- **Q:** Which methods protect against both pregnancy and STIs?
  **A:** Abstinence (100%); external and internal condoms reduce both risks. Using a condom plus another method ('dual protection') gives better pregnancy prevention and STI protection.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decisions
*Student:* Apply the decision-making model to a scenario about sexual health choices.

*How to teach:* Apply DECIDE to a sexual-health scenario.

- **Q:** What values and consequences should be considered?
  **A:** Personal/family values, readiness, risk of pregnancy and STIs, emotional impact, goals, and partner's respect for consent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a quiz matching methods to typical-use effectiveness and STI protection, and write which methods protect against both pregnancy and STIs.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** Typical-use effectiveness of the pill?
  **A:** About 93% (about 7 pregnancies per 100 users per year).

**Assessment:** Quiz on contraception and decisions.

**Answer key:** Quiz key (approximate CDC typical-use effectiveness): abstinence 100%; implant >99%; IUD >99%; shot (Depo-Provera) 96%; pill/patch/ring 93%; external condom 87%; internal condom 79%; withdrawal 80%; fertility awareness 77–98%; spermicide 79%. STI protection: only condoms (and abstinence). Written answer: abstinence prevents both completely; condoms reduce risk of both.
