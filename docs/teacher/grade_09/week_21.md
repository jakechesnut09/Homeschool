# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 21

## English 9: Transforming source material: Tennyson's "Ulysses"; epic hero essay

**Prepare:** Print Tennyson's 'Ulysses.' Prepare an essay planner. Prepare the Unit 5 test on the Odyssey.

**Watch for:**
- Assuming Tennyson's Ulysses is content at home: he is restless and wants to sail again.
- Essays that retell the plot: each body paragraph should argue one heroic quality.
- Using evidence from only one or two books: require at least four.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tennyson's "Ulysses"
*Student:* Read the dramatic monologue; compare the aging Ulysses's restlessness with Homer's homecoming.

*How to teach:* Read the monologue aloud; compare with Homer's homecoming.

- **Q:** How does Tennyson's Ulysses feel about being home?
  **A:** Restless and bored — 'It little profits that an idle king, / By this still hearth' — he wants to travel and 'drink life to the lees.'
- **Q:** What is the poem's final line?
  **A:** 'To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transformation
*Student:* Write a paragraph analyzing how Tennyson transforms Homer's hero and why.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph on how and why Tennyson transforms Homer's hero.

- **Q:** How does Tennyson's hero differ from Homer's?
  **A:** Homer's hero longs for home; Tennyson's, once home, longs for adventure and continued striving even in old age.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Essay plan
*Student:* Plan an essay: What qualities define Odysseus as an epic hero, and are they still admired today?

*How to teach:* Plan the essay on Odysseus as an epic hero.

- **Q:** Name three epic-hero qualities Odysseus shows.
  **A:** Cleverness (Trojan horse, 'Nobody'), courage/endurance (Sirens, twenty years), loyalty/devotion to home (leaves Calypso) — also flaws like pride.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the 800-word essay with evidence from at least four books of the Odyssey.

*How to teach:* Draft the 800-word essay with evidence from at least four books.

- **Q:** How do you cite the Odyssey?
  **A:** By book and line number (or page) depending on the translation, e.g., (Homer 9.502).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Revise, edit, and submit the essay; take the Unit 5 test.

*How to teach:* Revise, edit, submit; Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** Who is Tiresias?
  **A:** The blind prophet Odysseus consults in the Underworld.

**Assessment:** Epic hero essay and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric (4 levels): 4 = precise, arguable thesis; logically ordered body paragraphs each with a topic sentence, embedded quotations cited correctly, and commentary that explains significance; effective transitions; conclusion extends the idea; few errors. 3 = clear thesis, adequate evidence and explanation, some summary, minor errors. 2 = vague thesis, evidence thin or poorly explained, organization uneven. 1 = no clear thesis, little evidence, errors interfere. Unit 5 test answers (sample): invocation; in medias res; xenia examples; 'Nobody' trick; hubris boast; Circe and moly; Tiresias's warning about Helios's cattle; Sirens and wax; Scylla vs. Charybdis; Eumaeus; Argos; the bow and axes; the olive-tree bed; Tennyson's 'To strive, to seek, to find, and not to yield.'

## Algebra I: Scatter plots, regression, and correlation

**Prepare:** Tape measure for height and arm span of 8–10 people (or download a class data set). Desmos regression: enter a table, then y₁ ~ mx₁ + b. Find a few misleading correlation headlines.

**Watch for:**
- Reading r near 1 as 'the model is perfect' or as causation: r measures strength of a linear relationship only.
- Computing residual as predicted − actual: residual = actual − predicted.
- Interpreting the y-intercept when x = 0 is meaningless (a person with height 0).

### Day 1 (Monday): Scatter plots
*Student:* Measure height and arm span of family members (or use a provided data set) and make a scatter plot.

*How to teach:* Measure in inches or cm; plot height on x and arm span on y. Your child describes direction, form, strength, and any outliers.

- **Q:** Describe a scatter plot where points rise left to right in a tight line.
  **A:** Strong, positive, linear association.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Regression
*Student:* Use Desmos to find a linear regression and interpret slope and intercept.

*How to teach:* Run linear regression in Desmos. Interpret slope and intercept in context.

- **Q:** Arm span ≈ 0.98(height) + 2 (inches). Interpret the slope.
  **A:** For each additional inch of height, arm span increases about 0.98 inch on average.
- **Q:** Predict arm span for height 65 in.
  **A:** 0.98(65) + 2 = 65.7 in.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Residuals and r
*Student:* Plot residuals and interpret the correlation coefficient.

*How to teach:* Compute residuals and plot them; a random scatter means the linear model fits. Interpret r.

- **Q:** Actual arm span 66 in; predicted 64.5 in. Residual?
  **A:** 66 − 64.5 = 1.5 in (the point is above the line).
- **Q:** r = 0.95. Interpret.
  **A:** Strong positive linear relationship.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Correlation vs. causation
*Student:* Analyze 6 headlines (e.g., ice cream sales and drownings) for confounding variables.

*How to teach:* Analyze headlines: ice cream sales and drownings both rise in summer — hot weather is a confounding variable. Your child analyzes 6 headlines.

- **Q:** Why doesn't a correlation between ice cream sales and drownings mean ice cream causes drowning?
  **A:** A lurking variable — hot weather — increases both swimming and ice cream sales.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 statistics test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test (about 50 minutes).

- **Q:** A residual plot shows a U-shape. What does that tell you?
  **A:** A linear model is not a good fit; the relationship is curved.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test sample answers: 1) Data 5, 7, 8, 8, 12, 20 → mean 10, median 8; median better (skewed). 2) IQR for Q1 = 7, Q3 = 12 → 5; 20 is an outlier? Fence 12 + 7.5 = 19.5 → yes. 3) Two-way table conditional frequencies (compute row %). 4) Regression y = 3.2x + 10: slope = 3.2 units of y per unit x; predict at x = 5 → 26. 5) Residual for (5, 24) → −2. 6) r = −0.85 → strong negative. 7) Correlation ≠ causation; name a confounder. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Mitosis, meiosis, and variation

**Prepare:** Pipe cleaners in 2 colors (maternal/paternal), beads for centromeres. A comparison table template.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking meiosis makes identical cells: it makes four genetically different haploid cells.
- Confusing chromosomes and chromatids: after replication, each chromosome has two sister chromatids.
- Believing differentiated cells have different DNA: they have the same DNA but express different genes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cell cycle
*Student:* Model the cell cycle and mitosis with pipe-cleaner chromosomes.

*How to teach:* Model interphase and mitosis stages (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase) and cytokinesis.

- **Q:** In a human body cell, how many chromosomes do the two daughter cells have after mitosis?
  **A:** 46 each — identical to the parent cell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Meiosis
*Student:* Model meiosis I and II and explain haploid gametes.

*How to teach:* Model meiosis I and II.

- **Q:** How many cells and how many chromosomes result from meiosis in humans?
  **A:** Four haploid cells with 23 chromosomes each.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crossing over
*Student:* Demonstrate crossing over and independent assortment with colored pipe cleaners.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate crossing over and independent assortment.

- **Q:** How does crossing over increase variation?
  **A:** Homologous chromosomes exchange segments in prophase I, creating new allele combinations.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mitosis vs. meiosis
*Student:* Complete a comparison table and explain differentiation.

*How to teach:* Complete the mitosis vs. meiosis table; explain differentiation.

- **Q:** Give one purpose of mitosis and one of meiosis.
  **A:** Mitosis: growth and repair. Meiosis: making gametes (eggs and sperm).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on cell division.

*How to teach:* Cell division quiz.

- **Q:** In which phase do chromosomes line up in the middle?
  **A:** Metaphase.

**Assessment:** Cell division quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Phases of mitosis in order (PMAT). 2) Mitosis: 2 identical diploid cells. 3) Meiosis: 4 unique haploid cells. 4) Crossing over in prophase I. 5) Independent assortment in metaphase I. 6) Differentiation = different gene expression.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Family history and oral history methods

**Prepare:** Recording device; consent/release form (simple statement giving permission to record and share); 12 open-ended questions. Arrange an interviewee early.

**Watch for:**
- Asking yes/no questions: open-ended questions ('Tell me about…') produce stories.
- Recording without permission: always get informed consent and respect requests to stop or keep things private.
- Treating memory as perfect fact: oral histories are valuable perspectives; cross-check facts with other sources.

### Day 1 (Monday): Oral history methods
*Student:* Learn interview ethics and write 12 open-ended questions.

*How to teach:* Learn interview ethics; write 12 questions.

- **Q:** Turn 'Did you like school?' into an open-ended question.
  **A:** 'What do you remember most about school when you were my age?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Conduct interview
*Student:* Interview an elder relative or community member for 30 minutes and record it with permission.

*How to teach:* Conduct and record a 30-minute interview with permission.

- **Q:** What should you do before you start recording?
  **A:** Explain the purpose, how it will be used, and get the person's permission (ideally in writing).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Transcribe
*Student:* Transcribe a 5-minute excerpt and highlight key memories.

*How to teach:* Transcribe a 5-minute excerpt.

- **Q:** Should a transcript 'fix' the speaker's grammar?
  **A:** No — transcribe accurately; you may note [inaudible] or clarify in brackets.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Connect to history
*Student:* Write a 1-page reflection linking the interview to a historical event or migration.

*How to teach:* Write a 1-page reflection linking the interview to history.

- **Q:** How do you connect a personal story to history?
  **A:** Identify the time and place, research the broader event (e.g., a war, migration, economic change), and explain how the person's experience reflects or differs from it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share your oral history excerpt with family and turn in the transcript with your reflection paragraph.

*How to teach:* Share excerpt; submit transcript and reflection.

- **Q:** Why are oral histories valuable sources?
  **A:** They preserve perspectives and experiences often missing from official records.

**Assessment:** Oral history transcript and reflection.

**Answer key:** Oral history rubric: 4 = consent obtained, open-ended questions, accurate transcript excerpt, reflection connecting to a specific historical event with outside research; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = shallow connection; 1 = incomplete.

## Spanish II: En la ciudad: directions and por vs. para

**Prepare:** Print a tourist map of central Madrid (Puerta del Sol, Plaza Mayor, Museo del Prado, Parque del Retiro) or Mexico City's Centro Histórico. Prepare a por/para chart. Find a short Spanish tourism video (e.g., Turismo de España or Visit Mexico).

**Watch for:**
- Using 'para' for 'through' or 'for (in exchange)': por = through/along, duration, exchange, means, cause; para = destination, purpose, deadline, recipient, opinion.
- Confusing 'doblar' with 'double': in directions it means 'turn' (Dobla a la derecha).
- Mixing tú and usted when asking strangers: use usted (Disculpe, ¿me puede decir...?).

### Day 1 (Monday): Directions
*Student:* Learn direction phrases and trace routes on a map of Madrid.

*How to teach:* Teach siga derecho/sigue derecho, dobla/doble a la derecha/izquierda, cruza la calle, a dos cuadras, en la esquina, está al lado de/enfrente de. Your child traces routes on the Madrid map as you give directions.

- **Q:** Translate: 'Go straight two blocks and turn left.'
  **A:** Sigue derecho dos cuadras y dobla a la izquierda. (Usted: Siga derecho... y doble...)
- **Q:** What does 'la esquina' mean?
  **A:** The (street) corner.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Por vs. para
*Student:* Learn por vs. para rules (duration, exchange, through vs. purpose, destination, deadline) and complete 20 sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach a few anchors: por = 'through, by, for (exchange/duration), because of'; para = 'for (purpose), to (destination), by (deadline), for (recipient).' Your child completes 20 sentences with reasons.

- **Q:** Choose por or para: 'Gracias ___ la ayuda.'
  **A:** por (cause/exchange)
- **Q:** Choose: 'Salimos ___ México el viernes.'
  **A:** para (destination)

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Role-play
*Student:* Role-play a tourist asking for directions politely (Disculpe, ¿cómo llego a...?).

*How to teach:* You are a passerby in Madrid; your child is the tourist: 'Disculpe, ¿cómo llego al Museo del Prado?' Give 3-step directions; your child repeats them back.

- **Q:** Ask politely in usted form: 'Excuse me, where is the station?'
  **A:** Disculpe, ¿dónde está la estación?

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tourism video
*Student:* Watch a Spanish tourism video and list five recommended sites.

*How to teach:* Your child watches the tourism video and lists five recommended sites with one Spanish descriptive sentence each.

- **Q:** Name two famous sites in Madrid.
  **A:** Examples: el Museo del Prado, el Parque del Retiro, la Plaza Mayor, el Palacio Real, la Puerta del Sol.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Por/para quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the por/para quiz and score the role-play.

- **Q:** Choose: 'Necesito el informe ___ mañana.'
  **A:** para (deadline)

**Assessment:** Por/para quiz and role-play.

**Answer key:** Por/para quiz sample key: 1) Salgo ___ Madrid mañana — para (destination) 2) Caminamos ___ el parque — por (through) 3) Pagué 20 euros ___ la entrada — por (exchange) 4) Estudio ___ ser guía — para (purpose) 5) La tarea es ___ el lunes — para (deadline) 6) Viajamos ___ avión — en (or por) avión; accept 'en' as most common 7) Estuve en Sevilla ___ tres días — por (duration) 8) Este regalo es ___ ti — para (recipient). Role-play rubric: 4 = polite opening with usted, asks for directions, understands and repeats 3+ steps; 3 = minor errors; 2 = needs help; 1 = cannot complete.

## Visual Art I: Clay project: functional or sculptural vessel

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay, acrylic paint, sealant (note: air-dry clay is not food-safe). Images: Maria Martinez's black-on-black pottery (San Ildefonso Pueblo) and Japanese Jōmon cord-marked pottery.

**Watch for:**
- Carving designs when clay is too wet or dry. Correct: carve sgraffito at leather-hard.
- Painting before fully dry. Correct: let air-dry clay dry completely (often several days) first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Inspiration
*Student:* Study Pueblo pottery by Maria Martinez and Japanese Jomon pottery; sketch surface designs.

*How to teach:* Study Martinez and Jōmon; sketch surface designs.

- **Q:** What is Maria Martinez known for?
  **A:** Black-on-black pottery from San Ildefonso Pueblo, New Mexico — polished black surfaces with matte black designs, developed with her husband Julian.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the vessel or sculpture using your chosen method.

*How to teach:* Build the vessel or sculpture.

- **Q:** Why is Jōmon pottery significant?
  **A:** It is among the oldest pottery in the world (over 10,000 years old), named for its cord-marked ('jōmon') surfaces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Surface
*Student:* Add texture or sgraffito designs at the leather-hard stage.

*How to teach:* Add texture or sgraffito at leather-hard.

- **Q:** What is leather-hard?
  **A:** Clay that is partly dry — firm enough to hold shape but still carvable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Paint and seal
*Student:* After drying, paint and seal the piece.

*How to teach:* Paint and seal after drying.

- **Q:** What is sgraffito?
  **A:** Scratching through a surface layer (slip or paint) to reveal the color beneath.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an artist statement explaining your cultural inspiration.

*How to teach:* Write an artist statement explaining cultural inspiration.

- **Q:** How do you respectfully use cultural inspiration?
  **A:** Learn and credit the tradition and its meaning, take inspiration from techniques or forms rather than copying sacred designs, and explain the connection.

**Assessment:** Finished clay piece and statement.

**Answer key:** Clay piece and statement: 4 = well-constructed form using a chosen method, surface texture/sgraffito, neat painting and sealing, statement accurately credits and explains the cultural inspiration; 3 = minor flaws; 2 = weak construction or no cultural connection; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Water safety on land

**Prepare:** Life jacket (Coast Guard–approved), rope or garden hose, empty gallon jug with a little water, a pole/broom. American Red Cross water-safety video. NOAA rip-current diagrams.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking drowning looks like splashing and yelling. Correct: it's often silent — head low, mouth at water level, unable to call or wave.
- Jumping in to rescue. Correct: reach or throw, don't go — untrained rescuers often drown.

### Day 1 (Monday): Drowning facts
*Student:* Watch an American Red Cross water-safety video and list the silent signs of drowning; then do a 30-minute cardio workout.

*How to teach:* Watch the video; list silent signs; 30-minute cardio.

- **Q:** List three silent signs of drowning.
  **A:** Head low with mouth at water level, head tilted back, eyes glassy or closed, vertical body with no kick, trying to roll onto the back, climbing-ladder motion.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rip currents
*Student:* Learn to spot and escape a rip current (swim parallel to shore) using NOAA diagrams; draw an escape plan for a California beach.

*How to teach:* Learn to spot (gap in waves, darker narrow channel, foam moving seaward) and escape a rip current; draw an escape plan.

- **Q:** How do you escape a rip current?
  **A:** Don't fight it; swim parallel to shore until free, then angle back to shore; or float and wave for help.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reach-throw-don't go
*Student:* Practice reaching assists with a pole and throwing a rope or jug to a target 15 feet away, 10 times each.

*How to teach:* Reach with a pole and throw a rope or jug to a target 15 ft away, 10 times each.

- **Q:** Why lie down or brace when reaching?
  **A:** To keep a low center of gravity so you aren't pulled into the water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Life jackets
*Student:* Fit a Coast Guard–approved life jacket correctly and explain how to choose the right size; finish with a 20-minute core circuit.

*How to teach:* Fit a life jacket: USCG label, correct size by weight/chest, buckled, snug — lift at shoulders and it shouldn't rise past the chin/ears. 20-minute core circuit.

- **Q:** How do you check a life jacket fits?
  **A:** Fasten it, then have someone pull up at the shoulders; it shouldn't ride up over the chin or ears.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Water safety quiz and demonstration of a throwing assist.

*How to teach:* Quiz and throwing-assist demo.

- **Q:** What does 'Reach, Throw, Don't Go' mean?
  **A:** Help from land: reach with an object or throw a floating object; don't swim to a struggling person.

**Assessment:** Water safety quiz and throwing-assist demonstration.

**Answer key:** Water safety quiz key: 1) drowning is often silent 2) signs: head low, vertical, no waving 3) rip current: swim parallel 4) reach, throw, don't go 5) life jacket USCG-approved, snug, sized by weight 6) swim with a buddy and near lifeguards 7) enter unknown water feet-first. Throwing assist: proficient = braced low stance, calls to victim, throws past and pulls the line to the victim, 7/10 within reach.

## Health & Wellness: Gender identity, sexual orientation and respect

**Prepare:** Find media samples (ads, music, a movie scene). Read your school or co-op's anti-discrimination policy; note California Education Code 220 prohibits discrimination based on sex, gender, gender identity, gender expression and sexual orientation in schools.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing sex, gender identity, gender expression and orientation. Correct: they are distinct concepts.
- Thinking 'just joking' about identity is harmless. Correct: it contributes to harassment and poorer mental health.

### Day 1 (Monday): Key terms
*Student:* Learn medically accurate definitions of sex, gender identity, gender expression and sexual orientation.

*How to teach:* Define terms with a medically accurate glossary.

- **Q:** Distinguish sex assigned at birth from gender identity.
  **A:** Sex assigned at birth is based on physical anatomy/biology; gender identity is a person's internal sense of their gender.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gender norms
*Student:* Analyze gender stereotypes in ads and media and how they affect relationships.

*How to teach:* Analyze gender stereotypes in ads and how they affect relationships.

- **Q:** How might 'boys don't show feelings' affect relationships?
  **A:** It discourages communication and help-seeking, harming emotional intimacy and mental health.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Respect and inclusion
*Student:* Discuss how to treat all people with respect and identify school anti-discrimination protections.

*How to teach:* Discuss respect and school protections (Ed. Code 220, Seth's Law).

- **Q:** Name one California protection for students.
  **A:** Schools must not discriminate based on gender identity, expression or sexual orientation and must address bullying and harassment.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Media and relationships
*Student:* Analyze how movies and music portray relationships and compare to healthy relationship traits.

*How to teach:* Compare media portrayals of relationships to healthy traits.

- **Q:** What do romantic movies often show that's unhealthy?
  **A:** Grand gestures after ignoring 'no,' jealousy as love, possessiveness, no communication about consent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a reflection on how media influences relationship expectations.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection.

- **Q:** How can media shape relationship expectations?
  **A:** Repeated portrayals normalize behaviors (jealousy, pressure) and create unrealistic standards.

**Assessment:** Media analysis reflection.

**Answer key:** Media analysis reflection: 4 = analyzes at least two specific media examples, identifies stereotypes or unhealthy messages, compares to healthy traits, uses terms accurately and respectfully; 3 = one example analyzed well; 2 = general opinion; 1 = incomplete.
