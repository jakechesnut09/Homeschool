# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 13

## English 9: Figurative language and imagery

**Prepare:** Print Dickinson's 'Hope is the thing with feathers' and Frost's 'The Road Not Taken' and 'Nothing Gold Can Stay' (Poetry Foundation). Prepare a TP-CASTT template (Title, Paraphrase, Connotation, Attitude, Shifts, Title again, Theme) and 20 connotation pairs.

**Watch for:**
- Reading 'The Road Not Taken' as simple praise of nonconformity: the speaker says the roads were 'really about the same'; the 'sigh' and future retelling suggest we create meaning for our choices afterward.
- Ignoring the speaker: the poet and speaker aren't necessarily the same person.
- Treating connotation as definition: connotation is the emotional association (thrifty = positive, stingy = negative).

### Day 1 (Monday): Extended metaphor
*Student:* Read Dickinson's "Hope is the thing with feathers"; map each line of the bird metaphor to a quality of hope.

*How to teach:* Read the poem twice. Your child maps each part of the bird metaphor to hope.

- **Q:** What qualities of hope does Dickinson show through the bird?
  **A:** It lives in the soul ('perches in the soul'), sings constantly without words, is sweetest in hardship ('in the Gale'), survives in 'the chillest land,' and never asks anything in return ('It asked a crumb — of me').

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Frost's choices
*Student:* Read "The Road Not Taken" twice; debate the common misreading and support your interpretation with the final stanza.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Road Not Taken' twice; debate the common misreading using the final stanza.

- **Q:** What does the speaker say about how worn the two roads were?
  **A:** They were worn 'really about the same' — neither was clearly less traveled.
- **Q:** How does the last stanza support the 'misreading' interpretation?
  **A:** The speaker imagines telling the story 'with a sigh' 'ages and ages hence,' claiming he took 'the one less traveled by' — suggesting he will reshape the memory to give the choice meaning.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compressed meaning
*Student:* Read "Nothing Gold Can Stay"; annotate allusion and imagery and write a TP-CASTT analysis.

*How to teach:* Read 'Nothing Gold Can Stay'; annotate allusion and imagery; complete TP-CASTT.

- **Q:** What allusion appears in the poem, and what does it add?
  **A:** 'So Eden sank to grief' — the biblical Garden of Eden; it expands the theme from leaves to the human loss of innocence.
- **Q:** State the theme.
  **A:** Beauty, youth, and innocence are precious but brief; all perfect moments fade.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Connotation
*Student:* Sort 20 word pairs by connotation (e.g., "thrifty/stingy") and rewrite a stanza swapping connotations to change the tone.

*How to teach:* Sort 20 word pairs by connotation; your child rewrites a stanza swapping connotations to change tone.

- **Q:** Give the connotation of 'confident' vs. 'arrogant.'
  **A:** Confident = positive (self-assured); arrogant = negative (overly proud).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph analyzing one figurative device in an unseen Dickinson poem.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a paragraph analyzing a figurative device in an unseen Dickinson poem (e.g., 'Because I could not stop for Death').

- **Q:** In 'Because I could not stop for Death,' how is Death personified?
  **A:** As a kind, patient gentleman caller who gives the speaker a carriage ride.

**Assessment:** TP-CASTT chart and figurative-language paragraph.

**Answer key:** TP-CASTT chart for 'Nothing Gold Can Stay' — proficient: Paraphrase: new leaves first appear golden like blossoms but soon become ordinary green; Eden fell; dawn becomes day. Connotation: gold = precious; 'subsides,' 'sank,' 'goes down' = decline. Attitude: wistful, accepting. Shift: line 6 moves from nature to Eden/humanity. Theme: precious beginnings never last. Paragraph rubric: 4 = arguable claim, two well-chosen quotations embedded and cited, commentary that explains HOW the evidence proves the claim; 3 = claim and evidence with thinner commentary; 2 = summary instead of analysis or only one piece of evidence; 1 = off-topic or no evidence.

## Algebra I: Linear models, transformations, and inverses

**Prepare:** Desmos with sliders (type f(x) = x, then y = f(x) + k and y = a·f(x)). Prepare phone-plan, gym, and tea-cooling tables.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking f(x) + k shifts the graph right: it shifts up by k.
- Writing the inverse as 1/f(x): an inverse undoes the function (swap x and y and solve), not a reciprocal.
- Assuming a linear model fits everything: cooling tea is only roughly linear over short times (it is really exponential) — check the table's differences.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build a model
*Student:* Write a linear function for a phone plan, a gym membership, and a cooling cup of tea (table data).

*How to teach:* Model writing a linear function from a description (gym: $40 + $25/month → C = 25m + 40) and from a table (find rate, then starting value). Your child writes all three and comments on whether the tea data are truly linear.

- **Q:** A gym costs $40 to join and $25 per month. Write the cost after m months and find the cost for a year.
  **A:** C = 25m + 40; C(12) = $340.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transformations
*Student:* Use Desmos sliders to describe how f(x)+k and kf(x) change the graph of f(x)=x.

*How to teach:* In Desmos, slide k in f(x) + k and a in a·f(x). Your child writes a sentence describing each effect.

- **Q:** Describe y = x + 3 compared to y = x.
  **A:** Shifted up 3 units (same slope).
- **Q:** Describe y = −2x compared to y = x.
  **A:** Twice as steep and reflected (now decreasing).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Inverse functions
*Student:* Find inverses of linear functions like the Celsius–Fahrenheit conversion and interpret them.

*How to teach:* Find the inverse by swapping input and output: F = (9/5)C + 32 → C = (5/9)(F − 32). Your child finds inverses and explains what each input and output means.

- **Q:** Find the inverse of f(x) = 2x + 6 and evaluate f⁻¹(10).
  **A:** f⁻¹(x) = (x − 6)/2; f⁻¹(10) = 2.
- **Q:** What does the inverse of the C→F conversion do?
  **A:** It converts Fahrenheit back to Celsius: C = 5/9(F − 32).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Comparing models
*Student:* Compare two cell-plan functions and find when one becomes cheaper.

*How to teach:* Write both plans as functions and set them equal. Your child solves and interprets which plan is cheaper for which usage.

- **Q:** Plan A: $20 + $0.50/GB. Plan B: $35 + $0.25/GB. When are they equal, and which is cheaper beyond that?
  **A:** 20 + 0.5g = 35 + 0.25g → g = 60 GB; Plan B is cheaper above 60 GB.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on modeling, transformations, and inverses.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key.

- **Q:** Inverse of f(x) = 3x − 4?
  **A:** f⁻¹(x) = (x + 4)/3.

**Assessment:** Modeling quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Table (0, 15), (2, 21), (4, 27) → f(x) = 3x + 15. 2) y = x + 5 vs y = x → shifted up 5. 3) y = 3x vs y = x → steeper by factor 3. 4) Inverse of f(x) = 3x − 4 → (x + 4)/3. 5) Inverse of f(x) = x/2 + 1 → 2(x − 1). 6) Two plans: $30 + $10/mo vs $60 + $5/mo → equal at 6 months; second cheaper after 6. 7) Interpret slope and intercept of your phone-plan model.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Natural selection

**Prepare:** Dried beans or pasta in 4–5 colors (50 of each), two fabrics with different patterns, timer (15 seconds per predation round). Darwin's finches reading.

**Watch for:**
- Believing individuals evolve or 'try' to adapt: populations evolve; individuals don't change their genes in response to need.
- 'Survival of the fittest' = strongest: fitness means reproductive success in a specific environment.
- Thinking mutations arise because they're needed: variation is random; selection is not.

### Day 1 (Monday): Darwin's ideas
*Student:* Read about Darwin's finches and list the four factors of natural selection.

*How to teach:* Read about Darwin's finches; list the four factors.

- **Q:** What are the four factors of natural selection?
  **A:** Variation; inheritance (traits are heritable); overproduction/competition; differential survival and reproduction.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bean predation simulation
*Student:* Scatter colored beans on patterned fabric, act as predators for several rounds, and record survivors.

*How to teach:* Spread beans; 'predator' picks as many as possible in 15 seconds; survivors 'reproduce' (add one of same color per survivor).

- **Q:** Predict which bean colors will survive best on the patterned fabric.
  **A:** Colors that match the background (camouflaged), because predators find conspicuous beans faster.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data analysis
*Student:* Graph changes in bean color frequency across generations.

*How to teach:* Graph color frequency over generations.

- **Q:** If camouflaged beans went from 20% to 45% of the population, what happened?
  **A:** Natural selection increased the frequency of the better-camouflaged trait.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Real examples
*Student:* Analyze peppered moths and antibiotic resistance as natural selection.

*How to teach:* Analyze peppered moths and antibiotic resistance.

- **Q:** Why did dark peppered moths increase during the Industrial Revolution?
  **A:** Soot darkened tree bark, so dark moths were better hidden from birds and survived to reproduce more.
- **Q:** Why is finishing antibiotics as prescribed important?
  **A:** Stopping early can leave the most resistant bacteria alive to multiply, selecting for resistance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on natural selection.

*How to teach:* Natural selection quiz.

- **Q:** Does an individual bacterium become resistant because it was exposed to an antibiotic?
  **A:** No — resistant variants already exist (from mutation); the antibiotic kills the susceptible ones, so resistant ones multiply.

**Assessment:** Simulation lab report and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Quiz answers: 1) Four factors (above). 2) Fitness = reproductive success. 3) Peppered moth explanation. 4) Antibiotic resistance explanation. 5) Variation arises from mutation and sexual reproduction. 6) Populations, not individuals, evolve.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Economic geography and development

**Prepare:** CIA World Factbook, Gapminder, list of 25 jobs.

**Watch for:**
- Equating GDP with well-being: HDI adds health and education.
- Assuming development is only about effort: geography, colonial history, trade, and governance shape outcomes.
- Ignoring change: many countries (e.g., China, India) rose rapidly in recent decades.

### Day 1 (Monday): Economic sectors
*Student:* Classify 25 jobs (farmer, autoworker, nurse, software engineer) into primary–quaternary sectors and compare the U.S. and Ethiopia's workforce mix.

*How to teach:* Classify 25 jobs into sectors; compare the U.S. and Ethiopia.

- **Q:** Classify: farmer, autoworker, nurse, software engineer.
  **A:** Primary; secondary; tertiary; quaternary (information/knowledge).
- **Q:** How do U.S. and Ethiopian workforces differ?
  **A:** Most U.S. workers are in services (tertiary/quaternary), with ~1–2% in agriculture; most Ethiopian workers are in agriculture (primary).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measuring development
*Student:* Collect GDP per capita, HDI, literacy, and life expectancy for 8 countries from the World Factbook and rank them two ways.

*How to teach:* Collect indicators for 8 countries; rank two ways.

- **Q:** What three dimensions does the Human Development Index combine?
  **A:** Health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling), and standard of living (income per person).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Gapminder trends
*Student:* Use Gapminder's bubble chart to trace China, India, and Nigeria from 1960 to today and describe what changed.

*How to teach:* Trace China, India, Nigeria on Gapminder from 1960.

- **Q:** What major change does Gapminder show for China since 1960?
  **A:** Huge increases in income and life expectancy, especially after economic reforms starting in 1978.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Why uneven development?
*Student:* Read about colonial legacies, geography (landlocked states), and trade; write a paragraph linking one cause to a broader trend.

*How to teach:* Paragraph linking one cause of uneven development to a trend.

- **Q:** Why are landlocked countries often poorer?
  **A:** No direct sea access raises transportation and trade costs and makes them depend on neighbors' ports.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on sectors and development indicators with a data-table interpretation.

*How to teach:* Quiz with a data table.

- **Q:** Country A: GDP per capita $60,000, life expectancy 72. Country B: $40,000, life expectancy 83. Which may rank higher on HDI?
  **A:** Possibly Country B — HDI also weighs health and education, not just income.

**Assessment:** Development data analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: four sectors; HDI components; GDP per capita definition; development indicators (literacy, infant mortality); causes of uneven development (colonialism, landlocked status, trade, governance). Data analysis proficient: correct ranking by two indicators and explanation of why rankings differ.

## Spanish II: La comida: food vocabulary and gustar-type verbs

**Prepare:** Gather sticky notes and a marker; collect a Spanish grocery ad (Vallarta, Northgate González, Cardenas, or a Spanish-language Walmart flyer). Chart indirect object pronouns (me, te, le, nos, os, les).

**Watch for:**
- Making gustar agree with the person (*Yo gusto las fresas): agree with the thing liked — Me gustan las fresas.
- Omitting the pronoun with 'a + person' (*A mi hermano gusta): it must be A mi hermano le gusta.
- Using 'molestar' only as 'molest': in Spanish it simply means 'to bother/annoy' (Me molesta el ruido).

### Day 1 (Monday): Food vocabulary
*Student:* Label 40 foods in your kitchen with Spanish sticky notes.

*How to teach:* Your child labels 40 foods with sticky notes, including articles (la manzana, el pan, las zanahorias). Quiz by pointing at items for a quick recall round.

- **Q:** Give the Spanish for: rice, chicken, cheese, lettuce.
  **A:** el arroz, el pollo, el queso, la lechuga

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gustar-type verbs
*Student:* Practice gustar, encantar, interesar, molestar with indirect object pronouns.

*How to teach:* Show the pattern: (A mí) me + gusta(n) + thing. Teach encantar (love), interesar, molestar. Your child writes 10 sentences about family members using 'a + person' + pronoun.

- **Q:** Translate: 'My brothers love pizza.'
  **A:** A mis hermanos les encanta la pizza.
- **Q:** Translate: 'Spicy food bothers me.'
  **A:** Me molesta la comida picante.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grocery ads
*Student:* Read a Spanish grocery ad and create a shopping list with a budget.

*How to teach:* Read the grocery ad; your child creates a shopping list of 10 items with prices and a total under a budget (e.g., $40), writing quantities in Spanish (una libra de, una docena de).

- **Q:** How do you say 'a dozen eggs' and 'a pound of beef'?
  **A:** una docena de huevos; una libra de carne de res

### Day 4 (Thursday): Survey
*Student:* Survey family about food preferences and report results.

*How to teach:* Your child surveys family (¿Qué comida te encanta? ¿Qué comida te molesta?) and writes a report in third person.

- **Q:** Report: 'My mom is interested in Mexican food.'
  **A:** A mi mamá le interesa la comida mexicana.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Vocabulary and gustar quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and score the survey report.

- **Q:** Correct: 'A mi abuela gusta los frijoles.'
  **A:** A mi abuela le gustan los frijoles.

**Assessment:** Quiz and survey report.

**Answer key:** Quiz sample key: 1) (yo) encantar / las fresas — Me encantan las fresas 2) (ella) interesar / la cocina — Le interesa la cocina 3) (nosotros) molestar / la comida picante — Nos molesta la comida picante 4) (ellos) gustar / cocinar — Les gusta cocinar 5) la verdura = vegetable; la fruta = fruit; picante = spicy. Survey report rubric: 4 = 5+ people reported with correct pronouns (A mi papá le encanta..., A mis hermanos les molesta...) and a summary sentence; 3 = minor errors; 2 = many pronoun errors; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Line dance and rhythm basics

**Prepare:** Music player, open space; songs at several tempos; 'Cupid Shuffle' or 'Electric Slide' tutorial video; dance-fitness video and HR monitor.

**Watch for:**
- Starting on the wrong count. Correct: listen for the downbeat ('1') of the phrase.
- Watching feet instead of listening. Correct: learn steps slowly, then add music.

### Day 1 (Monday): Finding the beat
*Student:* Clap and step to songs at different tempos; count 8-count phrases.

*How to teach:* Clap and step to songs; count 8-count phrases.

- **Q:** What is tempo?
  **A:** The speed of the beat, measured in beats per minute (bpm).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Basic steps
*Student:* Learn grapevine, step-touch, pivot turn, and kick-ball-change.

*How to teach:* Teach grapevine (side, behind, side, touch), step-touch, pivot turn, kick-ball-change.

- **Q:** Describe a grapevine right.
  **A:** Step right, cross left behind, step right, touch (or tap) left beside right.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Line dance
*Student:* Learn a line dance such as the "Cupid Shuffle" or "Electric Slide."

*How to teach:* Learn the line dance.

- **Q:** In the Cupid Shuffle, what are the first moves?
  **A:** Step/shuffle to the right four counts, then to the left four counts, then kick heels, then walk it by and turn.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cardio dance
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute dance-fitness video and track heart rate.

*How to teach:* 30-minute dance-fitness video; track HR.

- **Q:** Was your HR in the target zone?
  **A:** Answers vary; compare to the 60–85% zone calculated in week 2.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the line dance and self-assess timing.

*How to teach:* Perform the line dance; self-assess timing.

- **Q:** Why is dance considered a lifetime activity?
  **A:** It can be done at any age, alone or socially, and builds cardio fitness and coordination.

**Assessment:** Line dance performance.

**Answer key:** Line dance performance: 4 = all steps correct, on the beat, smooth transitions, completes full sequence with wall changes; 3 = minor errors; 2 = frequent off-beat; 1 = cannot complete.

## Health & Wellness: Bullying, social media and communication

**Prepare:** Phone for screen-time tracking; role-play scenarios for I-messages, active listening and a family conflict. Write the Unit 3 test. Look up California's Safe Place to Learn Act and Seth's Law (AB 9, 2011).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cyberbullying isn't real bullying. Correct: it is repeated harm through digital means and can be more pervasive.
- Believing conflict resolution means one person wins. Correct: aim for win-win solutions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Forms of bullying
*Student:* Define physical, verbal, relational and cyberbullying and identify CA laws requiring schools to address bullying.

*How to teach:* Define four forms of bullying; explain CA law (Seth's Law) requires schools to have anti-bullying policies and respond to reports.

- **Q:** What is relational bullying?
  **A:** Harming someone's relationships or reputation — exclusion, rumors, manipulating friendships.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Social media audit
*Student:* Track your screen time for one day and evaluate how each app affects your mood.

*How to teach:* Track screen time and rate mood after each app.

- **Q:** Which app made you feel best or worst, and why?
  **A:** Answers vary; look for honest evaluation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Communication skills
*Student:* Practice I-messages and active listening in three role-plays.

*How to teach:* Teach I-messages ('I feel __ when __ because __') and active listening (eye contact, paraphrase, ask questions). Role-play three times.

- **Q:** Change 'You never listen!' into an I-message.
  **A:** 'I feel frustrated when I'm interrupted because I want to finish my thought.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conflict resolution
*Student:* Apply a six-step conflict-resolution model to a family disagreement scenario.

*How to teach:* Apply a six-step model: cool down, each tells their side, identify the problem, brainstorm solutions, choose win-win, follow up.

- **Q:** Why cool down first?
  **A:** Strong emotions block clear thinking and escalate conflict.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on mental health, stress, help-seeking and communication.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** What's active listening?
  **A:** Fully focusing on the speaker, showing interest, and paraphrasing to check understanding.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key: signs of mental wellness; emotion/body cues; resilience; adrenaline and cortisol; eustress vs distress; coping strategies; anxiety, depression, ADHD, PTSD symptoms; CBT; stigma; suicide warning signs; ACT; 988/741741; four bullying types; Seth's Law; I-message structure; conflict resolution steps.
