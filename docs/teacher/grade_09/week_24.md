# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 24

## English 9: Romeo and Juliet, Act 3: the turning point

**Prepare:** R&J Act 3. Note: Act 3 contains two deaths and Capulet's harsh threats; prepare to discuss.

**Watch for:**
- Blaming Romeo alone for Mercutio's death: Tybalt stabs Mercutio under Romeo's arm as Romeo tries to stop the fight — Mercutio and Tybalt also chose to fight.
- Missing the shift in genre: before 3.1 the play has comedy; after, tragedy.
- Thinking oxymorons are random: Juliet's oxymorons express her conflict between love for Romeo and grief for Tybalt.

### Day 1 (Monday): The duel
*Student:* Read 3.1; explain how Mercutio's death and "A plague o' both your houses" turn comedy to tragedy.

*How to teach:* Read 3.1; explain how Mercutio's death turns the play to tragedy.

- **Q:** Why does Romeo refuse to fight Tybalt at first?
  **A:** He is now secretly married to Juliet, so Tybalt is his kinsman.
- **Q:** What does Mercutio's curse 'A plague o' both your houses!' mean?
  **A:** He blames both families' feud for his death; it foreshadows the tragedy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Juliet's reaction
*Student:* Read 3.2; analyze Juliet's oxymorons about Romeo.

*How to teach:* Read 3.2; analyze Juliet's oxymorons.

- **Q:** Give two of Juliet's oxymorons about Romeo.
  **A:** 'Beautiful tyrant! fiend angelical! / Dove-feathered raven!' — also 'damnèd saint, an honorable villain.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Banishment
*Student:* Read 3.3–3.4; compare Romeo's despair with the Friar's plan.

*How to teach:* Read 3.3–3.4; compare Romeo's despair with the Friar's plan.

- **Q:** Why does Romeo think banishment is worse than death?
  **A:** Exile from Verona means exile from Juliet — 'heaven is here, where Juliet lives.'
- **Q:** What is the Friar's plan in 3.3?
  **A:** Romeo spends the night with Juliet, then goes to Mantua until the marriage can be announced and a pardon obtained.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Parents and choice
*Student:* Read 3.5; analyze Lord Capulet's ultimatum and Juliet's isolation.

*How to teach:* Read 3.5; analyze Capulet's ultimatum and Juliet's isolation.

- **Q:** What does Capulet threaten if Juliet won't marry Paris?
  **A:** To disown her — she can 'hang, beg, starve, die in the streets.'
- **Q:** How does the Nurse betray Juliet?
  **A:** She advises Juliet to marry Paris and forget Romeo.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Act 3 quiz and a paragraph analyzing 3.1 as the play's turning point.

*How to teach:* Act 3 quiz; turning-point paragraph.

- **Q:** What does Romeo cry after killing Tybalt?
  **A:** 'O, I am fortune's fool!'

**Assessment:** Act 3 quiz and turning-point paragraph.

**Answer key:** Act 3 quiz answers: 1) Tybalt kills Mercutio. 2) Romeo kills Tybalt. 3) The Prince banishes Romeo. 4) Juliet's oxymorons show conflicted feelings. 5) Romeo goes to Mantua. 6) Capulet sets the wedding to Paris for Thursday. 7) Lark vs. nightingale = morning has come. 8) The Nurse advises marrying Paris. Turning-point paragraph: Paragraph rubric: 4 = arguable claim, two well-chosen quotations embedded and cited, commentary that explains HOW the evidence proves the claim; 3 = claim and evidence with thinner commentary; 2 = summary instead of analysis or only one piece of evidence; 1 = off-topic or no evidence.

## Algebra I: Exponential models and comparing growth

**Prepare:** Calculator, Desmos. Formulas: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt); half-life A = A₀(½)^(t/h).

**Watch for:**
- Using r = 5 instead of 0.05 in formulas: convert percents to decimals.
- Believing linear functions with large slopes always stay ahead: any increasing exponential eventually exceeds any linear function.
- Dividing the annual rate by 12 to get the equivalent monthly rate for a given annual growth factor: the equivalent monthly factor is the 12th root (1.15^(1/12) ≈ 1.0117).

### Day 1 (Monday): Compound interest
*Student:* Model savings at 5% compounded yearly and monthly; compare results.

*How to teach:* Model compounding: $1,000 at 5% yearly for 10 years = 1000(1.05)^10; monthly = 1000(1 + 0.05/12)^120. Your child computes and compares.

- **Q:** Find the value of $1,000 at 5% for 10 years compounded yearly and monthly.
  **A:** Yearly: $1,628.89. Monthly: $1,647.01.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eventually exceeds
*Student:* Graph y=100x and y=1.1^x and find where exponential overtakes linear.

*How to teach:* Graph y = 100x and y = 1.1^x in Desmos; zoom out until they cross. Your child finds the crossing point and writes what it shows.

- **Q:** After the graphs first cross near x = 0.01, where does 1.1^x overtake 100x for good?
  **A:** Between x = 96 and x = 97 (1.1^96 ≈ 9,412 < 9,600; 1.1^97 ≈ 10,354 > 9,700).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rewriting exponentials
*Student:* Rewrite 1.15^t as (1.15^(1/12))^(12t) to find monthly rate.

*How to teach:* Rewrite 1.15^t = (1.15^(1/12))^(12t) ≈ 1.0117^(12t). Interpret: 15% yearly growth ≈ 1.17% monthly growth.

- **Q:** What monthly growth rate is equivalent to 15% annual growth?
  **A:** 1.15^(1/12) ≈ 1.0117 → about 1.17% per month.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Half-life
*Student:* Model medicine leaving the body with a half-life function.

*How to teach:* Model a 200 mg dose with a 6-hour half-life: A = 200(½)^(t/6). Your child computes remaining amounts and graphs.

- **Q:** How much of a 200 mg dose remains after 24 hours if the half-life is 6 hours?
  **A:** 24/6 = 4 half-lives: 200/16 = 12.5 mg.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on exponents and exponential functions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test (about 50 minutes).

- **Q:** Simplify 2⁻²·2⁵.
  **A:** 2³ = 8.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test sample answers: 1) (3x²)³ = 27x⁶. 2) 81^(3/4) = 27. 3) √75 = 5√3. 4) Linear or exponential: 5, 10, 20, 40 → exponential (ratio 2). 5) $2,000 at 3% compounded yearly for 5 years → $2,318.55. 6) A town of 12,000 grows 2% per year → P = 12,000(1.02)^t. 7) Half-life 10 days, 80 g → 10 g after 30 days. 8) Which eventually exceeds: 50x or 1.05^x? 1.05^x. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Mutations and genetic technology

**Prepare:** UV-sensitive beads (online or craft store), sunscreen, a clear plastic bag. Mutation worksheet with a sentence of three-letter words (THE FAT CAT ATE THE RAT). Prepare the Unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all mutations are harmful: many are neutral, and some are beneficial (e.g., sickle-cell carriers resist malaria).
- Believing a point mutation always changes the protein: a silent mutation may code the same amino acid.
- Confusing CRISPR with cloning: CRISPR edits specific DNA sequences.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mutation types
*Student:* Model point and frameshift mutations by changing a sentence of three-letter words.

*How to teach:* Model substitution, insertion, and deletion with THE FAT CAT ATE THE RAT.

- **Q:** Delete the first A: what happens and what type of mutation is it?
  **A:** THE FTC ATA TET HER AT — every word after the deletion changes: a frameshift mutation.
- **Q:** Which is usually more damaging, a substitution or a frameshift?
  **A:** Frameshift — it changes every codon after the mutation.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Environmental mutagens
*Student:* Read about UV and mutation; test UV beads in sunlight vs. sunscreen.

*How to teach:* Expose UV beads to sunlight with and without sunscreen.

- **Q:** Predict what happens to beads covered with sunscreen.
  **A:** They change color less, because sunscreen blocks some UV — the same UV that can damage DNA in skin cells.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sickle cell
*Student:* Explain how one base change causes sickle cell disease and malaria resistance.

*How to teach:* Explain sickle cell.

- **Q:** What single change causes sickle-cell disease?
  **A:** An A→T substitution in the β-globin gene (GAG → GTG) replaces glutamic acid with valine.
- **Q:** Why is the sickle allele common where malaria is common?
  **A:** Carriers (heterozygotes) are more resistant to malaria, so they survive and reproduce more.

### Day 4 (Thursday): CRISPR debate
*Student:* Research CRISPR and write a claim about its ethical use.

*How to teach:* Research CRISPR; write a claim about its ethical use.

- **Q:** What does CRISPR-Cas9 do?
  **A:** A guide RNA leads the Cas9 enzyme to a specific DNA sequence, where it cuts so the DNA can be edited.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** Transcribe DNA template TAC CTT into mRNA.
  **A:** AUG GAA.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test sample answers: DNA structure and base pairing; semiconservative replication; transcription/translation of a given sequence; mitosis vs. meiosis; Punnett squares (3:1, 1:2:1, 9:3:3:1); blood types; sex-linked cross; pedigree pattern; mutation types; sickle cell; CRISPR. Scoring: 80%+ proficient. CRISPR claim rubric: 4 = clear claim, scientific explanation, ethical reasoning considering benefits and risks.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Native activism and sovereignty

**Prepare:** The 1969 Alcatraz Proclamation (Indians of All Tribes); readings on termination (1953) and relocation (Relocation Act of 1956); news about a California tribe today.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Alcatraz was just a protest stunt: the 19-month occupation (1969–71) drew national attention to Native rights and influenced federal policy.
- Assuming tribal sovereignty means separation: tribes are sovereign nations with government-to-government relations with the U.S.
- Missing satire: the Proclamation's offer to buy Alcatraz for glass beads and cloth mocks historical land deals.

### Day 1 (Monday): Termination and relocation
*Student:* Read about 1950s federal termination and relocation to Los Angeles and the Bay Area.

*How to teach:* Read about termination and relocation.

- **Q:** What were the federal termination and relocation policies of the 1950s?
  **A:** Termination ended federal recognition of some tribes and their services; relocation encouraged Native people to move from reservations to cities like Los Angeles and the San Francisco Bay Area.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Alcatraz Proclamation
*Student:* Analyze the 1969 Proclamation's satire and claims.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Alcatraz Proclamation's satire and claims.

- **Q:** What did the Proclamation offer to pay for Alcatraz, and why?
  **A:** $24 in glass beads and red cloth — satirizing the legendary purchase of Manhattan and unfair land deals.
- **Q:** Why did it say Alcatraz was suitable for an Indian reservation?
  **A:** Satirically: it lacked running water, sanitation, jobs, health care, and productive land — just like many reservations.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Self-determination
*Student:* Read about the 1970 end of termination policy and the 1975 Indian Self-Determination Act and explain how Alcatraz activism contributed.

*How to teach:* Connect Alcatraz to self-determination policy.

- **Q:** Which 1975 law expanded tribal control over programs?
  **A:** The Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act.
- **Q:** How did President Nixon change policy in 1970?
  **A:** He called for ending termination and supporting tribal self-determination.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Contemporary sovereignty
*Student:* Research a California tribe today (e.g., land back, language revitalization) and write a profile.

*How to teach:* Research and profile a California tribe today.

- **Q:** Give an example of contemporary Native sovereignty work in California.
  **A:** Language revitalization programs, land-back efforts (e.g., land returned to tribes), and tribal management of resources and cultural burning.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Complete a unit assessment with a document-based question.

*How to teach:* Unit 8 DBQ.

- **Q:** When did the Alcatraz occupation take place?
  **A:** November 1969 to June 1971.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 DBQ.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 DBQ rubric: 4 = thesis answering the question (e.g., how California Native peoples resisted and survived from the Mission era to today), uses at least four documents accurately with sourcing, adds outside knowledge, explains change over time; 3 = thesis and three documents; 2 = summary of documents; 1 = no thesis or inaccurate. Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.

## Spanish II: En el consultorio: formal (usted) commands

**Prepare:** Find a CDC en español pamphlet (cdc.gov/spanish) on handwashing or flu. Chart usted/ustedes commands: from yo, drop -o, opposite vowel (tome, beba, descanse); irregulars dé, esté, vaya, sea, sepa. Reflexive commands attach the pronoun (Lávese, Siéntense).

**Watch for:**
- Using tú forms with a patient (*Toma): formal commands are tome, beba, descanse.
- Dropping the accent when attaching pronouns: Lávese, Tómelo (accent keeps the stress).
- Forgetting spelling changes: buscar → busque, llegar → llegue, empezar → empiece.

### Day 1 (Monday): Formal commands
*Student:* Learn usted/ustedes command forms, including irregulars (vaya, sea, dé), and conjugate 20 verbs.

*How to teach:* Show the formation from yo (tengo → tenga, hago → haga). Your child conjugates 20 verbs for usted and ustedes.

- **Q:** Give usted commands for tomar, comer, and salir.
  **A:** tome, coma, salga
- **Q:** Give the ustedes command of ir.
  **A:** vayan

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Health pamphlet
*Student:* Read a CDC en español pamphlet and list commands it uses.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the pamphlet and lists the commands it uses with English meanings.

- **Q:** A pamphlet says 'Cúbrase la boca al toser.' What does it mean?
  **A:** Cover your mouth when you cough.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Advice
*Student:* Write 10 health tips for a doctor's office poster using formal commands (Lávese las manos).

*How to teach:* Your child writes 10 poster tips with formal commands (Lávese las manos con jabón por 20 segundos. Beba mucha agua.).

- **Q:** Write: 'Get a lot of rest.'
  **A:** Descanse mucho.
- **Q:** Write: 'Don't touch your eyes.'
  **A:** No se toque los ojos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pharmacy
*Student:* Role-play buying medicine at a farmacia, describing symptoms and understanding the pharmacist's instructions.

*How to teach:* You are the pharmacist. Your child describes symptoms; you give instructions (Tome una pastilla cada ocho horas). Your child repeats them back to confirm.

- **Q:** What does 'Tome dos pastillas cada seis horas con comida' mean?
  **A:** Take two pills every six hours with food.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Formal commands quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the formal commands quiz.

- **Q:** Make negative: 'Tome café.'
  **A:** No tome café.

**Assessment:** Commands quiz.

**Answer key:** Formal commands quiz sample key: tomar — tome/tomen; beber — beba/beban; descansar — descanse/descansen; evitar — evite/eviten; ir — vaya/vayan; ser — sea/sean; dar — dé/den; hacer — haga/hagan; lavarse las manos — Lávese las manos; negative with pronoun — No se toque la cara.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Aquatic fitness and survival skills

**Prepare:** Pool access; pace clock; chest-deep area for water aerobics; HELP position diagram.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you can't get a hard workout in water. Correct: water resistance and swimming can reach high heart rates.
- Diving into unknown water. Correct: always enter feet-first unless the depth is known to be safe (at least 9 ft for diving).

### Day 1 (Monday): Interval swim
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute interval set (e.g., 8 × 50 yards on 1:30) and record heart rate.

*How to teach:* 8×50 yd on 1:30; record HR.

- **Q:** What does '8 × 50 on 1:30' mean?
  **A:** Swim 50 yards eight times, starting each one every 1 minute 30 seconds; rest is whatever time is left.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water aerobics
*Student:* Do a 25-minute water-aerobics routine of jogs, jumping jacks, and cross-country skis in chest-deep water.

*How to teach:* 25-minute water-aerobics routine.

- **Q:** Why is water exercise easier on joints?
  **A:** Buoyancy reduces body weight on joints, while water resistance still works muscles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Survival skills
*Student:* Practice survival floating and the HELP position, and enter the water feet-first safely.

*How to teach:* Survival floating, HELP position (with life jacket: knees to chest, arms crossed), feet-first entry.

- **Q:** What is the HELP position and why use it?
  **A:** Heat Escape Lessening Posture: in a life jacket, draw knees to chest and hug them to reduce heat loss in cold water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Endurance swim
*Student:* Swim continuously for 15–20 minutes using any strokes.

*How to teach:* Continuous 15–20 minute swim.

- **Q:** How do you pace a long swim?
  **A:** Start easy, breathe rhythmically, switch strokes to rest if needed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Final aquatics assessment: 100 yards mixed strokes, 3-minute tread, and water safety questions.

*How to teach:* Final assessment: 100 yd mixed, 3-minute tread, safety questions.

- **Q:** If you fall into cold water, what should you do first?
  **A:** Stay calm and control breathing for the first minute (cold shock), keep your head above water, then float or get out.

**Assessment:** Final aquatics assessment.

**Answer key:** Final aquatics assessment: 100 yards mixed strokes continuously (pass), tread 3 minutes (pass), safety questions: silent drowning signs, rip current escape, reach-throw-don't go, life jacket fit, feet-first entry, HELP position — 5/6 correct. Proficient = pass both swims and 5/6.

## Health & Wellness: Sexually transmitted infections and HIV

**Prepare:** CDC STI fact sheets and HIV.gov basics; CDC GetTested locator (gettested.cdc.gov). Blank six-infection chart; 15-question quiz. Opt-out alternative: communicable disease project.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you'd know if you had an STI. Correct: many are asymptomatic; testing is the only way to know.
- Believing an STI once cured can't come back. Correct: you can be reinfected.

### Day 1 (Monday): Common STIs
*Student:* Research chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, HPV and herpes: symptoms, testing and treatment.

*How to teach:* Research chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis (bacterial, curable with antibiotics), HPV and herpes (viral, treatable not curable).

- **Q:** Which common STIs are curable?
  **A:** Chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis (and trichomoniasis), with the right antibiotics/medication.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): HIV
*Student:* Learn how HIV attacks the immune system and how it is and isn't transmitted.

*How to teach:* Explain HIV attacks CD4 (T helper) cells; untreated it can progress to AIDS. Transmitted by blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk; not by saliva, hugging, sharing dishes or mosquitoes.

- **Q:** How does HIV damage the immune system?
  **A:** It infects and destroys CD4 T cells, weakening the body's ability to fight infections.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prevention
*Student:* Learn prevention methods including abstinence, condoms, HPV vaccine, PrEP and PEP.

*How to teach:* Prevention: abstinence, condoms, HPV vaccine, PrEP (daily pill or injection before exposure), PEP (within 72 hours after possible exposure), testing.

- **Q:** What is the difference between PrEP and PEP?
  **A:** PrEP is taken before possible exposure to prevent HIV; PEP is an emergency medicine started within 72 hours after possible exposure.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Testing and services
*Student:* Locate confidential testing services for teens in California using the CDC GetTested locator.

*How to teach:* Use the CDC locator; note CA minors 12+ can consent to STI testing and treatment confidentially.

- **Q:** Can a 15-year-old in California get an STI test without parental consent?
  **A:** Yes — minors 12 and older can consent to STI testing and treatment.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Finish an STI fact chart (cause, symptoms, curable or treatable, prevention, testing) for six infections and take a 15-question quiz.

*How to teach:* Complete the chart; give the 15-question quiz.

- **Q:** What does U=U mean?
  **A:** Undetectable = Untransmittable: a person with HIV who maintains an undetectable viral load on treatment does not transmit HIV sexually.

**Assessment:** STI quiz.

**Answer key:** STI chart key: chlamydia – bacteria, often no symptoms, curable (antibiotics), condoms/abstinence, urine/swab test; gonorrhea – bacteria, often none or discharge/burning, curable (drug resistance rising), same prevention, urine/swab; syphilis – bacteria, painless sore then rash, curable (penicillin), blood test; HPV – virus, often none or warts, not curable but vaccine prevents, Pap/HPV test for cervical changes; herpes (HSV) – virus, painful blisters, treatable not curable (antivirals), swab/blood; HIV – virus, flu-like early then none, treatable with ART not curable, PrEP/condoms, blood/oral test. Quiz: 12/15 proficient.
