# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 12

## English 9: Writing and debating an argument essay

**Prepare:** Research log and two sources. A parallel-structure worksheet (10 faulty sentences). Prepare the Unit 3 test on rhetoric and argument.

**Watch for:**
- Weak counterclaims (a straw man): state the opposing view fairly and in its strongest form before rebutting.
- Evidence without reasoning: explain how each statistic or quote supports the claim.
- Faulty parallelism such as 'I like hiking, to swim, and biking' → 'hiking, swimming, and biking.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft the introduction and claim
*Student:* Write an introduction with context and a precise claim; draft one body paragraph with cited evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an introduction with context and a precise claim, then one cited body paragraph.

- **Q:** Make this claim more precise: 'School should start later.'
  **A:** Sample: 'High schools in our district should start no earlier than 8:30 a.m. because later start times improve teen sleep, attendance, and safety.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Counterclaim paragraph
*Student:* Draft a paragraph that fairly states the opposing view and rebuts it with evidence and reasoning.

*How to teach:* Draft the counterclaim-rebuttal paragraph.

- **Q:** What are the two parts of a counterclaim paragraph?
  **A:** A fair statement of the opposing view (with its reasoning), then a rebuttal using evidence and reasoning.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar: parallel structure
*Student:* Fix 10 faulty parallel sentences, then revise your essay to include two deliberate parallel structures.

*How to teach:* Fix 10 faulty parallel sentences; revise the essay to include two deliberate parallel structures.

- **Q:** Fix: 'The coach told us to stretch, hydrating, and that we should rest.'
  **A:** 'The coach told us to stretch, hydrate, and rest.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final draft
*Student:* Finish, revise, and edit the 800–1,000-word argument essay; check citations.

*How to teach:* Finish, revise, and edit the 800–1,000-word essay; check citations.

- **Q:** Where should in-text citations appear for paraphrased evidence?
  **A:** Right after the paraphrased information, in parentheses (author's last name and page if available), even when not quoting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold a 10-minute mini-debate with a family member on your issue; submit the essay and take the Unit 3 test.

*How to teach:* Hold a 10-minute mini-debate; submit the essay; give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** In a debate, what should a rebuttal do?
  **A:** Directly address the opponent's point and explain with evidence why it is weaker than your argument.

**Assessment:** Argument essay (W.9-10.1 rubric), debate observation checklist, Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Argument essay rubric (W.9-10.1): 4 = precise claim, logically sequenced reasons with relevant cited evidence, fair counterclaim with convincing rebuttal, formal tone, strong conclusion, correct MLA; 3 = clear claim and evidence, counterclaim present but thin; 2 = claim unclear or evidence weak, no real counterclaim; 1 = opinion without support. Debate checklist: states claim, uses evidence, listens and responds to opponent, respectful tone. Unit 3 test sample answers: 1) Henry's purpose — rally armed resistance. 2) JFK 'Ask not…' — antithesis/chiasmus. 3) King: 'Injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere' — answers the 'outsider' charge. 4) King's just law definition. 5) Douglass — the Fourth reveals injustice to the slave. 6) Identify fallacies in three items. 7) Short analysis of an unseen paragraph's appeals.

## Algebra I: Writing and graphing linear equations

**Prepare:** Graph paper, ruler, Desmos. Make a card with the three forms: y = mx + b, y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), Ax + By = C.

**Watch for:**
- Using the x-intercept as b: b is where the line crosses the y-axis (x = 0).
- Sign errors in point-slope form: through (2, −5) it is y + 5 = m(x − 2).
- Perpendicular slope as just the negative: it is the negative reciprocal (2 → −1/2).

### Day 1 (Monday): Slope-intercept form
*Student:* Graph 12 equations y=mx+b and interpret m and b in two contexts.

*How to teach:* Model graphing y = −2x + 3: plot (0, 3), go down 2 and right 1. Interpret m and b in context (taxi y = 2.5x + 3). Your child graphs 12 equations and writes two context interpretations.

- **Q:** In the taxi fare y = 2.5x + 3 (x = miles), what do 2.5 and 3 mean?
  **A:** $2.50 per mile; $3 starting fee.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Point-slope form
*Student:* Write equations through given points and slopes; convert to slope-intercept form.

*How to teach:* Model writing through (2, 5) with slope 3: y − 5 = 3(x − 2), then y = 3x − 1. Your child writes equations from points and slopes and converts forms.

- **Q:** Write the line through (2, 5) with slope 3 in slope-intercept form.
  **A:** y = 3x − 1.
- **Q:** Write the line through (1, 2) and (3, 8).
  **A:** m = 3; y − 2 = 3(x − 1) → y = 3x − 1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Standard form
*Student:* Graph using intercepts for equations like 3x+4y=24 (budget for two items).

*How to teach:* Graph 3x + 4y = 24 using intercepts (set x = 0 → y = 6; y = 0 → x = 8). Interpret as a $24 budget for items costing $3 and $4. Your child graphs the standard-form set and interprets one.

- **Q:** Find the intercepts of 3x + 4y = 24 and interpret them as a budget for $3 and $4 items.
  **A:** x-intercept 8 (buy eight $3 items, no $4), y-intercept 6 (six $4 items, no $3).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Parallel and perpendicular
*Student:* Write equations of lines parallel or perpendicular to a given line.

*How to teach:* Parallel lines share slopes; perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals. Your child writes lines through given points parallel/perpendicular to given lines.

- **Q:** Line parallel to y = 2x + 1 through (1, 4)?
  **A:** y = 2x + 2.
- **Q:** Line perpendicular to y = 2x + 1 through (1, 4)?
  **A:** slope −1/2: y = −½x + 4.5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on writing and graphing linear equations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key.

- **Q:** What is the slope of 2x + y = 7?
  **A:** −2 (y = −2x + 7).

**Assessment:** Linear equation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Through (0, −2) and (3, 4) → y = 2x − 2. 2) Slope −1 through (4, 1) → y = −x + 5. 3) 2x − 5y = 10 intercepts: x = 5, y = −2. 4) Parallel to y = −3x + 2 through (0, 7) → y = −3x + 7. 5) Perpendicular to y = ½x through (2, 1) → y = −2x + 5. 6) Gym: $40 joining fee plus $25/month → y = 25x + 40. 7) Graph y = −⅔x + 4 (plot (0, 4), down 2 right 3).

## Biology – The Living Earth: Evidence for evolution: anatomy, embryology, and molecules

**Prepare:** Raw chicken wing (from the grocery), gloves, scissors, paper towels; disinfect surfaces and wash hands (Salmonella). Cytochrome c comparison table (humans and chimpanzees identical; rhesus monkey differs by 1 amino acid; horse by about 12; yeast by about 44). Trait table for 6 animals.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing homologous and analogous: homologous = same underlying structure from common ancestry (human arm, bat wing); analogous = similar function, different origin (bird wing, insect wing).
- Reading a cladogram as a ladder of 'higher' animals: branch points show common ancestors; all living groups are equally 'evolved.'
- Thinking embryos look identical: they share features (pharyngeal pouches, tails) but differ.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chicken wing dissection
*Student:* Dissect a chicken wing and compare its bones with a human arm.

*How to teach:* Dissect the wing; identify humerus, radius, ulna, wrist/digits; compare to a human arm.

- **Q:** Which bones in the chicken wing match the human arm?
  **A:** Humerus (upper), radius and ulna (forearm), and reduced wrist and finger bones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Homologous vs. analogous
*Student:* Sort 12 structures and explain why homologous structures indicate common ancestry.

*How to teach:* Sort 12 structures.

- **Q:** Classify: bat wing and human hand; butterfly wing and bird wing.
  **A:** Homologous; analogous.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Molecular evidence
*Student:* Compare amino acid sequences of cytochrome c across species and rank relatedness.

*How to teach:* Rank relatedness by cytochrome c differences.

- **Q:** Human cytochrome c differs from rhesus monkey by 1 amino acid and from horse by about 12. Which is more closely related to humans?
  **A:** Rhesus monkey — fewer differences indicate a more recent common ancestor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cladograms
*Student:* Build a cladogram for 6 animals from a table of shared derived traits and explain what each branch point means.

*How to teach:* Build a cladogram from shared derived traits.

- **Q:** What does a branch point on a cladogram represent?
  **A:** A common ancestor where a new shared derived trait appeared.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a brochure communicating multiple lines of evidence for common ancestry.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a brochure communicating multiple lines of evidence.

- **Q:** Name four lines of evidence for common ancestry.
  **A:** Fossils, comparative anatomy (homologous structures), embryology, molecular (DNA/protein) similarities (also biogeography).

**Assessment:** Evidence brochure (HS-LS4-1).

**Answer key:** Brochure rubric (HS-LS4-1): 4 = at least four lines of evidence with specific examples (e.g., whale fossils, forelimb homology, vertebrate embryos, cytochrome c data), clear explanation of how each supports common ancestry, accurate visuals; 3 = three lines; 2 = two or vague; 1 = inaccurate.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Supranational organizations and cooperation

**Prepare:** UN (un.org) and EU (european-union.europa.eu) websites; UN Charter preamble; Antarctic Treaty summary. Prepare the Unit 4 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the UN is a world government: it relies on member states' cooperation; only the Security Council can make binding decisions.
- Confusing the EU with Europe: not all European countries are EU members (e.g., Norway, Switzerland, UK since 2020).
- Thinking Antarctica belongs to one country: territorial claims are frozen by the Antarctic Treaty.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cooperation
*Student:* Research the UN and EU and their functions.

*How to teach:* Research UN and EU functions.

- **Q:** When was the UN founded, and how many member states does it have?
  **A:** 1945; 193 member states.
- **Q:** What is one main purpose of the EU?
  **A:** Economic and political cooperation — a single market with free movement of goods, services, capital, and people (and the euro in many members).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Primary source
*Student:* Research the UN and EU: list members, main bodies, and two things each does, and compare them in a chart.

*How to teach:* Compare UN and EU in a chart.

- **Q:** Name the five permanent members of the UN Security Council.
  **A:** United States, United Kingdom, France, Russia, China.
- **Q:** How many members does the EU have?
  **A:** 27 (since the UK left in 2020).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Case study
*Student:* Read the UN Charter preamble (1945) and identify its four goals; explain how World War II shaped its wording.

*How to teach:* Read the UN Charter preamble; identify its goals.

- **Q:** What are the preamble's four main goals?
  **A:** Save future generations from war; reaffirm faith in fundamental human rights and equal rights; establish conditions for justice and respect for international law; promote social progress and better standards of life in larger freedom.
- **Q:** How did World War II shape it?
  **A:** 'The scourge of war, which twice in our lifetime has brought untold sorrow' — the world wars motivated preventing war and protecting human rights.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Debate
*Student:* Read a summary of the 1959 Antarctic Treaty and explain how countries agreed to share a continent without borders.

*How to teach:* Read the Antarctic Treaty summary.

- **Q:** What does the 1959 Antarctic Treaty require?
  **A:** Antarctica is used for peaceful purposes only; scientific cooperation and freedom; military activity and nuclear explosions banned; territorial claims frozen.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** What is a supranational organization?
  **A:** An organization of three or more states that cooperate for shared goals, giving up some sovereignty (e.g., EU, UN).

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test sample answers: culture; language families; religious hearths; diffusion types; state/nation; border types; Berlin Conference; UN founding/purpose and Security Council; EU purpose and members; Antarctic Treaty. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Spanish II: Unit 3 assessment: telling a story

**Prepare:** Find a learner-level version of La Llorona (note: it is a ghost legend about a weeping woman who lost her children — preview for younger siblings). Prepare IPA questions and write the Unit 3 test (preterite regular/irregular, preterite vs. imperfect, celebrations vocabulary).

**Watch for:**
- Using only the preterite in narratives: a strong story mixes background (imperfect) with events (preterite).
- Forgetting irregular preterites under pressure (*tení, *hací): review tuve, hice, fui, estuve, pude.

### Day 1 (Monday): Interpretive
*Student:* Read a short folktale (e.g., La Llorona) and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the folktale independently and answers questions. Discuss why legends like this were told (to warn children away from rivers at night and to teach about consequences).

- **Q:** What does 'llorona' mean and why is she called that?
  **A:** 'Weeping woman' — her ghost cries for her lost children.
- **Q:** Which tense describes the setting of the legend ('vivía una mujer hermosa')?
  **A:** The imperfect.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interpersonal
*Student:* Ask and answer questions about a memorable event.

*How to teach:* Take turns asking and answering about a memorable event: ¿Adónde fuiste? ¿Con quién fuiste? ¿Qué tiempo hacía? ¿Qué pasó? Your child must ask at least 6 questions.

- **Q:** Ask 'What was the weather like?' in the past.
  **A:** ¿Qué tiempo hacía?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Presentational
*Student:* Write a 200-word narrative about a memorable trip or event.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 200-word narrative about a memorable trip or event, with a scene-setting opening in the imperfect and events in the preterite.

- **Q:** Write an opening sentence setting the scene.
  **A:** Example: Era un sábado de julio y hacía mucho calor. Mi familia y yo estábamos en el carro.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Review vocabulary and tenses with a self-made study guide and a Conjuguemos mixed-tense drill.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a one-page study guide (charts of regular and irregular preterite, imperfect, and the tense-choice rules) and completes the Conjuguemos mixed-tense drill.

- **Q:** List three irregular imperfect verbs.
  **A:** ser (era), ir (iba), ver (veía)

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test and review errors.

- **Q:** Fill in: Nosotros ___ (estar) en la fiesta cuando ___ (llegar) el payaso.
  **A:** estábamos; llegó

**Assessment:** IPA and unit test.

**Answer key:** IPA rubric: Interpretive — 4 = sequences the main events of La Llorona (a woman loses her children in a river and her spirit wanders crying '¡Ay, mis hijos!') and answers detail questions accurately; Interpersonal — 4 = asks and answers 6+ questions about an event with correct past tenses; Presentational (200-word narrative) — 4 = clear beginning/middle/end, background in imperfect and events in preterite, 3+ connectors, few errors; 3 = minor errors; 2 = mostly one tense or under 150 words; 1 = fragments. Unit test sample: fui, hiciste, tuvimos, estuvieron; 'Hacía frío cuando llegamos' (imperfect + preterite).

## Visual Art I: Color and expression

**Prepare:** Paints, paper; images of Matisse 'Woman with a Hat' (1905), Munch 'The Scream' (1893), Kirchner's Berlin street scenes (1913–15).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking arbitrary color is random. Correct: Fauves and Expressionists chose non-naturalistic color deliberately for emotion.
- Confusing Fauvism and Expressionism. Correct: Fauvism (France) emphasized bold joyous color; German Expressionism emphasized anxiety and inner feeling with distortion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fauvism
*Student:* Analyze Matisse's "Woman with a Hat" and list arbitrary colors.

*How to teach:* Analyze 'Woman with a Hat'; list arbitrary colors.

- **Q:** Why were the Fauves called 'wild beasts'?
  **A:** A critic at the 1905 Salon d'Automne was shocked by their violent, non-natural colors and called them 'fauves.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Expressionism
*Student:* Analyze Munch's "The Scream" and Kirchner's street scenes.

*How to teach:* Analyze 'The Scream' and Kirchner's street scenes.

- **Q:** How does Munch show anxiety in 'The Scream'?
  **A:** Swirling blood-orange sky, distorted figure, wavy lines and a sharply angled bridge create a sense of panic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Expressive painting
*Student:* Paint a familiar place in arbitrary colors to express a mood.

*How to teach:* Paint a familiar place in arbitrary colors for a mood.

- **Q:** Which colors might express calm?
  **A:** Cool blues, greens and soft violets, with smooth strokes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Finish
*Student:* Finish the painting and title it.

*How to teach:* Finish and title.

- **Q:** Why does a title matter?
  **A:** It guides the viewer's interpretation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short artist statement explaining color choices.

*How to teach:* Write an artist statement explaining color choices.

- **Q:** What is arbitrary color?
  **A:** Color chosen for expression rather than to match reality.

**Assessment:** Expressive painting and statement.

**Answer key:** Expressive painting and statement: 4 = bold intentional arbitrary color clearly creating a named mood, expressive brushwork, statement links specific color choices to the mood and to Fauvist/Expressionist influence; 3 = mood clear, weaker explanation; 2 = colors mostly naturalistic; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Racket sports tournament

**Prepare:** Rackets/paddles, bracket sheet, scorecards. Invite family/friends. Line-calling rules: call your own side; if unsure, the ball is 'in.'

**Watch for:**
- Calling close balls 'out.' Correct: if not sure, it's in.
- Teaching too much at once. Correct: one cue at a time with demonstration.

### Day 1 (Monday): Officiating
*Student:* Learn to officiate and call lines fairly.

*How to teach:* Teach officiating and fair line calls.

- **Q:** In recreational play, who calls the lines?

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tournament day 1
*Student:* Play badminton games against family/friends.

*How to teach:* Badminton tournament games.

- **Q:** What's the score call before serving?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tournament day 2
*Student:* Play pickleball games.

*How to teach:* Pickleball tournament games.

- **Q:** What does '0-0-2' mean at the start of a doubles game?
  **A:** Serving team 0, receiving team 0, second server — the first serving team gets only one server to start.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teach a skill
*Student:* Teach a younger sibling or friend a basic stroke.

*How to teach:* Teach a younger sibling or friend a basic stroke.

- **Q:** What's a good teaching order?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a reflection on strategy and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Write reflection on strategy and sportsmanship.

## Health & Wellness: Suicide prevention and help-seeking

**Prepare:** Parental preview: suicide prevention. Read 988lifeline.org warning signs. Prepare role-play cards and a blank safety plan (Stanley-Brown style: warning signs, coping strategies, people/places, adults to ask, professionals, making environment safe). If your teen discloses suicidal thoughts, stay with them and call/text 988.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a friend's suicidal talk should be kept secret. Correct: always tell a trusted adult.
- Believing a sudden calm after depression means they're better. Correct: sudden calm can be a warning sign if a person has decided on a plan.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warning signs
*Student:* Learn the warning signs and risk factors of suicide from a credible prevention source.

*How to teach:* List warning signs (talking about wanting to die, hopelessness, withdrawing, giving away possessions, increased substance use) and risk factors (previous attempt, depression, access to lethal means, loss).

- **Q:** Name three warning signs of suicide.
  **A:** Talking about wanting to die or being a burden, searching for means, withdrawing, extreme mood swings, giving away possessions, saying goodbye, increased substance use.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): ACT model
*Student:* Practice the Acknowledge–Care–Tell steps through role-play scenarios with a parent.

*How to teach:* Teach ACT: Acknowledge (take it seriously, ask directly), Care (listen, show concern), Tell (a trusted adult). Role-play.

- **Q:** What does ACT stand for?
  **A:** Acknowledge, Care, Tell.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Resources
*Student:* Save 988 and Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741) in your phone and list three trusted adults.

*How to teach:* Save 988 and Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741); list three trusted adults.

- **Q:** What happens when you call or text 988?
  **A:** You reach a trained crisis counselor 24/7, free and confidential.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Self-care plan
*Student:* Complete a personal safety and support plan with coping strategies and people to contact.

*How to teach:* The child completes a personal safety and support plan.

- **Q:** Why include coping strategies and people in a plan?
  **A:** When distressed it's hard to think clearly; a written plan provides steps and contacts ready to use.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Answer scenario questions about how to respond to a friend in crisis.

*How to teach:* Give scenario questions.

- **Q:** A friend texts 'I won't be a problem anymore.' What do you do?
  **A:** Take it seriously: reply and ask directly if they're thinking about suicide, tell a trusted adult immediately and call 911 if they're in immediate danger; share 988.

**Assessment:** Scenario response check and completed support plan.

**Answer key:** Scenario key: each response should Acknowledge (ask directly), Care (listen, don't judge), Tell (adult now; 911 if immediate danger); never keep secret; never leave someone at immediate risk alone. Support plan: 4 = personal warning signs, 3 coping strategies, 2 people/places, 3 adults, 988/741741, safe-environment step.
