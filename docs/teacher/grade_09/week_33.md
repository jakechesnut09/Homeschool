# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 33

## English 9: Research questions and credible sources

**Prepare:** Library card and database access (e.g., Gale or EBSCO via your public library). Purdue OWL annotated bibliography page. A lateral-reading checklist (who is behind this source? what do other sites say about it?).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing questions that are too broad ('the Greeks') or answerable with one fact.
- Judging credibility by how a site looks or its .org domain: read laterally — leave the site and see what others say about it.
- Annotations that only summarize: also evaluate credibility and usefulness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a question
*Student:* Brainstorm topics connected to a text from this year (e.g., Greek hospitality, juvenile justice); narrow to one researchable question.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm topics from the year's texts; narrow to one researchable question.

- **Q:** Narrow 'juvenile justice' into a researchable question.
  **A:** Sample: 'Do diversion programs reduce repeat offenses among teens compared with juvenile detention?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lateral reading
*Student:* Learn to check sources by reading laterally; evaluate five websites with a credibility checklist.

*How to teach:* Teach lateral reading; your child evaluates five websites with the checklist.

- **Q:** What is lateral reading?
  **A:** Opening new tabs to research the source itself — who publishes it, their funding and reputation — instead of judging only from the site.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finding sources
*Student:* Locate six sources (two print or database) and write annotated bibliography entries for three.

*How to teach:* Locate six sources (two print/database); write three annotated entries.

- **Q:** What does an annotated bibliography entry include?
  **A:** The MLA citation, a summary of the source's main points, and an evaluation of its credibility and usefulness.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Summarizing sources
*Student:* Write objective summaries of the central idea of two key sources.

*How to teach:* Write objective summaries of two key sources.

- **Q:** What should an objective summary avoid?
  **A:** Personal opinion and minor details; it states the central idea and key supporting points.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit research question and annotated bibliography for approval.

*How to teach:* Submit question and bibliography for approval.

- **Q:** Why include print or database sources?
  **A:** They are usually edited/peer-reviewed and give more reliable, deeper information.

**Assessment:** Annotated bibliography (6 sources) and approved research question.

**Answer key:** Annotated bibliography rubric: 4 = six credible, relevant sources in correct MLA format with annotations summarizing and evaluating each; 3 = minor formatting errors or thin evaluations; 2 = fewer than six or weak sources; 1 = incomplete. Approved question: focused, debatable or explorable, researchable with available sources.

## Algebra I: Choosing methods and quadratic applications

**Prepare:** Desmos. Mixed equation list (16).

**Watch for:**
- Always using the quadratic formula: it works, but square roots or factoring are faster when the structure fits.
- Forgetting that a projectile hits the ground when h = 0 (not at the vertex).
- Judging growth from small x: x² beats 2^x for small values, but 2^x wins eventually.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing methods
*Student:* Solve 16 mixed equations, choosing factoring, square roots, completing the square, or the formula.

*How to teach:* For each equation, decide: no b-term → square roots; factors nicely → factoring; else formula or completing the square.

- **Q:** Solve x² + x − 12 = 0.
  **A:** (x + 4)(x − 3) = 0 → −4, 3.
- **Q:** Solve x² + 4x − 1 = 0.
  **A:** Completing the square: (x + 2)² = 5 → x = −2 ± √5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Projectile problems
*Student:* Solve when a launched object hits the ground or reaches a height.

*How to teach:* Set h(t) = 0 (or a target height) and solve.

- **Q:** When does h = −16t² + 32t + 48 hit the ground?
  **A:** −16(t² − 2t − 3) = 0 → (t − 3)(t + 1) = 0 → t = 3 s (reject −1).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Linear, quadratic, exponential
*Student:* Compare growth of 10x, x², and 2^x in a table and explain which eventually wins.

*How to teach:* Make a table of 10x, x², 2^x for x = 1..12. Your child describes which leads when.

- **Q:** Compare 10x, x², and 2^x at x = 10 and x = 12.
  **A:** x = 10: 100, 100, 1024. x = 12: 120, 144, 4096. 2^x wins.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rejecting solutions
*Student:* Solve applications where one solution isn't viable (negative time or length).

*How to teach:* Solve and check each solution in context; discard negative time or length.

- **Q:** A square garden's area increases by 33 ft² when each side is lengthened by 3 ft. Original side?
  **A:** (s + 3)² − s² = 33 → 6s + 9 = 33 → s = 4 ft.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on applications.

*How to teach:* Give the applications quiz.

- **Q:** Why reject t = −1 in a projectile problem?
  **A:** Time before launch isn't part of the situation.

**Assessment:** Applications quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) x² − 16 = 0 → ±4. 2) x² − 7x + 10 = 0 → 2, 5. 3) x² + 2x − 5 = 0 → −1 ± √6. 4) h = −16t² + 64t: lands at t = 4 s; max 64 ft at t = 2. 5) h = −16t² + 48t + 4 reaches 36 ft when 16t² − 48t + 32 = 0 → t = 1 s and 2 s. 6) Rectangle area 60 with length 4 more than width → 6 × 10.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Engineering solutions for biodiversity: defining the problem

**Prepare:** Local environmental reports (county or state parks, Xerces Society, CA Department of Fish and Wildlife). Engineering design process poster: define, research, brainstorm, prototype, test, iterate.

**Watch for:**
- Defining the problem as a solution ('we need a bee hotel'): define the need ('native bees lack nesting sites in our neighborhood').
- Vague criteria ('it should work well'): make criteria measurable (e.g., 'attracts at least 3 bee species in 4 weeks').
- Ignoring constraints such as cost, time, and safety.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choosing a problem
*Student:* Choose a local biodiversity problem (e.g., native pollinator decline).

*How to teach:* Choose a local biodiversity problem.

- **Q:** What makes a good engineering problem for this project?
  **A:** Specific, local, connected to biodiversity, and realistically addressable with available resources.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Criteria and constraints
*Student:* Define qualitative and quantitative criteria and constraints.

*How to teach:* Define criteria and constraints.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a criterion and a constraint?
  **A:** A criterion is a goal the solution should meet (what success looks like); a constraint is a limit (cost, time, materials).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Research
*Student:* Research existing solutions and their effectiveness.

*How to teach:* Research existing solutions.

- **Q:** Name one existing solution to native pollinator decline.
  **A:** Planting native flowering plants and pollinator gardens (also nesting habitats, reduced pesticides).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Breaking down
*Student:* Break the biodiversity problem into 3–4 smaller sub-problems (habitat, food source, public awareness) and rank them.

*How to teach:* Break the problem into 3–4 sub-problems and rank them.

- **Q:** Why break a big problem into sub-problems?
  **A:** Smaller parts are easier to solve and test, and ranking helps focus effort on what matters most.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit a problem statement with criteria and constraints for feedback and revise it.

*How to teach:* Submit the problem statement for feedback; revise.

- **Q:** Write a sample problem statement.
  **A:** 'Native bees in our neighborhood lack nesting sites and early-spring flowers; we need a low-cost solution under $30 that increases visits by native bees.'

**Assessment:** Problem statement and criteria list.

**Answer key:** Problem statement rubric: 4 = specific local problem with evidence, at least three measurable criteria and three realistic constraints, sub-problems ranked with reasoning; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = vague criteria; 1 = incomplete.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Filipino, Southeast Asian, and Pacific Islander communities

**Prepare:** Readings and oral histories: Filipino manongs and Stockton's Little Manila; Vietnamese, Cambodian, and Hmong refugee accounts; Native Hawaiian sovereignty or Samoan communities; Pew Research data on Asian American income by origin group. Prepare the Unit 11 assessment.

**Watch for:**
- Treating 'Asian American' as one group: it includes dozens of origin groups with different histories and outcomes.
- The model minority myth: averages hide groups with high poverty (e.g., some Southeast Asian communities) and pit minorities against each other.
- Grouping Pacific Islanders with Asian Americans without distinction: Native Hawaiians and Pacific Islanders have distinct Indigenous histories.

### Day 1 (Monday): Filipino Americans
*Student:* Read about manongs and Stockton's Little Manila.

*How to teach:* Read about manongs and Little Manila.

- **Q:** Who were the manongs?
  **A:** Filipino men who came in the 1920s–30s to work in agriculture and service jobs; restrictive laws (including anti-miscegenation laws) kept many single, and Stockton's Little Manila was their center.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Southeast Asian refugees
*Student:* Read accounts of Vietnamese, Cambodian, and Hmong refugees.

*How to teach:* Read refugee accounts.

- **Q:** Why did many Hmong refugees come to California?
  **A:** Many Hmong had allied with the U.S. during the CIA's 'Secret War' in Laos; after 1975 they faced persecution and fled, resettling in places like Fresno and Sacramento.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pacific Islanders
*Student:* Research Native Hawaiian sovereignty or Samoan communities.

*How to teach:* Research Native Hawaiian sovereignty or Samoan communities.

- **Q:** What happened in Hawaii in 1893?
  **A:** American businessmen, backed by U.S. Marines, overthrew Queen Liliʻuokalani; the U.S. formally apologized in 1993.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Model minority myth
*Student:* Analyze income and poverty data for different Asian American groups and explain why averages hide differences (the model minority myth).

*How to teach:* Analyze income data and the model minority myth.

- **Q:** Why does the 'model minority' label mislead?
  **A:** Averages for 'Asian Americans' mask large differences between groups — some have high median incomes while others (such as Hmong and Burmese Americans) have high poverty rates.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 11 assessment
*Student:* Complete a unit assessment: key terms and a document-based paragraph using EO 9066 and the Civil Liberties Act of 1988.

*How to teach:* Unit 11 assessment.

- **Q:** About how many Japanese Americans were incarcerated during WWII?
  **A:** About 120,000.

**Assessment:** Unit 11 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 11 assessment answers: Chinese Exclusion Act (1882); Angel Island; Wong Kim Ark (1898); EO 9066 (1942); Korematsu; Civil Liberties Act (1988); manongs; Southeast Asian refugees; 1893 overthrow; model minority myth. Document paragraph (EO 9066 and the 1988 Act): proficient = compares the government's 1942 action and language with its 1988 findings and apology, citing both. Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim, two specific pieces of evidence (with source names/dates), explanation of how evidence supports the claim, accurate history; 3 = claim and evidence with thin explanation; 2 = general statements without evidence; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish II: Music of the Spanish-speaking world

**Prepare:** Make a playlist: salsa (e.g., Celia Cruz), cumbia (Colombian cumbia), flamenco (Paco de Lucía), mariachi (Vicente Fernández or Mariachi Vargas), son jarocho ('La Bamba'). Print lyrics for 'La Bamba' (traditional son jarocho from Veracruz). Preview 'Latinoamérica' by Calle 13 if used (clean, but politically themed).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'La Bamba' is originally a rock song: it is a traditional son jarocho from Veracruz, Mexico, made famous in rock form by Ritchie Valens in 1958.
- Placing flamenco in Latin America: flamenco comes from Andalusia in southern Spain, with Romani (gitano), Moorish, and Andalusian roots.

### Day 1 (Monday): Genre tour
*Student:* Listen to salsa, cumbia, flamenco, mariachi, and son jarocho and note instruments and rhythm in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Play a clip of each genre; your child notes instruments and rhythm in Spanish (la guitarra, la trompeta, el violín, los tambores, las palmas).

- **Q:** Where is mariachi music from and what instruments does it use?
  **A:** Jalisco, Mexico — violins, trumpets, guitar, vihuela, and guitarrón (bass).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lyrics
*Student:* Analyze the lyrics of "La Bamba" (son jarocho) or "Latinoamérica" (Calle 13): identify the theme and figurative language.

*How to teach:* Read the 'La Bamba' lyrics; your child identifies the theme (dancing la bamba requires 'una poca de gracia') and playful lines like 'Yo no soy marinero, soy capitán.'

- **Q:** What does 'Para bailar la bamba se necesita una poca de gracia' mean?
  **A:** To dance la bamba you need a little grace.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Origins
*Student:* Research one genre's African, Indigenous, and European roots and write a paragraph.

*How to teach:* Your child researches one genre's African, Indigenous, and European roots and writes a paragraph.

- **Q:** What are the roots of cumbia?
  **A:** It began on Colombia's Caribbean coast, blending African drums and rhythms, Indigenous flutes (gaitas), and European/Spanish melodies and later instruments.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Playlist
*Student:* Create a 6-song playlist with a Spanish description of each song.

*How to teach:* Your child builds a 6-song playlist with a Spanish description of each (genre, country, opinion).

- **Q:** Write a description: 'This salsa song is very energetic and makes me want to dance.'
  **A:** Esta canción de salsa es muy enérgica y me dan ganas de bailar.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Listening quiz identifying genres and lyric comprehension.

*How to teach:* Give the listening quiz (identify genres) and lyric comprehension questions.

- **Q:** Where does flamenco come from?
  **A:** Andalusia, in southern Spain.

**Assessment:** Listening quiz and annotated playlist.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz key: identify each clip — salsa (Afro-Cuban rhythms, clave, brass, developed in New York); cumbia (Colombian Caribbean coast, African drums, Indigenous gaitas/flutes, European elements); flamenco (Andalusia, Spain: guitar, cante, palmas); mariachi (Jalisco, Mexico: violins, trumpets, vihuela, guitarrón); son jarocho (Veracruz, Mexico: jarana, requinto, harp, zapateado). Lyric comprehension: answers must match the provided lyrics. Annotated playlist rubric: 4 = 6 songs, genre/country, 2–3 accurate Spanish sentences each with opinion; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = fewer songs or English; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Designing a personal fitness plan

**Prepare:** Fitness portfolio, calendar, 4-week planning template with FITT for cardio, strength and flexibility.

**Watch for:**
- Making a plan that's too ambitious. Correct: start realistic and progress.
- Ignoring barriers. Correct: plan specific strategies for time, weather and cost.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review data
*Student:* Review your fall and spring fitness data and identify two priorities.

*How to teach:* Review data; identify two priorities.

- **Q:** How do you choose priorities?
  **A:** Pick components outside the Healthy Fitness Zone or furthest from goals, plus what matters to you.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan design
*Student:* Write a 4-week plan with FITT for cardio, strength, and flexibility.

*How to teach:* Write a 4-week plan with FITT.

- **Q:** FITT for flexibility?
  **A:** Example: F 3–5 days/week; I mild tension; T hold 15–30 s, 2–4 reps per stretch, 15 min; T static stretching/yoga.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Barriers
*Student:* Identify barriers (time, weather, cost) and strategies to overcome them.

*How to teach:* Identify barriers and strategies.

- **Q:** Give a strategy for 'no time.'
  **A:** Short 10-minute workouts, schedule exercise like an appointment, active transport.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Start plan
*Student:* Complete day 1 of your plan and log it.

*How to teach:* Start plan; log day 1.

- **Q:** Why log right away?
  **A:** Accurate records and accountability.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit the plan for feedback.

*How to teach:* Submit for feedback.

- **Q:** What feedback would improve the plan?
  **A:** Answers vary; check balance, rest days and progression.

**Assessment:** Personal fitness plan.

**Answer key:** Personal fitness plan: 4 = 4 weeks, FITT for cardio/strength/flexibility, progression week to week, rest days, warm-up/cool-down, barriers and strategies, tied to two data-based priorities; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing components; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Consumer health and the health care system

**Prepare:** Three health product ads; glossary of insurance terms (premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, out-of-pocket max, network); an OTC label (e.g., acetaminophen); medication/allergy list template.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a deductible is a monthly fee. Correct: the premium is the monthly fee; the deductible is what you pay for care before insurance starts paying.
- Taking two products with the same active ingredient. Correct: check active ingredients to avoid overdose (e.g., acetaminophen in cold medicines and pain relievers).

### Day 1 (Monday): Advertising claims
*Student:* Evaluate three health product ads (supplement, acne cream, fitness gadget) for claims, evidence and red flags of quackery.

*How to teach:* Evaluate claims and quackery red flags.

- **Q:** Name two quackery red flags.
  **A:** Miracle cure, 'secret formula,' cures many unrelated conditions, only testimonials, pressure to buy now.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Health insurance
*Student:* Learn insurance terms and how Medi-Cal and Covered California work.

*How to teach:* Teach insurance terms; Medi-Cal (CA Medicaid, low/no cost) and Covered California (marketplace with subsidies).

- **Q:** Define premium, deductible and copay.
  **A:** Premium: regular payment to have insurance. Deductible: amount you pay for covered care before insurance pays. Copay: fixed amount you pay for a visit or prescription.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Medications
*Student:* Compare brand-name vs. generic drugs and read an OTC drug label.

*How to teach:* Compare brand vs. generic; read the OTC Drug Facts label (active ingredient, uses, warnings, directions).

- **Q:** Where on an OTC label do you find the maximum dose?
  **A:** In the Directions section (and Warnings for overdose cautions).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Being a patient
*Student:* Prepare a list of questions and a medication/allergy list for a doctor visit, then role-play checking in and describing symptoms.

*How to teach:* Prepare questions and a med/allergy list; role-play a visit.

- **Q:** Give a good question for a doctor.
  **A:** 'What are the side effects of this medicine?' or 'When should I follow up?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Take a consumer health quiz on insurance terms, OTC labels and evaluating claims, including calculating a cost with a deductible and copay.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz with a deductible problem.

- **Q:** Your deductible is $500 and you've paid $0 so far. A $1,200 bill comes; after the deductible you pay 20% coinsurance. What do you owe?
  **A:** $500 + 20% of $700 = $500 + $140 = $640.

**Assessment:** Consumer health quiz.

**Answer key:** Consumer health quiz key: premium/deductible/copay/coinsurance/out-of-pocket max definitions; Medi-Cal vs Covered California; generics same active ingredient; OTC label sections; avoid doubling active ingredients; quackery red flags; sample: $500 deductible, 20% coinsurance on $1,200 → $640; $30 copay visit → pay $30.
