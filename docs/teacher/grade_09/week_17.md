# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 17

## English 9: The Odyssey: the Cyclops and Circe (Books 9–10)

**Prepare:** Odyssey Books 9–10. Prepare a cause-effect chain template and the seminar rubric (prepared, cites text, builds on others, asks questions).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking blinding the Cyclops causes all of Odysseus's troubles: it's his boast revealing his name that lets Polyphemus curse him through Poseidon.
- Equating 'hubris' with any pride: hubris is excessive pride that defies the gods or limits and brings punishment.
- Judging leadership only by results: weigh decisions, care for men, and mistakes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Book 9: Cyclops
*Student:* Read the Cyclops episode; list each clever choice and each mistake Odysseus makes.

*How to teach:* Read Book 9 (Cyclops); your child lists clever choices and mistakes.

- **Q:** What clever trick does Odysseus use with his name?
  **A:** He tells Polyphemus his name is 'Nobody,' so when Polyphemus cries that 'Nobody' is hurting him, the other Cyclopes don't help.
- **Q:** Name one mistake Odysseus makes in the cave episode.
  **A:** Staying in the cave to see the owner and gain gifts despite his men urging them to leave (or later boasting his real name).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hubris
*Student:* Analyze Odysseus's boast to Polyphemus and its consequences in a cause-effect chain.

*How to teach:* Analyze the boast and its consequences in a cause-effect chain.

- **Q:** What happens because Odysseus shouts his real name?
  **A:** Polyphemus prays to his father Poseidon to curse Odysseus — that he reach home late, alone, having lost all his men, and find trouble at home.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Book 10: Aeolus and Circe
*Student:* Read Book 10; compare how Odysseus leads his men here vs. in Book 9.

*How to teach:* Read Book 10; compare Odysseus's leadership with Book 9.

- **Q:** What happens with Aeolus's bag of winds?
  **A:** Within sight of Ithaca, while Odysseus sleeps, the crew opens the bag thinking it holds treasure; the winds blow them back.
- **Q:** How does Odysseus overcome Circe?
  **A:** Hermes gives him the herb moly to resist her drugs; he forces her to restore his men.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Seminar prep
*Student:* Prepare three open-ended discussion questions about leadership with text references.

*How to teach:* Your child writes three open-ended leadership questions with text references.

- **Q:** Example of an open-ended seminar question?
  **A:** 'Is Odysseus responsible for the loss of his men, or are they responsible for their own fates? Use Books 9 and 10.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold a 20-minute seminar discussion on "Is Odysseus a good leader?" and write a reflection.

*How to teach:* Hold a 20-minute seminar on whether Odysseus is a good leader; your child writes a reflection.

- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence that Odysseus is a good leader.
  **A:** He devises the escape under the rams' bellies and saves the surviving men from the Cyclops (or goes alone to rescue men from Circe).

**Assessment:** Seminar participation rubric and written reflection.

**Answer key:** Seminar rubric: 4 = cites specific episodes from Books 9–10, weighs both sides, builds on others' points, asks a deepening question; 3 = participates with some evidence; 2 = opinions without evidence; 1 = minimal participation. Reflection proficient: takes a position on Odysseus's leadership supported by at least two specific episodes and acknowledges a counterpoint.

## Algebra I: Linear inequalities in two variables and systems of inequalities

**Prepare:** Colored pencils, graph paper, Desmos. Write the bake-sale scenario: cookies take 10 minutes per batch, brownies 20 minutes per batch, 240 minutes available; ingredients limit total batches to 15.

**Watch for:**
- Solid vs. dashed boundary confusion: < and > use dashed lines; ≤ and ≥ use solid lines.
- Shading the wrong side: always test a point (like (0, 0)) not on the line.
- Accepting fractional or negative solutions in context: batches must be whole numbers ≥ 0.

### Day 1 (Monday): Half-planes
*Student:* Graph 10 inequalities in two variables using dashed/solid boundaries and test points.

*How to teach:* Model y > 2x − 1: dashed line, test (0, 0): 0 > −1 true, shade the side containing the origin. Your child graphs 10 inequalities with test points.

- **Q:** Graph y > 2x − 1. Is the line dashed or solid, and which side is shaded?
  **A:** Dashed; shade above the line (the side with (0, 0), since 0 > −1).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Systems of inequalities
*Student:* Graph systems and shade the overlapping solution region.

*How to teach:* Graph y ≤ x + 2 and y > −x in different colors; the overlap is the solution. Your child graphs systems and tests one point in the overlap in both inequalities.

- **Q:** Is (0, 1) a solution of y ≤ x + 2 and y > −x?
  **A:** Yes: 1 ≤ 2 and 1 > 0.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Constraints
*Student:* Model a bake-sale problem (time, ingredients) with inequalities and identify viable combinations.

*How to teach:* Define c = cookie batches, b = brownie batches. Write constraints: 10c + 20b ≤ 240, c + b ≤ 15, c ≥ 0, b ≥ 0. Your child graphs and lists several viable combinations.

- **Q:** Write the time constraint and check whether 10 cookie batches and 6 brownie batches is possible.
  **A:** 10c + 20b ≤ 240; 100 + 120 = 220 ≤ 240 ✓, and 10 + 6 = 16 > 15 ✗ (fails the ingredient limit), so not possible.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Viable or not
*Student:* Decide whether given points are viable solutions in context and explain.

*How to teach:* Test given points in all constraints and in context sense (whole batches). Your child decides viability for each point with a written reason.

- **Q:** Is (6, 4.5) viable for the bake sale?
  **A:** It satisfies the inequalities (60 + 90 = 150 ≤ 240; 10.5 ≤ 15) but half a batch may not make sense — viable only if half batches are allowed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on inequalities in two variables.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the key.

- **Q:** Which boundary is solid: y ≥ 3x or y < 3x?
  **A:** y ≥ 3x.

**Assessment:** Inequality systems quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) y ≤ −x + 3: solid line, shade below (contains origin). 2) y > ½x − 2: dashed, shade above. 3) Is (1, 1) a solution of y ≥ x and y < 3? Yes. 4) Is (4, 1) a solution of y ≥ x and y < 3? No (1 < 4). 5) A model: x + y ≤ 20 and 5x + 8y ≥ 120 — (12, 8) works (20 ≤ 20; 60 + 64 = 124 ≥ 120). 6) Explain why (−2, 5) is not viable for counts of items — negative quantity.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Extinction and changing environments

**Prepare:** Print a graph of extinction rates over the Phanerozoic showing the 'Big Five' (end-Ordovician ~445 mya, Late Devonian ~372 mya, end-Permian ~252 mya, end-Triassic ~201 mya, end-Cretaceous 66 mya). Information on the iridium layer and Chicxulub crater. Prepare the Unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Believing all dinosaurs vanished: birds are living dinosaurs.
- Thinking extinction is rare/unnatural: most species that ever lived are extinct; background extinction is normal, but mass extinctions are sudden spikes.
- Claiming a sixth mass extinction is either proven or a hoax: evaluate rates — current extinction rates are estimated far above background, but we have not (yet) lost ~75% of species.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mass extinctions
*Student:* Analyze graphs of the five mass extinctions and their causes.

*How to teach:* Analyze the extinction graph; list the five and causes.

- **Q:** Which was the largest mass extinction and what likely caused it?
  **A:** The end-Permian (~252 mya), when roughly 90% or more of marine species died; linked to massive Siberian Traps volcanism, warming, and ocean acidification/low oxygen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): K-Pg boundary
*Student:* Evaluate evidence for the asteroid impact (iridium layer, Chicxulub crater).

*How to teach:* Evaluate the asteroid evidence.

- **Q:** What evidence supports an asteroid impact at the K-Pg boundary?
  **A:** A worldwide iridium-rich clay layer (iridium is rare on Earth, common in asteroids), shocked quartz, tektites, and the Chicxulub crater in the Yucatán dated to 66 million years ago.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recovery
*Student:* Explain how mammals radiated after the dinosaurs.

*How to teach:* Explain the mammal radiation.

- **Q:** Why did mammals diversify after the dinosaurs went extinct?
  **A:** Many ecological niches were empty, so surviving small mammals radiated into new forms (adaptive radiation).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Current extinction
*Student:* Evaluate claims that a sixth mass extinction is underway.

*How to teach:* Evaluate the sixth-extinction claim with data.

- **Q:** What evidence supports a current extinction crisis?
  **A:** Extinction rates far above background rates and sharp population declines driven by habitat loss, invasive species, pollution, overharvesting, and climate change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on evolution.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** When did the non-avian dinosaurs go extinct?
  **A:** About 66 million years ago.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test sample answers: superposition; half-life math; transitional fossils; homologous vs. analogous; cytochrome c reasoning; cladogram reading; four factors of natural selection; peppered moths; frequency calculations; drift vs. selection; founder effect; species concept; isolation mechanisms; K-Pg evidence. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Globalization and California as a global region

**Prepare:** Two opposing articles on globalization; California relief map; Census data (California population about 10.6 million in 1950 and about 39.5 million in 2020).

**Watch for:**
- Treating globalization as all good or all bad: it brings both gains (cheaper goods, exports, exchange) and costs (job losses in some sectors, cultural homogenization).
- Thinking California is one region: its regions differ greatly in climate and economy.
- Recommending priorities without data.

### Day 1 (Monday): Globalization pros and cons
*Student:* Read two opposing articles on globalization and chart claims about jobs, culture, and environment.

*How to teach:* Chart claims about globalization.

- **Q:** Give one argument for and one against globalization.
  **A:** For: lower prices and access to global markets for exports. Against: manufacturing job losses and environmental harm from long supply chains.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California's regions
*Student:* Map California's eight geographic regions (North Coast, Central Valley, Sierra Nevada, Deserts, etc.) with economy and climate notes.

*How to teach:* Map California's regions with economy/climate notes.

- **Q:** Describe the Central Valley's economy and climate.
  **A:** Hot, dry summers and mild winters; one of the world's most productive agricultural regions (almonds, grapes, tomatoes, dairy) relying on irrigation.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Then and now
*Student:* Compare 1950 and present-day California census data on population, industries, and diversity.

*How to teach:* Compare 1950 and present census data.

- **Q:** How has California's population changed since 1950?
  **A:** From about 10.6 million to about 39.5 million (2020), with much greater ethnic diversity and a shift toward technology and service industries.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Planning the future
*Student:* Use drought, wildfire, and housing data to recommend one priority for California's next 20 years.

*How to teach:* Recommend a 20-year priority using data.

- **Q:** What makes a recommendation strong?
  **A:** Clear priority, supporting data, consideration of costs and trade-offs, and specific actions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short regional report on one California region.

*How to teach:* Short regional report.

- **Q:** What should a regional report include?
  **A:** Location and boundaries, physical features and climate, population and economy, and a current challenge.

**Assessment:** California regional report.

**Answer key:** California regional report rubric: 4 = accurate physical and human geography, a map, data with sources, analysis of one challenge; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = descriptive only; 1 = inaccurate.

## Spanish II: Semester review: tenses, pronouns, and commands

**Prepare:** Assemble a review packet from Units 1–4: present, reflexive, preterite, imperfect, object pronouns, gustar-type verbs, and tú commands. Print a 15-item error-analysis sheet and the NCSSFL-ACTFL can-do statements (Novice High). Prepare a timed practice exam.

**Watch for:**
- Double pronouns with affirmative commands: when attaching, add an accent to keep the stress (Prepáralo, Dámelo).
- Shifting tenses randomly in narratives: decide for each verb whether it is background (imperfect) or an event (preterite).

### Day 1 (Monday): Tense review
*Student:* Complete a mixed Conjuguemos drill on present, reflexive, preterite, and imperfect, then rewrite a short paragraph from present into past.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the mixed drill, then rewrites a present-tense paragraph into the past, deciding preterite or imperfect for each verb.

- **Q:** Rewrite in the past: 'Es sábado. Hace sol. Voy al mercado y compro frutas.'
  **A:** Era sábado. Hacía sol. Fui al mercado y compré frutas.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pronouns and commands
*Student:* Rewrite a recipe using pronouns and commands (Córtalo. No lo frías.) and correct a 15-item error-analysis sheet.

*How to teach:* Your child rewrites a recipe with commands and pronouns (Corta la cebolla → Córtala; No frías el huevo → No lo frías) and corrects the 15-item error sheet.

- **Q:** Fix: 'No lo cortas.' (negative tú command)
  **A:** No lo cortes.
- **Q:** Make affirmative with a pronoun: 'Pon la mesa.'
  **A:** Ponla.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speaking practice
*Student:* Answer 10 recorded conversation prompts (¿Qué hacías de niño? ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?) and self-score with the rubric.

*How to teach:* Your child records answers to 10 prompts, speaking for at least 30 seconds each, and self-scores with the rubric.

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?'
  **A:** Example: El sábado fui al cine con mi hermana y el domingo descansé en casa.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Writing practice
*Student:* Write a 200-word narrative about a holiday meal combining preterite, imperfect, and food vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 200-word narrative about a holiday meal, mixing imperfect (description) and preterite (events) with food vocabulary.

- **Q:** Write: 'The table was full of food and we ate for three hours.'
  **A:** La mesa estaba llena de comida y comimos por tres horas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Can-do check
*Student:* Rate yourself on NCSSFL-ACTFL can-do statements and take a timed practice exam.

*How to teach:* Your child rates the can-do statements, then takes the timed practice exam. Review errors and plan targeted review for next week.

- **Q:** Fill in: Mientras mi tía ___ (cocinar), mis primos ___ (llegar).
  **A:** cocinaba; llegaron

**Assessment:** Practice exam and can-do self-assessment.

**Answer key:** Practice exam: score each section out of 25 (grammar, reading, writing, speaking). Sample items: 1) Me ___ (despertar) a las seis — despierto 2) De niño, ___ (ir) al parque — iba 3) Ayer ___ (hacer) la tarea — hice 4) Rewrite 'Corta la cebolla' with a pronoun — Córtala 5) Negative — No la cortes. Can-do self-assessment: student marks each statement 'I can do this easily / with help / not yet'; plan practice for 'not yet' items.

## Visual Art I: Graphic design: the public poster

**Prepare:** Poster board or a free design app, markers; Library of Congress WPA poster collection (1936–43). Pick a real community event.

**Watch for:**
- Making all text the same size. Correct: create hierarchy — title largest, key info next, details smallest.
- Using too many colors and fonts. Correct: limit to three colors and two typefaces.

### Day 1 (Monday): WPA posters
*Student:* Analyze five 1930s WPA posters from the Library of Congress and note how each uses hierarchy, color, and lettering.

*How to teach:* Analyze five WPA posters.

- **Q:** What is visual hierarchy?
  **A:** Arranging elements by size, color and placement so viewers notice the most important information first.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Thumbnails
*Student:* Sketch six thumbnails for a poster promoting a real community event (library program, farmers market, park cleanup).

*How to teach:* Six thumbnails for a real event.

- **Q:** What information must an event poster include?
  **A:** What, when, where (and who/how to join).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lettering
*Student:* Practice hand-lettering the title in two styles and choose one that matches the event's mood.

*How to teach:* Hand-letter the title in two styles.

- **Q:** How can lettering show mood?
  **A:** Bold blocky letters feel strong; flowing script feels elegant; playful letters feel fun.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final poster
*Student:* Produce the final 11x17 poster using a limited palette of three colors.

*How to teach:* Final 11×17 poster in three colors.

- **Q:** Why limit the palette?
  **A:** It unifies the design and matches how WPA silkscreen posters were printed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Get feedback from someone who didn't see it in progress: can they find the event, date, and place in 5 seconds?

*How to teach:* 5-second test with a fresh viewer.

- **Q:** What does the 5-second test check?
  **A:** Whether the key information (event, date, place) is readable at a glance.

**Assessment:** Poster evaluated on hierarchy, readability, and design principles.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric: Hierarchy (title, then date/place, then details), Readability (passes 5-second test), Design principles (balance, emphasis, unity), Limited palette and lettering. 4 levels; proficient = 3+.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Balance and body management

**Prepare:** Mat or carpet; a tape line on the floor; space for partner balances with spotter.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking balance is just strength. Correct: balance depends on keeping the center of gravity over the base of support.
- Partner balances without communication. Correct: agree on signals and come down slowly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Center of gravity
*Student:* Experiment with base of support and center of gravity in one-foot and partner balances.

*How to teach:* Experiment: narrow vs. wide stance, high vs. low center of gravity.

- **Q:** Why is a wide, low stance more stable?
  **A:** A wider base of support and lower center of gravity keep the center of gravity over the base more easily.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Static balances
*Student:* Hold 10 balances (V-sit, arabesque, tripod) for 10 seconds each.

*How to teach:* Hold 10 balances 10 s each (V-sit, arabesque, tripod, etc.).

- **Q:** Why is the tripod stable?
  **A:** Head and two hands form a triangle — a wide three-point base under the center of gravity.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dynamic balance
*Student:* Walk a line forward/backward and perform turns on a balance line.

*How to teach:* Walk the line forward/backward; turns.

- **Q:** What is dynamic balance?
  **A:** Maintaining balance while moving.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner balances
*Student:* Perform safe partner balances with a family member.

*How to teach:* Safe partner balances (counterbalance and countertension).

- **Q:** Difference between counterbalance and countertension?
  **A:** Counterbalance: partners lean toward/push against each other; countertension: partners pull away from each other while holding on.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a balance sequence and explain center of gravity.

*How to teach:* Perform a sequence; explain center of gravity.

- **Q:** Define center of gravity.
  **A:** The point where the body's weight is evenly distributed; balance is kept when it stays above the base of support.

**Assessment:** Balance sequence.

**Answer key:** Balance sequence: 4 = 5+ balances (static, dynamic, partner) held 3+ s with smooth transitions, explanation of center of gravity and base of support with examples; 3 = minor wobbles or weak explanation; 2 = few balances; 1 = incomplete.

## Health & Wellness: Cannabis, prescription drugs, fentanyl and naloxone

**Prepare:** Parental preview: overdose. CDPH/DEA 'One Pill Can Kill' materials; a naloxone training video; a step checklist. Find a medicine take-back location (pharmacy kiosk or DEA Take Back Day).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pills bought online or from friends are real. Correct: DEA reports a large share of counterfeit pills contain potentially deadly fentanyl.
- Believing cannabis is harmless because it's legal for adults. Correct: it's illegal under 21 and high-potency THC harms memory, motivation and mental health.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cannabis
*Student:* Research cannabis effects on memory, motivation and mental health, including high-potency products.

*How to teach:* Research cannabis effects on memory, attention, motivation; high-potency products increase risk of addiction and psychosis; cannabis hyperemesis syndrome.

- **Q:** Why is high-potency cannabis risky for teens?
  **A:** Higher THC increases risk of cannabis use disorder, anxiety, psychosis and impaired memory and learning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prescription misuse
*Student:* Learn why sharing or misusing prescription drugs is dangerous, and how to dispose of medicine safely.

*How to teach:* Explain misuse (taking someone else's, wrong dose, to get high) and safe disposal (take-back kiosks; FDA guidance otherwise).

- **Q:** How should leftover prescription painkillers be disposed of?
  **A:** At a drug take-back site or pharmacy kiosk; if none, follow FDA instructions (some on the flush list, others mixed with coffee grounds/cat litter in a sealed bag in trash).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fentanyl
*Student:* Learn about fentanyl, counterfeit pills and why 'one pill can kill', using CDPH/DEA materials.

*How to teach:* Explain fentanyl is 50x stronger than heroin; 2 mg can be lethal; counterfeit pills look like real ones.

- **Q:** Why 'one pill can kill'?
  **A:** A single counterfeit pill can contain a lethal dose of fentanyl.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Overdose response
*Student:* Learn overdose signs and practice the steps for giving naloxone (Narcan) using a training video and checklist.

*How to teach:* Practice: check responsiveness, call 911, give naloxone in one nostril, rescue breathing, recovery position, second dose after 2–3 minutes, stay.

- **Q:** Can naloxone hurt someone who isn't overdosing on opioids?
  **A:** No — it has no effect without opioids present; it's safe to give if you suspect overdose.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete a scenario quiz on recognizing and responding to an overdose.

*How to teach:* Give the scenario quiz.

- **Q:** What are three signs of opioid overdose?
  **A:** Pinpoint pupils, unresponsive, slow or stopped breathing, blue/gray lips.

**Assessment:** Overdose response scenario quiz.

**Answer key:** Overdose scenario quiz key: recognize signs (pinpoint pupils, unresponsive, slow/no breathing, blue lips); steps in order: shout/sternum rub → call 911 → naloxone → support breathing → recovery position → second dose after 2–3 min → stay; CA Good Samaritan law protects callers. Proficient: all signs and steps correct.
