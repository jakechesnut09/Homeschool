# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 34

## English 9: Note-taking, outlining, and drafting the research paper

**Prepare:** Annotated bibliography; note cards or a digital notes document labeled by source and topic.

**Watch for:**
- Paraphrasing by swapping a few words: a paraphrase restates the idea in your own structure and words and still needs a citation.
- Outlining source-by-source: group evidence by idea so sources 'talk to each other' (synthesis).
- Dropping quotes without signal phrases ('According to …').

### Day 1 (Monday): Notes and paraphrase
*Student:* Take notes on sources; practice paraphrasing vs. quoting with 6 passages.

*How to teach:* Practice paraphrasing vs. quoting with 6 passages.

- **Q:** When should you quote rather than paraphrase?
  **A:** When the exact wording is powerful, precise, or authoritative; otherwise paraphrase.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Outline
*Student:* Build an outline that groups evidence by idea, not by source.

*How to teach:* Build an outline grouping evidence by idea.

- **Q:** What does 'synthesis' mean in research writing?
  **A:** Combining information from several sources to support one point, showing how they relate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft part 1
*Student:* Draft the introduction and first half of the 1,200–1,500-word research paper with signal phrases.

*How to teach:* Draft the introduction and first half with signal phrases.

- **Q:** Give an example of a signal phrase.
  **A:** 'According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, …' or 'Historian X argues that…'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft part 2
*Student:* Finish the draft, including a conclusion that answers the research question.

*How to teach:* Finish the draft with a conclusion that answers the research question.

- **Q:** What should the conclusion of a research paper do?
  **A:** Answer the research question, summarize key findings, and note significance or remaining questions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Format the Works Cited page in MLA and submit the full draft.

*How to teach:* Format the MLA Works Cited page; submit the full draft.

- **Q:** How is a Works Cited page ordered?
  **A:** Alphabetically by the first word of each entry (usually the author's last name), with hanging indents and double spacing.

**Assessment:** Full research draft with MLA Works Cited.

**Answer key:** Draft rubric: 4 = 1,200–1,500 words, clear thesis answering the question, evidence synthesized from multiple sources, signal phrases and in-text citations, conclusion answers the question, correct MLA Works Cited; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = source-by-source summary, citation problems; 1 = incomplete.

## Algebra I: Linear-quadratic systems and Unit 10 test

**Prepare:** Desmos. Prepare 10 linear-quadratic systems and the Unit 10 review/test.

**Watch for:**
- Expecting exactly one intersection: a line and parabola can meet 0, 1, or 2 times.
- Finding x-values but not the y-values for each solution.
- Interpreting only one of two valid solution times (going up and coming down).

### Day 1 (Monday): Graphing systems
*Student:* Graph a line and parabola and identify 0, 1, or 2 intersections.

*How to teach:* Graph y = x² and lines like y = 2x + 3, y = −1, y = 2x − 1 to see 2, 0, and 1 intersections.

- **Q:** How many intersections do y = x² and y = −1 have?
  **A:** None — the parabola never goes below 0.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Algebraic solutions
*Student:* Solve 10 linear-quadratic systems by substitution and check with Desmos.

*How to teach:* Substitute the line into the quadratic, set to 0, solve, then find y.

- **Q:** Solve y = x² and y = 2x + 3.
  **A:** x² − 2x − 3 = 0 → x = 3 or −1 → (3, 9) and (−1, 1).
- **Q:** Solve y = x² − 4 and y = x − 2.
  **A:** x² − x − 2 = 0 → x = 2 or −1 → (2, 0), (−1, −3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Context
*Student:* Solve when a thrown ball's path h=−16t²+48t+4 crosses a ramp line and interpret both solutions.

*How to teach:* Use h = −16t² + 48t + 4 and a platform/ramp at height h = 20 ft. Solve and interpret both times.

- **Q:** When is the ball at 20 ft?
  **A:** 16t² − 48t + 16 = 0 → t² − 3t + 1 = 0 → t = (3 ± √5)/2 ≈ 0.38 s (going up) and 2.62 s (coming down).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Review
*Student:* Complete a mixed Unit 10 review covering all solving methods and linear-quadratic systems.

*How to teach:* Your child completes the mixed Unit 10 review; correct and discuss method choices.

- **Q:** Solve 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0.
  **A:** (2x − 1)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 1/2, 3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 10 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 10 test.

- **Q:** Solve y = x² + 1, y = 2x.
  **A:** x² − 2x + 1 = 0 → x = 1: (1, 2), one solution.

**Assessment:** Unit 10 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 10 test sample answers: 1) x² − 9x + 20 = 0 → 4, 5. 2) (x + 1)² = 12 → −1 ± 2√3. 3) 2x² + x − 6 = 0 → 3/2, −2. 4) x² + 6x + 2 = 0 → −3 ± √7. 5) Discriminant of x² + x + 1 → −3, no real solutions. 6) y = x² − 2x, y = x → (0, 0), (3, 3). 7) h = −16t² + 80t: lands at t = 5 s; max 100 ft at t = 2.5. Scoring: 80%+ proficient.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Designing and simulating solutions

**Prepare:** Spreadsheet; recycled materials (cans, untreated wood blocks, hollow stems, cardboard tubes) for a prototype; drill (adult use) for bee-hotel holes.

**Watch for:**
- Picking the first idea: evaluate multiple solutions with a decision matrix.
- Simulations with unrealistic numbers: base rates on research data.
- Treating failure as the end: iteration is part of engineering.

### Day 1 (Monday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Brainstorm three solutions and evaluate with a decision matrix.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm three solutions; score with a decision matrix.

- **Q:** What is a decision matrix?
  **A:** A table that scores each solution against weighted criteria to compare options objectively.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Prototype
*Student:* Build a prototype (e.g., native bee hotel) or detailed design plan.

*How to teach:* Build a prototype or detailed plan.

- **Q:** What hole diameters suit native bee hotels?
  **A:** A range of roughly 3–10 mm, about 10–15 cm deep, with closed backs (various species prefer different sizes).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Simulation
*Student:* Create or revise a spreadsheet simulation of how your solution affects a population.

*How to teach:* Create a spreadsheet simulation of the solution's effect on a population.

- **Q:** If a bee population grows 15% per year with the solution vs. 2% without, starting at 100, what are populations after 3 years?
  **A:** With: 100 × 1.15³ ≈ 152; without: 100 × 1.02³ ≈ 106.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Iterate
*Student:* Revise the design based on simulation results and document what changed and why.

*How to teach:* Revise and document changes.

- **Q:** Why document design changes?
  **A:** To show what was learned from testing and justify decisions.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present progress (design, simulation graph, revisions) in a 3-minute check-in and record feedback.

*How to teach:* 3-minute check-in presentation; record feedback.

- **Q:** What three items should the check-in include?
  **A:** The design, the simulation graph, and revisions with reasons.

**Assessment:** Prototype or design plan with simulation results.

**Answer key:** Prototype/design rubric: 4 = design clearly addresses criteria and constraints, decision matrix justifies choice, simulation uses researched values and shows predicted impact, revisions documented with reasons; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = little iteration or unjustified simulation; 1 = incomplete.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Coalitions and multiracial social movements

**Prepare:** 1968 Third World Liberation Front demands (SF State); timeline cards for movements studied; strategy comparison chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking movements were separate: many overlapped and supported each other (e.g., TWLF coalition; Filipino and Mexican farmworkers).
- Claiming a single strategy 'caused' change: change usually results from many factors.
- Choosing an action project too big to act on: keep it local and specific.

### Day 1 (Monday): Movement timeline
*Student:* Build a timeline of every movement studied this semester (Alcatraz, UFW, walkouts, redress, Black Panthers) and mark where they overlapped in time and place.

*How to teach:* Build a movement timeline marking overlaps.

- **Q:** Put in order: Delano grape strike, Alcatraz occupation, East LA walkouts, Civil Liberties Act.
  **A:** Delano strike (1965), East LA walkouts (1968), Alcatraz occupation (1969), Civil Liberties Act (1988).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Third World Liberation Front
*Student:* Read the 1968 TWLF strike demands at SF State and identify which groups joined and why the coalition succeeded.

*How to teach:* Read the TWLF demands; identify coalition members.

- **Q:** Which groups formed the Third World Liberation Front at SF State?
  **A:** The Black Students Union with Latino, Asian American (Chinese, Filipino, Japanese American), and Native American student organizations.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Comparing strategies
*Student:* Compare strikes, boycotts, lawsuits, occupations, and art as strategies in a chart and judge which produced lasting change, noting limits on proving cause and effect.

*How to teach:* Compare strategies in a chart.

- **Q:** Give an example of a lawsuit that produced lasting change.
  **A:** Mendez v. Westminster (1946) or Brown v. Board (1954) ending legal school segregation.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Choose an issue
*Student:* Brainstorm local issues (park access, language access at clinics, school curriculum) and select one for a community action project.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm local issues and choose one.

- **Q:** What makes a good community action issue?
  **A:** Local, specific, researchable, connected to community well-being, with a decision-maker you can address.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Submit a one-page project proposal with research question, stakeholders, and intended action.

*How to teach:* Submit a one-page proposal.

- **Q:** What should the proposal include?
  **A:** Research question, stakeholders, background, and intended action.

**Assessment:** Approved project proposal and strategies chart.

**Answer key:** Strategies chart: proficient = at least five strategies with examples from the course, outcomes, and limits of attributing cause. Proposal approved if issue is local and specific, has a clear research question, identifies stakeholders and a decision-maker, and proposes a feasible respectful action.

## Health & Wellness: Environmental and community health

**Prepare:** AirNow; your water supplier's Consumer Confidence Report (search supplier name + 'CCR'); CalEnviroScreen (oehha.ca.gov/calenviroscreen); county public health website. Write Unit 7 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pollution affects everyone equally. Correct: communities near freeways, ports and industry (often low-income and communities of color) bear more exposure.
- Believing bottled water is always safer than tap. Correct: public tap water is regulated and tested; check the CCR.

### Day 1 (Monday): Air quality
*Student:* Check local AQI and research health effects of air pollution.

*How to teach:* Check AQI; research effects of PM2.5 and ozone (asthma, heart and lung disease).

- **Q:** What is PM2.5?
  **A:** Fine particles 2.5 micrometers or smaller that can enter deep into lungs and the bloodstream.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water quality
*Student:* Find your water supplier's Consumer Confidence Report, identify your water source and two contaminants tested, and compare to legal limits.

*How to teach:* Find the CCR; identify source and two contaminants vs. legal limits (MCLs).

- **Q:** What is an MCL?
  **A:** Maximum Contaminant Level — the legal limit for a contaminant in drinking water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Environmental justice
*Student:* Learn how pollution affects communities differently using CalEnviroScreen.

*How to teach:* Explore CalEnviroScreen for your area and another.

- **Q:** What does CalEnviroScreen show?
  **A:** Which California communities face the most pollution burden and population vulnerability, by census tract.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Public health
*Student:* Research the role of the county public health department.

*How to teach:* Research county public health roles (disease surveillance, inspections, vaccines, emergency response).

- **Q:** Give two jobs of a county public health department.
  **A:** Track and control disease outbreaks, inspect restaurants, run immunization clinics, issue health advisories, emergency preparedness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test on communicable and chronic disease, consumer health and environmental health.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Why can't antibiotics treat the flu?
  **A:** Influenza is a virus; antibiotics only kill bacteria (antivirals may help).

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test key: pathogens; transmission; immunity and vaccines; herd immunity; chronic disease risk factors; screenings (cervical at 21); dental/hearing/sun safety; quackery; insurance terms and cost calculation; generics; OTC labels; PM2.5/AQI; CCR/MCL; CalEnviroScreen/environmental justice; county public health roles.
