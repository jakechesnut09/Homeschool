# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 20

## English 9: The Odyssey: the suitors and the bow (Books 17–23)

**Prepare:** Odyssey Books 17–23 (selected). Parental note: Book 22 is violent (the slaughter of the suitors and the hanging of disloyal maids) — preview and discuss.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Penelope is passive: she outwits the suitors (the shroud trick) and tests Odysseus herself.
- Judging the revenge only by modern standards — or ignoring them: discuss both Greek ideas of justice (xenia violated) and whether the punishment is excessive.
- Mixing up the bow test and the bed test: the bow contest proves Odysseus's strength to the suitors; the bed proves his identity to Penelope.

### Day 1 (Monday): Beggar in the hall
*Student:* Read Books 17–18; note how the suitors' treatment of the beggar justifies what follows.

*How to teach:* Read Books 17–18; note how the suitors treat the beggar.

- **Q:** How do the suitors violate xenia toward the beggar?
  **A:** They insult and mock him; Antinous throws a footstool at him; they deny him proper hospitality.
- **Q:** What happens to Odysseus's dog, Argos?
  **A:** Old and neglected, Argos recognizes his master, wags his tail, and dies.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Penelope's test
*Student:* Read Book 19 and Book 21 (the bow); analyze Penelope's intelligence and caution.

*How to teach:* Read Books 19 and 21; analyze Penelope's intelligence.

- **Q:** How did Penelope delay the suitors?
  **A:** She said she would remarry when she finished weaving a shroud for Laertes, and secretly unraveled it each night.
- **Q:** Who recognizes Odysseus by his scar, and what is the contest?
  **A:** His old nurse Eurycleia; the contest is to string Odysseus's bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axe heads.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Slaughter of the suitors
*Student:* Read Book 22; debate in writing whether Odysseus's revenge is justice or excess.

*How to teach:* Read Book 22; your child debates in writing whether the revenge is justice or excess.

- **Q:** Whom does Odysseus kill first?
  **A:** Antinous, the most arrogant suitor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Recognition
*Student:* Read Book 23 (the bed); explain why the bed test proves Penelope is Odysseus's equal.

*How to teach:* Read Book 23; explain the bed test.

- **Q:** How does Penelope test Odysseus with the bed?
  **A:** She tells the nurse to move their bed; Odysseus angrily says it can't be moved because he built it around a living olive tree — a secret only they knew.
- **Q:** Why does this make Penelope Odysseus's equal?
  **A:** She uses his own kind of cunning and disguise to test him.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hold a family discussion on justice in the epic and complete a character chart for Penelope.

*How to teach:* Family discussion on justice; Penelope character chart.

- **Q:** Name two of Penelope's character traits with evidence.
  **A:** Clever (shroud trick, bed test) and loyal (waits twenty years).

**Assessment:** Discussion notes and Penelope character analysis chart.

**Answer key:** Discussion notes: proficient = at least two cited episodes and a reasoned view on justice vs. excess. Penelope chart (proficient): traits — cunning (shroud, bed), loyal (twenty years), cautious (tests the stranger), grieving/emotional (weeps for Odysseus) — each with a book reference.

## Algebra I: Comparing distributions and categorical data

**Prepare:** Reaction-time data for both hands (collect early in the week if needed). Write two survey questions with yes/no or category answers; plan to survey 20 people (family, friends, online form).

**Watch for:**
- Comparing distributions by only one number: compare center AND spread (and shape, outliers).
- Confusing joint and conditional frequencies: conditional divides by a row or column total, not the grand total.
- Thinking an association proves causation.

### Day 1 (Monday): Comparing distributions
*Student:* Compare reaction times with dominant vs. non-dominant hand using box plots.

*How to teach:* Make side-by-side box plots on the same scale. Your child writes a comparison of centers and spreads with numbers.

- **Q:** Dominant hand median 0.17 s (IQR 0.03); non-dominant median 0.20 s (IQR 0.06). Compare.
  **A:** The dominant hand is typically faster (median 0.03 s lower) and more consistent (smaller IQR).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Outliers
*Student:* Identify outliers with the 1.5×IQR rule and discuss their effect on mean and SD.

*How to teach:* Teach the 1.5×IQR rule. Your child checks their data for outliers and recomputes mean and SD without them to see the effect.

- **Q:** Q1 = 10, Q3 = 18. Is 35 an outlier?
  **A:** IQR = 8; upper fence 18 + 12 = 30; 35 > 30, so yes.
- **Q:** How does removing a high outlier affect the mean and SD?
  **A:** Both decrease; the median barely changes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two-way tables
*Student:* Survey 20 people on two categorical questions and build a two-way table.

*How to teach:* Your child surveys 20 people and organizes answers in a two-way table with totals.

- **Q:** In a two-way table, what are the marginal totals?
  **A:** The row and column totals (each variable's counts alone).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relative frequencies
*Student:* Compute joint, marginal, and conditional relative frequencies and describe associations.

*How to teach:* Use this table: Teens: dog 18, cat 12; Adults: dog 8, cat 12 (total 50). Compute joint, marginal, and conditional relative frequencies. Your child repeats with their survey data.

- **Q:** Using the table, find the joint frequency of teens who prefer dogs and P(dog | teen) vs. P(dog | adult).
  **A:** Joint: 18/50 = 36%. P(dog | teen) = 18/30 = 60%; P(dog | adult) = 8/20 = 40%.
- **Q:** Is there an association between age group and pet preference?
  **A:** Yes — teens prefer dogs at a higher rate (60% vs. 40%).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on comparing distributions and two-way tables.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the survey analysis.

- **Q:** Marginal relative frequency of dog lovers in the table above?
  **A:** 26/50 = 52%.

**Assessment:** Quiz and survey analysis.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Box plots A (median 40, IQR 10) vs B (median 50, IQR 25): B is higher and more variable. 2) Q1 = 20, Q3 = 30: fences 5 and 45; 50 is an outlier. 3) From table (Boys: sports 30, music 20; Girls: sports 15, music 35; total 100): joint boys&sports = 30%; marginal music = 55%; P(music | girl) = 35/50 = 70%; P(music | boy) = 20/50 = 40% → association. Survey analysis rubric: 4 = complete table, correct conditional percentages, clear statement of association with numbers; 3 = minor errors; 2 = table only; 1 = incomplete.

## Biology – The Living Earth: From DNA to protein

**Prepare:** Print a codon chart (mRNA codons). Beads or paper strips for amino acids. Prepare 5 DNA template sequences.

**Watch for:**
- Transcribing with T: RNA uses U (uracil) instead of T.
- Translating the DNA directly: translate the mRNA codons.
- Thinking each gene makes a trait directly: genes code for proteins, whose structure and function produce traits.

### Day 1 (Monday): Central dogma
*Student:* Draw and explain the central dogma (DNA → RNA → protein) with arrows labeled replication, transcription, translation.

*How to teach:* Draw the central dogma.

- **Q:** What are the three processes of the central dogma and where do they occur in eukaryotes?
  **A:** Replication (DNA → DNA, nucleus), transcription (DNA → mRNA, nucleus), translation (mRNA → protein, ribosomes in cytoplasm).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transcription
*Student:* Transcribe 5 DNA template sequences into mRNA and mark start and stop codons.

*How to teach:* Transcribe template sequences; mark start and stop codons.

- **Q:** Transcribe the DNA template TAC GGA TTC ACT into mRNA.
  **A:** AUG CCU AAG UGA.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Translation
*Student:* Translate mRNA into amino acid chains using a codon chart; build paper protein chains.

*How to teach:* Translate using the codon chart; build paper chains.

- **Q:** Translate AUG CCU AAG UGA.
  **A:** Met – Pro – Lys – stop.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Protein folding
*Student:* Explain how amino acid sequence determines protein shape and function (e.g., hemoglobin).

*How to teach:* Explain how sequence determines shape and function.

- **Q:** How can changing one amino acid change hemoglobin?
  **A:** In sickle-cell hemoglobin, valine replaces glutamic acid, causing molecules to stick together and deform red blood cells.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on protein synthesis.

*How to teach:* Protein synthesis quiz.

- **Q:** What is a codon?
  **A:** A sequence of three mRNA bases coding for one amino acid (or stop).

**Assessment:** Protein synthesis quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) Central dogma. 2) Transcribe TAC AAA CCG ATT → AUG UUU GGC UAA. 3) Translate AUG UUU GGC UAA → Met–Phe–Gly–stop. 4) tRNA carries amino acids, anticodons match codons. 5) Ribosome = site of translation. 6) Protein shape determines function.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Stereotypes, bias, and the levels of oppression

**Prepare:** Examples of vintage advertisements and mascots (Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture and other museums have online examples); a summary of Kenneth and Mamie Clark's doll study. Preview images — some are racist; frame them as evidence of history.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stereotypes are harmless if 'positive': all stereotypes flatten individuals and can justify unequal treatment.
- Seeing bias only as individual meanness: it also exists in institutions and ideas.
- Misidentifying internalized oppression as personal weakness: it results from repeated societal messages.

### Day 1 (Monday): Media analysis
*Student:* Analyze stereotypes in vintage advertisements and mascots; describe the message each sends.

*How to teach:* Analyze stereotypes in ads and mascots.

- **Q:** What message does a mascot that caricatures Native Americans send?
  **A:** It reduces diverse living peoples to a cartoonish image from the past, reinforcing stereotypes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Doll study
*Student:* Read about the Clarks' doll experiments cited in Brown v. Board (1954) and explain internalized bias.

*How to teach:* Read about the doll study.

- **Q:** What did the Clarks' doll study find, and how was it used?
  **A:** Many Black children preferred white dolls and assigned positive traits to them, showing internalized effects of segregation; it was cited in Brown v. Board of Education (1954).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Four levels
*Student:* Sort 12 scenarios into ideological, institutional, interpersonal, and internalized levels.

*How to teach:* Sort 12 scenarios into four levels.

- **Q:** Classify: a law banning certain groups from owning land.
  **A:** Institutional.
- **Q:** Classify: a child believing their home language is 'worse.'
  **A:** Internalized.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Counter-stories
*Student:* Find a book, film, or artist that challenges a stereotype and write a 300-word review.

*How to teach:* Write a 300-word review of a counter-story.

- **Q:** What is a counter-story?
  **A:** A narrative by or about a marginalized group that challenges dominant stereotypes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on terms and levels with scenario analysis.

*How to teach:* Quiz with scenario analysis.

- **Q:** Name the four levels (four I's) of oppression.
  **A:** Ideological, institutional, interpersonal, internalized.

**Assessment:** Scenario quiz and counter-story review.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: definitions of stereotype, prejudice, discrimination; four levels with correct classification of scenarios (e.g., a slur → interpersonal; redlining → institutional; belief that a group is inferior → ideological; self-doubt from messages → internalized); doll study significance. Counter-story review rubric: 4 = names the work, the stereotype it challenges, and specific evidence of how it does so.

## Spanish II: Geography of the Spanish-speaking world

**Prepare:** Print blank maps of Latin America, the Caribbean, and Spain. List the 21 Spanish-speaking countries and capitals (include Equatorial Guinea in Africa; Puerto Rico is a U.S. territory, often counted among the 21). Find an article on Machu Picchu or the Galápagos and weather reports for three capitals.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Brazil speaks Spanish: Brazil's language is Portuguese.
- Bolivia's capital: Sucre is the constitutional capital and La Paz is the seat of government — both are acceptable if explained.
- Using 'hace' with all weather: 'hace calor/frío/sol/viento' but 'está nublado,' 'llueve,' 'nieva.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Map study
*Student:* Label 21 Spanish-speaking countries and capitals on a blank map.

*How to teach:* Your child labels the countries and capitals, then quizzes with flashcards. Practice saying them with correct accents (Bogotá, Perú, Panamá).

- **Q:** What is the capital of Peru and of Argentina?
  **A:** Lima; Buenos Aires
- **Q:** Which Spanish-speaking country is in Africa?
  **A:** Equatorial Guinea (Guinea Ecuatorial), capital Malabo.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Geography vocabulary
*Student:* Describe landforms in Spanish (los Andes, la selva amazónica, el desierto de Atacama).

*How to teach:* Teach landform vocabulary (la montaña, la cordillera, la selva, el desierto, la costa, la isla, el río, el lago, el volcán). Your child writes sentences like 'Los Andes son la cordillera más larga del mundo.'

- **Q:** Where is the Atacama Desert and what is notable about it?
  **A:** Northern Chile; it is one of the driest (non-polar) deserts in the world.
- **Q:** Translate: 'Costa Rica has many volcanoes and beaches.'
  **A:** Costa Rica tiene muchos volcanes y playas.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reading
*Student:* Read a Spanish article about Machu Picchu or the Galápagos and answer questions.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the article and answers questions. For Machu Picchu: an Inca citadel built in the 1400s high in the Andes of Peru; for Galápagos: Ecuadorian islands whose unique wildlife influenced Darwin.

- **Q:** Who built Machu Picchu and in which country is it?
  **A:** The Inca, in Peru (near Cusco).
- **Q:** To which country do the Galápagos Islands belong?
  **A:** Ecuador.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Climate
*Student:* Compare climates in Spanish using the weather report from three capitals.

*How to teach:* Your child compares the weather in three capitals: 'En Quito hace fresco; en La Habana hace calor y está nublado.' Mention that seasons are reversed south of the equator.

- **Q:** If it is July, what season is it in Buenos Aires?
  **A:** Winter (el invierno) — the Southern Hemisphere's seasons are reversed.
- **Q:** Translate: 'It's raining in Bogotá.'
  **A:** Llueve (Está lloviendo) en Bogotá.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Map and geography quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the map and geography quiz.

- **Q:** What is the capital of Uruguay?
  **A:** Montevideo

**Assessment:** Map quiz.

**Answer key:** Map quiz key (country — capital): México — Ciudad de México; Guatemala — Ciudad de Guatemala; El Salvador — San Salvador; Honduras — Tegucigalpa; Nicaragua — Managua; Costa Rica — San José; Panamá — Ciudad de Panamá; Cuba — La Habana; República Dominicana — Santo Domingo; Puerto Rico — San Juan; Colombia — Bogotá; Venezuela — Caracas; Ecuador — Quito; Perú — Lima; Bolivia — La Paz/Sucre; Chile — Santiago; Argentina — Buenos Aires; Uruguay — Montevideo; Paraguay — Asunción; España — Madrid; Guinea Ecuatorial — Malabo. Geography items: los Andes = mountains along western South America; el desierto de Atacama = Chile, one of the driest places on Earth; la selva amazónica = rainforest mainly in Brazil, Peru, Colombia.

## Visual Art I: Clay: hand-building

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay, plastic knife, water cup, rolling pin, two slats for even slabs, fork or needle tool for scoring, plastic bag to keep clay moist. Safety: wipe surfaces with a damp sponge (no sweeping dry clay dust, which contains silica).

**Watch for:**
- Joining pieces without scoring. Correct: score both surfaces, add slip, press and smooth (score and slip).
- Uneven pinch pot walls. Correct: rotate and pinch evenly from the bottom up, about ¼ inch thick.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clay safety
*Student:* Learn clay safety (wet cleanup to avoid silica dust) and wedge clay.

*How to teach:* Clay safety; wedge clay.

- **Q:** Why clean clay with water instead of sweeping?
  **A:** Dry clay dust contains silica, which is harmful to breathe.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pinch pot
*Student:* Make a pinch pot with even walls.

*How to teach:* Make a pinch pot.

- **Q:** How do you start a pinch pot?
  **A:** Roll a ball, press the thumb into the center, then pinch and rotate to thin the walls evenly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coil
*Student:* Build a coil vessel using score and slip.

*How to teach:* Build a coil vessel with score and slip.

- **Q:** What is slip?
  **A:** Clay mixed with water to a creamy consistency, used as glue between scored pieces.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Slab
*Student:* Construct a slab box with a lid.

*How to teach:* Construct a slab box with lid.

- **Q:** How do you keep slabs even?
  **A:** Roll between two slats of equal thickness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare the three methods in a written reflection and choose one for the final project.

*How to teach:* Compare methods; choose one.

- **Q:** Name the three hand-building methods.
  **A:** Pinch, coil, slab.

**Assessment:** Three hand-building samples.

**Answer key:** Three samples: 4 = pinch pot with even walls, coil vessel with secure scored joins, slab box with square joins and fitted lid, reflection compares methods; 3 = minor cracking or unevenness; 2 = joins fail; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Core strength and stability

**Prepare:** Mat; core anatomy diagram (rectus abdominis, transverse abdominis, internal/external obliques, erector spinae, multifidus, glutes, pelvic floor, diaphragm). Stopwatch for plank test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking crunches are the best core exercise. Correct: the core's main job is stabilizing the spine; planks, bird-dogs and anti-rotation work train that.
- Sagging hips in plank. Correct: squeeze glutes and brace so the body is a straight line.

### Day 1 (Monday): Core anatomy
*Student:* Learn the core muscles and do a 15-minute core circuit.

*How to teach:* Teach core muscles; 15-minute circuit.

- **Q:** Name three core muscles and a job of each.
  **A:** Rectus abdominis – flexes the trunk; obliques – rotate and side-bend; transverse abdominis – braces/stabilizes; erector spinae – extends the spine.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Planks
*Student:* Practice plank, side plank, and bird-dog progressions.

*How to teach:* Plank, side plank, bird-dog progressions.

- **Q:** What does a side plank target?
  **A:** Obliques and the lateral hip muscles (gluteus medius) along with shoulder stability.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stability
*Student:* Combine balance and core in a stability circuit.

*How to teach:* Combine balance and core in a stability circuit.

- **Q:** Why combine balance and core?
  **A:** A strong core keeps the center of gravity controlled over the base of support.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pilates
*Student:* Complete a 30-minute Pilates-style workout.

*How to teach:* 30-minute Pilates-style workout.

- **Q:** What is a Pilates principle?
  **A:** Control, centering (core), concentration, precision, breath, flow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Plank hold test and curl-up retest.

*How to teach:* Plank hold test and curl-up retest.

- **Q:** When should a plank test stop?
  **A:** When form breaks (hips sag or pike) or the person can no longer hold the straight line.

**Assessment:** Core assessment.

**Answer key:** Core assessment: plank time and curl-ups recorded and compared to baseline; proficient = plank 60+ s with good form and curl-ups at or above Healthy Fitness Zone, or measurable improvement from baseline.

## Health & Wellness: Reproductive anatomy, puberty and development

**Prepare:** Labeled diagrams of the endocrine system and male and female reproductive anatomy (KidsHealth or a biology text). Prepare a labeling quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the menstrual cycle is always exactly 28 days. Correct: 21–35 days is normal for adults (21–45 in teens), and cycles can be irregular in the first years.
- Believing reproductive health exams are only for adults. Correct: teens should see a doctor for problems such as severe pain, missed periods or testicular pain/lumps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Endocrine system
*Student:* Learn how hormones drive puberty and label the endocrine glands.

*How to teach:* Explain hypothalamus → pituitary (LH, FSH) → ovaries (estrogen, progesterone) or testes (testosterone). Label glands: pituitary, thyroid, adrenals, pancreas, ovaries/testes.

- **Q:** Which gland is called the 'master gland'?
  **A:** The pituitary gland.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Female anatomy
*Student:* Label female reproductive anatomy and describe the menstrual cycle.

*How to teach:* Label ovaries, fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina; describe the cycle: menstruation, follicular phase, ovulation (about day 14 of a 28-day cycle), luteal phase.

- **Q:** What happens during ovulation?
  **A:** An ovary releases a mature egg into the fallopian tube.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Male anatomy
*Student:* Label male reproductive anatomy and describe its functions.

*How to teach:* Label testes, scrotum, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra, penis and describe functions.

- **Q:** Where is sperm stored and matured?
  **A:** In the epididymis.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Self-care
*Student:* Learn about reproductive health exams, hygiene and when to see a doctor.

*How to teach:* Discuss hygiene, HPV vaccine, testicular self-exam, menstrual products and when to see a doctor (severe cramps, no period by 15, lumps, pain).

- **Q:** Why is the scrotum outside the body?
  **A:** Sperm production needs a temperature slightly cooler than body temperature.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Complete an anatomy labeling quiz on both reproductive systems and explain the menstrual cycle phases in a short written response.

*How to teach:* Give the labeling quiz and written cycle response.

- **Q:** Why does menstruation occur?
  **A:** If the egg is not fertilized, estrogen and progesterone drop and the uterine lining sheds.

**Assessment:** Anatomy quiz.

**Answer key:** Anatomy quiz: 1 pt per correct label (female: ovary, fallopian tube, uterus, cervix, vagina; male: testis, scrotum, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, prostate, urethra, penis). Written cycle response (4): menstruation (lining sheds), follicular phase (FSH, follicle matures, estrogen rises, lining thickens), ovulation (LH surge releases egg), luteal phase (progesterone maintains lining; if no fertilization, hormones drop → menstruation).
