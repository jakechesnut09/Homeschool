# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 25

## English 9: Romeo and Juliet, Act 4: plans and dramatic irony

**Prepare:** R&J Act 4. Parental note: Juliet threatens suicide in 4.1 and drinks a potion she fears may kill her; discuss as a tragic choice, and check in with your teen.

**Watch for:**
- Believing the potion is poison: it causes a deathlike sleep (about 42 hours).
- Missing dramatic irony in 4.5: the audience knows Juliet is alive while the family mourns.
- Thinking the Friar's plan was 'safe': it depends on a letter reaching Romeo in time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Friar's plan
*Student:* Read 4.1; list every step of the plan and what could go wrong.

*How to teach:* Read 4.1; list the plan's steps and risks.

- **Q:** What are the steps of the Friar's plan?
  **A:** Juliet agrees to marry Paris, drinks the potion the night before, appears dead, is placed in the family tomb; the Friar sends a letter to Romeo, who will come to take her to Mantua when she wakes.
- **Q:** What could go wrong?
  **A:** The letter might not reach Romeo; she might wake early; the potion might fail.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Juliet's fears
*Student:* Read 4.3 soliloquy; annotate her fears and analyze her courage.

*How to teach:* Read the 4.3 soliloquy; annotate her fears.

- **Q:** Name three of Juliet's fears.
  **A:** The potion might not work; it might be poison from the Friar; she might wake before Romeo and suffocate or go mad in the tomb among the dead (and Tybalt's corpse).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Irony on the wedding morning
*Student:* Read 4.4–4.5; chart what the audience knows vs. what characters know.

*How to teach:* Read 4.4–4.5; chart audience vs. character knowledge.

- **Q:** What is ironic in 4.5 when the family finds Juliet?
  **A:** They grieve, believing she is dead, while the audience knows she is only sleeping — and wedding preparations turn to funeral ones.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Vocabulary in context
*Student:* Use context and footnotes to define 15 archaic words from Acts 3–4.

*How to teach:* Use context and footnotes for 15 archaic words.

- **Q:** Meaning of 'shrift'?
  **A:** Confession (to a priest).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Act 4 quiz and prediction letter written from Friar John's perspective.

*How to teach:* Act 4 quiz; prediction letter from Friar John's perspective.

- **Q:** Why does Capulet move the wedding up a day?
  **A:** Juliet pretends to agree, and he is so pleased he moves it to Wednesday.

**Assessment:** Act 4 quiz and creative letter.

**Answer key:** Act 4 quiz answers: 1) Juliet goes to the Friar's cell (meets Paris there). 2) She threatens to kill herself with a knife. 3) The potion lasts 42 hours. 4) Romeo will learn by letter. 5) The wedding moves to Wednesday. 6) Juliet's soliloquy fears (any two). 7) The Nurse discovers Juliet. Creative letter rubric: accurate to plot, Friar John's voice, explains he hasn't delivered the letter (he will be quarantined).

## Algebra I: Adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomials

**Prepare:** Algebra tiles (or paper squares/rectangles: x², x, 1 in two colors for positive/negative). Area-model template (2×2 and 2×3 grids).

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting to distribute the minus sign when subtracting polynomials: −(2x² + x − 6) = −2x² − x + 6.
- (a + b)² = a² + b²: it is a² + 2ab + b² — show with an area model.
- Adding exponents when adding like terms: 3x² + 2x² = 5x², not 5x⁴.

### Day 1 (Monday): Polynomial vocabulary
*Student:* Classify 15 polynomials by degree and number of terms; write in standard form.

*How to teach:* Define degree and number of terms; write in standard form (descending degree). Your child classifies 15 polynomials.

- **Q:** Classify 4x³ − 2x + 7.
  **A:** Degree 3 (cubic) trinomial; leading coefficient 4.
- **Q:** Write 5 − 3x² + x in standard form.
  **A:** −3x² + x + 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add and subtract
*Student:* Add and subtract polynomials with algebra tiles, then symbolically.

*How to teach:* Use tiles to combine like terms; zero pairs cancel. Then do it symbolically.

- **Q:** (3x² + 2x − 5) + (x² − 4x + 1)?
  **A:** 4x² − 2x − 4.
- **Q:** (5x² − 3x + 2) − (2x² + x − 6)?
  **A:** 3x² − 4x + 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Multiply
*Student:* Multiply monomials by polynomials and binomials by binomials with area models.

*How to teach:* Use an area model (grid) for (x + 3)(x + 5): fill in x², 5x, 3x, 15. Your child multiplies with area models.

- **Q:** Multiply (x + 3)(x + 5).
  **A:** x² + 8x + 15.
- **Q:** Multiply (2x − 1)(x + 4).
  **A:** 2x² + 7x − 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Special products
*Student:* Find patterns in (a+b)², (a−b)², and (a+b)(a−b).

*How to teach:* Have your child compute several (a + b)², (a − b)², (a + b)(a − b) products and describe the patterns.

- **Q:** Expand (x + 4)², (x − 3)², and (x + 5)(x − 5).
  **A:** x² + 8x + 16; x² − 6x + 9; x² − 25.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on polynomial operations; explain why polynomials are closed under multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; include the closure explanation.

- **Q:** Why are polynomials closed under multiplication?
  **A:** Each product of terms ax^m·bx^n = abx^(m+n) has a whole-number exponent, and the sum of such terms is a polynomial.

**Assessment:** Polynomial operations quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) −2x⁴ + x — degree 4 binomial. 2) (2x² − 3x + 1) + (x² + 5x − 4) = 3x² + 2x − 3. 3) (4x² + x) − (x² − 2x + 3) = 3x² + 3x − 3. 4) 3x(2x² − x + 4) = 6x³ − 3x² + 12x. 5) (x − 2)(x + 7) = x² + 5x − 14. 6) (3x + 2)² = 9x² + 12x + 4. 7) (2x − 5)(2x + 5) = 4x² − 25. 8) Closure explanation (see Day 5).

## Biology – The Living Earth: Cell structure and function

**Prepare:** Onion, iodine (adult handles), microscope or phone microscope clip, slides/cover slips (or clear tape on glass). Organelle list.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking plant cells lack mitochondria: plant cells have both chloroplasts and mitochondria.
- Believing prokaryotes have no DNA: they have DNA but no nucleus.
- Expecting to see chloroplasts in onion bulb cells: onion bulbs grow underground and don't photosynthesize.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cell theory
*Student:* Read about cell theory and compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

*How to teach:* Read about cell theory; compare prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

- **Q:** State the three parts of cell theory.
  **A:** All living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; all cells come from pre-existing cells.
- **Q:** Main difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?
  **A:** Eukaryotes have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; prokaryotes don't.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Microscope lab
*Student:* Prepare an onion skin slide stained with iodine and sketch observations.

*How to teach:* Prepare an onion slide; stain; sketch.

- **Q:** What structures will you see in stained onion cells?
  **A:** Cell walls (rectangular outlines), nuclei (stained dark), cytoplasm — but no chloroplasts.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Organelle functions
*Student:* Create an organelle analogy chart comparing a cell to a city (nucleus = city hall) and justify each match.

*How to teach:* Create a city analogy chart.

- **Q:** Give a city analogy for the mitochondrion and the ribosome.
  **A:** Mitochondrion = power plant (releases energy as ATP); ribosome = factory workers building proteins.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Specialized cells
*Student:* Compare nerve, muscle, and blood cells and connect structure to function.

*How to teach:* Compare nerve, muscle, and blood cells.

- **Q:** How does a nerve cell's shape relate to its function?
  **A:** Long extensions (axons and dendrites) carry signals over long distances.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on cell structure.

*How to teach:* Cell structure quiz.

- **Q:** Which organelle packages and ships proteins?
  **A:** The Golgi apparatus.

**Assessment:** Microscope sketch and quiz.

**Answer key:** Microscope sketch: proficient = labeled cell wall, nucleus, cytoplasm, magnification noted, accurate proportions. Quiz answers: nucleus (DNA), mitochondria (respiration), chloroplast (photosynthesis), ribosome (protein synthesis), ER (transport/synthesis), Golgi (packaging), cell membrane (controls entry/exit), cell wall (support, plants).

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): African resistance to slavery and the meaning of freedom

**Prepare:** Excerpt from Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (Chapter 6–7, learning to read); Biddy Mason biography; Archy Lee case summary.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking enslaved people only resisted through revolts: daily resistance (slowing work, preserving culture, learning to read) and escape were common.
- Believing slavery never existed in California: although California entered as a free state (1850), slaveholders brought enslaved people, and courts and the 1852 Fugitive Slave Law enabled it.
- Ignoring Black Californians' agency: individuals like Biddy Mason used the courts to win freedom.

### Day 1 (Monday): Douglass
*Student:* Read an excerpt of Douglass's Narrative on learning to read and analyze it as resistance.

*How to teach:* Read the Douglass excerpt.

- **Q:** Why did Mr. Auld forbid teaching Douglass to read, and how did Douglass respond?
  **A:** He said learning would 'unfit him to be a slave'; Douglass realized literacy was 'the pathway from slavery to freedom' and kept learning secretly (e.g., trading bread for lessons from poor white boys).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forms of resistance
*Student:* Categorize forms of resistance (daily, escape, revolt).

*How to teach:* Categorize forms of resistance.

- **Q:** Classify: breaking tools, the Underground Railroad, Nat Turner's 1831 rebellion.
  **A:** Daily resistance; escape; revolt.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): California and slavery
*Student:* Study Biddy Mason's 1856 freedom suit in Los Angeles.

*How to teach:* Study Biddy Mason's freedom suit.

- **Q:** How did Biddy Mason win her freedom?
  **A:** In 1856, when her enslaver planned to take her family to Texas, she petitioned a Los Angeles court; Judge Benjamin Hayes ruled she and her family were free because California was a free state.
- **Q:** What did she do afterward?
  **A:** Worked as a nurse and midwife, bought land in Los Angeles, became wealthy, and helped found the city's First AME Church.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Archy Lee case
*Student:* Analyze the 1858 Archy Lee case and the California Fugitive Slave Law.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Archy Lee case.

- **Q:** What happened in the Archy Lee case (1858)?
  **A:** The California Supreme Court allowed Charles Stovall to keep Archy Lee as a slave despite California being free; Black Californians organized, and Lee was later freed by a federal commissioner.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a source analysis on Biddy Mason or Archy Lee.

*How to teach:* Source analysis on Mason or Lee.

- **Q:** What did California's 1852 Fugitive Slave Law do?
  **A:** It allowed enslavers to reclaim people who had been brought to California before statehood and treated them as fugitives.

**Assessment:** Source analysis.

**Answer key:** Source-analysis rubric: 4 = identifies author, date, audience, and purpose (sourcing); accurately summarizes content; explains historical context; evaluates reliability/perspective and connects to the question with quotations; 3 = sourcing and content with limited context or evaluation; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate or missing.

## Spanish II: ¿Qué has hecho? The present perfect

**Prepare:** Chart haber (he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han) + participle (-ado/-ido) and irregular participles: hecho, dicho, escrito, visto, puesto, vuelto, roto, abierto, muerto, cubierto, resuelto. Set up a simple habit tracker for the week.

**Watch for:**
- Making the participle agree (*Ella ha comida): after haber the participle never changes — Ella ha comido.
- Splitting haber and the participle (*He no comido): negatives and pronouns go before haber — No lo he comido.
- Regularizing irregulars (*hacido, *escribido): hecho, escrito.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present perfect
*Student:* Learn haber + participle and irregular participles (hecho, dicho, escrito).

*How to teach:* Model: 'Hoy he comido fruta y he caminado una milla.' Your child forms participles for regular verbs and memorizes the irregular list.

- **Q:** Give the participles of abrir, ver, and romper.
  **A:** abierto, visto, roto
- **Q:** Translate: 'We have eaten.'
  **A:** Hemos comido.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Habits
*Student:* Write about healthy habits you have practiced this week.

*How to teach:* Your child writes about healthy habits from the tracker (Esta semana he dormido ocho horas cada noche; no he tomado refrescos).

- **Q:** Translate: 'This week I have drunk a lot of water.'
  **A:** Esta semana he bebido (he tomado) mucha agua.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a family member: ¿Has corrido un maratón?

*How to teach:* Your child interviews a family member with ¿Has...? questions (¿Has corrido un maratón? ¿Has visitado México?) and records answers.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Have you ever seen a volcano?'
  **A:** ¿Alguna vez has visto un volcán?
- **Q:** Answer negatively: 'No, I have never seen one.'
  **A:** No, nunca he visto uno.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bucket list
*Student:* Write a 10-item bucket list of things you have and haven't done yet (Nunca he visto..., Ya he aprendido...).

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 10-item bucket list using ya, todavía no, and nunca.

- **Q:** Translate: 'I have already learned to swim, but I have not traveled to Spain yet.'
  **A:** Ya he aprendido a nadar, pero todavía no he viajado a España.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present perfect quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the present perfect quiz.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Ellos han escribido la carta.'
  **A:** Ellos han escrito la carta.

**Assessment:** Present perfect quiz.

**Answer key:** Present perfect quiz sample key: comer/yo — he comido; hablar/tú — has hablado; vivir/ella — ha vivido; hacer/nosotros — hemos hecho; decir/ellos — han dicho; escribir/yo — he escrito; ver/tú — has visto; volver/ella — ha vuelto; poner/ustedes — han puesto; 'I have not done it yet' — Todavía no lo he hecho.

## Visual Art I: The figure: gesture and proportion

**Prepare:** Optional wooden manikin, pencils; image of Polykleitos's 'Doryphoros' (Spear-Bearer, c. 450–440 BCE, known from Roman marble copies). Family member to pose.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing heads too large. Correct: an adult figure is about 7.5 heads tall.
- Stiff, symmetrical poses. Correct: contrapposto shifts weight to one leg, tilting hips and shoulders in opposite directions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Figure proportions
*Student:* Learn the 7.5-heads rule and draw a proportion chart.

*How to teach:* Teach the 7.5-heads rule; draw a chart.

- **Q:** Where is the midpoint of an adult standing figure?
  **A:** Around the hips/groin (about 3.75–4 heads down).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gesture
*Student:* Do 20 one-minute gestures of a family member posing.

*How to teach:* Twenty one-minute gestures.

- **Q:** What is the 'line of action'?
  **A:** An imaginary curve through the body that shows the main direction and energy of the pose.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Contrapposto
*Student:* Study the Greek "Doryphoros" and draw a contrapposto pose.

*How to teach:* Study 'Doryphoros'; draw contrapposto.

- **Q:** What is contrapposto?
  **A:** A pose with weight on one leg so the hips tilt one way and the shoulders the other, creating a natural S-curve.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Long pose
*Student:* Draw a 30-minute seated figure from life.

*How to teach:* 30-minute seated figure from life.

- **Q:** How does sitting change proportions?
  **A:** The thighs foreshorten when they point toward the viewer, so they look shorter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Critique proportion with a partner.

*How to teach:* Critique proportion with a partner.

- **Q:** Why was the Doryphoros important?
  **A:** It illustrated Polykleitos's 'Canon' of ideal proportions and the use of contrapposto in Greek sculpture.

**Assessment:** Figure drawing.

**Answer key:** Figure drawing: 4 = proportions about 7.5 heads, convincing weight/contrapposto or seated foreshortening, gesture energy, value or line variation; 3 = minor proportion errors; 2 = stiff or distorted; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Strength training safety and technique

**Prepare:** Resistance bands, dumbbells or water jugs, mat, mirror; muscle chart; technique checklist for squat, lunge, hip hinge (Romanian deadlift), push-up, band row.

**Watch for:**
- Rounding the back in a hip hinge. Correct: push hips back with a neutral spine and slight knee bend.
- Lifting too heavy too soon. Correct: master form with body weight first; progress gradually.

### Day 1 (Monday): Muscle groups
*Student:* Label major muscle groups and pair each with one bodyweight exercise; complete a 20-minute circuit.

*How to teach:* Label muscle groups with bodyweight exercises; 20-minute circuit.

- **Q:** Pair: hamstrings, triceps, pectorals.
  **A:** Hamstrings – glute bridge or hip hinge; triceps – bench dips or close-grip push-ups; pectorals – push-ups.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Squat and hinge
*Student:* Learn bodyweight squat, lunge, and hip hinge with mirror feedback; 3 sets of 10 each.

*How to teach:* Squat, lunge, hip hinge with mirror; 3×10.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a squat and a hinge?
  **A:** A squat bends knees and hips more equally (more quad); a hinge pushes the hips back with little knee bend (more hamstrings/glutes).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Push and pull
*Student:* Learn push-ups (with modifications) and band rows; 3 sets of 10–12.

*How to teach:* Push-ups (with incline or knee modifications) and band rows; 3×10–12.

- **Q:** Give a push-up modification.
  **A:** Hands on a bench or wall (incline) or from knees.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Strength vs. endurance
*Student:* Compare low-rep heavy and high-rep light sets and record how each feels.

*How to teach:* Compare heavy low-rep vs. light high-rep sets.

- **Q:** Which rep range builds muscular endurance?
  **A:** Higher reps (about 12–20+) with lighter load.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Technique check on five lifts and a quiz on reps, sets, and safety.

*How to teach:* Technique check and quiz.

- **Q:** Define a rep and a set.
  **A:** A rep is one complete movement; a set is a group of reps done in a row.

**Assessment:** Technique check and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) rep vs set 2) strength: ~1–6 reps heavy (for teens, focus 6–12 with excellent form) 3) endurance: 12–20+ reps light 4) rest 48 h before training the same muscle hard 5) breathe out on effort 6) warm up first 7) spotter for heavy free weights 8) progressive overload. Technique check: 5 lifts scored 0–2 each; 8+/10 proficient.

## Health & Wellness: Sexual violence, trafficking and accessing services

**Prepare:** Parental preview: sexual violence and trafficking. Read Title IX basics, RAINN (1-800-656-4673) and Polaris/National Human Trafficking Hotline (1-888-373-7888, text 233733). List California minor-consent services. Write Unit 5 test; opted-out students submit the alternate project.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking assault is usually by strangers. Correct: most sexual assaults are committed by someone the victim knows.
- Believing victims are to blame for what they wore or drank. Correct: the perpetrator is always responsible.

### Day 1 (Monday): Harassment and assault
*Student:* Define sexual harassment and assault and learn your rights under Title IX.

*How to teach:* Define harassment and assault; explain Title IX protects students from sex-based discrimination including harassment, and schools must respond.

- **Q:** What is Title IX?
  **A:** A 1972 federal law prohibiting sex-based discrimination, including sexual harassment and violence, in schools that receive federal funding.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Human trafficking
*Student:* Learn how traffickers recruit teens online and in person, and warning signs.

*How to teach:* Recruitment tactics: false job/modeling offers, 'boyfriend' grooming, gifts, isolation, online contact; warning signs.

- **Q:** Name two warning signs someone is being trafficked.
  **A:** An older 'boyfriend' controlling them, unexplained gifts/money, not controlling own ID or phone, signs of abuse, sudden absences, scripted answers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Getting help
*Student:* Identify hotlines (RAINN, National Human Trafficking Hotline) and trusted adults.

*How to teach:* List hotlines and trusted adults.

- **Q:** What is the RAINN hotline?
  **A:** National Sexual Assault Hotline: 1-800-656-HOPE (4673), or online.rainn.org.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Minor consent
*Student:* Learn which health services California minors can access confidentially.

*How to teach:* Explain California minor consent: 12+ for STI testing/treatment, outpatient mental health counseling (if mature enough), sexual assault care, drug/alcohol counseling; any age for pregnancy-related care and contraception.

- **Q:** Can a minor get contraception confidentially in CA?
  **A:** Yes, at any age.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test; opted-out students submit their alternate project.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** Who is responsible for a sexual assault?
  **A:** Only the perpetrator — never the victim.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test or alternate project.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test key: healthy vs abusive relationships; affirmative consent; endocrine/puberty hormones; reproductive anatomy and menstrual cycle; definitions of sex, gender identity, expression, orientation; fertilization/implantation; prenatal care; Safely Surrendered Baby Law; abstinence 100%; method effectiveness; only condoms reduce STI risk; curable vs treatable STIs; PrEP/PEP; Title IX; RAINN and trafficking hotline; CA minor consent rules. Alternate project rubric: 4 = accurate, researched, cites sources, addresses healthy relationships and resources.
