# Teacher's Guide — 9th Grade, Week 26

## English 9: Romeo and Juliet, Act 5 and film adaptation

**Prepare:** R&J Act 5 and a film adaptation (Zeffirelli 1968 is rated PG; Luhrmann 1996 is PG-13 — preview). Parental note: Act 5 contains the double suicide.

**Watch for:**
- Blaming one event alone: the tragedy results from a chain (feud, haste, Tybalt's death, the hurried marriage to Paris, the undelivered letter).
- Arguing 'fate' without evidence: use lines such as 'star-crossed' and 'fortune's fool.'
- Judging a film as 'better/worse' without analyzing choices (what's added, cut, emphasized).

### Day 1 (Monday): The tomb
*Student:* Read 5.1–5.3; explain the chain of mistakes leading to the deaths.

*How to teach:* Read 5.1–5.3; your child lists the chain of mistakes.

- **Q:** Why doesn't Romeo receive the Friar's letter?
  **A:** Friar John was quarantined because of suspected plague and couldn't deliver it.
- **Q:** What news does Balthasar bring Romeo?
  **A:** That Juliet is dead and buried in the Capulet tomb.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fate or choice?
*Student:* Write a quick argument about whether fate or choices cause the tragedy using three quotations.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a quick argument about fate vs. choice with three quotations.

- **Q:** Quote one line supporting fate.
  **A:** 'A pair of star-crossed lovers' (Prologue) or 'O, I am fortune's fool!' (3.1).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Film comparison
*Student:* Watch the balcony or tomb scene in a film adaptation; note what the director adds, cuts, or emphasizes.

*How to teach:* Watch the balcony or tomb scene in a film; note additions, cuts, emphases.

- **Q:** In Shakespeare's text, why does the Friar leave Juliet in the tomb?
  **A:** He hears the watch coming and fears being caught; Juliet refuses to go.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Medium analysis
*Student:* Write a paragraph comparing the scene on the page with the film version.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a comparison paragraph on page vs. film.

- **Q:** What is one choice a director must make that the text leaves open?
  **A:** Setting, costume, which lines to cut, actors' tone and movements, music, camera angles.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Act 5 quiz and film comparison paragraph submitted.

*How to teach:* Act 5 quiz; submit film paragraph.

- **Q:** How does the play end?
  **A:** The families reconcile; Montague and Capulet promise gold statues; the Prince: 'For never was a story of more woe / Than this of Juliet and her Romeo.'

**Assessment:** Act 5 quiz; film comparison paragraph (RL.9-10.7).

**Answer key:** Act 5 quiz answers: 1) Balthasar brings Romeo the news. 2) Romeo buys poison from an apothecary. 3) Friar John's letter was not delivered due to quarantine. 4) Romeo kills Paris at the tomb. 5) Romeo drinks poison. 6) Juliet stabs herself with Romeo's dagger. 7) The feud ends. Film paragraph (RL.9-10.7): 4 = names specific director choices and explains their effect compared with the text; 3 = some comparison; 2 = summary; 1 = off-topic.

## Algebra I: Factoring polynomials

**Prepare:** Algebra tiles or area-model grids. A list of factor pairs and sums for practice.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the GCF first: 2x² + 10x + 12 = 2(x² + 5x + 6) = 2(x + 2)(x + 3).
- Sign errors with negative c: for x² − 2x − 15 you need factors of −15 that add to −2 (−5 and 3).
- Trying to factor a sum of squares (x² + 4) over the reals: it is prime.

### Day 1 (Monday): GCF
*Student:* Factor out greatest common factors from 15 polynomials.

*How to teach:* Find the largest factor common to every term. Your child factors out GCFs and checks by distributing.

- **Q:** Factor 6x³ + 9x².
  **A:** 3x²(2x + 3).
- **Q:** Factor 4x²y − 8xy².
  **A:** 4xy(x − 2y).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trinomials (a=1)
*Student:* Factor x²+bx+c using area models and sum-product reasoning.

*How to teach:* Find two numbers with product c and sum b (sum-product reasoning) and verify with an area model.

- **Q:** Factor x² + 7x + 12.
  **A:** (x + 3)(x + 4).
- **Q:** Factor x² − 2x − 15.
  **A:** (x − 5)(x + 3).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trinomials (a≠1)
*Student:* Factor ax²+bx+c by grouping (AC method) for 15 trinomials such as 6x²+11x−10.

*How to teach:* AC method on 6x² + 11x − 10: ac = −60; find 15 and −4; split 11x; group: 3x(2x + 5) − 2(2x + 5) = (3x − 2)(2x + 5).

- **Q:** Factor 6x² + 11x − 10.
  **A:** (3x − 2)(2x + 5).
- **Q:** Factor 2x² + 7x + 3.
  **A:** (2x + 1)(x + 3).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Special patterns
*Student:* Factor differences of squares and perfect-square trinomials, such as x⁴−16.

*How to teach:* Show the patterns a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b) and a² ± 2ab + b² = (a ± b)².

- **Q:** Factor x⁴ − 16 completely.
  **A:** (x² + 4)(x + 2)(x − 2).
- **Q:** Factor x² + 10x + 25.
  **A:** (x + 5)².

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Factoring quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the factoring quiz.

- **Q:** Factor 9x² − 49.
  **A:** (3x − 7)(3x + 7).

**Assessment:** Factoring quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: 1) 5x² − 15x = 5x(x − 3). 2) x² + 9x + 20 = (x + 4)(x + 5). 3) x² − x − 12 = (x − 4)(x + 3). 4) 3x² + 10x + 8 = (3x + 4)(x + 2). 5) 6x² + 11x − 10 = (3x − 2)(2x + 5). 6) x² − 36 = (x − 6)(x + 6). 7) 4x² − 12x + 9 = (2x − 3)². 8) 2x² − 8 = 2(x − 2)(x + 2). 9) x² + 9 is prime.

## Biology – The Living Earth: Cell membranes and transport

**Prepare:** Two raw eggs, white vinegar (shells dissolve in 24–48 hours — start before Day 2), corn syrup, water, kitchen scale, cups. Paper cutouts for the sodium-potassium pump.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking solutes move in osmosis: in osmosis, WATER moves across a membrane toward higher solute concentration.
- Confusing hypertonic and hypotonic: hypertonic solution has more solute than the cell (cell shrinks).
- Believing all transport needs energy: diffusion and osmosis are passive.

### Day 1 (Monday): Membrane model
*Student:* Draw the phospholipid bilayer and explain selective permeability.

*How to teach:* Draw the phospholipid bilayer.

- **Q:** Why is the membrane called selectively permeable?
  **A:** It lets some substances (small nonpolar molecules, water) cross easily while blocking or regulating others (ions, large molecules).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg osmosis lab
*Student:* Dissolve egg shells in vinegar, then place eggs in water and corn syrup; measure mass changes.

*How to teach:* Remove shells in vinegar; weigh eggs; place one in water, one in corn syrup.

- **Q:** Predict the mass change of an egg in corn syrup and in water.
  **A:** Corn syrup (hypertonic): mass decreases as water leaves. Water (hypotonic): mass increases as water enters.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data analysis
*Student:* Calculate percent mass change and explain using osmosis.

*How to teach:* Calculate percent mass change.

- **Q:** An egg went from 70 g to 80 g. Percent change?
  **A:** (80 − 70)/70 × 100 ≈ 14.3% increase.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Active transport
*Student:* Model the sodium-potassium pump with paper cutouts and explain why it requires ATP.

*How to teach:* Model the sodium-potassium pump.

- **Q:** Why does the sodium-potassium pump need ATP?
  **A:** It moves ions against their concentration gradients (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in per ATP) — active transport.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on membrane transport.

*How to teach:* Transport quiz.

- **Q:** What happens to a red blood cell placed in pure water?
  **A:** Water enters and it swells and may burst (hypotonic).

**Assessment:** Egg osmosis lab report and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab report rubric: 4 = testable question, hypothesis, identified variables (independent, dependent, controlled), clear data table and labeled graph, conclusion that uses data and explains the science, sources of error; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing graph or analysis; 1 = incomplete. Quiz answers: 1) Osmosis definition. 2) Hypertonic/hypotonic/isotonic. 3) Diffusion is passive. 4) Facilitated diffusion uses protein channels, no ATP. 5) Active transport uses ATP. 6) Endocytosis/exocytosis for large particles.

## World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2): Segregation, the Great Migration, and civil rights

**Prepare:** Mapping Inequality website (HOLC maps for Oakland or Los Angeles); Brown v. Board excerpt; Black Panther Party Ten-Point Program (1966); examples of Jim Crow laws and a Pauli Murray excerpt (from States' Laws on Race and Color, 1951).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking segregation existed only in the South: redlining, restrictive covenants, and school segregation shaped California cities too.
- Reducing the Black Panther Party to guns: it also ran community programs like free breakfasts and health clinics.
- Seeing nonviolence and Black Power as simple opposites: both sought freedom and equality with different strategies, and many people engaged with both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Jim Crow
*Student:* Read three Jim Crow laws from different states and a Pauli Murray excerpt; explain how the laws enforced segregation in daily life.

*How to teach:* Read three Jim Crow laws and a Pauli Murray excerpt.

- **Q:** Give an example of how Jim Crow laws controlled daily life.
  **A:** Separate schools, railroad cars, waiting rooms, and restrooms; bans on interracial marriage; poll taxes and literacy tests limiting voting.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Redlining
*Student:* Examine 1930s HOLC redlining maps for Oakland or Los Angeles and compare with present-day data.

*How to teach:* Examine HOLC maps; compare to present data.

- **Q:** What did a red 'D' grade on a 1930s HOLC map mean?
  **A:** 'Hazardous' for lending — often assigned because Black or immigrant families lived there, which blocked loans and investment.
- **Q:** What long-term effects can be seen today?
  **A:** Formerly redlined areas often have lower home values, less tree cover, more pollution, and poorer health outcomes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Brown v. Board
*Student:* Read the Brown decision excerpt and connect to the doll study.

*How to teach:* Read Brown v. Board; connect to the doll study.

- **Q:** What did the Court rule in Brown v. Board (1954)?
  **A:** 'Separate educational facilities are inherently unequal' — school segregation violated the 14th Amendment's Equal Protection Clause.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Movement strategies
*Student:* Compare nonviolence and Black Power, including the Black Panther Party's Oakland Ten-Point Program.

*How to teach:* Compare nonviolence and Black Power, including the Ten-Point Program.

- **Q:** When and where was the Black Panther Party founded and by whom?
  **A:** October 1966 in Oakland, by Huey P. Newton and Bobby Seale.
- **Q:** Name a Black Panther community program.
  **A:** Free Breakfast for Children (also free health clinics).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz with a short document analysis of a redlining map and a Brown v. Board excerpt.

*How to teach:* Quiz with document analysis.

- **Q:** Which Supreme Court case Brown overturned?
  **A:** Plessy v. Ferguson (1896), 'separate but equal.'

**Assessment:** Redlining analysis and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz answers: Jim Crow definition; HOLC grades; redlining effects; Brown v. Board ruling and date (May 17, 1954); doll study citation; Black Panther founding; Ten-Point Program demands (freedom, employment, decent housing, education, an end to police brutality). Redlining analysis rubric: 4 = reads map legend correctly, compares to current data with specifics, explains causal links.

## Spanish II: Health and wellness across cultures

**Prepare:** Find short readings on remedios caseros (té de manzanilla for stomachaches, té de jengibre, miel con limón for sore throats, sábila/aloe for burns) and a simple comparison of health systems in Cuba (universal, state-run), Mexico (public IMSS/ISSSTE plus private), and the U.S. (mix of private insurance and public Medicare/Medicaid). Write the Unit 6 test (body, doler, formal commands, present perfect).

**Watch for:**
- Presenting home remedies as medical cures: note they are cultural practices; serious symptoms need a doctor.
- Overgeneralizing about whole countries: say 'muchas personas' rather than 'todos'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Home remedies
*Student:* Read about remedios caseros (té de manzanilla) and compare with your family's.

*How to teach:* Read about remedios caseros; your child lists four and compares them to remedies their family uses.

- **Q:** What is té de manzanilla traditionally used for?
  **A:** Chamomile tea is traditionally used to calm an upset stomach and help with relaxation/sleep.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Comparison
*Student:* Compare health systems in Cuba, Mexico, and the U.S. in a short reading.

*How to teach:* Read the short comparison; your child fills a three-column chart and writes 3 comparison sentences.

  **A:** En Cuba, la atención médica es gratuita y pública.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): PSA

*How to teach:* Your child scripts a 1-minute PSA (e.g., wash hands, sleep, hydration) using formal ustedes commands.

  **A:** Lávense las manos a menudo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present

*How to teach:* Your child presents the PSA and explains the message in Spanish when you ask '¿Por qué es importante?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** Fill in: El médico ___ (decir) que yo ___ (tener) gripe. (present perfect + present)
  **A:** ha dicho; tengo

**Assessment:** PSA rubric and unit test.

**Answer key:** PSA rubric: 4 = 1 minute, clear health message, 4+ formal commands and accurate vocabulary, engaging visuals/delivery; 3 = minor errors; 2 = unclear message or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Unit test sample: 1) Me duelen las piernas 2) usted commands: beba, descanse, vaya 3) he hecho, ha dicho, hemos visto 4) la tos = cough; estornudar = to sneeze.

## Visual Art I: Portraiture across cultures

**Prepare:** Museum images: Fayum mummy portraits (Roman Egypt, 1st–3rd century CE), Egyptian pharaoh portraits, Benin bronze/brass heads (Kingdom of Benin, Nigeria), Mughal miniature portraits (India, 16th–17th century), Modigliani and Alice Neel portraits. Quiz on traditions.

**Watch for:**
- Judging non-Western portraits as 'less realistic' and therefore less skilled. Correct: styles reflect purpose — idealization, status, spiritual function.
- Comparison essays that just describe. Correct: compare purpose, style and context with evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Egyptian and Roman
*Student:* Compare Fayum mummy portraits with Egyptian pharaoh portraits.

*How to teach:* Compare Fayum and pharaoh portraits.

- **Q:** How do Fayum portraits differ from pharaoh portraits?
  **A:** Fayum portraits are naturalistic, individual faces painted in encaustic (wax) on wood panels placed over mummies; pharaoh portraits are idealized, formal and timeless to show divine kingship.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Benin and Mughal
*Student:* Study Benin bronze heads and Mughal miniature portraits.

*How to teach:* Study Benin bronzes and Mughal miniatures.

- **Q:** What were Benin commemorative heads made for and how?
  **A:** Cast in brass/bronze by the lost-wax method to honor deceased Obas (kings) on royal ancestral altars; many were looted by British forces in 1897.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Modern
*Student:* Study Amedeo Modigliani and Alice Neel.

*How to teach:* Study Modigliani and Alice Neel.

- **Q:** Describe Modigliani's portrait style.
  **A:** Elongated faces and necks, almond-shaped often blank eyes, simplified forms influenced by African and ancient sculpture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Write a comparison of two portraits from different cultures citing visual evidence.

*How to teach:* Write a comparison of two portraits citing visual evidence.

- **Q:** What are Mughal miniature portraits like?
  **A:** Small, detailed paintings often showing rulers in profile, with fine brushwork, rich color and decorative borders, signaling status.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on portrait traditions.

*How to teach:* Quiz.

- **Q:** What medium were Fayum portraits painted in?
  **A:** Encaustic (pigment in hot wax) or tempera on wood panels.

**Assessment:** Comparison essay and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Fayum – Roman Egypt, encaustic, naturalistic, funerary; Egyptian pharaohs – idealized, symbols of power; Benin – lost-wax brass heads honoring Obas, looted 1897; Mughal – detailed miniatures, profile portraits of rulers; Modigliani – elongated stylized faces; Neel – expressive, psychologically honest portraits of everyday New Yorkers. Essay: 4 = compares purpose, style, materials and context with specific visual evidence and a thesis.

## PE 9 (Course 1): Building a strength routine

**Prepare:** Bands, dumbbells or jugs; workout log (exercise, sets, reps, load, RPE).

**Watch for:**
- Doing the same workout forever with no change. Correct: progressive overload — small increases in reps, sets or resistance.
- Skipping rest days. Correct: muscles adapt during recovery; wait ~48 hours before working the same muscles hard.

### Day 1 (Monday): Design
*Student:* Design a 30-minute full-body routine with 6 exercises and correct sets/reps.

*How to teach:* Design a 30-minute routine with 6 exercises (squat, push-up, row, lunge, overhead press, plank), sets/reps.

- **Q:** Why include both push and pull exercises?
  **A:** To balance opposing muscle groups and support good posture and joint health.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Workout A
*Student:* Complete Workout A and log weight/reps.

*How to teach:* Complete Workout A; log.

- **Q:** What should your log include?
  **A:** Exercise, sets, reps, load, RPE and notes on form.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mobility and recovery
*Student:* Complete a 25-minute mobility session and learn why rest days matter.

*How to teach:* 25-minute mobility; why rest days matter.

- **Q:** Why are rest days important?
  **A:** Muscles repair and get stronger during recovery; rest reduces injury and overtraining risk.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Workout B
*Student:* Complete Workout A again, adding reps or resistance (overload).

*How to teach:* Repeat Workout A adding reps or resistance.

- **Q:** Give an example of overload.
  **A:** Going from 3×10 to 3×12 with the same band, or to a heavier band at 3×10.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Film one set of each exercise and self-correct form.

*How to teach:* Film one set of each exercise and self-correct.

- **Q:** Name one thing to check on video for a squat.
  **A:** Knees tracking over toes, neutral back, depth, heels down.

**Assessment:** Strength routine plan and log.

**Answer key:** Strength plan and log: 4 = 6 exercises covering push/pull/legs/core with sets/reps/rest, two logged workouts showing overload, form self-corrections noted; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = incomplete logging; 1 = no plan.

## Health & Wellness: Unintentional injuries: home, water, sports and concussion

**Prepare:** Look up CDC WISQARS or 'Leading Causes of Death' for ages 15–19. Print a home safety checklist (falls, fire, poisoning, firearm storage). Find a 'drowning doesn't look like drowning' video. Read CA concussion law (AB 2127).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking drowning involves splashing and yelling. Correct: drowning is usually quiet — head low, mouth at water level, no waving.
- Believing you should 'play through' a ding to the head. Correct: any suspected concussion means removal from play that day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Injury data
*Student:* Analyze CDC data on leading causes of teen injury and death.

*How to teach:* Analyze data: leading causes of death for teens are unintentional injuries (motor-vehicle crashes, poisoning/overdose), homicide and suicide.

- **Q:** What is the leading cause of death for U.S. teens?
  **A:** Unintentional injuries (accidents), led by motor-vehicle crashes and, increasingly, drug overdoses.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Home safety
*Student:* Complete a home safety audit for falls, fire, poisoning and firearms storage.

*How to teach:* Complete the audit with the child: smoke alarms, fall hazards, medicines locked, chemicals labeled, firearms locked and unloaded with ammunition separate.

- **Q:** How should firearms be stored?
  **A:** Unloaded, locked (safe or lock box/cable lock), with ammunition stored and locked separately.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water safety
*Student:* Watch a video on what drowning really looks like, list the signs, and write water safety rules for pools, rivers and the ocean (rip currents).

*How to teach:* Watch the video, list signs and write rules.

- **Q:** List two signs of drowning.
  **A:** Head low in the water with mouth at surface, head tilted back, glassy eyes, vertical body, not able to wave or call for help.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Concussion
*Student:* Learn concussion signs and California's return-to-play law (AB 2127).

*How to teach:* Concussion signs: headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, light sensitivity, memory problems. AB 2127: removed for the rest of the day, written clearance from a licensed health care provider, and a graduated return-to-play protocol of at least 7 days.

- **Q:** What does California law require after a suspected concussion in high school sports?
  **A:** Removal from activity for the remainder of the day, evaluation and written clearance by a licensed health care provider, and a graduated return-to-play protocol of no less than 7 days.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Present your home safety audit to family with the three highest-priority fixes and complete one fix (e.g., test smoke alarms, lock up medicines).

*How to teach:* Present the audit and complete one fix.

- **Q:** Why test smoke alarms monthly?
  **A:** Working alarms halve the risk of dying in a home fire; batteries die.

**Assessment:** Home safety audit.

**Answer key:** Home safety audit: 4 = checks falls, fire, poisoning, firearms, water; identifies three prioritized fixes with reasons and completes one; 3 = two areas missing detail; 2 = partial; 1 = incomplete.
