# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 33

## ELA 4: Revising and publishing the report

**Prepare:** Computer, printer, images (with source credits), a blank map of California.

**Watch for:**
- Captions that just name the picture ('Half Dome'). A good caption explains what it shows and why it matters.
- Lowercasing proper nouns (sierra nevada). Names of specific places, mountains and deserts are capitalized.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise for clarity
*Student:* Reread the draft aloud, mark confusing sentences, and revise each section so its first sentence states the section's main idea.

*How to teach:* The child rereads aloud, marks confusing sentences and revises so each section begins with its main idea.

- **Q:** What should the first sentence of a section do?
  **A:** State the main idea of that section.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Edit conventions
*Student:* Edit for capitalization of proper nouns (Sierra Nevada, Mojave Desert), commas and spelling using a checklist and dictionary.

*How to teach:* Edit for proper nouns, commas and spelling with a checklist and dictionary.

- **Q:** Correct: 'the mojave desert is in southern california.'
  **A:** 'The Mojave Desert is in Southern California.' (southern may be lowercase as a direction; both accepted)

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Visuals
*Student:* Add a labeled map of the wonder's location and two captioned images, and explain in the caption what each shows.

*How to teach:* Add a labeled map and two captioned images.

- **Q:** Write a caption for a photo of Half Dome.
  **A:** Example: 'Half Dome rises about 4,700 feet above Yosemite Valley; glaciers helped shape its sheer face.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish
*Student:* Type and print the final report with headings, visuals and a source list in a consistent format.

*How to teach:* Type and print the final report with headings, visuals and source list.

- **Q:** What final checks before printing?
  **A:** Headings in place, images credited, page numbers/title, source list complete, spelling checked.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-assess the report against the research rubric and write two sentences on its strongest and weakest parts.

*How to teach:* Self-assess with the rubric; write two sentences on strongest and weakest parts.

- **Q:** What is a useful self-assessment sentence?
  **A:** One that names a specific part and evidence: 'My strongest part is the Glaciers section because...'

**Assessment:** Published research report.

**Answer key:** Research report rubric: 4 = clear focus; intro hook; 3-4 headed sections with accurate paraphrased facts; at least one cited quote; labeled map and 2 captioned images; conclusion; correct proper nouns and commas; consistent source list of 3+ credible sources. 3 = all parts, minor errors. 2 = missing visuals or sources, several errors. 1 = incomplete.

## Math 4: Measuring angles with a protractor

**Prepare:** Protractor, ruler, a printed sheet of 10 angles, pattern blocks (hexagon, square, triangle, trapezoid, blue rhombus, tan rhombus).

**Watch for:**
- Reading the wrong scale on the protractor (calling a 40° angle 140°). First decide if the angle is acute or obtuse, then choose the scale that matches.
- Not lining up the center point and the zero line. Put the center hole on the vertex and one ray on 0.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading a protractor
*Student:* Measure 10 printed angles, deciding which scale to read.

*How to teach:* Model placing the protractor's center on the vertex, one ray on 0, and reading where the other ray crosses, starting from the 0 used. The child measures 10 angles and estimates first.

- **Q:** An acute angle's ray crosses at 50 on one scale and 130 on the other. Which is right?
  **A:** 50° — an acute angle is less than 90°.
- **Q:** Measure a right angle. What should it read?
  **A:** 90°.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drawing angles
*Student:* Draw angles of 35, 90, 125 and 160 degrees and have a parent check with the protractor.

*How to teach:* Model drawing a 35° angle: draw a ray, place the protractor, mark 35, connect. The child draws 35°, 90°, 125° and 160° and you check.

- **Q:** Is a 125° angle acute, right or obtuse?
  **A:** Obtuse.
- **Q:** When drawing 160°, about how open should it look?
  **A:** Nearly a straight line — only 20° short of 180°.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Angle addition
*Student:* Split a right angle into two angles and write the equation; solve 6 unknown-angle problems.

*How to teach:* Draw a ray inside a right angle to split it and measure both parts; the parts add to 90°. The child solves 6 unknown-angle problems.

- **Q:** A right angle is split into 35° and x. Find x.
  **A:** x = 55° (90 - 35).
- **Q:** A straight angle is split into 110° and y. Find y.
  **A:** y = 70° (180 - 110).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pattern block angles
*Student:* Find the angles of pattern block shapes by fitting them around a point (360 degrees).

*How to teach:* Fit copies of each block around a point; since a full turn is 360°, divide by the number of copies.

- **Q:** How many hexagons fit around a point? What is each angle?
  **A:** 3; 360 ÷ 3 = 120°.
- **Q:** Six triangles fit around a point. Each angle?
  **A:** 60°.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on measuring, drawing and adding angles.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; you check drawn angles with the protractor (±2°).

- **Q:** Angles of 25° and 40° together make what angle?
  **A:** 65°.

**Assessment:** Protractor quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Measuring items should be checked against the printed sheet (±2°). Sample answers: 1) Measure angle shown at 70°: 70° 2) Draw 45°, 3) draw 120° (checked ±2°) 4) x + 30° = 90°: x = 60° 5) 180° - 75° = 105° 6) 25° + 40° = 65° 7) A square block corner = 90° 8) Four 90° angles around a point = 360°. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: Generating and comparing solutions

**Prepare:** Paper, ruler, colored pencils; straws, skewers, cardstock for blade tests; box fan; timer.

**Watch for:**
- Judging ideas during brainstorming. First generate many ideas, then evaluate.
- Choosing the 'coolest' design instead of the one that best meets criteria. Use the decision matrix.

### Day 1 (Monday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Brainstorm at least 5 blade and axle ideas without judging, sketching each quickly.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm at least 5 blade and axle ideas, sketching quickly without judging.

- **Q:** Why brainstorm without judging?
  **A:** So more ideas come out; a strange idea might lead to a good one.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Blade mini-test
*Student:* Test 3 blade shapes (flat, angled, cupped) on a straw axle in front of the fan and count rotations in 10 seconds.

*How to teach:* Test flat, angled and cupped blades on a straw axle; count rotations in 10 seconds.

- **Q:** Which blade shape spun fastest?
  **A:** Answer from data; angled or cupped blades usually turn faster than flat blades facing straight into the wind.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Detailed sketches
*Student:* Draw three full designs with labels, materials and how energy changes from wind to motion to lifting.

*How to teach:* Draw three full designs with labels and energy flow.

- **Q:** Describe the energy changes in your design.
  **A:** Wind (moving air) → blades rotate → axle turns and winds string → pennies are lifted (stored energy from height).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decision matrix
*Student:* Score each design 1-3 on each criterion and constraint in a decision matrix.

*How to teach:* Score each design 1-3 on each criterion and constraint in a matrix.

- **Q:** How does a decision matrix help?
  **A:** It compares designs fairly on the same criteria using scores.

### Day 5 (Friday): Choose & justify
*Student:* Choose one design and write a paragraph explaining why it best meets the criteria.

*How to teach:* The child chooses one design and justifies it in a paragraph.

- **Q:** What should the justification include?
  **A:** Matrix scores, test data (e.g., rotation counts), and how the design meets the criteria and constraints.

**Assessment:** Decision matrix and justification paragraph.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = matrix with 3 designs scored on all criteria/constraints, chosen design has the highest or well-justified score, paragraph cites blade-test data; 3 = matrix complete, weak justification; 2 = incomplete matrix; 1 = none.

## California, A Changing State: Industry, education and culture

**Prepare:** California map, Ansel Adams Yosemite photographs, library books on Steinbeck, Lange, Hollywood and Walt Disney. Facts: the 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education organized the University of California, California State University and community college systems; Disneyland opened in Anaheim in 1955; Steinbeck's 'The Grapes of Wrath' (1939) told of Dust Bowl migrants. Unit 10 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Silicon Valley is a real valley named for sand. It is the Santa Clara Valley area, nicknamed for silicon computer chips.
- Thinking art doesn't change history. Adams' photos helped protect wilderness; Lange's and Steinbeck's work drew attention to migrant workers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Industries map
*Student:* Map aerospace (Southern California), electronics (Silicon Valley), agriculture (Central Valley) and Pacific trade ports.

*How to teach:* Map industries.

- **Q:** Where is Silicon Valley and what is made there?
  **A:** The Santa Clara Valley/San Jose area; computer and technology industries.
- **Q:** Where is aerospace concentrated?
  **A:** Southern California.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Public education
*Student:* Read about California's schools, community colleges, CSU and UC systems and the 1960 Master Plan.

*How to teach:* Read about California's public education system.

- **Q:** Name the three parts of public higher education in the 1960 Master Plan.
  **A:** University of California, California State University, community colleges.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Artists
*Student:* Study Ansel Adams' Yosemite photos, read about John Steinbeck and Dorothea Lange, and discuss their impact.

*How to teach:* Study Adams' photos; read about Steinbeck and Lange.

- **Q:** How did Ansel Adams' photographs matter?
  **A:** They showed the beauty of wilderness like Yosemite and helped support conservation of national parks.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Entertainment
*Student:* Read about Hollywood and Walt Disney and how film spread California culture.

*How to teach:* Read about Hollywood and Walt Disney.

- **Q:** How did Hollywood spread California culture?
  **A:** Movies shown worldwide made California's places, styles and dreams famous.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 10 test on water projects, the Depression and WWII, industries, education and culture.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 10 test.

- **Q:** What is EO 9066?
  **A:** The 1942 order that led to the incarceration of Japanese Americans.

**Assessment:** Unit 10 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 10 test key: 1) Rainfall north vs. population south 2) LA Aqueduct 1913, Mulholland, Owens Valley conflict 3) Hetch Hetchy and Muir 4) Great Depression and Dust Bowl migrants 5) 'Migrant Mother' by Dorothea Lange 6) WWII shipyards, aircraft, women workers 7) EO 9066 and Manzanar 8) Industries: aerospace, Silicon Valley, Central Valley agriculture 9) UC, CSU, community colleges 10) Contributions of Adams, Steinbeck, Disney. Proficient: 80%.

## Art & Music 4: Curating a portfolio

**Prepare:** Collect all of the year's artwork; have a camera or phone, folder, and good natural light for photos. Read about the Getty Conservation Institute or a museum's preservation methods.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a portfolio is a pile of all your work. A curated portfolio selects pieces with a purpose.
- Photographing art in dim light or at an angle. Shoot straight on in even daylight.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review the year
*Student:* Lay out every artwork from the year, sort by unit, and note which skills each shows.

*How to teach:* Lay out every artwork, sort by unit, and note skills each shows. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Which skills did your landscape show?
  **A:** Answer varies; e.g., atmospheric perspective, watercolor wash, overlap.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Select
*Student:* Select 6 pieces for an exhibition using one set of criteria (skill, idea, growth) and record a score for each.

*How to teach:* Select 6 pieces using one criteria set; score each. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why use criteria to select?
  **A:** So choices are fair and show growth, skill and ideas.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Digital portfolio
*Student:* Photograph each piece in good light and create a digital portfolio slideshow with titles.

*How to teach:* Photograph each piece; build a digital slideshow with titles. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why make a digital portfolio?
  **A:** It preserves and shares the art even if the originals are damaged.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Preservation
*Student:* Read how museums such as the Getty protect artworks (light, humidity, digital archives) and list 3 ways technology helps preserve art.

*How to teach:* Read about museum preservation; list 3 ways technology helps. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Name three ways museums protect art.
  **A:** Control light and humidity, digital scanning/archives, climate-controlled storage, protective glass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Curator's statement
*Student:* Write a one-paragraph curator's statement explaining the exhibition's theme and choices.

*How to teach:* Write a curator's statement on theme and choices. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What does a curator's statement explain?
  **A:** The exhibition's theme and why the works were chosen.

**Assessment:** Curated selection, digital portfolio and statement.

**Answer key:** Curated selection: 6 pieces with criteria scores. Digital portfolio: clear, straight-on photos with titles. Statement: 4 = clear theme and reasons for each choice; 3 = theme and some reasons; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## PE 4: Design-a-game

**Prepare:** Equipment from the year (balls, cones, ropes, paddles, hoops), poster paper and markers. A new player (sibling, friend) for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Making rules that leave players waiting. Good games keep everyone active most of the time.
- Forgetting a safety rule. Every game needs at least one safety rule (e.g., no throwing at heads, tag below the shoulders).

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Invent a game combining at least two skills (e.g., foot dribble and throwing) and list equipment and boundaries.

*How to teach:* Invent a game combining at least two skills (e.g., foot dribble and throwing). List equipment, number of players and boundaries.

- **Q:** Which two skills does your game use?
  **A:** Answers vary; must name two skills from the year.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rules
*Student:* Write 5 rules, a scoring system and a safety rule on a poster.

*How to teach:* Write 5 rules, a scoring system and a safety rule on a poster.

- **Q:** What makes a rule clear?
  **A:** It is specific and easy to follow, telling exactly what players can and can't do.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Play-test the game with family and note problems.

*How to teach:* Play-test with family; note problems (unfair scoring, too much waiting, unsafe moments).

- **Q:** Why play-test a new game?
  **A:** To find problems in the rules before the final version.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Revise rules to make the game fairer and more active.

*How to teach:* Revise rules to make the game fairer and more active.

- **Q:** Give an example of a revision.
  **A:** E.g., 'Added a second ball so fewer players wait,' or 'Made the goal smaller so scoring is fairer.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Teach & play
*Student:* Teach the final game to a new player and lead a full game.

*How to teach:* Teach the final game to a new player and lead a full game.

- **Q:** How do you teach a new game well?
  **A:** Explain the goal first, then the main rules and safety, demonstrate, and play a practice round.

**Assessment:** Game poster and led game.

**Answer key:** Game poster and led game rubric: 4 = poster has name, equipment, boundaries, 5 clear rules, scoring and a safety rule; uses 2+ skills; revised after play-testing; child teaches it clearly and leads a full game safely; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = rules unclear or no revision; 1 = incomplete.

## Health 4: Decisions and the drug-free pledge

**Prepare:** Decision-making worksheet, pledge card, 3 ATOD scenarios (e.g., finding beer at a sleepover, a cousin offers a vape, a friend wants to take his sister's prescription pills). Prepare the Unit 9 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a pledge alone keeps you drug-free. A pledge works best with a plan, healthy activities and supportive people.
- Thinking stress requires a 'quick fix.' Healthy ways to manage stress include exercise, talking to someone, hobbies, rest and deep breathing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Decision model
*Student:* Apply the decision model to 3 ATOD scenarios and write consequences.

*How to teach:* Apply the 5-step model (Stop, Think of options, Consider consequences, Choose, Check) to the 3 ATOD scenarios and write consequences for each option.

- **Q:** A friend wants to take his sister's prescription pills. What is the healthy choice?
  **A:** Refuse, explain it's dangerous and illegal to take someone else's prescription, and tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Name a consequence of saying yes to a vape.
  **A:** Nicotine addiction, harm to the developing brain, breaking family rules and the law, losing trust.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Healthy alternatives
*Student:* List 10 healthy activities to have fun and manage stress.

*How to teach:* List 10 healthy activities to have fun and manage stress.

- **Q:** Name 3 healthy ways to manage stress.
  **A:** Any 3: exercise, talking with a trusted adult or friend, drawing or music, reading, deep breathing, getting enough sleep, spending time outside.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pledge
*Student:* Write a personal pledge to stay alcohol-, tobacco- and drug-free, with one action you will take to keep it.

*How to teach:* Write a personal pledge with one specific action to keep it (e.g., 'I will leave any situation where people are using drugs and call my mom').

- **Q:** Why add a specific action to your pledge?
  **A:** It gives you a plan for what to do, making it easier to keep the promise.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Share your pledge with a trusted adult and discuss support.

*How to teach:* Share the pledge with a trusted adult; discuss how they will support it.

- **Q:** How can a trusted adult support your pledge?
  **A:** Being available to call, giving rides, talking about pressure, and praising good choices.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on influences, refusal and decisions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test (see key).

- **Q:** Name the 4 refusal steps.
  **A:** Say no, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test and pledge.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test key: 1) Name 2 types of peer pressure — spoken/unspoken or positive/negative. 2) Example of positive peer pressure — a friend encouraging you to join a team or study. 3) 4 refusal steps — say no, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away. 4) Assertive vs. aggressive — assertive is firm and respectful; aggressive is hostile. 5) 5 decision steps — Stop, Think of options, Consider consequences, Choose, Check. 6) Two media techniques used to sell tobacco/alcohol — e.g., glamour, celebrities, flavors, bright colors, 'everyone does it.' 7) What does '#ad' mean — paid promotion. 8) Two healthy ways to manage stress — exercise, talking to someone, hobbies, deep breathing. Proficient = 6 of 8. Pledge: written with a specific action and shared.
