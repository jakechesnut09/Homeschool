# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 11

## ELA 4: Visual information and note-taking

**Prepare:** Two nonfiction books or sites on a California animal (e.g., gray whale, banana slug, desert tortoise, California quail), note cards, a short online video about the animal.

**Watch for:**
- Copying sentences onto note cards. Teach: read, cover the source, write key words in your own words.
- Using progressive tenses incorrectly ('She is swam'). Progressive = a form of be + -ing verb: was swimming, is swimming, will be swimming.

### Day 1 (Monday): Text features
*Student:* Interpret a diagram, a map and a chart in a nonfiction book and explain what each adds.

*How to teach:* Look at a diagram, map and chart in the nonfiction book. The child explains what each adds that the text alone does not.

- **Q:** What does a range map show?
  **A:** Where the animal lives (its geographic range).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choose a topic
*Student:* Choose a California animal for an explanatory report and generate research questions.

*How to teach:* The child chooses an animal and writes 5-6 research questions (habitat, diet, adaptations, life cycle, threats).

- **Q:** Is 'Are sea lions cute?' a good research question? Why?
  **A:** No — it is an opinion. A good one can be answered with facts, e.g., 'What do sea lions eat?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Note-taking
*Student:* Take paraphrased notes on note cards from two sources, listing each source.

*How to teach:* Model paraphrasing one fact. The child takes notes from two sources, writing the source on each card.

- **Q:** What information should you record about each source?
  **A:** Title, author (if any), and publisher or website.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Progressive tenses
*Student:* Practice past, present and future progressive verbs in 12 sentences about the animal.

*How to teach:* Teach the three progressive tenses. The child writes 12 sentences about the animal.

- **Q:** Write 'The whale swims' in past, present and future progressive.
  **A:** The whale was swimming. The whale is swimming. The whale will be swimming.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Paraphrase a 2-minute video segment aloud and sort notes into categories.

*How to teach:* Watch a 2-minute video; the child paraphrases it aloud, then sorts all notes into categories.

- **Q:** What are good categories for animal notes?
  **A:** Habitat, diet, adaptations, life cycle, threats/protection.

**Assessment:** Notes and source list check.

**Answer key:** Notes check (4-point): 4 = at least 12 notes in own words from 2 sources, each labeled with its source, sorted into 3+ categories; 3 = 8-11 notes or some copied phrases; 2 = notes mostly copied or one source; 1 = few notes. Source list must give title and author/site for each.

## Math 4: Two-digit by two-digit multiplication

**Prepare:** Grid paper, colored pencils. Prepare 8 partial-products problems and 5 word problems.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting the 'cross' products in 2-digit × 2-digit (23 × 14 = 20×10 + 3×4 = 212). An area model shows all four parts.
- Multiplying 20 × 30 as 60 instead of 600. Think 2 tens × 3 tens = 6 hundreds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Area model 2x2
*Student:* Draw and shade area models for 23 x 14 and 36 x 25 showing four partial products.

*How to teach:* Draw a rectangle for 23 × 14 split into 20 + 3 by 10 + 4 and shade each of the four parts a different color. Find each part and add. The child repeats with 36 × 25.

- **Q:** 23 × 14 using an area model.
  **A:** 200 + 80 + 30 + 12 = 322.
- **Q:** 36 × 25 using an area model.
  **A:** 600 + 150 + 120 + 30 = 900.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Partial products list
*Student:* Solve 8 problems with the partial products method, labeling each product (20 x 10, 20 x 4...).

*How to teach:* Model listing the four partial products with labels (40 × 30, 40 × 6, 7 × 30, 7 × 6). The child solves 8 problems the same way.

- **Q:** 47 × 36 with partial products.
  **A:** 1,200 + 240 + 210 + 42 = 1,692.
- **Q:** 52 × 13 = ?
  **A:** 500 + 150 + 20 + 6 = 676.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Estimate first
*Student:* Round factors to estimate 6 products, then compute and compare.

*How to teach:* The child rounds both factors to the nearest ten, estimates, then computes exactly and compares.

- **Q:** Estimate, then solve 48 × 31.
  **A:** Estimate 50 × 30 = 1,500; exact 1,488.
- **Q:** Estimate 62 × 19.
  **A:** 60 × 20 = 1,200 (exact 1,178).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 5 problems such as rows of seats in a theater or cases of oranges from a Central Valley farm.

*How to teach:* Read each word problem together and have the child draw a quick model, then solve and write a sentence answer.

- **Q:** A theater has 24 rows with 18 seats each. How many seats?
  **A:** 432 seats.
- **Q:** A Central Valley farm ships 35 cases of oranges with 64 oranges per case. How many oranges?
  **A:** 2,240 oranges.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on 2-digit by 2-digit multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently; any strategy is allowed but work must show. Correct together.

- **Q:** 32 × 21 = ?
  **A:** 672.

**Assessment:** Two-digit multiplication quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 32 × 21 = 672 2) 45 × 18 = 810 3) 67 × 53 = 3,551 4) 89 × 24 = 2,136 5) 70 × 40 = 2,800 6) 15 × 15 = 225 7) 28 boxes of 36 crayons = 1,008 crayons 8) Estimate 58 × 42: 60 × 40 = 2,400 (exact 2,436). Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: Patterns that carry information

**Prepare:** Flashlight, Morse code chart (A .-, B -..., C -.-., E ., H ...., I .., L .-.., O ---, S ..., T -), grid paper for pixel pictures, a drum or pot.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking codes only work if long. A code works if sender and receiver agree on the pattern.
- Speed over accuracy. A fast code with errors doesn't transfer information well.

### Day 1 (Monday): Morse code
*Student:* Send messages with a flashlight using Morse code.

*How to teach:* Teach a few Morse letters; send short words with the flashlight (short flash = dot, long = dash).

- **Q:** What is SOS in Morse code?
  **A:** ... --- ... (three short, three long, three short).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Binary pictures
*Student:* Encode a pixel picture in 1s and 0s and have a partner decode it.

*How to teach:* The child colors a simple picture on an 8×8 grid and writes each row as 1s (filled) and 0s (empty); you decode it.

- **Q:** Decode the row 00111100.
  **A:** Two empty squares, four filled, two empty.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Drum signals
*Student:* Invent a drum-beat code for 10 words and send 3 messages to a family member in another room.

*How to teach:* Invent a drum code for 10 words; send 3 messages to someone in another room.

- **Q:** What must both people have to use the code?
  **A:** The same code key (agreed patterns).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare codes
*Student:* Compare which code is fastest and most accurate and why.

*How to teach:* Compare which code was fastest and most accurate and why.

- **Q:** Why might binary be accurate?
  **A:** It has only two symbols (1 and 0), so there are fewer ways to misread it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph about how patterns transfer information.

*How to teach:* The child writes a paragraph about how patterns transfer information.

- **Q:** How do computers and phones send information?
  **A:** They turn information into patterns of digital signals (1s and 0s) sent by electricity, light or radio waves.

**Assessment:** Code comparison paragraph.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = explains that information can be turned into patterns (flashes, beats, 1s and 0s), compares speed and accuracy of at least two codes with evidence, connects to modern technology; 3 = compares codes with little evidence; 2 = describes one code; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: Spanish colonization begins: the Sacred Expedition

**Prepare:** Map, Calisphere set 'Hispanic Americans: Spanish Colonization,' an excerpt of Juan Crespí's diary (many textbook/online excerpts). Facts: in 1769 Gaspar de Portolá (military leader) and Father Junípero Serra founded San Diego (presidio and mission); Portolá marched north, missed Monterey Bay, and his scouts saw San Francisco Bay; Monterey was founded in 1770. Preview: Kumeyaay resisted the newcomers (an attack on the San Diego mission in 1769 and a larger revolt in 1775).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Spanish colonization was peaceful for everyone. Native people lost land and freedom and often resisted.
- Confusing Portolá and Serra's roles. Portolá led soldiers and was governor; Serra led the Franciscan missionaries.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why colonize?
*Student:* Read about Spain's reasons for colonizing Alta California.

*How to teach:* Read about Spain's reasons for colonizing Alta California.

- **Q:** Give three reasons Spain colonized Alta California.
  **A:** To keep Russia and England out, to spread Catholicism to Native people, and to expand the empire and provide ports.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Portolá and Serra
*Student:* Trace the 1769 Portolá-Serra expedition route to San Diego and Monterey.

*How to teach:* Trace the 1769 route.

- **Q:** What was founded at San Diego in 1769?
  **A:** The first presidio and mission (San Diego de Alcalá).
- **Q:** What did Portolá's party see that later became important?
  **A:** San Francisco Bay.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Crespí's diary
*Student:* Read an excerpt from Juan Crespí's expedition diary and list his observations of the land and people.

*How to teach:* Read Crespí's diary excerpt and list observations.

- **Q:** Why is Crespí's diary a primary source?
  **A:** He was a Franciscan priest on the expedition and wrote what he saw at the time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perspectives
*Student:* Discuss how the Kumeyaay might have viewed the newcomers.

*How to teach:* Discuss the Kumeyaay perspective.

- **Q:** How might the Kumeyaay have viewed the newcomers?
  **A:** With curiosity at first, then concern or anger as soldiers took land and resources and tried to change their way of life; some resisted.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write about the goals of soldiers, missionaries and Native people.

*How to teach:* Write about the goals of soldiers, missionaries and Native people.

- **Q:** What were the soldiers' goals?
  **A:** Protect Spain's claim, guard missions and control the land.

**Assessment:** Perspectives writing.

**Answer key:** Writing rubric: 4 = explains soldiers (protect claim/control), missionaries (convert to Catholicism, build missions), Native people (keep land, families, beliefs and way of life) with specific examples; 3 = all three groups, general; 2 = two groups; 1 = one group.

## Art & Music 4: Watercolor landscape and Ansel Adams photography

**Prepare:** Gather watercolors, watercolor paper, masking tape and brushes. Find Ansel Adams' 'Monolith, the Face of Half Dome' (1927) online. A phone camera with a black-and-white setting works for photos.

**Watch for:**
- Painting foreground first in watercolor. Work light to dark, sky and background first.
- Thinking black-and-white photos are boring. Adams used strong contrast and value to create drama.

### Day 1 (Monday): Techniques
*Student:* Practice flat wash, gradated wash and wet-on-wet skies.

*How to teach:* Practice flat wash, gradated wash (light to dark) and wet-on-wet skies. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is wet-on-wet?
  **A:** Painting on wet paper so colors spread and blend softly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paint
*Student:* Paint the week 10 landscape in watercolor, painting the sky first and the foreground last.

*How to teach:* Paint the week 10 landscape: sky first, foreground last. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why paint the sky first?
  **A:** Watercolor goes light to dark; background layers go under foreground details.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ansel Adams
*Student:* Study Ansel Adams' 'Monolith, the Face of Half Dome' and discuss value and contrast.

*How to teach:* Study Adams' 'Monolith' and discuss value and contrast. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How did Ansel Adams create drama in 'Monolith'?
  **A:** Strong contrast: a very dark sky (he used a red filter) against the bright granite face of Half Dome.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Photograph
*Student:* Take 5 black-and-white photos of your neighborhood using Adams' ideas of contrast.

*How to teach:* Take 5 black-and-white photos with strong contrast. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is contrast?
  **A:** A big difference between light and dark areas.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & share
*Student:* Compare your painting and photos in a short written response.

*How to teach:* Compare painting and photos in writing. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** How are painting and photography alike?
  **A:** Both use composition, value and contrast to show a scene and mood.

**Assessment:** Watercolor and photo comparison.

**Answer key:** Watercolor: sky first, at least two techniques, depth shown. Photos: 5 black-and-white images with clear contrast. Comparison: 4 = two similarities and two differences with art terms; 3 = some; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## PE 4: Soccer skills assessment

**Prepare:** Soccer ball, cones for stations (dribble course, gates, goal), whistle. Plan a 15-minute family game. Score sheet for the foot skills test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the referee is the enemy. The referee keeps the game fair and safe; respect calls.
- Arguing to win a disputed call. Use a quick fair method like rock-paper-scissors or a do-over.

### Day 1 (Monday): Skills stations
*Student:* Rotate through dribbling, passing and shooting stations.

*How to teach:* Rotate 5 minutes each through dribbling (cone weave), passing (gates) and shooting (laces shots on goal).

- **Q:** Which surface of the foot is best for a powerful shot?
  **A:** The laces (top of the foot), toe pointed down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Conflict resolution
*Student:* Practice resolving a disputed call with rock-paper-scissors.

*How to teach:* Practice resolving a disputed call: both players state what they saw calmly, then use rock-paper-scissors or a do-over.

- **Q:** Why use rock-paper-scissors for a disputed call?
  **A:** It is quick and fair, so the game can continue without arguing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Game day
*Student:* Play a family soccer game with kick-ins, no hands and fair-play rules for 15 minutes.

*How to teach:* Play a 15-minute family game: kick-ins when the ball goes out of bounds, no hands except the goalie, fair-play rules.

- **Q:** What happens if the ball goes out over the sideline?
  **A:** The other team restarts with a kick-in (or throw-in) from where it went out.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Refereeing
*Student:* Referee a short game, making calls for out-of-bounds and handballs and explaining each.

*How to teach:* The child referees a short game, making and explaining calls for out-of-bounds and handballs.

- **Q:** What is a handball?
  **A:** When a field player deliberately touches the ball with their hand or arm; the other team gets a free kick.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Complete a foot skills test: cone-weave dribble, 10 gate passes and 5 traps scored by a parent.

*How to teach:* Foot skills test (see key).

- **Q:** When trapping, what does 'give' mean?
  **A:** Pulling the foot back as the ball arrives to cushion it and keep it close.

**Assessment:** Foot skills test.

**Answer key:** Foot skills test (scored by parent): Cone-weave dribble — 4 = all cones with close control using both feet; 3 = one miss; 2 = several misses; 1 = cannot. 10 gate passes — count successes (proficient = 7+). 5 traps — count controlled traps that stop within one step (proficient = 4+). Overall proficient = 3+ on dribble, 7+ passes, 4+ traps.

## Health 4: Advertising and food choices

**Prepare:** Cereal boxes (one sugary, kid-marketed) and 3 food ads you have previewed (YouTube or recorded TV). Poster paper or an empty cereal box for the ad design.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking ads just give information. Food ads are designed to make you want the product, often using fun, prizes or characters rather than nutrition facts.
- Thinking 'part of a complete breakfast' means the cereal is complete. The phrase usually means it's eaten alongside other foods like milk and fruit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ad detectives
*Student:* Watch 3 food commercials and record techniques used (cartoons, prizes, celebrities, catchy songs).

*How to teach:* Watch 3 food ads. Record techniques: cartoons/characters, prizes or toys, celebrities, catchy songs, 'everyone has it' (bandwagon), fun/excitement, health claims.

- **Q:** Name 3 advertising techniques aimed at kids.
  **A:** Any 3: cartoon characters, prizes/toys, celebrities, catchy jingles, bandwagon, bright colors, health claims, 'limited time.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cereal box analysis
*Student:* Compare the front of a sugary cereal box with its Nutrition Facts label and note what the front leaves out.

*How to teach:* Compare the front of the sugary cereal box (claims like 'good source of vitamin D,' characters, games) with its Nutrition Facts (added sugars per serving).

- **Q:** What might the front of a cereal box leave out?
  **A:** How much added sugar it has, the small serving size, or low fiber.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Influences web
*Student:* Make a web of influences on your food choices: family, culture, friends, media, cost, taste.

*How to teach:* Make a web with 'My food choices' in the center and branches: family, culture, friends, media/ads, cost, taste, convenience. Give an example for each.

- **Q:** Give an example of how culture influences food choices.
  **A:** E.g., a family eats tamales at holidays or rice with most meals because of their cultural traditions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Create an ad
*Student:* Design a cereal box or poster that advertises a healthy food using the same techniques honestly.

*How to teach:* Design a cereal box or poster for a healthy food using ad techniques honestly (true claims, an appealing character).

- **Q:** How can you use an ad technique honestly?
  **A:** Use attractive design and true facts (e.g., 'high in fiber' only if it's 20% DV or more) without exaggerating.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on how ads try to influence kids and how to think critically about them.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph on how ads influence kids and 3 critical-thinking questions to ask.

- **Q:** What questions should you ask about an ad?
  **A:** Who made it and why? What are they trying to sell? What techniques are used? What's left out? What does the label say?

**Assessment:** Healthy ad design and paragraph.

**Answer key:** Healthy ad and paragraph rubric: 4 = ad promotes a healthy food using at least 2 techniques with only true claims; paragraph names at least 3 techniques, explains that ads aim to sell, and gives critical questions to ask; 3 = 2 techniques, basic paragraph; 2 = ad makes false claims or paragraph vague; 1 = incomplete.
