# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 6

## ELA 4: Greek myths and allusions

**Prepare:** A Wonder-Book for Girls and Boys (Hawthorne, Gutenberg) — 'The Golden Touch' and 'The Paradise of Children' — or D'Aulaires' Book of Greek Myths. Paper for a 5-box comic, index cards for allusions and roots.

**Watch for:**
- Using an allusion literally ('My dad has a Midas touch' means he turns things to gold). Allusions borrow the meaning: everything he tries succeeds or makes money.
- Mixing up roots: tele = far, photo = light, graph = write, micro = small, scope = see/look at.

### Day 1 (Monday): The Golden Touch
*Student:* Read Hawthorne's 'The Golden Touch' (King Midas) and summarize it in a 5-box comic strip.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Golden Touch' (King Midas). The child summarizes in a 5-box comic: wish, joy, problem, loss, solution.

- **Q:** Why does Midas come to regret his wish?
  **A:** His food turns to gold so he can't eat, and when he hugs his daughter Marygold she turns into a gold statue.
- **Q:** How is the golden touch undone?
  **A:** The stranger tells him to bathe in a river and sprinkle its water on anything he wants changed back; Marygold is restored.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pandora
*Student:* Read 'The Paradise of Children' (Pandora) and determine its theme.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Paradise of Children.' Discuss what Pandora's curiosity releases and what remains.

- **Q:** What comes out of the box, and what is left?
  **A:** Troubles (cares, sorrows, sickness) fly out into the world; Hope remains to comfort people.
- **Q:** State a theme.
  **A:** Example: Curiosity can cause trouble, but hope helps people endure hardship.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Allusions
*Student:* Explain 8 mythological allusions (Midas touch, Pandora's box, Achilles' heel, Herculean) and use 4 in sentences.

*How to teach:* Explain each allusion with its source story. The child explains 8 and uses 4 in sentences.

- **Q:** What does 'Achilles' heel' mean?
  **A:** A weak spot — Achilles could only be wounded in his heel.
- **Q:** What is a 'Herculean' task?
  **A:** A task needing enormous strength or effort, like the labors of Hercules.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Greek roots
*Student:* Learn roots tele, photo, graph, micro, scope and build 12 words.

*How to teach:* Teach the five Greek roots and build words together. The child builds 12 words and defines them.

- **Q:** What does 'telescope' literally mean?
  **A:** 'Far' + 'see': a tool to see far away.
- **Q:** Use the roots to define 'micrograph.'
  **A:** A picture (writing/recording) of something very small, made through a microscope.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on allusions and Greek roots; 1-minute fluency read of a myth passage.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz, then do a 1-minute fluency read of a myth passage and record WCPM.

- **Q:** What does 'opening Pandora's box' mean?
  **A:** Starting something that causes many unexpected problems.

**Assessment:** Allusion and roots quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Allusions — 1) Midas touch: everything one does succeeds/makes money 2) Pandora's box: a source of many troubles 3) Achilles' heel: a weakness 4) Herculean: needing great strength/effort. Roots — 5) tele = far 6) photo = light 7) graph = write/draw 8) micro = small 9) scope = see 10) photograph = picture made with light; telephone = sound from far; microscope = tool to see small things. Proficient: 8/10.

## Math 4: Subtraction with the standard algorithm

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks and grid paper. Prepare 12 'across zeros' problems and 5 worked problems with planted mistakes (examples below).

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger in each column (602 - 158 = 556). Ask 'Is the top digit big enough?' before each column; if not, regroup.
- Regrouping across zeros incorrectly (40,000 - 13,586). Teach: rename 4 ten-thousands as 3 ten-thousands, 9 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones.

### Day 1 (Monday): Regrouping with blocks
*Student:* Model 5,203 - 1,476 with blocks, then record with the standard algorithm.

*How to teach:* Build 5,203 with blocks and take away 1,476, trading a ten for ones and a hundred for tens as needed. Then record the same steps in the standard algorithm and match each trade to a crossed-out digit.

- **Q:** 5,203 - 1,476 = ?
  **A:** 3,727.
- **Q:** Why did you have to trade in the tens place?
  **A:** There were 0 tens after trading one for the ones, so a hundred had to be traded for 10 tens.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Across zeros
*Student:* Solve 12 problems like 40,000 - 13,586 and explain the regrouping in one.

*How to teach:* Model 40,000 - 13,586 by renaming 40,000 as 3 ten-thousands, 9 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones. The child solves 12 problems and writes a sentence explaining the regrouping for one.

- **Q:** 40,000 - 13,586 = ?
  **A:** 26,414.
- **Q:** 7,000 - 2,345 = ?
  **A:** 4,655.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Check with addition
*Student:* Check each of 8 differences by adding the difference to the subtrahend.

*How to teach:* For each of 8 subtraction problems, the child adds the answer to the number subtracted and checks that it equals the starting number.

- **Q:** Check 8,000 - 2,345 = 5,655.
  **A:** 5,655 + 2,345 = 8,000, so it is correct.
- **Q:** Is 9,210 - 4,836 = 4,474 correct?
  **A:** No: 4,474 + 4,836 = 9,310. The correct difference is 4,374.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Find and fix the mistakes in 5 worked subtraction problems and write what the student did wrong.

*How to teach:* Show a worked problem with a mistake and ask the child to be the teacher: find the error, fix it and write what went wrong. Repeat for all five.

- **Q:** A student wrote 602 - 158 = 556. What went wrong?
  **A:** They subtracted smaller from larger in each column instead of regrouping. Correct answer: 444.
- **Q:** A student wrote 3,005 - 1,238 = 2,867. Find the error.
  **A:** Regrouping across zeros was wrong; correct answer 1,767 (1,767 + 1,238 = 3,005).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on subtraction including across zeros.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and the error-analysis item independently. Have the child check each answer with addition before turning it in.

- **Q:** 50,000 - 24,618 = ?
  **A:** 25,382.

**Assessment:** Subtraction quiz plus error-analysis task.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 7,302 - 2,857 = 4,445 2) 50,000 - 24,618 = 25,382 3) 63,415 - 28,739 = 34,676 4) 9,006 - 3,478 = 5,528 5) 800,000 - 1 = 799,999 6) 41,250 - 9,875 = 31,375 7) 6,004 - 2,999 = 3,005. Error analysis (8,000 - 3,627 written as 5,627): the student did not regroup across the zeros (subtracted 0 from 7, 0 from 2, etc. instead of renaming 8,000 as 7 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones); correct answer 4,373, check 4,373 + 3,627 = 8,000. Proficient: 6/7 computations plus a correct written explanation.

## Science 4: Heat energy

**Prepare:** Kitchen thermometer, ice cubes, metal/plastic/wooden spoons, mugs, foil, cloth, paper, timer. Safety: the adult pours hot (not boiling) tap water; test spoon handles with fingertips carefully.

**Watch for:**
- Saying a coat 'makes heat.' Insulators slow heat transfer; they keep your body's heat in.
- Thinking cold flows into warm things. Heat moves from warmer to cooler objects.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hot water spoons
*Student:* Place metal, plastic and wooden spoons in hot water and feel which handle warms first.

*How to teach:* Place three spoons in a mug of hot water; after 1-2 minutes, carefully touch each handle.

- **Q:** Which handle warmed first? Why?
  **A:** The metal spoon — metal is a good conductor of heat; plastic and wood are insulators.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ice cube race
*Student:* Wrap ice cubes in foil, cloth and paper and time melting.

*How to teach:* Wrap equal ice cubes in foil, cloth and paper and one unwrapped; time melting.

- **Q:** Which wrapping kept the ice from melting longest?
  **A:** Usually the cloth (it traps air and insulates); report your actual data.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Thermometer data
*Student:* Measure temperature of warm water every 2 minutes as it cools and graph.

*How to teach:* Measure warm water temperature every 2 minutes for 20 minutes; graph it.

- **Q:** What pattern does the graph show?
  **A:** Temperature drops quickly at first, then more slowly as it nears room temperature.
- **Q:** Where does the heat go?
  **A:** Into the cooler air and the cup around it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Insulator design
*Student:* Design a cup cozy to keep water warm and test it.

*How to teach:* The child designs a cup cozy from household materials and tests it against a bare cup (same starting temperature, 20 minutes).

- **Q:** How do you make the test fair?
  **A:** Same amount and starting temperature of water, same cups, same time, same place.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on conductors, insulators and heat transfer.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz on conductors, insulators and heat transfer.

- **Q:** Is foil a conductor or insulator?
  **A:** Conductor (it is metal), although shiny foil can reflect heat.

**Assessment:** Insulator design results and quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Heat moves from warmer to cooler 2) Conductor examples: metal spoon, pot, foil 3) Insulator examples: wood, plastic, cloth, foam 4) The metal spoon handle heats first 5) A cozy works by slowing heat transfer 6) Why pot handles are plastic: insulator protects hands. Design rubric: 4 = cozy tested fairly against a control with recorded temperatures and a conclusion; 3 = data but test not fully fair; 2 = no data; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: Trade, technology and the environment

**Prepare:** Calisphere set 'Native Americans: Arts and Traditions,' paper and yarn for basket-pattern drawing, a California map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Native people simply 'lived off the land' without managing it. They actively managed land with burning, pruning and harvesting practices.
- Thinking there was no trade. Extensive trade networks moved shells, obsidian, salt and other goods.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tomol canoes
*Student:* Read about the Chumash tomol plank canoe and draw a labeled diagram.

*How to teach:* Read about the Chumash tomol and draw a labeled diagram.

- **Q:** How was a tomol built?
  **A:** Planks (often redwood driftwood or pine) were split, shaped, sewn together with plant-fiber cord and sealed with a tar (asphaltum) and pine-pitch mixture called yop.
- **Q:** Why was the tomol important?
  **A:** It allowed travel and trade between the mainland and Channel Islands and deep-sea fishing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Basketry
*Student:* Study basket images from the Calisphere set 'Native Americans: Arts and Traditions' and describe patterns and uses.

*How to teach:* Study basket images; describe patterns and uses.

- **Q:** Name three uses of baskets.
  **A:** Cooking (with hot stones), storing food, gathering seeds, carrying babies, and ceremonies.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Land stewardship
*Student:* Read about controlled burns and explain how they improved plant and animal resources.

*How to teach:* Read about controlled burns.

- **Q:** How did controlled burns help?
  **A:** They cleared brush, encouraged new growth of food plants and basketry materials, improved hunting grounds and reduced big wildfires.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trade routes
*Student:* Map trade of shell beads, obsidian and salt between nations.

*How to teach:* Map trade of shell beads, obsidian and salt.

- **Q:** Why was obsidian valuable?
  **A:** It is volcanic glass that can be chipped into very sharp arrowheads and knives.
- **Q:** Where did shell beads come from?
  **A:** Coastal peoples like the Chumash made beads from shells; they were traded inland and used like money.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on technology, trade and environmental management.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What is asphaltum used for?
  **A:** Waterproofing canoes and baskets and attaching tools.

**Assessment:** Trade and technology quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Tomol = Chumash plank canoe sealed with tar 2) Baskets used for cooking, storage, carrying 3) Controlled burns improved plant growth and hunting and reduced wildfires 4) Shell beads used as money/trade 5) Obsidian traded for tools 6) Salt traded from areas where it was gathered 7) Trade connected coastal, valley, mountain and desert nations. Proficient: 6/7.

## Art & Music 4: Recorder basics: B, A, G

**Prepare:** Get a soprano recorder and fingering chart. B = left thumb + finger 1; A = thumb + 1, 2; G = thumb + 1, 2, 3. 'Hot Cross Buns' = B A G, B A G, G G G G A A A A, B A G.

**Watch for:**
- Blowing hard. Use a gentle, warm breath ('doo').
- Using fingertips. Cover holes with the soft pads so no air leaks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Recorder care
*Student:* Learn posture, left-hand-on-top hold and gentle 'doo' tonguing; play B.

*How to teach:* Show how to hold the recorder: sit tall, left hand on top, right thumb supporting behind, lips lightly around the mouthpiece (not teeth). Start each note with a soft 'doo' and play B (thumb + finger 1) in long, steady tones.

- **Q:** Which hand goes on top?
  **A:** The left hand.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): B and A
*Student:* Play B and A in quarter and half notes from notation.

*How to teach:* Add A (thumb + fingers 1, 2). Your child reads a short line of quarter and half notes on B and A, counting aloud before playing.

- **Q:** How long do you hold a half note?
  **A:** Two beats.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add G
*Student:* Add G and play 'Hot Cross Buns' from the staff.

*How to teach:* Add G (thumb + 1, 2, 3). Clap and say the rhythm of 'Hot Cross Buns,' say the note names, then play it from the staff.

- **Q:** Where is G on the treble staff?
  **A:** On the second line from the bottom.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Note reading
*Student:* Write letter names for B, A, G on a staff worksheet.

*How to teach:* Your child writes letter names under notes on a staff worksheet (B = middle line, A = second space, G = second line), then plays each line of the worksheet.

- **Q:** Where is A?
  **A:** In the second space from the bottom.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play 'Hot Cross Buns' and 'Gently Sleep' for a parent.

*How to teach:* Your child performs 'Hot Cross Buns' and 'Gently Sleep' for you; give one compliment and one tip (tone, steady beat or tonguing).

- **Q:** What causes squeaks?
  **A:** Blowing too hard or leaky fingers.

**Assessment:** Recorder playing check.

**Answer key:** Recorder check: 4 = correct notes, steady beat, clear tone, tonguing each note in both songs; 3 = 1–2 errors; 2 = several errors; 1 = cannot play.

## PE 4: Throwing to a moving target

**Prepare:** Flying disc, junior football, cones, a partner who can jog. Place cones at 10, 15 and 20 feet for the target game.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing at the receiver instead of ahead of them. Lead the receiver by aiming at where they will be.
- Throwing a disc with the arm only. Step toward the target and snap the wrist, releasing the disc flat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lead passes
*Student:* Throw to a partner jogging across the yard, aiming ahead of them.

*How to teach:* Partner jogs across the yard; the child throws lead passes ahead of them. Adjust lead for speed.

- **Q:** If your partner runs faster, how should your aim change?
  **A:** Aim farther ahead of them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Disc throw
*Student:* Learn the backhand flying-disc throw and complete 20 partner throws.

*How to teach:* Teach the backhand disc throw: thumb on top, fingers under the rim, side stance, curl across the body, step and snap the wrist, release flat. 20 partner throws.

- **Q:** Why must the disc be flat when released?
  **A:** A flat disc flies straight; a tilted disc curves or dives.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Football spiral
*Student:* Practice gripping and throwing a junior football in a spiral.

*How to teach:* Teach the football grip (fingers on laces, toward the back), throw over the top and let the ball roll off the fingertips for a spiral.

- **Q:** Which fingers touch the laces?
  **A:** Usually the ring and pinky fingers rest across the laces, with the thumb underneath and the index finger near the back tip of the ball.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Target game
*Student:* Play a 'hit the cone' accuracy game from three distances.

*How to teach:* Hit-the-cone accuracy: 5 throws from each distance (10, 15, 20 ft). Count hits.

- **Q:** Hits: 4 at 10 ft, 3 at 15 ft, 1 at 20 ft. Total hits out of 15?
  **A:** 8 of 15.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Score accuracy on 10 throws to a moving partner.

*How to teach:* Score 10 throws to a jogging partner (catchable passes).

- **Q:** What makes a pass 'catchable'?
  **A:** It arrives ahead of the receiver at chest height, at a speed they can handle.

**Assessment:** Moving-target accuracy score.

**Answer key:** Moving-target accuracy score: number of catchable lead passes out of 10 (any throw type). 4 = 8–10; 3 = 6–7; 2 = 4–5; 1 = 0–3. Note whether the child adjusted lead for speed.

## Health 4: MyPlate food groups

**Prepare:** Paper plate, markers, the MyPlate Kids poster (myplate.gov), magazines, scissors, glue, measuring cups, a cereal bowl, cereal, milk and fruit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking each group gets an equal quarter. Fruits and vegetables fill half the plate (vegetables a bit more than fruits); grains a bit more than protein; dairy on the side.
- Thinking a portion is whatever fits in the bowl. Measure servings sometimes to learn what a cup or ounce looks like.

### Day 1 (Monday): MyPlate
*Student:* Label a paper plate with the 5 MyPlate food groups and recommended proportions.

*How to teach:* Label a paper plate: vegetables (largest section), fruits, grains, protein, with dairy as a cup on the side.

- **Q:** Which two groups fill half the plate?
  **A:** Fruits and vegetables.
- **Q:** Which section is the largest?
  **A:** Vegetables.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Food group collage
*Student:* Cut magazine pictures and glue them into the correct food groups.

*How to teach:* Cut magazine pictures and glue them into the correct food group sections.

- **Q:** Which group does yogurt belong to?
  **A:** Dairy.
- **Q:** Which group do beans belong to?
  **A:** Protein (they can also count as vegetables).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Portions
*Student:* Measure portion sizes of cereal, milk and fruit and compare to what you usually eat.

*How to teach:* Pour cereal into a bowl as usual, then measure it; compare to the serving on the box (often about 1 cup). Do the same with milk (1 cup) and fruit.

- **Q:** You poured 2 cups of cereal and the serving size is 1 cup. How many servings did you pour?
  **A:** 2 servings.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cultural foods
*Student:* Identify food groups in 3 cultural dishes (tamales, stir-fry, pasta).

*How to teach:* Identify food groups in tamales (masa = grains, meat or beans = protein, cheese = dairy, chile sauce = vegetables), stir-fry (vegetables, protein like chicken or tofu, rice = grains), pasta (grains, tomato sauce = vegetables, cheese = dairy, meatballs = protein).

- **Q:** Which food group is the masa in a tamale?
  **A:** Grains (corn).
- **Q:** What food groups are in vegetable stir-fry with tofu over rice?
  **A:** Vegetables, protein (tofu) and grains (rice).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz naming the five food groups, example foods and the plate proportions for each.

*How to teach:* Quiz (see key).

- **Q:** At least how much of your grains should be whole grains?
  **A:** At least half.

**Assessment:** MyPlate quiz.

**Answer key:** MyPlate quiz key: 1) Name the 5 food groups — fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy. 2) Which groups make up half the plate? — fruits and vegetables (more vegetables). 3) Give 2 examples of grains — e.g., brown rice, oatmeal, whole-wheat bread, tortillas, pasta. 4) Give 2 examples of protein — e.g., chicken, fish, eggs, beans, nuts, tofu. 5) Give 2 dairy foods — milk, yogurt, cheese, fortified soy milk. 6) Where does dairy go on MyPlate? — the cup on the side. 7) How much of your grains should be whole? — at least half. Proficient = 6 of 7.
