# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 12

## ELA 4: Writing an explanatory report

**Prepare:** Notes from week 11, computer, paper for a diagram. Prepare a Unit 3 informational reading test using a new passage (rubric/key below).

**Watch for:**
- Writing sections that wander from their heading. Each section's sentences should all be about that heading.
- Ending without a conclusion. A conclusion sums up the main points and leaves a final thought.

### Day 1 (Monday): Organize
*Student:* Arrange notes into an outline with an introduction, 3 headed sections and a conclusion.

*How to teach:* The child arranges notes into an outline: introduction, 3 headed sections, conclusion.

- **Q:** What goes in the introduction of a report?
  **A:** A hook and a sentence telling what the report is about.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft sections
*Student:* Draft the introduction and two sections using linking words (also, for example, because).

*How to teach:* The child drafts the introduction and two sections using linking words (also, for example, because).

- **Q:** Use 'for example' in a sentence about your animal.
  **A:** Example: 'Sea otters use tools. For example, they crack shellfish on rocks resting on their bellies.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish draft
*Student:* Draft the last section and conclusion; add one diagram with a caption.

*How to teach:* The child drafts the last section and conclusion and draws a labeled diagram with a caption.

- **Q:** What does a caption do?
  **A:** Explains what the picture or diagram shows.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise & publish
*Student:* Revise for precise vocabulary, edit, and type the final report.

*How to teach:* Revise for precise vocabulary (domain words like 'habitat,' 'predator'), edit, and type the report.

- **Q:** Replace the vague word: 'Condors eat stuff.'
  **A:** 'Condors eat carrion (dead animals).'

### Day 5 (Friday): Present & assess
*Student:* Present the report to family; take the Unit 3 informational reading test.

*How to teach:* The child presents the report to family, then takes the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** What should a good presenter do?
  **A:** Speak clearly at a good pace, make eye contact, and explain facts in order.

**Assessment:** Explanatory report (rubric) and Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Report rubric: 4 = clear introduction, 3 headed sections with accurate facts in own words, linking words, domain vocabulary, labeled diagram with caption, conclusion, few errors; 3 = all parts with some weak sections; 2 = missing a section or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Unit 3 test: main idea (1 sentence covering whole passage), 3 key details, objective summary, text structure correctly named, 2 vocabulary words defined from context, meaning of a text feature explained. Proficient: 80%.

## Math 4: Multiplication problem solving

**Prepare:** Grid paper. Prepare 6 problems for Day 1 and 4 multistep problems for Day 2; review cards for Day 4; Unit 3 test for Day 5 (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Using the wrong order in multistep problems (adding before multiplying when the story says otherwise). Write the equation with parentheses to show the order.
- Picking a slow strategy for easy numbers (area model for 25 × 4). Discuss when mental math or the algorithm is most efficient.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a strategy
*Student:* Solve 6 problems choosing area model, partial products or algorithm and justify one choice in writing.

*How to teach:* The child solves 6 problems, choosing a strategy for each, and writes 2-3 sentences justifying one choice. Accept any correct strategy with a clear reason.

- **Q:** 25 × 16 = ? Which strategy is quick?
  **A:** 400 — e.g., 25 × 4 = 100, and 16 = 4 × 4, so 100 × 4 = 400.
- **Q:** 34 × 12 = ?
  **A:** 408.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Multistep problems
*Student:* Solve 4 multistep problems combining multiplication with addition or subtraction.

*How to teach:* Model writing a plan and an equation with a letter. The child solves 4 multistep problems.

- **Q:** Leo bought 15 packs of 24 cards and gave away 75 cards. How many are left?
  **A:** 15 × 24 = 360; 360 - 75 = 285 cards.
- **Q:** A store had 6 boxes of 125 pens and sold 290. How many remain?
  **A:** 750 - 290 = 460 pens.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Real-world model
*Student:* Plan a class field trip budget: tickets for 24 students at $14 plus 3 buses at $215.

*How to teach:* Present the field trip budget: tickets for 24 students at $14 and 3 buses at $215. The child computes each cost and the total, then explains the steps.

- **Q:** What is the total cost?
  **A:** 24 × $14 = $336; 3 × $215 = $645; total $981.
- **Q:** How much more do the buses cost than the tickets?
  **A:** $645 - $336 = $309.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 3 review
*Student:* Mixed review game: comparison, factors, multiplication strategies.

*How to teach:* Play the review game with cards on comparison, factors and multiplication. Have the child explain each answer.

- **Q:** Is 57 prime or composite?
  **A:** Composite: 57 = 3 × 19.
- **Q:** 36 is 4 times as many as what number?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on multiplicative comparison, factors and multiples, and multi-digit multiplication.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test independently. Score and correct together.

- **Q:** 54 × 27 = ?
  **A:** 1,458.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key: 1) 28 = 4 × 7 as a comparison: 28 is 4 times as many as 7. 2) A pine is 9 times as tall as a 5-ft bush: 45 ft. 3) Factor pairs of 30: 1×30, 2×15, 3×10, 5×6. 4) 43 is prime. 5) Common multiples of 4 and 6 below 30: 12, 24. 6) 6 × 3,208 = 19,248. 7) 54 × 27 = 1,458. 8) 12 bags of 48 marbles plus 36 loose = 612 marbles. 9) 9 × 400 = 3,600. 10) 73 × 46 = 3,358. Proficient: 8/10.

## Science 4: Designing an information system

**Prepare:** Flashlight, string and cups, paper, poster board for signs. Unit 3 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Picking a solution without testing. Engineers test each option against the criteria.
- Changing many things at once in an improvement. Change one thing so you know what helped.

### Day 1 (Monday): Define challenge
*Student:* Challenge: send a 5-word message across the yard without speaking.

*How to teach:* State the challenge: send a 5-word message across the yard without speaking. Define criteria (accuracy, speed) and constraints.

- **Q:** What are the criteria?
  **A:** The message arrives correctly (accurate) and quickly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brainstorm
*Student:* Brainstorm 3 solutions and list pros and cons.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm 3 solutions (flashlight Morse, picture signs, hand signals) with pros and cons.

- **Q:** What is a con of flashlight Morse in daytime?
  **A:** The light may be hard to see in bright sunlight.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Test each solution and record speed and accuracy.

*How to teach:* Test each solution; record time and number of errors.

- **Q:** How do you measure accuracy?
  **A:** Count correctly received words out of 5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Improve the most accurate solution to send the message faster, then retest it twice.

*How to teach:* Improve the most accurate solution to be faster and retest twice.

- **Q:** What is one way to speed up a code?
  **A:** Use shorter signals for common words, or abbreviations.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on waves and information.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test.

- **Q:** Label a wave's amplitude.
  **A:** Distance from the resting line to the crest.

**Assessment:** Design comparison and Unit 3 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key: 1) crest/trough/amplitude/wavelength labeled 2) bigger amplitude = more energy 3) water moves up and down, not along 4) waves can move sand and objects 5) sound travels as waves 6) a code is a pattern for sending information 7) SOS = ... --- ... 8) compare two codes for speed and accuracy. Design comparison rubric: 4 = three solutions tested with data on time and accuracy, best chosen and improved with retest data; 3 = data incomplete; 2 = no testing data; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: Mapping the missions

**Prepare:** California map, list of the 21 missions with founding dates (from San Diego de Alcalá 1769 to San Francisco Solano in Sonoma 1823), Calisphere set 'California Missions.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking missions were placed randomly. They were placed near water, fertile soil and Native villages, about a day's travel apart along El Camino Real.
- Thinking missions were only churches. They were large communities with farms, workshops and livestock.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mission map
*Student:* Map all 21 missions from San Diego de Alcalá (1769) to Sonoma (1823) along El Camino Real and label founding dates.

*How to teach:* Map all 21 missions with dates along El Camino Real.

- **Q:** Which was the first mission and which the last?
  **A:** First: San Diego de Alcalá (1769). Last: San Francisco Solano in Sonoma (1823).
- **Q:** About how far apart were missions?
  **A:** About a day's journey (roughly 30 miles) along El Camino Real.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Site factors
*Student:* Identify why missions were placed near water, fertile soil and Native villages.

*How to teach:* Identify why missions were placed near water, soil and villages.

- **Q:** Why build near Native villages?
  **A:** Missionaries wanted to convert Native people and needed their labor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spread of influence
*Student:* Explain how missions expanded Spain's and Catholicism's influence across New Spain.

*How to teach:* Explain how missions expanded Spain's and Catholicism's influence.

- **Q:** How did missions help Spain claim land?
  **A:** They created permanent settlements with people, farms and churches that showed Spanish control.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Calisphere images
*Student:* Study images from the Calisphere set 'California Missions' and compare two missions' layouts.

*How to teach:* Compare two missions' layouts in images.

- **Q:** What was the quadrangle?
  **A:** The central courtyard surrounded by the church, living quarters and workshops.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mission locations and placement factors.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz.

- **Q:** What does El Camino Real mean?
  **A:** 'The Royal Road' — the route connecting the missions.

**Assessment:** Mission map quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 21 missions 2) First: San Diego de Alcalá 1769 3) Last: San Francisco Solano, Sonoma, 1823 4) Connected by El Camino Real 5) Placed near fresh water, fertile soil, Native villages, and about a day apart 6) Locate 3 missions on a map (e.g., San Carlos Borromeo in Carmel, Santa Barbara, San Gabriel). Proficient: 5/6.

## Art & Music 4: Evaluating landscape art

**Prepare:** Find images of a Bierstadt landscape (e.g., 'Among the Sierra Nevada, California'), Hokusai's 'The Great Wave off Kanagawa,' and a California Impressionist landscape. Have your child's finished landscapes.

**Watch for:**
- Judging art only by 'like' or 'don't like.' Use criteria and evidence.
- Thinking all landscapes should look realistic. Different artists and cultures have different styles and goals.

### Day 1 (Monday): Criteria
*Student:* Develop criteria for a successful landscape (depth, color, mood, technique).

*How to teach:* Develop criteria (depth, color, mood, technique). Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why use criteria to evaluate art?
  **A:** So judgments are fair, specific and based on evidence.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluate masters
*Student:* Apply the criteria to 3 landscapes by different artists (Bierstadt, Hokusai, a California Impressionist).

*How to teach:* Apply criteria to three master landscapes. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** How does Hokusai show depth in 'The Great Wave'?
  **A:** The huge wave in the foreground makes tiny Mount Fuji in the background look far away (size and overlapping).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Self-evaluate
*Student:* Apply the same criteria to your own landscape.

*How to teach:* Apply criteria to your own landscape. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Which criterion did your landscape meet best? Give evidence.
  **A:** Answer varies; should cite a specific feature.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Add finishing details to improve one criterion.

*How to teach:* Add finishing details to improve one criterion. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Give an example of improving depth.
  **A:** Darken and add detail in the foreground, lighten distant mountains.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & share
*Student:* Present the landscape and evaluation to family.

*How to teach:* Present the landscape and evaluation. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What mood does your landscape show, and how?
  **A:** Answer varies; should connect colors/light to mood.

**Assessment:** Written evaluation.

**Answer key:** Written evaluation: 4 = applies all four criteria to three master works and own work with specific evidence; 3 = most; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 4: Individual jump rope

**Prepare:** Jump rope sized to the child's height (stand on the center; handles reach the armpits), stopwatch, interval log (interval #, jumps, heart rate). Flat, non-slip surface.

**Watch for:**
- Using big arm circles to turn. Keep elbows close to the body and turn with the wrists.
- Crossing arms at the elbows for the criss-cross. Cross at the waist so the hands go past the hips, making a big enough loop to jump through.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rope sizing & basic bounce
*Student:* Size the rope (handles reach armpits when standing on its center) and practice the basic two-foot bounce for 4 sets of 30 seconds.

*How to teach:* Size the rope. Basic bounce: feet together, small jumps on the balls of the feet. 4 sets of 30 seconds with rests.

- **Q:** How do you check rope size?
  **A:** Stand on the center with one foot; the handles should reach the armpits.
- **Q:** How high should you jump?
  **A:** Only about 1–2 inches, just enough to clear the rope.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Skier and bell
*Student:* Learn the skier (side-to-side) and bell (front-to-back) jumps and do 20 of each without missing.

*How to teach:* Teach skier (side-to-side) and bell (front-to-back). Practice over a line, then with the rope; 20 of each without missing.

- **Q:** Which jump moves side to side?
  **A:** The skier.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Criss-cross
*Student:* Practice the criss-cross by crossing arms at the waist on every third jump; count successful crosses.

*How to teach:* Criss-cross: on every third jump, cross arms at the waist, jump through the loop, then uncross. Count successful crosses.

- **Q:** Where should arms cross for a criss-cross?
  **A:** At the waist/hips, crossing far enough that the hands reach past the opposite hips.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Endurance intervals
*Student:* Jump five 30-second intervals with 30-second rests, recording jumps per interval and heart rate.

*How to teach:* Five 30-second jumping intervals with 30-second rests; record jumps per interval and heart rate after the last.

- **Q:** Intervals: 52, 50, 48, 47, 45 jumps. What is the total?
  **A:** 242 jumps.
- **Q:** Why did the count drop each interval?
  **A:** Muscles tired (muscular fatigue) and heart and lungs worked harder.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a 4-trick routine (bounce, skier, bell, criss-cross) for a parent who scores it.

*How to teach:* The child performs a 4-trick routine (bounce, skier, bell, criss-cross) for a parent who scores it.

- **Q:** Name your 4 tricks in order.
  **A:** Bounce, skier, bell, criss-cross (or the order the child planned).

**Assessment:** Scored 4-trick routine and interval log.

**Answer key:** 4-trick routine scoring: each trick 0–2 (0 = not attempted, 1 = attempted with misses, 2 = 5+ in a row with good form) = 8 points. 4 = 7–8; 3 = 5–6; 2 = 3–4; 1 = 0–2. Interval log: all 5 intervals recorded with heart rate.

## Health 4: Communicating about food at home

**Prepare:** Health journal, a family recipe and the family cook's time for an interview. Prepare 3 role-play situations (restaurant, friend's house, grocery store). Prepare the Unit 3 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking asking for healthy food means complaining about family food. Use respectful I-messages and offer to help.
- Thinking traditional dishes can't be healthy. Most traditional foods contain several food groups and can be adjusted with small changes.

### Day 1 (Monday): I-messages
*Student:* Practice I-messages to ask for healthy food ('I feel more energy when we have fruit at breakfast; could we buy berries?').

*How to teach:* Teach the I-message formula: 'I feel ___ when ___. Could we ___?' Practice 3 I-messages asking for healthy foods.

- **Q:** What are the parts of an I-message?
  **A:** I feel (feeling) when (situation); could we (request)?
- **Q:** Turn 'We never have good snacks!' into an I-message.
  **A:** E.g., 'I feel hungry after school when there's only chips; could we buy apples or yogurt?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Role-play
*Student:* Role-play asking for a healthy option at a restaurant, a friend's house and the grocery store.

*How to teach:* Role-play asking for a healthy option at a restaurant (water instead of soda, fruit instead of fries), at a friend's house (politely asking for water), and at the grocery store (asking to buy a new vegetable).

- **Q:** How could you politely ask for a healthier side at a restaurant?
  **A:** E.g., 'May I please have the fruit cup instead of fries?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family traditions
*Student:* Interview a family member about a traditional family dish and identify its food groups.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about a traditional family dish: its history, ingredients, and when it's eaten. Identify the food groups.

- **Q:** Name a good interview question about a family dish.
  **A:** E.g., 'Who taught you to make it?' or 'When does our family eat it?'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Healthy twist
*Student:* Suggest one healthy twist to the family recipe and discuss it with the cook.

*How to teach:* Suggest one healthy twist (more vegetables, whole grains, baking instead of frying, less salt) and discuss respectfully with the cook.

- **Q:** Give an example of a healthy twist.
  **A:** E.g., use brown rice instead of white, add spinach, or bake instead of fry.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 3 test on labels, influences and communication.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test (see key).

- **Q:** What does the 5/20 rule say?
  **A:** 5% DV or less of a nutrient is low; 20% DV or more is high.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test and family interview.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test key: 1) What should you check first on a label? — serving size and servings per container. 2) 2 servings at 120 calories each = 240 calories. 3) 5/20 rule: 5% DV or less is low; 20% or more is high. 4) Is 30% DV sodium high or low? — high. 5) Name 2 ad techniques — e.g., cartoon characters, prizes, celebrities, jingles. 6) Name 3 influences on food choices — family, culture, friends, media, cost, taste. 7) Write an I-message asking for a healthy food — must include feeling, situation and request. 8) Why is a whole apple better than fruit snacks? — more fiber and vitamins, no added sugar. Proficient = 6 of 8. Family interview: complete with dish, ingredients, food groups and one healthy twist.
