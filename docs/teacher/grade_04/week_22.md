# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 22

## ELA 4: Planning an opinion essay

**Prepare:** Two short articles on single-use plastic bags (note: California banned most single-use plastic carryout bags at grocery stores starting in 2016 through Proposition 67, and a stronger 2024 law, SB 1053, bans plastic film bags at checkout starting 2026). Writer's notebook.

**Watch for:**
- Using modals incorrectly ('We must to recycle'). Modals are followed by the base verb with no 'to': must recycle.
- Choosing reasons that overlap. Each reason should be different (environment, wildlife, cost).

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a topic
*Student:* Choose a question (Should California ban single-use plastic bags?) and state an opinion.

*How to teach:* The child chooses a question and writes a clear opinion statement.

- **Q:** Write an opinion statement on plastic bags.
  **A:** Example: 'California should ban all single-use plastic bags because they harm wildlife and pollute our beaches.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gather evidence
*Student:* Gather facts from two short articles and record them with sources.

*How to teach:* The child gathers facts from two articles and records each with its source.

- **Q:** Why should you record the source of each fact?
  **A:** So you can give credit and check the information later.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Modal auxiliaries
*Student:* Practice can, may, must, should in 12 sentences.

*How to teach:* Teach can (ability), may (permission/possibility), must (necessity), should (advice). The child writes 12 sentences.

- **Q:** Choose the modal: 'You ___ bring a reusable bag; it's a good idea.'
  **A:** should.
- **Q:** Choose the modal: 'Drivers ___ stop at a red light.'
  **A:** must.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Outline
*Student:* Plan the essay: introduction, 3 reasons with evidence, conclusion.

*How to teach:* The child plans introduction, 3 reasons with evidence, conclusion.

- **Q:** What goes in each body paragraph?
  **A:** One reason, at least two pieces of evidence, and an explanation.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Debate the topic with a parent arguing the opposite side.

*How to teach:* Debate with your child taking the opposite side; this strengthens counterargument thinking.

- **Q:** What is a counterargument against banning bags?
  **A:** Example: paper bags also use resources and cost more; some people reuse plastic bags.

**Assessment:** Essay outline.

**Answer key:** Outline check (4-point): 4 = clear opinion, 3 distinct reasons each with 2 pieces of sourced evidence, planned hook and conclusion; 3 = 3 reasons, some evidence missing; 2 = reasons overlap or little evidence; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 4: Adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, number lines.

**Watch for:**
- Adding denominators (3/8 + 4/8 = 7/16). The pieces stay eighths; count how many eighths you have.
- Subtracting from 1 without renaming (1 - 3/8 = 2/8?). Rename 1 as 8/8 first: 8/8 - 3/8 = 5/8.

### Day 1 (Monday): Joining parts
*Student:* Add fractions with like denominators using fraction strips and write equations (10 problems).

*How to teach:* Model 3/8 + 4/8 with strips and say 'three eighths plus four eighths is seven eighths.' The child solves 10 problems writing equations.

- **Q:** 3/8 + 4/8 = ?
  **A:** 7/8.
- **Q:** Why isn't 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/10?
  **A:** The pieces are still fifths; you have 3 fifths, 3/5. The size of the pieces doesn't change when you add.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Taking away
*Student:* Subtract like fractions using number lines including from 1 whole (1 - 3/8).

*How to teach:* On a number line show 1 - 3/8 by starting at 8/8 and jumping back 3 eighths. The child solves several subtraction problems.

- **Q:** 1 - 3/8 = ?
  **A:** 5/8.
- **Q:** 7/12 - 5/12 = ?
  **A:** 2/12 (= 1/6).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems like 'Maya painted 3/6 of a fence and Leo 2/6; how much is painted?'

*How to teach:* The child solves 6 word problems, drawing a strip or number line for each and writing a sentence.

- **Q:** Maya painted 3/6 of a fence and Leo painted 2/6. How much is painted? How much is left?
  **A:** 5/6 painted; 1/6 left.
- **Q:** A pizza had 8 slices; Sam ate 3/8 and Kim ate 2/8. How much was eaten?
  **A:** 5/8 of the pizza.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sums greater than 1
*Student:* Add like fractions with sums greater than 1 and rename as mixed numbers.

*How to teach:* Add 5/6 + 4/6 with strips and show that 6/6 make a whole, leaving 3/6 more: 1 3/6. The child renames several sums.

- **Q:** 5/6 + 4/6 = ?
  **A:** 9/6 = 1 3/6 (or 1 1/2).
- **Q:** 3/4 + 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 6/4 = 1 2/4 (or 1 1/2).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on adding and subtracting like fractions.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently. Correct together.

- **Q:** 1 - 2/5 = ?
  **A:** 3/5.

**Assessment:** Fraction addition/subtraction quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 2/8 + 5/8 = 7/8 2) 4/10 + 3/10 = 7/10 3) 9/12 - 4/12 = 5/12 4) 1 - 3/8 = 5/8 5) 4/5 + 3/5 = 7/5 = 1 2/5 6) 5/6 - 1/6 = 4/6 7) Word problem: a rope 7/8 yard long, 3/8 yard cut off: 4/8 yard left 8) 2/3 + 2/3 = 4/3 = 1 1/3. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: Rock layers and fossils

**Prepare:** Clear jar with lid, sand, soil, gravel, water; shells and leaves; clay; white glue or plaster of Paris. Photo of Grand Canyon rock layers. (Painted Cave in Santa Barbara has Chumash rock paintings, not fossils; a California fossil site such as the La Brea Tar Pits works well as a second photo.)

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the top layer is oldest. In undisturbed layers the bottom is oldest (law of superposition).
- Thinking fossils are the actual animals. Most fossils are imprints, casts or minerals that replaced the original material.

### Day 1 (Monday): Layer jar
*Student:* Make layers of sediment in a jar and observe settling.

*How to teach:* Shake sediments with water in a jar; let settle overnight.

- **Q:** Which material settled first and why?
  **A:** Gravel settled first at the bottom (heaviest/largest), then sand, then fine soil on top.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Superposition
*Student:* Order 6 rock layer drawings from oldest to youngest using the law of superposition and explain one.

*How to teach:* Order 6 layer drawings from oldest to youngest.

- **Q:** In a stack of rock layers, which is oldest?
  **A:** The bottom layer, because it was deposited first (superposition).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fossil casts
*Student:* Press shells and leaves into clay to make imprint fossils and fill one with plaster or glue to make a cast.

*How to teach:* Press shells/leaves into clay for imprints; fill one with glue or plaster for a cast.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a mold and a cast?
  **A:** A mold is the hollow imprint; a cast forms when the mold is filled and hardens into the shape of the organism.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grand Canyon & Painted Cave
*Student:* Analyze a photo of rock layers and fossils and infer past environments.

*How to teach:* Analyze the Grand Canyon photo; infer past environments.

- **Q:** What does a fish or shell fossil in a desert canyon tell us?
  **A:** The area was once covered by water (an ocean or lake).
- **Q:** What do you infer from a layer with fern fossils?
  **A:** The area was once a warm, wet environment where ferns grew.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain changes in a landscape over time using layers.

*How to teach:* The child explains changes in a landscape over time using layers.

- **Q:** Explain how a canyon with layers formed.
  **A:** Layers of sediment were deposited over long periods (oldest at bottom); later a river eroded down through them, exposing the layers.

**Assessment:** Rock layer explanation.

**Answer key:** Explanation rubric: 4 = uses superposition (bottom oldest), fossils as evidence of past environments (e.g., marine fossils = once under water), and erosion exposing layers; 3 = two of these; 2 = one; 1 = inaccurate.

## California, A Changing State: Gold discovered!

**Prepare:** Calisphere set 'The Gold Rush Era: Everyday Life and People'; a pie tin, sand, pebbles, gravel painted gold, a tub of water. Facts: James Marshall found gold on January 24, 1848 at John Sutter's sawmill at Coloma on the South Fork of the American River; Sutter wanted it kept secret; in May 1848 Sam Brannan (a merchant who also published the California Star newspaper) ran through San Francisco shouting 'Gold! Gold from the American River!'; President Polk confirmed the discovery in December 1848, setting off the 1849 rush.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Gold Rush began in 1849. Gold was found in January 1848; '49ers' arrived in huge numbers in 1849.
- Thinking most miners got rich. Most found little; many merchants earned more.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sutter's Mill
*Student:* Read about James Marshall's January 1848 discovery at Sutter's Mill in Coloma and John Sutter's reaction.

*How to teach:* Read about Marshall's discovery.

- **Q:** Who found gold, when and where?
  **A:** James Marshall, January 24, 1848, at Sutter's Mill in Coloma on the American River.
- **Q:** How did John Sutter react?
  **A:** He wanted to keep it secret, fearing his workers would leave and his land would be overrun — which happened.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The news spreads
*Student:* Read the 1848 newspaper announcement excerpt (Sam Brannan's 'Gold! Gold from the American River!') and track how news spread worldwide.

*How to teach:* Read Brannan's announcement; track how news spread.

- **Q:** Why did Sam Brannan spread the news?
  **A:** He owned a store and wanted to sell mining supplies (he had bought up pans and shovels).
- **Q:** How did the news spread around the world?
  **A:** By newspapers, ships, letters and President Polk's December 1848 message to Congress.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mining methods
*Student:* Practice panning with sand and painted gravel and compare pans, rockers and sluices in a chart.

*How to teach:* Pan in the tub; compare pans, rockers and sluices.

- **Q:** Why does gold stay in the pan?
  **A:** Gold is much heavier (denser) than sand and gravel, so it sinks to the bottom while water washes lighter material out.
- **Q:** Which method could process the most dirt?
  **A:** The sluice (a long box with ridges), worked by teams.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Primary source photos
*Student:* Analyze two daguerreotypes from the Calisphere set 'The Gold Rush Era: Everyday Life and People' using See-Think-Wonder.

*How to teach:* Analyze two daguerreotypes with See-Think-Wonder.

- **Q:** What is a daguerreotype?
  **A:** An early type of photograph on a silver-coated copper plate (1840s-1850s).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a diary entry as a Forty-Niner arriving at the diggings, using five facts.

*How to teach:* Write a Forty-Niner diary entry with five facts.

- **Q:** Why were miners called Forty-Niners?
  **A:** Most arrived in 1849.

**Assessment:** Forty-Niner diary entry.

**Answer key:** Diary rubric: 4 = first-person entry with at least five accurate facts (e.g., Sutter's Mill 1848, route taken, high prices, panning/rockers, tents/camp life, diverse miners) and vivid detail; 3 = 3-4 facts; 2 = 1-2 facts; 1 = inaccurate.

## Art & Music 4: Composing rhythms and melodies

**Prepare:** Recorder, staff paper, and Chrome Music Lab Song Maker (set to 8 bars).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a composition must be long and complicated. Good melodies repeat and vary a short idea.
- Ending on a random note. Ending on G (home note) makes a melody sound finished.

### Day 1 (Monday): Improvise
*Student:* Improvise 4-beat answers on recorder using B, A and G to a parent's 4-beat questions.

*How to teach:* Improvise 4-beat answers with B, A, G to your 4-beat questions. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** What makes a good musical answer?
  **A:** It fits the beat, relates to the question and ends on G (home).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Motive
*Student:* Create a 2-measure melodic motive and repeat it with changes.

*How to teach:* Create a 2-measure motive; repeat it with changes. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is a motive?
  **A:** A short musical idea that can be repeated and changed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Song Maker
*Student:* Compose an 8-measure melody in Chrome Music Lab Song Maker.

*How to teach:* Compose an 8-measure melody in Song Maker. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why repeat your motive?
  **A:** Repetition makes a melody memorable and unified.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Notate
*Student:* Notate the melody on staff paper with rhythm and pitch.

*How to teach:* Notate the melody on staff paper. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why write the melody down?
  **A:** So it can be played again exactly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play the 8-measure composition on recorder and record it for next week's revision.

*How to teach:* Play on recorder; record for revision. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** How should your melody end?
  **A:** On the home note, G.

**Assessment:** Composition draft.

**Answer key:** Composition draft: 8 measures, uses a repeated/varied motive, notated rhythm and pitch, ends on G. Proficient = all four.

## PE 4: Shooting and offense-defense

**Prepare:** Basketball and a lowered hoop or a laundry basket on a chair; phone for video; BEEF checklist (Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through).

**Watch for:**
- Shooting with two hands pushing from the chest. Use one shooting hand with the guide hand on the side; elbow under the ball.
- Watching the ball instead of the target. Eyes stay on the rim (front or back) throughout the shot.

### Day 1 (Monday): BEEF shooting
*Student:* Shoot 30 shots close to the hoop using BEEF cues.

*How to teach:* Teach BEEF: Balance (feet shoulder-width, knees bent), Eyes (on the target), Elbow (under the ball, in an L), Follow-through (snap wrist, 'hand in the cookie jar'). 30 shots close to the hoop.

- **Q:** What does BEEF stand for?
  **A:** Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Give-and-go
*Student:* Practice give-and-go: pass to a partner, cut toward the basket and receive the return pass.

*How to teach:* Give-and-go: pass to a partner, cut toward the basket, receive the return pass and shoot.

- **Q:** What is a give-and-go?
  **A:** Passing to a teammate, then cutting toward the basket to get a return pass.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Defense
*Student:* Practice defensive slide and keeping between the player and the basket.

*How to teach:* Defensive slide: low stance, slide feet without crossing, stay between your player and the basket.

- **Q:** Why not cross your feet when sliding on defense?
  **A:** Crossing makes you slower to change direction and easier to trip.

### Day 4 (Thursday): 2-on-1
*Student:* Play 2-on-1 with a passer, cutter and defender.

*How to teach:* 2-on-1: one passer, one cutter, one defender. Attackers use passes and cuts to get an open shot.

- **Q:** In 2-on-1, when should the ball handler pass?
  **A:** When the defender commits to them, leaving the teammate open.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record 5 shots on video and self-assess each BEEF cue on a checklist.

*How to teach:* Record 5 shots and self-assess each BEEF cue.

- **Q:** Your video shows your elbow sticking out. Which cue do you fix?
  **A:** Elbow — keep it under the ball.

**Assessment:** BEEF self-assessment.

**Answer key:** BEEF self-assessment: for 5 recorded shots, check Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through (20 checks). 4 = 17–20 and accurate self-assessment matching the video; 3 = 13–16; 2 = 8–12; 1 = below 8.

## Health 4: Safe medicine use

**Prepare:** Parent preview: an EMPTY OTC box or the parent holds the bottle while the child reads the Drug Facts label. Highlighter. Never let the child handle real medicines unsupervised.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking more medicine works faster or better. Taking more than the label or doctor says can cause an overdose.
- Thinking expired medicine is fine. It may not work or may be unsafe; an adult should dispose of it properly (drug take-back).

### Day 1 (Monday): Drug Facts label
*Student:* With an adult, find active ingredient, uses, warnings, directions and expiration date on a Drug Facts label.

*How to teach:* With an adult, find and highlight on the Drug Facts label: active ingredient, uses, warnings, directions (dose by age/weight), and expiration date.

- **Q:** Which part of the Drug Facts label tells how much to take?
  **A:** The Directions section.
- **Q:** What does the active ingredient do?
  **A:** It is the part of the medicine that makes it work.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Side effects
*Student:* Learn what side effects are and why you tell an adult if you feel unwell after medicine.

*How to teach:* Explain side effects (unwanted effects like drowsiness, upset stomach, rash). Tell an adult right away if you feel unwell after medicine; trouble breathing or swelling is an emergency (call 911).

- **Q:** What is a side effect?
  **A:** An unwanted effect of a medicine, like sleepiness or an upset stomach.
- **Q:** What should you do if you get a rash after taking medicine?
  **A:** Tell a parent or trusted adult right away; if you have trouble breathing or swelling, call 911.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Safe rules
*Student:* Write 5 safe medicine rules (only from a trusted adult, never share prescriptions, follow dose).

*How to teach:* Write 5 safe medicine rules: only take medicine from a trusted adult; follow the label or doctor's directions exactly; never share or take someone else's prescription; never take medicine that's not labeled or that you find; store medicine out of reach in its original container.

- **Q:** Why shouldn't you take a friend's prescription?
  **A:** It was prescribed for their body and illness; it could be the wrong medicine or dose for you and could harm you.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Scenario practice
*Student:* Act out what to do if a friend offers you a pill or you find medicine on the floor.

*How to teach:* Act out: a friend offers a pill 'that helps you focus'; you find a pill on the floor. Practice saying no and telling an adult.

- **Q:** A friend offers you a pill at school. What do you do?
  **A:** Say 'No thanks,' don't take it, walk away and tell a trusted adult (teacher or school nurse).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Create a safe medicine checklist and explain it to a younger sibling or parent.

*How to teach:* Create a safe medicine checklist and teach it to a younger sibling or parent.

- **Q:** Name 3 items on your safe medicine checklist.
  **A:** Any 3 rules: from a trusted adult only; follow directions; check expiration date; never share; tell an adult about side effects; store out of reach.

**Assessment:** Safe medicine checklist.

**Answer key:** Safe medicine checklist rubric: 4 = at least 5 accurate rules (only from trusted adult, follow directions/dose, never share prescriptions, check expiration, report side effects, store safely), clearly explained to another person; 3 = 4 rules; 2 = 2–3 rules or an unsafe rule; 1 = incomplete.
