# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 24

## ELA 4: Publishing and speaking: formal vs informal

**Prepare:** Computer, note cards, a sample text message and a short speech on the same topic. Unit 6 test for Day 5 (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Joining two complete sentences with only a comma (comma splice). Use a comma + and/but/or/so.
- Using informal language in formal writing ('gonna,' 'kinda,' 'super cool').

### Day 1 (Monday): Edit
*Student:* Edit for commas in compound sentences, spelling and capitalization.

*How to teach:* The child edits for commas in compound sentences, spelling and capitalization.

- **Q:** Add a comma: 'Plastic bags blow into the ocean and animals eat them.'
  **A:** 'Plastic bags blow into the ocean, and animals eat them.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Publish
*Student:* Type the final opinion essay in one sitting, formatting the title and paragraphs, and proofread it once aloud.

*How to teach:* The child types the final essay in one sitting, formats title and paragraphs, and proofreads aloud.

- **Q:** What should the title of an opinion essay do?
  **A:** Hint at the opinion and catch interest, e.g., 'Bag the Plastic!'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Formal vs informal
*Student:* Compare a text message and a speech on the same topic and rewrite informal sentences formally.

*How to teach:* Compare a text message and a speech. The child rewrites informal sentences formally.

- **Q:** Rewrite formally: 'Plastic bags r super bad lol.'
  **A:** 'Plastic bags are very harmful to the environment.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Speech
*Student:* Turn the essay into a 2-minute speech on note cards and rehearse.

*How to teach:* The child condenses the essay to a 2-minute speech on note cards (key words only) and rehearses.

- **Q:** Why use key words instead of full sentences on note cards?
  **A:** So you look up and talk to the audience instead of reading.

### Day 5 (Friday): Deliver & assess
*Student:* Deliver the 2-minute speech to family, then take the Unit 6 test on reasons and evidence and opinion writing.

*How to teach:* The child delivers the speech, then takes the Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** What makes evidence strong?
  **A:** It is accurate, specific, relevant and from a reliable source.

**Assessment:** Published essay, speech and Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Essay rubric: as week 23 plus conventions (4 = nearly error-free). Speech rubric: 4 = clear opinion and reasons in about 2 minutes, eye contact, good volume and pace, formal language; 3 = minor issues; 2 = reads from cards; 1 = incomplete. Unit 6 test key: 1) fact vs. opinion sort (fact = provable) 2) identify claim of a passage 3) list 3 reasons 4) match evidence to reasons 5) strong vs. weak evidence with explanation 6) modal choice: must = necessity, should = advice, may = permission, can = ability 7) linking words: for instance, in addition, because 8) formal rewrite of an informal sentence. Proficient: 80%.

## Math 4: Multiplying a fraction by a whole number

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, measuring cups (including 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 3/4 cup if available), flour or rice for scooping, a muffin recipe that uses 3/4 cup amounts.

**Watch for:**
- Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the whole number (3 × 2/5 = 6/15). Think 3 groups of 2 fifths = 6 fifths = 6/5.
- Not knowing where the answer lies between whole numbers. Rename the improper fraction as a mixed number: 10/3 = 3 1/3 is between 3 and 4.

### Day 1 (Monday): Multiples of unit fractions
*Student:* Show 5/4 = 5 x 1/4 with fraction strips and write 6 similar equations.

*How to teach:* Lay out five 1/4 strips and write 5/4 = 5 × 1/4. The child writes 6 similar equations from strips.

- **Q:** Write 5/4 as a multiple of a unit fraction.
  **A:** 5/4 = 5 × 1/4.
- **Q:** 7/3 = ? × 1/3
  **A:** 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Whole times fraction
*Student:* Solve 3 x 2/5 as 6 x 1/5 with number lines (10 problems).

*How to teach:* On a number line in fifths, make 3 jumps of 2/5 and see they land at 6/5. The child solves 10 problems.

- **Q:** 3 × 2/5 = ?
  **A:** 6/5 = 1 1/5 (3 × 2/5 = 6 × 1/5).
- **Q:** 4 × 3/8 = ?
  **A:** 12/8 = 1 4/8 (= 1 1/2).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Recipe scaling
*Student:* Triple a muffin recipe with 3/4 cup amounts using measuring cups, then record the multiplication.

*How to teach:* Measure 3/4 cup three times into a bowl and see how many cups result. Record 3 × 3/4 = 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups, then scale 2 more ingredients.

- **Q:** Triple 3/4 cup of milk.
  **A:** 3 × 3/4 = 9/4 = 2 1/4 cups.
- **Q:** Triple 2/3 cup of sugar.
  **A:** 6/3 = 2 cups.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems like '5 students each drink 2/3 cup of juice; how much juice?' and find whole numbers between which the answer lies.

*How to teach:* The child solves 6 word problems and names the two whole numbers each answer lies between.

- **Q:** 5 students each drink 2/3 cup of juice. How much juice? Between which whole numbers?
  **A:** 10/3 = 3 1/3 cups; between 3 and 4.
- **Q:** A recipe uses 3/8 cup of oil per batch. How much for 6 batches?
  **A:** 18/8 = 2 2/8 (= 2 1/4) cups; between 2 and 3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on multiplying fractions by whole numbers.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently. Correct together.

- **Q:** 4 × 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 12/4 = 3.

**Assessment:** Fraction multiplication quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 4 × 1/5 = 4/5 2) 3 × 2/5 = 6/5 = 1 1/5 3) 6 × 3/8 = 18/8 = 2 2/8 4) 2 × 5/6 = 10/6 = 1 4/6 5) 7/3 = 7 × 1/3 6) 5 × 2/3 = 10/3 = 3 1/3 cups, between 3 and 4 7) 4 × 3/4 = 3 8) 8 × 1/2 = 4. Simplified equivalents accepted. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: Maps of Earth's features

**Prepare:** Modeling clay, dental floss or string to slice, a ruler, a marker, paper. Printed topographic map of Half Dome (USGS or nps.gov), an ocean floor map, a world map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking contour lines show roads or paths. Each line connects points of equal elevation.
- Thinking widely spaced lines mean steep. Close lines = steep; far apart = gentle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clay mountain
*Student:* Build a clay mountain and slice contour lines to make a topographic map.

*How to teach:* Build a clay mountain; mark heights every 1 cm with a ruler; slice horizontally and trace each slice on paper.

- **Q:** What does each traced line represent?
  **A:** All points at the same elevation (a contour line).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read a topo map
*Student:* Read a topographic map of Half Dome and find the steepest side by looking at contour line spacing.

*How to teach:* Find the steepest side of Half Dome on the topo map.

- **Q:** How can you tell which side is steepest?
  **A:** Where contour lines are closest together — Half Dome's sheer northwest face.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ocean floor
*Student:* Read an ocean floor map and identify trenches and ridges.

*How to teach:* Read the ocean floor map; find trenches and ridges.

- **Q:** What is an ocean trench?
  **A:** A long, very deep, narrow valley in the ocean floor (e.g., the Mariana Trench, the deepest).
- **Q:** What is a mid-ocean ridge?
  **A:** An underwater mountain chain, such as the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

### Day 4 (Thursday): World patterns
*Student:* Mark the Sierra Nevada, Andes, Rockies and Himalayas on a world map and note where they lie relative to coasts.

*How to teach:* Mark the Sierra Nevada, Andes, Rockies and Himalayas on the world map.

- **Q:** What pattern do you notice?
  **A:** The Sierra Nevada, Andes and Rockies run along western edges of the Americas near the Pacific; the Himalayas are inland, where India meets Asia.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on reading contour lines, elevation and ocean-floor features.

*How to teach:* Give the map quiz.

- **Q:** Contour interval is 100 ft. A hill has 5 lines above the 0 ft line. About how tall is it?
  **A:** About 500 ft (at least 500 ft).

**Assessment:** Map reading quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Contour lines connect equal elevations 2) Close lines = steep 3) Far apart = gentle 4) Circles inside circles = hill/peak 5) Find elevation by counting lines × interval 6) Trench = deep underwater valley 7) Ridge = underwater mountain range 8) Many mountain ranges lie near coasts/plate edges. Proficient: 7/8.

## California, A Changing State: The Gold Rush transforms California

**Prepare:** Calisphere sets 'Growth of Cities in the Gold Rush Era' and 'Environmental Impact in the Gold Rush Era'; population figures (approx.): California's non-Native population about 14,000 in 1848; about 100,000 by late 1849; about 250,000 by 1852. San Francisco grew from about 500-800 people in 1847-48 to tens of thousands by 1850. A tray of soil and spray bottle. Unit 7 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Gold Rush helped everyone. California's Native population fell catastrophically (roughly 150,000 in 1848 to about 30,000 by 1870) from violence, disease and loss of land.
- Thinking hydraulic mining only affected the hills. Debris flooded rivers and farms far downstream; it was largely stopped by a court ruling in 1884.

### Day 1 (Monday): Boom cities
*Student:* Compare images of San Francisco in 1847 and 1851 from the Calisphere set 'Growth of Cities in the Gold Rush Era' and list changes.

*How to teach:* Compare San Francisco images from 1847 and 1851.

- **Q:** List two changes in San Francisco.
  **A:** It grew from a small village into a crowded city with many buildings, wharves and hundreds of abandoned ships in the harbor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Population data
*Student:* Graph California's non-Native population from 1848 to 1852 and explain the change.

*How to teach:* Graph population from 1848 to 1852.

- **Q:** About how much did the non-Native population grow from 1848 to 1852?
  **A:** From about 14,000 to about 250,000 — more than 15 times larger.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hydraulic mining model
*Student:* Spray water at a soil hillside in a tray to model hydraulic mining and observe the debris.

*How to teach:* Spray water at a soil hillside to model hydraulic mining.

- **Q:** What did the model show?
  **A:** High-pressure water washed away the hillside, sending mud and debris downhill — like real hydraulic mining clogging rivers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Environmental impact
*Student:* Analyze images from the Calisphere set 'Environmental Impact in the Gold Rush Era' and list effects on rivers and land.

*How to teach:* Analyze environmental impact images.

- **Q:** List two environmental effects of the Gold Rush.
  **A:** Hillsides washed away by hydraulic mining; rivers clogged with sediment and mercury pollution; forests cut; salmon runs harmed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 7 test and complete a cause-effect chart of the Gold Rush.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test and cause-effect chart.

- **Q:** Name one long-term effect of the Gold Rush.
  **A:** Rapid statehood (1850), growth of cities like San Francisco and Sacramento, diverse population.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test and cause-effect chart.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test key: 1) Marshall, Sutter's Mill, January 24, 1848 2) Brannan spread the news 3) Forty-Niners 4) Mining methods: pan, rocker, sluice, hydraulic 5) Diverse miners from many nations 6) Foreign Miners' Tax unfair 7) Biddy Mason won freedom 1856 8) Merchants like Levi Strauss profited 9) Population boom; San Francisco grows 10) Environmental damage and Native population decline. Cause-effect chart: proficient lists 4+ accurate effects (statehood, cities, immigration, environmental damage, harm to Native peoples, economic growth).

## Art & Music 4: Presenting compositions and evaluating music

**Prepare:** Recorder, recording device; find two youth recorder or band performances online. Prepare criteria (accuracy, tone, expression, stage presence).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the composer can't perform. Many composers premiere their own music.
- Judging others harshly. Use criteria and specific, kind language.

### Day 1 (Monday): Program note
*Student:* Write a program note explaining your composition's title and intent.

*How to teach:* Write a program note with title and intent. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What is a program note?
  **A:** A short explanation of a piece for the audience.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the ABA piece with dynamics and tempo markings.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with dynamics and tempo markings. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why add dynamics and tempo to your composition?
  **A:** They tell the performer how to express the mood.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Premiere
*Student:* Perform the premiere of your composition for family.

*How to teach:* Perform the premiere for family. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What is a premiere?
  **A:** The first public performance of a piece.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluate
*Student:* Watch two recorded youth performances online and evaluate them with criteria.

*How to teach:* Evaluate two youth performances with criteria. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Give a specific, kind evaluation comment.
  **A:** 'The tone was clear, but the tempo sped up in the middle.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-evaluate your premiere using the same criteria.

*How to teach:* Self-evaluate the premiere. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Which criterion was strongest in your premiere?
  **A:** Answer varies; cite evidence.

**Assessment:** Composition premiere and evaluation.

**Answer key:** Premiere rubric (1–4 each): accuracy, tone, expression, stage presence, program note. Evaluation: uses all criteria with specific examples.

## PE 4: Mexican folk dance

**Prepare:** A video of a ballet folklórico performance (many are online) and a recording of 'La Raspa' (a traditional Mexican folk dance tune) — preview both. Hard floor, shoes.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all Mexican folk dances are the same. Each region of Mexico has its own dances, music and costumes.
- Hopping heavily in the bleking step. It is a light hop, extending one heel forward at a time in rhythm.

### Day 1 (Monday): Watch & discuss
*Student:* Watch a ballet folklórico performance video and describe the footwork and costumes.

*How to teach:* Watch ballet folklórico. Describe the footwork (zapateado — rhythmic stamping), the wide, colorful skirts dancers swirl, and the charro suits and sombreros.

- **Q:** What is zapateado?
  **A:** Rhythmic stamping footwork in Mexican folk dance, making the feet sound like percussion.
- **Q:** Why do dancers swirl their skirts?
  **A:** The wide, colorful skirts add to the visual beauty and help show the movement and story of the dance.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): La Raspa steps
*Student:* Learn the La Raspa bleking step (alternating heels) in time with the music.

*How to teach:* Teach the bleking step: hop and extend the right heel forward, hop and switch to the left heel, hop switch to right, then pause (clap twice). Repeat starting with left.

- **Q:** Describe the bleking step.
  **A:** Light hops switching feet so one heel at a time touches forward — right, left, right, then hold/clap.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner figure
*Student:* Learn the elbow-swing partner figure of La Raspa and connect it to the bleking section.

*How to teach:* Teach the elbow swing: partners hook right elbows and skip around 8 counts, then left elbows 8 counts. Connect to the bleking section.

- **Q:** How many counts do you swing each way?
  **A:** 8 counts with the right elbow, then 8 with the left.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Full dance
*Student:* Perform La Raspa from start to finish with a family member three times.

*How to teach:* Perform La Raspa start to finish with a family member 3 times.

- **Q:** What helps you know when to switch from bleking to the elbow swing?
  **A:** Listening for the change in the music (the second section of the tune).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform for family and explain where the dance comes from.

*How to teach:* Perform for family and explain the dance is a Mexican folk dance (commonly associated with Veracruz), danced at celebrations and fiestas.

- **Q:** Where does La Raspa come from?
  **A:** Mexico (it is a Mexican folk dance commonly associated with Veracruz).

**Assessment:** La Raspa performance.

**Answer key:** La Raspa performance rubric: 4 = bleking step on the beat, smooth elbow-swing section, transitions with the music, performs 3 times with a partner, explains origin (Mexico, danced at celebrations); 3 = minor timing errors; 2 = frequent errors or needs cues; 1 = cannot perform.

## Health 4: Tobacco and the body

**Prepare:** Parent preview: tobacco unit. Two kitchen sponges, molasses or dark syrup, a bowl, straws (one per person), a lungs diagram (KidsHealth or the American Lung Association). Stop the straw activity immediately if the child feels dizzy; skip it for children with asthma.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking smoking only harms the lungs. Tobacco damages the heart, blood vessels, mouth, teeth, skin and nearly every organ.
- Thinking a person can easily quit whenever they want. Nicotine is very addictive; most people who smoke want to quit but find it hard.

### Day 1 (Monday): What's in tobacco
*Student:* Learn about nicotine, tar and carbon monoxide and what each does to the body.

*How to teach:* Teach 3 harmful substances: nicotine (addictive drug that speeds up heart rate and raises blood pressure), tar (sticky substance that coats the lungs and contains cancer-causing chemicals), carbon monoxide (poison gas that takes the place of oxygen in the blood).

- **Q:** What does tar do to the lungs?
  **A:** It coats the lungs with a sticky residue, damages the cilia and air sacs, and contains chemicals that can cause cancer.
- **Q:** Why is carbon monoxide harmful?
  **A:** It takes the place of oxygen in the blood, so the body gets less oxygen.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lung model
*Student:* Model healthy vs smoker's lungs by soaking one sponge in dark syrup and comparing.

*How to teach:* Soak one sponge in water and one in dark syrup. Squeeze both and compare how easily they take in and release liquid, like healthy lungs vs. tar-coated lungs.

- **Q:** What does the syrup sponge represent?
  **A:** A smoker's lungs coated with tar, which can't take in and push out air as well.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Straw breathing
*Student:* Jog in place, then breathe through a straw for 30 seconds to feel restricted breathing (stop if dizzy).

*How to teach:* Jog in place 30 seconds, then breathe only through a straw for up to 30 seconds (stop if dizzy). Discuss how it felt — like lung disease (emphysema) that makes breathing hard.

- **Q:** How did breathing through a straw feel, and what does it model?
  **A:** Hard to get enough air; it models how damaged lungs (like emphysema from smoking) make breathing difficult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Addiction
*Student:* Read how nicotine causes addiction and why quitting is hard.

*How to teach:* Read how nicotine causes addiction: the brain gets used to it and craves more; without it a person feels irritable, anxious and restless (withdrawal).

- **Q:** What is addiction?
  **A:** When the body and brain depend on a drug and a person feels a strong need to keep using it, even when it causes harm.
- **Q:** What are withdrawal symptoms?
  **A:** Uncomfortable feelings when a person stops using a drug, like irritability, anxiety, trouble focusing and cravings.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw a body diagram labeling 6 effects of tobacco.

*How to teach:* Draw a body outline and label 6 effects of tobacco.

- **Q:** Name 2 effects of tobacco besides on the lungs.
  **A:** Any 2: heart disease and stroke, yellow teeth and bad breath, gum disease, mouth/throat cancer, wrinkled skin, less sense of taste and smell, less energy for sports.

**Assessment:** Body effects diagram.

**Answer key:** Body effects diagram rubric: 6 accurate labeled effects placed on the right body part, e.g., brain — nicotine addiction; mouth — bad breath, stained teeth, gum disease, mouth cancer; lungs — tar buildup, lung cancer, emphysema, coughing; heart — faster heart rate, heart disease; blood — carbon monoxide reduces oxygen; skin — early wrinkles; also stroke, less stamina. 4 = 6 accurate effects; 3 = 4–5; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.
