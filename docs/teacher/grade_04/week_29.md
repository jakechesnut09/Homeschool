# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 29

## ELA 4: Retelling Oz: narrative and comparison

**Prepare:** Alice's Adventures in Wonderland chapter 1 'Down the Rabbit-Hole' (Lewis Carroll, Gutenberg); the narrative checklist from week 4.

**Watch for:**
- Losing Dorothy's character when writing a new adventure. Keep her traits (brave, kind, wants to go home) and her companions consistent.
- Long dialogue with no paragraphs. New speaker, new paragraph.

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare quests
*Student:* Read chapter 1 of Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and compare its journey pattern with Oz.

*How to teach:* Read Alice chapter 1. Compare its journey pattern with Oz.

- **Q:** How does each girl enter the strange world?
  **A:** Alice follows the White Rabbit and falls down a rabbit-hole; Dorothy is carried by a cyclone.
- **Q:** What happens when Alice drinks from the 'DRINK ME' bottle?
  **A:** She shrinks to ten inches tall.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a new adventure for Dorothy in a California setting.

*How to teach:* The child plans a new episode for Dorothy in a California setting with problem, events and resolution.

- **Q:** Name a California setting with built-in adventure.
  **A:** Examples: a redwood forest, Death Valley, a Pacific tide pool, Yosemite's waterfalls.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the episode with dialogue and description.

*How to teach:* The child drafts the episode with dialogue and sensory details.

- **Q:** What should Dorothy's dialogue show?
  **A:** Her personality — kind, brave, practical — and move the plot forward.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise & edit
*Student:* Revise the episode with the narrative checklist (hook, dialogue, sensory details, ending) and edit for quotation marks.

*How to teach:* Revise with the checklist and edit for quotation marks.

- **Q:** Punctuate: lets follow the yellow trail said Dorothy
  **A:** "Let's follow the yellow trail," said Dorothy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share & assess
*Student:* Read the episode aloud; scored with the narrative rubric.

*How to teach:* The child reads the episode aloud; score with the narrative rubric.

- **Q:** What makes a satisfying ending?
  **A:** The problem is resolved in a way that fits the characters, and the reader feels closure.

**Assessment:** New-episode narrative.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (4-point): 4 = engaging hook, Dorothy and companions in character, clear problem and resolution in a California setting, correctly punctuated dialogue, sensory details, satisfying ending; 3 = all elements, minor errors; 2 = weak plot or few details; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 4: Measurement units and conversions

**Prepare:** Meter stick and tape measure, kitchen scale (grams and ounces), measuring cups, a 1-liter bottle and a 250 mL cup, a clock. Key facts: 1 m = 100 cm; 1 km = 1,000 m; 1 kg = 1,000 g; 1 lb = 16 oz; 1 L = 1,000 mL; 1 hr = 60 min; 1 min = 60 s; 1 ft = 12 in; 1 yd = 3 ft.

**Watch for:**
- Converting in the wrong direction (3 m = 3/100 cm). A bigger unit becomes MORE of the smaller unit, so multiply.
- Confusing mass and size (a big balloon weighs more than a small rock). Weigh to check.

### Day 1 (Monday): Length
*Student:* Measure the hallway in meters and centimeters and make a conversion table for 1-10 meters.

*How to teach:* Measure the hallway in meters and leftover centimeters and convert to centimeters. The child completes a table for 1-10 meters.

- **Q:** How many centimeters are in 3 meters? In 10 meters?
  **A:** 300 cm; 1,000 cm.
- **Q:** The hallway is 4 m 35 cm. How many centimeters?
  **A:** 435 cm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mass and weight
*Student:* Weigh 5 kitchen items in grams and ounces; build kg-g and lb-oz tables.

*How to teach:* Weigh 5 items and record grams and ounces. The child builds kg-g and lb-oz tables for 1-5.

- **Q:** How many grams are in 4 kilograms?
  **A:** 4,000 g.
- **Q:** How many ounces are in 3 pounds?
  **A:** 48 oz.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Liquid volume
*Student:* Fill a liter bottle with 250 ml cups and record a liter-milliliter table.

*How to teach:* Fill the liter bottle with the 250 mL cup and count (4 cups). The child makes a liter-milliliter table for 1-5 liters.

- **Q:** How many 250 mL cups fill 1 liter?
  **A:** 4 (4 × 250 = 1,000 mL).
- **Q:** How many milliliters are in 3 liters?
  **A:** 3,000 mL.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Time
*Student:* Convert hours to minutes and minutes to seconds and make a table for a soccer schedule.

*How to teach:* The child converts hours to minutes and minutes to seconds and makes a soccer schedule table (e.g., two 25-minute halves and a 10-minute break).

- **Q:** How many minutes is 2 hours?
  **A:** 120 minutes.
- **Q:** A game has two 25-minute halves and a 10-minute halftime. How long is it?
  **A:** 60 minutes = 1 hour.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on unit size and conversion tables.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently. Correct together.

- **Q:** Which is longer, 1 km or 900 m?
  **A:** 1 km, because 1 km = 1,000 m.

**Assessment:** Conversion quiz with tables.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 7 m = 700 cm 2) 4 kg = 4,000 g 3) 3 lb = 48 oz 4) 2 L = 2,000 mL 5) 5 hr = 300 min 6) 4 min = 240 s 7) 6 ft = 72 in 8) 1 km > 900 m 9) Complete the table yd → ft: 1→3, 2→6, 3→9, 4→12 10) Which unit to measure a pencil's mass: grams. Proficient: 8/10.

## Science 4: Renewable energy investigations

**Prepare:** Paper/cardstock for a pinwheel, a pencil, straw, thread, paper clip, a fan; a plastic plate, small cups, tape, a skewer for a water wheel; two identical containers (one painted black or wrapped in black paper), thermometer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking one energy source is best everywhere. Each depends on local conditions (sun, wind, rivers).
- Forgetting reliability. Solar doesn't work at night; wind varies; storage or backup is needed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pinwheel turbine
*Student:* Build a pinwheel turbine that lifts a paper clip.

*How to teach:* Build a pinwheel on a straw axle with thread wound around it; use the fan to lift a paper clip.

- **Q:** What energy conversion happens?
  **A:** Wind (motion energy of air) → turning motion of the pinwheel → lifting the paper clip.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Water wheel
*Student:* Build a water wheel from a plastic plate and cups.

*How to teach:* Build a water wheel; pour water to turn it.

- **Q:** How does a hydroelectric dam use this idea?
  **A:** Falling water turns turbines connected to generators that make electricity.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solar
*Student:* Compare the temperature of water in a black container in sun vs shade every 10 minutes for 40 minutes.

*How to teach:* Place water in the black container in sun and shade; record temperature every 10 minutes for 40 minutes.

- **Q:** What did the data show?
  **A:** The container in the sun warmed more — sunlight energy is absorbed and turned to heat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare wind, water and solar models in a chart by output, reliability and needs.

*How to teach:* Make a chart comparing wind, water and solar models by output, reliability and needs.

- **Q:** Which needs the most specific location?
  **A:** Hydroelectric — it needs a river or falling water.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an explanation of which source fits California best.

*How to teach:* The child writes which source fits California best and why.

- **Q:** Give a good reason for solar in California.
  **A:** California has many sunny days, especially in the Central Valley and deserts, so solar panels produce a lot of electricity.

**Assessment:** Energy comparison chart and explanation.

**Answer key:** Chart: proficient compares all three on output, reliability (e.g., solar only in daytime) and needs. Explanation rubric: 4 = clear choice, 2+ reasons using California conditions (sunshine, windy passes like Altamont, rivers and dams) and at least one drawback; 3 = choice with one reason; 2 = vague; 1 = no choice.

## California, A Changing State: Immigration and the Chinese Exclusion Act

**Prepare:** Calisphere sets 'Chinese Exclusion Act' and 'Diversity in the Changing State,' world map. Facts: the Chinese Exclusion Act (1882) banned Chinese laborers from immigrating — the first U.S. law to bar a specific ethnic group; it was extended and not repealed until 1943. Chinese Americans fought back in court (e.g., Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 1886; Wong Kim Ark, 1898, which confirmed citizenship for people born in the U.S.).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the act was about all immigrants. It specifically targeted Chinese workers.
- Thinking immigrants accepted unfair laws silently. Many organized, sued and won important court cases.

### Day 1 (Monday): Who came?
*Student:* Map countries of origin of California immigrants 1850-1900 (China, Mexico, Ireland, Germany, Chile, Japan).

*How to teach:* Map immigrant origins 1850-1900.

- **Q:** Name four countries California immigrants came from 1850-1900.
  **A:** China, Mexico, Ireland, Germany (also Chile, Japan, Italy).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Diverse state
*Student:* Analyze images from the Calisphere set 'Diversity in the Changing State' and list communities that formed.

*How to teach:* Analyze 'Diversity in the Changing State' images; list communities.

- **Q:** Name a community that formed in this era.
  **A:** Chinatown in San Francisco (also Japanese, Italian, Portuguese, Mexican communities).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chinese Exclusion Act
*Student:* Read about the 1882 Chinese Exclusion Act and analyze a document from the Calisphere set 'Chinese Exclusion Act'.

*How to teach:* Read about the Chinese Exclusion Act; analyze a document.

- **Q:** What did the Chinese Exclusion Act do?
  **A:** It stopped Chinese laborers from immigrating to the U.S. (from 1882) and kept Chinese immigrants from becoming citizens.
- **Q:** When was it repealed?
  **A:** 1943.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Accords
*Student:* Read about people and groups who worked together or defended immigrants' rights.

*How to teach:* Read about people who defended immigrants' rights.

- **Q:** What did the Wong Kim Ark case decide?
  **A:** A person born in the U.S. (even to Chinese parents) is a U.S. citizen (1898).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a response: was the Chinese Exclusion Act fair? Use two pieces of evidence.

*How to teach:* Write whether the act was fair, with two pieces of evidence.

- **Q:** Give one piece of evidence that it was unfair.
  **A:** It singled out one ethnic group, even though Chinese workers had built the railroad and contributed to California's economy.

**Assessment:** Evidence-based response.

**Answer key:** Response rubric: 4 = clear answer (most will argue it was unfair), two specific pieces of evidence (targeted one group; Chinese contributions to railroad/farming; families separated; repealed in 1943), conclusion; 3 = one piece of evidence; 2 = opinion only; 1 = off-topic.

## Art & Music 4: Mariachi and Mexican music in California

**Prepare:** Find a recording/video of 'El Son de la Negra' (e.g., Mariachi Vargas de Tecalitlán). Learn the instruments: violin, trumpet, vihuela (small, round-backed, 5-string strummed guitar), guitarrón (large bass guitar).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the guitarrón is just a big guitar playing chords. It plays the bass line.
- Thinking mariachi is only background music. It's central to celebrations and California's Mexican American heritage.

### Day 1 (Monday): Listen
*Student:* Listen to 'El Son de la Negra' and identify instruments: violin, trumpet, vihuela, guitarrón.

*How to teach:* Listen and identify violin, trumpet, vihuela and guitarrón. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Which mariachi instrument plays the bass line?
  **A:** The guitarrón.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Timbre
*Student:* Describe the timbre of each instrument with adjectives.

*How to teach:* Describe each instrument's timbre with adjectives. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Describe the timbre of mariachi trumpets.
  **A:** Bright, brassy, bold.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Context
*Student:* Read about mariachi at celebrations in California communities.

*How to teach:* Read about mariachi at California celebrations (weddings, quinceañeras, Día de los Muertos, Cinco de Mayo).

- **Q:** Name two celebrations with mariachi.
  **A:** Weddings, quinceañeras, birthdays, festivals, Día de los Muertos.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rhythm
*Student:* Clap the 3/4 + 6/8 alternating rhythm (sesquialtera).

*How to teach:* Clap the alternating 3/4 + 6/8 rhythm: 1-2-3-4-5-6 / 1-2-3-4-5-6 with accents ONE two three FOUR five six, then ONE two THREE four FIVE six.

- **Q:** What is sesquialtera?
  **A:** Alternating between 6/8 (two groups of three) and 3/4 (three groups of two).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write how mariachi connects to family celebrations.

*How to teach:* Write how mariachi connects to family celebrations. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Where did mariachi music originate?
  **A:** Jalisco, Mexico.

**Assessment:** Listening response.

**Answer key:** Listening response: 4 = identifies 4 instruments with timbre words, explains the rhythm and connects mariachi to celebrations; 3 = three of these; 2 = two; 1 = one.

## PE 4: Volleying

**Prepare:** Beach ball or volleyball trainer (soft), a rope tied between two chairs or posts at about head height.

**Watch for:**
- Hitting a volley with the palms. A set (overhead volley) uses the finger pads in a triangle shape above the forehead.
- Swinging the arms for a bump. Keep a flat platform with arms straight and lift with the legs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hand volley
*Student:* Volley a beach ball upward with fingertips 20 times.

*How to teach:* Overhead volley: hands above forehead, thumbs and index fingers forming a triangle, push up with finger pads and extend legs. 20 in a row.

- **Q:** Where are your hands for an overhead volley?
  **A:** Above the forehead, forming a triangle 'window' with thumbs and index fingers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Forearm pass
*Student:* Practice the bump with a platform of forearms.

*How to teach:* Forearm pass (bump): clasp hands, arms straight forming a flat platform, contact on the forearms, lift with legs.

- **Q:** Where should the ball hit in a bump?
  **A:** On the flat part of the forearms, just above the wrists.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner rally
*Student:* Rally a beach ball with a partner using sets and bumps, counting hits.

*How to teach:* Rally with a partner using sets and bumps; count hits.

- **Q:** When do you use a bump instead of a set?
  **A:** For low balls (below the chest).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Net game
*Student:* Play a cooperative over-the-net game, counting consecutive hits.

*How to teach:* Cooperative over-the-net game: count consecutive hits across the net.

- **Q:** What is cooperative play?
  **A:** Working together toward a shared goal (like a long rally) rather than against each other.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record your best cooperative rally of 3 tries and compare it to the start of the week.

*How to teach:* Record best rally of 3 tries; compare to the start of the week.

- **Q:** Monday best: 6. Friday best: 14. Improvement?
  **A:** 8 more hits.

**Assessment:** Rally count.

**Answer key:** Rally count: record the best cooperative rally of 3 tries and compare to the week's start. 4 = 15+ hits with correct set and bump form; 3 = 10–14; 2 = 5–9; 1 = fewer than 5.

## Health 4: Family rules and influences about alcohol

**Prepare:** Parent preview: think about how you want to explain your family's rules on alcohol, tobacco and medicine. Family interview sheet (5 questions). Prepare the Unit 8 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking family rules are just about control. Rules exist to keep children safe and healthy, and families can explain the reasons behind them.
- Thinking all influences are negative. Family, friends and media can also be positive influences toward healthy choices.

### Day 1 (Monday): Family interview
*Student:* Interview a parent about family rules on alcohol, tobacco and medicine and why they exist.

*How to teach:* Interview a parent about family rules on alcohol, tobacco and medicine and why each exists. The child records answers.

- **Q:** Give a good interview question.
  **A:** E.g., 'What is our family rule about medicine, and why?' or 'What would you want me to do if someone offered me alcohol?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Influences
*Student:* List positive and negative influences from family, friends and media.

*How to teach:* Make a T-chart of positive and negative influences from family, friends and media.

- **Q:** Give one positive and one negative media influence.
  **A:** Positive: an anti-vaping PSA or a show where a character says no. Negative: an ad or movie showing drinking as glamorous with no consequences.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trusted adults
*Student:* Review trusted adults you can talk to about alcohol or drug concerns.

*How to teach:* Review the trusted adults hand from week 3. Practice how to start a conversation about alcohol or drug concerns.

- **Q:** How could you start a talk with a trusted adult about a worry?
  **A:** E.g., 'Can I talk to you about something that's bothering me?' then explain what happened.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family agreement
*Student:* Write a family agreement about staying alcohol- and drug-free.

*How to teach:* Write a family agreement about staying alcohol- and drug-free, including the child's promises and the parents' support (e.g., calling for a ride with no questions asked at that moment).

- **Q:** What should a family agreement include?
  **A:** Clear promises from the child, how parents will support them, and a plan for difficult situations.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on alcohol's effects, social norms and family influences.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test (see key).

- **Q:** What kind of drug is alcohol?
  **A:** A depressant.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test and family agreement.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test key: 1) Alcohol is a — depressant. 2) Legal drinking age — 21. 3) Two short-term effects — slowed reactions, poor balance, poor judgment, slurred speech, blurred vision. 4) Organ that breaks down alcohol — liver. 5) A long-term effect — liver disease (cirrhosis), heart disease, memory problems, addiction. 6) Brain part for balance — cerebellum. 7) Why alcohol is especially harmful to young people — brain still developing until about 25. 8) True or false: most young people drink — false. 9) What to do if a driver has been drinking — don't ride; call a parent/trusted adult. 10) One positive influence — e.g., family rules, friends who don't drink, PSAs. Proficient = 8 of 10. Family agreement: complete, signed by child and parent.
