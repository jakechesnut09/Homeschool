# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 34

## ELA 4: Presenting research

**Prepare:** Slides software or poster board, a timer, a recording device. Prepare the Unit 8 test with two short texts about the same event (one firsthand, one secondhand) — key below.

**Watch for:**
- Reading slides word for word. Slides show key facts and pictures; the speaker explains.
- Answering audience questions with guesses. Say 'I'm not sure, but my notes say...' or look it up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan presentation
*Student:* Turn the report into 5 slides or a poster, using one key fact and one visual per section.

*How to teach:* The child turns the report into 5 slides or a poster: one key fact and visual per section.

- **Q:** How much text belongs on a slide?
  **A:** A title and a few key words or one fact — not paragraphs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the 3-minute talk with a timer, practicing eye contact, volume and pace.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a 3-minute talk with a timer, practicing eye contact, volume and pace.

- **Q:** What should you do if you finish early?
  **A:** Add an example or a fact from your notes; aim for about 3 minutes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Audio
*Student:* Record a 30-second narrated introduction or add a nature sound clip that fits the wonder.

*How to teach:* Record a 30-second narrated intro or add a fitting nature sound clip.

- **Q:** Why add audio?
  **A:** To engage listeners and help them imagine the place.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present to family, then answer at least three audience questions using facts from the notes.

*How to teach:* Present to family and answer at least three questions with facts from notes.

- **Q:** How do you answer a question well?
  **A:** Listen fully, repeat the question, answer with a specific fact, check if it answered them.

### Day 5 (Friday): Assess
*Student:* Take the Unit 8 test on firsthand/secondhand accounts and integrating information from two texts.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** What does it mean to integrate information from two texts?
  **A:** Combine facts from both to explain a topic more completely than either text alone.

**Assessment:** Presentation and Unit 8 test.

**Answer key:** Presentation rubric: 4 = organized 3-minute talk, slides/poster with key facts and visuals, eye contact, clear volume and pace, accurate answers to 3 questions; 3 = minor issues; 2 = reads notes or very short; 1 = incomplete. Unit 8 test key: 1) Identify each text as firsthand or secondhand 2) Focus of the firsthand text: personal experience and feelings 3) Focus of the secondhand: facts, dates, overview 4) One piece of information only in text A, one only in text B 5) Write a paragraph integrating both (proficient: uses information from each with transition words). Proficient: 80%.

## Math 4: Classifying shapes and symmetry

**Prepare:** Paper shapes (12 triangles: right, acute, obtuse; and squares, rectangles, rhombuses, parallelograms, trapezoids), a small mirror, scissors, printed capital letters. Unit 9 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a square isn't a rectangle. A rectangle has 4 right angles; a square has 4 right angles plus 4 equal sides, so every square is a rectangle.
- Calling a diagonal of a rectangle a line of symmetry. Fold to test: the halves must match exactly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Triangles
*Student:* Sort 12 triangles into right, acute and obtuse and recognize right triangles.

*How to teach:* Use the index card corner to check each triangle's angles; sort into right (one 90°), obtuse (one more than 90°) and acute (all less than 90°).

- **Q:** A triangle has angles 30°, 60°, 90°. What type is it?
  **A:** Right triangle.
- **Q:** Can a triangle have two right angles?
  **A:** No — two 90° angles would already be 180°, leaving nothing for the third angle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Quadrilaterals
*Student:* Sort quadrilaterals by parallel and perpendicular sides into a hierarchy chart.

*How to teach:* Sort quadrilaterals by parallel sides and right angles into a hierarchy chart: quadrilateral → trapezoid/parallelogram → rectangle, rhombus → square.

- **Q:** Which quadrilaterals have two pairs of parallel sides?
  **A:** Parallelograms, including rectangles, rhombuses and squares.
- **Q:** Is every square a rhombus?
  **A:** Yes — it has 4 equal sides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Symmetry
*Student:* Fold paper shapes and letters to find lines of symmetry and check with a mirror.

*How to teach:* Fold shapes and letters to find lines where halves match; check with the mirror.

- **Q:** How many lines of symmetry does a square have? A rectangle (non-square)?
  **A:** 4; 2.
- **Q:** How many lines of symmetry in the letters A and H?
  **A:** A has 1 (vertical); H has 2 (vertical and horizontal).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 9 review
*Student:* Shape riddle review game covering angles and classification.

*How to teach:* Play shape riddles: you give clues, the child names the shape.

- **Q:** I have 4 equal sides and no right angles. What am I?
  **A:** A rhombus.
- **Q:** I have exactly one pair of parallel sides.
  **A:** A trapezoid.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 9 test on lines, angles, protractor use, shape classification and symmetry.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test independently. Correct together.

- **Q:** How many lines of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have?
  **A:** 3.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test key: 1) Ray: one endpoint 2) Perpendicular lines form right angles 3) 65° = acute, 90° = right, 115° = obtuse 4) Measure/draw 140° (±2°) 5) x + 55° = 90°: x = 35° 6) Triangle with angles 100°, 40°, 40°: obtuse 7) A square is a rectangle and a rhombus 8) A trapezoid has exactly one pair of parallel sides (by the definition used in this course) 9) Lines of symmetry: square 4, rectangle 2, equilateral triangle 3 10) Letter with no line of symmetry: e.g., F, G, J, L, N, P, R, S, Z. Proficient: 8/10.

## Science 4: Building and testing the prototype

**Prepare:** Cups, straws, skewers, cardstock, string, tape, 10 pennies, box fan, meter stick, stopwatch, phone camera.

**Watch for:**
- Changing the fan distance or speed between trials. Keep conditions identical for a fair test.
- Hiding failures. Failures show what to improve; record them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build day 1
*Student:* Build the base and axle of the lifter following the chosen sketch.

*How to teach:* Build the base and axle from the chosen sketch.

- **Q:** Why must the axle turn freely?
  **A:** Friction wastes energy; a smooth axle lets more wind energy go into lifting.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build day 2
*Student:* Attach blades, string and the penny cup and do a first trial run.

*How to teach:* Attach blades, string and penny cup; first trial run.

- **Q:** What happened on the first run?
  **A:** Record actual results; common issues: string slips off axle, blades hit the base.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a fair test
*Student:* Write a test plan keeping fan distance, fan speed and load the same for every trial.

*How to teach:* Write the test plan: fan distance, fan speed and load constant.

- **Q:** Name the variables kept the same.
  **A:** Fan distance, fan speed setting, number of pennies, string length.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Run trials
*Student:* Run 5 trials, recording lift height and time in a data table.

*How to teach:* Run 5 trials; record lift height and time.

- **Q:** How do you find the average time?
  **A:** Add the 5 times and divide by 5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Failure points
*Student:* Identify where the device fails (blades slip, string tangles, axle wobbles) and photograph or sketch each.

*How to teach:* Identify and sketch/photograph failure points.

- **Q:** Give an example of a failure point and fix.
  **A:** Axle wobbles → add a second support or tape a straw bearing; blades slip → glue blades to a cork hub.

**Assessment:** Test plan, data table and failure-point analysis.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = test plan lists controlled variables; data table with 5 trials (height, time, average); at least two failure points identified with evidence and possible fixes; 3 = data complete, analysis weak; 2 = fewer than 5 trials; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: Constitutions: U.S. and California

**Prepare:** U.S. Constitution (DocsTeach or archives.gov), California Constitution preamble ('We, the People of the State of California, grateful to Almighty God for our freedom, in order to secure and perpetuate its blessings, do establish this Constitution.'). Article I of the California Constitution is the Declaration of Rights.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the state constitution is the same as the U.S. Constitution. States have their own constitutions; they can't conflict with the U.S. Constitution.
- Mixing up branches. Legislative makes laws; executive carries them out; judicial interprets them.

### Day 1 (Monday): U.S. Constitution
*Student:* Read the Preamble and identify the six goals of the U.S. government.

*How to teach:* Read the Preamble; identify six goals.

- **Q:** List the six goals in the Preamble.
  **A:** Form a more perfect union, establish justice, insure domestic tranquility, provide for the common defense, promote the general welfare, secure the blessings of liberty.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Three branches
*Student:* Make a chart of the legislative, executive and judicial branches.

*How to teach:* Chart the three branches.

- **Q:** Name the branches and their jobs.
  **A:** Legislative (Congress) makes laws; executive (President) carries out laws; judicial (Supreme Court and courts) interprets laws.
- **Q:** Why have three branches?
  **A:** Separation of powers and checks and balances so no branch gets too much power.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): California Constitution
*Student:* Read the California Constitution's preamble and Declaration of Rights excerpts.

*How to teach:* Read the California preamble and Declaration of Rights.

- **Q:** What is the California Declaration of Rights?
  **A:** Article I of the California Constitution, listing people's rights (like free speech, privacy and equal protection).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Complete a Venn diagram comparing federal and state governments.

*How to teach:* Venn diagram comparing federal and state governments.

- **Q:** How are they alike?
  **A:** Both have constitutions and three branches (legislature, executive, courts).
- **Q:** How are they different?
  **A:** Federal: President, Congress, U.S. Supreme Court, national issues (defense, money). State: Governor, Legislature (Assembly and Senate), California Supreme Court, state issues (schools, highways).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz on the U.S. and California constitutions and the three branches of government.

*How to teach:* Give the constitution quiz.

- **Q:** What branch is the President in?
  **A:** Executive.

**Assessment:** Constitution quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 'We the People' — government's power comes from the people 2) Six Preamble goals 3) Legislative = makes laws (Congress; CA Legislature) 4) Executive = carries out laws (President; Governor) 5) Judicial = interprets laws (U.S. Supreme Court; CA Supreme Court) 6) Checks and balances 7) California Declaration of Rights = Article I 8) State constitution cannot conflict with the U.S. Constitution. Proficient: 7/8.

## Art & Music 4: Preparing the exhibition

**Prepare:** Gather cardstock for mats, painter's tape, index cards; choose a wall with even light away from direct sun.

**Watch for:**
- Hanging art too high. Center work at about eye level (around 57–60 inches).
- Writing long labels. Keep labels short and informative.

### Day 1 (Monday): Venues compared
*Student:* Compare an art museum, a commercial gallery and a community art walk in a chart: purpose, who visits, what experience they give.

*How to teach:* Compare an art museum, commercial gallery and community art walk in a chart. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** How is a commercial gallery different from a museum?
  **A:** A gallery sells art; a museum collects, preserves and displays art for education.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mat and label
*Student:* Mount the 6 artworks on cardstock mats and write museum-style labels (title, artist, medium, year, one-sentence description).

*How to teach:* Mat 6 artworks; write museum-style labels. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What goes on a museum label?
  **A:** Title, artist, medium, year and a short description.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Protecting art
*Student:* Decide how to protect each piece where it will hang (away from sunlight, sculpture on a stable shelf) and explain why.

*How to teach:* Decide how to protect each piece. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why keep a sculpture on a stable shelf?
  **A:** So it won't fall and break.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Layout and install
*Student:* Sketch a floor plan of the exhibit wall, then hang the work at eye level with even spacing.

*How to teach:* Sketch a floor plan; hang work at eye level with even spacing. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Why use even spacing?
  **A:** It looks organized and gives each piece attention.

### Day 5 (Friday): Walkthrough
*Student:* Lead a family member on a guided tour and revise any label that was unclear.

*How to teach:* Lead a family tour; revise unclear labels. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What did you revise after the tour?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name a specific label change.

**Assessment:** Installed exhibition and venue comparison.

**Answer key:** Installed exhibition: matted, labeled, evenly spaced at eye level, protected. Venue chart: purpose, visitors and experience for all three venues correctly compared.

## PE 4: Fitness post-assessment

**Prepare:** Same setup as week 2: PACER lane and audio (or mile route), mat, measuring tools, cadence recording, baseline record sheet. Graph paper.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing scores with friends. Compare to your own baseline and the Healthy Fitness Zone.
- Thinking a lower time is worse. For the mile, a lower time is better; for laps and reps, higher is better.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm-up & PACER
*Student:* Lead your dynamic warm-up and complete the PACER (or 1-mile run) and record the result.

*How to teach:* The child leads the dynamic warm-up, then completes the PACER (or mile) and records it.

- **Q:** Baseline PACER 18 laps; today 26. Improvement?
  **A:** 8 laps more.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Curl-ups & push-ups
*Student:* Complete the cadence curl-up and 90-degree push-up tests and record results.

*How to teach:* Retest cadence curl-ups and 90-degree push-ups.

- **Q:** Why use the same cadence as the pre-test?
  **A:** So the comparison is fair.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trunk lift & sit-and-reach
*Student:* Complete the trunk lift and sit-and-reach tests.

*How to teach:* Retest trunk lift and sit-and-reach.

- **Q:** Is a trunk lift of 14 inches recorded as 14?
  **A:** No — the maximum recorded is 12 inches.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Graph pre- and post-test results side by side and check which goals you met.

*How to teach:* Make a double bar graph of pre- and post-test results; mark which SMART goals were met.

- **Q:** Baseline mile 11:20; post 10:05. How much faster?
  **A:** 1 minute 15 seconds faster.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining what training helped most.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph explaining what training helped most, with evidence.

- **Q:** Give a sentence linking training to results.
  **A:** E.g., 'Doing the circuit three times a week helped my push-ups go from 6 to 12.'

**Assessment:** Post-assessment results and comparison graph.

**Answer key:** Post-assessment results and graph: proficient work includes all 5 test results recorded (PACER/mile, curl-ups, push-ups, trunk lift, sit-and-reach), correct differences from baseline, a labeled double bar graph, goals marked met/not met, and a paragraph linking specific training to results. 4 = all; 3 = one missing element; 2 = several missing; 1 = incomplete.

## Health 4: Planning a health campaign

**Prepare:** Planning sheet (topic, audience, key message, 5 facts with sources, 2 products, timeline). Bookmark reliable sources: KidsHealth, MyPlate.gov, CDC, FDA 'The Real Cost,' NIDA for Teens.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any fact found online can be used. Facts must come from reliable sources and be written in your own words with the source named.
- Trying to reach everyone with one message. Choose a specific audience and shape the message for them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose topic
*Student:* Choose a topic: drink water, eat the rainbow, be active, or stay vape-free.

*How to teach:* Choose a topic (drink water, eat the rainbow, be active, stay vape-free) and explain why it matters to the child.

- **Q:** Why choose a topic you care about?
  **A:** You'll be more motivated and your message will be more convincing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Audience
*Student:* Identify the audience (family, neighbors, younger kids) and what message will reach them.

*How to teach:* Identify the audience and what message and format will reach them (younger kids: pictures and songs; neighbors: flyers).

- **Q:** How would your message change for younger kids vs. adults?
  **A:** Younger kids: simple words, pictures, fun; adults: facts, statistics and practical tips.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Facts
*Student:* Gather 5 facts from reliable sources for the campaign.

*How to teach:* Gather 5 facts from reliable sources and record each source.

- **Q:** Is a fact from a company selling sports drinks a good source for a water campaign?
  **A:** No — it has a reason to be biased; use CDC, KidsHealth or MyPlate instead.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan products
*Student:* Plan 2 campaign products (poster, short video, flyer, skit).

*How to teach:* Plan 2 products (poster, short video, flyer, skit) and sketch each.

- **Q:** Why plan 2 different products?
  **A:** Different formats reach different people and repeat the message.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Get feedback from a parent on the campaign plan.

*How to teach:* Present the plan to a parent for feedback and revise.

- **Q:** What feedback question could you ask?
  **A:** E.g., 'Is my message clear?' 'Would this convince you?'

**Assessment:** Campaign plan.

**Answer key:** Campaign plan rubric: 4 = clear topic and specific audience, a one-sentence key message, 5 accurate facts each with a reliable source, 2 planned products with sketches, and revisions from parent feedback; 3 = 4 facts or one missing element; 2 = vague audience or unreliable sources; 1 = incomplete.
