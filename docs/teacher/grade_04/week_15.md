# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 15

## ELA 4: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 15-22

**Prepare:** Novel (chapters 15-22). List 10 context-clue words from chapters 18-19.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up theme and topic ('friendship'). Write a full sentence: 'Friendship can grow even between enemies.'
- Guessing word meaning without rereading the sentence before and after.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rontu
*Student:* Read chapters 15-17 and analyze how Karana's relationship with Rontu reveals her values.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 15-17. Discuss how Karana's choice to spare and care for the wounded leader dog shows her values.

- **Q:** Why doesn't Karana kill the wounded leader dog?
  **A:** She feels pity once she sees him hurt and helpless; she cares for him, names him Rontu ('Fox Eyes'), and he becomes her loyal companion.
- **Q:** What does this show about her?
  **A:** She is compassionate and lonely; she values companionship over revenge.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Context clues
*Student:* Use context to define 10 words from chapters 18-19.

*How to teach:* Model using context clues. The child defines 10 words and checks with a dictionary.

- **Q:** What is a strategy for context clues?
  **A:** Reread the sentences around the word, look for a definition, example, synonym or contrast, then test your meaning in the sentence.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tutok
*Student:* Read chapters 18-20 and discuss friendship across conflict.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 18-20 and discuss Karana's friendship with Tutok.

- **Q:** Who is Tutok, and why is the friendship surprising?
  **A:** An Aleut girl who comes with the hunters; Karana's people fought the Aleuts, yet the two become friends.
- **Q:** How does Karana show trust in Tutok?
  **A:** She tells Tutok her secret name, Won-a-pa-lei, and they exchange gifts (Tutok gives her a necklace of black stones).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Theme
*Student:* Read chapters 21-22 and draft a theme statement with supporting details.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 21-22. The child drafts a theme statement with two supporting details.

- **Q:** Write a theme statement for the novel so far.
  **A:** Example: 'Forgiveness and kindness can replace anger and loneliness' — Karana spares Rontu and befriends Tutok.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer comprehension questions and share the theme statement aloud.

*How to teach:* The child answers comprehension questions and shares the theme statement aloud.

- **Q:** What does Karana learn from Tutok's leaving?
  **A:** She feels how much she has missed friendship; loneliness returns after Tutok sails away.

**Assessment:** Theme statement and questions.

**Answer key:** Theme statement rubric: 4 = a complete sentence that applies beyond the book, supported by two accurate details (e.g., Rontu, Tutok) and explained; 3 = theme with one detail; 2 = topic word only; 1 = summary. Questions key: Rontu = the wild dog leader Karana spares; Tutok = Aleut girl who becomes her friend; secret name Won-a-pa-lei; Karana's values: compassion, friendship. Proficient: 80%.

## Math 4: Four-digit dividends

**Prepare:** Grid paper. Prepare a missing-digit puzzle sheet and 3 worked problems with errors for Day 4 (samples below).

**Watch for:**
- Leaving out a zero in the quotient (1,824 ÷ 6 written as 34). Estimate first: 1,800 ÷ 6 = 300, so the answer must be in the hundreds.
- Stopping partial quotients too early. Keep going until what's left is less than the divisor.

### Day 1 (Monday): Four-digit partial quotients
*Student:* Solve 6 problems like 3,624 / 6 using partial quotients.

*How to teach:* Model 3,624 ÷ 6 using big chunks first (6 × 600 = 3,600, then 6 × 4 = 24). The child solves 6 problems.

- **Q:** 3,624 ÷ 6 = ?
  **A:** 604.
- **Q:** 5,215 ÷ 5 = ?
  **A:** 1,043.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Zeros in quotients
*Student:* Solve 6 problems like 1,824 / 6 where a quotient digit is zero and explain why.

*How to teach:* Solve 1,824 ÷ 6 and estimate first (about 300). Discuss why there are 0 tens in the quotient (after taking 1,800 only 24 is left, which is less than 6 tens). The child solves 6 problems.

- **Q:** 1,824 ÷ 6 = ?
  **A:** 304.
- **Q:** Why is there a zero in the quotient of 2,436 ÷ 4?
  **A:** After 4 × 600 = 2,400, only 36 is left; 36 is not enough for 4 groups of 10, so the tens digit is 0. Answer 609.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Which strategy?
*Student:* Solve the same 3 problems with area model and partial quotients and decide which is more efficient for each.

*How to teach:* The child solves the same 3 problems with an area model and with partial quotients, then writes which was faster for each and why.

- **Q:** 7,248 ÷ 8 = ?
  **A:** 906.
- **Q:** 4,875 ÷ 3 = ?
  **A:** 1,625.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Division puzzles
*Student:* Complete a 'missing digit' division puzzle sheet and an error analysis of 3 worked problems.

*How to teach:* Work the puzzle sheet together for the first item, then independently. For error analysis, the child finds and fixes each mistake.

- **Q:** A student wrote 2,436 ÷ 4 = 69. What's wrong?
  **A:** They dropped the zero; 69 × 4 = 276, not 2,436. Correct answer 609.
- **Q:** Fill the blank: ___ ÷ 7 = 908 R1.
  **A:** 6,357 (908 × 7 = 6,356, + 1).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz with 8 four-digit by one-digit division problems, including zeros in the quotient.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz with 8 problems; require an estimate before each. Correct together.

- **Q:** 9,000 ÷ 9 = ?
  **A:** 1,000.

**Assessment:** Four-digit division quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 3,624 ÷ 6 = 604 2) 1,824 ÷ 6 = 304 3) 4,875 ÷ 3 = 1,625 4) 2,436 ÷ 4 = 609 5) 7,248 ÷ 8 = 906 6) 5,215 ÷ 5 = 1,043 7) 6,357 ÷ 7 = 908 R1 8) 9,000 ÷ 9 = 1,000. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: Light energy and color

**Prepare:** Glass of water or prism, sunny window, 2 thermometers, black and white paper, a pizza box, foil, plastic wrap, black paper, chocolate or s'mores ingredients. Unit 4 test for Day 5. Safety: never look at the sun.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking white light is one color. It contains all the colors of the rainbow.
- Thinking black things are 'cold.' Black absorbs more light, which turns into heat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rainbow
*Student:* Make a rainbow with a glass of water and sunlight.

*How to teach:* Place a glass of water on a sunny windowsill and find the rainbow on the floor or paper.

- **Q:** Where do the rainbow colors come from?
  **A:** White sunlight is made of all colors; the water bends (refracts) each color a different amount, separating them.
- **Q:** Name the colors in order.
  **A:** Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Color heat
*Student:* Place thermometers under black and white paper in the sun and record temperature.

*How to teach:* Place thermometers under black and white paper in the sun; read every 5 minutes for 20 minutes.

- **Q:** Which thermometer got warmer? Why?
  **A:** Under the black paper — black absorbs more light energy, which turns into heat; white reflects more.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Solar energy
*Student:* Build a simple solar oven from a pizza box and foil.

*How to teach:* Build the solar oven: foil flap to reflect sunlight, black paper inside to absorb, plastic wrap window to trap heat.

- **Q:** What does each part of the oven do?
  **A:** Foil reflects sunlight in; black paper absorbs it and heats up; plastic wrap traps warm air.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test oven
*Student:* Melt chocolate or s'mores in the solar oven and record temperature.

*How to teach:* Put chocolate in the oven in full sun; record temperature and melt time.

- **Q:** What energy change happens in the solar oven?
  **A:** Light energy changes into heat energy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test on light and sight.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** How does light allow us to see?
  **A:** Light reflects off objects into our eyes.

**Assessment:** Solar oven data and Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test key: 1) Light travels in straight lines 2) Mirrors reflect light 3) We see when light reflects off objects into our eyes 4) Pupil shrinks in bright light 5) Black absorbs more light → warmer 6) White light contains all colors 7) Solar oven: foil reflects, black absorbs, plastic traps heat 8) Draw a seeing model with arrows source → object → eye. Proficient: 80%. Solar oven data: proficient includes temperature readings over time and a conclusion.

## California, A Changing State: Missions from many perspectives

**Prepare:** Short accounts from a Franciscan, a soldier and a Native leader; a reading on Toypurina (Tongva woman who in 1785 helped plan a revolt at Mission San Gabriel). Unit 4 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking there was one 'true' view of the missions. Different groups experienced them differently; historians use multiple sources.
- Treating Native people only as victims. Many resisted — revolts, running away, keeping traditions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Perspectives
*Student:* Read short accounts from a Franciscan, a soldier and a Native leader (e.g., Toypurina).

*How to teach:* Read three perspective accounts.

- **Q:** How might a Franciscan priest view the missions differently from a Native leader?
  **A:** The priest saw them as saving souls and 'civilizing'; a Native leader saw loss of land, freedom and culture.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Toypurina
*Student:* Read about Toypurina's 1785 resistance at Mission San Gabriel.

*How to teach:* Read about Toypurina.

- **Q:** Who was Toypurina and what did she do?
  **A:** A Tongva spiritual leader who in 1785 helped organize a revolt against Mission San Gabriel; it was stopped, and she was put on trial and exiled.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Legacy
*Student:* Discuss the legacy of the missions today, including place names, architecture and Native descendants' perspectives.

*How to teach:* Discuss the legacy of missions today.

- **Q:** Name a legacy of the missions.
  **A:** Place names (San Diego, Santa Barbara), Spanish-style architecture, mission buildings as museums, and painful history for Native descendants.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Essay
*Student:* Write an informative essay on how the missions affected different groups.

*How to teach:* The child writes an informative essay on how missions affected different groups.

- **Q:** What groups should the essay cover?
  **A:** Native Californians, Spanish missionaries and soldiers/settlers.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test on Spanish colonization, mission placement, daily life and economic change.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** Which mission is the 'first'?
  **A:** San Diego de Alcalá (1769).

**Assessment:** Essay and Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test key: 1) Spain's reasons for colonizing 2) 1769 Portolá and Serra at San Diego 3) 21 missions along El Camino Real 4) Placement near water, soil, villages 5) Mission daily life ruled by bells 6) Economic change to farming and ranching 7) Disease reduced Native populations 8) Presidio and pueblo purposes 9) Toypurina's 1785 resistance. Essay rubric: 4 = introduction, paragraphs on 2-3 groups with accurate facts, conclusion; 3 = two groups; 2 = one group; 1 = incomplete. Proficient: 80%.

## Art & Music 4: Expressive qualities: dynamics and tempo

**Prepare:** Recorder, a metronome app, and a recording of Grieg's 'In the Hall of the Mountain King.' Vocabulary: piano (p) = soft, forte (f) = loud, crescendo = gradually louder; largo = very slow, moderato = medium, allegro = fast.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking faster means louder. Tempo (speed) and dynamics (volume) are separate.
- Playing piano by blowing weakly so the note goes flat. On recorder, soft dynamics are limited; play gently but keep steady air.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dynamics
*Student:* Play 'Clementine' piano and then forte, and add a crescendo to one phrase.

*How to teach:* Play 'Clementine' piano, then forte; add a crescendo to one phrase. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What does forte mean?
  **A:** Loud.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tempo
*Student:* Play 'Ode to Joy' at largo, moderato and allegro tempos with a metronome app.

*How to teach:* Play 'Ode to Joy' at largo (~50 bpm), moderato (~100) and allegro (~130). Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Which tempo is fastest: largo, moderato or allegro?
  **A:** Allegro.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening map
*Student:* Listen to 'In the Hall of the Mountain King' and draw a listening map of its growing tempo and dynamics.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Mountain King'; draw a listening map that grows bigger and closer together. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How do tempo and dynamics change in 'In the Hall of the Mountain King'?
  **A:** It starts slow and soft, then gets faster and louder (accelerando and crescendo) to a wild ending.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpretation plan
*Student:* Mark dynamics and tempo on a folk song's music to match the story's mood.

*How to teach:* Mark dynamics and tempo on a folk song's music to match its mood. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** What tempo and dynamics fit a sad verse of 'Clementine'?
  **A:** Slower tempo and softer dynamics (piano).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the folk song with your planned expression and explain each choice.

*How to teach:* Perform with planned expression and explain choices. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What is a crescendo?
  **A:** Gradually getting louder.

**Assessment:** Expressive performance with explanation.

**Answer key:** Expressive performance: 4 = clearly different dynamics and tempo that match the mood, each choice explained; 3 = choices performed but not all explained; 2 = little change; 1 = none.

## PE 4: Jump rope fitness show

**Prepare:** Jump rope, a music track with a steady beat (about 1 minute, previewed), a Tabata timer (20 s on / 10 s off × 8 rounds), stopwatch for the 1-minute speed test, long rope and turners.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Tabata means jumping as fast as possible for 4 minutes straight. It is 8 rounds of 20 seconds hard work with 10 seconds of rest.
- Choosing tricks you can't do yet for the show. Choose tricks you can do reliably and practice the transitions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tabata jump
*Student:* Do a 4-minute Tabata (20 seconds jumping, 10 seconds rest) and record heart rate after.

*How to teach:* Do a Tabata: 8 rounds of 20 seconds jumping and 10 seconds rest (4 minutes). Take heart rate right after.

- **Q:** How many rounds are in a Tabata, and how long is it?
  **A:** 8 rounds of 20 s work + 10 s rest = 4 minutes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choreograph
*Student:* Choose a song and choreograph a 1-minute routine with at least 5 tricks matched to the beat.

*How to teach:* Choose a song and plan a 1-minute routine with at least 5 tricks matched to the beat (write counts for each).

- **Q:** If each trick lasts 8 counts and your song is at 120 beats per minute, how many counts fit in 1 minute?
  **A:** 120 counts, so about 15 tricks of 8 counts.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the routine 5 times, fixing any spot where you miss.

*How to teach:* Rehearse 5 times; mark where misses happen and practice that spot slowly.

- **Q:** What's a good way to fix a trouble spot?
  **A:** Practice just that transition slowly several times, then add it back into the full routine.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the routine for family as a 'Jump Rope Show'.

*How to teach:* Perform the 'Jump Rope Show' for family.

- **Q:** How do you recover from a miss during a show?
  **A:** Reset the rope quickly and rejoin on the next beat without stopping the music.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Complete the jump rope skills test: 1-minute speed count, 3 tricks and long-rope entry.

*How to teach:* Skills test: 1-minute speed count (count right-foot landings ×2, or all jumps), 3 tricks, long-rope entry.

- **Q:** In a 1-minute speed test, what form keeps speed high?
  **A:** Small, quick jumps on the balls of the feet and fast wrist turns.

**Assessment:** Jump rope skills test.

**Answer key:** Jump rope skills test: 1-minute speed count recorded (compare to week 12 practice); 3 tricks performed 5 in a row each (1 point each); long-rope entry and 5 jumps (1 point). 4 = speed recorded + 4 points; 3 = 3 points; 2 = 2 points; 1 = 0–1.

## Health 4: Food safety

**Prepare:** Glitter (or lotion with cinnamon) to model germs, soap, an instant-read food thermometer, a refrigerator thermometer. Safe cooking temperatures (USDA): poultry 165°F; ground beef and pork 160°F; whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb 145°F with a 3-minute rest; fish 145°F; leftovers reheat to 165°F. Refrigerator 40°F or below; freezer 0°F.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you can tell if meat is done by color. Use a food thermometer; color isn't reliable.
- Leaving leftovers out to cool before refrigerating. Refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour if it's above 90°F).

### Day 1 (Monday): Clean
*Student:* Use glitter on hands to model germ spread, then practice 20-second handwashing.

*How to teach:* Put glitter on the child's hands, have them touch objects, and see how 'germs' spread. Practice 20-second handwashing: wet, lather (backs, between fingers, under nails), scrub 20 seconds, rinse, dry.

- **Q:** When should you wash your hands in the kitchen?
  **A:** Before cooking and eating, after touching raw meat, eggs or poultry, after using the bathroom, and after touching trash or pets.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Separate
*Student:* Learn why raw meat stays separate from produce and sort grocery items for safe storage.

*How to teach:* Explain cross-contamination. Use separate cutting boards for raw meat and produce; store raw meat on the bottom shelf in a sealed container. Sort grocery items for safe storage.

- **Q:** Why store raw meat on the bottom shelf?
  **A:** So its juices can't drip onto ready-to-eat foods and spread bacteria.
- **Q:** What is cross-contamination?
  **A:** When germs spread from one food or surface (like raw chicken) to another food that won't be cooked.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cook
*Student:* Learn safe cooking temperatures and check one with a food thermometer.

*How to teach:* Learn safe internal temperatures. With an adult, insert a thermometer into the thickest part of cooked food and read it.

- **Q:** What is the safe temperature for chicken?
  **A:** 165°F.
- **Q:** Where do you put the thermometer?
  **A:** In the thickest part of the food, not touching bone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chill
*Student:* Check the refrigerator temperature (40°F or below) and list leftovers rules.

*How to teach:* Check the refrigerator temperature (40°F or below). Learn leftovers rules: refrigerate within 2 hours; eat within 3–4 days; reheat to 165°F.

- **Q:** What temperature should a refrigerator be?
  **A:** 40°F or below.
- **Q:** How long can leftovers stay at room temperature?
  **A:** No more than 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a Clean-Separate-Cook-Chill poster for the kitchen.

*How to teach:* Make a Clean–Separate–Cook–Chill poster with one rule and picture for each.

- **Q:** Name the 4 food safety steps.
  **A:** Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill.

**Assessment:** Food safety poster.

**Answer key:** Food safety poster rubric: 4 = all 4 steps with an accurate rule each — Clean (wash hands 20 seconds and surfaces), Separate (raw meat away from ready-to-eat foods, separate boards), Cook (use a thermometer; chicken 165°F, ground beef 160°F), Chill (refrigerate within 2 hours; fridge 40°F or below) — clear pictures; 3 = all 4 steps, one rule vague; 2 = 2–3 steps; 1 = incomplete.
