# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 13

## ELA 4: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 1-6

**Prepare:** Island of the Blue Dolphins (Scott O'Dell); preview chapters 1-6, which include a battle in which many island men, including Karana's father, are killed. Print a map of the Channel Islands (San Nicolas is the farthest out, about 60 miles off the coast).

**Watch for:**
- Making inferences without evidence. An inference = clue from the text + what I know.
- Breaking words into syllables randomly. Split between double consonants, before consonant-le, and keep vowel teams together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Setting
*Student:* Read chapters 1-2 and map San Nicolas Island; describe how the setting shapes the people's lives.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1-2 together. Locate San Nicolas Island on the map and discuss how the island setting (sea, little wood, otters) shapes life.

- **Q:** Who narrates the novel and what island does she live on?
  **A:** Karana, a girl from the village of Ghalas-at, on the Island of the Blue Dolphins (San Nicolas Island) off California.
- **Q:** Why have the Aleuts come to the island?
  **A:** With Captain Orlov, to hunt sea otters for their pelts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Inferences
*Student:* Read chapters 3-4 and write 3 inferences about Captain Orlov using text evidence.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 3-4. The child writes 3 inferences about Captain Orlov, each with a clue from the text.

- **Q:** What can you infer about Captain Orlov? Give evidence.
  **A:** He is not trustworthy — he offers a deal for the otters, but Karana's father (Chief Chowig) is suspicious, and Orlov later tries to sail away without paying the agreed share.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conflict
*Student:* Read chapters 5-6 and discuss the battle and departure with a parent.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5-6 and discuss the battle and its effects with your child.

- **Q:** Why does the fight with the Aleuts happen?
  **A:** Orlov tries to leave without paying the agreed price; Chowig confronts him and a battle breaks out.
- **Q:** What are the effects of the battle on Karana's people?
  **A:** Many men, including her father Chowig, are killed; the village has few men left, and women must take on new work.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word study
*Student:* Practice syllabication of 15 multisyllabic words from the novel.

*How to teach:* Teach syllable rules. The child divides 15 multisyllabic words from the novel.

- **Q:** Divide 'dolphin' and 'abalone' into syllables.
  **A:** dol-phin; ab-a-lo-ne.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer 6 text-dependent questions on chapters 1-6.

*How to teach:* The child answers 6 text-dependent questions on chapters 1-6 in complete sentences with evidence.

- **Q:** Why does Karana's father being careless with his secret name matter?
  **A:** Her people believed using one's secret name with a stranger weakened its power; Chowig told Orlov his real name, which seemed to foreshadow trouble.

**Assessment:** Comprehension questions on chapters 1-6.

**Answer key:** Key (6 questions): 1) Narrator: Karana. 2) Setting: the Island of the Blue Dolphins (San Nicolas), off the California coast. 3) Aleuts came with Captain Orlov to hunt sea otters. 4) Inference about Orlov: dishonest/greedy — tries to leave without paying fairly. 5) Battle effect: many men, including Chief Chowig, die. 6) Change in village life: women take on men's work; a new chief (Kimki) eventually leaves to find a new homeland. Each answer must include a detail from the text. Proficient: 5/6.

## Math 4: Division concepts and mental division

**Prepare:** 30 counters, base-ten blocks. Write the 4 remainder stories for Day 3 and 6 estimation problems for Day 4.

**Watch for:**
- Ignoring the context when there is a remainder (answering '4 R5 vans'). Always reread the question: do we need another van (round up), drop the leftovers, or is the leftover the answer?
- A remainder bigger than the divisor (23 ÷ 4 = 4 R7). If the remainder is as big as the divisor, another group can be made.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sharing and grouping
*Student:* Model 23 / 4 with counters both as sharing and as grouping and record the remainder.

*How to teach:* Model 23 ÷ 4 two ways: share 23 counters into 4 piles (how many in each?) and make groups of 4 (how many groups?). Record 23 ÷ 4 = 5 R3 for both and discuss what the 3 means each time.

- **Q:** 23 ÷ 4 = ?
  **A:** 5 remainder 3.
- **Q:** Share 17 counters among 5 plates. How many on each, and how many left?
  **A:** 3 on each plate, 2 left over (17 ÷ 5 = 3 R2).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dividing multiples of 10
*Student:* Use basic facts to solve 240 / 6, 2,400 / 6 and 10 similar problems.

*How to teach:* Use the fact 24 ÷ 6 = 4 to reason that 24 tens ÷ 6 = 4 tens. The child solves 12 problems saying the 'basic fact plus place value' aloud.

- **Q:** 240 ÷ 6 and 2,400 ÷ 6.
  **A:** 40 and 400.
- **Q:** 3,500 ÷ 7 = ?
  **A:** 500 (35 hundreds ÷ 7 = 5 hundreds).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interpreting remainders
*Student:* Solve 4 problems where the remainder is dropped, rounded up, or is the answer (e.g., vans needed for 29 kids).

*How to teach:* Solve four stories with the child deciding what to do with the remainder each time and writing a sentence answer.

- **Q:** 29 kids go on a trip; each van holds 6. How many vans?
  **A:** 29 ÷ 6 = 4 R5, so 5 vans (round up so everyone rides).
- **Q:** 50 cookies go in boxes of 8. How many full boxes?
  **A:** 6 full boxes (50 ÷ 8 = 6 R2; the 2 extra are dropped).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Estimating quotients
*Student:* Use compatible numbers to estimate 6 quotients like 418 / 7.

*How to teach:* Model choosing a nearby number that divides easily (418 ÷ 7: 420 is 7 × 60). The child estimates 6 quotients and explains the compatible number chosen.

- **Q:** Estimate 418 ÷ 7.
  **A:** About 60, because 420 ÷ 7 = 60.
- **Q:** Estimate 356 ÷ 6.
  **A:** About 60 (360 ÷ 6).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mental division and remainders.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently. For remainder stories, require a sentence telling what was done with the remainder.

- **Q:** 59 ÷ 9 = ?
  **A:** 6 R5.

**Assessment:** Division concepts quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 35 ÷ 8 = 4 R3 2) 3,600 ÷ 9 = 400 3) 480 ÷ 6 = 80 4) 27 kids, 4 per canoe: 7 canoes (round up) 5) 46 in of ribbon, 6 in per bow: 7 bows (drop the 4 in) 6) Estimate 356 ÷ 6 ≈ 360 ÷ 6 = 60 7) 2,800 ÷ 7 = 400 8) 59 ÷ 9 = 6 R5. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: How light travels

**Prepare:** Flashlight, 3 index cards with a hole punched in the same spot, clay to stand them, 2 small mirrors, a milk/juice carton, tape, foil, white and black paper. Safety: never shine light or point mirrors at eyes.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking light travels around corners on its own. It travels in straight lines until it hits something and reflects.
- Thinking only mirrors reflect light. All objects we see reflect some light; mirrors and shiny surfaces reflect it in one direction.

### Day 1 (Monday): Straight lines
*Student:* Line up index cards with holes and shine a flashlight through to show light travels straight.

*How to teach:* Stand cards in clay in a line so holes align; shine light through. Then move the middle card.

- **Q:** Why does the light stop reaching the wall when one card is moved?
  **A:** Light travels in straight lines; when the holes don't line up, the card blocks it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mirror reflection
*Student:* Use mirrors to bounce a flashlight beam to a target.

*How to teach:* Use a mirror to bounce the flashlight beam to a target on the wall.

- **Q:** What happens to light when it hits a mirror?
  **A:** It reflects (bounces off) at the same angle it hit.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Periscope
*Student:* Build a periscope from a milk carton and two mirrors.

*How to teach:* Build a periscope: mirrors at 45° at top and bottom of the carton, openings facing opposite ways.

- **Q:** How does a periscope let you see over a wall?
  **A:** Light from the object reflects off the top mirror down to the bottom mirror and then into your eye.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Absorb vs reflect
*Student:* Compare how light reflects from foil, white paper and black paper.

*How to teach:* Shine light on foil, white paper and black paper; see how much bounces onto a nearby card.

- **Q:** Which reflected the most light? The least?
  **A:** Foil (and white paper) reflected the most; black paper absorbed most of the light.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw light ray diagrams with arrows for the index card, mirror and periscope setups.

*How to teach:* The child draws ray diagrams with arrows for each setup.

- **Q:** In a ray diagram, what do arrows show?
  **A:** The direction the light is traveling.

**Assessment:** Light ray diagrams.

**Answer key:** Ray diagram rubric: 4 = straight-line arrows from the source through aligned holes; arrows reflecting off the mirror; periscope path source → top mirror → bottom mirror → eye; 3 = one error; 2 = arrows missing or curved; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: Life in the missions

**Prepare:** Calisphere sets 'California Missions' and 'Native Americans and Contact.' Preview: readings describe forced labor, punishment and disease; the Native population fell sharply during the mission period.

**Watch for:**
- Presenting mission life only through the missionaries' view. Include Native experiences and resistance.
- Thinking disease was caused by bad behavior. European diseases like measles and smallpox were new to Native people, who had no immunity.

### Day 1 (Monday): Daily schedule
*Student:* Read about a typical mission day (bells, prayer, work, meals) and make an hour-by-hour schedule.

*How to teach:* Make an hour-by-hour schedule of a typical mission day.

- **Q:** What signaled the times of day at a mission?
  **A:** Bells (for prayer, meals and work).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Native experience
*Student:* Read accounts of Native life at missions, including forced labor and disease.

*How to teach:* Read accounts of Native life at missions.

- **Q:** Why did many Native people die at missions?
  **A:** Diseases like measles and smallpox spread quickly in crowded conditions; poor food and hard labor also weakened people.
- **Q:** Could neophytes (baptized Native people) leave freely?
  **A:** No — they were usually not allowed to leave without permission and were sometimes brought back by soldiers and punished.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Economic change
*Student:* Explain how missions changed California from hunter-gatherer to agriculture and ranching.

*How to teach:* Explain economic change to agriculture and ranching.

- **Q:** How did the missions change California's economy?
  **A:** They introduced cattle, sheep, wheat, grapes and orchards, shifting from hunting and gathering to farming and ranching.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Primary source analysis
*Student:* Analyze an image from the Calisphere set 'Native Americans and Contact' using See-Think-Wonder.

*How to teach:* Analyze an image with See-Think-Wonder.

- **Q:** What are the three steps of See-Think-Wonder?
  **A:** See: describe what is shown. Think: what it might mean. Wonder: questions it raises.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph about how the missions changed Native lives.

*How to teach:* Write a paragraph about how missions changed Native lives.

- **Q:** Name two changes.
  **A:** Loss of land and freedom; new religion, language and work; disease and population decline; loss of traditional food sources.

**Assessment:** Paragraph on change.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = topic sentence plus at least three accurate changes (loss of land/freedom, forced labor, disease, new religion/language, farming) with a detail and a concluding sentence; 3 = two changes; 2 = one change; 1 = inaccurate.

## Art & Music 4: Melody: steps, skips and leaps

**Prepare:** Recorder and fingering chart; staff paper; recordings of 'Twinkle, Twinkle,' 'Ode to Joy' and 'Somewhere Over the Rainbow.' Low E = thumb + left 1, 2, 3 + right 1, 2; low D = thumb + left 1, 2, 3 + right 1, 2, 3 (only the bottom hole open). For the D-to-D scale you'll also need F-sharp — find it on your fingering chart (it differs slightly between baroque and German recorders).

**Watch for:**
- Squeaks on low notes from blowing too hard. Low notes need slow, gentle 'warm' air.
- Thinking a skip is any jump. A step moves to the very next note; a skip jumps over one note; a leap is bigger than a skip.

### Day 1 (Monday): Melodic contour
*Student:* Listen to 3 melodies ('Twinkle', 'Ode to Joy', 'Somewhere Over the Rainbow') and draw each melody's up-and-down shape.

*How to teach:* Play the three melodies; your child draws each melody's up-and-down shape as a line. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Which song begins with a big leap up?
  **A:** 'Somewhere Over the Rainbow' — 'Some-where' leaps up an octave.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Low E and D
*Student:* Learn low E and D fingerings, covering holes fully, and play long tones on each.

*How to teach:* Teach low E and low D; play long tones (4 beats each), checking every hole is sealed. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Why do low notes squeak more easily?
  **A:** Any tiny leak or too much breath causes squeaks; low notes need all holes sealed and gentle air.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Steps and skips
*Student:* Circle steps, skips and leaps in the written melody of 'Ode to Joy' and then play it.

*How to teach:* Circle steps, skips and leaps in 'Ode to Joy' notation; then play it. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** 'Ode to Joy' starts B B C D D C B A. Are these steps, skips or leaps?
  **A:** Mostly steps (plus repeated notes).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Note naming
*Student:* Name 20 notes from low D to high D on a treble staff worksheet.

*How to teach:* Name 20 notes from low D to high D on a worksheet. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Name the treble staff lines from bottom to top.
  **A:** E, G, B, D, F.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Play a D-to-D scale up and down and sight-read a 4-measure stepwise melody.

*How to teach:* Play the D-to-D scale up and down; sight-read a 4-measure stepwise melody. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** What is melodic contour?
  **A:** The shape of a melody as it goes up, down or stays the same.

**Assessment:** Scale and sight-reading check.

**Answer key:** Scale check: D–E–F#–G–A–B–C–D up and down with correct fingerings and steady tone (4 = no errors; 3 = 1–2; 2 = several; 1 = cannot). Sight-reading: correct notes and rhythm in a 4-measure stepwise melody (proficient = 3 of 4 measures correct).

## PE 4: Long-rope jumping

**Prepare:** Long rope about 16 feet, two turners (or tie one end to a post at about waist height). Practice in a clear area. Look up the traditional chant 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear' (public domain).

**Watch for:**
- Running in at the wrong moment. Front door: the rope comes over the top toward you and hits the ground — run in right after it hits. Back door: the rope comes up toward you and goes over away — wait until it passes over and hits the ground, then follow it in.
- Jumping near the turner. Jump in the middle where the rope is highest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Turning
*Student:* Practice turning a long rope with a partner, making full circles that tap the ground on each beat.

*How to teach:* Teach turning: hold the handle at waist level, turn big circles from the shoulder so the rope just taps the ground at the bottom on each beat, staying in rhythm with the other turner.

- **Q:** What makes a good turn?
  **A:** Big, steady circles that tap the ground every time, in rhythm with the other turner.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Front door
*Student:* Enter the 'front door' as the rope comes toward you, jump 5 times and run out the far side.

*How to teach:* Front door entry: stand next to a turner; as the rope comes over toward you and hits the ground, run in to the middle, jump 5 times, and exit out the far side right after a jump.

- **Q:** Where do you exit the rope?
  **A:** Out the far side, diagonally, right after the rope passes under your feet.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Back door
*Student:* Enter through the 'back door' (rope moving away) by waiting for it to hit the ground and following it in.

*How to teach:* Back door entry: the rope comes up toward you and over away. Wait for it to pass over and hit the ground, then follow it in. Practice 10 times.

- **Q:** Why is back door harder?
  **A:** The rope is coming up toward your face, so you must wait for it to go over and down before running in.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chants
*Student:* Jump to traditional rope chants like 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear' while doing the actions.

*How to teach:* Jump to 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear' doing the actions (turn around, touch the ground).

- **Q:** How do you turn around while jumping?
  **A:** Turn a quarter turn on each jump so you don't break rhythm.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a front-door entry, 10 jumps with a turn-around, and a clean exit.

*How to teach:* Perform: front door entry, 10 jumps including a turn-around, clean exit.

- **Q:** Name 2 safety rules for long rope.
  **A:** Jump in the middle; turners hold handles firmly and don't let go; wait your turn outside the rope's path.

**Assessment:** Long-rope entry/exit check.

**Answer key:** Long-rope entry/exit check: front door entry without stopping the rope (1), 10 consecutive jumps (1), turn-around while jumping (1), clean exit (1). 4 = all 4; 3 = 3; 2 = 2; 1 = 0–1. Bonus: back door entry.

## Health 4: Planning a balanced meal

**Prepare:** MyPlate template, 3 sample dinner menus (e.g., spaghetti with garlic bread; chicken nuggets and fries; tacos with beans, lettuce, cheese and salsa), a local grocery flyer or online store prices, calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a meal must be expensive to be healthy. Beans, eggs, rice, oats, frozen vegetables and seasonal produce are healthy and affordable.
- Forgetting fruit, vegetables or dairy in a meal plan. Check the plan against all 5 groups.

### Day 1 (Monday): Meal analysis
*Student:* Analyze 3 sample dinners and decide which food groups are missing.

*How to teach:* Analyze 3 sample dinners: list the food groups present and missing in each.

- **Q:** Chicken nuggets and fries: which groups are missing?
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables (fries are mostly starch and fried) and dairy; it's also low in whole grains.
- **Q:** Spaghetti with meat sauce and garlic bread: what is missing?
  **A:** Fruit and dairy (and more vegetables would help).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan a dinner
*Student:* Plan a family dinner that includes all 5 food groups.

*How to teach:* Plan a family dinner with all 5 food groups (e.g., bean and chicken tacos on corn tortillas, lettuce and tomato, cheese, a side of mango, milk).

- **Q:** Check your dinner: name a food for each of the 5 groups.
  **A:** The child names one food for each of fruits, vegetables, grains, protein and dairy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Budget
*Student:* Use a grocery flyer to price the ingredients and stay within a $20 budget.

*How to teach:* Price each ingredient from the flyer and total the cost. Stay within $20.

- **Q:** Chicken $6.50, tortillas $2.25, beans $1.20, lettuce $1.80, cheese $3.50, mangoes $2.00. Total? Under $20?
  **A:** $17.25; yes, $2.75 under $20.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Snack plan
*Student:* Plan two healthy snacks for the week, each combining two food groups (apple with peanut butter, carrots with hummus).

*How to teach:* Plan two healthy snacks for the week, each combining two food groups.

- **Q:** Why combine two food groups in a snack?
  **A:** It adds more nutrients and staying power, e.g., fiber plus protein keeps you full longer.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the menu to family and explain how it follows MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Present the menu and budget to family and explain how it follows MyPlate.

- **Q:** How does your plan follow MyPlate?
  **A:** It includes all 5 groups with half the plate fruits and vegetables and whole grains when possible.

**Assessment:** Meal plan with budget.

**Answer key:** Meal plan with budget rubric: 4 = dinner includes all 5 food groups with half the plate fruits/vegetables, ingredients priced correctly with an accurate total at or under $20, two snacks each with 2 food groups, and a clear MyPlate explanation; 3 = one small error; 2 = missing a food group or total over budget; 1 = incomplete.
