# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 23

## ELA 4: Drafting and revising the opinion essay

**Prepare:** Outline, computer, opinion rubric (4 levels: opinion, reasons, evidence, organization/linking words, conclusion, conventions).

**Watch for:**
- Restating the hook in the conclusion word for word. Restate the opinion in new words and add a call to action.
- Using the same linking word repeatedly. Vary: for instance, in addition, because, therefore, also.

### Day 1 (Monday): Introduction
*Student:* Draft an introduction with a hook and clear opinion statement.

*How to teach:* The child drafts an introduction with a hook (question, surprising fact) and an opinion statement.

- **Q:** Write a hook for the plastic bag essay.
  **A:** Example: 'Did you know a plastic bag can take hundreds of years to break down?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body
*Student:* Draft three body paragraphs, each with a reason, two pieces of evidence and a linking phrase (for instance, in addition, because).

*How to teach:* The child drafts three body paragraphs: reason, two pieces of evidence, linking phrase.

- **Q:** Name three linking phrases for opinions.
  **A:** For instance, in addition, because (also: therefore, as a result).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conclusion
*Student:* Draft a conclusion that restates the opinion and calls readers to action.

*How to teach:* The child drafts a conclusion that restates the opinion and calls readers to action.

- **Q:** What is a call to action?
  **A:** Telling readers what to do, e.g., 'Bring a reusable bag every time you shop.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Revise for precise words and varied sentences.

*How to teach:* Revise for precise words and varied sentence beginnings and lengths.

- **Q:** Make this sentence more precise: 'Bags are bad for animals.'
  **A:** Example: 'Sea turtles mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish and can choke.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Peer conference
*Student:* Conference with a parent using the opinion rubric and plan edits.

*How to teach:* Conference with the rubric; the child sets 2-3 edit goals.

- **Q:** Which rubric row is your strongest? Weakest?
  **A:** Child names rows with evidence from the draft.

**Assessment:** Complete revised draft.

**Answer key:** Complete revised draft (4-point): 4 = hook and clear opinion, 3 body paragraphs each with a reason and 2 relevant pieces of evidence, varied linking words, conclusion with call to action, evidence of revision; 3 = all parts, one weak paragraph; 2 = missing a paragraph or evidence; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 4: Adding and subtracting mixed numbers

**Prepare:** Fraction strips (several wholes). Write 8 addition and 8 subtraction mixed-number problems plus trail/rainfall problems.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving an improper part in the answer (3 6/4). Regroup: 6/4 = 1 2/4, so 3 6/4 = 4 2/4.
- Subtracting the smaller fraction from the larger when the top fraction is too small (3 1/4 - 1 3/4 = 2 2/4). Rename 1 whole: 3 1/4 = 2 5/4.

### Day 1 (Monday): Adding mixed numbers
*Student:* Add 2 3/8 + 1 2/8 by adding wholes and parts, then with renamed fractions (8 problems).

*How to teach:* Model 2 3/8 + 1 2/8: add wholes (3), add parts (5/8). Then show the same by renaming as 19/8 + 10/8 = 29/8 = 3 5/8. The child solves 8 problems.

- **Q:** 2 3/8 + 1 2/8 = ?
  **A:** 3 5/8.
- **Q:** 4 1/5 + 2 3/5 = ?
  **A:** 6 4/5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Regrouping in sums
*Student:* Add mixed numbers where parts exceed 1 whole and regroup.

*How to teach:* Model 1 3/4 + 2 3/4 = 3 6/4 and regroup to 4 2/4. The child solves problems and regroups each answer.

- **Q:** 1 3/4 + 2 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 3 6/4 = 4 2/4 (= 4 1/2).
- **Q:** 2 5/6 + 1 4/6 = ?
  **A:** 3 9/6 = 4 3/6 (= 4 1/2).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Subtracting mixed numbers
*Student:* Subtract mixed numbers, including renaming 1 whole as 4/4 (8 problems).

*How to teach:* Model 3 1/4 - 1 3/4 by trading one whole for 4/4 (2 5/4 - 1 3/4 = 1 2/4). The child solves 8 problems.

- **Q:** 3 1/4 - 1 3/4 = ?
  **A:** 1 2/4 (= 1 1/2).
- **Q:** 5 - 2 1/3 = ?
  **A:** 2 2/3 (rename 5 as 4 3/3).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Measurement context
*Student:* Solve trail-length and rainfall problems using mixed numbers.

*How to teach:* The child solves trail and rainfall problems, writing units in each answer.

- **Q:** A trail has two parts: 3 5/8 miles and 2 7/8 miles. How long is it?
  **A:** 6 4/8 miles (= 6 1/2 miles).
- **Q:** It rained 3 7/10 inches in January and 2 4/10 inches in February. How much more in January?
  **A:** 1 3/10 inches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on mixed number operations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently. Correct together.

- **Q:** 4 2/6 - 1 5/6 = ?
  **A:** 2 3/6 (= 2 1/2).

**Assessment:** Mixed number quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 2 3/8 + 1 2/8 = 3 5/8 2) 1 3/4 + 2 3/4 = 4 2/4 3) 3 1/4 - 1 3/4 = 1 2/4 4) 5 - 2 1/3 = 2 2/3 5) 4 2/6 - 1 5/6 = 2 3/6 6) 6 3/10 + 2 9/10 = 9 2/10 7) Trail 3 5/8 + 2 7/8 = 6 4/8 miles 8) 7 1/5 - 3 4/5 = 3 2/5. Equivalent simplified forms (e.g., 4 1/2 for 4 2/4) accepted. Proficient: 7/8.

## Science 4: California landscapes over time

**Prepare:** Photos of Yosemite Valley (U-shaped valley, Half Dome, El Capitan), coastal cliffs at Pacifica and Big Sur, a sea arch (e.g., Natural Bridges State Beach in Santa Cruz). Sugar cubes, a tray, water. Unit 6 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a river alone carved Yosemite Valley. The Merced River started it, but glaciers widened and deepened it into a U shape.
- Thinking coastal erosion happens evenly. Weaker rock erodes faster, forming caves, arches and stacks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Yosemite
*Student:* Read about how glaciers carved Yosemite Valley.

*How to teach:* Read about Yosemite's glaciers.

- **Q:** What shape does a glacier carve a valley into?
  **A:** A U shape (rivers usually carve V shapes).
- **Q:** What evidence of glaciers can be seen in Yosemite?
  **A:** U-shaped valley, polished granite, hanging valleys with waterfalls (like Bridalveil Fall), and large boulders moved by ice (erratics).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Coastal erosion
*Student:* Analyze photos of eroding cliffs at Pacifica and Big Sur and list the agents causing the erosion.

*How to teach:* Look at Pacifica and Big Sur photos; list agents of erosion.

- **Q:** What causes coastal cliffs at Pacifica to erode?
  **A:** Waves pounding the base, storms, rain and runoff, and landslides/gravity; houses near the edge are at risk.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sea caves model
*Student:* Model sea arch formation with sugar cubes and water.

*How to teach:* Line sugar cubes as a headland; squirt water at a weak spot to make a cave, then an arch.

- **Q:** How does a sea arch form?
  **A:** Waves erode a cave into a weak part of a headland; when the cave cuts all the way through, it becomes an arch. When the top collapses, a sea stack remains.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sketch
*Student:* Create a before/after landscape sketch with explanation.

*How to teach:* The child sketches a before/after landscape with an explanation.

- **Q:** What should the explanation include?
  **A:** Which agent (water, wind, ice, gravity) changed the land and about how long it took.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on weathering, erosion and rock layers.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test.

- **Q:** Weathering or erosion: a river carrying sand to the ocean.
  **A:** Erosion (and deposition at the ocean).

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test key: 1) Weathering breaks down; erosion moves 2) Physical example: ice wedging; chemical: acid dissolving limestone 3) Steeper slope → more erosion 4) Wind forms dunes 5) Glaciers carve U-shaped valleys (Yosemite) 6) Bottom rock layer is oldest 7) Marine fossils on land show it was once under water 8) Sea arches form from wave erosion of caves 9) Deposition forms deltas/beaches 10) Before/after sketch explained. Proficient: 80%.

## California, A Changing State: Gold Rush lives and communities

**Prepare:** Calisphere sets 'African Americans in the Gold Rush' and 'Asian Americans: Gold Rush Era to 1890s'; excerpt of Louise Clappe's 'Shirley Letters' (1851-52, written from Rich Bar on the Feather River). Facts: Biddy Mason was brought to California enslaved, won her freedom in a Los Angeles court in 1856, became a nurse/midwife and landowner, and helped found the First AME Church of Los Angeles. California's 1850 Foreign Miners' Tax charged $20 a month (later a $3/month tax targeted Chinese miners). Preview: violence against Native Californians during this era was severe.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all miners were white Americans. Miners came from Mexico, Chile, China, Europe, Hawaii, and included African Americans and Native Californians.
- Thinking the Foreign Miners' Tax was fair because it applied to 'foreigners.' It was enforced mainly against Mexican, Chilean and Chinese miners to push them out.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dame Shirley
*Student:* Read an excerpt of Louise Clappe's 'Shirley Letters' from Rich Bar and list details of camp life.

*How to teach:* Read Dame Shirley's excerpt; list camp-life details.

- **Q:** Why are the Shirley Letters a valuable primary source?
  **A:** Louise Clappe lived in a mining camp and described daily life, people and events as they happened.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Biddy Mason
*Student:* Read about Biddy Mason, who won her freedom in a Los Angeles court in 1856 and became a landowner and philanthropist.

*How to teach:* Read about Biddy Mason.

- **Q:** How did Biddy Mason gain her freedom?
  **A:** She petitioned a Los Angeles court in 1856, which ruled that she and her family were free because California was a free state.
- **Q:** What did she do with her freedom?
  **A:** Worked as a nurse and midwife, bought land, became wealthy, and helped found the First AME Church and helped people in need.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diverse miners
*Student:* Analyze images from the Calisphere sets 'African Americans in the Gold Rush' and 'Asian Americans: Gold Rush Era to 1890s'; note who came and from where.

*How to teach:* Analyze images; note who came and from where.

- **Q:** Name three places Gold Rush miners came from.
  **A:** China, Mexico, Chile (also Peru, Ireland, Germany, France, Australia, the eastern U.S.).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conflict
*Student:* Read about the Foreign Miners' Tax and violence against Native Californians, Chinese and Mexican miners; discuss fairness.

*How to teach:* Read about the Foreign Miners' Tax and violence; discuss fairness.

- **Q:** Why was the Foreign Miners' Tax unfair?
  **A:** It was aimed at non-white and foreign miners to drive them from the gold fields, and was enforced unequally.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining who profited most from the Gold Rush (miners or merchants like Levi Strauss) with evidence.

*How to teach:* Write who profited most from the Gold Rush with evidence.

- **Q:** Give evidence that merchants profited.
  **A:** Prices were very high (eggs and tools cost many times normal); merchants like Levi Strauss (dry goods, later jeans) and Sam Brannan grew rich selling supplies.

**Assessment:** Evidence paragraph on Gold Rush profits.

**Answer key:** Paragraph rubric: 4 = clear claim (usually merchants), two or more pieces of specific evidence (high prices, Levi Strauss, Sam Brannan; most miners found little), counterpoint acknowledged, conclusion; 3 = claim and one piece of evidence; 2 = opinion without evidence; 1 = off-topic.

## Art & Music 4: Composing in ABA form

**Prepare:** Staff paper, Song Maker, recorder, and the week 22 melody (A section). Prepare a 3-question feedback form: What did you like? Is B different enough? What could be clearer?

**Watch for:**
- Making B too similar to A. Change tempo, dynamics, rhythm or pitch range.
- Forgetting to return to A exactly. ABA means A returns.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan contrast
*Student:* Plan an ABA piece: what will make B different (tempo, dynamics, pitch range)?

*How to teach:* Plan contrast for B (tempo, dynamics, pitch range). Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Name two ways to make B contrast with A.
  **A:** Change tempo, dynamics, rhythm or move to higher/lower notes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write B
*Student:* Compose an 8-measure B section that contrasts with the A section from week 22.

*How to teach:* Compose an 8-measure contrasting B section. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is contrast in music?
  **A:** A noticeable difference between sections.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feedback
*Student:* Play the draft for a parent and gather feedback with a 3-question form.

*How to teach:* Play the draft for a parent; gather feedback. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why ask for feedback?
  **A:** Listeners notice things the composer may miss.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Revise two measures based on feedback and record the changes.

*How to teach:* Revise two measures; record changes. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What did you revise and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; should cite feedback.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a short description of the piece's form and mood.

*How to teach:* Write a description of form and mood. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Describe ABA form in your own words.
  **A:** A section, different B section, A section returns.

**Assessment:** Revised ABA composition.

**Answer key:** Revised ABA composition: 4 = clear A, contrasting B, A returns, two revised measures documented; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = little contrast; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 4: Small-sided basketball games

**Prepare:** Basketball, hoop or basket, cones for stations, scoring sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking any hand switch is a double dribble. Double dribble is dribbling with both hands at once or starting to dribble again after stopping (holding) the ball.
- Thinking a foul means any touch. A foul is illegal contact that affects play, like pushing, holding or hitting arms.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rules
*Student:* Learn rules for traveling, double dribble and fouls and demonstrate each.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate traveling (taking steps while holding the ball without dribbling), double dribble (two hands at once, or dribble-stop-dribble) and common fouls (pushing, holding, reaching in and hitting arms).

- **Q:** What is traveling?
  **A:** Moving your feet (taking extra steps) while holding the ball without dribbling.
- **Q:** What is a double dribble?
  **A:** Dribbling with both hands at the same time, or dribbling, stopping (holding the ball) and then dribbling again.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Half-court game
*Student:* Play a 2-on-2 half-court basketball game to 7 points using the rules learned.

*How to teach:* Play 2-on-2 half-court to 7 points using the rules. After a defensive rebound or steal, 'clear' the ball past the 3-point line (or a cone) before scoring.

- **Q:** Why 'clear' the ball in half-court?
  **A:** It gives both teams a fair reset after the ball changes possession.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Conflict scenarios
*Student:* Act out 3 disputed calls and resolve each with a fair strategy.

*How to teach:* Act out 3 disputed calls; resolve each fairly (honest call, do-over, rock-paper-scissors).

- **Q:** You're not sure if you traveled. What's a fair way to settle it?
  **A:** Be honest; if unsure, agree on a do-over or use rock-paper-scissors.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Skills stations
*Student:* Rotate through dribble, pass and shoot stations.

*How to teach:* Rotate through dribble, pass and shoot stations.

- **Q:** Which pass is best to get the ball under a defender's raised arms?
  **A:** A bounce pass.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Complete the hand dribble and passing skills test.

*How to teach:* Skills test (see key).

- **Q:** What does a defender try to stay between?
  **A:** The offensive player and the basket.

**Assessment:** Basketball skills test.

**Answer key:** Basketball skills test: Hand dribble — complete the cone course with both hands and a crossover (score 1–4: 4 = no losses, eyes up). Passing — 5 chest, 5 bounce, 5 overhead passes to target hands (proficient = 12+ of 15). Rules quiz: traveling, double dribble, foul defined correctly (3 points).

## Health 4: Household poisons and inhalants

**Prepare:** Parent preview: with an adult, look at household product labels (cleaners, bleach, bug spray, aerosol cans) WITHOUT opening them. Lock up chemicals. Write the Poison Control number 1-800-222-1222 near the phone. Prepare the Unit 6 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'natural' or common products are harmless. Cleaners, bleach, button batteries, essential oils and even vitamins can poison children.
- Thinking sniffing markers, glue or sprays is harmless fun. Inhaling fumes can damage the brain, heart and lungs and can cause death even the first time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Poison hunt
*Student:* With an adult, find 10 household products with warning labels and note signal words (Caution, Warning, Danger).

*How to teach:* With an adult, find 10 products with warning labels. Record the signal word. Explain: CAUTION = least harmful, WARNING = more harmful, DANGER = most harmful (POISON with skull and crossbones = highly toxic).

- **Q:** Which signal word means the most dangerous?
  **A:** DANGER (especially DANGER–POISON).
- **Q:** Put in order from least to most harmful: Danger, Caution, Warning.
  **A:** Caution, Warning, Danger.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Inhalants
*Student:* Learn why sniffing fumes from products like markers, glue or aerosols harms the brain and can be deadly.

*How to teach:* Explain inhalants: chemicals in some products (glue, aerosols, permanent markers, gasoline) make fumes that are poisonous to breathe on purpose. Sniffing can cause brain damage, heart problems and sudden death ('sudden sniffing death').

- **Q:** Why is sniffing fumes from products dangerous?
  **A:** The chemicals go straight to the brain and can damage it, make the heart beat irregularly, and can cause death even the first time.
- **Q:** Why use markers and glue only in a well-ventilated room?
  **A:** Fresh air keeps fumes from building up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Safe storage
*Student:* Help organize cleaning products out of reach and away from food.

*How to teach:* With an adult, help organize cleaning products in a high or locked cabinet, away from food, in original containers.

- **Q:** Why keep products in their original containers?
  **A:** So the label and warnings stay with them and no one mistakes them for food or drink.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poison Control
*Student:* Learn the Poison Control number (1-800-222-1222) and role-play calling it.

*How to teach:* Learn the Poison Control number. Role-play a call: say what was swallowed/touched, how much, the person's age and weight, and follow directions. Call 911 if the person is unconscious, having seizures or trouble breathing.

- **Q:** What number do you call for a possible poisoning?
  **A:** Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222 (or 911 if the person can't breathe, is unconscious or has a seizure).
- **Q:** What information should you have ready?
  **A:** The product container, what and how much was swallowed or touched, when it happened, and the person's age and weight.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 6 test on medicines and household safety.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test (see key).

- **Q:** What should you do if you find a cleaning product open on the floor?
  **A:** Don't touch or smell it; keep younger kids away and tell an adult.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test key: 1) Prescription vs. OTC — prescription needs a doctor's order for one person; OTC can be bought without one. 2) Where to find dose — Directions section of the label. 3) What is a side effect — an unwanted effect of a medicine. 4) Should you share prescriptions — no. 5) Signal words from least to most harmful — Caution, Warning, Danger. 6) Poison Control number — 1-800-222-1222. 7) Why are inhalants dangerous — fumes damage the brain/heart and can cause death. 8) Where to store cleaners — high or locked, away from food, in original containers. 9) If you find a pill — don't touch it, tell an adult. 10) When to call 911 instead of Poison Control — unconscious, not breathing, seizure. Proficient = 8 of 10.
