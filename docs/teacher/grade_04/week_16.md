# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 16

## ELA 4: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 23-29

**Prepare:** Novel (chapters 23-29). Prepare a novel comprehension test (key below). Preview: Rontu dies in this section.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a summary should include every event. Select only the most important events: beginning, key turning points, ending.
- Reading fast instead of expressively. Fluency = accuracy, phrasing and expression that matches meaning.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 23-25
*Student:* Read and track how Karana's attitude toward animals changes.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 23-25. Track how Karana's attitude toward animals changes.

- **Q:** How does Karana's attitude toward animals change?
  **A:** She decides not to kill animals anymore (except for food from the sea she needs) — she tames birds and an otter and sees animals as friends.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 26-29
*Student:* Finish the novel and discuss whether the ending is happy, sad or both.

*How to teach:* Finish the novel and discuss whether the ending is happy, sad or both.

- **Q:** How does Karana leave the island?
  **A:** After many years alone, a ship comes and its crew take her to the mainland; she leaves with her dog Rontu-Aru.
- **Q:** Is the ending happy, sad or both? Why?
  **A:** Both — she is rescued and will be among people, but she learns the ship that took her people had sunk and she leaves her island home.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Summary
*Student:* Write a one-paragraph summary of the whole novel.

*How to teach:* The child writes a one-paragraph summary of the whole novel.

- **Q:** What must the summary include?
  **A:** Who Karana is, why she was left alone, how she survives and changes, and how she finally leaves.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fluency
*Student:* Rehearse and record a 2-minute expressive reading of a favorite passage.

*How to teach:* The child rehearses a favorite passage 3 times and records a 2-minute expressive reading.

- **Q:** What makes a reading expressive?
  **A:** Changing voice for feelings and dialogue, pausing at punctuation, and reading at a natural pace.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take the novel comprehension test.

*How to teach:* Give the novel comprehension test.

- **Q:** Who are Rontu and Rontu-Aru?
  **A:** Rontu is the dog Karana tamed; Rontu-Aru ('son of Rontu') is the young dog she captures after Rontu dies.

**Assessment:** Novel comprehension test.

**Answer key:** Novel test key: 1) Narrator: Karana 2) Setting: Island of the Blue Dolphins (San Nicolas), off California, 1800s 3) Why she is alone: jumped from the ship to stay with Ramo, who was then killed by wild dogs 4) Law she breaks: women making weapons 5) Rontu: wild dog leader she spares and tames 6) Tutok: Aleut girl who becomes her friend 7) Change: from anger and revenge to compassion — she stops killing animals 8) Ending: rescued after many years, goes to the mainland with Rontu-Aru; learns her people's ship sank 9) Theme (accept supported): courage and self-reliance; forgiveness; harmony with nature. Proficient: 7/9 with evidence.

## Math 4: Multistep problems with all four operations

**Prepare:** Bar model templates. Prepare 4 multistep problems, the packing problems and the Yosemite camping task card: 7 people, 3 days; 1 food pack per person per meal, 3 meals a day; food packs come in boxes of 4 and cost $7 each; tents sleep 2; campsite costs $35 a night for 3 nights. Unit 4 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Using the wrong operation order in multistep problems. Write the plan in steps and an equation like (6 × 48) ÷ 8 = s.
- Answering with a remainder when the question asks 'how many full bags' or 'how many boxes needed.' Reread the question before writing the answer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the steps
*Student:* Solve 4 multistep problems by first writing a plan and an equation with a letter for the unknown.

*How to teach:* Model writing a plan ('First... then...') and an equation with a letter. The child solves 4 problems the same way.

- **Q:** 4 boxes of 36 pencils are shared equally among 9 students. How many each? Write an equation.
  **A:** (4 × 36) ÷ 9 = p; 144 ÷ 9 = 16 pencils each.
- **Q:** Sara saved $15 a week for 8 weeks and spent $47. How much is left?
  **A:** 8 × 15 = 120; 120 - 47 = $73.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Remainders in context
*Student:* Solve 'packing boxes' problems: 1,250 oranges packed 8 per bag; how many full bags and how many left?

*How to teach:* Solve the oranges problem together, discussing what the quotient and remainder each mean. The child does 3 more packing problems.

- **Q:** 1,250 oranges are packed 8 per bag. How many full bags and how many left?
  **A:** 156 full bags and 2 oranges left (156 × 8 = 1,248).
- **Q:** 100 students ride buses that hold 30 each. How many buses?
  **A:** 4 buses (100 ÷ 30 = 3 R10; round up).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Performance task
*Student:* Plan a 3-day camping trip at Yosemite for 7 people: food packs, tent spaces and cost per person.

*How to teach:* Hand over the camping task card and let the child plan the steps. Check each answer and require written explanations.

- **Q:** How many food packs are needed and how many boxes of 4?
  **A:** 7 people × 3 meals × 3 days = 63 packs; 63 ÷ 4 = 15 R3, so 16 boxes.
- **Q:** How many tents? What does the trip cost per person (63 packs at $7 plus campsite)?
  **A:** 7 ÷ 2 = 3 R1, so 4 tents. 63 × $7 = $441; 3 × $35 = $105; total $546; $546 ÷ 7 = $78 per person.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 4 review
*Student:* Mixed review of division strategies and multistep problems.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of division strategies and multistep problems; the child self-checks with multiplication.

- **Q:** 845 ÷ 5 = ?
  **A:** 169.
- **Q:** 6 packs of 48 stickers shared among 8 friends. How many each?
  **A:** 288 ÷ 8 = 36.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test on division strategies, remainders and multistep problems.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test independently. Score and correct together.

- **Q:** 912 ÷ 6 = ?
  **A:** 152.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test plus camping performance task.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test key: 1) 7,236 ÷ 4 = 1,809 2) 1,250 oranges in bags of 8: 156 bags, 2 left 3) 100 students, buses of 30: 4 buses 4) (6 × 48) ÷ 8 = 36 stickers 5) 845 ÷ 5 = 169 6) 912 ÷ 6 = 152 7) 4,200 ÷ 7 = 600 8) 75 ÷ 4 = 18 R3. Camping task rubric: 4 = all answers correct (63 packs, 16 boxes, 4 tents, $546 total, $78 per person) with clear steps and remainders interpreted; 3 = one computation error but correct reasoning; 2 = several errors or remainders not interpreted; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 4: Plant structures

**Prepare:** Celery stalk with leaves, food coloring, clear cup, a few flowers (lilies or tulips have large parts), magnifying glass, plastic knife, a carrot with top and a clump of grass with roots.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking plants get food from the soil. Roots take in water and minerals; leaves make food using sunlight.
- Thinking all roots look the same. Taproots (carrot) store food and reach deep; fibrous roots (grass) spread out and hold soil.

### Day 1 (Monday): Celery transport
*Student:* Put celery in colored water and observe xylem transport after 24 hours.

*How to teach:* Put celery in colored water; after 24 hours cut across the stalk and look at colored dots.

- **Q:** What do the colored dots in the celery show?
  **A:** The tubes (xylem) that carry water up from the roots to the leaves.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flower dissection
*Student:* Dissect a flower and label petals, stamens and pistil.

*How to teach:* Dissect a flower: count petals, find stamens (pollen) and the pistil.

- **Q:** What is the job of the stamen and pistil?
  **A:** The stamen makes pollen; the pistil receives pollen and contains the ovary, where seeds form.
- **Q:** Why are petals often brightly colored?
  **A:** To attract pollinators like bees and hummingbirds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Root systems
*Student:* Compare roots of grass and a carrot and explain their functions.

*How to teach:* Compare the carrot taproot and the grass's fibrous roots.

- **Q:** How are the two root systems' functions different?
  **A:** The carrot's taproot reaches deep and stores food; grass's fibrous roots spread wide to absorb water near the surface and hold soil in place.

### Day 4 (Thursday): California plants
*Student:* Research structures of a cactus and a redwood for their environments.

*How to teach:* Research a cactus and a coast redwood.

- **Q:** How does a cactus survive in the desert?
  **A:** Thick stem stores water; spines (modified leaves) reduce water loss and protect; waxy skin; shallow, wide roots catch rain quickly.
- **Q:** How do redwoods get water in summer?
  **A:** Their needles absorb moisture from coastal fog (and fog drip waters roots); thick bark protects them from fire.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an argument for how one plant structure supports survival.

*How to teach:* The child writes an argument (claim, evidence, reasoning) on how one plant structure supports survival.

- **Q:** Give an example claim.
  **A:** A cactus's thick stem helps it survive drought because it stores water for dry months.

**Assessment:** Plant structure argument.

**Answer key:** Argument rubric: 4 = clear claim about one structure's function, evidence from the week's labs or research, reasoning linking structure to survival/growth/reproduction; 3 = claim and evidence; 2 = description only; 1 = incorrect.

## California, A Changing State: Mexican independence and Alta California

**Prepare:** Maps of North America in 1819 and 1822; an excerpt from Richard Henry Dana's 'Two Years Before the Mast' (1840) describing hides — he called them 'California bank-notes.' Facts: Mexico's war for independence began in 1810 (Father Miguel Hidalgo's call, Sept 16) and ended in 1821.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking California became American right after Spain. It was part of Mexico from 1822 to 1848.
- Confusing Mexican Independence Day (September 16) with Cinco de Mayo (a later battle in 1862).

### Day 1 (Monday): Independence
*Student:* Read about the 1810-1821 Mexican War for Independence.

*How to teach:* Read about the 1810-1821 Mexican War for Independence.

- **Q:** When did Mexico win independence from Spain?
  **A:** 1821.
- **Q:** Who began the independence movement in 1810?
  **A:** Father Miguel Hidalgo.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map boundaries
*Student:* Compare maps of North America before and after 1821.

*How to teach:* Compare maps before and after 1821.

- **Q:** Who controlled Alta California after 1821?
  **A:** Mexico.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Effects
*Student:* List effects on Alta California's government and trade.

*How to teach:* List effects on Alta California's government and trade.

- **Q:** How did trade change under Mexico?
  **A:** Mexico allowed trade with foreign ships (American, British), unlike Spain's restrictions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Trade opens
*Student:* Read about hide and tallow trade with Yankee ships (Two Years Before the Mast excerpt).

*How to teach:* Read about the hide and tallow trade and the Dana excerpt.

- **Q:** What did Yankee ships trade for California hides?
  **A:** Manufactured goods like cloth, shoes, tools and furniture; hides went to New England to make leather.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a cause-and-effect chart showing how Mexican independence changed Alta California's government, trade and borders.

*How to teach:* Complete the cause-and-effect chart.

- **Q:** What was one effect of Mexican independence on the missions?
  **A:** Mexico moved to secularize the missions (done in 1834).

**Assessment:** Cause-and-effect chart.

**Answer key:** Chart key: cause — Mexico wins independence (1821); effects — Alta California becomes part of Mexico; new Mexican governors; foreign trade allowed (hide and tallow trade with American ships); missions secularized (1834); land grants create ranchos. Proficient: 4 accurate effects.

## Art & Music 4: Melody unit performance

**Prepare:** Recorder, song sheets from this unit, recording device, and a 4-point rubric (tone, rhythm, notes, expression).

**Watch for:**
- Practicing only from the beginning. Isolate the hardest measures.
- Stopping after mistakes in a run-through. Keep going to build performance stamina.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose repertoire
*Student:* Choose two recorder pieces learned this unit and explain why they fit your skill level and interests.

*How to teach:* Choose two pieces and explain why they fit skill level and interests. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why choose pieces that fit your skill level?
  **A:** So you can perform confidently and accurately while still being challenged.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Section rehearsal
*Student:* Rehearse the hardest 4 measures of each piece slowly, then at tempo.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the hardest 4 measures of each piece slowly, then at tempo. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** Why practice the hard part slowly first?
  **A:** Slow practice builds correct finger patterns before adding speed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Record
*Student:* Record a full run-through of both pieces without stopping.

*How to teach:* Record a full run-through of both pieces without stopping. Talk through ideas aloud first, then have your child write; check that the work uses this week's vocabulary and specific examples rather than general statements.

- **Q:** Why not stop when you make a mistake in a run-through?
  **A:** In a performance you keep going; it builds recovery skill.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluate
*Student:* Listen back and score tone, rhythm, notes and expression with a 4-point rubric; fix two problems.

*How to teach:* Listen and score tone, rhythm, notes, expression; fix two problems. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** Name a way to fix a rhythm problem.
  **A:** Count aloud or use a metronome slowly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Perform
*Student:* Perform both pieces for family with a spoken introduction for each.

*How to teach:* Perform for family with spoken introductions. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What should an introduction include?
  **A:** The title, composer or origin, and something interesting about the piece.

**Assessment:** Recorded and live performance (rubric).

**Answer key:** Performance rubric (1–4 each): tone, rhythm, notes, expression; plus introduction. Proficient = 3+ in each.

## PE 4: Balance and base of support

**Prepare:** Mat or carpet, pillows for safe falls, a timer.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking balance is just 'standing still.' Balance is controlling your center of gravity over your base of support, whether still or moving.
- Thinking a narrow stance is more stable. A wider base of support and a lower center of gravity are more stable.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bases of support
*Student:* Hold 8 balances on different body parts (two hands and one foot, one knee and one hand) for 5 seconds each.

*How to teach:* Call out 8 balances on different bases of support (two hands and one foot; one knee and one hand; seat only; one foot; etc.), holding each 5 seconds.

- **Q:** What is a base of support?
  **A:** The parts of the body touching the ground and the area between them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stable or not?
*Student:* Test narrow vs wide stances and explain which is more stable using 'center of gravity'.

*How to teach:* Test standing on one foot, feet together, and feet wide while a parent gently nudges. Explain center of gravity (the balance point of the body, near the belly button).

- **Q:** Which is more stable: feet together or feet wide? Why?
  **A:** Feet wide — the base of support is bigger, so the center of gravity stays over it more easily.
- **Q:** How does bending your knees help balance?
  **A:** It lowers your center of gravity, making you more stable.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Yoga balances
*Student:* Practice tree, warrior III and boat pose, holding each for 15 seconds.

*How to teach:* Practice tree, warrior III and boat pose, holding each 15 seconds; focus eyes on one spot.

- **Q:** In boat pose, what is your base of support?
  **A:** Your seat (sitting bones).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safety
*Student:* Learn safe falling (tuck and roll sideways) and spotting rules.

*How to teach:* Teach safe falling: tuck chin, bend knees, roll sideways onto the side of the body, don't reach out with a straight arm. Spotting rules: spot only when asked and taught, hands ready at the waist or hips.

- **Q:** Why shouldn't you catch a fall with a straight arm?
  **A:** It can break your wrist or arm; rolling spreads out the force.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform 3 balances of your choice and explain the base of support for each.

*How to teach:* Perform 3 balances of choice and explain the base of support for each.

- **Q:** Explain your hardest balance's base of support.
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g., 'Warrior III — one foot, small base, so I keep my core tight.'

**Assessment:** Balance demonstration and explanation.

**Answer key:** Balance demonstration rubric: 4 = 3 different balances held still for 5+ seconds each, and the child correctly names the base of support and explains stability using center of gravity and base size; 3 = holds balances with a wobble and explains basics; 2 = holds 1–2 or cannot explain; 1 = unable.

## Health 4: Eating out and family meals

**Prepare:** A restaurant kids' menu and a fast-food restaurant's online nutrition information. Conversation question cards for a family meal. Prepare the Unit 4 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking restaurant portions are single servings. Many restaurant portions are 2–3 MyPlate servings; share, box half, or order smaller sizes.
- Thinking every fast-food choice is equally unhealthy. Grilled options, fruit sides, water or milk, and smaller sizes are better choices.

### Day 1 (Monday): Menu choices
*Student:* Read a restaurant kids' menu and choose a balanced meal and drink.

*How to teach:* Read the kids' menu and choose a balanced meal and drink that covers at least 3–4 food groups.

- **Q:** Which drink is the best choice on a kids' menu?
  **A:** Water or low-fat/plain milk.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Portions out
*Student:* Compare restaurant portion sizes with MyPlate recommendations.

*How to teach:* Compare restaurant portions with MyPlate amounts (e.g., a large plate of pasta may be 3–4 cups, many servings of grains).

- **Q:** A restaurant pasta dish is 3 cups and the grain serving is about ½ cup cooked pasta (1 oz). How many ounce-equivalents is that?
  **A:** 3 cups = 6 half-cups, so 6 ounce-equivalents of grains — more than a whole day for many kids.
- **Q:** What can you do with a large restaurant portion?
  **A:** Share it, eat half and take the rest home, or order a smaller size.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fast food swaps
*Student:* Find 3 healthier swaps at a fast-food restaurant using online nutrition info.

*How to teach:* Use online nutrition info to find 3 swaps (grilled instead of fried chicken, apple slices instead of fries, water instead of soda) and note the differences.

- **Q:** Why is grilled chicken a healthier swap than fried?
  **A:** It has less fat and fewer calories because it isn't breaded and cooked in oil.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family meal
*Student:* Plan a screen-free family meal with conversation questions.

*How to teach:* Plan a screen-free family meal with 5 conversation questions.

- **Q:** Name 2 benefits of family meals.
  **A:** Family connection and conversation, healthier eating, better manners and communication skills.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 4 test on meal planning and food safety.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test (see key).

- **Q:** What are the 4 food safety steps?
  **A:** Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test key: 1) Name the 4 food safety steps — Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill. 2) How long to wash hands — 20 seconds. 3) Safe temperature for chicken — 165°F. 4) Refrigerator temperature — 40°F or below. 5) Leftovers must be refrigerated within — 2 hours. 6) Plan a dinner with all 5 food groups — any meal with one food from each group. 7) Name a healthy restaurant swap — e.g., water for soda, fruit for fries, grilled for fried. 8) Knife safety grip — claw grip. 9) Why use separate cutting boards — prevent cross-contamination from raw meat. 10) One way to handle a large restaurant portion — share it or take half home. Proficient = 8 of 10.
