# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 8

## ELA 4: Writing an original myth

**Prepare:** Writer's notebook, computer. Show photos of Golden Gate fog and Half Dome. Prepare 12 sentence-combining items with relative pronouns. Unit 2 test for Day 5 (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Using 'which' for people ('the girl which...'). Use who for people, which for things, that for either, whose for ownership.
- Writing a myth without explaining the phenomenon. The ending must clearly show how the natural feature came to be.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan a myth
*Student:* Choose a California phenomenon (fog over the Golden Gate, Half Dome's shape) and plan an origin myth.

*How to teach:* Review features of myths (gods/heroes, explains nature). The child picks a California phenomenon and plans hero, problem, events and explanation.

- **Q:** What is an origin myth?
  **A:** A traditional-style story that explains how something in nature or a custom came to be.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Relative pronouns
*Student:* Combine sentences using who, whose, which, that, where, when, why (12 items).

*How to teach:* Model combining: 'I met a girl. She surfs.' → 'I met a girl who surfs.' The child combines 12 pairs.

- **Q:** Combine: 'This is the trail. We hiked it yesterday.'
  **A:** 'This is the trail that (which) we hiked yesterday.'
- **Q:** Combine: 'That is the boy. His dog ran away.'
  **A:** 'That is the boy whose dog ran away.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the myth with a hero, a problem and an explanation of the phenomenon.

*How to teach:* The child drafts the myth with a hero, problem and explanation of the phenomenon.

- **Q:** What must the ending of your myth include?
  **A:** How the hero's actions caused the natural feature (e.g., why fog creeps over the Golden Gate each afternoon).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise & edit
*Student:* Revise to add at least two relative clauses and edit with a checklist.

*How to teach:* The child revises to add at least two relative clauses and edits with the checklist.

- **Q:** Underline the relative clause: 'The giant, who loved the sea, breathed fog over the bay.'
  **A:** 'who loved the sea'

### Day 5 (Friday): Storytelling & assessment
*Student:* Tell the myth aloud as a storyteller; take the Unit 2 test on theme, allusions and comparison.

*How to teach:* The child tells the myth aloud as a storyteller (eye contact, expression). Give the Unit 2 test.

- **Q:** What is the moral of 'The Ant and the Grasshopper'?
  **A:** Prepare today for the needs of tomorrow; hard work pays off.

**Assessment:** Published myth and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Myth rubric: 4 = hero, clear problem, events that build, explanation of the phenomenon, two relative clauses used correctly, storytelling with expression; 3 = all parts, minor errors; 2 = explanation unclear; 1 = incomplete. Unit 2 test key: 1) Theme of 'The Golden Touch': greed can cost what matters most (love/family) 2) Pandora's box = source of troubles 3) Herculean = requiring great strength 4) Midas touch = success at making money 5) Moral of 'The Tortoise and the Hare': slow and steady wins the race 6) Compare Coyote and Anansi: both are tricksters who use cleverness (accept supported differences) 7) Achilles' heel = weakness 8) Relative pronoun for people: who. Proficient: 80%.

## Math 4: Multiplicative comparison

**Prepare:** Counters (beans or coins), tape diagram templates. Write 10 word problems for Day 3 mixing 'how many more' and 'how many times as many.'

**Watch for:**
- Confusing 'times as many' with 'more than': 4 times as many as 3 is 12, not 7. Build both with counters to see the difference.
- In unknown-smaller problems (300 ft is 6 times as tall as the oak), multiplying 300 × 6 instead of dividing. Draw the tape: the big bar is 6 small bars.

### Day 1 (Monday): Times as many
*Student:* Build 'Ana has 3 shells, Ben has 4 times as many' with counters and write 12 = 4 x 3 statements.

*How to teach:* Build Ana's 3 shells, then 4 groups of 3 for Ben. Model the sentence '12 is 4 times as many as 3' and the equation 12 = 4 × 3; the child builds and writes 5 more.

- **Q:** Ana has 3 shells. Ben has 4 times as many. How many does Ben have?
  **A:** 12 shells; 12 = 4 × 3.
- **Q:** Write 35 = 5 × 7 as a comparison sentence.
  **A:** 35 is 5 times as many as 7 (or 7 times as many as 5).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tape diagrams
*Student:* Draw tape diagrams for 6 comparison problems and solve for the larger quantity or the multiplier.

*How to teach:* Draw a tape diagram with one small bar and a large bar made of copies of it. The child solves 6 problems, finding either the larger amount or the multiplier.

- **Q:** A rope is 8 feet long. Another rope is 5 times as long. How long is it?
  **A:** 40 feet (5 × 8).
- **Q:** A tree is 42 feet tall and a bush is 7 feet tall. How many times as tall is the tree?
  **A:** 6 times (42 ÷ 7 = 6).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Additive vs multiplicative
*Student:* Sort 10 word problems into 'how many more' and 'how many times as many' and solve each.

*How to teach:* The child reads each of 10 problems and sorts it into 'how many more' (subtract) or 'how many times as many' (multiply/divide), then solves.

- **Q:** Sam has 18 stickers and Ana has 6. How many more does Sam have? How many times as many?
  **A:** 12 more (18 - 6); 3 times as many (18 ÷ 6).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Division comparison
*Student:* Solve comparison problems with an unknown smaller quantity (e.g., 'a redwood is 6 times as tall as a 50-ft oak...').

*How to teach:* Model the redwood problem both ways on a tape diagram. The child solves comparison problems where the smaller quantity is unknown by dividing.

- **Q:** A redwood is 6 times as tall as a 50-ft oak. How tall is the redwood?
  **A:** 300 feet (6 × 50).
- **Q:** A giraffe is 18 feet tall, which is 3 times as tall as a man. How tall is the man?
  **A:** 6 feet (18 ÷ 3).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz: write a comparison statement for 4 equations and solve 4 word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz independently; the child must draw a tape diagram for each word problem. Correct together.

- **Q:** Write a comparison statement for 24 = 6 × 4.
  **A:** 24 is 6 times as many as 4 (or 4 times as many as 6).

**Assessment:** Comparison quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: Statements — 1) 35 = 5 × 7: 35 is 5 times as many as 7. 2) 24 = 6 × 4: 24 is 6 times as many as 4. 3) 56 = 8 × 7: 56 is 8 times as many as 7. 4) 45 = 9 × 5: 45 is 9 times as many as 5 (either order accepted). Problems — 5) A blue ribbon is 9 in; a red one is 4 times as long: 36 in. 6) Kim has 48 cards, 6 times as many as her brother: brother has 8. 7) 72 is how many times as many as 8? 9 times. 8) A cat weighs 7 lb and a dog 35 lb: the dog is 5 times as heavy and 28 lb heavier. Proficient: 7/8 with tape diagrams.

## Science 4: Energy conversion

**Prepare:** Batteries, LEDs or a buzzer, foil, a clothespin or cardboard flaps, tape, wire. Unit 2 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking devices 'make' energy. They convert energy from one form to another.
- Mixing criteria and constraints. Criteria = what success looks like; constraints = limits (materials, time, cost).

### Day 1 (Monday): Conversion hunt
*Student:* List devices at home that convert energy (toaster, fan) and identify input and output.

*How to teach:* List 8 home devices and identify input and output energy.

- **Q:** What energy goes into a toaster and comes out?
  **A:** Electrical in; heat (and some light) out.
- **Q:** What about a fan?
  **A:** Electrical in; motion (and some sound/heat) out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Define problem
*Student:* Define a problem: a light-up alarm for a door; list criteria and constraints.

*How to teach:* Define the door alarm problem with criteria (lights/buzzes when the door opens, loud enough to hear) and constraints (only household materials, safe battery power).

- **Q:** Give a criterion and a constraint.
  **A:** Criterion: the buzzer sounds every time the door opens. Constraint: must use only batteries and household materials.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build the door alarm with a foil switch and buzzer or LED.

*How to teach:* Build the alarm: a foil switch that closes when the door opens (or a clothespin with foil on its jaws, held apart by a cardboard tab tied to the door).

- **Q:** What energy conversion happens in your alarm?
  **A:** Stored chemical energy in the battery → electrical → sound (buzzer) or light (LED).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test and improve
*Student:* Test the alarm 5 times by opening the door, record each result and fix one failure point (loose foil, weak contact).

*How to teach:* Test 5 times; record results and fix a failure point.

- **Q:** Why test more than once?
  **A:** To find whether the alarm works reliably and to identify failure points.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on energy transfer.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test.

- **Q:** Name a material that conducts electricity.
  **A:** Copper (any metal, foil).

**Assessment:** Door alarm prototype and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test key: 1) Conductors: metals; insulators: plastic, rubber, wood 2) A complete circuit is needed for current 3) Switch opens/closes the circuit 4) Series vs. parallel 5) Heat flows from warm to cool 6) Toaster: electrical → heat 7) Sound: produced by vibrations 8) Energy can be transferred by sound, light, heat and electric currents. Prototype rubric: 4 = works in 5/5 trials, data recorded, one improvement documented; 3 = works 3-4/5; 2 = partially works; 1 = not built.

## California, A Changing State: California Indians today

**Prepare:** Calisphere set 'Native Americans: 20th Century to Today,' tribal websites (e.g., Yurok Tribe yuroktribe.org, Chumash, Tongva). Unit 2 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking California Indian cultures disappeared. Today California has more Native people than any other state and over 100 federally recognized tribes.
- Assuming all tribes are the same today. Each has its own government, language programs and traditions.

### Day 1 (Monday): Living communities
*Student:* Explore images from the Calisphere set 'Native Americans: 20th Century to Today' and list what has continued and changed.

*How to teach:* Explore images; list what continued and changed.

- **Q:** Name something that has continued.
  **A:** Basket weaving, ceremonies (e.g., World Renewal), language, tomol voyages (the Chumash revived them), tribal governments.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Language revival
*Student:* Read about a language revitalization program (e.g., Yurok language classes) and summarize it.

*How to teach:* Read about Yurok language revitalization.

- **Q:** Why are language programs important?
  **A:** Many California Indian languages were nearly lost; teaching them to children keeps culture, knowledge and identity alive. Yurok is now taught in some local schools.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Questions
*Student:* Write 5 questions you would ask a tribal historian.

*How to teach:* Write 5 questions for a tribal historian.

- **Q:** Give one respectful, open question.
  **A:** 'What traditions are most important to your community today?'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nation project
*Student:* Create a mini-book on one nation: land, food, shelter, beliefs and today's community.

*How to teach:* Create a mini-book on one nation.

- **Q:** What sections belong in the mini-book?
  **A:** Land, food, shelter, beliefs and today's community.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on California's first peoples.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test.

- **Q:** Name two California Indian nations and their regions.
  **A:** E.g., Chumash — Santa Barbara coast; Yurok — Klamath River.

**Assessment:** Nation mini-book and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test key: 1) Locate Chumash, Tongva, Ohlone, Miwok, Pomo, Yurok, Mojave, Kumeyaay on a map 2) Acorn processing steps 3) Tomol canoe 4) Controlled burns 5) Trade goods: shell beads, obsidian, salt 6) Oral tradition preserves history 7) Primary vs. secondary 8) World Renewal ceremony purpose 9) Example of revitalization today. Mini-book rubric: 4 = accurate sections on land, food, shelter, beliefs and today with illustrations; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = missing sections; 1 = incomplete. Proficient: 80%.

## Art & Music 4: Rhythm ensemble performance

**Prepare:** Recorder, a bucket drum (or pot) with a wooden spoon, and a metronome app set to 80 beats per minute. Choose a recorder song your child plays well ('Hot Cross Buns' or 'Merrily').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the ostinato player can change the pattern. An ostinato repeats the same pattern the whole time.
- Speeding up when playing together. Listen to the metronome and each other.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ostinato
*Student:* Create a 4-beat rhythm ostinato to accompany a recorder song.

*How to teach:* Your child creates a 4-beat ostinato (e.g., ta ta ti-ti ta) and repeats it 8 times. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What is an ostinato?
  **A:** A short rhythm or melody pattern repeated over and over.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Layering
*Student:* Play the recorder melody while a family member plays the ostinato.

*How to teach:* Your child plays the recorder melody while you play the ostinato on the bucket (then switch).

- **Q:** What is an ensemble?
  **A:** A group performing music together.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the melody and ostinato together with a metronome at 80 beats per minute.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with a metronome at 80 bpm. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why practice with a metronome?
  **A:** It keeps the tempo steady so performers stay together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Etiquette
*Student:* Practice performer and audience etiquette (bow, quiet listening, applause).

*How to teach:* Practice etiquette: bow, quiet listening, applause at the end. Start with a slow demonstration, then have your child repeat it several times while you give one piece of specific feedback at a time and praise effort.

- **Q:** When should the audience clap?
  **A:** At the end of the piece, when the performer finishes and bows.

### Day 5 (Friday): Mini-concert
*Student:* Perform for family and evaluate with a 3-point rubric.

*How to teach:* Perform the mini-concert; evaluate with a 3-point rubric (steady beat, correct notes, etiquette).

- **Q:** What does a performer do after finishing?
  **A:** Bows to thank the audience.

**Assessment:** Mini-concert performance.

**Answer key:** Mini-concert rubric (each 1–3): steady beat with the ostinato; correct notes on recorder; performer etiquette (introduction, bow). Proficient = 7/9 or higher.

## PE 4: Foot dribbling

**Prepare:** Soccer ball (size 4), 6 cones spaced about 4 feet apart, a whistle, stopwatch.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking the ball too far ahead. Use small touches so the ball stays within a stride.
- Looking only at the ball. Glance up between touches to see space and defenders.

### Day 1 (Monday): Touches
*Student:* Practice toe taps, inside-inside touches and sole rolls for 5 minutes.

*How to teach:* Practice toe taps (alternate soles on top of the ball), inside-inside touches (ball between insides of feet), sole rolls (roll the ball sideways with the sole) for 5 minutes.

- **Q:** Which surfaces of the foot are used for dribbling?
  **A:** Inside, outside and sole (and laces for speed).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cone weave
*Student:* Dribble through 6 cones using inside and outside of the foot.

*How to teach:* Dribble through 6 cones using inside and outside of the foot, keeping close control.

- **Q:** When weaving left around a cone, which part of the right foot might you use?
  **A:** The inside of the right foot (or outside of the left foot) to cut the ball left.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speed changes
*Student:* Dribble at walking, jogging and running speed on a whistle.

*How to teach:* On the whistle, change dribble speed: walk, jog, run. Bigger touches at speed, smaller when slow.

- **Q:** How do touches change when dribbling fast in open space?
  **A:** Touches can be slightly bigger and pushed ahead with the laces so you can run.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Red light, green light
*Student:* Play dribbling red light, green light, stopping the ball with the sole.

*How to teach:* Play red light, green light: on 'red,' stop the ball with the sole; late stops go back to the start.

- **Q:** How do you stop the ball quickly?
  **A:** Put the sole of your foot on top of it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Time a cone-weave dribble 3 times and record the best.

*How to teach:* Time 3 cone-weave runs and record the best.

- **Q:** Times 21.4, 19.8 and 20.6 seconds. Best?
  **A:** 19.8 seconds.

**Assessment:** Cone-weave time.

**Answer key:** Cone-weave time: record best of 3. Form rubric: 4 = weaves all 6 cones with close control using inside and outside of both feet, eyes up at times; 3 = misses one cone or ball gets away once; 2 = several misses; 1 = cannot complete with the ball.

## Health 4: Food tracking

**Prepare:** Print a 3-day food log (columns: time, food/drink, amount, food group) with an activity-minutes row. MyPlate daily amounts for ages 9–13 (moderately active): about 2 cups vegetables (girls) to 2½ cups (boys), 1½ cups fruit, 5–6 oz grains, 5 oz protein, 3 cups dairy — check myplate.gov/myplate-plan for your child.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting drinks, snacks and sauces in the log. Everything eaten and drunk counts.
- Counting mixed foods as only one group. A burrito can count for grains, protein, vegetables and dairy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Food log day 1
*Student:* Record everything you eat and drink today in a food log by food group.

*How to teach:* Model logging breakfast. The child records everything eaten and drunk today with amounts and food groups.

- **Q:** Why include amounts in a food log?
  **A:** To compare how much you ate from each group with the recommended amounts.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Food log day 2
*Student:* Continue the food log and add activity minutes.

*How to teach:* Continue logging and add minutes of activity.

- **Q:** A turkey and cheese sandwich on whole-wheat bread with lettuce counts for which groups?
  **A:** Grains, protein, dairy and vegetables.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Food log day 3
*Student:* Complete the third day of the food log, including a weekend meal, and total the servings per food group.

*How to teach:* Log a third day including a weekend meal. Total servings (cups/ounces) per group for each day.

- **Q:** Why include a weekend day?
  **A:** Eating patterns are often different on weekends, so it gives a truer picture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analyze
*Student:* Compare your 3-day log to MyPlate and identify one food group to increase.

*How to teach:* Compare totals to MyPlate recommendations. Identify one group to increase and plan how.

- **Q:** Your log shows 1 cup of vegetables a day and the goal is about 2 cups. What could you change?
  **A:** Add about 1 more cup a day, e.g., carrot sticks at lunch and a salad or broccoli at dinner.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 2 test on nutrients and MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 test (see key).

- **Q:** What is 1 teaspoon of sugar in grams?
  **A:** About 4 grams.

**Assessment:** Food log analysis and Unit 2 test.

**Answer key:** Food log analysis: proficient = 3 complete days with amounts and groups, totals for each group, comparison to MyPlate, and one group to increase with specific foods. Unit 2 test key: 1) Main job of carbohydrates — energy. 2) Main job of protein — build and repair tissues. 3) A healthy fat source — avocado, nuts, olive oil or fish. 4) Why whole grains — fiber, longer-lasting energy. 5) A food high in calcium — milk, yogurt, cheese. 6) A food high in vitamin C — orange, strawberry, bell pepper. 7) Best everyday drink — water. 8) 40 g of sugar ≈ how many teaspoons — 10. 9) The 5 food groups — fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy. 10) What fills half the plate — fruits and vegetables. Proficient = 8 of 10.
