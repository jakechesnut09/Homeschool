# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 36

## ELA 4: Writing portfolio and celebration

**Prepare:** Writing folder with all pieces from the year; a cumulative grammar test (key below); three on-demand opinion prompts (e.g., Should kids have homework? Is it better to live in a city or the country? Should every student learn an instrument?).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing portfolio pieces only by length. Pick pieces that show your best skills and growth.
- Rushing on-demand writing without planning. Spend 5 minutes planning reasons before drafting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose pieces
*Student:* Select the best narrative, explanatory report, opinion essay and poem from the writing folder and label why each was chosen.

*How to teach:* The child selects the best narrative, report, opinion essay and poem and labels why each was chosen.

- **Q:** What is a good reason to choose a piece?
  **A:** It shows a specific skill well (e.g., strong dialogue, well-supported reasons) or shows growth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection letter comparing the week 4 narrative with a recent piece, naming two specific improvements.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection letter comparing week 4's narrative with a recent piece, naming two improvements.

- **Q:** Give an example of a specific improvement.
  **A:** 'In September I had no dialogue; now I punctuate dialogue correctly and use it to show feelings.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Grammar review
*Student:* Take a cumulative grammar and conventions assessment (relative pronouns, progressive tenses, modals, commas, quotation marks).

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative grammar assessment.

- **Q:** Choose the correct relative pronoun: 'The girl ___ bike is red lives next door.'
  **A:** whose.
- **Q:** Write 'I read' in the future progressive.
  **A:** I will be reading.

### Day 4 (Thursday): On-demand writing
*Student:* Write an on-demand opinion piece in one 45-minute sitting on a choice of three prompts.

*How to teach:* The child chooses one prompt and writes an opinion piece in 45 minutes.

- **Q:** What should the first 5 minutes be used for?
  **A:** Planning: opinion, three reasons and quick evidence notes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebration
*Student:* Share the portfolio with family in an author celebration and write three grade 5 reading and writing goals.

*How to teach:* Author celebration: share the portfolio and write three grade 5 goals.

- **Q:** Write one specific goal.
  **A:** Example: 'I will read 25 books next year, including 5 biographies.'

**Assessment:** Portfolio and on-demand writing sample.

**Answer key:** Grammar test key (sample): 1) who/whose/which/that items — The boy who...; the dog whose...; the book that/which... 2) Progressive: was walking / is walking / will be walking 3) Modals: must (necessity), may (permission), can (ability), should (advice) 4) Commas: 'I packed apples, cheese, and bread.' 'After lunch, we hiked.' 'It rained, but we played.' 5) Quotation marks: "Where are you going?" asked Dad. Proficient: 80%. On-demand writing rubric: 4 = clear opinion, 3 reasons with supporting details, linking words, conclusion, few errors; 3 = 2 reasons or some errors; 2 = weak organization; 1 = off-topic. Portfolio: complete with 4 pieces, labels and reflection letter.

## Math 4: Year in review and celebration

**Prepare:** Print the week 1 diagnostic for comparison, a self-check review key, an end-of-year test (key below), poster paper and markers.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing on cumulative tests. Teach: estimate first, then check each answer with the inverse operation.
- Forgetting units and labels on project work. Every answer gets a unit (miles, dollars, hours).

### Day 1 (Monday): Whole number review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of place value, multiplication and division with a self-check key.

*How to teach:* The child completes the mixed whole-number review, then self-checks and redoes misses.

- **Q:** Round 476,512 to the nearest ten thousand.
  **A:** 480,000.
- **Q:** 6,432 ÷ 8 = ?
  **A:** 804.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fraction & decimal review
*Student:* Complete a mixed review of fractions, decimals and measurement.

*How to teach:* The child completes the fraction, decimal and measurement review and self-checks.

- **Q:** Compare 3/4 and 0.7.
  **A:** 3/4 = 0.75 > 0.7.
- **Q:** 2 3/5 + 1 4/5 = ?
  **A:** 4 2/5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): End-of-year assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year cumulative assessment and compare with the week 1 diagnostic.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative assessment without help, then compare scores with the week 1 diagnostic together and celebrate growth.

- **Q:** 38 × 26 = ?
  **A:** 988.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Math fair project
*Student:* Create a poster solving a multistep 'Plan a California road trip' problem with distance, cost and time.

*How to teach:* The child plans a California road trip poster: distances, gas cost and drive time, showing all work.

- **Q:** A 360-mile trip at 60 miles per hour takes how long?
  **A:** 6 hours.
- **Q:** Gas costs $5 per gallon and the car uses 12 gallons. What is the cost?
  **A:** $60.

### Day 5 (Friday): Present & reflect
*Student:* Present the project to family and write a reflection on the most improved skill.

*How to teach:* The child presents the poster to family and writes a reflection on the most improved skill.

- **Q:** What skill improved most this year, and what evidence shows it?
  **A:** Answers vary; a strong answer names a skill (e.g., fraction comparison) and cites a test score or example from work.

**Assessment:** End-of-year cumulative assessment and project presentation.

**Answer key:** End-of-year assessment key (sample items): 1) 504,309 in word form: five hundred four thousand, three hundred nine 2) Round 476,512 to nearest ten thousand: 480,000 3) 63,207 - 28,519 = 34,688 4) 4 × 2,345 = 9,380 5) 38 × 26 = 988 6) 6,432 ÷ 8 = 804 7) 3/4 = 9/12 8) 2 3/5 + 1 4/5 = 4 2/5 9) 5 × 2/3 = 3 1/3 10) 0.75 > 0.7 11) 3 hr 20 min = 200 min 12) Perimeter of 9 ft by 4 ft: 26 ft; area 36 sq ft 13) Angle of 120° is obtuse 14) Factor pairs of 20: 1×20, 2×10, 4×5. Proficient: 80%. Project presentation rubric: 4 = all computations correct and labeled, clear explanation, answers questions; 3 = minor errors; 2 = several errors or unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 4: Science year in review

**Prepare:** Science notebook, index cards, a ruler, a photo of California coastal cliffs; end-of-year assessment (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing units (e.g., calling erosion 'weathering'). Review vocabulary cards.
- Memorizing without explaining. Practice explaining each idea in your own words with an example.

### Day 1 (Monday): Energy & waves review
*Student:* Make concept-map flashcards for energy, collisions, circuits, waves and light, and quiz yourself.

*How to teach:* The child makes concept-map flashcards for energy, collisions, circuits, waves and light and self-quizzes.

- **Q:** What is the relationship between speed and energy?
  **A:** The faster an object moves, the more energy it has.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Life science review
*Student:* Redo the ruler-drop reaction test and explain the senses-brain-response model from memory.

*How to teach:* Redo the ruler drop and explain the senses-brain-response model.

- **Q:** Explain the model from memory.
  **A:** Sense organ detects a stimulus → nerves send a signal to the brain → brain processes and sends a signal to muscles → response.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Earth science review
*Student:* Explain a photo of California coastal cliffs using weathering, erosion and rock layer vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Explain the cliff photo using weathering, erosion and rock layer vocabulary.

- **Q:** Which agent most likely shaped the cliffs?
  **A:** Waves eroding the base, with weathering (water, wind, plant roots) breaking the rock.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cumulative test
*Student:* Take the end-of-year science assessment covering all units.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year assessment.

- **Q:** How do we see objects?
  **A:** Light reflects off them into our eyes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Notebook showcase
*Student:* Choose your three favorite investigations from the notebook and explain them to family.

*How to teach:* The child presents three favorite investigations to family.

- **Q:** What should each explanation include?
  **A:** The question, what was done, the results, and what it showed.

**Assessment:** End-of-year cumulative assessment.

**Answer key:** End-of-year key (sample): 1) faster = more energy 2) energy transfers in collisions and becomes sound/heat 3) complete circuit needed; metals conduct 4) heat moves warm → cool 5) label wave crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength 6) codes transfer information 7) light reflects into eyes 8) plant/animal structures support survival (example) 9) senses → brain → response 10) weathering vs. erosion 11) bottom rock layer oldest 12) Ring of Fire pattern 13) one hazard solution with trade-off 14) renewable vs. nonrenewable examples. Proficient: 80%.

## California, A Changing State: Local government and year review

**Prepare:** County map, local government websites (city, county, school district), information on a nearby California tribal government (rancheria or reservation). End-of-year assessment (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking tribes are 'part of' county government. Federally recognized tribes are sovereign nations with their own governments.
- Confusing city and county roles. Cities run local services like police and parks in town; counties run courts, elections, public health and services for unincorporated areas.

### Day 1 (Monday): Local governments
*Student:* Identify your city, county, school district and nearest rancheria or reservation.

*How to teach:* Identify your city, county, school district and nearest rancheria or reservation.

- **Q:** What does a county government do?
  **A:** Runs elections, courts, jails, public health, property records and services for areas outside cities.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): City council
*Student:* Watch part of a city council meeting online and list topics discussed.

*How to teach:* Watch part of a city council meeting; list topics.

- **Q:** Who leads a city?
  **A:** A city council and a mayor (sometimes a city manager).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Tribal government
*Student:* Read about a California tribal government and its services.

*How to teach:* Read about a California tribal government.

- **Q:** What services might a tribal government provide?
  **A:** Health clinics, housing, education and language programs, environmental protection, courts and police.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Year timeline
*Student:* Build a California history timeline from first peoples to today.

*How to teach:* Build a California history timeline.

- **Q:** Put in order: missions begin, statehood, Gold Rush, Chinese Exclusion Act.
  **A:** Missions begin 1769, Gold Rush 1848, statehood 1850, Chinese Exclusion Act 1882.

### Day 5 (Friday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year HSS assessment covering geography, first peoples, missions, Gold Rush, statehood and government.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year HSS assessment.

- **Q:** What year did California become a state?
  **A:** 1850.

**Assessment:** End-of-year assessment.

**Answer key:** End-of-year key (sample): 1) California's four regions 2) latitude/longitude definitions 3) two California Indian nations and adaptations 4) Cabrillo 1542 5) first mission San Diego 1769; 21 missions 6) Mexico independent 1821; ranchos 7) Bear Flag Revolt 1846 8) Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo 1848 9) Gold discovered 1848 at Sutter's Mill 10) Statehood Sept 9, 1850 11) Transcontinental railroad 1869 12) Chinese Exclusion Act 1882 13) Three branches of government 14) Assembly 80, Senate 40. Proficient: 80%. Timeline: proficient includes 10+ correctly ordered events.

## Art & Music 4: Exhibition opening and recorder concert

**Prepare:** Set up the exhibition wall, gather recorder, concert programs, and a recording device. Invite guests.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking reflection means only listing what went well. Honest reflection includes growth and next steps.
- Skipping warm-ups. Long tones and scales prepare tone and fingers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm-up routine
*Student:* Lead your own 10-minute warm-up: long tones, a D-to-D scale and the toughest phrase from each piece.

*How to teach:* Your child leads a 10-minute warm-up: long tones, D-to-D scale, toughest phrase from each piece.

- **Q:** Why play long tones in a warm-up?
  **A:** They develop steady breath and clear tone.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Exhibition opening
*Student:* Host the exhibition opening, giving guests a 3-minute tour and answering questions about techniques.

*How to teach:* Host the exhibition opening with a 3-minute tour. Treat it like a real event: quiet audience, a short spoken introduction and a bow; then debrief together using the week's rubric, starting with what went well.

- **Q:** What should you tell guests on the tour?
  **A:** The theme, techniques and stories behind a few pieces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Concert
*Student:* Perform the 4-piece recorder concert with introductions and proper stage etiquette.

*How to teach:* Perform the 4-piece concert with introductions and etiquette. Set up a simple 'stage' and audience, have your child introduce the work, and hold applause until the end; afterward ask them to name one strength and one thing to improve before you add one specific comment.

- **Q:** What should you do if you make a mistake?
  **A:** Keep going without stopping.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a one-page reflection comparing week 1 drawings and recorder playing to now, using the year's vocabulary.

*How to teach:* Write a one-page reflection comparing week 1 to now. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** How has your drawing changed since week 1?
  **A:** Answer varies; should cite specific skills (value, detail, perspective).

### Day 5 (Friday): Goal setting
*Student:* Watch the concert recording, evaluate it with the class rubric, and set two art and music goals for grade 5.

*How to teach:* Watch the recording, evaluate with the rubric, set two goals. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Write one art goal and one music goal for grade 5.
  **A:** Example: 'Learn to draw in perspective' and 'Learn F and high E on recorder.'

**Assessment:** Concert and exhibition evaluated with rubric; written reflection.

**Answer key:** Concert and exhibition rubric (1–4 each): tone, notes/rhythm, expression, stage etiquette, exhibition tour. Reflection: 4 = specific comparisons in art and music using vocabulary and two goals; 3 = mostly; 2 = vague; 1 = missing.

## PE 4: Field day

**Prepare:** Cones, ropes, balls, water, pillowcases for sacks, a tug-of-war rope, station signs, score cards, sportsmanship award certificates. Shade, sunscreen and water for all.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking field day is only about winning. It celebrates skills, effort and teamwork.
- Setting up stations too close together. Space stations so players can move safely.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan field day
*Student:* Plan 6 stations (sack race, accuracy throw, jump rope contest, dribble relay, tug-of-war, water relay) and make station signs.

*How to teach:* Plan 6 stations (sack race, accuracy throw, jump rope contest, dribble relay, tug-of-war, water relay). Make signs with rules and safety notes.

- **Q:** Give a safety rule for tug-of-war.
  **A:** Don't wrap the rope around hands or body; release the rope only on signal; pull on a soft surface.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Set up
*Student:* Measure and set up stations safely and prepare a score card.

*How to teach:* Measure and set up stations with safe spacing; prepare a score card.

- **Q:** Why measure distances for stations?
  **A:** So everyone competes under the same conditions (fair) and stations are spaced safely.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Field day
*Student:* Run field day with family or friends, rotating through all stations.

*How to teach:* Run field day, rotating through all stations.

- **Q:** How do you keep everyone safe and hydrated?
  **A:** Water breaks between stations, shade, sunscreen, and following each station's safety rule.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Awards
*Student:* Make sportsmanship awards for every participant.

*How to teach:* Make a sportsmanship award for every participant naming a specific kind action.

- **Q:** Example award?
  **A:** E.g., 'Great Encourager Award — for cheering for every team in the relay.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Year reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on your favorite unit and the skill you improved most.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on your favorite unit and the skill you improved most, with evidence.

- **Q:** Give evidence of improvement.
  **A:** E.g., 'My PACER went from 18 to 26 laps' or 'My wall rally went from 6 to 17.'

**Assessment:** Field day participation and reflection.

**Answer key:** Field day participation and reflection rubric: 4 = helps plan and set up stations safely, participates in all 6 with effort and sportsmanship, makes a specific award for each participant, and writes a reflection naming a favorite unit and most-improved skill with numerical evidence; 3 = all parts, less detail; 2 = partial participation or reflection; 1 = minimal.

## Health 4: Presenting the campaign and year review

**Prepare:** Campaign materials, permission for a display location, a review game (e.g., Jeopardy-style board with categories Nutrition, Activity, Medicines, Tobacco/Vaping, Alcohol). Prepare the final assessment (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking health learning ends with the school year. Healthy habits and goals continue all summer and for life.
- Thinking presenting means reading the poster aloud. Speak to the audience, make eye contact, and explain why the message matters.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Present the campaign to family or a community group.

*How to teach:* Present the campaign to family or a community group: introduce the topic, share the facts, show the products, and give the call to action.

- **Q:** What should you do at the end of your presentation?
  **A:** Give the call to action and invite questions.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Display
*Student:* Display posters in a visible place (with permission).

*How to teach:* Display posters (with permission) at home, a library, community center or co-op.

- **Q:** Why ask permission before posting?
  **A:** To respect property and rules of the place.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Year review
*Student:* Review nutrition, activity and ATOD key ideas with a game.

*How to teach:* Play a review game covering nutrition, activity and ATOD.

- **Q:** How many teaspoons of sugar in 24 g of sugar?
  **A:** 6 teaspoons (24 ÷ 4).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final assessment
*Student:* Take the end-of-year health assessment covering nutrition, physical activity, medicines, tobacco and alcohol.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year assessment (see key).

- **Q:** What does the 5/20 rule help you do?
  **A:** Decide whether a food is low (5% DV or less) or high (20% DV or more) in a nutrient.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on your health goals and set a goal for summer.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on the year's health goals and set a SMART summer goal.

- **Q:** Give an example of a SMART summer goal.
  **A:** E.g., 'I will swim or bike for 45 minutes 4 days a week from June 15 to August 15 and track it on a calendar.'

**Assessment:** Campaign presentation and final assessment.

**Answer key:** Final assessment key (15): 1) 3 sides of health triangle — physical, mental/emotional, social. 2) 5 food groups — fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy. 3) Main job of protein — build and repair tissues. 4) 5/20 rule — ≤5% DV low, ≥20% DV high. 5) Best everyday drink — water. 6) 4 food safety steps — Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill. 7) Daily activity for kids — 60+ minutes. 8) Prescription vs. OTC — prescription needs a doctor's order for one person. 9) Poison Control — 1-800-222-1222. 10) Addictive drug in tobacco and vapes — nicotine. 11) Vape aerosol is not just water vapor; name one thing in it — nicotine, heavy metals, ultrafine particles, flavor chemicals. 12) Alcohol is a — depressant. 13) Organ that breaks down alcohol — liver. 14) 4 refusal steps — say no, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away. 15) Legal age for alcohol/tobacco in CA — 21. Proficient = 12 of 15. Campaign presentation rubric: 4 = clear, confident delivery with eye contact, accurate sourced facts, call to action; 3 = clear with minor issues; 2 = reads from notes, few facts; 1 = incomplete.
