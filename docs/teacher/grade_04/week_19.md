# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 19

## ELA 4: Narrative poetry and performance

**Prepare:** 'Paul Revere's Ride' (Longfellow, public domain), an audio recording of the poem (many free on YouTube/LibriVox), a short factual account (e.g., from the Paul Revere House website). Key facts: on April 18-19, 1775, Revere and William Dawes rode to warn that British troops were coming; Revere was captured before reaching Concord; Samuel Prescott carried the warning to Concord; the lantern signal was sent from the Old North Church to patriots in Charlestown.

**Watch for:**
- Treating a narrative poem as history. Longfellow wrote in 1860 to inspire readers and changed facts for drama.
- Reading poetry in a sing-song rhythm. Pause at punctuation, not at line ends.

### Day 1 (Monday): Paul Revere's Ride
*Student:* Read Longfellow's poem and summarize the events stanza by stanza.

*How to teach:* Read the poem aloud. The child summarizes events stanza by stanza.

- **Q:** What is the meaning of 'One if by land, and two if by sea'?
  **A:** The number of lanterns hung in the Old North Church tower to signal how the British were moving: one lantern for land, two for crossing by water.
- **Q:** What towns does Revere ride through in the poem?
  **A:** Medford, Lexington and Concord.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare to audio
*Student:* Listen to a recorded reading and note how the reader's pacing changes the effect.

*How to teach:* Listen to a recording and note how the reader's pace and volume change (faster during the ride, slower at suspense).

- **Q:** Why would a reader speed up during the ride stanzas?
  **A:** To match the galloping horse and build excitement.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fact vs poem
*Student:* Read a short factual account and list what Longfellow changed.

*How to teach:* Read the factual account. The child lists what Longfellow changed.

- **Q:** Name two things the poem changes from history.
  **A:** Revere did not ride alone — William Dawes (and later Samuel Prescott) also rode; Revere was captured and did not reach Concord; the lanterns were a signal to others, not to Revere.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse 3 stanzas with expression and record them.

*How to teach:* The child rehearses 3 stanzas with expression and records them.

- **Q:** What is one fluency goal for your recording?
  **A:** Pause at punctuation, vary volume for excitement, and read at a pace listeners can follow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Perform
*Student:* Perform with a background sound effect or slides; self-evaluate fluency.

*How to teach:* The child performs with a sound effect or slides and self-evaluates fluency.

- **Q:** How will you rate your accuracy, pace and expression?
  **A:** Child rates each on a 1-4 scale and names one improvement.

**Assessment:** Recorded performance with fluency self-evaluation.

**Answer key:** Performance rubric: 4 = accurate reading of 3 stanzas, pace varies with action, expressive voice, clear volume, thoughtful self-evaluation; 3 = mostly fluent, some flat sections; 2 = several errors or monotone; 1 = incomplete. Fact vs. poem list: proficient names at least 2 accurate differences (other riders, capture, lantern signal purpose).

## Math 4: Comparing fractions

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, number lines. Write 12 fractions on cards for sorting (e.g., 1/3, 3/8, 2/5, 4/10, 1/2, 3/6, 6/12, 5/8, 2/3, 3/4, 7/10, 5/6).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking larger denominators mean larger fractions (1/8 > 1/4). Show strips: more pieces means smaller pieces.
- Comparing numerators only when denominators differ (3/5 < 4/10?). Rename to a common denominator or use a benchmark first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Benchmark 1/2
*Student:* Sort 12 fractions as less than, equal to, or greater than 1/2 and explain reasoning.

*How to teach:* Model: a fraction is half when the numerator is half the denominator. The child sorts 12 cards into <1/2, =1/2, >1/2 and explains each.

- **Q:** Is 3/8 less than, equal to or greater than 1/2?
  **A:** Less — half of 8 is 4, and 3 < 4.
- **Q:** Compare 2/5 and 5/8 using 1/2.
  **A:** 2/5 < 1/2 and 5/8 > 1/2, so 2/5 < 5/8.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Common numerators
*Student:* Compare 3/5 and 3/8 by thinking about piece size; solve 8 similar comparisons.

*How to teach:* Compare 3/5 and 3/8 with strips: same number of pieces, but fifths are bigger. The child solves 8 more and explains with piece size.

- **Q:** Compare 3/5 and 3/8.
  **A:** 3/5 > 3/8 — both have 3 pieces, but fifths are larger than eighths.
- **Q:** Compare 2/3 and 2/5.
  **A:** 2/3 > 2/5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Common denominators
*Student:* Rename to compare 5/6 and 7/8; record with >, =, < (10 problems).

*How to teach:* Model renaming 5/6 and 7/8 to 24ths (20/24 and 21/24). The child solves 10 problems.

- **Q:** Compare 5/6 and 7/8.
  **A:** 5/6 = 20/24, 7/8 = 21/24, so 5/6 < 7/8.
- **Q:** Compare 3/4 and 7/12.
  **A:** 3/4 = 9/12 > 7/12.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ordering
*Student:* Order sets of four fractions on a number line and justify in writing.

*How to teach:* The child places sets of four fractions on a 0-1 number line and writes a justification.

- **Q:** Order 1/2, 1/3, 3/4, 5/12 from least to greatest.
  **A:** 1/3 (4/12), 5/12, 1/2 (6/12), 3/4 (9/12).
- **Q:** Which is closest to 1: 7/8, 4/5, 9/10?
  **A:** 9/10 — it is only 1/10 away; 4/5 is 1/5 away and 7/8 is 1/8 away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on comparing fractions with explanations.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz; each comparison needs a reason (benchmark, piece size or common denominator).

- **Q:** Compare 7/10 and 68/100.
  **A:** 7/10 = 70/100 > 68/100.

**Assessment:** Comparing fractions quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) 2/3 > 2/5 2) 3/10 < 1/2 3) 5/6 < 7/8 4) 4/8 = 2/4 5) 3/4 > 7/12 6) 1/5 < 1/4 7) Order 1/4, 3/8, 2/3, 5/6 (already least to greatest) 8) 7/10 > 68/100. Explanation rubric: proficient answers name a valid method (benchmark 1/2, same numerator/piece size, or common denominator) for each.

## Science 4: Senses and the brain

**Prepare:** A 30-cm ruler, blindfold, apple and raw potato cubes (peeled), spices, fabrics and textures (sandpaper, silk, felt, foil...). Unit 5 test for Day 5. Check food allergies before tasting.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the senses alone do the sensing and responding. Sense organs send signals to the brain, which processes them and sends signals to muscles to respond.
- Thinking taste works alone. Much of 'flavor' comes from smell.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ruler drop
*Student:* Measure reaction time with a ruler drop test 5 times.

*How to teach:* Hold the ruler at 0 cm between the child's open fingers; drop without warning; record cm caught, 5 times.

- **Q:** Describe the path of information in the ruler drop.
  **A:** Eyes see the ruler fall → signal to brain → brain sends signal to hand muscles → fingers close.
- **Q:** A 15 cm catch is about how long a reaction?
  **A:** About 0.17 seconds (rough chart: 10 cm ≈ 0.14 s, 20 cm ≈ 0.20 s).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Smell and taste
*Student:* Taste apple and potato pieces blindfolded with nose plugged, then unplugged, and record how smell affects taste.

*How to teach:* Blindfolded and nose plugged, the child tastes apple and potato cubes and guesses; repeat unplugged.

- **Q:** Why is it hard to tell apple from potato with your nose plugged?
  **A:** Flavor depends on smell; without it the tongue only senses basic tastes and texture, which are similar.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Touch test
*Student:* Identify 8 fabrics and textures by touch while blindfolded and record which were hardest.

*How to teach:* Identify 8 textures blindfolded; record which were hardest.

- **Q:** What senses touch?
  **A:** Receptors (nerve endings) in the skin, which send signals to the brain.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Animal senses
*Student:* Research owls, bats and sharks and their special senses.

*How to teach:* Research owls, bats and sharks.

- **Q:** How do bats find food in the dark?
  **A:** Echolocation — they make high sounds and listen to the echoes.
- **Q:** What special sense do sharks have?
  **A:** They can sense tiny electric fields from prey (and have a strong sense of smell).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 5 test and draw a sense-brain-response model.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test and have the child draw a sense → brain → response model.

- **Q:** Draw the model for touching a hot pan.
  **A:** Skin senses heat → nerves carry signal to brain/spinal cord → signal to arm muscles → hand pulls away.

**Assessment:** Sense model and Unit 5 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test key: 1) Plant structures: roots absorb water, stem supports and transports, leaves make food, flowers reproduce 2) Celery shows water transport 3) Camouflage helps animals hide 4) Beak shape matches food 5) Lungs/diaphragm breathing 6) Heart pumps blood; pulse rises with exercise 7) Senses send information to the brain 8) Brain processes and sends signals to muscles 9) Echolocation in bats 10) Model: stimulus → sense organ → brain → response. Proficient: 80%.

## California, A Changing State: Overland trails to California

**Prepare:** U.S. map, Calisphere set 'Transportation in California History,' Virginia Reed's 1847 letter excerpt (widely reprinted). Facts: the Bidwell-Bartleson party (1841) was the first organized emigrant group to cross overland to California (they abandoned their wagons before the Sierra); James Beckwourth found Beckwourth Pass (1850); the Donner Party (1846-47) took the untested Hastings Cutoff, was trapped by snow near what is now Donner Lake, and about half died. Preview: the Donner story involves starvation; choose an age-appropriate account.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the overland trail was the fastest route. It took about 4-6 months; the Panama route could take 6-8 weeks if ships connected.
- Thinking push and pull factors are the same. Push = reasons to leave home; pull = reasons attracted to California.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why come?
*Student:* List push and pull factors that brought emigrants in the 1840s (land, trade, adventure) and rank them.

*How to teach:* List and rank push and pull factors.

- **Q:** Give a push factor and a pull factor.
  **A:** Push: hard times or crowded farms back East. Pull: free or cheap land, mild climate, trade, adventure.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): John Bidwell
*Student:* Read about the 1841 Bidwell-Bartleson party, the first overland wagon group, and trace its route over the Sierra.

*How to teach:* Read about the Bidwell-Bartleson party; trace its route.

- **Q:** Why is the 1841 Bidwell-Bartleson party important?
  **A:** It was the first organized group of American emigrants to travel overland to California; John Bidwell later founded Chico.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): James Beckwourth
*Student:* Read about James Beckwourth, an African American mountain man who found Beckwourth Pass, and mark it on the map.

*How to teach:* Read about James Beckwourth; mark Beckwourth Pass.

- **Q:** Who was James Beckwourth?
  **A:** An African American mountain man and trapper who found a lower, easier pass through the northern Sierra Nevada (Beckwourth Pass) and led emigrants over it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): The Donner Party
*Student:* Read an age-appropriate account of the Donner Party and a letter excerpt by survivor Virginia Reed; list decisions that led to disaster.

*How to teach:* Read about the Donner Party and Virginia Reed's letter; list decisions that led to disaster.

- **Q:** What decisions led to the Donner Party's disaster?
  **A:** Taking the untested Hastings Cutoff, which cost weeks; starting late in the season; delays and conflicts; reaching the Sierra just as heavy snow began.
- **Q:** What advice did Virginia Reed give?
  **A:** 'Never take no cutoffs and hurry along as fast as you can.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare the sea route around Cape Horn, the Panama route and the overland trail in a chart (time, cost, dangers).

*How to teach:* Compare the three routes in a chart.

- **Q:** What was the main danger on the Panama route?
  **A:** Tropical diseases such as malaria, yellow fever and cholera.

**Assessment:** Route comparison chart.

**Answer key:** Chart key: Cape Horn — about 5-8 months by ship, around South America (13,000+ miles), storms, seasickness, scurvy, moderate-to-high cost; Panama — 6-8 weeks if connections worked, ship + crossing the Isthmus by canoe/mule (railroad from 1855), tropical diseases, most expensive; Overland — 4-6 months, about 2,000 miles by wagon from Missouri, river crossings, deserts, mountains, snow, sickness, cheapest. Proficient: accurate time, cost and dangers for all three.

## Art & Music 4: Chinese brush painting

**Prepare:** Gather a soft round watercolor brush, black watercolor or ink, water, and rice paper or newsprint. Find a classical Chinese bamboo painting (e.g., by Wen Tong or a Qing-dynasty ink bamboo) and information about Tyrus Wong (Chinese American artist whose paintings inspired the look of Disney's 'Bambi').

**Watch for:**
- Filling every part of the paper. In Chinese brush painting, empty space is an important part of the composition.
- Holding the brush like a pencil at a slant. Hold it upright and move from the arm for confident strokes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look
*Student:* Study a classical Chinese bamboo painting and discuss balance and empty space.

*How to teach:* Study the bamboo painting; discuss balance and empty space. Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Why do Chinese painters leave empty space?
  **A:** It creates balance and calm, suggests air or mist, and lets the viewer's imagination fill in.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strokes
*Student:* Practice the four basic strokes: bamboo stalk, leaf, dot and line.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate the four strokes: stalk (press-lift segments), leaf (press then lift to a point), dot and line. Your child practices each on newsprint, holding the brush upright.

- **Q:** How do you make a bamboo leaf stroke?
  **A:** Touch the tip down, press to widen, then lift as you pull to make a pointed end.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bamboo painting
*Student:* Paint a bamboo stalk with leaves using ink washes of different values.

*How to teach:* Mix three values of ink wash (dark, medium, light). Paint a bamboo stalk with leaves, darker leaves in front and lighter behind.

- **Q:** How do you make lighter ink?
  **A:** Add more water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Context
*Student:* Read about Chinese American artists in California (e.g., Tyrus Wong) and their influence.

*How to teach:* Read about Tyrus Wong: born in China, came to California as a boy through Angel Island, his misty paintings inspired 'Bambi's' backgrounds.

- **Q:** How did Tyrus Wong influence American art?
  **A:** His Chinese-style landscape paintings gave Disney's 'Bambi' its soft, misty look; he also painted and designed kites and greeting cards.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & share
*Student:* Finish a painting with a seal-style signature and compare it to your earlier work.

*How to teach:* Finish a painting with a red seal-style signature (draw a small square with initials) and compare with earlier work.

- **Q:** What is a seal in Chinese painting?
  **A:** A carved stamp with the artist's name printed in red ink as a signature.

**Assessment:** Brush painting.

**Answer key:** Brush painting: 4 = confident stalk and leaf strokes, at least three ink values, balanced composition with intentional empty space, seal signature; 3 = most; 2 = hesitant strokes or one value; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 4: Gymnastics routine

**Prepare:** Mat, routine planning sheet (start shape, balance 1, balance 2, roll, jump, finish; level for each), phone for video.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing through elements. Hold each balance for 3 seconds and show a clear start and finish.
- Using only one level. Plan high (jumps, relevé), medium (standing balances) and low (rolls, seated balances) levels.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a routine with a start shape, 2 balances, 1 roll, 1 jump and a finish, using high, medium and low levels.

*How to teach:* Plan a routine: start shape, 2 balances, 1 roll, 1 jump (soft landing), finish shape. Label each with high, medium or low level.

- **Q:** Give an element for each level.
  **A:** High: straight jump or tiptoe balance; medium: standing scale/arabesque; low: forward roll or V-sit.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transitions
*Student:* Practice smooth transitions between each element.

*How to teach:* Practice transitions between elements so there are no extra steps or stops.

- **Q:** What is a transition?
  **A:** The movement that links one skill to the next.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the routine 5 times, then record a final run-through on video.

*How to teach:* Rehearse 5 times, then record a final run-through.

- **Q:** Why rehearse several times?
  **A:** To build memory and smoothness so the routine is controlled.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peer feedback
*Student:* Watch the recording with a parent and revise one transition.

*How to teach:* Watch the video with a parent and revise one transition.

- **Q:** Which transition did you change and how?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g., 'I rolled straight into my V-sit instead of standing first.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit assessment
*Student:* Perform the final routine; scored on control, levels, transitions and safety.

*How to teach:* Perform the final routine; score with the rubric.

- **Q:** How do you land a jump safely?
  **A:** On the balls of both feet, bending knees and hips, arms out for balance.

**Assessment:** Gymnastics routine (rubric).

**Answer key:** Gymnastics routine rubric (score 1–4 each): Control (balances held 3 seconds, steady landings), Levels (high, medium and low all used), Transitions (smooth, no extra steps), Safety (chin tucked in roll, soft landing). Proficient = 3 or higher in each category.

## Health 4: Physical activity goal

**Prepare:** Goal tracker and calendar, stickers, health journal. The week 18 logs for reference.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking barriers mean you can't be active. Most barriers have solutions (rainy day = indoor activity menu).
- Giving up after missing a day. Adjust the plan and keep going; progress, not perfection.

### Day 1 (Monday): Barriers
*Student:* List barriers to being active (weather, time, screens) and a solution for each.

*How to teach:* List barriers to activity (weather, time, screens, no one to play with, darkness) and a solution for each.

- **Q:** Barrier: it's raining. Solution?
  **A:** Do an indoor activity: dance, yoga video, jumping jacks circuit, active games inside.
- **Q:** Barrier: no time after homework. Solution?
  **A:** Be active in short bursts (three 10-minute sessions), walk or bike to errands, or play before homework.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Set goal
*Student:* Write a 3-week SMART activity goal (e.g., 30 minutes of outdoor play after school 5 days a week).

*How to teach:* Write a 3-week SMART activity goal.

- **Q:** Make this SMART: 'I'll play outside more.'
  **A:** E.g., 'I will play outside for 30 minutes after school 5 days a week for the next 3 weeks and mark it on my calendar.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Activity menu
*Student:* Create an 'activity menu' of indoor and outdoor choices for rainy and sunny days.

*How to teach:* Make an activity menu with indoor and outdoor choices for rainy and sunny days.

- **Q:** Why have a menu of choices?
  **A:** So you always have an option no matter the weather or time, which helps you stick to the goal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Track
*Student:* Begin tracking the goal on a calendar with stickers and note how you feel each day.

*How to teach:* Begin tracking with stickers and a one-word feeling each day.

- **Q:** Why note how you feel each day?
  **A:** To see the connection between activity and mood/energy, which motivates you.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Check progress after 5 days and adjust the plan if needed.

*How to teach:* After 5 days, check progress and adjust the plan if needed.

- **Q:** You met your goal 3 of 5 days. What could you adjust?
  **A:** Identify what blocked the other days and plan a solution (e.g., an indoor option or earlier time), or adjust the goal to be achievable.

**Assessment:** Goal tracker check-in.

**Answer key:** Goal tracker check-in rubric: 4 = SMART goal written with all 5 parts, barriers listed with realistic solutions, activity menu with indoor/outdoor options, 5 days tracked with feelings, and a thoughtful adjustment based on data; 3 = one part missing; 2 = tracking incomplete; 1 = no goal or tracking.
