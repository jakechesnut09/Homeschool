# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 35

## ELA 4: Fluency and reading reflection

**Prepare:** A grade 4 passage of similar difficulty to week 1, graph paper for WCPM, reading log; end-of-year assessment with one literary and one informational passage (e.g., from ReadWorks or CommonLit, using their answer keys); library visit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking reading faster is always better. Fluency includes accuracy and expression.
- Summary instead of reflection. A reflection explains why the book mattered to you, with scenes as evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fluency check
*Student:* Do a final 1-minute fluency read of a grade 4 passage, graph words-correct-per-minute, and compare with the week 1 baseline.

*How to teach:* The child reads 1 minute; compute WCPM, graph it against week 1, and celebrate growth.

- **Q:** If you read 132 words with 4 errors in 1 minute, what is your WCPM?
  **A:** 128.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Reading reflection
*Student:* Write a one-page reflection on the favorite book of the year, citing two scenes that made it memorable.

*How to teach:* The child writes a one-page reflection citing two memorable scenes.

- **Q:** What makes a reflection strong?
  **A:** Clear statement of why the book mattered, two specific scenes described, and the reader's feelings or lessons.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Book talk
*Student:* Give a 2-minute book talk recommending a book read this year, including a short read-aloud excerpt.

*How to teach:* The child gives a 2-minute book talk with a short read-aloud excerpt.

- **Q:** What should a book talk avoid?
  **A:** Giving away the ending.

### Day 4 (Thursday): End-of-year reading
*Student:* Take the end-of-year reading assessment with a literary and an informational passage.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year reading assessment.

- **Q:** What strategies help on a reading test?
  **A:** Read questions first, reread to find evidence, cross out wrong answers, check that answers are supported by the text.

### Day 5 (Friday): Summer list
*Student:* Browse the library and build a summer reading list of 10 books across genres, with a reason for each.

*How to teach:* Build a summer reading list of 10 books across genres, with reasons.

- **Q:** Name four genres to include.
  **A:** Examples: realistic fiction, fantasy, mystery, biography, informational, poetry, graphic novel, historical fiction.

**Assessment:** End-of-year reading assessment.

**Answer key:** End-of-year reading assessment: use the passage source's answer key; score literary questions (theme, character, inference, vocabulary in context, summary) and informational questions (main idea, text structure, text features, evidence). Proficient: 80%. Fluency: compare WCPM to week 1 and to grade 4 end-of-year norms (roughly 120-140 WCPM).

## Math 4: Patterns and rules

**Prepare:** Pattern blocks, graph paper. Prepare 6 input/output tables (rules like +5, ×4, ×2 + 1).

**Watch for:**
- Assuming a pattern rule from only two terms. Check the rule against every term in the table.
- Describing only the step ('add 3') without noticing features. Ask: what else do you notice (odd/even, ones digits)?

### Day 1 (Monday): Number patterns
*Student:* Generate patterns for 'start at 1, add 3' and describe why terms alternate odd and even.

*How to teach:* Generate 'start at 1, add 3' together for 10 terms. Ask why the terms alternate odd and even (adding an odd number switches odd to even and back).

- **Q:** List the first 6 terms of 'start at 1, add 3'.
  **A:** 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16.
- **Q:** Why do the terms alternate odd and even?
  **A:** Odd + odd (3) = even, and even + odd = odd, so each step switches.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shape patterns
*Student:* Build growing patterns with pattern blocks and predict the 10th figure.

*How to teach:* Build figure 1 (4 tiles), figure 2 (7 tiles), figure 3 (10 tiles), adding 3 each time. The child predicts figure 10 without building it.

- **Q:** If figure 1 has 4 tiles and each figure adds 3, how many in figure 10?
  **A:** 4 + 9 × 3 = 31 tiles.
- **Q:** How many in figure 5?
  **A:** 16 tiles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Input/output tables
*Student:* Complete 6 tables and write the rule for each.

*How to teach:* The child completes 6 input/output tables and writes the rule for each, testing it on every pair.

- **Q:** In: 2, 5, 7 → Out: 8, 20, 28. What is the rule?
  **A:** Multiply by 4.
- **Q:** In: 1, 3, 6 → Out: 3, 7, 13. What is the rule?
  **A:** Multiply by 2, then add 1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pattern project
*Student:* Design an original pattern with a rule and write 3 observations about it.

*How to teach:* The child designs an original pattern with a rule and writes 3 observations about it.

- **Q:** Rule: start at 5, add 5. Name 2 features.
  **A:** Terms: 5, 10, 15, 20...; ones digits alternate 5 and 0; every term is a multiple of 5; terms alternate odd and even.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Quiz on patterns and rules.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and collect the project.

- **Q:** Rule 'start at 2, multiply by 2'. List 5 terms.
  **A:** 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.

**Assessment:** Pattern quiz plus project.

**Answer key:** Quiz key: 1) Start at 3, add 4: 3, 7, 11, 15, 19 2) Feature: all terms are odd 3) Start at 50, subtract 6: 50, 44, 38, 32, 26 4) Table In 2, 4, 6 → Out 7, 9, 11: rule add 5 5) In 3, 5, 8 → Out 9, 15, 24: rule ×3 6) Figure n has 3n + 1 tiles pattern: figure 10 = 31 tiles. Project rubric: 4 = clear rule, at least 6 correct terms, 3 accurate observations; 3 = rule and terms correct, observations weak; 2 = errors in terms; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 4: Improving and presenting the design

**Prepare:** Prototype and spare materials, graph paper, poster board or slides.

**Watch for:**
- Changing several things at once in the redesign. Change one variable to know what caused the improvement.
- Comparing single trials instead of averages.

### Day 1 (Monday): Redesign
*Student:* Change one variable (blade angle, number of blades or axle material) to fix the biggest failure point.

*How to teach:* Change one variable to fix the biggest failure point.

- **Q:** Why change only one variable?
  **A:** So you know that change caused any difference in results.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Retest
*Student:* Run 5 new trials under the same conditions and record results.

*How to teach:* Run 5 new trials under the same conditions.

- **Q:** Why must conditions match the first tests?
  **A:** To compare fairly; otherwise you can't tell if the design change made the difference.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare results
*Student:* Graph before-and-after averages and decide whether the change helped.

*How to teach:* Graph before-and-after averages; decide whether the change helped.

- **Q:** If average lift time went from 40 s to 28 s, did it improve?
  **A:** Yes — it lifted 12 seconds faster on average.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the device, explaining each energy conversion (wind to rotation to lifting) and the data.

*How to teach:* Present the device and data, explaining each energy conversion.

- **Q:** Explain the energy conversions.
  **A:** Electrical energy runs the fan → moving air (wind) → rotating blades and axle → string winds → pennies gain height (stored energy).

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Write a reflection on what you would change with more time and materials.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection on what they'd change with more time.

- **Q:** What makes a strong reflection?
  **A:** Specific next steps tied to data or failures observed.

**Assessment:** Engineering presentation and reflection (rubric).

**Answer key:** Engineering rubric: 4 = one variable changed with reason; before/after data and graph of averages; correct conclusion; clear presentation of energy conversions; thoughtful reflection; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = no comparison data; 1 = incomplete.

## California, A Changing State: State government

**Prepare:** State government website (gov.ca.gov, legislature.ca.gov 'Find Your Representative' at findyourrep.legislature.ca.gov), a district map. Facts: the Legislature has an 80-member Assembly (2-year terms) and a 40-member Senate (4-year terms); the governor serves a 4-year term (maximum two terms); the California Supreme Court has a chief justice and six associate justices. Look up who currently holds your offices, since officials change after elections.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the governor makes laws alone. The Legislature passes bills; the governor signs or vetoes them.
- Confusing state and federal legislators. State: Assembly member and state senator; federal: U.S. Representative and U.S. Senators.

### Day 1 (Monday): Executive
*Student:* Read about the governor and other elected officials (lieutenant governor, attorney general).

*How to teach:* Read about the governor and other statewide officials.

- **Q:** What does the governor do?
  **A:** Leads the executive branch, signs or vetoes bills, proposes the state budget, and commands the state National Guard.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Legislature
*Student:* Read about the Assembly and Senate and how a bill becomes law.

*How to teach:* Read about the Assembly and Senate and how a bill becomes law.

- **Q:** List the steps of a bill becoming a law in California.
  **A:** A legislator introduces a bill → committee hearings → vote in the first house → same in the other house → governor signs it (or vetoes; the Legislature can override with a two-thirds vote).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Your legislators
*Student:* Look up your state Assembly member and senator and their committee roles.

*How to teach:* Look up your Assembly member and state senator.

- **Q:** How many members are in the Assembly and Senate?
  **A:** 80 Assembly members; 40 senators.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Courts
*Student:* Read about the California Supreme Court and state courts and explain the job of the judicial branch.

*How to teach:* Read about state courts.

- **Q:** What is the job of the judicial branch?
  **A:** To interpret laws and decide cases, including whether laws follow the constitution.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a letter to your state legislator about an issue that matters to you.

*How to teach:* Write a letter to your state legislator.

- **Q:** What parts belong in the letter?
  **A:** Date, greeting, who you are, the issue, your opinion with reasons, a specific request, polite closing and signature.

**Assessment:** Letter to legislator.

**Answer key:** Letter rubric: 4 = correctly addressed to the child's own legislator, clearly states an issue and opinion with 2-3 reasons, a specific request, formal tone, correct letter format; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = vague issue or informal; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 4: Concert preparation

**Prepare:** Recorder, all music from the year, metronome, and paper for programs.

**Watch for:**
- Raising tempo too quickly. Increase by small steps only after it's correct.
- Forgetting dynamics. Mark them on the music.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose program
*Student:* Choose 4 pieces (a folk song, a classical excerpt, your ABA composition and a free choice) and explain why each belongs.

*How to teach:* Choose 4 pieces (folk, classical excerpt, ABA composition, free choice); explain why each belongs.

- **Q:** Why include different types of pieces?
  **A:** Variety shows different skills and keeps the audience interested.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Targeted practice
*Student:* Practice the two hardest passages slowly with a metronome, raising the tempo 10 beats at a time.

*How to teach:* Practice the two hardest passages slowly, raising tempo 10 bpm at a time. Model it once yourself (or show a short video), let your child try, and give one specific cue-based correction before the next attempt; finish with a full run-through.

- **Q:** Why raise tempo in small steps?
  **A:** So fingers stay accurate as speed increases.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feedback round
*Student:* Record a run-through, get feedback from a family member, and mark dynamics changes on the music.

*How to teach:* Record a run-through, get feedback, mark dynamics. Let your child draft independently, then read it together and ask for one more specific detail or example wherever the answer is vague.

- **Q:** What does 'mf' mean?
  **A:** Mezzo forte — medium loud.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design program
*Student:* Design a printed concert program with piece titles, composers and short program notes.

*How to teach:* Design a printed concert program. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What belongs in a concert program?
  **A:** Piece titles, composers and short notes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Hold a full dress rehearsal including bows and spoken introductions, and fix one remaining problem.

*How to teach:* Hold a dress rehearsal with bows and introductions; fix one problem. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** What is a dress rehearsal?
  **A:** A full practice exactly like the real performance.

**Assessment:** Dress rehearsal checklist.

**Answer key:** Dress rehearsal checklist: all 4 pieces played; correct notes and rhythm; dynamics shown; spoken introductions; bows; one problem fixed. Proficient = 5 of 6.

## PE 4: Lifetime activity plan

**Prepare:** Quiz (see key), calendar template (4 weeks), heart-rate log, and a new activity option with safety gear (helmet, life jacket/supervision for swimming).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you need a gym or team to stay active. Many lifetime activities (walking, biking, swimming, dancing) need little equipment.
- Planning the same activity every day. Use the FITT principle and vary the type to build all fitness components.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fitness components quiz
*Student:* Take a quiz on the five fitness components and heart rate concepts.

*How to teach:* Quiz on the 5 fitness components and heart-rate concepts (see key).

- **Q:** If you count 30 beats in 15 seconds, what is your heart rate?
  **A:** 120 bpm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Favorite activities
*Student:* List 10 activities you enjoy and which fitness component each builds.

*How to teach:* List 10 enjoyable activities and the fitness component each builds.

- **Q:** Which component does yoga build most?
  **A:** Flexibility (also muscular endurance and balance).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Summer plan
*Student:* Write a 4-week summer plan with 60 minutes of activity daily using frequency, intensity, time and type.

*How to teach:* Teach FITT: Frequency (how often), Intensity (how hard), Time (how long), Type (what activity). Write a 4-week plan with 60 minutes daily.

- **Q:** What does FITT stand for?
  **A:** Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type.
- **Q:** Example plan entry using FITT.
  **A:** E.g., 'Bike (type) 5 days a week (frequency) at a moderate pace (intensity) for 30 minutes (time).'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Try something new
*Student:* Try a new activity (hiking trail, swimming, skating) and log heart rate.

*How to teach:* Try a new activity with safety gear and supervision; log heart rate.

- **Q:** What safety rule matters most for swimming?
  **A:** Always swim with an adult watching (lifeguard or parent) and stay where you can be supervised.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present your plan to family and post it on the fridge.

*How to teach:* Present the plan to family and post it on the fridge.

- **Q:** How many minutes of activity per week does 60 minutes a day add up to?
  **A:** 420 minutes (7 hours).

**Assessment:** Summer activity plan.

**Answer key:** Fitness components quiz key: 1) Five components — cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, body composition. 2) Test for cardiorespiratory endurance — PACER or mile. 3) Test for flexibility — sit-and-reach (or trunk lift/shoulder stretch). 4) Curl-ups measure — abdominal strength and endurance. 5) 25 beats in 15 s = 100 bpm. 6) Why heart rate rises — muscles need more oxygen. 7) FITT — Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type. Proficient = 6 of 7. Summer plan rubric: 4 = 4 weeks with 60 min/day, all FITT parts, at least 4 activity types covering multiple components; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = incomplete; 1 = missing.

## Health 4: Creating the campaign

**Prepare:** Poster paper, markers, a phone or tablet for video. Reliable source websites open for fact-checking.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a slogan must include all the facts. A slogan is short and catchy; the facts go on the poster or in the video.
- Skipping fact-checking because the information 'sounds right.' Every number must match a reliable source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Slogan
*Student:* Write a catchy slogan and a one-sentence key message for the campaign and test it on a family member.

*How to teach:* Write a short catchy slogan and a one-sentence key message. Test it on a family member: can they repeat it?

- **Q:** What makes a good slogan?
  **A:** Short, catchy, easy to remember, and clearly connected to the healthy behavior (e.g., 'Sip Smart — Choose Water!').

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Poster
*Student:* Create a campaign poster with the slogan, three facts with sources and a clear visual.

*How to teach:* Create the poster: slogan, 3 facts with sources, a clear visual, and a call to action.

- **Q:** Why include sources on the poster?
  **A:** So people know the facts are trustworthy and can check them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Video or skit
*Student:* Record a 1-minute public service video or rehearse a short skit that models the healthy behavior.

*How to teach:* Record a 1-minute PSA video or rehearse a short skit that models the healthy behavior.

- **Q:** Why model the behavior instead of just telling it?
  **A:** Seeing someone do it shows the audience how and makes it more memorable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fact check
*Student:* Fact-check every statistic and claim against KidsHealth, MyPlate, FDA or NIDA and correct any errors.

*How to teach:* Fact-check every statistic and claim against KidsHealth, MyPlate, FDA or NIDA; fix errors.

- **Q:** You wrote 'Kids need 30 minutes of activity a day.' Is it correct?
  **A:** No — kids need at least 60 minutes a day (CDC). Correct it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show the materials to two people, collect one suggestion from each, and revise the poster and video.

*How to teach:* Show materials to 2 people, collect one suggestion from each, and revise.

- **Q:** What is one revision you made and why?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g., 'I made the slogan bigger because my grandpa couldn't read it from far away.'

**Assessment:** Campaign materials.

**Answer key:** Campaign materials rubric: 4 = catchy slogan and key message; poster with slogan, 3 fact-checked facts with sources, strong visual and call to action; 1-minute video or skit that models the behavior; revisions from 2 people documented; 3 = all products with one weak element; 2 = missing a product or inaccurate fact; 1 = incomplete.
