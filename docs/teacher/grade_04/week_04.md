# Teacher's Guide — 4th Grade, Week 4

## ELA 4: Revising, publishing and theme

**Prepare:** Just So Stories ('The Cat That Walked by Himself' and 'How the Rhinoceros Got His Skin' for the reading check), editing checklist, computer and printer. Prepare a 5-question reading check on the Rhinoceros story (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Stating a topic instead of a theme ('independence'). A theme is a full sentence lesson: 'Being independent can mean being lonely, but it lets you live by your own rules.'
- Including small details or opinions in a summary. A summary tells only the most important events in order, in the reader's own words.

### Day 1 (Monday): Theme
*Student:* Read 'The Cat That Walked by Himself' and determine a theme supported by two details; write a 5-sentence summary.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Cat That Walked by Himself.' Help the child find a theme and two supporting details, then write a 5-sentence summary.

- **Q:** What bargain does the Cat make with the Woman?
  **A:** If she ever says one word in his praise he may come into the Cave; with a second, sit by the fire; with a third, drink the warm white milk. He earns all three by amusing the Baby and catching a mouse.
- **Q:** State a theme with evidence.
  **A:** Example: Independence has a cost — the Cat keeps his freedom ('all places are alike to me'), but Man and Dog never make a bargain with him, so they chase him and throw things at him.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise the narrative using a checklist: strong lead, clear sequence, dialogue, satisfying ending.

*How to teach:* The child rereads the narrative with the checklist (strong lead, clear sequence, dialogue, satisfying ending) and makes at least 3 revisions in colored pencil.

- **Q:** What makes a strong lead?
  **A:** It grabs the reader immediately — action, dialogue, a sound or a question — instead of 'One day I...'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Edit for capitals, commas, quotation marks and spelling with a parent conference.

*How to teach:* Conference: the child reads aloud while you both look for capitals, commas, quotation marks and spelling; the child makes the corrections.

- **Q:** Correct: "can we go to the beach" asked lily.
  **A:** "Can we go to the beach?" asked Lily.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish
*Student:* Type the final narrative (at least one page) and add a title.

*How to teach:* The child types the final narrative (at least one page) with a title, then proofreads once aloud.

- **Q:** Why read your final copy aloud?
  **A:** You hear missing words and awkward sentences that eyes skip over.

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebrate & assess
*Student:* Read the narrative aloud to family; take the Unit 1 reading check on a new Just So story.

*How to teach:* The child reads the narrative to family. Then give the reading check on 'How the Rhinoceros Got His Skin.'

- **Q:** In 'How the Rhinoceros Got His Skin,' why does the Parsee put cake crumbs in the Rhinoceros's skin?
  **A:** To get revenge because the Rhinoceros ate his cake; the crumbs make the skin itch so badly the Rhino rubs it into folds.

**Assessment:** Published personal narrative (rubric) and Unit 1 reading check.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric (4 levels): 4 = engaging lead, events in clear order with transitions, dialogue and sensory detail, reflective ending, few errors; 3 = all elements present, some errors; 2 = missing two elements or many errors; 1 = incomplete. Reading check key ('How the Rhinoceros Got His Skin'): 1) Setting: an uninhabited island on the Red Sea. 2) The Rhinoceros upsets the Parsee's stove and eats his cake. 3) Revenge: when the Rhino takes off his skin to bathe, the Parsee fills it with cake crumbs (and burnt currants). 4) Result: the Rhino rubs until the skin folds and the buttons come off, giving him folds and a bad temper. 5) It is a 'how' (origin) story explaining a feature of an animal. Proficient: 4/5.

## Math 4: Rounding multi-digit numbers

**Prepare:** Number line strips, place value chart. Approximate stadium attendance cards: Dodger Stadium game 43,651; Levi's Stadium 68,500; Oracle Park 41,915; Petco Park 40,209. Make a review game board or question cards for Day 4 and a written Unit 1 test for Day 5 (key below).

**Watch for:**
- Rounding 4,850 to the nearest hundred as 4,800 — the 5 rule: if the digit to the right is 5 or more, round up.
- Changing every digit when rounding (648,372 to nearest ten thousand written as 650,372). After rounding, every place to the right becomes 0.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rounding on a number line
*Student:* Round 3- and 4-digit numbers to the nearest ten and hundred by marking the two benchmark numbers and halfway point.

*How to teach:* Draw a number line for 367: mark 360 and 370 and the halfway point 365, then ask where 367 sits. The child rounds 10 numbers to the nearest ten and hundred using lines, then without them.

- **Q:** Round 367 to the nearest ten and nearest hundred.
  **A:** 370 (it is past 365); 400 (it is past 350).
- **Q:** Round 4,850 to the nearest hundred.
  **A:** 4,900 — it is exactly halfway, and halfway rounds up.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Round to any place
*Student:* Round 6-digit numbers to the nearest thousand, ten thousand and hundred thousand (15 problems).

*How to teach:* Model: underline the rounding place, look at the digit to its right, then round and fill zeros. The child completes 15 problems; check the first 3 before continuing.

- **Q:** Round 648,372 to the nearest thousand, ten thousand and hundred thousand.
  **A:** 648,000; 650,000; 600,000.
- **Q:** Round 295,600 to the nearest ten thousand.
  **A:** 300,000 (9 ten-thousands round up to 30 ten-thousands).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Estimation in context
*Student:* Round attendance at four California stadiums to the nearest thousand and estimate their total.

*How to teach:* Have the child round each stadium attendance to the nearest thousand and add the rounded numbers. Discuss when an estimate is good enough (planning, news headlines).

- **Q:** Estimate 43,651 + 41,915 by rounding to the nearest thousand.
  **A:** 44,000 + 42,000 = 86,000.
- **Q:** Round 40,209 to the nearest thousand.
  **A:** 40,000.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Unit 1 review
*Student:* Play a review game mixing number forms, comparison, ten-times reasoning and rounding.

*How to teach:* Play the review game, mixing cards on number forms, comparison, 'ten times' and rounding. For each miss, have the child explain the correct answer before moving on.

- **Q:** Write eight hundred four thousand, sixty in standard form.
  **A:** 804,060.
- **Q:** The 7 in 7,000 is how many times the 7 in 700?
  **A:** 10 times.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on place value, comparison and rounding.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test independently. Score it and have the child correct misses; reteach the weakest skill next week during warm-ups.

- **Q:** Round 85,499 to the nearest thousand.
  **A:** 85,000.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test (place value, comparison, rounding).

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test sample key: 1) 306,402 in word form: three hundred six thousand, four hundred two. 2) Expanded form of 58,217: 50,000 + 8,000 + 200 + 10 + 7. 3) Value of 6 in 462,018: 60,000. 4) The 3 in 30,000 is 10 times the 3 in 3,000. 5) 74,312 > 74,231. 6) Order 9,580; 9,058; 9,805 → 9,058 < 9,580 < 9,805. 7) Round 6,749 to nearest hundred: 6,700. 8) Round 351,826 to nearest ten thousand: 350,000. 9) Round 351,826 to nearest hundred thousand: 400,000. 10) Round 2,498 to nearest thousand: 2,000. Proficient = 8/10.

## Science 4: Energy in motion: Newton's cradle and roller coasters

**Prepare:** String, 5-6 metal washers, a ruler or stick for a frame, foam pipe insulation split in half (hardware store), marbles, tape, cups. Bookmark PhET 'Energy Skate Park: Basics' (free, phet.colorado.edu). Unit 1 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a marble can climb a hill higher than where it started. Without a push, it can't go higher than its starting height because some energy changes to heat and sound.
- Believing the pendulum chain moves the same balls back and forth. Energy passes through the middle balls to the last one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Newton's cradle
*Student:* Build a hanging-washer pendulum chain from string and observe energy transfer.

*How to teach:* Hang 5 washers side by side by strings so they just touch. Pull back one and release; observe the far washer swing out.

- **Q:** Why does the washer at the far end move?
  **A:** The energy of the first washer transfers through the middle ones to the last one.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Roller coaster design
*Student:* Build a foam pipe insulation marble coaster and measure speed at the bottom.

*How to teach:* Build a foam-tube coaster taped to a wall or chair; measure the marble's speed at the bottom (time over a fixed distance).

- **Q:** Where on the coaster is the marble moving fastest?
  **A:** At the bottom of the biggest drop.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Loops and hills
*Student:* Test whether the marble makes it over hills of different heights and explain using energy.

*How to teach:* Add hills of different heights after the first drop and test which the marble clears.

- **Q:** Can the marble go over a hill taller than its starting point? Why?
  **A:** No — it only has the energy from its starting height, and some changes to heat/sound from friction, so it can't climb higher than it started.

### Day 4 (Thursday): PhET simulation
*Student:* Use Energy Skate Park Basics to observe speed and height relationships.

*How to teach:* Use the Energy Skate Park simulation: change the starting height and watch speed.

- **Q:** In the simulation, where is the skater fastest?
  **A:** At the lowest point of the track.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 assessment
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on speed, energy and collisions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** Name two forms energy can change into during a collision.
  **A:** Sound and heat (also motion of the other object).

**Assessment:** Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test key: 1) Energy is the ability to cause motion or change 2) Faster objects have more energy 3) Higher ramp → faster marble → more energy → pushes cup farther 4) In a collision energy transfers to the other object 5) Collisions also produce sound and heat 6) Stored energy examples: stretched rubber band, lifted ball, wound spring 7) A coaster's marble is fastest at the bottom 8) It can't go higher than its start height. Proficient: 80%.

## California, A Changing State: Comparing California communities

**Prepare:** Photos of Los Angeles, Fresno suburbs and a Modoc County ranch; county population density chart (U.S. Census/QuickFacts); Calisphere (calisphere.org) set 'City Scenes in California History'; poster board. Unit 1 test for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking rural means poor or 'less important.' Rural areas grow much of the food and resources cities depend on.
- Confusing population with density. Density is people per square mile.

### Day 1 (Monday): Urban, suburban, rural
*Student:* Compare photos of Los Angeles, Fresno suburbs and a Modoc County ranch and record land use, architecture and transportation.

*How to teach:* Compare the photos for land use, architecture and transportation.

- **Q:** How does transportation differ between Los Angeles and a Modoc County ranch?
  **A:** LA has freeways, buses and trains with heavy traffic; rural Modoc has few roads, long distances and people drive trucks.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Population data
*Student:* Read a population density chart and rank 6 counties.

*How to teach:* Read the density chart and rank 6 counties.

- **Q:** Which county is California's most populous?
  **A:** Los Angeles County (about 10 million people).
- **Q:** What does population density mean?
  **A:** How many people live in each square mile.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Primary source photos
*Student:* Analyze 3 images from the Calisphere set 'City Scenes in California History' and describe what changed and stayed the same.

*How to teach:* Analyze 3 Calisphere images: what changed and what stayed the same?

- **Q:** What questions help analyze a historical photo?
  **A:** Who/what is shown, when and where was it taken, what has changed since then, what has stayed the same.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Community profile
*Student:* Create a community profile poster for your town: climate, land use, services and transportation.

*How to teach:* Create a community profile poster.

- **Q:** What services does a community provide?
  **A:** Schools, libraries, police, fire, parks, water and trash.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 test
*Student:* Take the Unit 1 test on geography, regions and communities.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test.

- **Q:** Name an urban, a suburban and a rural community.
  **A:** Urban: Los Angeles or San Francisco; suburban: neighborhoods around Fresno or Sacramento; rural: a Modoc County ranch area.

**Assessment:** Community profile and Unit 1 test.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test key: 1) Latitude/longitude definitions 2) California in Northern and Western Hemispheres 3) Four regions 4) Capital Sacramento 5) Sierra Nevada, Central Valley, Mojave located on map 6) Rivers meet in the Delta; Sacramento + American Rivers at Sacramento 7) Passes help travel through mountains 8) Urban/suburban/rural definitions 9) Population density = people per square mile 10) Why one city grew where it did. Community profile rubric: 4 = climate, land use, services, transportation with accurate details and visuals; 3 = 3 areas; 2 = 2; 1 = incomplete. Proficient: 80%.

## Art & Music 4: Drawing critique and revision

**Prepare:** Gather drawings from weeks 1–3 and the sketchbook. Choose a household object for the final drawing (a sneaker, teapot or stuffed animal).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking feedback means you did something wrong. Feedback helps you improve.
- Revising by erasing everything. Revise by adding to what's there (more value, more detail).

### Day 1 (Monday): Criteria
*Student:* Make a 4-point checklist for a strong drawing (fills page, line variety, value range, detail).

*How to teach:* Make a 4-point checklist: fills page, line variety, value range, detail. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** Why make criteria before judging?
  **A:** So the judgment is fair and specific.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Critique
*Student:* Discuss two of your drawings with a parent using 'I notice / I wonder / I suggest'.

*How to teach:* Discuss two drawings using 'I notice / I wonder / I suggest.' Pause often to have your child explain what they see, hear or read in their own words, and write down two or three key points together.

- **Q:** Give an 'I suggest' comment.
  **A:** 'I suggest darkening the shadow under the mug so it looks heavier.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise
*Student:* Revise one drawing based on feedback, adding value or detail.

*How to teach:* Revise one drawing based on feedback. Demonstrate the first step briefly, then let your child work independently; coach with one specific tip at a time rather than fixing the work for them.

- **Q:** What did you change and why?
  **A:** Answer varies; should name a criterion (e.g., more value).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final drawing
*Student:* Create a finished observational drawing of a household object using all skills.

*How to teach:* Create a finished observational drawing using all skills. Show a quick example, watch your child's first few minutes of work, then step back and check in with one specific suggestion.

- **Q:** Which skills should the final drawing show?
  **A:** Contour, line variety, value shading, detail, filling the page.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & share
*Student:* Self-assess the final drawing with the checklist and display it.

*How to teach:* Self-assess with the checklist and display. Go through it once together, then a second time while your child notes what they observe; prompt with 'What do you notice? What makes you say that?'

- **Q:** Which criterion is your strongest?
  **A:** Answer varies; should cite evidence from the drawing.

**Assessment:** Final drawing scored with criteria.

**Answer key:** Final drawing scored 1–4 on each criterion: fills page, line variety, value range (at least 5 values), detail. Proficient = 12/16 or higher.

## PE 4: Overhand throw mechanics

**Prepare:** Tennis balls (10–30), a taped wall target (about 2 feet square), a 15-foot throwing line, open field for distance, measuring tape, phone for slow-motion video. Print a 5-cue checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Stepping with the throwing-side foot. Step with the opposite foot to allow hip rotation and more force.
- Throwing 'all arm' while facing the target. Start sideways and rotate the hips and shoulders toward the target.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cues
*Student:* Learn the cues: side to target, arm back, step with opposite foot, rotate hips, follow through.

*How to teach:* Teach 5 cues: side to target, arm back (making an 'L'), step with opposite foot, rotate hips (and shoulders), follow through across the body. Shadow-throw 20 times.

- **Q:** List the 5 overhand throw cues.
  **A:** Side to target, arm back, step with opposite foot, rotate hips, follow through.
- **Q:** A left-handed thrower steps with which foot?
  **A:** The right foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wall throws
*Student:* Throw 30 balls at a taped wall target from 15 feet, focusing on stepping.

*How to teach:* Throw 30 balls at the wall target from 15 feet, focusing on a big step with the opposite foot. Tally hits.

- **Q:** You hit 18 of 30. How many missed?
  **A:** 12.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Video check
*Student:* Record your throw in slow motion and check each cue.

*How to teach:* Record a throw in slow motion. Pause the video at each stage and check off each cue.

- **Q:** What should the video show at release?
  **A:** Opposite foot stepped forward, hips and shoulders turned toward the target, arm coming through high.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Distance throws
*Student:* Throw for distance 10 times and measure the best three.

*How to teach:* Throw for distance 10 times; measure the best three.

- **Q:** Best three throws: 62, 58, 65 feet. What's the average?
  **A:** (62 + 58 + 65) ÷ 3 = 185 ÷ 3 ≈ 61.7 feet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate a throw while a parent scores the 5 cues.

*How to teach:* Parent scores 5 throws on the 5 cues.

- **Q:** Why does hip rotation add force?
  **A:** Turning the hips and shoulders uses the big muscles of the body to whip the arm forward faster.

**Assessment:** Throwing cue checklist.

**Answer key:** Throwing cue checklist: score 5 throws × 5 cues (side to target, arm back, opposite-foot step, hip rotation, follow through) = 25 points. 4 = 22–25; 3 = 17–21; 2 = 11–16; 1 = 0–10.

## Health 4: Nutrients: carbohydrates, proteins and fats

**Prepare:** Collect food packages (bread, cereal, peanut butter, cheese, crackers, oil) and pictures of foods; chart paper. Have a white bread and a 100% whole-wheat bread label.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all fat is bad. The body needs some fat; unsaturated fats (avocados, nuts, olive oil, fish) are healthier choices than lots of saturated fat and fried foods.
- Thinking carbohydrates are only sugar. Carbs include sugars, starches and fiber; whole grains, fruits and vegetables are healthy carb sources.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nutrient jobs
*Student:* Learn what carbohydrates, proteins and fats do and make a 3-column chart.

*How to teach:* Teach the jobs: carbohydrates = main energy source; proteins = build and repair muscles, skin and other tissues; fats = stored energy, help absorb some vitamins, protect organs and keep you warm. Make a 3-column chart with jobs and example foods.

- **Q:** What is the main job of carbohydrates?
  **A:** To give the body (and brain) energy.
- **Q:** What is the job of protein?
  **A:** To build and repair muscles, skin and other body tissues.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Food sort
*Student:* Sort 20 food pictures or packages by their main macronutrient.

*How to teach:* Sort 20 foods by main macronutrient: carbs (bread, rice, pasta, fruit, potatoes, oatmeal), protein (chicken, fish, eggs, beans, tofu), fats (butter, oil, avocado, nuts — note nuts also have protein).

- **Q:** Which macronutrient is rice mainly?
  **A:** Carbohydrate.
- **Q:** Which macronutrient are eggs mainly?
  **A:** Protein (they also have some fat).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Whole grains
*Student:* Compare white and whole-wheat bread labels and explain why whole grains give longer-lasting energy.

*How to teach:* Compare the two bread labels: whole wheat lists 'whole wheat flour' first and has more fiber. Explain fiber slows digestion, so energy lasts longer and you feel full.

- **Q:** Why do whole grains give longer-lasting energy?
  **A:** They keep the bran and germ, which have fiber; fiber slows digestion so energy is released slowly and you stay full longer.
- **Q:** How can you tell bread is whole grain on the label?
  **A:** The first ingredient says 'whole' (e.g., whole wheat flour).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Healthy fats
*Student:* Learn the difference between fats in avocados and nuts and fats in fried foods.

*How to teach:* Compare healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil, fish — mostly unsaturated) with fats in fried foods and many baked snacks (more saturated and sometimes trans fats).

- **Q:** Which is a healthier fat source: avocado or french fries?
  **A:** Avocado — it has unsaturated fat and nutrients; fries are fried and high in less healthy fat and salt.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a quiz matching carbohydrates, proteins and fats to their jobs and to 10 example foods.

*How to teach:* Quiz: match carbs/proteins/fats to their jobs and to 10 foods (see key).

- **Q:** Which macronutrient protects organs and helps absorb some vitamins?
  **A:** Fat.

**Assessment:** Macronutrient quiz.

**Answer key:** Macronutrient quiz key: Jobs — carbohydrates: main energy source; proteins: build and repair body tissues; fats: stored energy, protect organs, help absorb vitamins A, D, E, K. Foods — 1) bread: carbohydrate 2) chicken: protein 3) olive oil: fat 4) banana: carbohydrate 5) beans: protein (also carbs/fiber) 6) butter: fat 7) pasta: carbohydrate 8) fish: protein 9) avocado: fat 10) oatmeal: carbohydrate. Proficient = 11 of 13.
