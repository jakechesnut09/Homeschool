# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 18

## ELA 3: Poetry elements: stanza, rhythm, rhyme

**Prepare:** Print 'My Shadow' and 'The Swing' from Stevenson's A Child's Garden of Verses (Project Gutenberg); recording device.

**Watch for:**
- Calling stanzas 'paragraphs' — poems are made of lines grouped into stanzas.
- Reading poems in a sing-song way — pause at punctuation, not automatically at each line end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Stanzas
*Student:* Read Stevenson's 'My Shadow' and label lines and stanzas; describe what each stanza adds.

*How to teach:* Read 'My Shadow' aloud twice. Number the lines and bracket the stanzas. Ask what each stanza adds to the picture of the shadow.

- **Q:** How many stanzas and lines per stanza does 'My Shadow' have?
  **A:** 4 stanzas of 4 lines each.
- **Q:** What happens to the shadow in the last stanza?
  **A:** Early in the morning, before the sun is up, the speaker goes out and the 'lazy' shadow has stayed home 'fast asleep in bed.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rhythm
*Student:* Clap the rhythm of 'The Swing' and practice reading it aloud with expression; spell VCCV multisyllable words.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Swing' and clap the beat together. The child practices reading it aloud with expression three times. Then divide and spell VCCV words between the two consonants (nap-kin).

- **Q:** How does the speaker feel about swinging?
  **A:** He loves it — he calls it 'the pleasantest thing ever a child can do.'
- **Q:** Divide 'napkin' and 'basket' into syllables.
  **A:** nap-kin, bas-ket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Literal vs. nonliteral
*Student:* Find nonliteral phrases in poems and draw literal vs. real meanings.

*How to teach:* Explain literal (exactly what words say) vs. nonliteral (a creative meaning). The child finds 2 nonliteral lines and draws a silly literal picture and a real-meaning picture for each.

- **Q:** Is the shadow really 'fast asleep in bed'? What does it mean?
  **A:** No — the sun wasn't up yet, so there was no shadow to be seen.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write a poem
*Student:* Write a rhyming poem in 2 stanzas about a shadow or swing.

*How to teach:* The child writes a rhyming poem of 2 stanzas (4 lines each) about a shadow or a swing, then reads it aloud. Model a quick couplet first ('My shadow runs beside me / as quickly as can be'), then let the child write.

- **Q:** What separates stanzas on the page?
  **A:** A blank line.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record a fluent reading of a Stevenson poem and complete a poetry terms check.

*How to teach:* Record the child reading a Stevenson poem fluently and give the poetry terms check. Play the recording back and have the child name one thing to improve.

- **Q:** Define rhythm in a poem.
  **A:** The beat, or pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.

**Assessment:** Poetry terms check; fluency recording

**Answer key:** Terms check: line → one row of words in a poem; stanza → a group of lines; rhyme → words ending with the same sound; rhythm → the beat; speaker → the voice talking in the poem; poet → the writer. Proficient 5+/6. Fluency: 3 = smooth, expressive, pauses at punctuation; 2 = some choppy spots; 1 = word-by-word.

## Math 3: Two-step word problems with four operations

**Prepare:** Bar model templates; a pretend party price list (plates $4 per pack of 10, cups $3 per pack of 8, $40 to spend). Write the quiz in the answer key.

**Watch for:**
- Stopping after step one — underline the question and check that the final answer answers it.
- Using the wrong operation in step two — draw a bar model for each step.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the steps
*Student:* Underline the question and plan both steps for 5 two-step problems before solving.

*How to teach:* Read a two-step problem aloud, underline the question, and write 'Step 1: find …, Step 2: find …' before doing any math. The child plans 5 problems, then solves them.

- **Q:** Mia has 3 packs of 8 pencils and gives away 5. How many are left?
  **A:** Step 1: 3 × 8 = 24. Step 2: 24 − 5 = 19 pencils.
- **Q:** Tom buys 4 books at $6 each and pays with $30. How much change?
  **A:** 4 × 6 = 24; 30 − 24 = $6.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Equations with letters
*Student:* Write a single equation with a letter for the unknown for each two-step problem.

*How to teach:* Write a single equation with a letter for the unknown, e.g., p = 3 × 8 − 5, doing multiplication or division before adding or subtracting (use parentheses to make it clear). The child writes and solves 5.

- **Q:** Write and solve an equation: 5 bags of 6 apples, then 7 apples are eaten.
  **A:** a = 5 × 6 − 7; a = 23.
- **Q:** Solve n = 48 ÷ 6 + 9.
  **A:** n = 8 + 9 = 17.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Estimate first
*Student:* Estimate answers with rounding and mental math before solving; check reasonableness.

*How to teach:* Before solving, round and estimate; after solving, compare. The child writes 'reasonable' or 'not reasonable' for each.

- **Q:** Estimate, then solve: 198 + 315 − 107.
  **A:** Estimate 200 + 300 − 100 = 400; exact 406 — reasonable.
- **Q:** Lee says 7 × 9 − 20 = 83. Is that reasonable?
  **A:** No; 7 × 9 = 63, and 63 − 20 = 43.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Party planning
*Student:* Solve a multi-step party budget problem (packs of plates, cost, change).

*How to teach:* Pose the party problem: 24 guests, plates in packs of 10 for $4, cups in packs of 8 for $3, and $40 to spend. The child finds the packs needed, the cost and the change.

- **Q:** How many packs of 10 plates are needed for 24 guests, and what do they cost?
  **A:** 3 packs (2 packs is only 20 plates) — $12.
- **Q:** How many packs of 8 cups for 24 guests, what do they cost, and how much change is left from $40 after plates and cups?
  **A:** 3 packs = $9; $12 + $9 = $21; change is $19.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a two-step problem quiz and explain one solution with a bar model.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and have the child explain one solution with a bar model. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Jen reads 15 pages a day for 4 days, then 12 more pages. How many pages in all?
  **A:** 60 + 12 = 72 pages.

**Assessment:** Two-step problem quiz

**Answer key:** 1) 6 boxes of 8 crayons, 9 lost → 39 2) $50 minus 3 games at $9 each → $23 3) 36 kids in 4 equal teams, then 2 more kids join each team → 11 per team 4) 125 + 248 − 90 → 283 5) 7 rows of 6 chairs, 12 empty → 30 filled 6) 45 ÷ 5 + 18 → 27. Proficient: 5+/6 and a bar-model explanation with both steps labeled.

## Science 3: What fossils tell us

**Prepare:** Clay or salt dough, shells or leaves, plaster of Paris (adult handles mixing; avoid breathing dust) or extra dough, petroleum jelly, a short fossil formation video, Smithsonian or La Brea Tar Pits website images.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fossils are actual bones — most are minerals that replaced the original material, or impressions.
- Thinking fossils form quickly — it takes thousands to millions of years.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fossil formation
*Student:* Watch a fossil video and sequence the steps of fossil formation.

*How to teach:* Watch a short video. The child sequences: organism dies → buried quickly in sediment → soft parts rot → minerals replace hard parts over a very long time → rock forms → erosion exposes the fossil.

- **Q:** Why must an organism be buried quickly to become a fossil?
  **A:** So it isn't eaten or rotted away before sediment covers and protects it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Make fossils
*Student:* Make imprint fossils by pressing shells into clay; fill with plaster to make casts.

*How to teach:* Press a shell into clay to make a mold. Coat lightly with petroleum jelly and fill with plaster to make a cast; let it dry overnight.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a mold and a cast?
  **A:** A mold is the hollow imprint; a cast is the filled-in copy of the shape.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fossil types
*Student:* Compare body fossils, trace fossils and imprints using Smithsonian images.

*How to teach:* Compare body fossils (bones, teeth, shells), trace fossils (footprints, burrows) and imprints using museum images.

- **Q:** Is a dinosaur footprint a body or trace fossil?
  **A:** A trace fossil — it shows the animal's activity, not its body.

### Day 4 (Thursday): La Brea
*Student:* Learn about the La Brea Tar Pits and the animals found there.

*How to teach:* Learn about the La Brea Tar Pits in Los Angeles and the Ice Age animals trapped there.

- **Q:** Name two animals found at La Brea.
  **A:** Saber-toothed cats, dire wolves, mammoths and giant ground sloths.
- **Q:** How did animals get trapped?
  **A:** They got stuck in sticky asphalt (tar) seeps and were preserved.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how fossils form in a comic strip.

*How to teach:* The child draws a comic strip of at least 5 panels showing how a fossil forms.

- **Q:** What is the last step before people find a fossil?
  **A:** Erosion or digging exposes it at the surface.

**Assessment:** Fossil formation comic strip

**Answer key:** Comic strip rubric: 4 = 5+ panels in correct order (death, quick burial, decay of soft parts, mineral replacement over long time, exposure) with captions; 3 = 4 correct steps; 2 = steps missing or out of order; 1 = inaccurate.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Oral history

**Prepare:** A recording device (phone), an interview question sheet; arrange a time with an older relative or neighbor.

**Watch for:**
- Asking yes/no questions. Open-ended questions ('What was school like?') get stories.
- Interrupting. Listen fully, then ask a follow-up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Questions
*Student:* Write 10 open-ended interview questions for an older relative or neighbor about how the community has changed.

*How to teach:* The child writes 10 open-ended questions.

- **Q:** Change 'Did you like your town?' into an open-ended question.
  **A:** Example: 'What did you like most about the town when you were growing up?'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interview
*Student:* Conduct and record the interview, listening and asking follow-up questions.

*How to teach:* Conduct and record the interview with permission.

- **Q:** Why ask permission to record?
  **A:** It's respectful and the person has the right to decide.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Notes
*Student:* Listen to the recording and take notes on the 5 most important details.

*How to teach:* Listen to the recording and note the 5 most important details.

- **Q:** What makes a detail 'important'?
  **A:** It tells how the community was or how it changed, or is a meaningful personal story.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Share 3 interesting stories from the interview with family.

*How to teach:* Share 3 stories at dinner.

- **Q:** Is an oral history a primary or secondary source?
  **A:** Primary; it's a firsthand account.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a one-page summary of the oral history and add key events to the timeline.

*How to teach:* The child writes the summary and adds events to the timeline.

- **Q:** What is one way the community changed according to your interview?
  **A:** Answers vary by community; answer cites the interviewee.

**Assessment:** Oral history summary

**Answer key:** Oral history summary rubric: 4 = one page naming who was interviewed, when, and their connection to the community; 5 important details in the child's own words; at least one comparison of then and now; key events added to the timeline; 3 = 3–4 details; 2 = 1–2 details; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Texture

**Prepare:** Crayons with paper peeled off, thin copy paper, textured objects (leaves, coins, window screen, bark, sandpaper, basket, sole of a shoe). Pull up Albrecht Dürer's 'Young Hare' (1502, Albertina Museum, Vienna). Scissors, glue, background paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking texture is only something you touch. Actual texture can be felt; visual (implied) texture is drawn or painted to look like it would feel a certain way.
- Pressing too hard or using the crayon tip for rubbings. Use the side of a peeled crayon and rub evenly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rubbings
*Student:* Make crayon rubbings of 10 textured surfaces (leaves, coins, screens, bark).

*How to teach:* Place copy paper over each textured surface and rub with the side of a peeled crayon. Collect 10 rubbings and label each.

- **Q:** What is texture?
  **A:** How something feels or looks like it would feel (rough, smooth, bumpy, fuzzy).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Visual texture
*Student:* Draw 6 textures (fur, bark, scales, feathers, brick, water) in a sketchbook grid.

*How to teach:* Make a 6-box grid in the sketchbook and draw visual texture for fur (short flicking lines), bark (long wavy lines and cracks), scales (overlapping U-shapes), feathers, brick and water.

- **Q:** What is the difference between actual and visual texture?
  **A:** Actual texture can be felt with your hands; visual texture is drawn to look rough, smooth, etc., but feels flat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dürer
*Student:* Look closely at Albrecht Dürer's 'Young Hare' and speculate how he painted each hair.

*How to teach:* Look closely at 'Young Hare.' Explain Dürer painted it in watercolor and gouache over 500 years ago. Ask how he made each hair (tiny fine brushstrokes in different directions, light and dark).

- **Q:** How did Dürer make the hare look furry?
  **A:** He painted thousands of tiny, fine brushstrokes in the direction the fur grows, using light and dark colors.
- **Q:** Is the hare's fur actual or visual texture?
  **A:** Visual texture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Texture collage
*Student:* Cut your rubbings into shapes to build a textured animal collage.

*How to teach:* Draw a simple animal outline on background paper. Cut the rubbings into shapes and glue them to build the animal (e.g., bark texture for legs, screen for body).

- **Q:** Why choose different rubbings for different parts?
  **A:** Different textures show different parts, like fur on the body and scales or rough skin on feet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Present the collage and name the texture used for each part.

*How to teach:* The child presents the collage and names the texture used for each part.

- **Q:** Which rubbing did you use for the body, and what real texture does it show?
  **A:** Answers vary, e.g., 'The basket rubbing looks like the turtle's patterned shell.'

**Assessment:** Texture animal collage

**Answer key:** Texture animal collage rubric: 4 = at least 5 different rubbings cut and arranged to form a recognizable animal, textures chosen to match parts, neat gluing, and the child names each texture; 3 = 3–4 textures; 2 = 2 textures or animal unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Kicking and passing

**Prepare:** Soccer ball, a small goal or two cones 6 feet apart for the target, 15-foot mark, open field.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking with the toe for passes. Pass with the inside of the foot for accuracy; shoot with the laces (top of the foot).
- Planting the support foot far behind the ball. Plant the non-kicking foot beside the ball, toe pointing to the target.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pass
*Student:* Make 30 inside-foot passes to a partner with the plant foot beside the ball.

*How to teach:* Teach the inside-foot pass: plant foot beside the ball pointing at the target, turn the kicking foot out, strike the middle of the ball with the inside, follow through. Make 30 passes with a partner about 15 feet apart.

- **Q:** Where does your plant foot go for a pass?
  **A:** Beside the ball, toes pointing toward your target.
- **Q:** Which part of the foot passes the ball?
  **A:** The inside of the foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Target
*Student:* Kick to a target between two cones from 15 feet; tally successes.

*How to teach:* From 15 feet, kick 20 passes through two cones 6 feet apart and tally successes. Narrow the gap if it's easy.

- **Q:** You got 14 of 20 through. How many missed?
  **A:** 6.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Shoot
*Student:* Shoot on goal using laces and follow-through.

*How to teach:* Teach the laces shot: approach at an angle, plant beside the ball, toe pointed down, strike with the laces and follow through toward the goal. Take 15 shots.

- **Q:** Which part of the foot do you shoot with?
  **A:** The laces (top of the foot) with the toe pointed down.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Moving pass
*Student:* Pass to a partner jogging across the field.

*How to teach:* Partner jogs across the field; the child passes ahead of them (lead pass) with the inside of the foot.

- **Q:** Where should you pass to a moving teammate?
  **A:** Into the space in front of them, so they can run onto it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a kicking check (pass accuracy and shot) scored on cues.

*How to teach:* Kicking check: 5 inside-foot passes through the cones and 5 laces shots on goal; score each on the cues.

- **Q:** Why is the inside of the foot more accurate than the toe?
  **A:** It's a bigger, flatter surface, so the ball goes where you aim.

**Assessment:** Kicking check

**Answer key:** Kicking check (score each of 10 kicks on cues: plant foot beside ball, correct foot surface, follow through toward target): 4 = 4–5 accurate passes and 3+ shots on goal with all cues; 3 = 3 accurate passes, 2 shots on goal; 2 = 1–2 passes accurate or toe-kicking; 1 = no accurate kicks.

## Health 3: Fire safety

**Prepare:** Graph paper and pencils; know where your smoke alarms are and how to test them; choose an outside meeting place (a mailbox or a tree). Parent note: keep the tone calm; focus on 'we have a plan.'

**Watch for:**
- Hiding from fire under a bed or in a closet. Get out and stay out; firefighters can't find you if you hide.
- Going back inside for pets or toys. Never go back in; tell firefighters who or what is still inside.
- Standing up and running through smoke. Smoke rises; crawl low where the air is cleaner.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fire rules
*Student:* Learn rules about matches, lighters, stoves and space heaters, and find hazards in a home picture.

*How to teach:* Teach rules: matches and lighters are tools for adults only; tell an adult if you find one; keep 3 feet away from stoves and space heaters. Child finds hazards in a home picture.

- **Q:** What should you do if you find matches or a lighter?
  **A:** Don't touch them; tell an adult right away.
- **Q:** How far should you stay from space heaters and stoves?
  **A:** At least 3 feet (a 'kid-free zone').

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Smoke alarms
*Student:* Test home smoke alarms with an adult and learn what the sound means.

*How to teach:* Push the test button on each smoke alarm with the child so they know the sound. Explain it means 'get out now.'

- **Q:** What should you do when you hear the smoke alarm?
  **A:** Get out of the house right away and go to the meeting place.
- **Q:** How often should smoke alarms be tested?
  **A:** Once a month.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Escape plan
*Student:* Draw a home floor plan with two exits from every room and an outside meeting place.

*How to teach:* The child draws the home's floor plan, marks two exits from each room and the meeting place.

- **Q:** Why do you need two ways out of every room?
  **A:** In case fire or smoke blocks one exit.
- **Q:** Where do you go once you are outside?
  **A:** To the family meeting place, and stay there.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fire drill
*Student:* Practice a home fire drill, crawling low under 'smoke' and timing how long it takes.

*How to teach:* Run a drill: sound the alarm, child crawls low, feels a door with the back of the hand, exits and goes to the meeting place. Time it.

- **Q:** Why crawl low in smoke?
  **A:** Smoke and heat rise, so cleaner, cooler air is near the floor.
- **Q:** What should you do if a door is hot?
  **A:** Don't open it; use your second way out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate stop, drop and roll and explain your escape plan.

*How to teach:* The child demonstrates stop, drop and roll and explains the escape plan.

- **Q:** If your clothes catch fire, what do you do?
  **A:** Stop, drop to the ground, cover your face, and roll until the fire is out.

**Assessment:** Escape plan and drill

**Answer key:** Escape plan and drill key: floor plan shows every room with two exits (door and window), arrows to exits, and a marked outside meeting place; the drill is completed with the child crawling low, feeling doors with the back of the hand before opening, and going straight to the meeting place (aim: out in under 2 minutes). Stop, drop and roll: stop where you are, drop to the ground, cover your face with your hands, roll back and forth until the flames are out. Proficient: all parts correct.
