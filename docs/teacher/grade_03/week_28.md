# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 28

## ELA 3: The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, chapters 19–24

**Prepare:** The Wonderful Wizard of Oz chapters 19–24 and a map of Oz (included in many editions or found online). Write the end-of-novel quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the silver shoes were useless — Glinda reveals they could have carried Dorothy home from the start.
- Leaving out apostrophes in contractions (dont, cant).

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 19–21
*Student:* Read chapters 19–21 and track the journey south on a map of Oz.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 19–21. The child traces the route south on the map and labels the Fighting Trees, the China Country and the forest.

- **Q:** Why do they travel south?
  **A:** To ask Glinda, the Good Witch of the South, for help getting Dorothy home.
- **Q:** How does the Lion become King of Beasts?
  **A:** He kills a giant spider that was terrorizing the forest animals.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 22–24
*Student:* Finish the novel and recount the main events in a summary.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 22–24. The child writes a 5–6 sentence summary of the main events of the whole novel.

- **Q:** How does Dorothy get home?
  **A:** Glinda tells her to knock her heels together three times and command the silver shoes; they carry her to Kansas (and are lost on the way).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spoken vs. written
*Student:* Rewrite informal spoken lines into formal written English and vice versa; spell contractions.

*How to teach:* Show the difference between informal spoken English and formal written English. The child rewrites 5 lines each way. Then spell contractions.

- **Q:** Make this formal: 'Gonna head home now.'
  **A:** I am going to go home now.
- **Q:** Write the contraction for 'do not' and 'I will'.
  **A:** don't, I'll.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a new Oz adventure for Dorothy with a new character and setting.

*How to teach:* The child plans a new Oz adventure for Dorothy with a story map that includes a new character and a new place in Oz. Encourage the child to borrow Oz places or characters but invent a new problem.

- **Q:** What should the plan include?
  **A:** Characters (including a new one), a new setting in Oz, a problem, events and a solution.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete an end-of-novel quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-novel quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What happens to the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman and Lion at the end?
  **A:** The Scarecrow rules the Emerald City, the Tin Woodman rules the Winkies, and the Lion rules the forest animals.

**Assessment:** End-of-novel quiz

**Answer key:** 1) What did Glinda reveal? → the silver shoes could have taken Dorothy home all along 2) How does the Lion become king? → kills the giant spider 3) What is the China Country like? → tiny, fragile people and animals made of china 4) Who rules the Emerald City? → the Scarecrow 5) What happens to the silver shoes? → they fall off and are lost in the desert 6) How does the book end? → Dorothy is home with Aunt Em. Proficient 5+/6.

## Math 3: Mass

**Prepare:** Kitchen scale (grams), paper clips, a 1 kg bag of rice or flour, a coat hanger with string and two paper cups for a balance. Write the Unit 7 test in the answer key.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bigger objects always have more mass — compare a large balloon with a small rock.
- Mixing up grams and kilograms — a paper clip is about 1 gram; a 1 kg bag of rice is 1,000 grams.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gram and kilogram
*Student:* Hold a paper clip (about 1 g) and a 1 kg bag of rice as benchmarks; estimate 8 objects.

*How to teach:* Hold a paper clip (about 1 g) and the 1 kg bag. Estimate 8 objects and say whether each is better measured in grams or kilograms.

- **Q:** Would you measure a pencil in grams or kilograms?
  **A:** Grams.
- **Q:** Would you measure a dog in grams or kilograms?
  **A:** Kilograms.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Weigh objects
*Student:* Use a kitchen scale to measure the mass of 8 objects and compare to estimates.

*How to teach:* Weigh 8 objects on the kitchen scale and compare with the estimates. Have the child record estimate and actual mass side by side and circle the closest estimate.

- **Q:** How many grams are in 1 kilogram?
  **A:** 1,000.
- **Q:** An apple is about 200 g. Is that more or less than 1 kg?
  **A:** Less.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Balance scale
*Student:* Build a hanger balance and compare masses of two objects.

*How to teach:* Build a hanger balance with a cup on each end and compare two objects; the lower side is heavier. Ask the child to predict which side will go down before placing each pair.

- **Q:** Which side of a balance goes down?
  **A:** The side with more mass.
- **Q:** If the balance is level, what do you know?
  **A:** The two masses are equal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mass problems
*Student:* Solve one-step word problems about mass of fruit and packages.

*How to teach:* Solve one-step mass word problems. Have the child draw a simple picture and write an equation with units for each problem.

- **Q:** 3 bags of apples have a mass of 4 kg each. What is the total mass?
  **A:** 12 kg.
- **Q:** A box has a mass of 45 kg. 18 kg is removed. What is its mass now?
  **A:** 27 kg.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 7 test on time, liquid volume and mass.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Is 500 grams more or less than 1 kilogram?
  **A:** Less — it is half a kilogram.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test: 1) Read 2:38 2) 3:15 to 3:55 → 40 min 3) Starts 5:40, lasts 35 min → 6:15 4) More or less than 1 L: drinking glass → less; bathtub → more 5) 6 jugs × 2 L → 12 L 6) 24 L ÷ 4 → 6 L 7) Grams or kilograms: paper clip → grams; bicycle → kilograms 8) 9 kg + 6 kg → 15 kg 9) 50 g − 18 g → 32 g 10) 4 bags × 5 kg → 20 kg. Proficient: 8+/10.

## Science 3: Climates of the world

**Prepare:** World map, colored pencils, NASA Climate Kids (climatekids.nasa.gov), yearly temperature and rainfall for Anchorage, Singapore and Cairo.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing weather and climate — weather is what's happening now; climate is the usual pattern over many years.
- Thinking deserts are always hot — a desert is defined by very little precipitation; some deserts are cold.

### Day 1 (Monday): Weather vs. climate
*Student:* Sort 10 statements as weather (today's rain) or climate (rainy winters every year) and explain the difference.

*How to teach:* Read 10 statements aloud. The child sorts them into weather or climate and explains each.

- **Q:** Is 'It is raining today' weather or climate?
  **A:** Weather.
- **Q:** Is 'Seattle has rainy winters every year' weather or climate?
  **A:** Climate — a long-term pattern.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Climate zones
*Student:* Color a world map by climate zone (polar, temperate, tropical, desert) using a NASA Climate Kids reference.

*How to teach:* Using a reference map, the child colors polar, temperate, tropical and desert zones and makes a key.

- **Q:** Where are the tropical zones?
  **A:** Near the equator.
- **Q:** Where are polar climates?
  **A:** Near the North and South Poles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare cities
*Student:* Compare yearly temperature and rainfall for Anchorage, Singapore and Cairo in a table.

*How to teach:* Make a table comparing yearly temperatures and rainfall of Anchorage, Singapore and Cairo.

- **Q:** Which city is hot and rainy all year?
  **A:** Singapore.
- **Q:** Which city is hot and very dry?
  **A:** Cairo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): People adapt
*Student:* Research how people in one climate region dress, build homes and farm.

*How to teach:* The child researches how people in one climate dress, build homes and farm.

- **Q:** How do people in hot deserts often build homes?
  **A:** With thick walls, small windows and light colors to stay cool.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a description of one climate region with at least 3 data facts.

*How to teach:* The child writes a description of one climate region with at least 3 data facts.

- **Q:** Describe Anchorage's climate.
  **A:** Cold, snowy winters well below freezing and cool summers; a subarctic climate.

**Assessment:** Climate region description

**Answer key:** Description: 3 = names region and location, 3 accurate data facts (temperature, rainfall, seasons), one way people adapt; 2 = 1–2 facts; 1 = confuses weather and climate. Sorting: proficient 9+/10.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): National symbols

**Prepare:** Images of the U.S. flag, bald eagle, Statue of Liberty and Liberty Bell (Library of Congress). The lines from 'The New Colossus': 'Give me your tired, your poor, / Your huddled masses yearning to breathe free.' The Liberty Bell's inscription: 'Proclaim LIBERTY throughout all the Land unto all the Inhabitants thereof.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the flag always had 50 stars. The first flag had 13 stars; stars were added as states joined (50 since 1960).
- Thinking the Statue of Liberty was built by the U.S. It was a gift from France, dedicated in 1886.

### Day 1 (Monday): Flag
*Student:* Learn the history and meaning of the stars and stripes and the Pledge of Allegiance.

*How to teach:* Learn flag history and meaning; say the Pledge and explain hard words (allegiance = loyalty; indivisible = cannot be split apart).

- **Q:** What do the 13 stripes stand for?
  **A:** The 13 original colonies.
- **Q:** What do the 50 stars stand for?
  **A:** The 50 states.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bald eagle
*Student:* Learn why the bald eagle was chosen as a national symbol and how it was saved from extinction.

*How to teach:* Learn why the bald eagle was chosen and how it was saved.

- **Q:** How was the bald eagle saved from extinction?
  **A:** The pesticide DDT, which weakened eggshells, was banned in 1972, and eagles were protected by law.
- **Q:** What does the bald eagle stand for?
  **A:** Strength and freedom.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Statue of Liberty
*Student:* Learn the history of the Statue of Liberty and read lines from Emma Lazarus's 'The New Colossus.'

*How to teach:* Learn the statue's history and read the lines from 'The New Colossus.'

- **Q:** Who gave the Statue of Liberty to the United States?
  **A:** France.
- **Q:** What do the lines 'Give me your tired, your poor' mean?
  **A:** America welcomes immigrants who seek freedom and a better life.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Liberty Bell
*Student:* Learn the story of the Liberty Bell and the words inscribed on it.

*How to teach:* Learn the Liberty Bell's story and inscription.

- **Q:** Where is the Liberty Bell?
  **A:** Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.
- **Q:** What famous feature does the Liberty Bell have?
  **A:** A large crack.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a symbols booklet with a picture and the ideal each symbol represents.

*How to teach:* The child makes a symbols booklet.

- **Q:** What ideal do most of these symbols share?
  **A:** Freedom (liberty).

**Assessment:** Symbols booklet

**Answer key:** Symbols booklet key: Flag — 13 stripes for the original colonies, 50 stars for the states; stands for the nation and its unity. Bald eagle — chosen in 1782 for the Great Seal; stands for strength and freedom; saved after the pesticide DDT was banned (1972) and removed from the endangered list in 2007. Statue of Liberty — gift from France, 1886, in New York Harbor; stands for freedom and welcome to immigrants. Liberty Bell — in Philadelphia, inscribed 'Proclaim LIBERTY…'; stands for freedom, used by abolitionists. Rubric: 4 = 4 symbols with picture, correct facts and ideal; 3 = one error; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Improvising melodies

**Prepare:** Xylophone or a keyboard/piano app, a drum or a metronome/drum-loop app for a steady beat, and a phone to record. Prepare 3 short story scenes (storm, sneaking, celebration).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking improvising means playing randomly. Improvising is making up music on the spot while following a beat, a scale or an idea.
- Thinking a mistake ruins an improvisation. Keep going; a 'wrong' note can become a new idea.

### Day 1 (Monday): Question-answer
*Student:* Play a 4-beat melodic 'answer' to a parent's melodic 'question' on a keyboard app.

*How to teach:* Play a 4-beat melodic 'question' on the keyboard (ending on a note that feels unfinished, like re or sol). The child answers with a 4-beat phrase that ends on do (C). Repeat 8–10 times.

- **Q:** Which note makes an answer sound finished?
  **A:** Do (the home note, C if playing in C).
- **Q:** How long should the answer be?
  **A:** The same length as the question — 4 beats.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pentatonic
*Student:* Improvise freely on black keys (pentatonic) over a steady drum beat.

*How to teach:* Start a steady drum beat. The child improvises freely using only the black keys (a pentatonic scale), which always sound good together.

- **Q:** Why do the black keys sound good together?
  **A:** They form a pentatonic (5-note) scale with no clashing half steps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story music
*Student:* Improvise music for a story scene (storm, sneaking, celebration).

*How to teach:* For each scene, improvise music: storm (low, loud, fast, crashing), sneaking (soft, short, detached, maybe low), celebration (high, bright, fast, bouncy).

- **Q:** How would you make 'sneaking' music?
  **A:** Soft (piano), short separate notes, slow or tiptoe tempo, maybe low pitches.
- **Q:** What musical elements show a storm?
  **A:** Loud dynamics, low and rumbling pitches, fast tempo, sudden crashes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Record
*Student:* Record your 3 best improvisations.

*How to teach:* Record the 3 best improvisations and label each.

- **Q:** Why record improvisations?
  **A:** So you can remember good ideas and develop them later.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Listen back and choose a favorite idea to develop into a song.

*How to teach:* Listen back and choose a favorite idea (a short motive) to develop into a song next week. The child plays the motive and explains why they like it.

- **Q:** What is a motive?
  **A:** A short musical idea that can be repeated and developed.

**Assessment:** Improvisation recordings

**Answer key:** Improvisation recordings rubric: 4 = 3 labeled recordings that keep a steady beat; question-answer phrases end on do; scene music clearly uses dynamics, tempo and pitch to match the story; a favorite idea is chosen and explained; 3 = recordings complete, one scene unclear; 2 = beat unsteady or fewer than 3 recordings; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Cooperative challenges

**Prepare:** Gather a hula hoop, an old blanket or tarp, 3 balloons, a blindfold or scarf, and soft 'mines' (shoes, pillows, cones). Gather 3+ family members. Timer for each challenge.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the fastest person should do all the work. Teamwork means every member has a role and the group plans together.
- Thinking cooperative games have no winner so effort doesn't matter. The team competes against the clock or a goal, so everyone's effort counts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hula hoop pass
*Student:* Pass a hula hoop around a hand-holding family circle without letting go, timing each round.

*How to teach:* Stand in a circle holding hands. Pass the hula hoop all the way around by stepping through it without letting go of hands. Time 3 rounds and talk about strategies that made it faster.

- **Q:** What strategy helped your team get faster?
  **A:** E.g., talking/helping each other, dipping a shoulder, the next person getting ready early.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Blanket flip
*Student:* Work as a group to flip a blanket over while everyone stays standing on it.

*How to teach:* Everyone stands on the blanket. Without anyone stepping off, the group flips the blanket over. Plan first, then try.

- **Q:** Why is planning before trying important?
  **A:** It gives everyone a role and an idea so the team works together instead of guessing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Balloon keep-up
*Student:* Keep 3 balloons in the air as a team for 60 seconds using only assigned body parts.

*How to teach:* Keep 3 balloons in the air for 60 seconds using only assigned body parts (e.g., elbows, heads, knees). Restart the timer if one touches the ground.

- **Q:** What did you do when a balloon was about to fall near someone else?
  **A:** Called out to warn them or moved to help, while keeping my own balloon up.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Teamwork talk
*Student:* Play 'minefield' (guiding a blindfolded partner through obstacles with words) and discuss trust.

*How to teach:* Set up 'minefield': one partner is blindfolded, the other guides them through the mines using only words. Switch. Discuss trust and clear directions.

- **Q:** What makes a good guide?
  **A:** Clear, calm, specific directions ('two small steps forward, stop, step left'), and keeping your partner safe.
- **Q:** Why is trust important in this game?
  **A:** The blindfolded partner can't see and must believe the guide will keep them safe.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a timed team challenge and name the encouraging words that helped.

*How to teach:* Repeat the fastest challenge, time it, and have the child name encouraging words that helped.

- **Q:** Name two encouraging things teammates said.
  **A:** E.g., 'You've got this,' 'Nice job,' 'Almost there,' 'Let's try again.'

**Assessment:** Team challenge

**Answer key:** Team challenge rubric: 4 = participates fully, shares an idea in planning, uses encouraging words, follows safety rules, and names specific encouraging words; 3 = participates and encourages others with prompting; 2 = participates but discourages others or ignores the plan; 1 = refuses to participate.

## Health 3: Germs and handwashing

**Prepare:** Glitter, lotion, soap, a spray bottle of water, large paper. Know the CDC 5 steps: wet, lather, scrub (at least 20 seconds), rinse, dry.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a quick rinse with water kills germs. Soap and 20 seconds of scrubbing are needed to remove germs.
- Thinking antibiotics cure colds. Colds are viruses; antibiotics only work on bacteria.

### Day 1 (Monday): Germs
*Student:* Learn the difference between bacteria and viruses and how they spread.

*How to teach:* Explain that germs are tiny living things. Bacteria are single-celled living things (some helpful, some cause strep throat); viruses are much smaller and cause colds and flu. Germs spread by touch, droplets, food and water.

- **Q:** What is the difference between bacteria and viruses?
  **A:** Bacteria are tiny one-celled organisms (some helpful, some cause illness like strep throat); viruses are much smaller and can only multiply inside living cells (they cause colds and flu).
- **Q:** Name two ways germs spread.
  **A:** Touching hands or surfaces, coughs and sneezes, contaminated food or water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Glitter germs
*Student:* Put lotion and glitter 'germs' on hands, shake hands with family, and see how far they spread.

*How to teach:* Put lotion and glitter on the child's hands, then shake hands with family members and touch a doorknob. Count how many people and objects have glitter.

- **Q:** What did the glitter show?
  **A:** How easily germs spread from hands to other people and objects.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Handwashing
*Student:* Practice the CDC 5-step handwashing method for 20 seconds, then test with glitter.

*How to teach:* Wash with the 5 steps for 20 seconds (hum 'Happy Birthday' twice). Then try with glitter: water only vs. soap and scrub.

- **Q:** What are the 5 handwashing steps?
  **A:** Wet, lather, scrub for 20 seconds, rinse, dry.
- **Q:** Name three times you should wash your hands.
  **A:** Before eating, after the bathroom, after coughing/sneezing, after touching animals, after being outside.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Cough and sneeze
*Student:* Spray a water bottle 'sneeze' at paper to see droplet spread, then practice covering with an elbow.

*How to teach:* Spray water at paper from a few feet to show droplet spread. Practice coughing or sneezing into the elbow.

- **Q:** Why sneeze into your elbow rather than your hand?
  **A:** So germs don't get on your hands and then spread to things and people you touch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a handwashing sign for the bathroom with all 5 steps.

*How to teach:* The child makes a handwashing sign for the bathroom.

- **Q:** How long should you scrub?
  **A:** At least 20 seconds.

**Assessment:** Handwashing sign

**Answer key:** Handwashing sign key: the 5 CDC steps in order — 1) Wet hands with clean running water; 2) Lather with soap (backs, between fingers, under nails); 3) Scrub for at least 20 seconds (hum 'Happy Birthday' twice); 4) Rinse; 5) Dry with a clean towel or air dry. Rubric: 4 = all 5 steps in order, 20 seconds mentioned, pictures; 3 = 4 steps; 2 = steps out of order; 1 = incomplete.
