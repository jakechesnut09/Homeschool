# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 24

## ELA 3: Opinion essay: revising and publishing

**Prepare:** Computer or tablet for typing (a free typing site such as typing.com is optional), the opinion rubric, index cards. Write the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Reading a speech word-for-word — use note cards with key words and look up often.
- Forgetting to restate the opinion in the conclusion.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise
*Student:* Revise reasons and conclusion using the opinion rubric.

*How to teach:* Score the draft together with the rubric. The child revises the weakest reason and rewrites the conclusion so it restates the opinion.

- **Q:** What should an opinion essay's conclusion do?
  **A:** Restate the opinion and sum up the reasons.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Type it
*Student:* Type the final essay, practicing keyboarding; study Greek roots tele, phon, scope.

*How to teach:* The child types the final essay, using both hands on the home row. Then study Greek roots tele (far), phon (sound) and scope (see).

- **Q:** What does 'telephone' mean, using roots?
  **A:** Far sound — a device for hearing sound from far away.
- **Q:** What does a telescope do?
  **A:** Helps you see things that are far away.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Speech
*Student:* Turn the essay into a 2-minute speech with note cards.

*How to teach:* Turn each paragraph into one note card with key words only. The child practices the 2-minute speech twice with a timer.

- **Q:** What goes on a speech note card?
  **A:** Key words and short phrases, not full sentences.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Deliver
*Student:* Deliver the speech to family, speaking clearly and making eye contact.

*How to teach:* The child delivers the speech to family, standing tall, speaking clearly and making eye contact. Listeners give one compliment.

- **Q:** Name two speaking skills to use.
  **A:** Speak clearly at a steady pace, and make eye contact with the audience.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 6 test on biographies, conjunctions and sentence types.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test and score the published essay with the rubric. Return the scored essay rubric and discuss one strength and one goal.

- **Q:** Combine with a conjunction: Ruby was scared. She went to school.
  **A:** Ruby was scared, but she went to school.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test; published opinion essay rubric

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test: 1) Biography vs. autobiography → by someone else vs. by the person 2) Who taught Helen Keller? → Anne Sullivan 3) What did Ruby Bridges do? → integrated an all-white New Orleans school in 1960 4) What did Jackie Robinson do? → first Black player in modern MLB, 1947 5) Combine with 'so': It rained. We stayed in. → It rained, so we stayed in. 6) Complex sentence using 'because' (any correct) 7) Label: 'I ran.' → simple 8) 'I ran, and I won.' → compound. Proficient 6+/8. Essay rubric: 4 = clear opinion, 3 reasons with text evidence, linking words, strong conclusion, few errors; 3 = proficient; 2 = weak evidence; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 3: Comparing fractions

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, paper fraction circles, index cards with fractions for fraction war (use denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 1/6 > 1/3 because 6 > 3 — with the same numerator, a bigger denominator means smaller pieces.
- Comparing fractions of different-size wholes — half of a large pizza can be more than half of a small one, so compare only with the same whole.

### Day 1 (Monday): Same denominator
*Student:* Compare fractions like 3/8 and 5/8 with strips and record with <, >, =.

*How to teach:* Compare 3/8 and 5/8 with strips: the pieces are the same size, so more pieces is more. Record with <, >, =.

- **Q:** Compare 3/8 and 5/8.
  **A:** 3/8 < 5/8 — same-size pieces, and 5 pieces is more.
- **Q:** Compare 4/6 and 2/6.
  **A:** 4/6 > 2/6.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Same numerator
*Student:* Compare 2/3 and 2/6 and explain why more pieces means smaller pieces.

*How to teach:* Compare 2/3 and 2/6: the same number of pieces, but thirds are bigger. Show with circles.

- **Q:** Compare 2/3 and 2/6.
  **A:** 2/3 > 2/6 — thirds are bigger pieces than sixths.
- **Q:** Compare 1/8 and 1/4.
  **A:** 1/8 < 1/4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Same whole
*Student:* Explain why comparisons only work when the wholes are the same size, using two different pizzas.

*How to teach:* Draw a big pizza and a small pizza; shade 1/2 of the small one and 1/4 of the big one. Ask which is more pizza — it can be the 1/4! Conclude that comparisons only work with same-size wholes.

- **Q:** Is 1/2 always more than 1/4?
  **A:** Only when the wholes are the same size.
- **Q:** Why must we compare fractions of the same whole?
  **A:** Because the size of each piece depends on the size of the whole.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fraction war
*Student:* Play fraction war with cards, justifying each comparison with a drawing.

*How to teach:* Play fraction war: each flips a card; compare only pairs with the same numerator or same denominator (flip again otherwise). The child draws to justify each win.

- **Q:** Compare 3/4 and 3/6.
  **A:** 3/4 > 3/6 — fourths are bigger than sixths.
- **Q:** Compare 5/6 and 6/6.
  **A:** 5/6 < 6/6 (6/6 is one whole).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare 12 pairs of fractions and justify 3 in writing.

*How to teach:* Give 12 comparisons; the child justifies 3 in writing. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Justify 2/4 < 2/3.
  **A:** Same numerator; thirds are bigger pieces than fourths, so 2 thirds is more than 2 fourths.

**Assessment:** Comparing fractions check

**Answer key:** 1) 1/4 < 3/4 2) 5/8 > 2/8 3) 1/2 > 1/3 4) 1/6 < 1/3 5) 2/3 > 2/8 6) 4/6 > 3/6 7) 3/4 > 3/8 8) 1/8 < 1/2 9) 2/2 = 1 10) 5/6 > 5/8 11) 7/8 > 6/8 12) 2/4 < 2/3. Proficient: 10+/12, and justifications name same numerator/denominator and piece size.

## Science 3: Solutions to environmental problems

**Prepare:** Articles or videos on wildlife crossings (e.g., the Wallis Annenberg Wildlife Crossing in Los Angeles County), fish ladders, dam removal and habitat restoration; poster supplies. Write the Unit 7 quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking one solution fixes every problem — each has benefits and drawbacks (trade-offs).
- Ignoring cost or effects on people when choosing a solution.

### Day 1 (Monday): Solutions
*Student:* Read about wildlife crossings, fish ladders and habitat restoration.

*How to teach:* Read about three solutions. For each, the child writes the problem it solves and how it helps animals.

- **Q:** What is a fish ladder?
  **A:** A series of small water steps that lets fish swim up and around a dam.
- **Q:** What is a wildlife crossing?
  **A:** A bridge or tunnel that lets animals safely cross a busy road.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evaluate
*Student:* Compare 2 solutions for the salmon problem using criteria.

*How to teach:* Make a table comparing two salmon solutions (fish ladder vs. removing a dam) by criteria: how many fish it helps, cost, and effects on people.

- **Q:** What is a trade-off of removing a dam?
  **A:** It helps fish reach spawning streams, but people lose the water storage or power the dam provided.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Claim
*Student:* Make a claim about the best solution with evidence.

*How to teach:* The child writes a claim about the best solution with evidence from the table.

- **Q:** Write a model claim.
  **A:** Removing old dams helps salmon most because it reopens whole rivers for spawning, even though it costs a lot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poster
*Student:* Create a poster about the chosen solution.

*How to teach:* The child makes a poster showing the problem, the chosen solution, how it helps and one trade-off.

- **Q:** What must the poster include?
  **A:** The problem, the solution, how it helps organisms, evidence, and a trade-off.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit quiz on habitats and environmental change.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What is habitat restoration?
  **A:** Repairing a damaged habitat so organisms can live there again.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 quiz; solution poster

**Answer key:** Unit 7 quiz: 1) What all organisms need → food, water, air, shelter, space 2) Desert organism that survives well → cactus or lizard 3) Pill bugs prefer → damp 4) One redwood adaptation → thick bark or using fog 5) Drought effect on salmon → low warm water kills eggs and young 6) One solution for salmon → fish ladder or dam removal. Proficient 5+/6. Poster: 3 = problem, solution, evidence and a trade-off.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Making a difference in civic life

**Prepare:** A letter template; look up your city council members' contact (city website). Paper ballots. A short article about a kid change-maker (e.g., a child who started a food drive or cleanup). Prepare the Unit 5 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking officials won't read kids' letters. Elected officials represent all residents, including kids, and a clear polite letter can lead to change.

### Day 1 (Monday): Community issues
*Student:* Identify a local issue (crosswalk safety, park repairs, litter) by observing your neighborhood.

*How to teach:* Walk the neighborhood and identify an issue.

- **Q:** What issue did you notice?
  **A:** Answers vary by community; e.g., a crosswalk without a light, broken playground equipment, litter.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Letter to an official
*Student:* Write a polite letter to a city council member about the issue with a suggested solution.

*How to teach:* Write a polite letter to a council member.

- **Q:** Name the parts of a letter.
  **A:** Date, greeting, body, closing, signature.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family vote
*Student:* Hold a family vote on a real decision using secret ballots and count the majority.

*How to teach:* Hold a family vote with secret ballots on a real decision; count the majority.

- **Q:** With 5 voters, how many votes are needed for a majority?
  **A:** 3 (more than half).
- **Q:** Why use a secret ballot?
  **A:** So people can vote how they truly want without pressure.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Change makers
*Student:* Read about a kid who made a difference in their community and discuss how.

*How to teach:* Read about a kid change-maker and discuss what they did.

- **Q:** What steps did the kid take to make a difference?
  **A:** Answer should come from the article: noticed a problem, made a plan, got help, took action.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit test on rules, laws and citizenship.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test.

- **Q:** Who makes the laws for a city?
  **A:** The city council.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test; letter to official

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test key: 1) Rules are for groups; laws are made by governments for everyone. 2) Laws keep people safe and protect rights. 3) The Constitution was written in 1787. 4) The Bill of Rights is the first 10 amendments. 5) First Amendment example: freedom of speech. 6) Citizen rights and responsibilities (one each). 7) Majority = more than half of the votes. 8) City council makes local laws. Letter rubric: 4 = proper greeting, names the issue with specific details, explains why it matters, suggests a solution, polite closing and signature; 3 = one part missing; 2 = issue but no solution; 1 = incomplete. 7–8 = proficient.

## Art & Music 3: Folk song performance

**Prepare:** Choose a folk song recording with a translation (e.g., 'De Colores' — a traditional Spanish-language song popular in Mexico and the Southwest; 'Sakura' — a traditional Japanese song about cherry blossoms; 'Funga Alafia' — a West African welcome song). Shakers, drum, sticks.

**Watch for:**
- Singing words without knowing what they mean. Learn the translation so the expression matches the meaning.
- Using an accompaniment that clashes with the style. Choose instruments and rhythm that fit (e.g., gentle sounds for 'Sakura,' a steady drum for 'Funga Alafia').

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose
*Student:* Choose a folk song from another culture (e.g., 'De Colores,' 'Sakura,' 'Funga Alafia') and explain your choice.

*How to teach:* Listen to 2–3 folk songs. The child chooses one and explains why (sound, culture, meaning).

- **Q:** What is a folk song?
  **A:** A traditional song passed down through generations in a community, often by ear, whose original composer is usually unknown.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Learn
*Student:* Learn the words and melody, including their meaning.

*How to teach:* Learn the words phrase by phrase by echo. Read the meaning in English and talk about what the song is about.

- **Q:** What is 'Sakura' about?
  **A:** Cherry blossoms (sakura) in spring in Japan.
- **Q:** What is 'De Colores' about?
  **A:** The colors of nature in spring — fields, birds and rainbows — and joy/love.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Accompany
*Student:* Add a simple percussion accompaniment that fits the song's style.

*How to teach:* Add a simple percussion part that fits the song's style and keep a steady beat while singing.

- **Q:** Why choose a gentle accompaniment for a slow song?
  **A:** So it supports the mood and doesn't cover the voice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the folk song for family, introducing where it comes from.

*How to teach:* Perform for family, first introducing the song's country/culture and meaning.

- **Q:** Where does your song come from, and what is it about?
  **A:** The child correctly states the culture and meaning of the chosen song.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Reflect in writing on what went well and what you'd practice next time.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection: 2 things that went well and 1 to practice next time.

- **Q:** What makes a useful reflection?
  **A:** Specific details about what went well and a clear plan for what to practice.

**Assessment:** Folk song performance and reflection

**Answer key:** Folk song performance and reflection rubric: 4 = sings the song with accurate words and melody, steady fitting accompaniment, introduces its origin and meaning correctly, and writes a reflection with 2 specific strengths and 1 practice goal; 3 = minor errors; 2 = needs support to sing or reflection vague; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Rhythm and movement

**Prepare:** Choose 4 songs with clearly different tempos (slow, medium, fast) that you have previewed for content. Clear space. For Thursday, choose a GoNoodle dance video (gonoodle.com, free account) and preview it.

**Watch for:**
- Moving faster than the beat. Listen first and clap the steady beat before moving.
- Thinking dance has to be fancy. Simple movements done on the beat with changes in level and direction make a strong dance.

### Day 1 (Monday): Beat
*Student:* March, clap and step to the beat of 4 songs with different tempos.

*How to teach:* Play each song; first clap the steady beat, then march, then step side to side to it. Discuss which songs are fast (high tempo) and slow.

- **Q:** What is tempo?
  **A:** How fast or slow the beat of the music is.
- **Q:** How do you find the beat?
  **A:** Listen for the steady pulse and clap or tap along to it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Levels
*Student:* Dance using high, middle and low levels on a caller's signal.

*How to teach:* Call 'high' (on tiptoes, arms up), 'middle' (standing normal), 'low' (crouched, near the floor) while dancing; switch every 8 counts.

- **Q:** Name the 3 levels in dance.
  **A:** High, middle and low.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mirror
*Student:* Mirror a partner's slow-motion dance moves, then switch leaders.

*How to teach:* Face each other; the leader moves in slow motion and the follower mirrors exactly, like a reflection. Switch every minute.

- **Q:** If the leader raises their right arm, which arm does the mirror raise?
  **A:** Their left arm (like a reflection in a mirror).

### Day 4 (Thursday): GoNoodle
*Student:* Learn and perform a GoNoodle dance video.

*How to teach:* Watch the GoNoodle dance once, then follow along twice, then try to do parts without watching.

- **Q:** How do you learn a dance from a video?
  **A:** Watch first, follow along, then practice small parts until you can do them without looking.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Create and perform an 8-count movement phrase to music.

*How to teach:* Count '1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8' to the music. The child creates 8 counts of movement (e.g., 4 steps right, 2 claps, a turn) and performs it 3 times in a row on the beat.

- **Q:** How many counts in a dance phrase like ours?
  **A:** 8 counts.

**Assessment:** 8-count phrase

**Answer key:** 8-count phrase rubric: 4 = original 8-count phrase performed on the beat, repeated 3 times the same way, uses at least 2 levels; 3 = on the beat with small changes between repeats; 2 = off the beat often or fewer than 8 counts; 1 = cannot create a phrase.

## Health 3: Medicine safety

**Prepare:** Empty medicine boxes (pain reliever, cough medicine, vitamins); remove any pills. Store real medicine out of reach. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking more medicine works faster or better. Taking more than the label says can be dangerous.
- Thinking medicine prescribed for someone else is safe for you. Prescriptions are made for one person's body and illness only.
- Thinking vitamins are candy. They're medicine-like and can make you sick in large amounts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Medicine vs. drug
*Student:* Define medicine and drug and sort examples.

*How to teach:* Define: a drug is any substance that changes how the body or mind works; a medicine is a drug used to prevent or treat illness. Sort examples (cough syrup, caffeine, nicotine, antibiotics, alcohol).

- **Q:** What is the difference between a medicine and a drug?
  **A:** All medicines are drugs, but a medicine is used to prevent or treat illness; some drugs (like nicotine and alcohol) are harmful and not medicine.
- **Q:** Is nicotine a medicine?
  **A:** No, it is a harmful, addictive drug.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Labels
*Student:* Read an over-the-counter medicine box for dosage, age and warnings.

*How to teach:* Read a medicine box together: find the dose, the age or weight it's for, how often, and warnings.

- **Q:** Why does a medicine label list ages?
  **A:** Doses are different for different ages and sizes; the wrong amount can be harmful.
- **Q:** What is the difference between over-the-counter and prescription medicine?
  **A:** Over-the-counter can be bought without a doctor's order; prescription medicine requires a doctor's order and is for one specific person.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Safe use
*Student:* Learn the rule: take medicine only from a parent or trusted adult, never alone.

*How to teach:* Teach the rule: only take medicine from a parent or trusted adult, never alone.

- **Q:** You find a pill on the floor. What do you do?
  **A:** Don't touch or take it; tell an adult.
- **Q:** Your friend offers you some of her allergy medicine. What should you say?
  **A:** No thanks. Only take medicine from your own parent or trusted adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Look-alikes
*Student:* Compare pictures of candy and medicine look-alikes and explain the danger.

*How to teach:* Show pictures of medicine and candy that look alike. Ask the child to guess which is which and discuss why that's dangerous.

- **Q:** Why is it dangerous that some medicines look like candy?
  **A:** Young children might eat them thinking they are candy and could get very sick.
- **Q:** What number do you call if someone swallows medicine by accident?
  **A:** Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222, or 911 if they are not breathing or unconscious.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write 5 medicine safety rules for a younger child.

*How to teach:* The child writes 5 rules for a younger child, in simple words with pictures.

- **Q:** What is the most important medicine rule?
  **A:** Only take medicine given by a parent or trusted adult.

**Assessment:** Medicine safety rules

**Answer key:** Medicine safety rules key (5 rules for a younger child, any 5 accurate): 1) Only take medicine from a parent or trusted adult. 2) Never take someone else's medicine. 3) Medicine is not candy. 4) Follow the label or doctor's directions exactly. 5) Never take medicine you find. 6) Tell an adult if you feel sick after taking medicine. Rubric: 4 = 5 accurate rules in kid-friendly words; 3 = 4; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.
