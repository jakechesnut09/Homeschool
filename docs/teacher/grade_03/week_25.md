# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 25

## ELA 3: The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, chapters 1–6

**Prepare:** The Wonderful Wizard of Oz by L. Frank Baum (Project Gutenberg, ideally with W.W. Denslow illustrations) and a reader's notebook. Note: the book differs from the 1939 film — Dorothy's shoes are silver, and the Good Witch of the North (not Glinda) meets her first.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up the book and the movie — in the book the shoes are silver and Oz is a real place, not a dream.
- Comparative adverb errors ('more faster') — use -er or 'more', never both.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chapters 1–2
*Student:* Read chapters 1–2 and contrast Kansas and Oz with descriptive details.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 1–2 together. The child makes a two-column chart contrasting Kansas and Oz with descriptive words quoted from the book.

- **Q:** How does Baum describe Kansas?
  **A:** Everything is gray — the prairie, the house, and even Aunt Em and Uncle Henry.
- **Q:** What happens when the house lands in Oz?
  **A:** It lands on and kills the Wicked Witch of the East; the Munchkins thank Dorothy and she is given the witch's silver shoes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chapters 3–4
*Student:* Read chapters 3–4 and describe the Scarecrow; spell silent-letter words (kn, wr, gh).

*How to teach:* Read chapters 3–4. The child describes the Scarecrow's looks and personality with evidence. Then spell silent-letter words (knee, write, light).

- **Q:** What does the Scarecrow want, and why?
  **A:** Brains, because he thinks he is foolish since his head is stuffed with straw.
- **Q:** Which letter is silent in 'knee' and in 'write'?
  **A:** k in knee; w in write.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Adverbs
*Student:* Use comparative and superlative adverbs (faster, most carefully) in sentences about the journey.

*How to teach:* Teach comparative adverbs (faster, more carefully) and superlative adverbs (fastest, most carefully). The child writes 6 sentences about the journey.

- **Q:** Fill in: The Lion ran (fast) of all.
  **A:** fastest.
- **Q:** Fill in: Dorothy walked (carefully) than the Scarecrow.
  **A:** more carefully.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Chapters 5–6
*Student:* Read chapters 5–6 and write whether you agree with the Tin Woodman's view that a heart matters most.

*How to teach:* Read chapters 5–6. Ask whether a heart or brains matters more. The child writes a paragraph agreeing or disagreeing with the Tin Woodman, with reasons.

- **Q:** Why does the Tin Woodman want a heart?
  **A:** He once loved a Munchkin girl, but after his body was replaced with tin he had no heart and could not love; he believes a heart is better than brains.
- **Q:** Who joins Dorothy in chapter 6?
  **A:** The Cowardly Lion.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a chapters 1–6 quiz and a point-of-view journal entry.

*How to teach:* Give the chapters 1–6 quiz. The child writes a journal entry from one character's point of view about the first days of the journey.

- **Q:** Who kisses Dorothy's forehead to protect her?
  **A:** The Good Witch of the North.

**Assessment:** Chapters 1–6 quiz; point-of-view journal

**Answer key:** Quiz: 1) How does Dorothy get to Oz? → a cyclone carries her house 2) What color are the shoes in the book? → silver 3) Where must Dorothy go for help? → the Emerald City to see Oz 4) Scarecrow wants → brains 5) Tin Woodman wants → a heart 6) Lion wants → courage. Proficient 5+/6. Journal: proficient if written in first person from one character's view with accurate events and feelings.

## Math 3: Fractions review and application

**Prepare:** Measuring cups (1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 cup), a simple recipe card (e.g., pancakes), a garden rectangle drawing, 3 stations set up (number line, comparing, equivalence). Write 5 worked problems with errors for Thursday and the Unit 6 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a 1/4 cup holds more than a 1/3 cup — fill each and pour into a 1-cup measure to compare.
- In error analysis, accepting a larger denominator as a larger fraction — recheck with strips.

### Day 1 (Monday): Recipe fractions
*Student:* Read a simple recipe and model 1/2, 3/4 and 1/3 cup amounts with measuring cups.

*How to teach:* Read the recipe and measure 1/2, 3/4 and 1/3 cup amounts with water or flour. Ask how many 1/4 cups make 3/4 cup.

- **Q:** How many 1/4 cups make 3/4 cup?
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** How many 1/2 cups make 1 cup?
  **A:** 2.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Garden plot
*Student:* Partition a garden drawing into equal areas and write each as a unit fraction.

*How to teach:* Draw a garden rectangle and split it into 4 or 6 equal plots; label each as a unit fraction of the whole garden. Ask the child to prove the plots are equal by counting squares or measuring.

- **Q:** A garden is split into 6 equal plots and 2 are tomatoes. What fraction is tomatoes?
  **A:** 2/6 (equal to 1/3).
- **Q:** Each of 4 equal beds is what fraction of the garden?
  **A:** 1/4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fraction stations
*Student:* Rotate through number line, comparison and equivalence stations.

*How to teach:* Rotate through the three stations, about 12 minutes each; check work together at the end. Circulate between stations and ask the child to explain one answer at each.

- **Q:** Place 3/4 and 1/2 on a 0–1 line. Which is farther right?
  **A:** 3/4.
- **Q:** 1/3 = ?/6
  **A:** 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Find and fix errors in 5 worked fraction problems.

*How to teach:* Show 5 worked problems with mistakes (e.g., '1/6 > 1/3', '3/4 is between 1 and 2', '2/4 is not equal to 1/2'). The child finds and fixes each.

- **Q:** Fix: 1/6 > 1/3.
  **A:** 1/6 < 1/3 — sixths are smaller pieces.
- **Q:** Fix: 2/4 is not equal to 1/2.
  **A:** 2/4 = 1/2 — they cover the same length on a strip.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 6 test on fractions.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Which is greater, 2/8 or 2/4?
  **A:** 2/4.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 test

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test: 1) 1 of 3 equal parts shaded → 1/3 2) 5 of 8 → 5/8 3) Place 2/4 on a 0–1 number line → halfway 4) 6/6 = 1 5) 4/2 = 2 6) 1/2 = 3/6 7) 2/3 = 4/6 8) 3/8 < 5/8 9) 2/3 > 2/6 10) Why can 1/4 of a big pizza be more than 1/2 of a small pizza? → the wholes are different sizes. Proficient: 8+/10.

## Science 3: Weather tools

**Prepare:** Straight-sided jar, ruler, tape, an outdoor thermometer, a straw, an index card, a pencil with an eraser, a straight pin, a phone compass app. An adult should handle the pin.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a wind vane points where the wind is going — it points to the direction the wind is coming FROM.
- Thinking it is hottest at noon — the warmest time is usually mid-afternoon, after the ground has been heated all day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Weather words
*Student:* Define temperature, precipitation, wind and cloud cover and set up a daily weather log page in your science notebook.

*How to teach:* Define temperature (how hot or cold), precipitation (water falling: rain, snow, hail), wind (moving air) and cloud cover. The child sets up a weather log page with a column for each.

- **Q:** What is precipitation?
  **A:** Water that falls from clouds — rain, snow, sleet or hail.
- **Q:** What does temperature measure?
  **A:** How hot or cold the air is.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rain gauge
*Student:* Build a rain gauge by taping a ruler to a straight-sided jar and place it in an open spot outdoors.

*How to teach:* Tape a ruler to the outside of a straight-sided jar with 0 at the bottom. Place it in an open spot away from roofs and trees. The child explains why the sides must be straight.

- **Q:** What does a rain gauge measure?
  **A:** How much rain falls, in inches or centimeters.
- **Q:** Why should the gauge be away from trees and roofs?
  **A:** So they don't block or add extra water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Wind vane
*Student:* Build a wind vane from a straw, index-card arrow and pencil eraser, and mark N/S/E/W with a compass app.

*How to teach:* Make a wind vane: cut an arrow point and tail from the card, slide them into slits at the straw's ends, pin the straw's middle to the eraser. Mark N, S, E, W around it using a compass app.

- **Q:** If the vane points north, which way is the wind blowing from?
  **A:** From the north (a north wind).
- **Q:** Why is the tail bigger than the point?
  **A:** The wind pushes the larger tail away so the point turns into the wind.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Thermometer
*Student:* Read an outdoor thermometer at 8 a.m., noon and 4 p.m. and explain why temperatures change through the day.

*How to teach:* Read the outdoor thermometer at 8 a.m., noon and 4 p.m. and record. The child explains the pattern.

- **Q:** Why is it usually warmer in the afternoon than in the morning?
  **A:** The sun has been heating the ground and air all day.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain what each homemade instrument measures and record a full day's weather using all of them.

*How to teach:* The child explains what each instrument measures and records a full day's weather with all of them.

- **Q:** Which instrument measures wind direction?
  **A:** A wind vane.

**Assessment:** Instrument explanation and first log entries

**Answer key:** Instrument explanation: rain gauge → amount of precipitation; wind vane → wind direction (the direction it comes from); thermometer → air temperature. Proficient if all three are correct and the first log entries include date, time, temperature, rainfall, wind direction and cloud cover.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Three branches of government

**Prepare:** Ben's Guide to the U.S. Government (bensguide.gpo.gov). Look up current leaders: the President (as of 2026, Donald Trump), your U.S. representative (house.gov 'find your representative'), California's two senators (as of 2026, Alex Padilla and Adam Schiff), and the Chief Justice (John Roberts).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the President makes the laws. Congress makes laws; the President carries them out (and can sign or veto bills).
- Thinking the Supreme Court is in charge of everything. It decides whether laws follow the Constitution.

### Day 1 (Monday): Branches
*Student:* Use Ben's Guide to learn what each branch does and who works in it.

*How to teach:* Use Ben's Guide to learn each branch.

- **Q:** Which branch makes laws?
  **A:** The legislative branch (Congress).
- **Q:** Which branch carries out laws?
  **A:** The executive branch, led by the President.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Checks and balances
*Student:* Play a role game where one branch makes, one carries out and one judges a family 'law.'

*How to teach:* Role game: one person writes a family 'law' (legislative), one carries it out (executive), one decides if it's fair (judicial). Try a veto.

- **Q:** What is a veto?
  **A:** When the President rejects a bill passed by Congress.
- **Q:** What are checks and balances?
  **A:** Ways each branch can limit the others so no branch has too much power.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): National leaders
*Student:* Identify the current President, your members of Congress and the Supreme Court.

*How to teach:* Identify current leaders.

- **Q:** Who are California's two U.S. senators?
  **A:** As of 2026, Alex Padilla and Adam Schiff (check senate.gov for changes).
- **Q:** How many justices are on the Supreme Court?
  **A:** Nine.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Three-branch chart
*Student:* Make a tree chart of the three branches with jobs and leaders.

*How to teach:* Make a tree chart of the branches.

- **Q:** What are the two parts of Congress?
  **A:** The Senate and the House of Representatives.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer questions on the branches and explain why power is divided.

*How to teach:* Ask the questions and have the child explain why power is divided.

- **Q:** Why is power divided among three branches?
  **A:** So no one person or group has too much power.

**Assessment:** Three-branch chart

**Answer key:** Three-branch chart key: Legislative — Congress (Senate with 100 senators, House of Representatives with 435 members) — makes laws. Executive — the President, Vice President and Cabinet — carries out and enforces laws; the President can veto. Judicial — the Supreme Court (9 justices) and other federal courts — interprets laws and decides if they follow the Constitution. Questions: power is divided so no branch becomes too powerful (checks and balances). Proficient: all three branches with correct jobs and leaders.

## Art & Music 3: Form and clay

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay (about 1 lb per child), a fork or toothpick for scoring, a small cup of water for slip (or make slip by mixing clay and water to a paste), a placemat, household texture tools (fork, bottle caps, lace, buttons). Pots dry 2–3 days.

**Watch for:**
- Pressing two clay pieces together without scoring. Joints crack as clay dries; scratch both surfaces (score), add slip, press and smooth.
- Making the bottom too thick and walls too thin. Aim for even walls about as thick as a finger.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shape vs. form
*Student:* Compare a drawn circle to a ball and roll basic forms (sphere, cylinder, cube) in clay.

*How to teach:* Draw a circle on paper and hold a clay ball. Explain shape (flat, 2D: length and width) vs. form (3D: length, width and depth). Roll a sphere, cylinder and cube.

- **Q:** What is the difference between shape and form?
  **A:** A shape is flat (2D); a form is three-dimensional with height, width and depth.
- **Q:** What form matches a ball? a can?
  **A:** A ball is a sphere; a can is a cylinder.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pinch pot
*Student:* Make a pinch pot with even walls by pressing and turning.

*How to teach:* Roll a ball about the size of an orange. Press the thumb into the center and pinch while turning the ball, working from the bottom up, keeping walls even.

- **Q:** How do you keep a pinch pot's walls even?
  **A:** Pinch gently and evenly while turning the pot a little each time, working from the bottom up.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Coil pot
*Student:* Build a coil pot, joining coils with score-and-slip.

*How to teach:* Make a flat base. Roll coils like snakes, about pencil-thick. Score and slip each layer, then smooth coils together on the inside.

- **Q:** What do score and slip mean?
  **A:** Score = scratch lines into both clay surfaces; slip = clay mixed with water used like glue to join them.
- **Q:** Why smooth coils on the inside?
  **A:** It blends the coils together so the pot is strong and doesn't fall apart.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Texture
*Student:* Add carved or pressed textures to both pots with household tools.

*How to teach:* Press or carve textures into both pots with household tools.

- **Q:** Name a tool you used and the texture it made.
  **A:** Answers vary, e.g., 'A fork made parallel lines like grass.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Evaluate the pots for even walls and secure joins.

*How to teach:* When dry, evaluate both pots: even walls, secure joins (no cracks), stable base, texture.

- **Q:** If your coil pot cracked between coils, what went wrong?
  **A:** The coils were not scored, slipped and smoothed well enough (or dried too fast).

**Assessment:** Pinch and coil pots

**Answer key:** Pinch and coil pots rubric: 4 = pinch pot with even walls and smooth rim; coil pot with scored-and-slipped coils smoothed inside, no cracks, stable base; both have intentional texture; child evaluates using criteria; 3 = minor unevenness or a small crack; 2 = walls very uneven or pot collapsing; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Folk dances

**Prepare:** Find instrumental music for each dance (a lively fiddle reel for the Virginia Reel and the traditional Jarabe Tapatío music, both widely available) and a short video of each to preview. Need at least 2 people for the reel (more is better).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Mexican Hat Dance is just about a hat. The Jarabe Tapatío is Mexico's national folk dance from Jalisco; the hat comes from the part where the man's sombrero is placed on the ground and the woman dances around its brim.
- Thinking folk dances were made up recently or by one person. Folk dances grow out of the traditions of a community and are passed down over generations.

### Day 1 (Monday): Virginia Reel
*Student:* Learn the Virginia Reel figures (forward and back, do-si-do, reel) with family.

*How to teach:* Teach Virginia Reel figures in two facing lines: forward and back (4 steps in, 4 out), right-hand turn, left-hand turn, two-hand turn, do-si-do (pass right shoulders, back to back, return). Then the head couple sashays down the middle and back.

- **Q:** What is a do-si-do?
  **A:** Partners walk forward, pass right shoulders, step side by side back to back, and walk backward to their places without turning around.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jarabe Tapatío
*Student:* Learn the basic heel-toe steps of the Mexican Hat Dance.

*How to teach:* Teach the basic heel-toe pattern: hop slightly and touch the right heel forward, then the left heel, then a quick three-step stamp (zapateado) pattern. Practice slowly counting aloud.

- **Q:** Which country does the Jarabe Tapatío come from?
  **A:** Mexico (the state of Jalisco).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice
*Student:* Practice both dances to music, counting steps aloud.

*How to teach:* Practice each dance to music, counting steps aloud. Slow the music or count slowly at first.

- **Q:** Why count aloud when learning a dance?
  **A:** It keeps the steps matched to the beat and helps you remember the order.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Culture
*Student:* Learn where each dance comes from and why it is danced.

*How to teach:* Share background: the Virginia Reel is an American country dance with roots in English and Scottish reels, popular in colonial and pioneer times at social gatherings. The Jarabe Tapatío is Mexico's national dance from Jalisco ('tapatío' means from Guadalajara), a courtship dance performed at celebrations and fiestas. Find both places on a map.

- **Q:** Where do the roots of the Virginia Reel come from?
  **A:** English and Scottish country dances (reels), brought to America.
- **Q:** Why are folk dances danced?
  **A:** To celebrate and share a community's culture at gatherings, festivals and holidays.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Perform both dances for family.

*How to teach:* Perform both dances for family. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** Tell your audience one fact about each dance.
  **A:** Virginia Reel: American country dance from English/Scottish reels; Jarabe Tapatío: Mexico's national dance from Jalisco.

**Assessment:** Folk dance performance

**Answer key:** Folk dance performance rubric: 4 = performs the main figures of the Virginia Reel (forward and back, turns, do-si-do) and the Jarabe heel-toe steps in order and on the beat, and states where each dance comes from; 3 = most figures correct with a few prompts; 2 = needs frequent prompts or off beat; 1 = cannot perform figures.

## Health 3: Tobacco and vaping

**Prepare:** Read the KidsHealth article on vaping (kidshealth.org) ahead. Find a healthy vs. smoker's lung image (CDC or American Lung Association). Parent note: the images can be upsetting; preview and keep the tone factual.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking vapes are just harmless water vapor. Vape aerosol contains nicotine and other chemicals and tiny particles that harm the lungs.
- Thinking you can try it once without problems. Nicotine is highly addictive, especially for young brains.
- Thinking secondhand smoke only matters indoors for a long time. Any secondhand smoke is harmful; there's no safe level.

### Day 1 (Monday): Effects
*Student:* Compare images of healthy and smoker's lungs and list effects on the heart and lungs.

*How to teach:* Compare the healthy and smoker's lung images. The child lists effects on the lungs (damage, coughing, cancer, trouble breathing) and heart (faster heartbeat, heart disease).

- **Q:** Name two effects of smoking on the body.
  **A:** Any two: lung damage, lung cancer, heart disease, coughing, shortness of breath, stained teeth, bad breath.
- **Q:** What is the addictive drug in tobacco?
  **A:** Nicotine.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Vaping
*Student:* Read a kid-level article on vaping and learn that vapes contain addictive nicotine.

*How to teach:* Read the vaping article. The child highlights 3 facts.

- **Q:** Is vape 'smoke' just water vapor?
  **A:** No. It's an aerosol with nicotine, flavoring chemicals and tiny particles that can harm the lungs.
- **Q:** Why is nicotine especially harmful for kids?
  **A:** Their brains are still developing, so they can get addicted more easily, and it can affect attention and learning.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Secondhand smoke
*Student:* Discuss secondhand smoke and how to avoid it.

*How to teach:* Discuss secondhand smoke and how to avoid it (move away, ask to open a window, ask adults not to smoke in the car).

- **Q:** What is secondhand smoke?
  **A:** Smoke breathed in by people near someone smoking.
- **Q:** How can you avoid secondhand smoke?
  **A:** Move away from smokers, go to a smoke-free area, and ask adults politely not to smoke around you or in the car.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Influences
*Student:* Identify how flavors, packaging and peers can make vaping seem appealing.

*How to teach:* Look at how vapes are marketed: candy flavors, bright packaging, social media, friends. Discuss why companies do this.

- **Q:** Why do vapes come in candy or fruit flavors?
  **A:** To make them appealing to young people and hide the harsh taste, which is a trick to get new users.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write 5 facts about tobacco and vaping.

*How to teach:* The child writes 5 facts about tobacco and vaping.

- **Q:** What is the legal age to buy tobacco or vapes in California?
  **A:** 21.

**Assessment:** Tobacco facts

**Answer key:** Tobacco facts key (any 5 accurate): cigarettes and vapes contain nicotine, which is addictive; smoking damages lungs and causes lung cancer and heart disease; smoking makes the heart beat faster and narrows blood vessels; vape aerosol is not just water vapor and contains harmful chemicals; secondhand smoke harms people nearby; the legal age to buy tobacco and vapes is 21; smoking stains teeth and causes bad breath and less stamina. Rubric: 4 = 5 accurate facts; 3 = 4; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.
