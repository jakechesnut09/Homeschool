# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 20

## ELA 3: Poetry anthology and recording

**Prepare:** Poems from the unit, paper or a notebook for the anthology, colored pencils, a recording device. Write the Unit 5 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every poem must rhyme — acrostics and cinquains usually don't.
- Miscounting cinquain lines — use the 2-4-6-8-2 syllable pattern and clap syllables.

### Day 1 (Monday): Word connections
*Student:* Describe people and places in your life with words from the poems (cozy, gentle, noisy).

*How to teach:* Reread favorite poem words (cozy, gentle, noisy, trembling) and talk about real people and places they fit. The child writes 5 sentences describing their own life with these words.

- **Q:** Use 'cozy' to describe a place you know.
  **A:** Example: My bedroom feels cozy with my soft blanket and the lamp on.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Poem types
*Student:* Read and write an acrostic and a cinquain; spell consonant-le words (table, puzzle).

*How to teach:* Show an example acrostic and cinquain. The child writes one of each. Then spell consonant-le words (table, puzzle, candle).

- **Q:** What is an acrostic poem?
  **A:** A poem where the first letters of the lines spell a word.
- **Q:** What is the syllable pattern of a cinquain?
  **A:** 2, 4, 6, 8, 2 syllables in five lines.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Anthology
*Student:* Compile 6 favorite poems (3 published, 3 original) into an illustrated anthology.

*How to teach:* The child picks 3 published poems and 3 original poems, copies them neatly, illustrates them and makes a title page and table of contents. Help them check that each poem lists the poet and that original poems are labeled with their name.

- **Q:** What should the table of contents list?
  **A:** Each poem's title, poet and page number.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Record
*Student:* Record a poetry podcast reading 3 poems fluently.

*How to teach:* The child records a short 'poetry podcast': introduce each of 3 poems by title and poet, then read it fluently. Listen back together and note one strength.

- **Q:** What should you say before reading each poem?
  **A:** The title and the poet's name.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 assessment
*Student:* Complete a poetry test on terms, nonliteral language and imagery.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 test and review the anthology and recording with the rubric. Listen to the recording together and score fluency with the rubric.

- **Q:** What does a stanza do in a poem?
  **A:** It groups related lines together, like a paragraph in prose.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 test; poetry anthology and recording

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test: 1) Define stanza → group of lines 2) rhythm → beat 3) rhyme → same ending sounds 4) imagery → words that appeal to the senses 5) Nonliteral: 'The rain sings a lullaby' → rain makes a soft, soothing sound 6) Order: nibble, eat, gobble (least to most) 7) What does 'trees bow down their heads' show? → the wind is blowing them 8) Name a free-verse poem → 'April Rain Song.' Proficient 6+/8. Anthology/recording: 3 = 6 illustrated poems and fluent reading; 2 = missing parts; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 3: Unit fractions and partitioning

**Prepare:** Paper rectangles, circles and squares; scissors; 12-inch paper strips (6 per child) for fraction strips; markers; an envelope to store strips.

**Watch for:**
- Calling unequal parts 'fourths' — fractions need equal parts; cut and stack the parts to compare.
- Thinking a larger denominator means a bigger piece — 1/8 is smaller than 1/4 because the whole is cut into more parts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Equal parts
*Student:* Fold paper rectangles and circles into halves, thirds, fourths, sixths and eighths.

*How to teach:* Fold rectangles and circles into halves, thirds (fold into a Z), fourths, sixths and eighths. Count the parts and name them.

- **Q:** A pizza is cut into 6 equal slices. What is one slice called?
  **A:** One sixth (1/6).
- **Q:** How many fourths make a whole?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Unit fractions
*Student:* Label each part as a unit fraction and explain why parts must be equal.

*How to teach:* Label each part with a unit fraction 1/b. Show a circle cut into 4 unequal parts and ask if each part is 1/4.

- **Q:** Is a shape cut into 3 unequal pieces showing thirds?
  **A:** No — thirds must be 3 equal parts.
- **Q:** What does the 8 in 1/8 tell you?
  **A:** The whole is cut into 8 equal parts.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Same area, different shape
*Student:* Partition squares into fourths in 3 different ways and prove each part has equal area.

*How to teach:* Partition paper squares into fourths three ways (4 strips, 4 small squares, 4 triangles using both diagonals). Cut and overlay to prove each part has equal area.

- **Q:** Can triangle-shaped fourths and strip-shaped fourths of the same square be equal?
  **A:** Yes — each is 1/4 of the same square, so each has the same area even though the shapes differ.
- **Q:** A square made of 16 small squares is split into 4 equal parts. How many small squares are in each part?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fraction strips
*Student:* Make a set of fraction strips (1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8) to use throughout the unit.

*How to teach:* Make strips all the same length: whole, halves, thirds, fourths, sixths, eighths; label every piece. Save them in an envelope for the unit.

- **Q:** Which is longer, 1/3 or 1/6?
  **A:** 1/3.
- **Q:** How many eighths fit on one half?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Identify and draw unit fractions for 10 shapes.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-item identification check. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Draw a rectangle and shade 1/5.
  **A:** A rectangle split into 5 equal parts with 1 part shaded.

**Assessment:** Unit fraction identification check

**Answer key:** 1) Circle in 2 equal parts, 1 shaded → 1/2 2) Rectangle in 4, 1 shaded → 1/4 3) Hexagon in 6 → 1/6 4) Bar in 8 → 1/8 5) Triangle in 3 equal parts → 1/3 6) Square in 4 unequal parts → 'not fourths — the parts aren't equal' 7–10) Draw 1/2, 1/3, 1/6, 1/8 with equal parts. Proficient: 9+/10.

## Science 3: Extinct organisms and modern relatives

**Prepare:** Smithsonian Learning Lab (free) images of a woolly mammoth, an elephant, a dinosaur fossil such as Archaeopteryx, and a modern bird; index cards for museum cards. Write the Unit 6 quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking extinct animals have no living relatives — elephants are related to mammoths, and birds descended from dinosaurs.
- Thinking people lived at the same time as dinosaurs like T. rex — non-bird dinosaurs died out about 66 million years before humans.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mammoth and elephant
*Student:* Compare a mammoth and an elephant and infer the mammoth's environment.

*How to teach:* Put a mammoth picture beside an elephant picture. The child lists similarities and differences, then infers the mammoth's environment from its traits.

- **Q:** What environment did woolly mammoths live in, and what is the evidence?
  **A:** A cold, icy environment — they had thick shaggy fur, small ears and a layer of fat to stay warm.
- **Q:** How are mammoths and elephants alike?
  **A:** Both have trunks, tusks and similar body shapes; they are related.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Dinosaurs and birds
*Student:* Compare dinosaur and bird fossils.

*How to teach:* Compare a feathered dinosaur fossil (like Archaeopteryx) with a modern bird skeleton. The child circles features they share.

- **Q:** What evidence links birds and dinosaurs?
  **A:** Some dinosaur fossils show feathers, hollow bones and wishbones like modern birds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Research
*Student:* Research one extinct organism and its environment.

*How to teach:* The child chooses one extinct organism (saber-toothed cat, dodo, T. rex, giant ground sloth) and researches when and where it lived, its environment and a modern relative.

- **Q:** What does 'extinct' mean?
  **A:** Every member of the species has died out; none are alive today.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present a museum card for the organism.

*How to teach:* The child makes a museum card with a drawing, name, when and where it lived, environment, one surprising fact and a modern relative, then presents it like a museum guide.

- **Q:** What belongs on a museum card?
  **A:** Name, picture, when and where it lived, its environment, an interesting fact and a modern relative.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit quiz on fossils.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What two big things can fossils tell us?
  **A:** What organisms lived long ago and what their environments were like.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 quiz; museum card

**Answer key:** Unit 6 quiz: 1) How do fossils form? → organism buried quickly; minerals replace hard parts over a long time 2) Trace fossil example → footprint or burrow 3) Fish fossil in a desert means → the area was once underwater 4) Oldest rock layer → the bottom 5) Modern relative of the mammoth → elephant 6) Where are the La Brea Tar Pits? → Los Angeles. Proficient 5+/6. Museum card: proficient if facts and environment are accurate and a modern relative is named.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Community history timeline

**Prepare:** Poster board, copies of photos, maps and clippings collected this unit. Prepare the Unit 4 test below.

**Watch for:**
- Spacing timeline events unevenly. Decide on a scale (e.g., 1 inch = 10 years) so events line up correctly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Organize
*Student:* Organize events from the unit into eras on a poster timeline.

*How to teach:* Organize events into eras (first peoples, Spanish/Mexican, early American, 1900s, today).

- **Q:** What is an era?
  **A:** A long period of time marked by certain events or ways of life.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Images
*Student:* Add copies of photos, maps and newspaper clippings with captions.

*How to teach:* Add images with captions.

- **Q:** What should a caption tell?
  **A:** What the image shows, when, and where it came from.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Land changes
*Student:* Add notes on how the land changed in each era (farms, roads, dams, buildings).

*How to teach:* Add notes on how the land changed in each era.

- **Q:** How did land use change after the railroad came?
  **A:** Answers vary by community; e.g., more farms, stores and houses near the tracks.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the timeline to family, explaining 3 turning points.

*How to teach:* Present the timeline and explain 3 turning points.

- **Q:** What is a turning point?
  **A:** An event that caused big changes afterward.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit test on community history and primary sources.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test.

- **Q:** Is a textbook a primary or secondary source?
  **A:** Secondary.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 test; illustrated timeline

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test key: 1) Your town's founding year and reason (local). 2) Primary source = made at the time (diary, photo, letter). 3) Secondary source = written later (textbook). 4) Chronicling America holds old newspapers. 5) Oral history = an interview about the past. 6) Entrepreneur = starts a business and takes risks. 7) Then-and-now photos show change and continuity. 8) Railroad/river/harbor helped towns grow. Illustrated timeline rubric: 4 = eras in correct order with dates, 6+ captioned images, notes on land changes, 3 turning points explained; 3 = most elements; 2 = few images or errors in order; 1 = incomplete. 7–8 = proficient.

## Art & Music 3: Cultural textiles and prints

**Prepare:** Images of Ghanaian adinkra cloth and symbols (the Metropolitan Museum and Smithsonian National Museum of African Art websites have them). Fabric scrap or paper, foam or cardboard to make stamps, paint or ink, ruler for a grid. Image of Hokusai's 'Under the Wave off Kanagawa' ('The Great Wave').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking adinkra symbols are just decoration. Each symbol has a name and meaning (often a proverb or value) for the Akan people of Ghana.
- Thinking a print is only one copy. Printmaking makes many copies, which is why woodblock prints could spread pictures widely.

### Day 1 (Monday): Adinkra
*Student:* Learn about Ghanaian adinkra cloth and the meanings of 6 symbols.

*How to teach:* Explain adinkra cloth comes from the Akan (Asante) people of Ghana; symbols were traditionally stamped with carved calabash (gourd) stamps. Learn 6 symbols: Gye Nyame (supremacy of God), Sankofa (learn from the past), Dwennimmen (ram's horns: humility and strength), Akoma (heart: patience and love), Adinkrahene (chief of symbols: leadership/greatness), Funtunfunefu (joined crocodiles: unity, sharing).

- **Q:** What country does adinkra cloth come from?
  **A:** Ghana (West Africa), from the Akan/Asante people.
- **Q:** What does the Sankofa symbol mean?
  **A:** Go back and get it — learn from the past to move forward.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Personal symbols
*Student:* Design 3 personal symbols that represent your family values.

*How to teach:* The child picks 3 family values (kindness, courage, togetherness) and designs a simple, bold symbol for each that could be cut as a stamp.

- **Q:** Why should a stamp symbol be simple and bold?
  **A:** Fine details don't print clearly; bold shapes make strong, readable prints.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Symbol cloth
*Student:* Print your symbols in a grid on fabric or paper using stamps.

*How to teach:* Make stamps (foam shapes glued to cardboard). Rule a grid on fabric or paper and print the symbols in a repeating pattern in the squares.

- **Q:** How are your symbols arranged?
  **A:** In a grid, repeating in a pattern, like traditional adinkra cloth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hokusai
*Student:* Look at Hokusai's woodblock print 'The Great Wave' and discuss how prints spread stories.

*How to teach:* Show 'The Great Wave' by Katsushika Hokusai (about 1831), a woodblock print from Japan in the series 'Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji.' Explain woodblock prints were made in many copies and sold cheaply, so many people could own and share pictures and stories.

- **Q:** What is small and far away in 'The Great Wave'?
  **A:** Mount Fuji, in the background beneath the wave.
- **Q:** How did printmaking help art spread?
  **A:** Many copies could be printed from one carved block, so art was affordable and could be shared widely.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Present your symbol cloth and explain what each symbol means.

*How to teach:* The child presents the symbol cloth and explains each symbol's meaning and how adinkra inspired it.

- **Q:** What value does each of your symbols stand for?
  **A:** The child names each symbol and the value it represents.

**Assessment:** Symbol cloth presentation

**Answer key:** Symbol cloth presentation rubric: 4 = 3 original bold symbols printed clearly in an organized grid pattern; the child explains each meaning and correctly states adinkra is from Ghana with symbols that carry meanings; 3 = all printed, one meaning unclear; 2 = disorganized or only 1–2 symbols; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Striking with paddles

**Prepare:** Gather a paddle or short-handled racket (ping-pong paddle, paddleball or badminton racket), balloons and a tennis or foam ball. A flat wall such as a garage door for Thursday.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging the whole arm wildly. Keep the paddle face flat and use small, controlled movements with a firm wrist.
- Gripping too tight or holding the paddle at the end of the handle. Use a 'shake hands' grip close to the face for control.

### Day 1 (Monday): Balloon
*Student:* Keep a balloon up with a paddle 20 times in a row.

*How to teach:* Teach the shake-hands grip. The child keeps a balloon up by tapping it gently with a flat paddle face, counting hits. Goal: 20 in a row.

- **Q:** How do you hold the paddle?
  **A:** With a 'shake hands' grip, firm but relaxed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Up bounces
*Student:* Bounce a ball upward on the paddle and count consecutive hits.

*How to teach:* Bounce a ball upward on the paddle (paddle face flat, waist high), letting it rise only a few inches. Count consecutive hits in 3 trials.

- **Q:** Why keep the paddle face flat?
  **A:** A flat face sends the ball straight up; a tilted face sends it sideways.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Down dribble
*Student:* Bounce a ball downward with the paddle like a dribble while walking.

*How to teach:* Turn the paddle face down and 'dribble' the ball toward the ground while standing, then while walking slowly.

- **Q:** How is a paddle down-dribble like a hand dribble?
  **A:** You push the ball down at a steady height and keep control while moving.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Wall hits
*Student:* Hit a ball against a wall, letting it bounce once between hits.

*How to teach:* Stand 6–8 feet from a wall. Drop the ball, let it bounce, hit it to the wall, let it bounce once, hit again. Count hits.

- **Q:** How many bounces are allowed between wall hits?
  **A:** One.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Record your best counts for up bounces and wall hits.

*How to teach:* The child does 3 trials of up bounces and 3 of wall hits and records the best of each.

- **Q:** Up bounce trials: 9, 15, 12. Best?
  **A:** 15.

**Assessment:** Paddle control counts

**Answer key:** Paddle control counts: record best counts. Benchmarks for proficient: balloon 20 in a row, up bounces 10+, wall hits 5+ with one bounce between hits, with a flat paddle face and shake-hands grip. 4 = meets all benchmarks; 3 = meets 2; 2 = meets 1; 1 = none.

## Health 3: Water and outdoor safety

**Prepare:** A properly sized U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket. Preview a rip current graphic (weather.gov/safety/ripcurrent). Write 4 outdoor scenario cards.

**Watch for:**
- Swimming straight back to shore in a rip current. Swim parallel to the shore until out of the current, or float and signal for help.
- Jumping in to save a struggling swimmer. 'Reach or throw, don't go': extend a pole or throw something that floats and call for an adult.
- Thinking pool toys or water wings replace life jackets. Only a Coast Guard-approved life jacket is a safety device.

### Day 1 (Monday): Water rules
*Student:* Learn pool, lake and ocean safety rules, including what a rip current is and how to escape one.

*How to teach:* Teach pool, lake and ocean rules. Show the rip current graphic and practice the escape motion (swim sideways/parallel).

- **Q:** What is a rip current, and how do you escape one?
  **A:** A strong, narrow current flowing away from shore. Don't fight it; swim parallel to the shore until free, then swim in, or float and wave for help.
- **Q:** Who should always be watching when you swim?
  **A:** An adult who knows how to swim (and a lifeguard at beaches and public pools).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Life jackets
*Student:* Try on a life jacket and check it is the right size and fastened correctly.

*How to teach:* The child puts on the life jacket. Check: label shows it is Coast Guard-approved and fits the child's weight; all buckles fastened; lift at the shoulders — it shouldn't ride above the chin or ears.

- **Q:** How do you check if a life jacket fits?
  **A:** Buckle it, then have an adult lift it at the shoulders; it should not slide up past the chin or ears.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Buddy system
*Student:* Practice the buddy system and 'reach or throw, don't go' rescue rule.

*How to teach:* Explain the buddy system. Practice 'reach or throw, don't go' with a towel or pool noodle.

- **Q:** If your friend is struggling in the water, what should you do?
  **A:** Don't jump in. Reach with a pole or towel or throw something that floats, and shout for an adult.
- **Q:** What is the buddy system?
  **A:** Always swimming with a partner who keeps track of you and can get help.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Decisions
*Student:* Use decide-act-check steps for 4 outdoor scenarios (thunderstorm at the beach, getting lost on a hike).

*How to teach:* Use decide-act-check on 4 scenarios: thunder at the beach (get out of the water and go indoors); lost on a hike (stop, stay put, hug a tree, blow a whistle); etc.

- **Q:** What should you do if you hear thunder at the beach?
  **A:** Get out of the water and go inside a building or hard-topped car; 'when thunder roars, go indoors.'
- **Q:** What should you do if you get lost on a hike?
  **A:** Stop and stay where you are, stay calm, blow a whistle or call out, and wait for help.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a water safety poster with 5 rules.

*How to teach:* The child makes a water safety poster with 5 rules and pictures.

- **Q:** Name one rule for diving.
  **A:** Never dive into shallow or unknown water; go in feet first.

**Assessment:** Water safety poster

**Answer key:** Water safety poster key (any 5 correct rules): swim only with an adult watching; always swim with a buddy; wear a life jacket on boats and in open water; swim parallel to shore to escape a rip current; reach or throw, don't go; get out of the water when you hear thunder; feet first into water you don't know; swim near a lifeguard. Rubric: 4 = 5 accurate rules with pictures; 3 = 4; 2 = 2–3; 1 = 0–1.
