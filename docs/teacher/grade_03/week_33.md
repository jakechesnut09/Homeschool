# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 33

## ELA 3: Publishing and presenting research

**Prepare:** Computer or tablet, poster board or slideshow software, a printer for a photo and map. Write the Unit 8 test with a new informational passage.

**Watch for:**
- Putting too many words on slides or posters — use headings, pictures and a few key facts.
- Reading the poster aloud instead of talking — the speaker explains; the visual supports.

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise and edit
*Student:* Revise and edit the report with an adult.

*How to teach:* Read the draft together. First check facts and organization, then spelling, capitals and punctuation, with the child making the changes.

- **Q:** What should you check first when revising?
  **A:** Facts and organization, then spelling and punctuation.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Publish
*Student:* Type and publish the report with a photo and map.

*How to teach:* The child types the final report and adds a photo and the captioned map, crediting the photo source. Have the child proofread the typed copy aloud to catch typing errors.

- **Q:** Why should you credit where a photo came from?
  **A:** It is someone else's work, and readers can see the source.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Visual display
*Student:* Create a slideshow or poster for the presentation.

*How to teach:* The child makes a poster or 4–6 slides with a title, headings, pictures and key facts. Remind them that each slide or section should match one report paragraph.

- **Q:** What belongs on a presentation visual?
  **A:** A title, headings, pictures and a few key facts — not whole paragraphs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present research to the family and answer questions.

*How to teach:* The child presents to the family using the visuals and answers questions. Have each family member ask one question, and praise answers that use report facts.

- **Q:** What should you say if you don't know the answer to a question?
  **A:** 'I don't know, but I can find out,' and then look it up.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 8 test on informational reading and research skills.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test and score the report and presentation with the rubric. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What does a glossary do?
  **A:** Gives the definitions of important words in a text.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test; research report and presentation rubric

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test (new passage): 1) Main idea 2) Two key details 3) Purpose of one text feature 4) Context-clue meaning of a word 5) Meaning of a prefixed word 6) Compare with a second short text: one similarity 7) one difference 8) Name a reliable source type. Proficient 6+/8. Report rubric: 4 = accurate facts in the child's own words, organized into paragraphs with topic sentences, an introduction and conclusion, text features or visuals, few errors; 3 (proficient) = organized and accurate with minor gaps; 2 = facts listed with weak organization or copied wording; 1 = incomplete. Presentation: clear voice, eye contact, visuals used.

## Math 3: Perimeter and area relationships

**Prepare:** Square tiles (at least 24), grid paper. Write the mixed problem set.

**Watch for:**
- Believing shapes with the same area must have the same perimeter — compare 1 × 24 (P = 50) with 4 × 6 (P = 20).
- Using area when a question needs perimeter — ask 'Are we covering it or going around it?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Same area
*Student:* Build all rectangles with area 24 and record their perimeters.

*How to teach:* Build every rectangle with 24 tiles (1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, 4 × 6) and record each perimeter in a table. Ask the child what they notice: the closer to a square, the smaller the perimeter.

- **Q:** What are the perimeters of all the rectangles with area 24?
  **A:** 1 × 24: 50; 2 × 12: 28; 3 × 8: 22; 4 × 6: 20.
- **Q:** Which has the smallest perimeter?
  **A:** 4 × 6 (the one closest to a square).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Same perimeter
*Student:* Draw all rectangles with perimeter 20 and record their areas.

*How to teach:* Draw every rectangle with whole-number sides and perimeter 20 (1 × 9, 2 × 8, 3 × 7, 4 × 6, 5 × 5) and record areas. Have the child describe the pattern: the closer to a square, the larger the area.

- **Q:** What are the areas for the perimeter-20 rectangles?
  **A:** 9, 16, 21, 24, 25.
- **Q:** Which has the greatest area?
  **A:** 5 × 5 = 25.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Best pen
*Student:* Find the rectangular pen with the most area using 24 feet of fence.

*How to teach:* With 24 ft of fence, list the pens: 1 × 11 (11 sq ft), 2 × 10 (20), 3 × 9 (27), 4 × 8 (32), 5 × 7 (35), 6 × 6 (36). Have the child sketch each pen on grid paper and circle the winner.

- **Q:** Which rectangular pen made with 24 ft of fence has the most area?
  **A:** 6 ft by 6 ft, with 36 sq ft.
- **Q:** What is the area of the 2 × 10 pen?
  **A:** 20 sq ft.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Area or perimeter?
*Student:* Decide whether 10 real-life situations need area or perimeter.

*How to teach:* Decide whether each of 10 real situations needs area or perimeter, explaining why. Sort the situations into two piles and discuss any the child is unsure about.

- **Q:** Buying carpet for a floor: area or perimeter?
  **A:** Area.
- **Q:** Putting ribbon around a picture frame?
  **A:** Perimeter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve a mixed area/perimeter problem set.

*How to teach:* Give the mixed area/perimeter problem set. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** A 5 × 3 rectangle: what are its area and perimeter?
  **A:** Area 15 sq units; perimeter 16 units.

**Assessment:** Area vs. perimeter problem set

**Answer key:** 1) 6 × 4 rectangle: A = 24, P = 20 2) 8 × 2: A = 16, P = 20 3) Can rectangles have the same perimeter but different areas? → yes (8 × 2 and 5 × 5 both have P = 20; areas 16 and 25) 4) All rectangles with area 36: 1 × 36, 2 × 18, 3 × 12, 4 × 9, 6 × 6 5) Tiling a floor → area 6) Fencing a yard → perimeter 7) Square with P = 28 → side 7, A = 49. Proficient: 6+/7.

## Science 3: Engineering: define the problem

**Prepare:** A design notebook, photos of storm-resistant buildings (hurricane straps, shutters, raised houses), and the materials list (cardboard, tape, 1 plastic bag).

**Watch for:**
- Starting to build before planning — engineers define the problem and criteria first.
- Confusing criteria and constraints.

### Day 1 (Monday): Problem
*Student:* Define the challenge: protect a small model house from strong wind and heavy rain.

*How to teach:* Present the challenge: protect a small model house from strong wind and heavy rain. The child writes the problem in their own words.

- **Q:** What is the problem?
  **A:** Design a model house that stays standing in strong wind and stays dry inside in heavy rain.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Criteria and constraints
*Student:* List criteria (stays standing at fan high speed, inside stays dry) and constraints (cardboard, tape, 1 plastic bag, 3 days).

*How to teach:* The child lists criteria and constraints in the notebook.

- **Q:** Name a criterion and a constraint.
  **A:** Criterion: stays standing at high fan speed. Constraint: only cardboard, tape and 1 plastic bag.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Research
*Student:* Look at how real hurricane-resistant houses use roof straps, shutters and raised foundations.

*How to teach:* Look at photos of real storm-resistant houses and list features.

- **Q:** How do roof straps help in a hurricane?
  **A:** They tie the roof to the walls so wind can't lift it off.
- **Q:** Why are some houses built on stilts?
  **A:** To keep living areas above floodwater.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sketch
*Student:* Sketch 3 different house designs with labels.

*How to teach:* The child sketches 3 different house designs with labels.

- **Q:** Why sketch 3 designs?
  **A:** To compare ideas before choosing the best.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Score each design against the criteria and choose one to build.

*How to teach:* Score each design against the criteria in a table and choose one.

- **Q:** How do you choose the best design?
  **A:** Pick the one that best meets the criteria within the constraints.

**Assessment:** Design notebook with scored sketches

**Answer key:** Design notebook: proficient (3) = problem statement, 2+ criteria and constraints, 3 labeled sketches using ideas from real buildings, a scoring table and a justified choice; 2 = missing scoring; 1 = one sketch only.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Where goods come from

**Prepare:** 20 household items with 'Made in' labels, a world map and sticky dots. Plan a farmers market visit.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'Made in USA' means made in your town. Local means nearby; national means anywhere in the U.S.
- Thinking trade happens only because other countries are cheaper. Climate (bananas don't grow well here) and specialization also matter.

### Day 1 (Monday): Label hunt
*Student:* Check labels on 20 household items and record where each was made.

*How to teach:* Check labels on 20 items and record where each was made.

- **Q:** Where was most of your stuff made?
  **A:** Answer from the child's data, e.g., 'China' or 'the United States.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map
*Student:* Place dots on a world map for each item's origin and count local, national and foreign goods.

*How to teach:* Place dots on a world map; count local, national and foreign goods.

- **Q:** Which continent had the most dots?
  **A:** Answer from the child's data.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Local goods
*Student:* Visit a farmers market and list goods made or grown locally.

*How to teach:* At a farmers market, list goods grown or made locally.

- **Q:** Why buy local goods?
  **A:** They are fresh, travel a shorter distance, and support local farmers and businesses.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Why trade
*Student:* Discuss why we buy goods from other places (cost, climate, specialization).

*How to teach:* Discuss why we trade: cost, climate, specialization.

- **Q:** Why do we buy bananas from other countries?
  **A:** Bananas need a hot, humid tropical climate that most of the U.S. doesn't have.
- **Q:** What is specialization?
  **A:** When people or places focus on making what they make best and trade for other things.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write about one item's journey from where it was made to your home.

*How to teach:* The child writes about one item's journey.

- **Q:** How might a toy made in China get to your home?
  **A:** Made in a factory → shipped by cargo ship across the Pacific → unloaded at a port like Los Angeles or Long Beach → trucked to a warehouse → store or delivery → home.

**Assessment:** Origins map and journey writing

**Answer key:** Origins map and journey writing rubric: map has a dot for each of 20 items with correct country and a count of local, national and foreign; writing traces one item's journey in order (made in factory/farm → shipped by boat/plane/truck → warehouse → store → home) with at least 4 steps and the country of origin; 4 = all accurate; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = few steps; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Community mural

**Prepare:** Butcher paper (about 3 × 5 feet), pencil, ruler or yardstick, tempera paints in many colors plus white and black, large and small brushes, drop cloth. The week 32 sketch.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing the large version freehand without guides. A grid lets you copy each small square onto a big square so proportions stay right.
- Painting details first. Paint large background areas first, then middle shapes, then fine details on top.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grid transfer
*Student:* Draw a grid on your sketch and the butcher paper to scale up the design.

*How to teach:* Draw a grid on the sketch (e.g., 2-inch squares) and the same number of squares on the butcher paper (e.g., 1-foot squares). Number rows and columns on both.

- **Q:** If the sketch grid has 4 rows × 6 columns, how many squares should the big paper have?
  **A:** The same: 4 × 6 = 24 squares.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draw
*Student:* Draw the design lightly onto the large paper.

*How to teach:* Copy the drawing square by square, lightly in pencil, looking at what crosses each square's edges.

- **Q:** Why draw lightly?
  **A:** So lines can be erased or covered by paint.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Background
*Student:* Paint the sky, land and large background shapes.

*How to teach:* Paint sky, land and large background shapes with big brushes.

- **Q:** Why paint the background first?
  **A:** Big areas are easier first, and details can be painted on top without being covered.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Details
*Student:* Paint people, buildings and details, using tints and shades.

*How to teach:* Paint people, buildings and details with small brushes, using tints and shades for form.

- **Q:** How can you use shades on a building?
  **A:** Paint the side away from the sun a darker shade of the same color to make it look 3D.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Evaluate the mural with family and add finishing touches.

*How to teach:* Step back with family, evaluate (clear story, color, details) and add finishing touches such as outlines.

- **Q:** Why step back to look at a mural?
  **A:** Murals are meant to be seen from a distance; stepping back shows what is clear and what needs fixing.

**Assessment:** Community mural

**Answer key:** Community mural rubric: 4 = design scaled up accurately with the grid, background fully painted, details use tints and shades, clear community story, neat finishing touches; 3 = small proportion issues or unfinished details; 2 = story unclear or large areas unpainted; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Fuel and hydration

**Prepare:** Water bottle and a cup-tally sheet, snack cards (e.g., banana, apple, whole-grain crackers, yogurt, candy bar, chips, soda, cookies, orange slices, water), 6 stations for the circuit, timer and heart-rate log.

**Watch for:**
- Waiting until you are thirsty to drink. Drink water before, during and after activity; thirst means you are already starting to dry out.
- Thinking sports drinks or soda are needed for normal play. For kids' everyday activity, water is the best choice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hydration
*Student:* Track water cups drunk before, during and after a 20-minute active play session.

*How to teach:* Before a 20-minute active play session, drink a cup of water; drink small sips during and a cup after. Tally cups. Talk about signs of dehydration (thirst, dry mouth, headache, dizziness, dark yellow urine).

- **Q:** When should you drink water during exercise?
  **A:** Before, during (small sips at breaks) and after.
- **Q:** Name two signs your body needs more water.
  **A:** Any two: thirst, dry mouth, headache, feeling tired or dizzy, dark yellow urine.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fuel
*Student:* Sort snack cards into good pre-activity fuel vs. sometimes foods, then do a 15-minute jog-and-skip workout.

*How to teach:* Sort snack cards: good pre-activity fuel (fruit, whole-grain crackers, yogurt, a small sandwich) vs. sometimes foods (candy, chips, soda, cookies). Explain that carbohydrates give energy. Then do a 15-minute jog-and-skip workout.

- **Q:** Why is a banana a good snack before playing?
  **A:** It gives carbohydrates for energy (and potassium) without making you feel heavy or sick.
- **Q:** Why are candy and soda 'sometimes' foods?
  **A:** They have lots of sugar and few nutrients, so the energy doesn't last and they don't help your body grow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Circuit
*Student:* Complete a 6-station fitness circuit (1 minute each) and record heart rate after each round.

*How to teach:* Complete a 6-station circuit, 1 minute each. After each round, take a 15-second pulse × 4 and record.

- **Q:** Round 1 count: 30 beats in 15 seconds. What is the heart rate per minute?
  **A:** 30 × 4 = 120 bpm.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heart and lungs
*Student:* Explain how heart and breathing rates changed during the circuit using your data.

*How to teach:* Look at the circuit data and explain how heart rate and breathing changed and why.

- **Q:** Why did your heart rate and breathing rise during the circuit?
  **A:** Muscles needed more oxygen; the heart beat faster to pump blood and the lungs breathed faster to bring in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide.
- **Q:** What happened when you rested?
  **A:** Heart rate and breathing slowed back down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a short quiz on fueling, hydration and the body's response to exercise.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz below orally or on paper.

- **Q:** Is water or soda the better drink for exercise?
  **A:** Water.

**Assessment:** Fuel quiz

**Answer key:** Fuel quiz (5 questions): 1) Best drink for everyday exercise? — Water. 2) Name a good pre-activity snack. — Any healthy carbohydrate food, e.g., banana, apple, whole-grain crackers, yogurt. 3) When should you drink water? — Before, during and after activity. 4) What happens to heart rate during exercise and why? — It goes up to pump more oxygen-rich blood to working muscles. 5) Name a sign of dehydration. — Thirst, dry mouth, headache, dizziness or dark urine. Proficient = 4 of 5 correct.

## Health 3: Healthy community

**Prepare:** Gloves, trash bags, a clipboard with a tally sheet, grabbers if possible. Choose a safe location. Safety rules: gloves on, don't touch sharp items or needles (tell an adult), stay away from traffic and water edges, wash hands after. Prepare Unit 6 quiz below.

**Watch for:**
- Picking up sharp or dangerous items. Never touch glass shards, needles or chemicals; point them out to an adult.
- Thinking one family's cleanup doesn't matter. Data and sharing results can inspire others.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose a park, beach or street to clean and plan supplies and safety rules.

*How to teach:* Choose a site, list supplies and write 4 safety rules together.

- **Q:** What should you do if you find a needle or broken glass?
  **A:** Don't touch it; tell an adult right away.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cleanup
*Student:* Carry out a 1-hour cleanup with family and tally types of trash collected.

*How to teach:* Do the cleanup for 1 hour, tallying items by type (wrappers, bottles, cans, cigarette butts, etc.).

- **Q:** Why wear gloves during a cleanup?
  **A:** To protect your hands from germs and sharp objects.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Data
*Student:* Graph the trash collected and identify the most common item.

*How to teach:* The child makes a bar graph of the tallies and identifies the most common item.

- **Q:** What does the tallest bar on your graph tell you?
  **A:** The most common type of trash collected.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Write a short letter or post for a community board sharing results and a request to help.

*How to teach:* The child writes a short letter or post sharing results and asking others to help.

- **Q:** What should your letter include?
  **A:** What you did, what you found (data), why it matters for health, and a request for others to help.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Complete a quiz on germs, dental care, sleep, health helpers and the environment.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 quiz.

- **Q:** How many hours of sleep do kids need?
  **A:** 9–12 hours.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 quiz; cleanup data graph

**Answer key:** Unit 6 quiz key: 1) Wash hands for 20 seconds with soap. 2) Sneeze into your elbow. 3) Bacteria vs. viruses: bacteria are single-celled; viruses are smaller and cause colds/flu. 4) Brush 2 minutes twice a day; floss once a day. 5) Kids need 9–12 hours of sleep. 6) Use SPF 30+ sunscreen. 7) A pharmacist prepares and explains medicine. 8) An AQI of 0–50 (green) is good. 9) Reduce, reuse, recycle. 10) Clean environments prevent illness. Graph proficient: bar graph with title, labeled axes, correct tallies, most common item identified. 8–10 = proficient.
