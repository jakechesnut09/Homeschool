# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 14

## ELA 3: Introduction to Greek myths

**Prepare:** Print 'The Paradise of Children' from Hawthorne's A Wonder-Book (Project Gutenberg); it is long and old-fashioned, so read aloud in two sittings or use a simpler retelling (e.g., D'Aulaires' Book of Greek Myths). Draw a blank chart for gods and symbols.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking myths are true history — myths are stories ancient people told to explain nature and teach values.
- Agreement errors ('The gods lives on Olympus') — a plural subject takes a verb without -s; a singular subject takes -s.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is a myth?
*Student:* Learn about Ancient Greece and the Olympian gods, then make a gods chart with symbols.

*How to teach:* Show Greece on a map and explain that ancient Greeks told myths about gods who lived on Mount Olympus. Read short descriptions of 6–8 Olympians and fill a chart with each god's power and symbol.

- **Q:** Who is king of the gods, and what is his symbol?
  **A:** Zeus; the thunderbolt.
- **Q:** Who is the goddess of wisdom, and what animal is her symbol?
  **A:** Athena; the owl.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pandora
*Student:* Read the Pandora myth ('The Paradise of Children') and recount it in sequence.

*How to teach:* Read the Pandora myth (Hawthorne's 'The Paradise of Children' or a retelling). Stop to predict before the box is opened. The child recounts the myth in sequence using First/Then/Next/Finally.

- **Q:** What happens when Pandora opens the box?
  **A:** Troubles fly out — in Hawthorne's version like stinging winged bugs — and the world is no longer carefree.
- **Q:** What is left in the box?
  **A:** Hope.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Agreement
*Student:* Practice subject-verb agreement with sentences about the gods; study Greek roots (bio, graph, photo).

*How to teach:* Model subject-verb agreement: 'Zeus rules' vs. 'The gods rule.' The child fixes 8 sentences about the gods. Then study Greek roots bio (life), graph (write) and photo (light) and build words.

- **Q:** Choose the verb: Zeus (rule/rules) the gods.
  **A:** rules.
- **Q:** What does 'biography' mean, using its roots?
  **A:** Writing (graph) about a life (bio) — a book about a person's life.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lesson
*Student:* Write what lesson the Pandora myth teaches about curiosity.

*How to teach:* Discuss what Pandora did wrong and whether curiosity is always bad. The child writes a paragraph stating the lesson and supporting it with events from the myth.

- **Q:** What lesson does the Pandora myth teach?
  **A:** Curiosity, or ignoring a warning, can cause trouble — but hope remains even in hard times.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Take a subject-verb agreement quiz and answer questions on the Pandora myth.

*How to teach:* Give the agreement quiz and ask the Pandora questions in the answer key. Afterward, have the child find the sentence in the myth that answers any missed question.

- **Q:** Who was Pandora's companion in Hawthorne's version?
  **A:** Epimetheus.

**Assessment:** Agreement quiz; myth recount

**Answer key:** Agreement quiz (choose the verb): 1) The god (rules) 2) The gods (rule) 3) Athena (weaves) 4) Pandora and Epimetheus (play) 5) The box (sits) 6) The Troubles (fly) 7) She (opens) 8) They (are). Proficient 7+/8. Pandora questions: what came out → Troubles; what remained → Hope; lesson → curiosity or disobeying a warning can cause harm, but hope remains. Recount: proficient if 4+ events are in correct order.

## Math 3: Multiplying by multiples of 10 and fluency

**Prepare:** Base-ten rods, fact cards, internet access for a Math Playground division game. Write the Unit 3 test in the answer key.

**Watch for:**
- 'Just add a zero' without understanding — say '4 × 3 tens = 12 tens = 120' so the zero comes from place value.
- Losing a zero when the basic fact already ends in 0 (5 × 40 = 20) — 5 × 4 tens = 20 tens = 200.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tens rods
*Student:* Model 4 × 30 with tens rods and record as 4 × 3 tens = 12 tens = 120.

*How to teach:* Lay out 4 groups of 3 rods; count 12 rods = 12 tens = 120. Record 4 × 30 = 4 × 3 tens = 12 tens = 120. The child models 6 more.

- **Q:** Find 4 × 30.
  **A:** 120.
- **Q:** Find 6 × 50 using tens.
  **A:** 6 × 5 tens = 30 tens = 300.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pattern practice
*Student:* Solve sets like 6 × 7, 6 × 70 and explain the place-value pattern.

*How to teach:* Solve pairs of facts: 6 × 7 = 42 and 6 × 70 = 420. The child explains the pattern: the product is 10 times bigger because one factor is 10 times bigger.

- **Q:** Find 6 × 7 and 6 × 70.
  **A:** 42 and 420.
- **Q:** Find 9 × 80.
  **A:** 720.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Word problems
*Student:* Solve 6 problems such as '8 boxes of 40 crayons' and check with skip counting by tens.

*How to teach:* Solve 6 word problems and check each by skip counting by tens or using the tens fact. Ask the child to say each problem as 'groups of tens' before solving (8 × 4 tens = 32 tens).

- **Q:** 8 boxes have 40 crayons each. How many crayons?
  **A:** 320.
- **Q:** A bus holds 60 people. How many people fit in 3 buses?
  **A:** 180.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Division fluency
*Student:* Practice division facts with fact family cards and a Math Playground fact game.

*How to teach:* Practice division facts with fact family cards, then play 10 minutes of an online fact game. Record facts that were slow.

- **Q:** 56 ÷ 8 = ?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** What is 81 ÷ 9, and what multiplication fact proves it?
  **A:** 9; 9 × 9 = 81.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 3 test on strategies, properties and multiples of 10, plus a fluency check.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test plus a 30-fact fluency check of mixed facts. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What is 7 × 60?
  **A:** 420.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test and fluency check

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test: 1) Use 2 × 7 to find 3 × 7 → 14 + 7 = 21 2) Use 2 × 8 to find 4 × 8 → 16 + 16 = 32 3) 6 × 9 = 5 × 9 + 1 × 9 → 45 + 9 = 54 4) (2 × 3) × 5 = 2 × (3 × 5) → 30 5) 9 × 8 → 72 6) 7 × 6 → 42 7) 5 × 70 → 350 8) 8 × 20 → 160 9) 9 × 40 → 360 10) 3 boxes of 50 pencils → 150. Proficient: 8+/10 on the test and 27+/30 on the fluency check.

## Science 3: Inherited traits

**Prepare:** Pictures of animal parents and offspring (cats and kittens, dogs and puppies), family photos, seeds from one plant (e.g., beans from one pod).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all traits are inherited — scars, haircuts and learned skills are not inherited.
- Tongue rolling is often taught as a simple inherited trait, but scientists have found it is not simply passed down — use eye color, hair color, freckles or earlobes instead and treat tongue rolling as a fun extra.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trait sort
*Student:* Sort animal parent and offspring pictures and identify inherited traits.

*How to teach:* Sort parent and offspring pictures. The child lists traits each young animal shares with its parents (fur color, ear shape, markings).

- **Q:** What is an inherited trait?
  **A:** A characteristic passed from parents to offspring, like eye color or fur pattern.
- **Q:** Is a dog's ability to sit on command inherited?
  **A:** No — it is learned.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family traits
*Student:* Survey family members for traits (earlobes, tongue rolling, hair color) and record data.

*How to teach:* Survey family members on traits (attached/free earlobes, hair color, eye color, dimples) and record in a tally table.

- **Q:** Is eye color inherited or learned?
  **A:** Inherited.
- **Q:** Is speaking English inherited?
  **A:** No — it is learned.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plants inherit
*Student:* Compare seeds from one plant to offspring plants.

*How to teach:* Compare beans from one plant to sprouts from those beans. The child notes shared traits like leaf shape and bean color.

- **Q:** Why do bean seedlings look like the parent bean plant?
  **A:** They inherited traits from the parent plant.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Graph
*Student:* Graph survey data on traits.

*How to teach:* The child makes a bar graph of one trait from the survey.

- **Q:** What does the tallest bar show?
  **A:** The most common trait in the group surveyed.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain what inherited traits are using examples.

*How to teach:* The child writes an explanation of inherited traits with 3 examples from the week.

- **Q:** Name one inherited and one non-inherited trait in people.
  **A:** Inherited: eye color. Not inherited: a scar or a haircut.

**Assessment:** Trait data graph and explanation

**Answer key:** Explanation: proficient (3) = defines inherited traits as passed from parents to offspring, gives 3 accurate examples (animal, plant, human) and one non-inherited example; 2 = 1–2 examples; 1 = confuses learned and inherited. Graph: title, labels and correct bars.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Ranchos and the Mexican era

**Prepare:** Search Calisphere for 'diseño' (hand-drawn rancho maps). A local street map to find Spanish or rancho names. Read a short page on ranchos and the hide and tallow trade.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking ranchos were small family farms. They were huge land grants (often thousands of acres) used mainly for cattle.
- Thinking vaqueros were cowboys from the American West. Vaqueros were Mexican and Native horsemen whose skills later shaped the American cowboy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ranchos
*Student:* Read about Mexican land grants, ranchos and the work of vaqueros.

*How to teach:* Read about Mexican land grants (after Mexico's independence in 1821 and the end of the missions in the 1830s) and vaquero work.

- **Q:** When did California become part of Mexico?
  **A:** In 1821, when Mexico won independence from Spain.
- **Q:** What did vaqueros do?
  **A:** Rode horses to herd, rope and care for cattle on ranchos.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Diseños
*Student:* Examine a hand-drawn rancho map (diseño) on Calisphere and identify landmarks used as boundaries.

*How to teach:* Look at a diseño and find landmarks used as boundaries (trees, rocks, creeks).

- **Q:** What did diseños use to mark boundaries?
  **A:** Natural landmarks such as creeks, hills, trees and rocks.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hide and tallow
*Student:* Learn how cattle hides and tallow were traded with ships and what was bought in return.

*How to teach:* Explain the hide and tallow trade: hides (called 'California banknotes') and tallow (fat for candles and soap) were traded to ships for manufactured goods.

- **Q:** What is tallow and what was it used for?
  **A:** Cattle fat, used to make candles and soap.
- **Q:** What did rancheros get in return for hides?
  **A:** Manufactured goods like cloth, shoes, tools, furniture and dishes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Place names
*Student:* Find rancho or Spanish place names on a local map and research one name's origin.

*How to teach:* Find rancho or Spanish place names on a local map and research one.

- **Q:** What does a Spanish place name near you mean?
  **A:** Answers vary by community; e.g., Los Angeles = 'the angels', Sacramento = 'sacrament', Rancho Cucamonga named for a rancho.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain in writing how the rancho economy worked and what names or traditions remain.

*How to teach:* The child writes how the rancho economy worked and what remains.

- **Q:** Name one rancho tradition that remains today.
  **A:** Rodeos, Spanish place names, ranch-style architecture, vaquero riding and roping skills.

**Assessment:** Rancho explanation

**Answer key:** Rancho explanation rubric: 4 = explains that after 1821 the Mexican government granted large ranchos; cattle were raised mainly for hides and tallow; vaqueros (many of them Native Californians) worked the cattle; hides and tallow were traded to ships (often from Boston) for goods like cloth, tools and furniture; and names a place name or tradition that remains (e.g., rodeo, a Rancho name on a street or city); 3 = most parts; 2 = some parts; 1 = inaccurate.

## Art & Music 3: Solfege and hand signs

**Prepare:** Print or display a solfege hand-sign chart (Curwen hand signs: do = fist, re = flat hand slanting up, mi = flat hand level, sol = palm facing body, la = hand drooping down). Songs: 'Rain, Rain, Go Away,' 'Ring Around the Rosie,' 'Hot Cross Buns,' 'Bow Wow Wow' (all traditional).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking solfege names are fixed letter names. Solfege shows a note's place in the scale (do is home), not a fixed pitch.
- Making hand signs at the same height. Place higher notes higher in front of the body to show pitch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sol-mi
*Student:* Learn sol and mi hand signs and sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' with them.

*How to teach:* Teach sol (higher) and mi (lower) hand signs. Sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' with sol-mi signs — it uses only these two notes.

- **Q:** Which is higher, sol or mi?
  **A:** Sol.
- **Q:** Which two notes does 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' use?
  **A:** Sol and mi.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Adding la
*Student:* Add la and sing 'Ring Around the Rosie' with hand signs.

*How to teach:* Add la (one step above sol). Sing 'Ring Around the Rosie' with signs (sol-sol-mi-la-sol-mi).

- **Q:** Where is la compared to sol?
  **A:** One step higher than sol.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Do-re-mi
*Student:* Add do, re and mi and sing the pentatonic scale up and down.

*How to teach:* Add do, re and mi and sing the pentatonic scale do-re-mi-sol-la up and down with signs.

- **Q:** Which notes are in the pentatonic scale we sang?
  **A:** Do, re, mi, sol, la (five notes).
- **Q:** Which two notes are skipped from the full scale?
  **A:** Fa and ti.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Folk song
*Student:* Sing a pentatonic folk song ('Bow Wow Wow' or 'Hot Cross Buns') in solfege.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Hot Cross Buns' in solfege: mi-re-do. Then sing it with words and signs.

- **Q:** What solfege notes begin 'Hot Cross Buns'?
  **A:** Mi, re, do.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Echo-sing 8 solfege patterns with correct hand signs.

*How to teach:* Sing 8 short solfege patterns; the child echoes with correct signs (e.g., sol-mi-la-sol, do-re-mi, mi-re-do).

- **Q:** Show the hand sign for do.
  **A:** A closed fist at about waist height.

**Assessment:** Solfege echo check

**Answer key:** Solfege echo check (8 patterns): suggested patterns — 1) sol-mi 2) sol-sol-mi 3) sol-la-sol-mi 4) mi-re-do 5) do-re-mi 6) do-mi-sol 7) la-sol-mi 8) do-re-mi-sol-la. 1 point for correct pitch and 1 for correct hand signs each (16 total). Proficient = 13+.

## PE 3: Throwing games

**Prepare:** Collect 10 plastic bottles (add a little sand or water so they stand), 2–3 balls, tape for a throw line, and the week 11 target tally. Graph paper for the accuracy graph. For hit-the-pin, set a bottle or cone 'pin' in front of each player.

**Watch for:**
- Faking means throwing for real. A fake is a pretend throw motion to make a defender move, then you pass to the open side.
- Thinking fair play only matters when winning. Fair play means following rules, taking turns and being honest whether winning or losing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Target game
*Student:* Set up 10 plastic bottles and knock them down with overhand throws from a set line.

*How to teach:* Set 10 bottles in a triangle or line. The child throws overhand from the line, using all 4 cues, and counts how many throws it takes to knock all 10 down.

- **Q:** Which cues help you hit the bottles?
  **A:** Side to target, arm back, step with the opposite foot, follow through toward the target.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Keep-away
*Student:* Play monkey in the middle, using fakes and quick passes.

*How to teach:* Play monkey in the middle: two throwers pass while the middle player tries to intercept. Teach the fake (start a throw one way, pass another way) and quick passes. Rotate after an interception.

- **Q:** Why use a fake?
  **A:** It makes the defender move the wrong way, opening space for a pass.
- **Q:** What kind of pass is hard for the monkey to catch?
  **A:** A quick, accurate pass away from the defender — over or around them, not right at them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Practice log
*Student:* Re-test the wall target and graph your accuracy improvement since week 11.

*How to teach:* Re-test 20 throws at the week 11 target from 15 feet. Make a bar graph comparing week 11 hits to today's hits.

- **Q:** Week 11: 7 hits. Today: 12 hits. How much did you improve?
  **A:** 12 − 7 = 5 more hits.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hit the pin
*Student:* Play a family game of hit-the-pin with turn-taking and fair-play rules.

*How to teach:* Play hit-the-pin: each player protects a pin; take turns throwing to knock down others' pins. Set fair-play rules first (take turns, no throwing at people, honest calls).

- **Q:** What should you do if your pin is knocked down?
  **A:** Admit it honestly, say 'Nice shot,' and follow the game rule (reset or rotate).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a throw and catch skill check scored on the cue checklist.

*How to teach:* Skill check: observe 5 throws and 5 catches; score each throw on the 4 cues and each catch on reach and give / correct hand position.

- **Q:** Show me how you'd catch a ball thrown at your belly button and one at your ankles.
  **A:** Belly button: thumbs together (or hands ready to pull in); ankles: pinkies together.

**Assessment:** Throw/catch skill check

**Answer key:** Throw/catch skill check: Throws (5) — 1 point per cue observed on each throw (side, arm back, opposite step, follow through), max 20. Catches (5) — 1 point each for a clean hands catch with correct hand position. Proficient = 16+ throw points and 4 of 5 catches. 4 = 18–20 and 5/5; 3 = 14–17 and 4/5; 2 = 10–13 or 3/5; 1 = below.

## Health 3: Healthy friendships

**Prepare:** Borrow 'The Invisible Boy' by Trudy Ludwig from the library (or another friendship picture book) and read it ahead. Paper for the friendship recipe.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a friend has to like everything you like. Good friends can be different; respect and kindness matter more than sameness.
- Thinking including others is only for adults or teachers to do. Kids can notice someone alone and invite them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Friend qualities
*Student:* List 8 qualities of a good friend and rank your top 3.

*How to teach:* The child lists 8 qualities of a good friend and ranks the top 3, explaining why.

- **Q:** Name three qualities of a good friend.
  **A:** Any three: kind, honest, loyal, good listener, shares, includes others, helpful, fun, respectful.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Empathy
*Student:* Read 'The Invisible Boy' (or similar) and discuss how Brian felt and what changed.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Invisible Boy' aloud. Discuss how Brian felt being left out and what changed when Justin arrived and was kind to him.

- **Q:** How did Brian feel at the beginning of the story?
  **A:** Invisible and lonely, because other kids didn't notice or include him.
- **Q:** What changed things for Brian?
  **A:** Brian was kind to the new boy, Justin, who then included him; when Brian was invited to work with Justin and another classmate, he began to feel seen.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Differences
*Student:* Interview a friend or relative about something different about them and something you share.

*How to teach:* The child interviews a friend or relative and records one thing different about them and one thing they share.

- **Q:** What is empathy?
  **A:** Understanding and caring about how someone else feels, like imagining being in their shoes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Inclusion
*Student:* Plan 3 ways to include someone left out at a park, co-op or team practice.

*How to teach:* Brainstorm 3 ways to include someone left out: invite them to play, sit with them, ask their name and what they like.

- **Q:** You see a kid alone at the park. What could you say?
  **A:** Example: 'Hi, I'm ___. Want to play with us?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a 'recipe for friendship' with ingredients and steps.

*How to teach:* The child writes a 'recipe for friendship' with ingredients and steps.

- **Q:** What is one 'ingredient' in your recipe and why?
  **A:** Any friend quality with a reason, e.g., 'honesty, so friends can trust each other.'

**Assessment:** Friendship recipe

**Answer key:** Friendship recipe rubric: 4 = lists at least 5 'ingredients' (qualities such as kindness, honesty, listening, sharing, including others) and 3+ steps that are actions (e.g., 'Mix in listening by asking about their day'); 3 = 3–4 ingredients and 2 steps; 2 = list only, no steps; 1 = incomplete.
