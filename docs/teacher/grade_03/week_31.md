# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 31

## ELA 3: Taking notes and comparing sources

**Prepare:** Note cards, the sources from last week, a two-column comparison chart.

**Watch for:**
- Copying sentences word for word — notes must be short and in your own words, with the source written on each card.
- Ignoring when sources disagree — check a third reliable source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Note cards
*Student:* Take notes in your own words on note cards from source 1.

*How to teach:* Model reading a paragraph, covering it, and writing a short note from memory. The child takes notes on cards from source 1, one fact per card, labeled with the source.

- **Q:** Write a note for: 'Half Dome is a granite dome that rises about 4,700 feet above the floor of Yosemite Valley.'
  **A:** Example: Half Dome — granite, about 4,700 ft above valley floor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sort notes
*Student:* Sort note cards into categories (location, plants and animals, history, visiting).

*How to teach:* The child sorts all cards into 4 piles: location, plants and animals, history, and visiting. Cards that don't fit go into an 'extra' pile.

- **Q:** Name the 4 categories for sorting notes.
  **A:** Location, plants and animals, history, visiting.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare sources
*Student:* Compare key points from two sources on your topic in a chart.

*How to teach:* The child takes notes from source 2 and fills a chart showing key points each source gives on the same topics. Model one row of the chart, then let the child complete the rest.

- **Q:** Why compare two sources?
  **A:** To check that facts agree and to find information one source left out.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Context clues
*Student:* Use context clues to figure out unknown words in your sources; review affixes.

*How to teach:* Find 4 unfamiliar words in the sources. Model using context clues (nearby words, examples) and affixes (un-, re-, -less) to figure out meanings, then check in a dictionary.

- **Q:** What does 'endangered' mean in 'The California condor is an endangered bird'?
  **A:** In danger of dying out completely.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph on what two sources agree and disagree on.

*How to teach:* The child writes a paragraph explaining what the two sources agree on and where they differ or give different information. Model the frame: 'Both sources say ___, but source 1 says ___ while source 2 says ___.'

- **Q:** What should you do if two sources disagree on a fact?
  **A:** Check a third reliable source (such as a National Park Service site).

**Assessment:** Notes and source comparison paragraph

**Answer key:** Proficient: 10+ notes in own words, each labeled with its source, sorted into the 4 categories; comparison paragraph names both sources and gives at least one point of agreement and one difference.

## Math 3: Quadrilaterals and attributes

**Prepare:** Shape cards (draw and cut squares, rectangles, rhombuses, trapezoids, kites, triangles, pentagons, hexagons), a geoboard with rubber bands or dot paper. Write the quiz in the answer key.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a square is not a rectangle — a square has 4 right angles, so it is a special rectangle.
- Thinking a tilted square is a 'diamond', not a square — rotate the card; attributes, not position, define a shape.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sort shapes
*Student:* Sort shape cards by sides, angles and parallel sides and name each sort.

*How to teach:* Sort shape cards by number of sides, then by right angles, then by parallel sides; name each sorting rule. Ask the child to say the attribute out loud each time they place a card in a group.

- **Q:** How many sides does a quadrilateral have?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** Which shapes have 4 right angles?
  **A:** Squares and rectangles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Quadrilateral family
*Student:* Make a chart showing how squares, rectangles and rhombuses are all quadrilaterals.

*How to teach:* Make a chart: quadrilaterals → rectangles (4 right angles) and rhombuses (4 equal sides); squares belong in both groups. Draw it as nested circles or a family tree, and have the child place a square card in every group it belongs to.

- **Q:** Why is a square a rhombus?
  **A:** It has 4 equal sides.
- **Q:** Why is a rectangle a quadrilateral?
  **A:** It has 4 sides.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geoboard builds
*Student:* Build quadrilaterals on a geoboard or dot paper that are not rectangles, rhombuses or squares.

*How to teach:* Build quadrilaterals on a geoboard that are not rectangles, rhombuses or squares (trapezoid, kite, irregular). Have the child count sides and check right angles with a paper corner to prove each shape fits.

- **Q:** Name a quadrilateral that is not a rectangle, rhombus or square.
  **A:** A trapezoid (or a kite, or any irregular 4-sided shape).
- **Q:** Is a trapezoid a quadrilateral?
  **A:** Yes — it has 4 sides.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Always, sometimes, never
*Student:* Discuss statements like 'A square is a rhombus' and justify.

*How to teach:* Discuss statements as always, sometimes or never true and justify each. Have the child draw an example or counterexample to support each answer.

- **Q:** 'A square is a rhombus.'
  **A:** Always — it has 4 equal sides.
- **Q:** 'A rectangle is a square.'
  **A:** Sometimes — only when all 4 sides are equal.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a shape attributes quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the shape attributes quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Does a rhombus always have right angles?
  **A:** No — only when it is a square.

**Assessment:** Quadrilateral classification quiz

**Answer key:** 1) A quadrilateral has → 4 sides 2) Is a square a rectangle? → yes 3) Is a rhombus a square? → sometimes 4) Draw a quadrilateral that is not a rectangle → e.g., trapezoid 5) Is a triangle a quadrilateral? → no 6) Shape with 4 equal sides and 4 right angles → square 7) A rectangle has 4 right angles: always/sometimes/never → always 8) Is a kite a quadrilateral? → yes. Proficient: 7+/8.

## Science 3: Weather-related hazards

**Prepare:** NOAA SciJinks, kid-friendly news articles about recent California floods, droughts or wildfires (preview for upsetting images), chart paper. Parent note: talk calmly about hazards and focus on how families prepare.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking hazards can be prevented — we can't stop storms, but we can reduce their impacts.
- Thinking hazards happen only elsewhere — California has floods, droughts, wildfires and heat waves.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hazards
*Student:* Read SciJinks pages and list 6 weather-related hazards with a sentence describing each.

*How to teach:* Read SciJinks pages. The child lists 6 weather hazards and writes a sentence for each.

- **Q:** Name 6 weather-related hazards.
  **A:** Floods, droughts, wildfires, thunderstorms/lightning, hurricanes, tornadoes (also blizzards, heat waves).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California hazards
*Student:* Research flooding, drought and wildfire in California, including a recent event.

*How to teach:* Research flooding, drought and wildfire in California, including one recent event.

- **Q:** Why are wildfires common in California in late summer and fall?
  **A:** Hot, dry weather dries out plants, and strong winds (like the Santa Ana winds) spread fires.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Impacts
*Student:* Make a chart of each hazard's impacts on people, homes, animals and land.

*How to teach:* Make a chart of each hazard's impacts on people, homes, animals and land.

- **Q:** Name an impact of a flood on homes.
  **A:** Water damage, mud, and people having to leave.
- **Q:** Name an impact of drought on land.
  **A:** Dry soil, dying crops and plants, and more fire risk.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Preparation
*Student:* Create a family emergency kit checklist and evacuation plan for the hazard most likely where you live.

*How to teach:* The child creates a family emergency kit checklist and evacuation plan for the most likely local hazard.

- **Q:** Name 5 items for an emergency kit.
  **A:** Water, nonperishable food, flashlight with batteries, first-aid kit, medicines (also radio, phone charger, copies of documents).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain in a paragraph how one hazard affects a community and how people prepare.

*How to teach:* The child writes a paragraph on how one hazard affects a community and how people prepare.

- **Q:** How can families prepare for wildfire?
  **A:** Clear dry brush near the home, make an evacuation plan, pack a go-bag, and sign up for local alerts.

**Assessment:** Hazard impact chart and paragraph

**Answer key:** Hazard chart: proficient if 3 hazards each have accurate impacts in all 4 categories. Paragraph: 3 = hazard, 2 impacts, 2 preparation steps; 2 = partial; 1 = inaccurate.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): American heroes: leaders for equality

**Prepare:** Text of the Gettysburg Address (November 19, 1863) and an excerpt of 'I Have a Dream' (August 28, 1963). A short biography of Anne Hutchinson. Prepare the Unit 7 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'Four score and seven years ago' is a date. A score is 20, so it means 87 years earlier, pointing to 1776.
- Thinking Dr. King's dream was only for one group. He dreamed of equality and brotherhood for all people.

### Day 1 (Monday): Abraham Lincoln
*Student:* Read the Gettysburg Address and discuss its message of 'a new birth of freedom.'

*How to teach:* Read the Gettysburg Address aloud and discuss 'a new birth of freedom' and 'government of the people, by the people, for the people.'

- **Q:** What does 'four score and seven years ago' mean?
  **A:** 87 years ago (a score is 20), meaning 1776.
- **Q:** What did Lincoln mean by 'a new birth of freedom'?
  **A:** That the nation should be reborn with freedom for all, including ending slavery.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Martin Luther King Jr.
*Student:* Read an excerpt of 'I Have a Dream' and list the dreams he describes.

*How to teach:* Read the 'I Have a Dream' excerpt and list the dreams.

- **Q:** Name one dream Dr. King described.
  **A:** That his four children would be judged by the content of their character, not the color of their skin; or that children of former slaves and former slave owners would sit together at the table of brotherhood.
- **Q:** Where did Dr. King give the speech?
  **A:** At the March on Washington, at the Lincoln Memorial, in 1963.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Anne Hutchinson
*Student:* Learn how Anne Hutchinson stood up for religious freedom.

*How to teach:* Learn how Anne Hutchinson held religious meetings in Massachusetts Bay Colony, was put on trial and banished (1638), and moved to Rhode Island.

- **Q:** How did Anne Hutchinson stand up for religious freedom?
  **A:** She held meetings to discuss her own religious ideas, even though colony leaders disagreed; she was banished and moved to Rhode Island.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hero report
*Student:* Write a one-page report on a chosen hero with a portrait.

*How to teach:* The child writes a one-page hero report with a portrait.

- **Q:** What makes your hero a hero?
  **A:** Answer names specific actions and why they helped others.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit test on symbols, documents and heroes.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** Who gave the Gettysburg Address?
  **A:** Abraham Lincoln.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 test; hero report

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test key: 1) 13 stripes = original colonies; 50 stars = states. 2) The Statue of Liberty was a gift from France. 3) The Liberty Bell is in Philadelphia. 4) The Declaration was adopted July 4, 1776. 5) Jefferson wrote most of it. 6) California's motto is 'Eureka.' 7) Douglass secretly learned to read. 8) Tubman guided enslaved people to freedom on the Underground Railroad. 9) Lincoln gave the Gettysburg Address in 1863. 10) Dr. King dreamed children would be judged by 'the content of their character,' not 'the color of their skin.' Hero report rubric: 4 = one page with birth/death dates, 3+ accomplishments, a challenge faced, why they are a hero, and a portrait; 3 = most parts; 2 = few facts; 1 = incomplete. 8–10 = proficient.

## Art & Music 3: Performing original music

**Prepare:** Final song and ostinato, a phone or tablet to record, paper for program notes, chairs for the 'audience.' Review concert etiquette from week 17.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking program notes retell every note. Program notes briefly tell the audience what the piece is about and how it was made.
- Only judging whether the performance was 'good.' Evaluate with specific criteria: steady beat, accurate pitch, expression.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse your song 3 times with accompaniment.

*How to teach:* Rehearse 3 times with accompaniment, focusing on starting together, steady tempo and a clear ending.

- **Q:** How do you start a performance together?
  **A:** Count off (e.g., '1, 2, 3, 4') in the tempo of the song.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Program notes
*Student:* Write program notes explaining your song's meaning and how you made it.

*How to teach:* Write 3–5 sentences of program notes: song title, what it is about, how you composed it, and something to listen for.

- **Q:** What should program notes include?
  **A:** The title, what the song is about, how it was made, and something to listen for.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Perform
*Student:* Perform the premiere for family, reading your program notes first.

*How to teach:* Premiere: read the program notes, perform, and bow. Record the performance.

- **Q:** What does 'premiere' mean?
  **A:** The very first public performance of a new piece.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluate
*Student:* Watch a recording and score it on criteria (steady beat, accurate pitch, expression).

*How to teach:* Watch the recording and score it 1–4 on steady beat, accurate pitch, and expression with evidence.

- **Q:** What evidence shows a steady beat?
  **A:** The tempo didn't speed up or slow down and the accompaniment stayed together with the voice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Write a reflection on the whole composing process.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection on the whole process (improvise → draft → revise → perform): what was hardest, what you're proud of, what you'd do next time.

- **Q:** Name the steps you used to compose your song.
  **A:** Improvise ideas, write lyrics, set rhythms, create a melody, notate, get feedback, revise, add accompaniment, rehearse, perform.

**Assessment:** Performance and reflection

**Answer key:** Performance and reflection rubric: Performance (steady beat, accurate pitch, expression) each scored 1–4; Reflection: 4 = describes each stage of the process with specific examples, names a challenge and a success and a next step; 3 = most stages with some detail; 2 = brief; 1 = missing. Proficient = 3+ average.

## PE 3: Kickball and base games

**Prepare:** Rubber playground ball (kickball), 4 bases (paper plates or cones) about 30–45 feet apart, a stopwatch, and players (family/co-op). Review rules on a card.

**Watch for:**
- Running straight to first base and stopping. Run through first base; when going for second, round first by curving out a little before the base.
- Thinking a fly ball means the runner keeps running. On a caught fly ball, runners must tag up (return to touch their base) before advancing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kickball rules
*Student:* Learn kickball rules (fair/foul, outs, innings) and practice rolling pitches.

*How to teach:* Teach rules: pitcher rolls the ball; the kicker kicks it; fair if it lands inside the first and third base lines; foul outside. Outs: caught fly, ball to base before runner, or tagging/hitting the runner below the shoulders (if your family allows). 3 outs switch sides. Practice smooth rolling pitches.

- **Q:** When is a kick foul?
  **A:** When it goes outside the first-base or third-base line (out of fair territory).
- **Q:** How many outs before the teams switch?
  **A:** 3 outs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Kicking for placement
*Student:* Practice kicking a rolled ball to open spaces in the field.

*How to teach:* Roll pitches. The child kicks to left, center or right, aiming between fielders (cones).

- **Q:** Where should you aim your kick?
  **A:** Into open space between or past fielders.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Base running
*Student:* Practice running bases, rounding first base and tagging up, with timed runs.

*How to teach:* Time runs to first (run through the base) and around all bases (rounding each by curving out before the base). Practice tagging up: start on the base and leave only after the catch.

- **Q:** What does tagging up mean?
  **A:** After a fly ball is caught, the runner must touch their base before running to the next one.
- **Q:** Why do you round a base?
  **A:** Curving out a little before the base lets you turn and keep running at full speed to the next base.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play a 3-inning kickball game, cheering for both teams with specific encouragement.

*How to teach:* Play 3 innings. Everyone cheers for both teams with specific encouragement ('Great kick to open space!').

- **Q:** Give an example of specific encouragement.
  **A:** E.g., 'Nice catch with your hands out!' or 'Great job rounding first!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a game play check on kicking, fielding and sportsmanship.

*How to teach:* Game play check: observe kicking (contact, aiming), fielding (moving to the ball, throwing to base), and sportsmanship.

- **Q:** If you are fielding a ground ball and a runner is going to first, where do you throw?
  **A:** To first base (to get the runner out).

**Assessment:** Game play check

**Answer key:** Game play check rubric: Kicking — makes contact and aims to open space; Fielding — moves to the ball, fields it and throws to the correct base; Sportsmanship — follows rules, encourages both teams. 4 = proficient in all 3; 3 = proficient in 2; 2 = proficient in 1; 1 = needs help in all.

## Health 3: Health helpers

**Prepare:** Arrange a short phone or in-person interview with a health worker (relative who is a nurse, family dentist, etc.). Plan a pharmacy visit at a quiet time. Interview questions sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking pharmacists just hand out boxes. They check that medicines are safe together and the dose is right, and answer questions.
- Thinking public health is only hospitals. Public health workers inspect restaurants, test water and track diseases.

### Day 1 (Monday): Helpers
*Student:* List health helpers (doctor, nurse, dentist, pharmacist, school nurse, optometrist) and what each does.

*How to teach:* The child lists 6 health helpers and their jobs.

- **Q:** What does an optometrist do?
  **A:** Examines eyes, checks vision, and prescribes glasses or contacts.
- **Q:** What does a pharmacist do?
  **A:** Prepares and gives out medicines, checks that they are safe, and explains how to use them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a health worker (in person or by phone) with 5 prepared questions.

*How to teach:* The child interviews a health worker with 5 prepared questions and takes notes.

- **Q:** Write one good interview question.
  **A:** Examples: 'What does a typical day look like?' 'What training did you need?' 'How do you help keep people healthy?'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pharmacy
*Student:* Visit a pharmacy counter and learn how pharmacists keep medicine safe.

*How to teach:* At the pharmacy counter, the child observes and asks (if appropriate) how pharmacists keep medicine safe.

- **Q:** Name one way a pharmacist keeps medicine safe.
  **A:** Checks the dose, checks for harmful interactions with other medicines, labels clearly, and explains how to take it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Public health
*Student:* Learn how public health workers keep water, food and restaurants safe.

*How to teach:* Explain public health: inspectors check restaurant kitchens, water treatment plants clean water, and health departments track outbreaks and give vaccines.

- **Q:** How do public health workers keep restaurants safe?
  **A:** They inspect kitchens to make sure food is stored, cooked and handled safely and the place is clean.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a 'health helpers' booklet with one page per helper.

*How to teach:* The child completes the booklet.

- **Q:** Who would you see for a toothache?
  **A:** A dentist.

**Assessment:** Health helpers booklet

**Answer key:** Health helpers booklet rubric: 4 = at least 6 pages (doctor – checks health and treats illness; nurse – cares for patients, gives shots; dentist – cares for teeth; pharmacist – fills prescriptions and explains medicine; school nurse – first aid and health at school; optometrist – checks eyes and prescribes glasses) each with an accurate job and picture, plus the interview page; 3 = 4–5 pages accurate; 2 = 2–3 pages or errors; 1 = incomplete.
