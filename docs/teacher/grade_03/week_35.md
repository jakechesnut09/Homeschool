# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 35

## ELA 3: Readers' theater

**Prepare:** A short readers' theater script adapted from a fable or Potter tale (search 'readers theater Aesop' for free scripts), highlighters, a recording device.

**Watch for:**
- Reading stage directions aloud — they tell actors what to do and are not spoken.
- Rushing lines — slow down, pause, and use expression.

### Day 1 (Monday): Script features
*Student:* Read a readers' theater script and identify cast list, scenes and stage directions.

*How to teach:* Read a sample script together. The child highlights the cast list, scene headings and stage directions and explains each one's purpose.

- **Q:** What are stage directions?
  **A:** Instructions in brackets or italics that tell actors what to do; they are not read aloud.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write a script
*Student:* Convert a fable into a short script, turning narrative into dialogue.

*How to teach:* Model turning one narration line into dialogue. The child converts a fable into a short script with a narrator, characters and 2–3 scenes.

- **Q:** Turn 'The fox asked the crow to sing' into a script line.
  **A:** FOX: Oh, beautiful Crow, won't you sing a song for me?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the script with expression and timing.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the script several times. Coach volume, expression and waiting for cues.

- **Q:** How should the narrator read?
  **A:** Clearly and steadily, setting the scene for the audience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Perform
*Student:* Perform for family and record it.

*How to teach:* Perform the script for the family and record it. Remind performers to face the audience and hold scripts below their faces.

- **Q:** Why record a performance?
  **A:** To watch it later and see what went well and what to improve.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Reflect on the performance and identify how scenes build on each other.

*How to teach:* Watch the recording together. The child reflects on what went well and explains how each scene built on the one before.

- **Q:** How do scenes build on each other in a play?
  **A:** Each scene moves the plot forward — introducing the problem, building to the turning point, then the ending.

**Assessment:** Script and performance

**Answer key:** Script: 3 = cast list, 2+ scenes, dialogue for each character, stage directions; 2 = missing one element; 1 = incomplete. Performance: 3 = expressive, clear and on cue; 2 = mostly clear; 1 = hard to follow.

## Math 3: Cumulative review: operations and fluency

**Prepare:** Fact cards, a review packet with problems from Units 1–5, a board game and dice for the target-number game. Write the cumulative test.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing fluency and making careless errors — encourage known-fact strategies instead of guessing.
- Skipping estimation on 3-digit problems — always check reasonableness.

### Day 1 (Monday): Fact fluency
*Student:* Complete a full multiplication and division fact assessment.

*How to teach:* Give a full multiplication and division fact assessment (all facts through 10 × 10). Record facts that still need practice.

- **Q:** 7 × 8 and 54 ÷ 9?
  **A:** 56; 6.
- **Q:** 9 × 6 and 63 ÷ 7?
  **A:** 54; 9.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add/subtract review
*Student:* Solve mixed 3-digit add/subtract problems with estimation.

*How to teach:* Solve mixed 3-digit addition and subtraction problems, estimating first. Have the child circle any answer that is far from the estimate and redo it.

- **Q:** Estimate, then solve 467 + 285.
  **A:** Estimate 470 + 290 = 760; exact 752 — reasonable.
- **Q:** Solve 803 − 457.
  **A:** 346.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Problem solving
*Student:* Solve 5 multi-step word problems and explain thinking.

*How to teach:* Solve 5 multi-step word problems and explain the thinking aloud. Ask the child to draw a bar model for any problem they find tricky.

- **Q:** A class has 4 tables of 6 kids, and 5 kids go home. How many are left?
  **A:** 24 − 5 = 19.
- **Q:** You have $100 and buy 3 items at $25 each. How much is left?
  **A:** $25.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Math games day
*Student:* Play a fact board game and a target-number game to practice operations.

*How to teach:* Play a fact board game, then a target-number game: roll dice and use any operations to hit a target. Keep it playful and have the child say each equation aloud as they play.

- **Q:** Use 3, 4 and 2 to make 24.
  **A:** 3 × 4 × 2 = 24.
- **Q:** Use 5, 6 and 1 to make 31.
  **A:** 5 × 6 + 1 = 31.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a cumulative operations test.

*How to teach:* Give the cumulative operations test. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What is 8 × 70?
  **A:** 560.

**Assessment:** Cumulative operations test

**Answer key:** 1) 345 + 278 → 623 2) 900 − 362 → 538 3) Round 649 to the nearest 10 → 650 4) 7 × 9 → 63 5) 48 ÷ 6 → 8 6) 6 × 40 → 240 7) ? × 7 = 42 → 6 8) 5 packs of 8 pencils, 12 used → 28 9) $60 − 4 × $9 → $24 10) Is 7 × 3 odd or even? → odd (21). Proficient: 8+/10.

## Science 3: Engineering: improve and present

**Prepare:** The improved model, poster board, graph paper, markers. Write the Unit 9 quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Making graphs without titles, labels or scales.
- Claiming the design 'works' without data — use test results as evidence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final test
*Student:* Run 3 final wind and rain trials on the improved model and record results.

*How to teach:* Run 3 final wind trials and 3 rain trials using the same conditions as before. The child records every result.

- **Q:** Why run 3 trials?
  **A:** To make sure results are reliable and not a one-time fluke.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Data
*Student:* Make a bar graph comparing performance before and after improvements.

*How to teach:* The child makes a bar graph comparing the original and improved designs (e.g., highest fan speed survived, number of wet spots).

- **Q:** What should the graph compare?
  **A:** Results before and after the improvements, with a title and labeled axes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poster
*Student:* Create a poster showing the problem, design, test data and improvements.

*How to teach:* The child makes a poster with the problem, criteria and constraints, design sketch, test data graph and improvements.

- **Q:** What belongs on the poster?
  **A:** The problem, criteria and constraints, the design, the data and the improvements made.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the solution and argue how well it meets the criteria and constraints.

*How to teach:* The child presents to the family and argues how well the design meets each criterion and constraint, using the data.

- **Q:** How do you prove the design meets the criteria?
  **A:** Point to test data, e.g., 'It stayed standing on high in all 3 trials and the towel stayed dry.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit quiz on weather hazards and the engineering design process.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Order the main steps of the engineering design process.
  **A:** Define the problem, research and brainstorm, plan, build, test, improve, and share.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 quiz; design presentation

**Answer key:** Unit 9 quiz: 1) Name 3 weather hazards → e.g., flood, wildfire, drought 2) One impact of flooding → damaged homes 3) How a levee helps and one trade-off → holds back floodwater; can push water downstream or fail 4) Criteria vs. constraints → what success looks like vs. limits 5) Design process steps in order 6) Why test more than once → reliable results. Proficient 5+/6. Presentation: 3 = problem, criteria, data and improvements explained with evidence; 2 = missing data; 1 = incomplete.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Human capital

**Prepare:** An interview sheet; arrange time with a family member about their job. Prepare the Unit 8 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking human capital means people are things. It means the skills and knowledge people have and can grow through learning and practice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Human capital
*Student:* Define human capital as the skills and knowledge people bring to work.

*How to teach:* Define human capital and give examples (reading, math, carpentry skills).

- **Q:** What is human capital?
  **A:** The skills and knowledge people have that help them do work.
- **Q:** How can a person increase their human capital?
  **A:** By going to school, training, practicing and learning new skills.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Skills
*Student:* List skills you have built in school this year and how each could be used in a job.

*How to teach:* The child lists skills built this year and how each could be used in a job.

- **Q:** How could being good at math help in a job?
  **A:** e.g., a carpenter measures, a cashier makes change, an engineer designs.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Career interview
*Student:* Interview a family member about the skills their job requires and how they learned them.

*How to teach:* The child interviews a family member about job skills.

- **Q:** What skills does your family member's job require?
  **A:** Answer from the interview.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Goals
*Student:* Set 2 learning goals for next year that build your human capital.

*How to teach:* Set 2 learning goals for next year.

- **Q:** Why does learning new skills help a community's economy?
  **A:** Skilled workers can do more and better work, helping businesses and the community grow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit test on resources, trade, choices and human capital.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** What is opportunity cost?
  **A:** The next-best choice you give up when you make a decision.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 test

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test key: 1) Natural resource example: water/soil/trees. 2) Human resource: workers and their skills. 3) Capital resource: tools, machines, buildings. 4) Goods are things you can touch; services are work done for others. 5) Specialization = focusing on what you make best. 6) Trade happens because of climate, cost and specialization. 7) Scarcity = limited resources. 8) Opportunity cost = the next-best choice given up. 9) Human capital = skills and knowledge people bring to work. 10) People build human capital through school, training and practice. 8–10 = proficient.

## Art & Music 3: Concert preparation

**Prepare:** List of songs from the year (unit 4 and 6 songs, the original song), accompaniment instruments, paper or a computer for designing a program.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the program order doesn't matter. Concerts usually open strong, vary tempos and moods, and end with a highlight (often the original piece).
- Skipping the dress rehearsal. A full run-through in order shows transitions and timing problems.

### Day 1 (Monday): Program
*Student:* Choose 4 songs from the year (including your original song) for a concert.

*How to teach:* Choose 4 songs (including the original) and decide the order. Explain the reasons (contrast, energy, ending strong).

- **Q:** Why vary fast and slow songs in a concert?
  **A:** Contrast keeps the audience interested and lets the performer rest their voice.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the songs learned in units 4 and 6.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the songs from units 4 and 6, reviewing words, pitch and expression markings.

- **Q:** What expression will you use in your folk song?
  **A:** Answers vary; e.g., soft dynamics for a gentle song, crescendo toward the end.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse original
*Student:* Rehearse your original song with accompaniment.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the original song with its ostinato accompaniment, focusing on a confident start and ending.

- **Q:** How will you start together with your accompanist?
  **A:** Count off in tempo (e.g., '1, 2, ready, go').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Run the full program in order with introductions.

*How to teach:* Dress rehearsal: run the whole program in order with spoken introductions and bows, without stopping.

- **Q:** What do you do if you make a mistake in a performance?
  **A:** Keep going without stopping, as if nothing happened.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Design and print a concert program.

*How to teach:* Design and print a concert program: title, date, performer, song titles in order with composer/origin (e.g., 'Sakura' — traditional Japanese; your song — by you).

- **Q:** What information goes in a concert program?
  **A:** Concert title, date, performer(s), the songs in order with composers or origins, and optional program notes.

**Assessment:** Concert program

**Answer key:** Concert program check: 4 = program lists 4 songs in a planned order with composer/origin for each (traditional songs labeled 'traditional' with culture, original song credited to the child), title, date and performer, attractively designed; dress rehearsal run without stopping; 3 = one missing element; 2 = several missing; 1 = no program.

## PE 3: Field day

**Prepare:** Arrange field day with family or co-op friends. Equipment: cones, measuring tape, stopwatch, softball, pillowcases, ties, water balloons, relay obstacle items (ball, hoops, cones). Have water and shade. Make simple sportsmanship award certificates.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only winners did well. Effort, improvement and kindness are also wins.
- Throwing water balloons too hard. Use a soft underhand toss and 'reach and give' to catch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Field day 1
*Student:* Compete in the 50-yard dash, standing long jump and softball throw with family or co-op friends.

*How to teach:* Run the 50-yard dash, standing long jump and softball throw. Record each result.

- **Q:** How do you start a sprint?
  **A:** Ready position: one foot forward, knees bent, leaning forward, eyes ahead; push off on 'Go.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Field day 2
*Student:* Complete sack race, three-legged race and water-balloon toss stations.

*How to teach:* Run the sack race, three-legged race and water-balloon toss (step back after each successful catch).

- **Q:** How do you catch a water balloon without breaking it?
  **A:** Reach out, catch with soft hands and 'give' by pulling it in gently.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Team relay
*Student:* Run a team obstacle relay that combines dribbling, jumping and throwing skills.

*How to teach:* Run the team obstacle relay: dribble through cones, jump into hoops, throw at a target, then tag the next runner.

- **Q:** Which skills from this year did you use in the relay?
  **A:** Dribbling, jumping/landing, and overhand throwing.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sportsmanship
*Student:* Make and present sportsmanship awards to teammates, naming specific kind actions.

*How to teach:* The child writes and presents a sportsmanship award to each teammate, naming a specific kind action.

- **Q:** Give an example of a specific sportsmanship award.
  **A:** E.g., 'Cheerful Teammate Award to Sam for clapping for everyone on the sack race.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Write a reflection comparing your field day results to your fall fitness scores.

*How to teach:* Write a reflection comparing field day results to fall fitness scores and earlier unit results.

- **Q:** Example comparison sentence?
  **A:** E.g., 'In fall my long jump was 47 inches and on field day it was 53 inches, 6 inches farther.'

**Assessment:** Field day participation

**Answer key:** Field day participation rubric: 4 = takes part in all events with full effort, records results, gives specific sportsmanship awards, and writes a reflection that compares at least 2 results with numbers; 3 = all events and a reflection with 1 comparison; 2 = partial participation or reflection without comparisons; 1 = minimal participation.

## Health 3: Health promotion project: create

**Prepare:** Poster board, markers, paper for a tri-fold brochure, a phone or tablet for recording.

**Watch for:**
- Putting too much text on a poster. A poster needs a big slogan, a picture and only a few words.
- Recording without practicing. Read the script aloud 2–3 times first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Poster
*Student:* Create a campaign poster with a catchy slogan and picture.

*How to teach:* The child creates the poster with a catchy slogan and picture.

- **Q:** What makes a slogan catchy?
  **A:** It is short, memorable and may rhyme or use rhythm, e.g., 'Buckle up, every trip!'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brochure
*Student:* Make a tri-fold brochure with facts and tips.

*How to teach:* Fold paper into thirds and make a brochure: cover, facts, tips and where to learn more.

- **Q:** What goes on the cover of a brochure?
  **A:** The title, a picture and the main message.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): PSA script
*Student:* Write a 1-minute PSA script.

*How to teach:* Write a PSA script: hook, problem, facts, action, slogan. Time it to about 1 minute.

- **Q:** What is a 'call to action'?
  **A:** Telling the audience exactly what to do, like 'Wear your helmet every time you ride.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Record
*Student:* Record the PSA video or audio.

*How to teach:* Record the PSA. Allow retakes.

- **Q:** What helps a recording sound clear?
  **A:** A quiet room, speaking slowly and loudly, and practicing first.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Check all facts against KidsHealth or MyPlate and practice presenting.

*How to teach:* Check each fact against KidsHealth or MyPlate, fix errors, and practice presenting.

- **Q:** Why check facts?
  **A:** So the campaign gives true information and people can trust it.

**Assessment:** Campaign materials

**Answer key:** Campaign materials rubric: 4 = poster has a readable slogan and picture; brochure is tri-fold with 3+ accurate facts and tips; PSA script is about 1 minute with a hook, facts and call to action; all facts checked against KidsHealth or MyPlate; 3 = all materials with a minor error; 2 = one material missing or several inaccuracies; 1 = mostly incomplete.
