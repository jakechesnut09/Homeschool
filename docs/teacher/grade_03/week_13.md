# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 13

## ELA 3: Publishing the animal report

**Prepare:** Report draft, poster board or folded paper for the published report, a short nature video about the child's animal (e.g., National Geographic Kids or PBS Nature), and a new informational passage for the Unit 3 test.

**Watch for:**
- Ending with 'That's all I know' — a conclusion restates the main idea and leaves the reader with an interesting thought.
- Thinking -er and -or mean different things — both can mean 'a person who' (teacher, actor).

### Day 1 (Monday): Revise
*Student:* Revise the report for definitions of key words and a strong conclusion; spell -ment and -er/-or words.

*How to teach:* Reread the draft and find 2–3 key words a reader might not know; add a definition right in the sentence. Model a strong conclusion that restates the main idea and ends with a 'wow' fact. Then spell -ment and -er/-or words.

- **Q:** What does the suffix -ment mean in 'movement'?
  **A:** The act or result of — movement is the act of moving.
- **Q:** Make 'act' and 'teach' into 'person who' words.
  **A:** actor, teacher.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add text features
*Student:* Add a heading, labeled diagram, caption and glossary to the published report.

*How to teach:* Add text features to the final copy: a heading for each section, a labeled diagram, a caption for a picture and a short glossary. Check that each feature matches the text.

- **Q:** What should a glossary include?
  **A:** Key words from the report in alphabetical order with their definitions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Video listening
*Student:* Watch a short nature video on your animal and note main ideas and details.

*How to teach:* Watch a 3–5 minute nature video about the animal. Pause twice so the child can jot main ideas and details, then compare the video's information to their report.

- **Q:** How is listening for main ideas like reading for them?
  **A:** You figure out what most of the video is about, then listen for details that support it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present your report to the family and answer 3 questions.

*How to teach:* The child presents the report to the family, holding up the diagram and reading clearly. Each listener asks a question; the child answers using facts from the report.

- **Q:** Name two things that make a presentation good.
  **A:** Speaking clearly and loudly enough, and looking at the audience (also pointing to visuals).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 3 test on main idea, text features and cause/effect with a new passage.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 test using a new animal passage (items in the answer key). Score with the report rubric too.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a cause and an effect?
  **A:** A cause is why something happens; an effect is what happens as a result.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 test; informational report rubric

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test on a new passage: 1) Main idea in one sentence (must cover the whole passage) 2–3) Two supporting details from the text 4) Name a heading and what it tells 5) Explain what a caption on the page shows 6) One cause/effect pair from the text 7) Define a bold word using context or the glossary. Proficient 6+/7. Report rubric: 4 = accurate facts in the child's own words, organized into paragraphs with topic sentences, an introduction and conclusion, text features or visuals, few errors; 3 (proficient) = organized and accurate with minor gaps; 2 = facts listed with weak organization or copied wording; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 3: Facts of 7, 8, 9 and the associative property

**Prepare:** Multiplication chart (blank and filled), fact cards, a simple path board game and a die. Write the 40 facts (all facts 0–10, mixed) for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the associative property changes the numbers — only the grouping changes: (2 × 4) × 3 = 2 × (4 × 3) = 24.
- Misusing the 9s finger trick (putting the wrong finger down) — check with 10s minus a group: 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nines patterns
*Student:* Find the tens-ones pattern in 9s and practice with the finger trick and 10s minus a group.

*How to teach:* List 9 × 1 through 9 × 10 and notice the tens digit is one less than the other factor and the digits add to 9. Teach 9 × n = 10 × n − n and the finger trick. The child practices 10 facts.

- **Q:** Find 9 × 7 using tens.
  **A:** 70 − 7 = 63.
- **Q:** What patterns do the 9s products have?
  **A:** The digits add to 9 (18, 27, 36 …), and the tens digit is one less than the other factor.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Eights
*Student:* Double 4s facts to find 8s; solve 2 × 4 × 3 two ways to show the associative property.

*How to teach:* Double 4s to find 8s (8 × 6 = 24 + 24 = 48). Then solve 2 × 4 × 3 as (2 × 4) × 3 = 8 × 3 and as 2 × (4 × 3) = 2 × 12; both are 24 — the associative property.

- **Q:** Find 8 × 7 by doubling.
  **A:** 4 × 7 = 28; double is 56.
- **Q:** Solve 2 × 4 × 3 two ways.
  **A:** (2 × 4) × 3 = 8 × 3 = 24 and 2 × (4 × 3) = 2 × 12 = 24.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sevens
*Student:* Use 5s + 2s to find 7s facts and practice with a fact board game.

*How to teach:* Split 7 into 5 + 2: 7 × 6 = 5 × 6 + 2 × 6 = 30 + 12 = 42. Play a board game where you answer a 7s fact to move ahead.

- **Q:** Find 7 × 8 using 5s and 2s.
  **A:** 5 × 8 = 40 and 2 × 8 = 16; 40 + 16 = 56.
- **Q:** What is 7 × 7?
  **A:** 49.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Table patterns
*Student:* Shade patterns in a multiplication table (diagonal squares, even products) and explain them.

*How to teach:* Shade the square numbers on the diagonal of a multiplication table and color all even products. Discuss why the table is symmetric (commutative property) and why every product in the 2s, 4s, 6s, 8s and 10s rows is even.

- **Q:** Why is every product in the 6s row even?
  **A:** 6 = 2 × 3, so 6 × any number is 2 × something — a double — which is always even.
- **Q:** Name the square numbers on the diagonal up to 10 × 10.
  **A:** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete a 40-fact mixed check and set a fluency goal.

*How to teach:* Give a 40-fact mixed check. The child counts correct facts and sets a goal, such as 'learn my 7s and 8s by next Friday.'

- **Q:** What is 8 × 9? What is 56 ÷ 7?
  **A:** 72; 8.

**Assessment:** 40-fact mixed fluency check

**Answer key:** Key for commonly missed facts: 7 × 8 = 56, 6 × 7 = 42, 8 × 8 = 64, 9 × 6 = 54, 7 × 9 = 63, 8 × 4 = 32, 7 × 4 = 28, 9 × 9 = 81, 8 × 6 = 48, 3 × 7 = 21, 9 × 3 = 27, 6 × 8 = 48, 7 × 7 = 49, 63 ÷ 9 = 7, 72 ÷ 8 = 9, 49 ÷ 7 = 7, 54 ÷ 6 = 9, 64 ÷ 8 = 8, 42 ÷ 7 = 6, 56 ÷ 8 = 7 (check all others against a multiplication chart). Proficient: 36+/40 (90%), with a written goal for missed facts.

## Science 3: Argument: groups help survival

**Prepare:** The completed evidence chart, writing paper. Write the Unit 4 quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Listing evidence without explaining it — reasoning tells how the evidence proves the claim.
- A claim that is a question or a fact list — a claim is a statement you can support.

### Day 1 (Monday): Claim
*Student:* Write a claim: living in groups helps [animal] survive.

*How to teach:* Explain claim–evidence–reasoning. The child chooses an animal and writes a claim such as 'Living in a pack helps wolves survive.'

- **Q:** What is a claim?
  **A:** A statement that answers a question and can be supported with evidence.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Evidence
*Student:* Choose 3 pieces of evidence from the evidence chart.

*How to teach:* The child selects 3 pieces of evidence from the chart that best support the claim.

- **Q:** Give a piece of evidence for 'Living in a group helps elephants survive.'
  **A:** Adult elephants surround calves to protect them from lions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Reasoning
*Student:* Explain how each piece of evidence supports the claim.

*How to teach:* For each piece of evidence, the child writes a 'This shows…because…' sentence explaining how it supports the claim.

- **Q:** Write reasoning for 'Wolves hunt together to catch elk.'
  **A:** This shows living in a pack helps wolves survive because they can catch large prey for food that one wolf couldn't catch alone.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the argument and answer questions.

*How to teach:* The child presents the argument and answers family questions.

- **Q:** How should you answer a question that challenges your claim?
  **A:** Point to evidence and explain your reasoning politely.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 assessment
*Student:* Complete the unit quiz on animal groups.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 quiz. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Name 4 ways groups help animals survive.
  **A:** Hunting, defense, raising young, and staying warm.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 quiz; claim-evidence-reasoning argument

**Answer key:** Unit 4 quiz: 1) Why do wolves hunt in packs? → catch large prey 2) How do fish schools help? → confuse predators, more eyes 3) How do penguins stay warm? → huddle 4) Name an animal that lives alone → e.g., leopard, tiger 5) What is a claim? → a statement that answers a question 6) What is reasoning? → explains how evidence supports the claim. Proficient 5+/6. CER rubric: 4 = clear claim, 3 accurate pieces of evidence, reasoning that links each piece to the claim with science ideas; 3 (proficient) = claim, 2+ pieces of evidence, reasoning for most; 2 = claim with evidence but little reasoning; 1 = claim only or inaccurate.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Missions, presidios and pueblos

**Prepare:** A California missions map (21 missions from San Diego to Sonoma), Calisphere mission photographs. Research the mission nearest you: name, founding year and founder. Parent preview: Native people at missions faced forced labor, harsh punishment and deadly diseases; present this honestly and calmly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking missions were just churches. They were large farms and work centers designed to convert Native people and make them Spanish subjects.
- Thinking Native people all came to the missions willingly and were content. Many were pressured or forced, could not leave freely, and resisted in many ways.

### Day 1 (Monday): Three institutions
*Student:* Learn the purposes of missions, presidios and pueblos and sort facts about each.

*How to teach:* Explain: missions (religious centers and farms run by priests), presidios (forts with soldiers), pueblos (farming towns). Sort facts about each.

- **Q:** What was a presidio?
  **A:** A Spanish military fort that protected the missions and the colony.
- **Q:** What was a pueblo?
  **A:** A Spanish farming town for settlers, such as Los Angeles or San José.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Nearest mission
*Student:* Locate the mission nearest your home on a map and research when and why it was founded.

*How to teach:* Locate the nearest mission and research its founding.

- **Q:** What is the mission nearest you and when was it founded?
  **A:** Answers vary by community; e.g., Mission San Diego de Alcalá (1769), Mission San Gabriel (1771), Mission Santa Barbara (1786), Mission Dolores (1776).
- **Q:** How many California missions were there?
  **A:** 21.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Native experience
*Student:* Read about how mission life changed the work, diet, health and culture of Native peoples.

*How to teach:* Read about the Native experience at missions.

- **Q:** How did mission life change Native people's daily lives?
  **A:** They had to work on mission farms and workshops, follow Spanish rules and religion, eat a different diet, and often could not leave; many died from European diseases.
- **Q:** Why did diseases spread so quickly?
  **A:** Native people had never been exposed to European diseases like measles and smallpox, and crowded mission living helped them spread.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Photo analysis
*Student:* Analyze a historic Calisphere photograph of a mission with a see-think-wonder chart.

*How to teach:* Analyze a mission photograph with a see-think-wonder chart.

- **Q:** What can a photograph tell a historian?
  **A:** What a place looked like at a time, buildings, clothing and activities, and clues for questions to research.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a paragraph explaining how the missions changed the region and its first peoples.

*How to teach:* The child writes the paragraph.

- **Q:** What were the three main goals of the missions?
  **A:** Spread Christianity, make Native people Spanish subjects and workers, and help Spain hold the land.

**Assessment:** Mission paragraph

**Answer key:** Mission paragraph rubric: 4 = topic sentence; explains the mission's purpose (spread Christianity, make Native people Spanish subjects, claim land); gives 2 specific changes to the region (farming, cattle, buildings) and 2 effects on first peoples (forced labor, loss of language and culture, disease, population loss); concluding sentence; 3 = 3 of these details; 2 = 1–2 details; 1 = inaccurate.

## Art & Music 3: High and low pitch

**Prepare:** A xylophone, keyboard, or free piano app. Prepare 10 sounds to compare (e.g., bird whistle, tuba recording, small bell, big drum, high and low piano keys). Melody maps: paper and crayons.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing high/low with loud/soft. Pitch is how high or low a sound is; dynamics is how loud or soft.
- Thinking bigger instruments are higher. Bigger, longer or thicker sound makers usually make lower pitches.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pitch sort
*Student:* Listen to 10 sounds and sort them as high or low.

*How to teach:* Play 10 sounds; the child stands tall for high and crouches for low, then sorts them on paper.

- **Q:** Is a tuba high or low in pitch?
  **A:** Low.
- **Q:** What is pitch?
  **A:** How high or low a sound is.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Direction
*Student:* Draw the up-down contour of 'Twinkle, Twinkle Little Star' as a line.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Twinkle, Twinkle Little Star' (traditional) slowly. The child draws a line that goes up when the melody goes up and down when it goes down.

- **Q:** Does the melody of 'Twinkle, Twinkle' go up or down at the start?
  **A:** It starts on one note, then leaps up and rises a little more, then steps back down.
- **Q:** How does the song end — higher or lower than the middle?
  **A:** Lower — it steps down back to the starting note.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Body levels
*Student:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' showing pitch with hand levels.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' while showing pitch with the hand (high hand = high note). Notice the 'merrily' part jumps high and then comes down.

- **Q:** Where is the highest part of 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat'?
  **A:** The 'merrily, merrily' part.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Melody maps
*Student:* Draw melody maps for 2 short songs and play them on a keyboard app.

*How to teach:* Draw melody maps (lines or dots going up and down) for 2 short songs, then find and play them on the keyboard app.

- **Q:** On a piano, which direction goes higher?
  **A:** To the right.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a listening quiz identifying melodies that go up, down or stay the same.

*How to teach:* Listening quiz: play 6 short melodies; the child marks each 'up,' 'down' or 'same.'

- **Q:** If notes go C-D-E-F-G, is the melody going up or down?
  **A:** Up.

**Assessment:** Pitch direction listening quiz

**Answer key:** Pitch direction listening quiz (6 items): play these on a keyboard and the key is — 1) C-D-E-F-G = up, 2) G-F-E-D-C = down, 3) E-E-E-E = same, 4) C-E-G-C' = up, 5) high C-A-F-C = down, 6) G-G-G-G = same. Proficient = 5 of 6.

## PE 3: Throwing and catching on the move

**Prepare:** Gather a playground ball, a flying disc, a small/junior football and 4–6 cones. Set a straight cone path for the receiver.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing to where the runner is now. A lead pass goes to where the runner will be.
- Flicking the disc with a straight arm and wobble. Curl the arm across the body, step toward the target, and snap the wrist keeping the disc flat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lead pass
*Student:* Throw ahead of a partner running a straight cone path so they catch without stopping.

*How to teach:* Set a straight cone path. The child jogs it while you throw a lead pass to the space ahead; switch so the child throws to you. Talk about aiming at the space ahead.

- **Q:** What is a lead pass?
  **A:** A throw to the space ahead of a moving teammate so they can catch it without slowing down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flying disc
*Student:* Learn the backhand disc throw and play catch for 15 minutes.

*How to teach:* Teach the backhand: grip with the thumb on top and fingers underneath on the rim, stand sideways, curl the disc across the body, step toward the target and snap the wrist, releasing it flat. Play catch 15 minutes; catch with a 'pancake' (one hand on top, one under).

- **Q:** How should the disc be when you release it?
  **A:** Flat (level with the ground), with a wrist snap to spin it.
- **Q:** What is a pancake catch?
  **A:** Catching the disc between one hand on top and one hand underneath, clapping it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Football
*Student:* Throw a small football with a spiral grip to a stationary then moving partner.

*How to teach:* Teach the football grip: fingers on the laces, ball held near the back, throw over the shoulder with the overhand cues and let the ball roll off the fingers for a spiral. Throw to a stationary partner, then to a moving one.

- **Q:** Where do your fingers go on the football?
  **A:** On the laces, toward the back half of the ball.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feedback
*Student:* Give a partner two specific compliments and one cue to improve after their throws.

*How to teach:* Watch a partner make 5 throws. The child gives two specific compliments ('Great step with the opposite foot') and one cue ('Follow through toward me').

- **Q:** What makes feedback specific?
  **A:** It names exactly what was done well or what to fix, like 'Your arm went way back,' not just 'Good job.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a 10-pass moving drill and record completions.

*How to teach:* Run a 10-pass moving drill: receiver jogs the cone path, thrower makes a lead pass; count completions out of 10.

- **Q:** You completed 7 of 10 passes. How many were incomplete?
  **A:** 3.

**Assessment:** Moving passing drill

**Answer key:** Moving passing drill scoring: count completions out of 10 lead passes. 4 = 8–10 completions with passes thrown ahead of the receiver; 3 = 6–7; 2 = 4–5 or passes thrown behind; 1 = 0–3. Also note whether the child gave 2 specific compliments and 1 cue on Day 4.

## Health 3: Managing stress and strong emotions

**Prepare:** A shoebox, stress ball, paper and crayons, a favorite book and index cards for breathing instructions.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all stress is bad. A little stress can help you get ready (before a test or game); it's too much stress for too long that needs managing.
- Thinking calm-down strategies only work once you're very upset. Practice them when calm so they're ready when needed.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is stress
*Student:* Discuss what stress feels like and list 5 things that cause you stress.

*How to teach:* Ask what stress feels like (tight muscles, worried thoughts, stomachache). The child lists 5 things that cause them stress.

- **Q:** What is stress?
  **A:** The feeling of pressure or worry the body and mind have when facing something hard or new.
- **Q:** Name two signs your body is stressed.
  **A:** Any two: fast heartbeat, stomachache, headache, tight muscles, trouble sleeping, sweaty hands.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Breathing
*Student:* Practice belly breathing and 5-finger breathing (trace your hand while breathing in and out) for 3 minutes.

*How to teach:* Model belly breathing (hand on belly, breathe in slowly through the nose so the belly rises, out through the mouth). Then 5-finger breathing: trace up a finger breathing in, down breathing out. Practice 3 minutes.

- **Q:** How do you do 5-finger breathing?
  **A:** Trace up each finger while breathing in and down while breathing out, for all 5 fingers.
- **Q:** Why does slow breathing help you calm down?
  **A:** It slows the heart rate and relaxes the body, telling the brain you're safe.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Calm-down kit
*Student:* Build a calm-down kit in a shoebox with a stress ball, drawing paper, a favorite book and breathing cards.

*How to teach:* The child decorates a shoebox and fills it: stress ball, paper and crayons, favorite book, breathing cards. Decide together where the kit lives.

- **Q:** What would you put in a calm-down kit and why?
  **A:** Items that soothe: a stress ball to squeeze, drawing supplies, a book, breathing cards, a soft item.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Move it out
*Student:* Try 3 movement stress relievers (stretching, a brisk walk, a dance break) and rate how each felt.

*How to teach:* Try 3 movement relievers for 2 minutes each (stretching, a brisk walk, a dance break). The child rates each 1–5 for how calm they felt.

- **Q:** Why can moving your body reduce stress?
  **A:** Exercise uses up stress energy and releases chemicals that improve mood.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a calm-down plan poster listing your 4 best strategies in order.

*How to teach:* The child makes a poster with their 4 best strategies in order (first try…, then…). Post it near the calm-down kit.

- **Q:** If breathing doesn't help, what's your next step?
  **A:** Answer names the next strategy on their poster, ending with talking to a trusted adult.

**Assessment:** Calm-down plan poster

**Answer key:** Calm-down plan poster rubric: 4 = lists 4 specific strategies the child tested (e.g., belly breathing, 5-finger breathing, dance break, calm-down kit) in a sensible order and names a trusted adult to go to; 3 = 4 strategies but not ordered or vague; 2 = 2–3 strategies; 1 = fewer than 2.
