# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 34

## ELA 3: Author study: Beatrix Potter

**Prepare:** The Tale of Peter Rabbit and The Tale of Benjamin Bunny by Beatrix Potter (Project Gutenberg editions include her illustrations).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Peter Rabbit is only a funny story — it shows the consequences of disobeying.
- Confusing plot (what happens) with theme (the message).

### Day 1 (Monday): Peter Rabbit
*Student:* Read 'The Tale of Peter Rabbit' and map its plot.

*How to teach:* Read The Tale of Peter Rabbit aloud, enjoying the pictures. The child maps the plot: beginning, problem, events, climax, resolution.

- **Q:** What did Peter's mother warn him about?
  **A:** Not to go into Mr. McGregor's garden, because his father had an accident there and was put in a pie by Mrs. McGregor.
- **Q:** How does Peter escape?
  **A:** He hides in a watering can, then finds the gate and squeezes under it; Mr. McGregor later uses Peter's lost jacket and shoes for a scarecrow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Benjamin Bunny
*Student:* Read 'The Tale of Benjamin Bunny' and map its plot.

*How to teach:* Read The Tale of Benjamin Bunny and map its plot the same way. Ask the child what is the same and what is new compared to the first tale.

- **Q:** Why do Peter and Benjamin go back to the garden?
  **A:** To get Peter's clothes back from the scarecrow.
- **Q:** Who rescues them from the cat?
  **A:** Old Mr. Benjamin Bunny, Benjamin's father.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Compare settings, plots and themes of the two tales in a chart; review dictionary syllable guides.

*How to teach:* Fill a chart comparing setting, characters, problem, solution and theme of the two tales. Show how a dictionary shows syllables (gar·den) and practice reading them.

- **Q:** What setting do the two tales share?
  **A:** Mr. McGregor's garden.
- **Q:** How is Peter's mood different in the second tale?
  **A:** He is nervous and scared, while Benjamin is bold and confident.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Response
*Student:* Write a comparison paragraph about the two tales.

*How to teach:* The child writes a comparison paragraph with at least 2 similarities and 2 differences. Provide a frame: 'Both tales ___. However, in the first tale ___, while in the second ___.'

- **Q:** What theme do both tales share?
  **A:** Disobeying and going where you shouldn't can lead to trouble.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer comparison questions and read a Potter passage aloud fluently.

*How to teach:* Ask the comparison questions in the key; the child reads a Potter passage aloud fluently. Time a one-minute read and note phrasing and expression.

- **Q:** What did Peter's mother give him when he got home sick?
  **A:** Chamomile tea.

**Assessment:** Comparison paragraph; fluency check

**Answer key:** Comparison questions: 1) Shared setting → Mr. McGregor's garden 2) Shared character → Peter 3) Who gets Peter out of trouble in each? → he escapes alone in the first; Old Mr. Benjamin Bunny in the second 4) Shared theme → disobeying leads to trouble. Paragraph rubric: 4 = 2 similarities and 2 differences with details; 3 = 1 each; 2 = summary only; 1 = inaccurate. Fluency: 3 = smooth and expressive; 2 = some hesitations; 1 = word-by-word.

## Math 3: Geometry project

**Prepare:** Large grid paper, colored pencils, a ruler. Write the Unit 9 test in the answer key.

**Watch for:**
- Calculating area for every section but forgetting perimeter (or the reverse) — use a table with both columns.
- Partitioning the picnic area into unequal parts — check equal areas by counting squares.

### Day 1 (Monday): Park design
*Student:* Begin designing a neighborhood park on grid paper with at least 4 rectangular areas.

*How to teach:* The child begins designing a park on grid paper (1 square = 1 meter) with at least 4 rectangular areas (playground, garden, picnic area, field). Encourage at least 4 sections of different sizes and labels for each.

- **Q:** If the playground is 6 squares by 5 squares, what is its area?
  **A:** 30 sq m.
- **Q:** What shape must each section be for this project?
  **A:** A rectangle drawn along the grid lines.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure the park
*Student:* Calculate area and perimeter for each section of the park.

*How to teach:* Fill in a table: section, length, width, area, perimeter. Check two rows of the table together with a calculator.

- **Q:** A garden is 4 m by 7 m. Area and perimeter?
  **A:** Area 28 sq m; perimeter 22 m.
- **Q:** A field is 10 m by 8 m. Area and perimeter?
  **A:** Area 80 sq m; perimeter 36 m.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fair share
*Student:* Partition the picnic area into equal parts for 4 families and name each fraction.

*How to teach:* Partition the picnic area into 4 equal parts, one for each family, and label each part 1/4. Have the child count squares in each part to prove they are equal.

- **Q:** An 8 × 4 picnic area (32 sq m) is split equally for 4 families. What does each get?
  **A:** 1/4 of the area, which is 8 sq m.
- **Q:** If it were split into 8 equal parts instead, what fraction is each?
  **A:** 1/8.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the park design and explain calculations to the family.

*How to teach:* The child presents the park design and explains each calculation to the family. Ask one follow-up question about area and one about perimeter.

- **Q:** How do you know which section has the largest area?
  **A:** Compare the products of length × width; the largest product is the largest area.
- **Q:** Explain how you found a perimeter.
  **A:** Add all four side lengths (or 2 × length + 2 × width).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 assessment
*Student:* Complete the Unit 9 test on geometry, area and perimeter.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Is a rhombus a quadrilateral?
  **A:** Yes.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 test and park design project

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test: 1) Is a square a rectangle? → yes 2) Draw a quadrilateral that is not a rectangle, rhombus or square → trapezoid or kite 3) Perimeter of 9 × 4 → 26 4) Area of 9 × 4 → 36 5) Rectangle with P = 24 and one side 8 → other side 4 6) L shape of 6 × 3 and 2 × 2 → 22 sq units 7) 2 × 8 and 4 × 4 have the same area (16); perimeters → 20 and 16 8) Shape split into 6 equal parts → each 1/6. Proficient: 7+/8. Park project: 4 = all sections' areas and perimeters correct, equal partition, clear presentation; 3 = 1–2 errors; 2 = several errors; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 3: Engineering: build and test

**Prepare:** Cardboard, tape, one plastic bag, a box fan, a spray bottle, paper towels, a measuring tape. Mark the fan distance on the floor with tape so it stays the same in every test.

**Watch for:**
- Changing the fan distance or number of sprays between tests — keep test conditions the same so results can be compared.
- Seeing a failure as the end — failures show engineers exactly what to improve.

### Day 1 (Monday): Build
*Student:* Build the model house from cardboard and tape following your chosen sketch.

*How to teach:* The child builds the house from the chosen sketch, using only the allowed materials. Remind them to check the sketch often and note any changes they make.

- **Q:** Why build from the sketch?
  **A:** So you are testing the design you planned and scored.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wind test
*Student:* Test the house in front of a box fan at low, medium and high, keeping distance the same; record failure points.

*How to teach:* Place the house at the marked distance and run the fan on low, medium and high for 15 seconds each. The child records what happens at each speed and where the house first fails.

- **Q:** What should stay the same in the wind test?
  **A:** The distance from the fan, the surface, the house's position and the time at each speed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rain test
*Student:* Place a dry paper towel inside and spray 30 squirts of water from above; record leaks.

*How to teach:* Put a dry paper towel inside, spray 30 squirts from above at the same height, then check the towel for wet spots. Record where water got in.

- **Q:** How do you know if the house leaked?
  **A:** The paper towel inside has wet spots; their location shows where the leak is.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Modify the weakest part of the design based on test results.

*How to teach:* Look at the test log together and pick the weakest part. The child changes only that part.

- **Q:** If the roof blew off at medium speed, what could you change?
  **A:** Tape the roof down more securely, add tape 'straps' over it, or make it lower and more sloped.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Retest the improved house under the same conditions and compare results in a table.

*How to teach:* Retest under exactly the same conditions and fill a before/after table. Discuss whether the change helped.

- **Q:** Why must the retest use the same conditions?
  **A:** So you know the improvement — not a different test — caused any change in results.

**Assessment:** Testing log with before/after table

**Answer key:** Testing log: 3 (proficient) = before and after data under the same conditions for wind and rain, failure points identified, one improvement explained; 2 = data incomplete or conditions changed; 1 = incomplete.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Choices and trade-offs

**Prepare:** Play money, a store flyer or restaurant menu.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking opportunity cost is the price in dollars. It's the next-best choice you gave up.
- Thinking scarcity only affects poor people. Everyone has limited money, time or resources.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scarcity
*Student:* Discuss why we can't have everything we want (limited money, time).

*How to teach:* Discuss scarcity: wants are unlimited but money and time are limited.

- **Q:** What is scarcity?
  **A:** Not having enough resources (money, time, goods) to have everything we want.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Trade-offs
*Student:* Choose items from a store flyer with a $20 play budget and name what you gave up.

*How to teach:* With $20 play money, choose items from a flyer; name what was given up.

- **Q:** You have $20. A toy costs $15 and a book costs $8. Can you buy both?
  **A:** No; together they cost $23, which is $3 more than $20.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Opportunity cost
*Student:* Identify the opportunity cost in 5 scenario cards (e.g., playing outside vs. watching a show).

*How to teach:* Identify the opportunity cost in 5 scenarios.

- **Q:** You choose to play outside instead of watching a show. What is the opportunity cost?
  **A:** Watching the show.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Party budget
*Student:* Plan a birthday party with a $50 budget, comparing costs and benefits of choices.

*How to teach:* Plan a party with a $50 budget comparing costs and benefits.

- **Q:** Pizza costs $24, cake $18, decorations $10. What's the total? Is it under $50?
  **A:** $52; no, it's $2 over, so something must change (e.g., cheaper decorations).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write about a real trade-off you made and its opportunity cost.

*How to teach:* The child writes about a real trade-off.

- **Q:** What is a trade-off?
  **A:** Giving up one thing to get another.

**Assessment:** Party budget and trade-off writing

**Answer key:** Party budget and trade-off writing key: budget lists items with prices totaling $50 or less (e.g., cake $15, pizza $20, decorations $8, juice $5 = $48), compares choices and states what was given up. Writing rubric: 4 = describes a real choice, names the option chosen and the opportunity cost (the best alternative given up), and explains why; 3 = choice and opportunity cost but no reason; 2 = confuses opportunity cost with price; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Preparing an exhibition

**Prepare:** The year's portfolio, construction paper slightly larger than each artwork, glue stick, index cards for labels, a slideshow app if doing a digital gallery.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing only favorite subjects. Choose works that show different media and skills (drawing, painting, printmaking, sculpture) and growth.
- Artist statements that only say 'I like it.' A statement tells what the work is, how it was made and what the artist wanted to show.

### Day 1 (Monday): Select
*Student:* Review the year's portfolio and select 6 best works.

*How to teach:* Review the whole portfolio and select 6 best works showing different media. Explain each choice.

- **Q:** Why include different kinds of art?
  **A:** To show the range of skills and media learned during the year.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mat
*Student:* Mount each artwork on a construction paper mat.

*How to teach:* Mount each work on a construction-paper mat with an even border, centered.

- **Q:** Why use a mat?
  **A:** It frames the art, separates it from the wall and makes it look finished.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Artist statements
*Student:* Write a 3-sentence artist statement for each selected work.

*How to teach:* Write a 3-sentence artist statement for each: what it is, how it was made (media/technique), what you wanted to show or what you learned.

- **Q:** Write an example artist statement sentence about technique.
  **A:** E.g., 'I used tints and shades of red to make the flower petals look curved.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan the space
*Student:* Compare a hallway wall and a digital slideshow gallery as exhibit spaces and choose one or both.

*How to teach:* Compare a hallway wall gallery and a digital slideshow: pros and cons (real size and texture vs. easy to share with far-away family). Choose one or both.

- **Q:** Name one advantage of a digital gallery.
  **A:** Family far away can see it; it can include music or narration; art isn't damaged.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Arrange the exhibit layout and make labels.

*How to teach:* Arrange the layout (eye level, even spacing, group by theme or media) and make labels: title, artist, medium, date.

- **Q:** What goes on a museum label?
  **A:** Title, artist's name, medium (materials) and date (year).

**Assessment:** Exhibit preparation

**Answer key:** Exhibit preparation checklist: 6 works selected showing at least 3 different media (1 pt), all mounted neatly (1), 6 artist statements with what/how/why (2), exhibit space chosen with a reason (1), layout planned with labels including title, artist, medium, date (1). 6 = exemplary; 5 = proficient; 3–4 = developing; below 3 = beginning.

## PE 3: Field day prep

**Prepare:** Cones, a baton (paper-towel tube), pillowcases for sack hops, a scarf or soft tie for the three-legged race, a softball, measuring tape. Mark a start/finish line and a 50-yard course if possible.

**Watch for:**
- Looking back and slowing down during a baton pass. The receiver starts moving and reaches back; the passer says 'Stick!' and places the baton in the hand.
- Rushing the three-legged start. Partners agree which legs are tied and say 'tied leg, outside leg' together to stay in step.

### Day 1 (Monday): Relays
*Student:* Practice baton-pass and shuttle relays with a family member.

*How to teach:* Practice a baton pass: receiver reaches back with an open hand while jogging; the runner places the baton in it. Then shuttle relays: run to a cone, tag the next runner.

- **Q:** In a shuttle relay, when can the next runner go?
  **A:** Only after being tagged (or receiving the baton) by the incoming runner.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sack race
*Student:* Practice pillowcase sack hops and three-legged race starts.

*How to teach:* Practice pillowcase hops (hold the top, small two-foot jumps). Then practice three-legged starts with a partner: tie inside legs, step 'tied leg first' together.

- **Q:** What's the trick to a fast three-legged race?
  **A:** Move together in rhythm, calling out which leg to step with.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Throw
*Student:* Practice the softball throw for distance using overhand cues.

*How to teach:* Use the overhand cues to throw a softball for distance. Do 3 throws, measure the best.

- **Q:** Which cue helps distance most?
  **A:** A big step with the opposite foot plus full rotation and follow through.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Jump
*Student:* Practice standing long jump and record best of 3.

*How to teach:* Do 3 standing long jumps and record the best; compare to week 5.

- **Q:** Week 5 best: 47 inches. Today: 52 inches. How much farther?
  **A:** 5 inches.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Run a mini field day of 4 events and record personal results.

*How to teach:* Run a mini field day with 4 events (e.g., relay, sack hop, softball throw, long jump). Record personal results on a sheet.

- **Q:** Why is it good to compare to your own past results?
  **A:** It shows your personal improvement no matter how others did.

**Assessment:** Mini field day

**Answer key:** Mini field day check: results sheet lists 4 events with a recorded time/distance for each, measured correctly, and the child shows good effort and sportsmanship. 4 = all 4 results recorded accurately plus a comparison to an earlier result; 3 = all 4 recorded; 2 = 2–3 recorded; 1 = fewer.

## Health 3: Health promotion project: plan

**Prepare:** Planning sheet with sections: topic, audience, message, 3 facts, materials. Have KidsHealth and MyPlate open for fact-checking.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a topic too broad ('be healthy'). Narrow to one action, like 'wash your hands for 20 seconds.'
- Writing facts from memory without checking. Every fact should come from a reliable source.

### Day 1 (Monday): Topic
*Student:* Choose a health topic from the year (eat a rainbow, wash hands, helmet use, drug-free) to promote.

*How to teach:* The child reviews the year's topics and chooses one to promote, explaining why it matters.

- **Q:** Why is your chosen topic important?
  **A:** Answer should give a health reason, e.g., 'Helmets prevent head injuries.'

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Audience
*Student:* Choose an audience (younger cousins, co-op class, family) and what they need to know.

*How to teach:* Choose an audience and list what they already know and need to know.

- **Q:** How would a message for 5-year-olds differ from one for adults?
  **A:** Simpler words, more pictures, shorter sentences and fun elements for young children.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Message
*Student:* Write a clear, positive message with 3 supporting facts.

*How to teach:* Write a positive message (e.g., 'Wash for 20, beat the germs!') with 3 facts.

- **Q:** What makes a health message 'positive'?
  **A:** It tells people what to do and the benefit, instead of only scaring them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan materials
*Student:* Plan materials: a poster, a brochure and a 1-minute public service announcement.

*How to teach:* Plan what goes on the poster, in the brochure and in the PSA.

- **Q:** What is a PSA?
  **A:** A public service announcement: a short message to inform the public about something important for their health or safety.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Share the plan with a parent and revise it.

*How to teach:* Share the plan with you; give 2 compliments and 1 suggestion; the child revises.

- **Q:** Is your message clear in one sentence?
  **A:** Yes, it states one action and the benefit.

**Assessment:** Campaign plan

**Answer key:** Campaign plan rubric: 4 = names one specific health topic, a specific audience and what they need, a short positive message, 3 accurate facts with sources, and the 3 planned materials (poster, brochure, 1-minute PSA); revised after parent feedback; 3 = all parts but one fact weak or unsourced; 2 = missing 2 parts; 1 = incomplete.
