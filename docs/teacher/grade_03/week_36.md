# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 36

## ELA 3: Year-end portfolio and reflection

**Prepare:** Writing folder, reading log, a folder for the portfolio. Write the end-of-year assessment (short passage plus spelling and grammar items).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing favorite pieces instead of best work — pick pieces that show your strongest skills.
- Writing general reflections ('I got better') — give specific examples.

### Day 1 (Monday): Reading review
*Student:* Review the reading log and choose top 3 books; write a recommendation.

*How to teach:* Look through the reading log together. The child chooses a top 3 and writes a recommendation for one.

- **Q:** What makes a good book recommendation?
  **A:** The title and author, a short summary without spoiling the ending, and reasons someone would enjoy it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Writing portfolio
*Student:* Select the best narrative, informative and opinion pieces for a portfolio.

*How to teach:* The child chooses their best narrative, informative and opinion pieces and writes a note for each explaining why it was chosen. Have the child explain what skill each piece shows best.

- **Q:** Why include all three types of writing?
  **A:** Each shows different skills: telling a story, teaching facts and supporting an opinion.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final assessment
*Student:* Complete the end-of-year reading, spelling and grammar assessment.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year assessment. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What is the past tense of 'bring'?
  **A:** brought.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a letter to your future 4th-grade self about your growth.

*How to teach:* The child writes a letter to their future 4th-grade self about growth, favorite books and goals. Encourage specific examples, like a book that was hard at first or a piece of writing they're proud of.

- **Q:** What should the letter include?
  **A:** Specific ways they grew as a reader and writer, favorite books, and goals for next year.

### Day 5 (Friday): Celebrate
*Student:* Share your portfolio at a family reading celebration and set a summer reading goal.

*How to teach:* Hold a family reading celebration where the child shares the portfolio and sets a summer reading goal. Let the child lead the sharing and celebrate effort as well as results.

- **Q:** Give an example of a good summer reading goal.
  **A:** Read 10 chapter books by August and keep a reading log.

**Assessment:** End-of-year assessment; portfolio

**Answer key:** End-of-year assessment: Reading (short fable or passage): main idea or moral, character trait with evidence, theme. Spelling (10): unhappy, reread, hopeful, careless, quickly, kindness, cries, hopping, hoping, famous. Grammar: 1) plural of child → children 2) past of write → wrote 3) the toys of the boys → the boys' toys 4) punctuate dialogue: "Come here," said Mom. 5) combine with but 6) capitalize a title. Proficient: 80% overall. Portfolio: proficient with all three writing types and a specific reflection.

## Math 3: Cumulative review: fractions, measurement, geometry

**Prepare:** Fraction strips, number lines, a clock, measuring cups and scale, a scavenger-hunt list of shapes, a portfolio folder. Write the end-of-year assessment.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting that equivalent fractions name the same point on the number line — revisit stacked number lines.
- Mixing up area and perimeter in review — ask 'Inside or around?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Fraction review
*Student:* Review equivalence and comparison with strips and number lines.

*How to teach:* Review equivalence and comparison with strips and number lines. Have the child explain one comparison to you using a strip or number line drawing.

- **Q:** Compare 3/4 and 3/8.
  **A:** 3/4 > 3/8.
- **Q:** 2/4 = ?/8
  **A:** 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measurement review
*Student:* Review time, volume, mass and area problems.

*How to teach:* Review time, liquid volume, mass and area problems. Mix 2 problems of each type and have the child name the unit for every answer.

- **Q:** How long is it from 9:40 to 10:15?
  **A:** 35 minutes.
- **Q:** What is the area of a 7 × 6 rectangle?
  **A:** 42 square units.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Geometry review
*Student:* Review quadrilaterals and perimeter with a scavenger hunt.

*How to teach:* Go on a scavenger hunt for rectangles, squares and other quadrilaterals around the house and measure perimeters. Have the child record the shape, its attributes and its perimeter for each find.

- **Q:** What is the perimeter of a 12 in × 8 in tray?
  **A:** 40 inches.
- **Q:** Is every square a rhombus?
  **A:** Yes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final assessment
*Student:* Complete the end-of-year math assessment.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year assessment without help. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** Which is greater, 1/3 or 1/6?
  **A:** 1/3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Build a math portfolio of best work and write a reflection on growth.

*How to teach:* The child chooses best work from each unit for a portfolio and writes a reflection on their growth. Help the child choose one piece per unit and write a sentence about why it shows growth.

- **Q:** What should a good reflection include?
  **A:** What they learned, what was hard, how they improved (with an example), and a goal for next year.

**Assessment:** End-of-year assessment and portfolio reflection

**Answer key:** 1) 538 + 276 → 814 2) 700 − 285 → 415 3) Round 763 to the nearest 100 → 800 4) 8 × 7 → 56 5) 72 ÷ 9 → 8 6) Place 3/4 on a number line → 3 of 4 equal spaces from 0 7) 2/3 = ?/6 → 4 8) Compare 5/8 and 3/8 → 5/8 > 3/8 9) 2:25 to 3:10 → 45 min 10) 3 bottles × 2 L → 6 L 11) Area of 5 × 8 → 40 sq units 12) Perimeter of 5 × 8 → 26 units 13) Is a square a rhombus? → yes 14) 3 packs of 6 juice boxes, 4 drunk → 14. Proficient: 11+/14. Reflection: 3 = specific examples and a goal; 2 = general statements; 1 = minimal.

## Science 3: Science year review

**Prepare:** The science notebook, a toy car and ramp, magnets, life cycle and trait cards for a sort, weather and fossil vocabulary cards. Write the end-of-year assessment.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting earlier units — use notebook entries to review.
- Mixing up vocabulary across units (e.g., calling a pupa a seedling).

### Day 1 (Monday): Forces review
*Student:* Review forces and magnets with a quick ramp and magnet demo.

*How to teach:* Do a quick ramp and magnet demo. Ask the child to explain what they see using 'balanced', 'unbalanced', 'friction', 'attract' and 'repel'.

- **Q:** What makes a still object start moving?
  **A:** Unbalanced forces.
- **Q:** What happens when two south poles meet?
  **A:** They repel.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Life science review
*Student:* Review life cycles, traits and habitats with a card sort.

*How to teach:* Mix life cycle, trait and habitat cards; the child sorts them into groups and explains each group.

- **Q:** Is eye color inherited or caused by the environment?
  **A:** Inherited.
- **Q:** What are the stages of complete metamorphosis?
  **A:** Egg, larva, pupa, adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Earth science review
*Student:* Review fossils, weather and hazards with a vocabulary game.

*How to teach:* Play a vocabulary game with fossil, weather and hazard words; the child must define each and give an example.

- **Q:** What does a fish fossil in a desert show?
  **A:** That the area was once underwater.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Final assessment
*Student:* Complete the end-of-year science assessment.

*How to teach:* Give the end-of-year science assessment. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** What is the difference between weather and climate?
  **A:** Weather is the conditions on a given day; climate is the usual pattern over many years.

### Day 5 (Friday): Showcase
*Student:* Share the science notebook and favorite investigation with family.

*How to teach:* The child picks a favorite investigation and shares the notebook with family, explaining the question, what they did and what they learned.

- **Q:** What makes a good science share?
  **A:** Explaining the question, the procedure, the data and the conclusion.

**Assessment:** End-of-year science assessment

**Answer key:** End-of-year: 1) Balanced vs. unbalanced forces → equal/opposite, no change in motion vs. change in motion 2) N and S poles → attract 3) Charged balloon near tissue → attracts 4) Plant life cycle in order → seed, seedling, adult, flower, fruit/seed 5) Complete metamorphosis stages → egg, larva, pupa, adult 6) Inherited trait vs. environmental effect → e.g., eye color vs. pale plant in the dark 7) How camouflage helps → harder for predators to find 8) Shark tooth fossil means → ocean 9) Rain gauge measures → precipitation 10) Weather vs. climate 11) One hazard solution → levee, fire break, lightning rod. Proficient 9+/11.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Community history showcase

**Prepare:** Gather the year's projects: maps, timeline, oral history, reports, symbols booklet. A tri-fold board, labels and table space.

**Watch for:**
- Making exhibit labels too long. Visitors read short labels: title, 2–3 sentences, and a source.
- Reading a tour script word for word. Use note cards and speak to visitors.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a 'Museum of Our Community' with sections for geography, first peoples, settlement, government and economy.

*How to teach:* Plan the museum sections and choose items for each.

- **Q:** What item will you put in the first peoples section?
  **A:** Answer names a project from Unit 2, e.g., the shelter model.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build exhibits from the year's maps, timeline, oral history and reports with labels.

*How to teach:* Build exhibits with labels.

- **Q:** What should a museum label include?
  **A:** A title, a short explanation and where the item or information came from.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse a docent tour script explaining each exhibit.

*How to teach:* Rehearse a docent tour script.

- **Q:** What is a docent?
  **A:** A museum guide who leads tours and explains exhibits.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Showcase
*Student:* Host the museum for family and friends and lead tours.

*How to teach:* Host the museum and lead tours.

- **Q:** What was the most interesting question a visitor asked?
  **A:** Answer from the event.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on how your community has changed and what has stayed the same.

*How to teach:* The child writes the reflection.

- **Q:** Name one way your community has changed and one way it has stayed the same.
  **A:** Answers vary by community; answer cites evidence from the year's projects.

**Assessment:** Community museum exhibit and reflection

**Answer key:** Museum exhibit and reflection rubric: Exhibit — 4 = five sections (geography, first peoples, settlement, government, economy), each with at least one artifact and an accurate label, and a confident docent tour; 3 = four sections or minor errors; 2 = few labels; 1 = incomplete. Reflection — 4 = names at least 2 changes and 2 things that stayed the same in the community with specific evidence from the year's work; 3 = 1 each; 2 = vague; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Art show and concert

**Prepare:** Hanging supplies (painter's tape), the matted art and labels, chairs and a performance area, concert programs, recording device. Invite family or friends.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking critique means finding what's wrong. A critique describes, analyzes, interprets and judges using criteria, including strengths.
- Thinking growth is only about 'talent.' Growth comes from practice, feedback and revision.

### Day 1 (Monday): Set up
*Student:* Hang the art exhibit and set up a performance area.

*How to teach:* Hang the exhibit at eye level with even spacing and labels; set up the performance area.

- **Q:** Why hang art at eye level?
  **A:** So viewers can see it comfortably and clearly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gallery walk
*Student:* Lead family on a gallery walk, explaining each artwork.

*How to teach:* The child leads a gallery walk, explaining each work using their artist statements.

- **Q:** Choose one work: what technique did you use?
  **A:** The child correctly names the media and technique, e.g., foam-plate printmaking or score-and-slip clay.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Concert
*Student:* Perform the concert program.

*How to teach:* Perform the concert program with introductions, using etiquette.

- **Q:** What do you do at the end of each piece?
  **A:** Pause and bow while the audience applauds.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Critique
*Student:* Evaluate one artwork and one song using criteria from the year.

*How to teach:* Critique one artwork and one song using criteria from the year (art: line, shape, color, value, texture, craftsmanship; music: steady beat, pitch, expression).

- **Q:** What are the steps of a critique?
  **A:** Describe what you see/hear, analyze how it's made, interpret its meaning or mood, and judge it with criteria.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write about your growth as an artist and musician this year.

*How to teach:* Write about growth as an artist and musician: compare a fall work to a spring work and name skills learned.

- **Q:** Give an example comparing early and late work.
  **A:** E.g., 'In September I drew symbols, but by spring I drew from observation with shading.'

**Assessment:** Exhibit and concert

**Answer key:** Exhibit and concert rubric: 4 = exhibit hung neatly with labels, gallery walk explains each work with art vocabulary, concert performed with etiquette, critique uses criteria for one artwork and one song, reflection compares early and late work with specific skills; 3 = all parts with less detail; 2 = some parts missing; 1 = minimal participation.

## PE 3: Summer fitness plan

**Prepare:** Print a summer calendar (June–August) and a weekly activity log. Plan a family hike or bike ride (bring water, sunscreen, helmet). Preview a new activity option for safety (e.g., swimming only with adult supervision, skateboarding with helmet and pads).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 60 minutes must happen all at once. Daily activity can add up in chunks (e.g., 20 + 20 + 20 minutes).
- Doing only one activity all summer. Mixing activity types works different fitness components and keeps it fun.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Create a summer activity calendar with 60 minutes of activity per day across at least 4 activity types.

*How to teach:* Explain that kids need at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity every day. The child fills a summer calendar with at least 4 activity types (e.g., swimming, biking, jump rope, soccer, dance, hiking).

- **Q:** How many minutes of activity should kids get each day?
  **A:** At least 60 minutes.
- **Q:** You biked 25 minutes and swam 20 minutes. How many more minutes to reach 60?
  **A:** 60 − 45 = 15 more minutes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Try something new
*Student:* Try a new activity (swimming, skateboarding, yoga) for 30 minutes and rate it.

*How to teach:* Try a new activity for 30 minutes with proper safety gear and supervision, then rate it 1–5 and explain the rating.

- **Q:** What safety gear would you need for skateboarding or biking?
  **A:** A helmet (and for skateboarding, knee pads, elbow pads and wrist guards).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family outing
*Student:* Go on a family hike or bike ride and measure the distance.

*How to teach:* Go on a family hike or bike ride and measure the distance with a phone app, map or odometer. Bring water and sunscreen.

- **Q:** Which fitness component does a long hike build most?
  **A:** Cardiorespiratory endurance (and leg muscular endurance).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Log
*Student:* Fill in a week of the summer activity log and set a summer skill goal (e.g., 50 consecutive rope jumps).

*How to teach:* Fill in a week of the summer log and write a summer skill goal that is SMART (e.g., 'I will jump rope 50 times in a row by August 1 by practicing 10 minutes 4 days a week').

- **Q:** Make your skill goal SMART.
  **A:** It must be specific, measurable (a number), achievable, relevant and have a date.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Share the summer plan and lead the family in your favorite warm-up.

*How to teach:* The child shares the summer plan with the family and leads their favorite warm-up.

- **Q:** Why warm up even in the summer?
  **A:** Warming up prepares muscles and the heart for activity and helps prevent injury every time.

**Assessment:** Summer plan

**Answer key:** Summer plan rubric: 4 = calendar shows 60 minutes of activity daily across 4+ activity types, a week of the log is filled in, and a SMART skill goal with number and date; child leads a correct warm-up; 3 = plan complete but goal missing one SMART part; 2 = plan incomplete or fewer than 4 activity types; 1 = no plan.

## Health 3: Health promotion project: present and reflect

**Prepare:** Campaign materials, an audience (family, cousins, co-op). Prepare the final quiz below.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a presentation is finished once it's given. Asking for feedback shows whether the message worked.
- Setting vague summer goals. Use SMART goals with numbers and times.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present
*Student:* Present the campaign to your chosen audience.

*How to teach:* The child presents the campaign to the chosen audience using the poster, brochure and PSA.

- **Q:** What is the one action you want your audience to take?
  **A:** Answer states the campaign's call to action.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Feedback
*Student:* Ask the audience what they learned and one change they will make.

*How to teach:* The child asks the audience what they learned and one change they'll make, and records answers.

- **Q:** Why ask for feedback?
  **A:** To find out whether the message was understood and how to improve it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final quiz
*Student:* Take the end-of-year health quiz covering all units.

*How to teach:* Give the final quiz.

- **Q:** How many food groups are on MyPlate?
  **A:** Five.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Summer goals
*Student:* Set 2 summer health goals (activity, nutrition, sleep or safety).

*How to teach:* The child sets 2 SMART summer health goals.

- **Q:** Make 'sleep better' a SMART goal.
  **A:** Example: 'I will be in bed by 8:30 p.m. at least 6 nights a week all summer.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on the healthiest change you made this year.

*How to teach:* The child writes a reflection on the healthiest change they made this year.

- **Q:** What made the change possible?
  **A:** Answer names a strategy or support, such as a tracker, family help or a reminder.

**Assessment:** Final quiz; campaign presentation

**Answer key:** Final quiz key (12 questions): 1) Five food groups: fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy. 2) Kids need 60 minutes of activity a day. 3) On labels, check serving size first. 4) The heart is a muscle that pumps blood. 5) Calcium builds bones. 6) I-message: I feel ___ when ___ because ___. 7) Bullying is repeated, on purpose, with a power imbalance. 8) Fire: get out, stay out, crawl low. 9) 911: name, address, what happened. 10) Only take medicine from a trusted adult. 11) Nicotine is addictive; legal age 21. 12) Wash hands 20 seconds. Presentation rubric: 4 = clear voice, eye contact, accurate facts, all materials, answers questions; 3 = minor issues; 2 = reads everything, little interaction; 1 = incomplete. 10–12 = proficient.
