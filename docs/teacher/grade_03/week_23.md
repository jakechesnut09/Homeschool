# Teacher's Guide — 3rd Grade, Week 23

## ELA 3: Opinion essay: drafting

**Prepare:** The unit biographies and an opinion organizer (opinion, 3 reasons with evidence, conclusion).

**Watch for:**
- Giving reasons with no evidence — each reason needs a fact or example from the text.
- Joining two sentences with only a comma — use a comma plus and/but/so.

### Day 1 (Monday): Opinion choice
*Student:* Choose which person showed the greatest courage and list reasons with evidence.

*How to teach:* Ask: Who showed the greatest courage — Helen Keller, Ruby Bridges or Jackie Robinson? The child chooses and lists at least 3 reasons, finding evidence for each in the books.

- **Q:** Give a reason with evidence for Ruby Bridges.
  **A:** She was only six and walked past shouting crowds every day to go to school.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Organizer
*Student:* Plan an essay with introduction, 3 reasons with evidence and conclusion; study roots struct and tract.

*How to teach:* The child fills in the organizer. Then study Latin roots struct (build) and tract (pull) and build words.

- **Q:** What does 'construct' mean? 'Tractor'?
  **A:** To build; a machine that pulls.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sentence variety
*Student:* Practice simple, compound and complex sentences about your person.

*How to teach:* Review simple, compound and complex sentences. The child writes one of each about their person.

- **Q:** Write a compound sentence about Jackie Robinson.
  **A:** Example: Fans shouted insults, but Jackie kept playing.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draft
*Student:* Draft the essay with linking words (because, therefore, for example).

*How to teach:* The child drafts the essay from the organizer, using linking words. Have them check off each part of the organizer as it goes into the draft.

- **Q:** Name three opinion linking words.
  **A:** Because, therefore, for example (also 'since', 'in addition').

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read the draft to a partner and get 2 compliments and 1 suggestion.

*How to teach:* The child reads the draft to a family member, who gives 2 specific compliments and 1 suggestion. The child writes the suggestion at the top of the draft for next week.

- **Q:** What makes a helpful suggestion?
  **A:** It is specific and kind, such as 'Add an example for your second reason.'

**Assessment:** Opinion essay draft

**Answer key:** Draft check — proficient if it has an introduction stating the opinion, 3 reasons each with evidence from a text, linking words, and a conclusion, even if not yet polished.

## Math 3: Equivalent fractions

**Prepare:** Fraction strips and stacked number lines (halves, fourths, eighths, all the same length). Make matching cards: 1/2 & 2/4, 1/2 & 4/8, 1/3 & 2/6, 3/4 & 6/8, 1 & 4/4, 2 & 4/2, 3 & 3/1.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 2/4 is bigger than 1/2 because the numbers are bigger — lay the strips side by side to show they cover the same length.
- Thinking whole numbers can't be fractions — 4/4 = 1 and 3/1 = 3.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strip equivalents
*Student:* Use fraction strips to find fractions equal to 1/2, 1/3 and 3/4.

*How to teach:* Lay down the 1/2 strip and find other strips that exactly match its length (2/4, 3/6, 4/8). Do the same for 1/3 and 3/4.

- **Q:** Name 3 fractions equal to 1/2.
  **A:** 2/4, 3/6, 4/8.
- **Q:** What fraction in sixths equals 1/3?
  **A:** 2/6.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Number line equivalents
*Student:* Stack number lines for halves, fourths and eighths and mark equivalent points.

*How to teach:* Stack 0–1 lines for halves, fourths and eighths so the 0s and 1s line up. Draw vertical lines through points that match.

- **Q:** Which fourths and eighths line up with 1/2?
  **A:** 2/4 and 4/8.
- **Q:** How many eighths equal 3/4?
  **A:** 6/8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Whole numbers
*Student:* Show that 3 = 3/1 and 1 = 4/4 = 6/6 with models.

*How to teach:* Show 3 wholes, each as one piece: 3 = 3/1. Show one whole cut into 4: 4/4 = 1; cut into 6: 6/6 = 1.

- **Q:** Write 1 as a fraction with denominator 6.
  **A:** 6/6.
- **Q:** Write 4 as a fraction.
  **A:** 4/1 (or 8/2, 12/3 …).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Equivalence game
*Student:* Play a matching game with equivalent fraction cards and explain matches.

*How to teach:* Play memory with the equivalent fraction cards; for each match the child proves it with strips or a number line. Make cards for 1/2, 2/4, 4/8, 1/3, 2/6, 3/4, 6/8, 1, 4/4, 2 and 4/2 before starting.

- **Q:** Is 2/3 equivalent to 4/6? Explain.
  **A:** Yes — 4 sixths cover the same length as 2 thirds.
- **Q:** Is 3/4 equivalent to 3/8?
  **A:** No — eighths are smaller, so 3/8 is less than 3/4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Complete an equivalent fractions quiz with models.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-item quiz with strips available for drawing. Read directions aloud if needed but give no hints. Afterward, score it with the answer key and have the child correct each missed item, explaining the fix to you.

- **Q:** 1/2 = ?/8
  **A:** 4.

**Assessment:** Equivalent fractions quiz

**Answer key:** 1) 1/2 = 2/4 2) 1/3 = 2/6 3) 3/4 = 6/8 4) 2/2 = 1 5) 3 = 3/1 6) 1 = 8/8 7) 2/6 = 1/3 8) 4/8 = 1/2 9) Is 2/3 = 4/6? Yes 10) Is 1/4 = 2/6? No. Proficient: 8+/10 with a model for at least two.

## Science 3: Environmental change

**Prepare:** Two similar potted plants, water, articles about the California drought and salmon (e.g., NOAA Fisheries or a kids' news site), a picture of a wetland.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all environmental change is caused by people — droughts, fires and floods also happen naturally.
- Thinking organisms can always adapt quickly — when change is fast, many must move or they die.

### Day 1 (Monday): Changes
*Student:* List natural and human-caused environmental changes (drought, fire, building).

*How to teach:* Brainstorm environmental changes. The child sorts them into natural (drought, wildfire, flood) and human-caused (building, dams, pollution).

- **Q:** Name one natural change and one human-caused change.
  **A:** Natural: a drought or wildfire. Human-caused: building a road or a dam.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Drought test
*Student:* Water one plant and withhold water from another; observe effects.

*How to teach:* Set up a test: water one plant normally and give the other no water for a week. The child predicts and records daily observations.

- **Q:** Predict what will happen to the unwatered plant.
  **A:** It will wilt, droop and may die without water.
- **Q:** What stays the same in this test?
  **A:** Type of plant, pot, soil and amount of light.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Case study
*Student:* Read about how the California drought affected salmon.

*How to teach:* Read about the recent California drought's effect on salmon. The child makes a cause-and-effect chain.

- **Q:** How did drought affect salmon?
  **A:** Rivers became low and warm, which killed many salmon eggs and young fish and made it harder for adults to reach spawning streams.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Predict
*Student:* Predict what happens to organisms when a wetland is drained.

*How to teach:* Show a wetland picture and ask what lives there. The child predicts what happens to each organism if the wetland is drained.

- **Q:** What happens to frogs and ducks if a wetland is drained?
  **A:** They lose their habitat; they must move somewhere else or they may die.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain cause-and-effect of environmental change in writing.

*How to teach:* The child writes a cause-and-effect paragraph about one environmental change, including what happens to organisms (survive, move or die).

- **Q:** Write a model cause-and-effect sentence.
  **A:** Because a drought lowers and warms rivers, fewer salmon eggs survive.

**Assessment:** Cause-effect explanation

**Answer key:** Cause/effect explanation: 3 = names a change, explains its effect on specific organisms, and whether they survive, move or die, with an example (salmon, wetland, plants); 2 = partial; 1 = inaccurate.

## Continuity and Change (Local History): Citizenship and public virtue

**Prepare:** Plan a small service project (park cleanup, food drive, cards for seniors) with supplies and permission.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking citizenship is only about voting as an adult. Kids practice citizenship by helping others, following laws and being honest.

### Day 1 (Monday): Citizens
*Student:* Define citizenship and list rights and responsibilities of citizens.

*How to teach:* Define citizen and list rights (free speech, vote at 18, fair trial) and responsibilities (obey laws, pay taxes, serve on juries, respect others).

- **Q:** Name one right and one responsibility of a citizen.
  **A:** Right: freedom of speech or voting (at 18). Responsibility: obeying laws, paying taxes, serving on a jury, respecting others.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Civic virtues
*Student:* Discuss honesty, respect, responsibility and courage with real examples.

*How to teach:* Discuss honesty, respect, responsibility and courage with examples.

- **Q:** Give an example of civic courage.
  **A:** Speaking up when something is unfair, or helping someone being treated badly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a service project
*Student:* Plan a small service project (park cleanup, food drive, cards for seniors).

*How to teach:* Plan the service project: goal, steps, supplies.

- **Q:** Why plan before serving?
  **A:** So you know what's needed, it's safe, and it really helps.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Serve
*Student:* Carry out the service project with family.

*How to teach:* Carry out the project.

- **Q:** How did your project help the community?
  **A:** Answer names a specific result.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write a reflection on how the project helped the community.

*How to teach:* The child writes the reflection.

- **Q:** Which civic virtue did you use most?
  **A:** Answer names one and explains with an example.

**Assessment:** Service project reflection

**Answer key:** Service project reflection rubric: 4 = describes what the project was, who it helped, a civic virtue used (responsibility, respect, honesty, courage), evidence of impact (e.g., 3 bags of trash, 25 cans collected) and what the child learned; 3 = most parts; 2 = describes only; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 3: Music around the world

**Prepare:** Videos to preview: a Japanese taiko performance, an Indonesian gamelan (Javanese or Balinese) performance, and a West African djembe lesson for kids. A bucket or drum, a world map, colored pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking world music is 'just noise' because it sounds unfamiliar. Each tradition has its own rules, scales and purposes.
- Hitting a drum the same way every time. On the djembe, different strokes (bass, tone, slap) make different sounds.

### Day 1 (Monday): Taiko
*Student:* Watch a Japanese taiko performance and copy a simple taiko rhythm with large arm motions.

*How to teach:* Watch taiko and notice the big, powerful arm motions and shouts (kiai). Copy a simple taiko rhythm on a bucket (e.g., DON DON doko DON) with big arm swings.

- **Q:** Which country does taiko drumming come from?
  **A:** Japan.
- **Q:** Why do taiko players use big arm motions?
  **A:** Movement is part of the performance and creates strong, powerful sound.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gamelan
*Student:* Listen to Indonesian gamelan and describe its layered, repeating patterns.

*How to teach:* Listen to gamelan. Point out the bronze gongs and metallophones (metal keyboards) and how different layers repeat patterns at different speeds.

- **Q:** Where does gamelan come from?
  **A:** Indonesia (especially the islands of Java and Bali).
- **Q:** How would you describe gamelan music?
  **A:** Layers of repeating, interlocking patterns played on gongs and metal keyboard instruments, with drums leading.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Djembe
*Student:* Learn bass, tone and slap strokes and a West African call-and-response rhythm on a bucket.

*How to teach:* Teach djembe strokes on a bucket: bass (flat palm in the center, low), tone (fingers together on the edge, clear), slap (relaxed fingers snap on the edge, high and sharp). Play call-and-response: you play a call, the child answers.

- **Q:** What are the 3 djembe strokes?
  **A:** Bass, tone and slap.
- **Q:** What is call and response?
  **A:** One person or group plays/sings a phrase (call) and another answers with a phrase (response).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Music map
*Student:* Mark each music tradition on a world map with a drawing of its main instrument.

*How to teach:* Mark Japan, Indonesia and West Africa (e.g., Mali or Guinea) on a world map with a drawing of the main instrument for each.

- **Q:** Where would you draw a djembe on the map?
  **A:** West Africa (e.g., Mali, Guinea, Senegal).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Discuss how each style reflects the culture and purpose that created it.

*How to teach:* Discuss how each style connects to its culture: taiko at festivals and ceremonies; gamelan for ceremonies, dance and shadow-puppet theater; djembe at community celebrations and gatherings.

- **Q:** What purpose does gamelan have in Indonesia?
  **A:** It accompanies ceremonies, dances and shadow-puppet (wayang) plays.

**Assessment:** World music map

**Answer key:** World music map key: Taiko — Japan (big barrel drums); Gamelan — Indonesia, Java/Bali (gongs, metallophones); Djembe — West Africa (goblet-shaped drum). Rubric: 4 = all 3 placed correctly with instrument drawings and a sentence on each culture's purpose; 3 = all placed, purposes partial; 2 = 1–2 placed correctly; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 3: Striking games

**Prepare:** Bat, tee, whiffle balls, paddles and a soft ball, bases (paper plates), cones for fielders; a score sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Hitting straight to the fielder. Look for open space between or behind fielders and aim there.
- Arguing calls. Agree on rules before playing and settle disagreements calmly (do-over or rock-paper-scissors).

### Day 1 (Monday): Whiffle ball
*Student:* Play a family whiffle ball game with tee or soft pitch.

*How to teach:* Play family whiffle ball with tee or soft underhand pitch: hit, run to first, simple outs (catch in air or ball to base before runner). Review bat safety.

- **Q:** How can a fielder get a runner out?
  **A:** Catch the ball in the air, or get the ball to the base before the runner arrives (or tag the runner).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paddle ball
*Student:* Play paddle ball over a line, scoring when the ball lands in open space.

*How to teach:* Play paddle ball over a line: serve underhand, rally, and score a point when the ball lands in open space on the opponent's side.

- **Q:** Where should you aim to score in paddle ball?
  **A:** Into open space away from your opponent.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Strategy
*Student:* Discuss and test placing hits away from fielders.

*How to teach:* Place 2 fielders (cones or people). Discuss where the gaps are; the child tries to hit balls into the gaps and counts successes.

- **Q:** Why place your hit away from fielders?
  **A:** So they can't catch it or field it quickly, giving you more time to run.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game
*Student:* Play a striking game of choice and keep score fairly.

*How to teach:* The child chooses a striking game and keeps score fairly for the family, calling the score after each point.

- **Q:** What does keeping score fairly mean?
  **A:** Counting points honestly for both sides and announcing the score accurately.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review
*Student:* Complete a striking unit check combining paddle and batting skills.

*How to teach:* Unit check: 5 paddle forehands to a target and 5 tee hits aimed at gaps.

- **Q:** Name one cue that is the same for batting and the paddle forehand.
  **A:** Side to target (or step toward the target / follow through).

**Assessment:** Striking unit check

**Answer key:** Striking unit check: paddle (5 forehands, 1 point each for landing in target zone with correct cues) + batting (5 tee hits, 1 point each for solid contact toward a gap) = 10. 4 = 9–10 plus fair, accurate scorekeeping; 3 = 7–8; 2 = 4–6; 1 = 0–3.

## Health 3: Online safety

**Prepare:** A computer or tablet. Look up guidance on passphrases. Prepare the family technology agreement template and the Unit 4 quiz below.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking people online are who they say they are. Anyone can pretend to be a kid online.
- Thinking it's fine to share a password with a best friend. Passwords are shared only with parents.

### Day 1 (Monday): Personal information
*Student:* Sort information into okay to share online (favorite color) and private (address, school, photos).

*How to teach:* Sort info cards into okay to share online (favorite color, pet's type) and private (full name, address, school, phone, photos, birthday, passwords).

- **Q:** Is it okay to post your school name online?
  **A:** No. It's private because it tells strangers where to find you.
- **Q:** Name two things that are private online.
  **A:** Any two: full name, address, phone number, school, passwords, photos of yourself, birthday.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Passwords
*Student:* Create a strong passphrase and learn why passwords are shared only with parents.

*How to teach:* Create a passphrase of 3–4 random words with a number or symbol. Explain only parents get to know it.

- **Q:** Who should you share your password with?
  **A:** Only your parents or guardians.
- **Q:** Which is stronger: 'max123' or 'purple-taco-river-7'?
  **A:** 'purple-taco-river-7', because it is longer and harder to guess.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Online strangers
*Student:* Discuss what to do if someone online asks personal questions or to meet.

*How to teach:* Discuss: if someone online asks personal questions, wants photos, asks to keep a secret or to meet, stop, don't reply, and tell a parent.

- **Q:** Someone in a game asks where you live. What do you do?
  **A:** Don't answer, stop chatting, and tell a parent.
- **Q:** Is it ever okay to meet an online friend in person alone?
  **A:** No. Never; always tell a parent.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family agreement
*Student:* Write a family technology agreement with screen time, apps and rules.

*How to teach:* Write a family tech agreement together: times, allowed apps, where devices are used, what to do if something online is upsetting. Everyone signs.

- **Q:** What should you do if you see something online that makes you uncomfortable?
  **A:** Close it or turn off the screen and tell a parent.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Complete a quiz on fire, traffic, water, emergency and online safety.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 quiz.

- **Q:** What should you do if you hear the smoke alarm?
  **A:** Get out right away and go to the meeting place.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 quiz; tech agreement

**Answer key:** Unit 4 quiz key: 1) Get out and stay out in a fire; crawl low under smoke. 2) Stop, drop and roll. 3) Test smoke alarms monthly. 4) Look left-right-left before crossing. 5) Helmet: eyes-ears-mouth test. 6) Escape a rip current by swimming parallel to shore. 7) Reach or throw, don't go. 8) Tell the 911 dispatcher your name, address and what happened. 9) Earthquake: drop, cover, hold on. 10) Never share your address, school or password online. Tech agreement proficient: includes screen time limits, allowed apps, what to do if something online feels wrong, and is signed. 8–10 = proficient.
