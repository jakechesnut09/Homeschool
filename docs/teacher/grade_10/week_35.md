# Teacher's Guide — 10th Grade, Week 35

## English 10: Portfolio revision

**Prepare:** All graded work from the year, a digital portfolio folder (Google Drive or similar).

**Watch for:**
- Revision that only fixes commas. Deep revision rethinks thesis, structure and evidence.
- Reflective letters that only praise. Require specific evidence of growth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Select pieces
*Student:* Select three best pieces (narrative, analysis, argument/research) for a final portfolio.

*How to teach:* The student reviews all graded work from the year and selects three best pieces (narrative, analysis, argument/research). They write a sentence explaining each choice. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Why include different types of writing?
  **A:** To show range — narrative, analytical and argumentative skills.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise piece 1
*Student:* Deeply revise one essay based on earlier feedback, tracking changes.

*How to teach:* The student deeply revises one essay based on earlier feedback, tracking changes. Focus on thesis, structure and analysis — not just commas. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What does deep revision look like?
  **A:** Strengthening the thesis, reorganizing, adding analysis, cutting weak evidence.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Revise piece 2
*Student:* Revise a second piece for style: sentence variety, precise verbs, parallelism.

*How to teach:* The student revises a second piece for style: varied sentence openers, precise verbs, parallelism. Highlight three before/after sentences to discuss. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Name two style improvements.
  **A:** Varying sentence openings, using precise verbs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Reflective letter
*Student:* Write a 600-word reflective letter explaining your growth as a reader and writer.

*How to teach:* The student writes a 600-word reflective letter explaining growth as a reader and writer. Require at least two before/after examples from their own work. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What makes a reflective letter strong?
  **A:** Specific examples of growth — before/after excerpts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Publish
*Student:* Publish the portfolio as a digital document or website.

*How to teach:* The student assembles the portfolio as a digital document or website with the three pieces and the letter. Score it with the portfolio rubric. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Why publish?
  **A:** To share work with an audience and preserve it.

**Assessment:** Portfolio rubric.

**Answer key:** Portfolio rubric (4-point each): three strong pieces (narrative, analysis, argument/research); evidence of substantial revision (tracked changes); reflective letter (600 words) that cites specific growth with examples; polished digital publication.

## Geometry: Cumulative review

**Prepare:** Assemble a cumulative review packet (one page per strand) and a practice final mirroring the final exam structure. Calculator, compass, protractor.

**Watch for:**
- Relying on memory for formulas without knowing when they apply. Have the student build a one-page formula sheet with a sample problem for each.
- Skipping diagrams on word problems. Require a labeled sketch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Transformations & proof
*Student:* Mixed review of transformations, congruence and proof.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of transformations, congruence and proof. The student works problems and one proof; check after each set.

- **Q:** Rotate (4, 1) 90° counterclockwise about the origin.
  **A:** (−1, 4).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Similarity & trig
*Student:* Mixed review of similarity and trigonometry.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of similarity and trigonometry: give 8–10 problems (similar-triangle proportions, special right triangles, SOH-CAH-TOA, Law of Sines/Cosines). The student works one set of five, you check, then they do the next set; any miss sends them back to their notes for that problem type.

- **Q:** A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse 12. Find both legs.
  **A:** 6 and 6√3 ≈ 10.39.
- **Q:** In right △ABC, angle A = 28°, adjacent side 15. Find the opposite side.
  **A:** 15 tan 28° ≈ 7.98.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Circles
*Student:* Mixed review of circles and coordinate geometry.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of circles and coordinate geometry: arcs and inscribed angles, tangents, arc length and sectors, circle equations by completing the square, and slope/distance proofs. The student works in sets of five with a check after each set and adds any formula they forgot to their one-page formula sheet.

- **Q:** Find the center and radius of x² + y² + 4x − 2y − 4 = 0.
  **A:** (x + 2)² + (y − 1)² = 9: center (−2, 1), radius 3.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Measurement & probability
*Student:* Mixed review of area, volume and probability.

*How to teach:* Mixed review of area, volume and probability: composite areas, surface area and volume of solids, the addition and multiplication rules, conditional probability and counting. The student works in sets of five; for each miss, they write the correct formula and redo a similar problem.

- **Q:** Find the volume of a sphere with diameter 10.
  **A:** (4/3)π(125) ≈ 523.6.
- **Q:** P(drawing a red card or an ace)?
  **A:** 26/52 + 4/52 − 2/52 = 28/52 = 7/13.

### Day 5 (Friday): Practice final
*Student:* Take a full-length practice final and grade it.

*How to teach:* Give the full practice final in the assessment key under timed conditions; grade it together. Allow no notes (formula sheet only if stated); about 30–50 minutes. Grade it together right away: the student reworks every missed item and explains the error aloud, and you note any skill to revisit next week.

- **Q:** Which three topics had the most errors?
  **A:** Answers vary; the student lists them to guide Week 36 targeted review.

**Assessment:** Practice final.

**Answer key:** Practice final (sample items with answers): 1) Reflect (2, −7) over the y-axis → (−2, −7). 2) Rhombus diagonals 6 and 8 → side 5. 3) △ABC ~ △DEF, k = 3, perimeter of △ABC 12 → △DEF perimeter 36. 4) Right triangle angle 30°, hypotenuse 18 → opposite 9. 5) Inscribed angle 40° → arc 80°. 6) Circle x² + y² − 10y = 0 → center (0, 5), r = 5. 7) Cone r = 6, h = 4 → 48π ≈ 150.8. 8) P(sum 9 with 2 dice) → 4/36 = 1/9. 9) 6C2 → 15. 10) Proof: base angles of an isosceles triangle are congruent. Grade: 90/80/70 cutoffs; use results to plan Week 36 review.

## Chemistry in the Earth System: Capstone: Chemistry solution for an Earth system challenge

**Prepare:** Help the student choose a challenge and gather supplies for the prototype (e.g., for a water filter: a cut plastic bottle, coffee filter, sand, gravel, activated charcoal from a pet store, muddy water — the filtered water is not safe to drink; for an insulated container: boxes, foam, foil, thermometer). Prepare the capstone rubric to share at the start.

**Watch for:**
- Students start building before defining criteria. Require written criteria and constraints first so the test has something to measure.
- Students cite only one website. Require three sources, including one government or peer-reviewed source.
- Students treat a prototype failure as failure. A failed test produces data for redesign; that is engineering.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose challenge
*Student:* Choose a challenge (urban air quality, ocean acidification, home energy) and define criteria and constraints.

*How to teach:* Discuss three options. The student chooses one and writes a problem statement, at least three criteria (measurable) and two constraints.

- **Q:** For an activated-charcoal water filter, give a measurable criterion.
  **A:** Example: reduces turbidity so text is readable through 10 cm of water, or removes a set amount of food dye color within 2 minutes.
- **Q:** Give a constraint.
  **A:** Example: total materials cost under $10, or uses only household materials.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Research
*Student:* Research the chemistry behind the problem with three sources.

*How to teach:* The student finds three sources (at least one .gov, .edu or peer-reviewed) and writes notes on the chemistry of the problem and solution, with citations.

- **Q:** How does activated charcoal remove contaminants?
  **A:** Adsorption: molecules stick to the charcoal's enormous internal surface area (hundreds of m² per gram).
- **Q:** Why is source quality important?
  **A:** Reliable sources reduce the chance of basing the design on wrong information.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prototype/test
*Student:* Build a simple model or test (e.g., activated charcoal water filter, insulated container).

*How to teach:* The student builds and tests the prototype, recording quantitative data and at least two trials.

- **Q:** Why run more than one trial?
  **A:** To check that results are repeatable and to estimate variability.
- **Q:** Is filtered muddy water safe to drink?
  **A:** No — the filter does not reliably remove bacteria or viruses; it must be disinfected or boiled.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evaluate
*Student:* Evaluate your solution against criteria and list trade-offs.

*How to teach:* The student evaluates the prototype against each criterion in a table (met / partly / not met) and lists trade-offs and improvements.

- **Q:** What is the difference between 'evaluating' and 'describing' a design?
  **A:** Evaluating judges performance against criteria using data; describing only tells what was built.

### Day 5 (Friday): Draft presentation
*Student:* Prepare a slide presentation.

*How to teach:* The student drafts a slide presentation (8–10 slides). Review with the rubric and give two specific suggestions.

- **Q:** What should the data slide include?
  **A:** A graph or table with labeled units and a one-sentence takeaway.

**Assessment:** Capstone checkpoint.

**Answer key:** Capstone checkpoint rubric (4 levels): 4 = Problem statement; at least three measurable criteria and two constraints; chemistry explained with three cited sources (e.g., adsorption of contaminants onto activated carbon's huge surface area; insulation reducing conduction/convection/radiation); prototype built and tested with quantitative data (e.g., turbidity rating before/after, temperature drop over time); evaluation against each criterion with trade-offs; draft slides with title, problem, chemistry, design, data, evaluation, next steps. 3 = all parts present but data or chemistry thin. 2 = missing testing or sources. 1 = idea only.

## Modern World History: Cumulative review

**Prepare:** Review packet, blank world and Europe maps, a timed document set, and a practice final modeled on the final exam (multiple choice, map, DBQ).

**Watch for:**
- Memorizing isolated facts without themes. Organize review around democracy, nationalism, imperialism, industrialization and conflict.
- Skipping map practice; maps carry real points on the final.

### Day 1 (Monday): Big themes
*Student:* Create a thematic timeline of democracy, nationalism and imperialism across the course.

*How to teach:* The student builds a thematic timeline with five colored strands: democracy, nationalism, imperialism, industrialization, global conflict. Each strand needs at least five events from Weeks 1–34. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Trace democracy from the course's start to its end in three events.
  **A:** Athenian democracy / Locke and the Enlightenment → the American and French Revolutions → post-1989 democratization in Eastern Europe or South Africa's 1994 election.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map review
*Student:* Complete a map review of key places.

*How to teach:* Give a blank map review: the student labels key places from each unit, then checks against an atlas. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Where is the Suez Canal, and why was it important?
  **A:** In Egypt, linking the Mediterranean and Red Seas; it shortened the route between Europe and Asia and was vital to the British Empire.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Document practice
*Student:* Practice a timed document set.

*How to teach:* Give a timed document set (40 minutes). Review sourcing and corroboration. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** What is corroboration?
  **A:** Comparing multiple sources to see where they agree or disagree, to judge reliability.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Seminar
*Student:* Seminar: What is the most important turning point of the modern era?

*How to teach:* Hold a seminar: What is the most important turning point of the modern era? Each participant defends one with three pieces of evidence. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Make a case for the Industrial Revolution as the key turning point.
  **A:** It transformed how people work and live, created modern cities and classes, drove imperialism for raw materials and markets, and enabled the industrial warfare of the 20th century.

### Day 5 (Friday): Practice exam
*Student:* Take a practice final.

*How to teach:* Give the practice final under timed conditions and grade it together. The student lists the three weakest topics for Week 36 review. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** What was decided at the Berlin Conference?
  **A:** European powers set rules for claiming African territory (1884–85), without African participation.

**Assessment:** Practice final.

**Answer key:** Practice final (sample answers): 1) Locke → natural rights; consent of the governed. 2) Haiti → independence 1804. 3) Factory Act 1833 → limited child labor. 4) Berlin Conference → 1884–85, divided Africa. 5) Article 231 → war guilt. 6) Enabling Act → Hitler's dictatorship (1933). 7) Truman Doctrine → containment (1947). 8) India's independence → 1947 with partition. 9) Berlin Wall → fell November 9, 1989. Map items: locate Sarajevo, the Somme, Stalingrad, Normandy, Hiroshima, Berlin, the Suez Canal, Korea's 38th parallel. Essay: score with the DBQ rubric. DBQ rubric (4-point per row): Thesis — directly answers the question with a defensible claim; Evidence — uses at least 4 documents accurately plus outside knowledge; Sourcing — explains author, purpose, audience or context for at least 2 documents; Analysis — groups documents and explains how they support the claim; Writing — organized, clear. 17–20 = proficient.

## PE 10 (Course 2): Final fitness test

**Prepare:** Testing equipment from Week 1; baseline and midyear logs; graph paper or spreadsheet.

**Watch for:**
- Students compare only final vs. baseline. Include midyear to see the trend.
- Students compute percent change from the new score. Use the original (baseline) as the denominator.
- Students set summer goals unrelated to results. Base them on the weakest components.

### Day 1 (Monday): Aerobic retest
*Student:* Complete the final PACER or 1-mile run.

*How to teach:* Final PACER or 1-mile run.

- **Q:** Mile time improved from 9:30 to 8:24. What's the percent change in time?
  **A:** 570 s → 504 s: (504 − 570) ÷ 570 × 100 ≈ −11.6% (11.6% faster).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Strength retest
*Student:* Complete final push-up and curl-up tests.

*How to teach:* Final push-up and curl-up tests.

- **Q:** Push-ups went from 12 to 20. Percent change?
  **A:** (20 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 ≈ 66.7% increase.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility retest
*Student:* Complete final sit-and-reach, trunk lift and body composition measures.

*How to teach:* Final sit-and-reach, trunk lift and body composition.

- **Q:** Why is body composition only one part of fitness?
  **A:** Fitness also includes aerobic capacity, strength, endurance and flexibility; healthy bodies vary.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Analyze
*Student:* Graph baseline, midyear and final results and calculate percent change.

*How to teach:* Graph baseline, midyear and final; calculate percent change.

- **Q:** What does a flat line between midyear and final suggest?
  **A:** A plateau; training may need to change (new stimulus or more consistency).

### Day 5 (Friday): Goals
*Student:* Write summer goals based on results.

*How to teach:* The student writes summer goals based on results.

- **Q:** Write a SMART summer goal.
  **A:** 'Run 3 times per week and reach 45 PACER laps by August 20.'

**Assessment:** Final test.

**Answer key:** Final test log: full credit for all tests repeated with the same protocols, graphed (baseline, midyear, final), percent change calculated correctly (e.g., push-ups 12 → 20 = +66.7%), HFZ status for each, and summer SMART goals tied to results.

## Computer Science Principles: Create Performance Task: refine and document

**Prepare:** Install or locate a screen recorder (built into Windows: Xbox Game Bar Win+G; Mac: Shift-Cmd-5; or a browser tool). Re-read the current Create task video and Personalized Project Reference (PPR) requirements on the College Board site.

**Watch for:**
- Video too long or with voice narration. The video must be 1 minute or less and must not contain voice narration (captions are allowed) and must not show personally identifying information.
- Including comments in the PPR code segments. The PPR code segments must not include comments.
- Forgetting to acknowledge others' code or media. Any code or media from others must be cited in code comments.

### Day 1 (Monday): User testing
*Student:* Have two users test the program, note usability and accessibility problems, and prioritize fixes.

*How to teach:* Two users test the program; note usability and accessibility problems (text size, contrast, unclear instructions); prioritize fixes.

- **Q:** Name one accessibility improvement for an app.
  **A:** Larger text and high color contrast, clear labels on buttons, not relying on color alone, alt text for images.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Refine
*Student:* Fix the top issues, add code comments, and acknowledge any code or media from others with correct license attribution.

*How to teach:* Fix top issues, add code comments, and add acknowledgments with license attribution for any outside code or media.

- **Q:** How should you acknowledge an image you used from another source?
  **A:** In a code comment: the source/author, URL and license (e.g. 'Image: "Cat" by J. Doe, CC BY 2.0, via Openverse').

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Video
*Student:* Record a 1-minute-or-less screen video showing input, program functionality and output.

*How to teach:* Record a 1-minute-or-less video showing input, functionality and output (no voice, no PII).

- **Q:** What must the video show?
  **A:** Input to the program, at least one aspect of the program's functionality, and output produced.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Written responses
*Student:* Capture your list and procedure code segments in a Personalized Project Reference and practice the written-response prompts about them.

*How to teach:* Capture list and procedure code segments in the PPR (no comments) and practice the written-response prompts.

- **Q:** Practice: explain how your program would be written differently without the list.
  **A:** A proficient answer describes the specific data, says it would require separate variables for each item and repeated code, and explains it couldn't handle more items without rewriting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Submit
*Student:* Submit the program code, video and PPR, and self-score with the College Board rubric.

*How to teach:* Submit the code, video and PPR (to AP Digital Portfolio if registered) and self-score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What does it mean that your procedure's parameter 'affects functionality'?
  **A:** Calling the procedure with different arguments produces different behavior or results.

**Assessment:** Create task self-score with the AP rubric.

**Answer key:** Create task self-score (current College Board rubric): check each row — program purpose and function demonstrated in the video (input, functionality, output); data abstraction (list stores data and is used); managing complexity (list makes the program simpler); procedural abstraction (procedure with parameter, called); algorithm implementation (sequencing, selection and iteration in the procedure); testing (two calls with different arguments producing different results). Proficient submission: video ≤ 1 minute, no narration, no PII; PPR includes list creation, list use, procedure definition and a call, no comments; written-response practice answers name exactly how the list and procedure work.
