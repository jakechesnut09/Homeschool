# Teacher's Guide — 10th Grade, Week 18

## English 10: Semester 1 final

**Prepare:** Semester notes; prepare the semester exam: a cold-read passage (e.g., a short story excerpt from CommonLit), terms list, grammar items, and an argument prompt.

**Watch for:**
- Over-reliance on summary in timed writing. Remind: claim, evidence, analysis.
- Studying terms only as definitions. Practice identifying them in passages.

### Day 1 (Monday): Final essay due
*Student:* Submit the polished Things Fall Apart essay with a Works Cited page.

*How to teach:* The student submits the polished Things Fall Apart essay with a Works Cited page. Score it with the rubric and return comments for the portfolio later in the year. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Format a Works Cited entry for Things Fall Apart (Anchor, 1994).
  **A:** Achebe, Chinua. Things Fall Apart. Anchor Books, 1994.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review terms
*Student:* Review literary and rhetorical terms from Units 1–4 with a self-made flashcard set.

*How to teach:* The student makes flashcards for the literary and rhetorical terms from Units 1–4 (term on front; definition and an example from a text on back). Quiz each other, setting aside missed cards for a second round.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a soliloquy and an aside?
  **A:** A soliloquy is a long speech alone on stage revealing thoughts; an aside is a brief remark to the audience others on stage don't hear.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cold read practice
*Student:* Complete a practice cold-read passage with multiple-choice and short-answer questions.

*How to teach:* Give a practice cold-read passage (CommonLit or a released test). The student reads questions first, annotates, then answers; go over the reasoning for each multiple-choice answer, not just the letter.

- **Q:** What's a good strategy for a cold-read passage?
  **A:** Read the questions first, annotate the passage for key details, then return to the text for evidence.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam: cold-read analysis, terms, grammar, and a timed argument essay.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam (about 2 hours, can be split): cold-read analysis, terms, grammar and a timed argument essay. Grade with the assessment key. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Define allusion and give an example from this semester.
  **A:** A reference to another work or event; Achebe's title alludes to Yeats's 'The Second Coming.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a semester reflection comparing your Week 1 writing sample to your Week 18 essay.

*How to teach:* Hand back the Week 1 reader autobiography beside the final essay. The student writes a one-page reflection naming specific growth with examples (thesis, evidence, sentence variety) and one goal for second semester.

- **Q:** Name two specific signs of growth to look for.
  **A:** Stronger thesis statements and better integrated, analyzed quotations (also sentence variety and fewer errors).

**Assessment:** Semester exam and final essay.

**Answer key:** Semester exam answer key (terms): foreshadowing, irony (verbal/dramatic/situational), unreliable narrator, tragic hero, hamartia, hubris, catharsis, chorus, stichomythia, soliloquy, iambic pentameter, ethos/pathos/logos, anaphora, antithesis, tricolon, proverb, allusion. Grammar: correct comma splices with semicolons; identify participial/appositive phrases; parallel structure. Timed argument essay rubric (4-point): claim, two pieces of evidence, counterclaim, organization, conventions. Final TFA essay rubric (4-point): thesis, evidence (8 quotations), analysis, organization, MLA format and Works Cited, conventions.

## Geometry: Semester 1 review and final

**Prepare:** Assemble a review packet (Khan Academy unit tests or CK-12 practice) covering Units 1–5, plus the semester exam described in the assessment key. Have compass, protractor, calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing proofs without marking the diagram. Require marking givens first on every review proof.
- Over-relying on memorized rules for rotations without sketching. Encourage a quick sketch to check every coordinate answer.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review transformations
*Student:* Mixed review of transformations and congruence problems.

*How to teach:* Work mixed transformation and congruence problems; the student works first, and you check each set of 5 before continuing.

- **Q:** Rotate (−1, 6) 180° about the origin, then reflect over the x-axis.
  **A:** (1, −6), then (1, 6).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review proof
*Student:* Practice four proofs from Units 3–4.

*How to teach:* The student completes four proofs from Units 3–4 with the reasons list; discuss one aloud as if teaching it to you.

- **Q:** In parallelogram ABCD, what reason justifies ∠BAC ≅ ∠DCA when AC is a diagonal?
  **A:** AB ∥ DC, so they are alternate interior angles (congruent).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review similarity
*Student:* Mixed similarity problems including indirect measurement.

*How to teach:* Mixed similarity practice including side splitter and indirect measurement problems.

- **Q:** A 6-ft person casts a 4-ft shadow when a flagpole casts a 22-ft shadow. How tall is the pole?
  **A:** 6/4 = h/22 → h = 33 ft.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam (about 90 minutes) from the assessment key. No notes; calculator and tools allowed. Allow no notes (formula sheet only if stated); about 30–50 minutes. Grade it together right away: the student reworks every missed item and explains the error aloud, and you note any skill to revisit next week.

- **Q:** (Self-check after exam) Which topic felt hardest, and what's one problem type to review?
  **A:** Answers vary; the student names a specific skill (e.g., overlapping-triangle proofs).

### Day 5 (Friday): Error analysis
*Student:* Correct exam errors and write explanations for each mistake.

*How to teach:* Return the graded exam. For each missed item the student writes what went wrong and redoes the problem correctly.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a careless error and a concept error?
  **A:** A careless error is a slip (arithmetic, sign) on a skill you know; a concept error is misunderstanding the idea, which needs reteaching.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 final exam.

**Answer key:** Semester 1 exam (sample items, 1 point unless noted): 1) Midpoint of (−2, 8) and (6, 0) → (2, 4). 2) Distance (−1, −1) to (5, 7) → 10. 3) Rotate (3, −4) 90° counterclockwise → (4, 3). 4) Reflect (5, 2) over y = x → (2, 5). 5) Supplement of 3x when x = 25 → 105°. 6) Interior angle of a regular 12-gon → 150°. 7) Rhombus diagonals 10 and 24 → side 13. 8) Line ⊥ to y = −4x + 1 through (0, 3) → y = ¼x + 3. 9) △ABC ~ △DEF, AB = 5, DE = 15, AC = 7 → DF = 21. 10) Shadow problem: 2 m pole, 3 m shadow; building shadow 30 m → 20 m. 11) Proof (4 pts): parallelogram opposite sides congruent. 12) Proof (4 pts): given AB ≅ AD and AC bisects ∠BAD, prove △ABC ≅ △ADC (SAS with reflexive AC). Grade: 90%+ A, 80% B, 70% C.

## Chemistry in the Earth System: Semester 1 review and final

**Prepare:** Gather the solar heater, semester notes, and a practice set. Build the semester exam from the key below (about 30 items), allowing 60 minutes with a periodic table, formula sheet and calculator.

**Watch for:**
- Students memorize formulas without units. Require units at every step on review problems.
- Students review only what they like. Use the Week 1–17 quiz error logs to target weak areas.
- Students treat the engineering summary as a list of changes. It must explain trade-offs (e.g., better insulation vs. cost or weight).

### Day 1 (Monday): Device refinement
*Student:* Refine and retest the solar heater design and write an engineering summary of trade-offs.

*How to teach:* The student changes one variable of the solar heater (more insulation, a reflector, a glazing layer), retests under similar sun, and writes an engineering summary comparing results and trade-offs.

- **Q:** Why should you change only one design feature at a time?
  **A:** So you can tell which change caused the difference in performance.
- **Q:** Give an example of a trade-off in the heater design.
  **A:** Adding a second plastic layer reduces heat loss but also blocks some sunlight and costs more material.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review atoms & bonds
*Student:* Review atomic structure, periodic trends and bonding with practice problems.

*How to teach:* Mixed practice: configurations, trends, ionic formulas, Lewis structures, moles. The student works problems, checking each with you before moving on; reteach anything missed twice.

- **Q:** Name and give the formula of the compound formed by magnesium and nitrogen.
  **A:** Magnesium nitride, Mg₃N₂.
- **Q:** Which is more electronegative, N or P?
  **A:** N.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Review energy
*Student:* Review calorimetry and thermal energy problems.

*How to teach:* Mixed practice on q = mcΔT, mixing problems, specific heat and phase-change plateaus.

- **Q:** How much heat is released when 300 g of water cools from 90 °C to 40 °C?
  **A:** 300 × 4.184 × 50 = 62,760 J ≈ 62.8 kJ.
- **Q:** A 40 g metal sample absorbs 720 J and warms 20 °C. Find its specific heat.
  **A:** c = 720 ÷ (40 × 20) = 0.90 J/g·°C (close to aluminum).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam.

*How to teach:* Give the semester exam in one 60-minute sitting with standard reference sheets. Do not help during the exam.

- **Q:** (Exam day) After the exam, have the student name the topic that felt hardest.
  **A:** Answers vary; use it to plan the Day 5 corrections.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Correct errors and update the driving-question board with answered questions.

*How to teach:* Return the exam. The student corrects every missed item with an explanation, then updates the driving-question board, moving answered candle questions to 'answered' with the evidence.

- **Q:** Which driving question about the candle can you now answer fully?
  **A:** Example: 'Where does the wax go?' — wax vapor reacts with oxygen to form CO₂ and H₂O gases, conserving atoms and mass.

**Assessment:** Semester 1 final and engineering summary.

**Answer key:** Semester exam key (sample of 15 core items): 1) Sig figs in 0.07020 — 4. 2) Balance C₂H₆ + O₂ — 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O. 3) 250 g water warmed 8.0 °C: 8370 J. 4) Average mass: 90% X-20, 10% X-22 → 20.2 u. 5) Configuration of Cl: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵. 6) Larger: Na or Mg — Na. 7) Alpha decay of U-238 → Th-234. 8) 64 g of an isotope after 3 half-lives — 8 g. 9) Formula of aluminum chloride — AlCl₃. 10) Shape of NH₃ — trigonal pyramidal. 11) Why H₂O has a high boiling point — hydrogen bonding. 12) Moles in 88.0 g CO₂ — 2.00 mol. 13) Mixing 200 g water at 60 °C with 100 g at 30 °C — 50 °C. 14) Why ice-water temperature stays at 0 °C — energy goes into melting (breaking attractions). 15) Driver of mantle convection — internal heat (radioactive decay + primordial heat). Engineering summary rubric: 4 = states original vs. revised temperature gain with numbers, explains why the change worked, and discusses at least two trade-offs (cost, materials, time, size).

**Assessment:** Semester exam.

**Answer key:** Semester exam (sample answers): 1) Locke's natural rights → life, liberty, property. 2) Montesquieu → separation of powers. 3) Haiti → first Black republic, 1804. 4) Napoleonic Code → legal equality for men. 5) Factory Act 1833 → limited child labor. 6) Berlin Conference → divided Africa without Africans. 7) MAIN causes. 8) Article 231 → war guilt. 9) April Theses → 'Peace, Land, Bread.' ## Computer Science Principles: Data app project and semester exam

**Prepare:** Pick an App Lab dataset with a categorical column for the dropdown. Gather 30 AP-style practice questions (Khan Academy AP CSP or Code.org unit assessments) and build/print a 50-question semester exam from Code.org unit assessments or AP practice materials covering Units 1–4.

**Watch for:**
- Not handling empty results. If no rows match, the app should display a message like 'No results' instead of a blank screen.
- Comparing dropdown text to data with different capitals or spaces. Make values match exactly or standardize them.
- Cramming instead of targeted review. Sort missed questions by topic and re-study the weakest topic first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose a dataset from the App Lab data library and plan an app that filters it based on user input.

*How to teach:* The student explores datasets and writes a plan: which dataset, which column filters, what results to show, and a sketch of the screen.

- **Q:** What should the plan specify?
  **A:** The dataset and columns used, the user input (dropdown options), the filtering condition, and what output (list and count) will be shown.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Build the data app so a dropdown choice filters the dataset with a traversal and displays matching results and a count.

*How to teach:* Build: load columns into lists with getColumn; on dropdown "change", loop through the lists, appending matching items to a results list; display results and results.length.

- **Q:** Why use parallel lists (e.g. names and categories) with the same index?
  **A:** Each index refers to the same row, so when categories[i] matches, names[i] is the matching item.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Have a user try three different inputs, record any wrong or empty results, and refine the app to handle them.

*How to teach:* A user tries three inputs; the student records expected vs. actual results and fixes problems like empty output.

- **Q:** What should the app display if no items match?
  **A:** A clear message such as 'No matches found' and a count of 0.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Semester review
*Student:* Review units 1–4 by completing 30 AP-style practice questions and making a one-page summary sheet of missed concepts.

*How to teach:* The student completes 30 practice questions, then makes a one-page summary of concepts missed, organized by unit.

- **Q:** Convert 1101₂ to decimal and 20 to binary.
  **A:** 13; 10100₂.

### Day 5 (Friday): Semester exam
*Student:* Take the semester exam: 50 AP-style multiple-choice questions covering data, the internet and programming through traversals.

*How to teach:* Give the 50-question semester exam (about 90 minutes). Grade and record weak topics for spring review.

- **Q:** What does this code display? L ← [2, 4, 6]; sum ← 0; FOR EACH n IN L { sum ← sum + n }; DISPLAY(sum)
  **A:** 12.

**Assessment:** Semester exam and data app.

**Answer key:** Data app rubric (4 levels): 4 = dropdown triggers a traversal that filters the dataset, displays matching results and an accurate count, handles empty results, tested with three inputs; 3 = works but doesn't handle empty/edge cases; 2 = partial filtering; 1 = doesn't run. Semester exam: score against the answer key that accompanies the question source you choose; target 80%+ (40/50). Representative items and answers: 110₂ + 11₂ = 1001₂ (9); 2^10 = 1,024; DNS converts domain names to IP addresses; lossless compression allows exact reconstruction; REPEAT 3 TIMES { x ← x + 2 } from x ← 1 gives 7; list [3,5,7] in AP pseudocode, L[2] = 5.
