# Teacher's Guide — 10th Grade, Week 20

## English 10: Night by Elie Wiesel, sections 1–3

**Prepare:** Night by Elie Wiesel (Marion Wiesel translation), USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia (ushmm.org). Parent preview: Night describes atrocities, including children burned and hangings; read it yourself first and plan to discuss feelings. Allow breaks.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Jews of Sighet 'should have known.' Discuss denial, propaganda, and how unimaginable the truth seemed.
- Reading Night as a novel. It is a memoir — Wiesel's own experience.

### Day 1 (Monday): Context
*Student:* Use the U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum encyclopedia to create a timeline of the Holocaust 1933–1945.

*How to teach:* Use the USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia to build a timeline 1933–1945 with about ten events (Hitler becomes chancellor 1933, Nuremberg Laws 1935, Kristallnacht 1938, WWII begins 1939, Wannsee Conference 1942, Hungary deportations 1944, liberation 1945). Discuss where Sighet fits.

- **Q:** When was Sighet's Jewish community deported?
  **A:** Spring 1944 (May), after Germany occupied Hungary in March 1944.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Section 1
*Student:* Read section 1 and analyze why the Jews of Sighet ignore Moishe the Beadle.

*How to teach:* Read section 1. The student analyzes why people ignore Moishe. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Why don't the Jews of Sighet believe Moishe?
  **A:** His story seems impossible; they think he's mad, want to believe the war will end soon, and hope for safety.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Section 2
*Student:* Read section 2 (the train and Madame Schächter) and discuss symbolism of fire.

*How to teach:* Read section 2. Discuss the fire symbolism. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** How do people treat Madame Schächter, and what does her vision foreshadow?
  **A:** They tie, gag and beat her to keep her quiet; her vision of fire foreshadows the crematoria at Birkenau.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Section 3
*Student:* Read section 3 (arrival at Birkenau) and track changes in Eliezer's faith with quotes.

*How to teach:* Read section 3. The student tracks Eliezer's faith with quotes. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** How does Eliezer react when others recite the Kaddish?
  **A:** He feels anger at God — 'Why should I bless His name?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflect
*Student:* Reading quiz and a journal entry responding to 'Never shall I forget' passage.

*How to teach:* Give the reading quiz. Then read the 'Never shall I forget' passage aloud; the student writes a journal response on what Wiesel says he will never forget and how the repetition affects the reader. Allow time to talk about feelings.

- **Q:** What literary device structures the 'Never shall I forget' passage?
  **A:** Anaphora — repetition of 'Never shall I forget' at the start of lines.

**Assessment:** Reading quiz and faith-tracking chart.

**Answer key:** Reading quiz (answers): 1) Who is Moishe the Beadle? → A poor, humble man who teaches Eliezer Kabbalah. 2) What happens to him? → Deported as a foreign Jew, he escapes a mass shooting in Poland and returns to warn Sighet; no one believes him. 3) What does Madame Schächter scream on the train? → 'Fire! I see a fire!' 4) What do they see at Birkenau? → Flames from a chimney/crematorium. 5) What advice is Eliezer given at selection? → Say he is eighteen (his father says forty). 6) What happens to his mother and sister Tzipora? → They are separated and killed. Faith chart: full credit for at least four quotes tracking change, including 'Never shall I forget those flames that consumed my faith forever.'

## Geometry: Trigonometric ratios

**Prepare:** Protractor, ruler, scientific calculator set to DEGREE mode (check this first!).

**Watch for:**
- Calculator in radian mode. sin 30 should be 0.5; if it shows −0.988, switch to degrees.
- Labeling 'opposite' and 'adjacent' without reference to an angle. They depend on which acute angle you are using.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ratios from similarity
*Student:* Draw three different-size right triangles with a 35° angle and compute side ratios to see they're constant.

*How to teach:* The student draws three right triangles of different sizes with a 35° angle, measures sides, and computes opposite/hypotenuse, adjacent/hypotenuse, opposite/adjacent. Discuss why the ratios match (AA similarity).

- **Q:** Why are the side ratios the same for every right triangle with a 35° angle?
  **A:** All such triangles are similar by AA, so corresponding side ratios are equal.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): SOH-CAH-TOA
*Student:* Write trig ratios for given triangles and find them on a calculator.

*How to teach:* Teach SOH-CAH-TOA, label sides relative to an angle, and check values on the calculator.

- **Q:** In right △ABC with right angle C, AC = 12, BC = 5, AB = 13. Find sin A, cos A, tan A.
  **A:** sin A = 5/13, cos A = 12/13, tan A = 5/12.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Complementary angles
*Student:* Explain why sin(x) = cos(90° – x) and solve equations using this.

*How to teach:* Show that the opposite side of one acute angle is the adjacent side of the other, so sin x = cos(90° − x). Solve equations using this.

- **Q:** cos 72° equals the sine of which angle?
  **A:** sin 18°.
- **Q:** Solve sin(x + 15)° = cos(2x)°.
  **A:** x + 15 + 2x = 90 → x = 25.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Find missing sides
*Student:* Use trig ratios to find missing side lengths in 12 problems.

*How to teach:* Model the steps: label, choose the ratio that uses the known side and the unknown, set up an equation, and solve (note when the unknown is in the denominator). The student completes 12 problems.

- **Q:** Angle 40°, hypotenuse 20. Find the opposite side.
  **A:** 20 sin 40° ≈ 12.86.
- **Q:** Angle 55°, adjacent 10. Find the hypotenuse.
  **A:** 10/cos 55° ≈ 17.43.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on trig ratios.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz in the assessment key. Allow no notes (formula sheet only if stated); about 30–50 minutes. Grade it together right away: the student reworks every missed item and explains the error aloud, and you note any skill to revisit next week.

- **Q:** Can the sine of an acute angle be greater than 1? Why?
  **A:** No — the opposite side is always shorter than the hypotenuse.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz with answers: 1) Right triangle with legs 3 (opposite A) and 4, hypotenuse 5: sin A = 3/5, cos A = 4/5, tan A = 3/4. 2) sin 35° ≈ 0.574, cos 35° ≈ 0.819, tan 35° ≈ 0.700. 3) If sin x = 0.6, what is cos(90° − x)? → 0.6. 4) Solve sin(2x + 10)° = cos(x + 20)° → 3x + 30 = 90, x = 20. 5) Hypotenuse 20, angle 40°, opposite side → 20 sin 40° ≈ 12.86. 6) Adjacent 15, angle 28°, opposite → 15 tan 28° ≈ 7.98. 7) Opposite 7, angle 50°, hypotenuse → 7/sin 50° ≈ 9.14.

## Chemistry in the Earth System: Types of chemical reactions

**Prepare:** Gather 3% hydrogen peroxide, active dry yeast, a tall glass or bottle, wooden splints or long matches, an iron nail (sanded clean), copper sulfate root killer (toxic — gloves, goggles, keep from children and pets, dispose per label) or vinegar + salt with dull pennies, Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate), washing soda (sodium carbonate — irritant, gloves), and clear cups. Print an activity series (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, (H), Cu, Ag, Au).

**Watch for:**
- Students think any metal can replace any other. A metal replaces another only if it is higher on the activity series.
- Students think the precipitate is the original solid coming back. It is a new insoluble compound formed by swapping ion partners.
- Students classify combustion and synthesis interchangeably. Combustion is a reaction with oxygen releasing energy (often a hydrocarbon making CO₂ and H₂O); synthesis is any A + B → AB.

### Day 1 (Monday): Decomposition
*Student:* Decompose hydrogen peroxide with yeast and test for oxygen with a glowing splint.

*How to teach:* Pour ~50 mL of 3% peroxide into a tall glass and add a teaspoon of yeast. Light a splint, blow it out so it glows, and lower it into the foam (adult present). The student records observations and writes the balanced equation.

- **Q:** Why does the glowing splint relight?
  **A:** The reaction produces oxygen gas, which makes the glowing wood burn faster.
- **Q:** What role does the yeast play?
  **A:** It contains the enzyme catalase, a catalyst that speeds decomposition; it is not used up.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Single replacement
*Student:* Place an iron nail in copper sulfate solution (or vinegar + salt with pennies) and observe copper plating.

*How to teach:* With gloves and goggles, place a clean iron nail in copper sulfate solution (or soak pennies in vinegar + salt, then add a nail). Observe over 20 minutes and the next day. The student writes the equation and explains using the activity series.

- **Q:** What is the reddish coating on the nail?
  **A:** Copper metal; iron replaced copper in solution because iron is more active.
- **Q:** Why does the blue color fade?
  **A:** Blue Cu²⁺ ions are being removed from the solution and replaced by pale Fe²⁺ ions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Double replacement
*Student:* Mix Epsom salt and washing soda solutions to form a precipitate.

*How to teach:* Dissolve a spoon of Epsom salt in one cup of water and a spoon of washing soda in another, then mix. The student observes the precipitate, writes the equation, and identifies the swapped partners.

- **Q:** Write the balanced equation and name the precipitate.
  **A:** MgSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → MgCO₃(s) + Na₂SO₄; the precipitate is magnesium carbonate.
- **Q:** Why is this called double replacement?
  **A:** The cations and anions of two compounds swap partners.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Predicting products
*Student:* Use the activity series to predict whether 12 reactions occur.

*How to teach:* Model how to use the activity series. The student predicts whether 12 reactions occur and writes products for those that do.

- **Q:** Will Al react with CuCl₂? If so, write the products.
  **A:** Yes; 2Al + 3CuCl₂ → 2AlCl₃ + 3Cu.
- **Q:** Will Pb react with ZnCl₂?
  **A:** No — lead is below zinc.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Quiz on reaction types.

*How to teach:* Give the quiz and grade together. Collect the lab notebook.

- **Q:** Classify: 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂
  **A:** Decomposition.

**Assessment:** Reaction lab notebook and quiz.

**Answer key:** Lab notebook: full credit for observations, balanced equations and reaction types for all three labs: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (decomposition; splint relights); Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (single replacement; copper coating, blue fades); MgSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → MgCO₃(s) + Na₂SO₄ (double replacement; white precipitate). Quiz key: 1) 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO — synthesis. 2) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ — decomposition. 3) Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂ — single replacement. 4) AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃ — double replacement. 5) CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O — combustion. 6) Does Cu + ZnSO₄ react? No (Cu below Zn). 7) Does Zn + CuSO₄ react? Yes → ZnSO₄ + Cu. 8) Does Ag + HCl react? No (Ag below H). 9) Does Mg + 2HCl react? Yes → MgCl₂ + H₂. 10) Does Fe + 2AgNO₃ react? Yes → Fe(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag.

## Modern World History: Aggression and appeasement

**Prepare:** Chamberlain's 'peace for our time' statement (September 30, 1938), Churchill's Commons speech on Munich (October 5, 1938), maps of 1930s aggression, readings on Nanjing (preview: mass killing and sexual violence), U.S. Neutrality Acts summary, Nazi-Soviet Pact.

**Watch for:**
- Judging appeasement only with hindsight. In 1938 many Britons feared another world war and believed Germany had legitimate grievances.
- Forgetting Asia. Japan's aggression began in Manchuria in 1931.

### Day 1 (Monday): Japan
*Student:* Map Japan's invasion of Manchuria and China and read accounts of Nanjing.

*How to teach:* On a map of East Asia, the student marks Manchuria (1931) and the invasion of China (1937). Read a short account of the Nanjing massacre (preview first) and discuss how the League of Nations' failure encouraged further aggression.

- **Q:** What happened at Nanjing (1937–38)?
  **A:** Japanese troops massacred tens of thousands to an estimated 200,000–300,000 Chinese civilians and prisoners and committed mass rapes.
- **Q:** How did the League of Nations respond to Manchuria?
  **A:** It condemned Japan, and Japan simply left the League — showing the League's weakness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Italy & Germany
*Student:* Map Ethiopia, Rhineland, Anschluss and Sudetenland.

*How to teach:* The student maps Ethiopia (1935), the Rhineland (1936), Anschluss (1938) and the Sudetenland (1938). Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Which treaty did Hitler violate by remilitarizing the Rhineland?
  **A:** The Treaty of Versailles (and Locarno).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Munich
*Student:* Compare Chamberlain's and Churchill's 1938 speeches on Munich.

*How to teach:* Source both speeches (Chamberlain returning from Munich, September 30, 1938; Churchill in the House of Commons, October 5, 1938). The student charts each speaker's claims and evidence and predicts which view events proved right.

- **Q:** What did Chamberlain claim after Munich?
  **A:** 'Peace for our time.'
- **Q:** How did Churchill describe Munich?
  **A:** 'A total and unmitigated defeat.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Isolationism
*Student:* Analyze U.S. Neutrality Acts and the Nazi-Soviet Pact.

*How to teach:* Analyze the U.S. Neutrality Acts and the Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 1939). Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** What did the Nazi-Soviet Pact secretly include?
  **A:** A plan to divide Poland and Eastern Europe between Germany and the USSR.

### Day 5 (Friday): Debate
*Student:* Debate: Could appeasement have been reasonable in 1938?

*How to teach:* Hold a 15-minute debate on whether appeasement could have been reasonable in 1938; the student argues one side with three pieces of evidence, then switches. End with the exit ticket in the assessment key.

- **Q:** What event ended appeasement?
  **A:** Germany's seizure of the rest of Czechoslovakia (March 1939) and invasion of Poland (September 1, 1939).

**Assessment:** Debate and exit ticket.

**Answer key:** Debate rubric (4-point): clear position; at least three pieces of evidence (e.g., British unpreparedness, public fear of war, Dominions' reluctance vs. Hitler's broken promises, Czechoslovakia's defenses lost, Churchill's warnings); rebuttal; civil delivery. Exit ticket answer: 'Appeasement was the policy of giving in to an aggressor's demands to avoid war; at Munich, Britain and France allowed Germany to annex the Sudetenland.'

## Spanish III: El presente perfecto

**Prepare:** Chart the present perfect: haber (he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han) + participle (-ado/-ido). List irregular participles: hecho, dicho, escrito, visto, puesto, roto, vuelto, abierto, muerto, cubierto, descubierto, resuelto. Note accent participles: leído, oído, creído, traído.

**Watch for:**
- Separating haber from the participle or putting pronouns between them (*He lo visto): pronouns go before haber — Lo he visto.
- Making the participle agree after haber (*Ella ha comida): with haber it never changes — Ella ha comido.
- Using tener as the helper (*Tengo comido): only haber forms the perfect tense.

### Day 1 (Monday): Formación
*Student:* Learn present perfect with regular participles.

*How to teach:* Teach the formation with regular participles. Your child writes 10 sentences about things done this week (Esta semana he limpiado...).

- **Q:** Translate: 'We have studied a lot this year.'
  **A:** Hemos estudiado mucho este año.
- **Q:** Give the participle of leer and of vivir.
  **A:** leído; vivido

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Irregulares
*Student:* Learn irregular participles (hecho, dicho, escrito, visto, puesto, roto, vuelto).

*How to teach:* Drill the irregular participles with flashcards and a quick oral game.

- **Q:** Give the participles of hacer, decir, escribir, ver, poner, romper, and volver.
  **A:** hecho, dicho, escrito, visto, puesto, roto, vuelto
- **Q:** Translate: 'Who has broken the window?'
  **A:** ¿Quién ha roto la ventana?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): ¿Alguna vez...?
*Student:* Interview someone using 'Alguna vez has...?' questions.

*How to teach:* Your child interviews you or a relative with 8 '¿Alguna vez has...?' questions and notes the answers in third person.

- **Q:** Ask: 'Have you ever eaten paella?'
  **A:** ¿Alguna vez has comido paella?
- **Q:** Report the answer: 'She has never seen the ocean.'
  **A:** Ella nunca ha visto el mar.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lista
*Student:* Write a bucket list of things you have and haven't done.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a bucket list: 8 things they have done (ya) and 8 they haven't done yet (todavía no).

- **Q:** Translate: 'I have already flown on a plane, but I haven't visited Spain yet.'
  **A:** Ya he volado en avión, pero todavía no he visitado España.

### Day 5 (Friday): Evaluación
*Student:* Quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the present-perfect quiz.

- **Q:** Correct: 'Mis padres han hacido la cena y yo he lo comido.'
  **A:** Mis padres han hecho la cena y yo la he comido.

**Assessment:** Quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz sample key: 1) hablar/yo — he hablado 2) comer/tú — has comido 3) hacer/ella — ha hecho 4) decir/nosotros — hemos dicho 5) escribir/ellos — han escrito 6) ver/ustedes — han visto 7) poner/yo — he puesto 8) romper/él — ha roto 9) volver/tú — has vuelto 10) abrir/nosotros — hemos abierto 11) 'Have you ever been to Mexico?' — ¿Alguna vez has estado en México? 12) 'I haven't finished yet' — Todavía no he terminado.

## PE 10 (Course 2): Soccer tactics

**Prepare:** Ball, cones, a field diagram sheet, and a professional goal clip.

**Watch for:**
- Students all chase the ball ('beehive soccer'). Attackers create width and depth; defenders keep shape.
- Students dive in on defense. The first defender delays and contains (pressure) while teammates cover and balance.
- Students think a through ball is any long kick. It's a pass into space behind the defense for a teammate running onto it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Attack principles
*Student:* Learn width, depth and penetration and practice a 3v1 rondo.

*How to teach:* Teach width, depth and penetration; play a 3v1 rondo.

- **Q:** Why do attackers spread wide?
  **A:** It stretches the defense, creating gaps to pass through.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Defending
*Student:* Learn pressure-cover-balance and practice 2v2 defending drills.

*How to teach:* Teach pressure–cover–balance; practice 2v2 defending.

- **Q:** What does the second defender (cover) do?
  **A:** Positions behind and to the side of the first defender to stop a dribble or pass through.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Match analysis
*Student:* Watch a professional goal sequence and diagram player movement on a field sheet.

*How to teach:* The student diagrams a professional goal sequence.

- **Q:** What's a give-and-go?
  **A:** A pass to a teammate followed by a run into space to receive the return pass.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game play
*Student:* Play small-sided games scoring bonus points for a give-and-go or through ball.

*How to teach:* Play small-sided games with bonus points for a give-and-go or through ball.

- **Q:** When is a through ball the right choice?
  **A:** When a teammate is making a run behind the defense and there's space to pass into.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Reflect on which team role (attacker, defender, connector) suits you and why.

*How to teach:* The student reflects on which team role suits them and why.

- **Q:** What does a 'connector' player do?
  **A:** Links defense and attack by receiving, keeping possession and distributing passes.

**Assessment:** Tactical diagram.

**Answer key:** Tactical diagram rubric: 4 = diagram of a goal sequence with player positions, movement arrows, passes (dashed) and dribbles (wavy), labels for width, depth and penetration, and a sentence explaining why the attack succeeded; plus a defensive diagram showing pressure, cover and balance. 3 = mostly accurate. 2 = incomplete labels. 1 = minimal.

## Driver Education (S1) & First Aid/CPR + Health II (S2): CPR and AED

**Prepare:** Get a CPR manikin (borrow or buy an inexpensive one) or use a firm cushion on the floor. Queue the American Heart Association Hands-Only CPR video and an AED trainer video. Have the 'Stayin' Alive' song (100–103 bpm) ready. Note: a home course does not certify the student; Week 23 covers certification.

**Watch for:**
- Students compress too gently or too slowly. Adults need compressions at least 2 inches (about 5 cm, but not more than 2.4 inches) deep at 100–120 per minute, allowing full chest recoil.
- Students fear hurting the person. Without CPR, a person in cardiac arrest will die; broken ribs are a possible but acceptable risk.
- Students think an AED could shock someone who doesn't need it. AEDs analyze the rhythm and only advise a shock when needed; just follow the prompts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chain of survival
*Student:* Learn the chain of survival.

*How to teach:* Explain the chain of survival; the student draws it and explains why each link matters (for every minute without CPR and defibrillation, survival falls).

- **Q:** Why is early CPR so important?
  **A:** It keeps oxygen-rich blood flowing to the brain and heart until a defibrillator and EMS arrive.
- **Q:** What's the first link in the out-of-hospital chain?
  **A:** Recognizing cardiac arrest and calling 911 (activating emergency response).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compressions
*Student:* Practice compressions at 100–120 per minute to 'Stayin' Alive' on a pillow or manikin.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate hand position and posture; the student does compressions on the manikin/cushion to 'Stayin' Alive' in 30-compression sets, counting aloud.

- **Q:** How deep and fast should adult compressions be?
  **A:** At least 2 inches (no more than about 2.4 inches) at 100–120 per minute.
- **Q:** Why allow the chest to fully rise between compressions?
  **A:** Full recoil lets the heart refill with blood.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Breaths
*Student:* Practice rescue breaths on a manikin.

*How to teach:* Demonstrate head-tilt/chin-lift and rescue breaths with a face shield on the manikin (1 second each, chest visibly rises). Practice 30:2 cycles.

- **Q:** How long should each rescue breath last?
  **A:** About 1 second, just enough to make the chest rise.
- **Q:** What if you're untrained or unwilling to give breaths?
  **A:** Do hands-only CPR: continuous compressions until help or an AED arrives.

### Day 4 (Thursday): AED
*Student:* Learn AED steps with a trainer video.

*How to teach:* Watch the AED trainer video and walk through the steps on paper: turn on, follow prompts, expose and dry chest, place pads, stand clear for analysis, shock if advised, resume CPR immediately.

- **Q:** What's the first thing to do when the AED arrives?
  **A:** Turn it on and follow its voice prompts.
- **Q:** What should everyone do while the AED analyzes or shocks?
  **A:** Stand clear — no one touches the person.

### Day 5 (Friday): Skills check
*Student:* Demonstrate CPR for 2 minutes.

*How to teach:* The student demonstrates 2 minutes of CPR from scene check through compressions/breaths; score with the checklist.

- **Q:** How many compressions and breaths are in one cycle?
  **A:** 30 compressions and 2 breaths.

**Assessment:** Skills check.

**Answer key:** Skills check (2 minutes): Proficient = checks scene, checks responsiveness and breathing (≤10 s), calls/sends someone for 911 and an AED, hand placement on the center of the chest (lower half of sternum), arms straight and shoulders over hands, depth about 2 inches, rate 100–120/min (count about 30 compressions in 15–18 seconds), full recoil, minimal interruptions, 30:2 with breaths if trained (or hands-only). Developing = 1–2 errors corrected with cues. Beginning = 3+ errors. Chain of survival (adult out-of-hospital): recognize cardiac arrest and activate EMS, early high-quality CPR, rapid defibrillation, advanced resuscitation by EMS, post-cardiac-arrest care, and recovery.

## Computer Science Principles: Libraries and APIs

**Prepare:** Read Code.org's App Lab documentation on libraries (Share → Library) and find a Code.org example library. Look up the p5.js license (LGPL 2.1) on its GitHub page.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a library is 'just copied code'. A library is reusable, documented code with a defined interface (an API) — you use it through its functions without reading the internals.
- Ignoring license terms of libraries. Licenses can require attribution, sharing changes, or forbid certain uses.
- Writing documentation that repeats the function name. Good documentation states purpose, parameters, return value and an example.

### Day 1 (Monday): Libraries
*Student:* Read App Lab library documentation and use a library function.

*How to teach:* Read App Lab documentation for a function (e.g. randomNumber or a library function) and use it. Point out each doc lists parameters, return value and examples.

- **Q:** What is an API?
  **A:** An Application Program Interface: the description of how to use a library's procedures — their names, parameters and return values.
- **Q:** Why do programmers use libraries?
  **A:** To reuse tested code for common tasks so they can build programs faster and more reliably.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Create a library
*Student:* Write your own library of three functions with documentation.

*How to teach:* Have the student write three related functions (e.g. a math or text library: average(list), maxOf(list), capitalize(word)) with documentation comments.

- **Q:** Write a documentation comment for average(list).
  **A:** // average: returns the mean of a list of numbers. Parameter list: a list of numbers. Returns: a number. Example: average([2,4,6]) returns 4.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Share
*Student:* Share your library and use a partner's or a Code.org example library.

*How to teach:* Publish the library in App Lab and import a partner's or a Code.org example library into a new project; use at least one function from it.

- **Q:** When using someone else's library, what do you need to know?
  **A:** What each function does, its parameters, its return value — from the documentation — and the license terms.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Licensing
*Student:* Research the license of a popular open-source library (e.g., p5.js) and explain usage limits.

*How to teach:* Look up the p5.js license (GNU LGPL 2.1). Have the student explain in their own words what they can and can't do.

- **Q:** Can you use p5.js in a program you share publicly?
  **A:** Yes — the LGPL allows use in your projects; if you modify the p5.js library itself and distribute it, you must share those changes under the LGPL and keep its license notices.

### Day 5 (Friday): Check
*Student:* Write a documentation comment (purpose, parameters, return value, example) for each function in your library and take a short quiz.

*How to teach:* The student finalizes documentation for each function, then takes the short quiz in the assessment key.

- **Q:** Name the four parts of a good documentation comment used this week.
  **A:** Purpose, parameters, return value, example.

**Assessment:** Library project and quiz.

**Answer key:** Library project rubric (4 levels): 4 = three working, general functions with parameters and return values, each with a complete documentation comment (purpose, parameters with types, return value, example), library shared and a partner/example library successfully used; 3 = all works, docs missing one element; 2 = functions work but are not documented or not general; 1 = incomplete. Quiz: 1) API = Application Program Interface, the specification of how to use a library's procedures. 2) Library = collection of procedures for use in programs. 3) Documentation explains how to use a procedure without reading its code. 4) p5.js is licensed under the GNU LGPL 2.1, which allows use in your own projects, but changes to the library itself must be shared under the LGPL. 5) Benefit of libraries: save time, use tested code.
