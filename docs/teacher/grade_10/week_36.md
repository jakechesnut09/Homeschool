# Teacher's Guide — 10th Grade, Week 36

## English 10: Final exam and presentation

**Prepare:** Review notes; a CAASPP-style practice performance task (search 'CAASPP ELA practice test grade 11' — the closest released high school test — at caaspp.org). Prepare the final exam.

**Watch for:**
- Spending too long on one part of the exam. Teach time budgeting.
- Reading only the first part of a prompt.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review
*Student:* Review key texts and terms with a self-made study guide.

*How to teach:* The student builds a one-page study guide: each major text with its themes, key quotes and terms. Quiz orally on the guide. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** Name three texts studied this year and their main themes.
  **A:** Antigone (divine vs. human law); Things Fall Apart (tradition vs. change); Fahrenheit 451 (censorship and thought).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice exam
*Student:* Complete a CAASPP-style practice performance task.

*How to teach:* Give a CAASPP-style practice performance task (read sources, answer questions, write an essay). Review the scoring and discuss time management. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What is a performance task?
  **A:** A multi-source research and writing task.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the final: cold reads (literary and informational), grammar, and a timed argument essay.

*How to teach:* Give the final exam (about 2 hours, can split): cold reads, grammar and a timed argument essay. Grade with the assessment key. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What is a strong argument essay structure?
  **A:** Introduction with claim, body paragraphs with evidence, counterclaim and rebuttal, conclusion.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Portfolio presentation
*Student:* Present the portfolio in a 6-minute talk with slides.

*How to teach:* The student presents the portfolio in a 6-minute talk with slides. Score with the rubric and celebrate the year's growth. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What should the presentation emphasize?
  **A:** Growth over the year with specific examples.

### Day 5 (Friday): Summer reading plan
*Student:* Create a summer reading list of five books and set reading goals.

*How to teach:* Help the student choose five books for summer (mix of genres, one classic, one contemporary). They set specific reading goals with dates. Close by asking the question(s) below; the student answers aloud and points to the exact line in the text (or their own draft) that supports the answer.

- **Q:** What makes a good reading goal?
  **A:** Specific, measurable, and realistic.

**Assessment:** Final exam and portfolio presentation.

**Answer key:** Final exam (sample answers): Literary cold read — identify theme, a device and its effect, with quoted evidence (2 points each). Informational cold read — central idea, author's purpose, rhetorical appeal. Grammar — fix comma splices, parallelism, semicolon use. Argument essay rubric (4-point): claim, evidence, counterclaim, organization, conventions. Portfolio presentation rubric: 6-minute talk, slides, specific growth examples, clear delivery (4-point).

## Geometry: Final exam and reflection

**Prepare:** Prepare the two-part final exam (assessment key). Have the student's error log from the practice final.

**Watch for:**
- Cramming new methods the day before the exam. Focus on the error log topics.
- Not checking answers for reasonableness (e.g., a leg longer than the hypotenuse).

### Day 1 (Monday): Targeted review
*Student:* Review topics missed on the practice final.

*How to teach:* The student reworks the problem types missed on the practice final, explaining each aloud.

- **Q:** What is the most common mistake you made on the practice final, and how will you prevent it?
  **A:** Answers vary; a good answer names a specific error (e.g., degree/radian mode) and a check (e.g., test sin 30 = 0.5).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final exam part 1
*Student:* Take Part 1 of the final (transformations through similarity).

*How to teach:* Give Part 1 of the final (transformations through similarity) — about 60 minutes.

- **Q:** (After) Dilate (6, −3) by ⅓ about the origin.
  **A:** (2, −1).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam part 2
*Student:* Take Part 2 (trig, circles, volume, probability).

*How to teach:* Give Part 2 (trig, circles, volume, probability) — about 60 minutes.

- **Q:** (After) Find the length of a tangent from a point 17 units from the center of a circle with radius 8.
  **A:** √(289 − 64) = 15.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Analyze errors and correct them with explanations.

*How to teach:* Return the exam; the student corrects every error and writes the reason for each.

- **Q:** Why write a reason for each correction rather than just the right answer?
  **A:** Explaining the error builds understanding and helps avoid repeating it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on your favorite proof and how geometry is used in a career of interest.

*How to teach:* The student writes a one-page reflection on a favorite proof and a career that uses geometry (architect, surveyor, game designer, nurse calculating dosages in volumes, etc.).

- **Q:** Give one specific way a surveyor uses geometry.
  **A:** Using the Law of Sines/Cosines or trig to find distances and property boundaries that can't be measured directly.

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam (sample items with answers). Part 1: 1) Distance (−3, 4) to (3, −4) → 10. 2) Reflect (1, 5) over y = −x → (−5, −1). 3) Interior angle sum of a 9-gon → 1260°. 4) Proof: SAS congruence with a shared side. 5) Dilate (6, −3) by ⅓ → (2, −1). 6) Side splitter: 6/9 = 8/x → x = 12. Part 2: 7) sin⁻¹(0.5) → 30°. 8) Law of Cosines a = 5, b = 6, C = 50° → c ≈ 4.74. 9) Tangent from point 17 from center, radius 8 → 15. 10) Arc length r = 12, 150° → 10π ≈ 31.4. 11) Cylinder r = 3, h = 7 → 63π ≈ 197.9. 12) P(two kings without replacement) → 1/221. 13) 7P2 → 42. Grade cutoffs 90/80/70. Reflection rubric (Day 5): 3 = explains a specific proof's logic accurately and names a career with a concrete geometry use; 2 = general; 1 = minimal.

## Chemistry in the Earth System: Final exam and capstone presentation

**Prepare:** Arrange the capstone presentation with family. Build a cumulative final exam from the key (about 40 items, 90 minutes, periodic table and formula sheet allowed). Have the Week 1 candle questions and the driving-question board ready.

**Watch for:**
- Students memorize answers to past quizzes rather than practicing new problems. Use fresh numbers in review.
- Students skip units on the final. Deduct for missing units on calculations.
- Students write the final candle explanation as a list. It should be a connected explanation linking atoms, bonds, energy and the Earth system.

### Day 1 (Monday): Present capstone
*Student:* Present the capstone to family with Q&A.

*How to teach:* The student presents the capstone (10 minutes) and answers questions. Score with the capstone rubric and share one strength and one improvement.

- **Q:** Ask: What would you change if you had another month?
  **A:** A strong answer names a specific weakness shown by the data and a concrete redesign.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Review
*Student:* Review all units with practice problems.

*How to teach:* Mixed review covering all units: equations, stoichiometry, energy, bonding, equilibrium, pH, climate. Reteach anything missed.

- **Q:** How many grams of CO₂ form from 0.10 mol of NaHCO₃ reacting with excess vinegar?
  **A:** 0.10 mol × 44.01 = 4.4 g.
- **Q:** A reaction is endothermic. On an energy diagram, are the products higher or lower than the reactants?
  **A:** Higher.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final exam
*Student:* Take the final exam.

*How to teach:* Give the final exam in one 90-minute sitting.

- **Q:** (After the exam) Which unit felt strongest and which weakest?
  **A:** Answers vary; use them to guide Day 4 corrections.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Correct errors on the exam.

*How to teach:* Return the exam. The student corrects every missed item and writes the reason for each error (concept, careless, units).

- **Q:** Why categorize errors?
  **A:** It shows whether mistakes come from misunderstanding or carelessness, guiding what to practice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Revisit phenomenon
*Student:* Write a final explanation of the burning candle using all you learned this year.

*How to teach:* The student writes a final, connected explanation of the burning candle using ideas from every unit and compares it to the Week 1 questions.

- **Q:** In one sentence, where does the candle's mass go and where does its energy come from?
  **A:** The wax's carbon and hydrogen atoms combine with oxygen to form CO₂ and H₂O gases that enter the air (conserving mass), and the energy comes from forming stronger bonds in those products than were broken in the wax and oxygen.

**Assessment:** Capstone rubric and final exam.

**Answer key:** Capstone rubric: 4 = clear problem and criteria, accurate chemistry, data-driven evaluation, trade-offs, confident delivery, answers questions. Final exam key (sample core items): 1) Balance C₃H₈ + O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O with 5O₂. 2) 200 g water warmed 15 °C absorbs 12,550 J. 3) Neutrons in ¹⁴C — 8. 4) 40 g of an isotope with half-life 10 yr after 30 yr — 5 g. 5) Ionic formula of calcium chloride — CaCl₂. 6) Shape of CO₂ — linear, nonpolar. 7) Moles in 88 g CO₂ — 2.0. 8) Grams CO₂ from 8.4 g NaHCO₃ (0.10 mol) — 4.4 g. 9) Limiting reactant: 3.0 mol H₂ and 1.0 mol O₂ → O₂ limits, 2.0 mol H₂O. 10) ΔH for CH₄ combustion from bond energies — −802 kJ/mol. 11) Exothermic reaction, temperature raised — shifts left. 12) pH 3 vs. pH 6 — 1,000× more acidic. 13) Why CO₂ is a greenhouse gas — absorbs infrared. 14) Ocean acidification reduces carbonate ions. 15) Positive feedback example — ice-albedo. Final candle explanation rubric: 4 = connects wax hydrocarbon structure, combustion equation and conservation of mass, bond energies explaining the release of energy, CO₂'s role in the greenhouse effect and the carbon cycle, and ocean uptake.

## Modern World History: Final exam

**Prepare:** The student's notes and practice-final error list; prepare the two-part final exam (Part 1 multiple choice and map; Part 2 timed DBQ, e.g., 'What caused the end of European empires after 1945?').

**Watch for:**
- Cramming new material instead of fixing known weaknesses.
- Misreading the DBQ prompt — underline the task verbs and time period.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review
*Student:* Targeted review of weak topics.

*How to teach:* Using the practice-final error list, the student reviews the three weakest topics with flashcards and one practice question each. Quiz orally at the end. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Explain one topic you missed on the practice final in your own words.
  **A:** Answers vary; a good answer corrects the error with specific evidence (e.g., 'The Treaty of Versailles was signed in 1919, not 1918; the armistice was November 11, 1918').

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Final part 1
*Student:* Multiple-choice and map sections of the final.

*How to teach:* Give Part 1 of the final: multiple choice and map (about 60 minutes). Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Where was the Western Front in WWI?
  **A:** A line of trenches across Belgium and northeastern France from the North Sea to Switzerland.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Final part 2
*Student:* Timed DBQ essay on the final.

*How to teach:* Give Part 2: the timed DBQ (about 60 minutes with 10 minutes to plan). Score with the DBQ rubric. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** What four things must a strong DBQ include?
  **A:** A thesis, evidence from documents and outside knowledge, sourcing, and analysis that connects evidence to the thesis.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error analysis
*Student:* Correct errors.

*How to teach:* Return the exam. The student corrects every error and writes a one-sentence explanation for each. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Why write explanations for corrections?
  **A:** Explaining why an answer is right builds understanding and prevents repeating the mistake.

### Day 5 (Friday): Reflection
*Student:* Write a reflection on how studying history changed your view of current events.

*How to teach:* The student writes a one-page reflection on how studying history changed their view of current events, with two specific examples. Check understanding with the question(s) below; on document days the student must name the source (author, date, purpose) in the answer.

- **Q:** Give an example of how history explains a current event.
  **A:** For example, borders drawn at the Berlin Conference or by post-WWI mandates help explain some conflicts in Africa and the Middle East today.

**Assessment:** Final exam.

**Answer key:** Final exam key: Part 1 — multiple-choice and map items drawn from the course (sample: Montesquieu → separation of powers; Napoleonic Code → legal equality; Sadler Report → child labor; Adwa → Ethiopia defeats Italy 1896; Zimmermann Telegram → Germany-Mexico offer; Holodomor → Ukraine famine 1932–33; Munich → appeasement 1938; Stalingrad → Eastern Front turning point; Marshall Plan → European recovery; Nkrumah → Ghana 1957; glasnost → openness). Part 2 — DBQ rubric (4-point per row): Thesis — directly answers the question with a defensible claim; Evidence — uses at least 4 documents accurately plus outside knowledge; Sourcing — explains author, purpose, audience or context for at least 2 documents; Analysis — groups documents and explains how they support the claim; Writing — organized, clear. 17–20 = proficient. Reflection rubric: 3 = connects at least two historical topics to specific current events with insight; 2 = general connections; 1 = minimal.

## Spanish III: Examen final y entrevista oral

**Prepare:** Assemble the final review packet and exam: an authentic article and podcast clip with questions, a 300-word essay prompt (e.g., ¿Cómo ha cambiado tu identidad como estudiante de español?), and an oral interview question list. Help your child choose a summer book or series in Spanish (e.g., a graded reader or a show such as a Spanish-language series with subtitles).

**Watch for:**
- Using only present tense in the oral interview: push for past, future, conditional, and subjunctive with follow-up questions (¿Qué hiciste...? ¿Qué harías si...?).
- Rushing the essay without planning: outline thesis, three points, and conclusion first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Repaso
*Student:* Review all tenses and moods.

*How to teach:* Your child reviews all tenses and moods with a mixed set and corrects errors.

- **Q:** Give: preterite yo of hacer, imperfect yo of ver, present perfect yo of escribir, conditional yo of poder, imperfect subjunctive yo of ser.
  **A:** hice, veía, he escrito, podría, fuera

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lectura y audio
*Student:* Final interpretive section with an authentic article and podcast.

*How to teach:* Give the interpretive section: an authentic article and a podcast clip with questions.

- **Q:** What should you do first with an authentic text?
  **A:** Read the title, subtitles, and questions, predict the topic, then read for gist before details.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Escritura
*Student:* Final writing section: a 300-word essay.

*How to teach:* Give the writing section: a 300-word essay.

- **Q:** Translate a thesis: 'Learning Spanish has changed how I see the world.'
  **A:** Aprender español ha cambiado mi manera de ver el mundo.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Entrevista oral
*Student:* Final 10-minute oral proficiency interview.

*How to teach:* Hold a 10-minute oral proficiency interview: warm-up, questions in several tenses, a role-play, and a wind-down.

- **Q:** Answer: '¿Qué harías si pudieras vivir en otro país?'
  **A:** Example: Si pudiera vivir en otro país, viviría en Argentina porque me encantaría aprender a bailar tango.

### Day 5 (Friday): Metas
*Student:* Set Spanish IV goals and choose a summer book or series in Spanish.

*How to teach:* Your child sets Spanish IV goals and chooses a summer book or series in Spanish.

- **Q:** Translate: 'This summer I will read a book in Spanish.'
  **A:** Este verano leeré un libro en español.

**Assessment:** Final exam and oral interview.

**Answer key:** Final exam: interpretive items keyed to the chosen article and podcast (main idea, 3 details, author's purpose, vocabulary in context). Essay rubric (300 words): 4 = clear thesis, organized paragraphs with transitions (además, sin embargo, por lo tanto), accurate use of at least 4 tenses/moods; 3 = minor errors; 2 = weak organization or frequent errors; 1 = incomplete. Oral proficiency interview (10 minutes): 4 = Intermediate Mid — creates with language, connected sentences, asks and answers questions, handles past/future/hypothetical with some errors; 3 = Intermediate Low; 2 = Novice High; 1 = memorized phrases only.
