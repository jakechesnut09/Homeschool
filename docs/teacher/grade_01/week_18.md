# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 18

## ELA 1: Vowel teams ee, ea, and pronouns; poems about feelings

**Prepare:** Stevenson's 'Bed in Summer,' pronoun cards (he, she, it, they, his, her, their), word cards (look, peek, glance, stare; big, huge, giant), and an ee/ea list (tree, feet, sleep, eat, sea, leaf, seed, team).

**Watch for:**
- Using 'him' or 'them' as the subject ('Him ran'): he/she/they come before the verb.
- Thinking ee and ea make different sounds: both usually say long e.
- Thinking shades of meaning are synonyms that mean exactly the same: they're close but different in strength.

### Day 1 (Monday): ee and ea
*Student:* Read tree, feet, sleep, eat, sea, leaf; sort and read in phrases.

*How to teach:* Read tree, feet, sleep, eat, sea, leaf. Sort into ee and ea and read in phrases ('a green leaf,' 'sleep in a tree').

- **Q:** What sound do ee and ea make?
  **A:** Long e /ē/.
- **Q:** Read: sleep, leaf.
  **A:** sleep, leaf.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pronouns
*Student:* Replace nouns with he, she, it, they in 6 sentences; then use his, her, their.

*How to teach:* Replace nouns with he, she, it, they in 6 sentences. Then use his, her, their.

- **Q:** Replace the noun: 'Mom reads.'
  **A:** She reads.
- **Q:** Replace: 'The kids play.'
  **A:** They play.
- **Q:** 'That is Sam's hat.'
  **A:** That is his hat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bed in Summer
*Student:* Read Stevenson's 'Bed in Summer'; talk about how the child feels and which words show it.

*How to teach:* Read 'Bed in Summer.' Discuss how the child feels and which words show it.

- **Q:** What is the child's complaint in summer?
  **A:** He has to go to bed while it is still daylight.
- **Q:** What can he still see and hear from bed?
  **A:** The birds hopping on the tree and grown-up people's feet going past in the street.
- **Q:** How does the child feel?
  **A:** It seems unfair/hard; he wants to play ('I should like so much to play').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shades of meaning
*Student:* Line up look, peek, glance, stare (and big, huge, giant) from weakest to strongest and act them out.

*How to teach:* Line up look, peek, glance, stare (and big, huge, giant) from weakest to strongest and act them out.

- **Q:** Order big, huge, giant from smallest to biggest.
  **A:** big, huge, giant (huge and giant are both very big; giant is often strongest).
- **Q:** What's the difference between peek and stare?
  **A:** Peek is a quick, secret look; stare is a long look.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* ee/ea dictation; pronoun worksheet; reread 'Bed in Summer' fluently.

*How to teach:* Dictate ee/ea words, complete a pronoun worksheet, and reread 'Bed in Summer' fluently.

- **Q:** Spell feet.
  **A:** f-e-e-t.
- **Q:** Spell eat.
  **A:** e-a-t.

**Assessment:** Pronoun substitution sentences and ee/ea dictation.

**Answer key:** Pronoun sentences (suggested): 1) Ben runs → He runs. 2) Lily sings → She sings. 3) The dog barks → It barks. 4) Mom and Dad cook → They cook. 5) Ana's bike → her bike. 6) The kids' ball → their ball. ee/ea dictation: tree, feet, sleep, eat, sea, leaf, seed, team — proficient 7–8.

## Math 1: Counting to 120

**Prepare:** A 120 chart (print one), dry-erase sleeve and marker, scissors to cut chart puzzle pieces, and a cup of 50–120 pieces of dry pasta.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'one hundred ten, one hundred eleven' after 109 incorrectly (e.g., 'two hundred'): practice 100–120 reading the chart: 'one hundred one... one hundred nine, one hundred ten.'
- Writing 1001 for one hundred one: show that 101 has 3 digits: 1 hundred, 0 tens, 1 one.
- Confusing teen and -ty numbers (14 vs 40): emphasize the ending: four-TEEN vs FOR-ty.

### Day 1 (Monday): Count by ones
*Student:* Count aloud from 47 to 120 and point on the 120 chart.

*How to teach:* Point to 47 and count aloud by ones to 120 together, pointing to each square. Pay special attention to crossing decades (59→60, 99→100, 109→110).

- **Q:** What comes after 89? After 109?
  **A:** 90; 110.
- **Q:** Count from 97 to 103.
  **A:** 97, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Patterns
*Student:* Color columns and rows on the 120 chart; describe what changes and what stays the same.

*How to teach:* Color the 5 column (5, 15, 25...) and the 30 row. Ask: going down a column, what changes? (tens digit goes up 1). Across a row? (ones digit goes up 1).

- **Q:** What is just below 34 on the chart?
  **A:** 44 — 10 more.
- **Q:** What pattern do numbers in the 7 column share?
  **A:** They all end in 7 (7, 17, 27 ... 117).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Missing numbers
*Student:* Fill in missing numbers on chart pieces (puzzle cut-outs).

*How to teach:* Cut a chart into small puzzle pieces with only one number showing. Your child fills in the missing numbers using the left/right = 1, up/down = 10 rules.

- **Q:** A piece shows 56. What is right of it? Below it?
  **A:** 57; 66.
- **Q:** What number is above 72?
  **A:** 62.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Count large collections
*Student:* Count 50–120 dry pasta pieces by grouping into tens.

*How to teach:* Your child counts a pile of 50–120 pasta pieces by making groups of 10, then counts by tens and ones (e.g., 8 tens and 3 → 83).

- **Q:** 9 groups of ten and 4 extra. How many?
  **A:** 94.
- **Q:** 11 groups of ten and 2 extra.
  **A:** 112.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write numbers 1–120 from a starting point; fill-in chart pieces.

*How to teach:* Your child writes numbers from a starting point (e.g., from 88 to 120) and fills in 3 chart pieces.

- **Q:** Write the numbers from 98 to 104.
  **A:** 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104.
- **Q:** What comes between 114 and 116?
  **A:** 115.

**Assessment:** 120 chart puzzle and counting check.

**Answer key:** Counting check: counts aloud from 47 to 120 with correct decade changes. Chart puzzle (suggested pieces): center 45 → left 44, right 46, above 35, below 55; center 78 → 77, 79, 68, 88; center 103 → 102, 104, 93, 113. Writing: from 88 to 120 every number in order with no reversals. Proficient: all decade transitions correct and 11–12 of 12 puzzle cells correct.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Stars at night

**Prepare:** A flashlight, black construction paper, a pushpin, paper cups, a star map showing the Big Dipper (or a free stargazing app), and a clear night. Note: from California in winter evenings, the Big Dipper is low in the northeast/north and rises higher later in the night.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking stars disappear in the daytime: they're still there; sunlight is so bright it hides them.
- Thinking stars are tiny: stars are huge suns very far away.
- Thinking the Big Dipper is the only constellation: it's one of many patterns (part of the Great Bear).

### Day 1 (Monday): Stargazing count
*Student:* On a clear night, look at the sky for 5 minutes, count visible stars in one window pane's view, and record the time.

*How to teach:* On a clear night, look for 5 minutes and count stars in one window pane's view; record the time.

- **Q:** Why look at one window pane?
  **A:** To count a small, fair section.
- **Q:** Why see more stars away from city lights?
  **A:** Less light pollution.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Why not in daytime?
*Student:* Shine a flashlight on the wall in a dark room, then in a bright sunny room; explain how sunlight hides the stars.

*How to teach:* Shine a flashlight on a wall in a dark room, then in a bright room.

- **Q:** Why is the flashlight hard to see in a bright room?
  **A:** The bright light outshines it.
- **Q:** How is that like stars in the day?
  **A:** Sunlight is so bright it hides the stars.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Constellation viewer
*Student:* Poke the Big Dipper pattern into the bottom of a paper cup and shine a flashlight through it onto the ceiling.

*How to teach:* Poke the Big Dipper pattern (7 stars) into a paper cup's bottom; shine a flashlight through onto the ceiling.

- **Q:** How many stars are in the Big Dipper?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** What shape does it make?
  **A:** A dipper or ladle — a bowl with a handle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Find the Big Dipper
*Student:* Use your cup map to search for the Big Dipper in the northern sky; draw what you find.

*How to teach:* Search for the Big Dipper in the northern sky; draw it.

- **Q:** Which direction do you look?
  **A:** North.
- **Q:** Is it always in the same place in the sky?
  **A:** No — it moves around the North Star during the night and the year.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Answer: 'Are stars still there during the day? How do you know?' using your flashlight evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child answers: 'Are stars still there during the day?'

- **Q:** Are stars there during the day?
  **A:** Yes — sunlight is too bright to see them.
- **Q:** What is your evidence?
  **A:** The flashlight was still on in the bright room but hard to see.

**Assessment:** Star count record and evidence-based answer.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = star count recorded with date/time and answer 'Yes, the stars are still there; the sun's light is too bright, like my flashlight in a bright room'; 3 = answer correct, evidence partial; 2 = answer without evidence; 1 = incorrect.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Schools long ago and today

**Prepare:** Calisphere/LOC photos of one-room schoolhouses, a small chalkboard or slate, chalk, a picture book about school long ago, and a then-and-now chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking children long ago didn't go to school: many did, often in one-room schools with all ages together.
- Thinking schools long ago had computers or lots of paper: they used slates, chalk, and a few books.
- Thinking everything about school changed: some things stayed the same (reading, writing, math, recess).

### Day 1 (Monday): Photo detective
*Student:* Study a Library of Congress photo of a one-room schoolhouse: what do you see, think, wonder?

*How to teach:* Study a one-room schoolhouse photo: see, think, wonder.

- **Q:** What do you see?
  **A:** One room, children of many ages, rows of desks, a teacher, a stove, a chalkboard.
- **Q:** Why were all ages in one room?
  **A:** Small towns had few children and one teacher.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slate lesson
*Student:* Do today's spelling on a slate like children long ago; discuss what it would be like with no paper.

*How to teach:* Do spelling on a slate; discuss life without paper.

- **Q:** Why use a slate?
  **A:** Paper was expensive; slates could be wiped and reused.
- **Q:** What's hard about a slate?
  **A:** You can't keep your work.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a then-and-now chart (desks, lunch, subjects, getting to school).

*How to teach:* Make a then-and-now chart.

- **Q:** How did children get to school long ago?
  **A:** Walked or rode horses.
- **Q:** What was lunch then?
  **A:** Food from home in a tin pail.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read a picture book about school long ago (e.g., 'Going to School in 1876' or similar from the library).

*How to teach:* Read a picture book about school long ago.

- **Q:** Name a detail from the book.
  **A:** Answers vary; check the book.
- **Q:** What stayed the same?
  **A:** Learning to read, write, and do math.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw yourself at school then and now with 3 labeled differences.

*How to teach:* Your child draws themselves at school then and now with 3 labeled differences.

- **Q:** Label one difference.
  **A:** Slate then / tablet or paper now.
- **Q:** Another?
  **A:** One room for all ages then / grades now.

**Assessment:** Then-and-now school drawing.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = two drawings with 3 accurate labeled differences (slate vs. paper/computer, one-room vs. many grades, walking vs. bus/car, wood stove vs. heaters, lunch pail vs. lunchbox); 3 = 2 differences; 2 = 1 difference; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Form vs. shape; instrument families

**Prepare:** Paper strips, tape; access to a Classics for Kids or PBS instrument-family video; instrument pictures.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a drawing of a ball is a form: drawings are flat shapes; forms are 3-D.
- Putting the saxophone in brass: it's metal but a woodwind (uses a reed).

### Day 1 (Monday): Form hunt
*Student:* Compare a circle drawing with a ball; find 5 forms at home.

*How to teach:* Compare a drawn circle with a real ball; find 5 forms (box, can, ball, cone).

- **Q:** What is the difference between shape and form?
  **A:** A shape is flat (2-D); a form is 3-D and you can hold or walk around it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paper sculpture
*Student:* Fold, curl and tape paper strips into a standing sculpture.

*How to teach:* Fold, curl and tape paper strips into a standing sculpture.

- **Q:** Is your sculpture a shape or a form?
  **A:** A form (3-D).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Instrument families
*Student:* Watch a PBS or Classics for Kids video on string, brass, woodwind and percussion families.

*How to teach:* Watch a video about strings, brass, woodwinds and percussion.

- **Q:** How do you play percussion instruments?
  **A:** Hit, shake or scrape them.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sorting
*Student:* Sort instrument pictures into families.

*How to teach:* Sort instrument pictures into families.

- **Q:** What family is a trumpet?
  **A:** Brass.
- **Q:** What family is a violin?
  **A:** Strings.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Present paper sculpture and name 4 instrument families.

*How to teach:* Present sculpture; name the 4 families.

- **Q:** Name the four instrument families.
  **A:** Strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion.

**Assessment:** Instrument family sort.

**Answer key:** Instrument sort: Strings - violin, viola, cello, double bass, harp; Woodwinds - flute, clarinet, oboe, bassoon (saxophone); Brass - trumpet, French horn, trombone, tuba; Percussion - drums, xylophone, cymbals, triangle, timpani. Proficient = 10+ correct and names all four families.

## PE 1: Catching

**Prepare:** A scarf, a beach ball, and a playground ball.

**Watch for:**
- Trapping the ball against the chest: catch with hands only, fingers spread.
- Closing eyes or turning away: watch the ball all the way into the hands.

### Day 1 (Monday): Scarf catch
*Student:* Toss and catch a scarf with two hands.

*How to teach:* Toss a scarf up and catch it with two hands. Then clap once before catching.

- **Q:** What do your eyes do when you catch?
  **A:** Watch the object all the way into your hands.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Beach ball catch
*Student:* Catch a beach ball tossed by a partner.

*How to teach:* Toss the beach ball gently from 5 feet. Remind 'ready hands.'

- **Q:** What does 'ready hands' look like?
  **A:** Hands up and out in front, fingers spread.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bounce catch
*Student:* Catch a ball after one bounce.

*How to teach:* Bounce the ball once to your child, who catches it after the bounce.

- **Q:** If the ball is low, which way do your pinkies point?
  **A:** Pinkies together, fingers down (thumbs together for high balls).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Catch challenge
*Student:* Count how many catches in a row you and a partner can make from 6 feet; try to beat your record 3 times.

*How to teach:* Count catches in a row from 6 feet; try 3 times to beat your record.

- **Q:** What was your best number in a row?
  **A:** Record the child's number.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Catch at least 5 of 10 gentle tosses with hands only (no trapping against the body).

*How to teach:* Do 10 gentle tosses; count catches made with hands only.

- **Q:** Should you catch against your chest?
  **A:** No, catch with your hands.

**Assessment:** Catching count.

**Answer key:** Catching count: catches out of 10 gentle tosses with hands only. 5+ = proficient (meets goal); 3-4 = developing; 0-2 = beginning. Check: ready hands, eyes on ball, gives with the catch.

## Health 1: Screen time and active play

**Prepare:** Activity log, a timer, and a list of active swaps.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all screen time is bad: balance matters; choose active play often.
- Not counting all screens: TV, tablets, phones, and video games all count.
- Thinking activity must be outside: indoor dancing and stretching count.

### Day 1 (Monday): Screen time count
*Student:* Count screen minutes for a day.

*How to teach:* Count screen minutes for a day.

- **Q:** Which screens count?
  **A:** TV, tablets, phones, computers, video games.
- **Q:** If you watched 30 minutes of TV and played 20 minutes of games, total?
  **A:** 50 minutes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Active swaps
*Student:* Make a list of active swaps for screen time.

*How to teach:* Make a list of active swaps.

- **Q:** Name an active swap.
  **A:** Dance party, bike ride, jump rope, build a fort.
- **Q:** When can you use a swap?
  **A:** When you feel bored or want a screen.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Outdoor play
*Student:* Play outside for 30 minutes with a family member.

*How to teach:* Play outside 30 minutes with a family member.

- **Q:** What did you play?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** How did you feel after?
  **A:** Answers vary — often happy, tired.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family challenge
*Student:* Do a family walk after dinner.

*How to teach:* Take a family walk after dinner.

- **Q:** Why walk after dinner?
  **A:** Helps digestion and is family time.
- **Q:** How many minutes?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare active and screen minutes.

*How to teach:* Compare active and screen minutes.

- **Q:** Which was more?
  **A:** Answers vary — aim for more active.
- **Q:** How can you balance better?
  **A:** Use active swaps.

**Assessment:** Activity vs. screen comparison.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = log records screen and active minutes for at least 3 days, compares them correctly, and names 2 active swaps; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = partial; 1 = missing.
