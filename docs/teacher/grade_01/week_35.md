# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 35

## ELA 1: Author study: Arnold Lobel and Cynthia Rylant

**Prepare:** 'Owl at Home' and 'Mouse Soup' by Arnold Lobel, and a 'Poppleton' book by Cynthia Rylant (library), word-category cards.

**Watch for:**
- Defining a word by an example only ('An owl is like Owl'): use category + attribute: 'An owl is a bird that hunts at night.'
- Comparing characters by looks only: compare what they do and how they feel.
- Reading a chapter too fast: read with expression, pausing at periods.

### Day 1 (Monday): Owl at Home
*Student:* Read 'Tear-Water Tea'; tell how Owl feels and why.

*How to teach:* Read 'Tear-Water Tea.' Your child tells how Owl feels and why.

- **Q:** How does Owl make tea?
  **A:** He thinks of sad things, cries tears into the kettle, and boils them.
- **Q:** Name one sad thing Owl thinks about.
  **A:** Examples: chairs with broken legs, songs that cannot be sung because the words are forgotten, spoons fallen behind the stove, pencils too short to use.
- **Q:** How does Owl feel at the end?
  **A:** Happy/content — the tea tastes a little salty but good.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mouse Soup
*Student:* Read the story frame; tell how Mouse tricks the weasel.

*How to teach:* Read the story frame of 'Mouse Soup.' Your child tells how Mouse tricks the weasel.

- **Q:** What does Mouse tell the weasel?
  **A:** That the soup will taste better with stories.
- **Q:** How does Mouse escape?
  **A:** He tells the weasel to go get the things from the stories (like a bees' nest, mud, stones, crickets, thorns) to add to the soup, and while the weasel is gone, Mouse runs home.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poppleton
*Student:* Read a Poppleton story independently; record one funny part.

*How to teach:* Your child reads a Poppleton story independently and records one funny part.

- **Q:** Who is Poppleton?
  **A:** A pig who lives in a small town, friends with Cherry Sue (a llama) and others.
- **Q:** Tell a funny part.
  **A:** Answers vary — check it matches the story read.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Word categories
*Student:* Sort words from the books into categories (animals, feelings, foods) and define 3 by category and attribute ('An owl is a bird that...').

*How to teach:* Sort words from the books into categories and define 3 by category and attribute.

- **Q:** Define owl.
  **A:** An owl is a bird that hunts at night.
- **Q:** Define weasel.
  **A:** A weasel is an animal that has a long body and eats small animals.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Compare two Lobel characters; read a chapter aloud for fluency.

*How to teach:* Compare two Lobel characters, then read a chapter aloud.

- **Q:** How are Owl and Mouse different?
  **A:** Owl is alone and a little silly at home; Mouse is clever and tricks a weasel.
- **Q:** How are Frog and Toad alike to Owl?
  **A:** Example: all are animal characters in short, funny Lobel stories.

**Assessment:** Author comparison and fluency read.

**Answer key:** Comparison: proficient = names one likeness and one difference with story evidence (e.g., Owl is emotional and stays home making tea; Mouse is clever and escapes from the weasel; both are animal characters written by Arnold Lobel). Fluency read: record WCPM and note expression/phrasing; proficient = smooth, mostly accurate, expression at punctuation.

## Math 1: Review: operations and place value

**Prepare:** Flash cards within 20, base-ten blocks, symbol cards, 6 story problems (see day 3), favorite games (cards, dice), and a 20-item cumulative review.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting earlier strategies (make ten, doubles) under pressure: post a strategy reminder chart.
- Mixing up 10 more and 1 more on place value problems: build with blocks to check.
- Reading compare stories as add problems: ask 'Are we finding a total or a difference?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Fact fluency
*Student:* Final fluency check within 10 and practice within 20.

*How to teach:* Give the final within-10 fluency check (30 facts in 2 minutes) and practice within 20 with flash cards. Note which strategies your child uses.

- **Q:** 8 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 15.
- **Q:** 16 − 9 = ?
  **A:** 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Place value review
*Student:* Build 5 two-digit numbers with blocks, find 10 more and 10 less for each, and compare pairs with >, <, =.

*How to teach:* Your child builds 5 two-digit numbers, finds 10 more/10 less for each, and compares pairs with >, <, =.

- **Q:** Build 48. 10 more? 10 less?
  **A:** 58; 38.
- **Q:** Compare 48 and 84.
  **A:** 48 < 84.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story problems
*Student:* Solve 6 mixed story problems (add to, take from, compare, three addends) and write an equation for each.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 6 mixed story problems and writes an equation for each: add to, take from, compare, three addends.

- **Q:** Mia had 12 shells and found 5 more. How many?
  **A:** 17.
- **Q:** Raj has 15 cards; Sam has 9. How many more does Raj have?
  **A:** 6.
- **Q:** 3 + 7 + 4 = ?
  **A:** 14.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Game day
*Student:* Play favorite math games from the year.

*How to teach:* Play favorite math games from the year (Make Ten Go Fish, War, Race to 100). Ask your child to say each fact aloud.

- **Q:** In Go Fish for 10, you hold a 4. What do you ask for?
  **A:** A 6.
- **Q:** Roll 6 and 6. Double?
  **A:** 12.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Cumulative review part 1: facts within 20, equal sign, and place value (20 items).

*How to teach:* Give the 20-item cumulative review part 1.

- **Q:** Is 9 + 3 = 6 + 6 true?
  **A:** True (12 = 12).
- **Q:** What is 7 tens 5 ones?
  **A:** 75.

**Assessment:** Cumulative review part 1.

**Answer key:** Cumulative part 1 (suggested): 1) 6+4=10 2) 9+8=17 3) 7+7=14 4) 5+8=13 5) 13−5=8 6) 15−9=6 7) 10−6=4 8) 18−9=9 9) T/F 5+5=9+1 → T 10) T/F 8=4+3 → F 11) 7+?=12 → 5 12) ?−4=9 → 13 13) 62 = 6 tens 2 ones 14) 80+3=83 15) 10 more than 57 = 67 16) 10 less than 100 = 90 17) 39 < 93 18) 71 > 17 19) 55 = 55 20) Count on from 117: 118, 119, 120. Proficient 18–20.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Engineering showcase project

**Prepare:** Cups, string, flashlight, cardboard, tape, foil, and a simple data table (trial, what happened).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a too-big project: pick something you can build in a day.
- Testing once: test 3 times.
- Changing many things after a test: change one feature.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a problem
*Student:* Choose a challenge: send a message across the yard using light or sound, or solve a human problem by copying an animal part.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses a challenge.

- **Q:** Name a challenge.
  **A:** Send a message across the yard with light or sound, or copy an animal part to solve a problem.
- **Q:** What is your problem statement?
  **A:** Example: I need to send a message to someone across the yard.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sketch and build
*Student:* Sketch two ideas, choose one and build a first prototype.

*How to teach:* Sketch two ideas, choose one, build.

- **Q:** Why two ideas?
  **A:** To compare.
- **Q:** Which did you choose and why?
  **A:** Answers vary — needs a reason.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test
*Student:* Test the prototype 3 times and record what happened in a simple data table.

*How to teach:* Test 3 times; record.

- **Q:** What do you record?
  **A:** What happened each trial.
- **Q:** Why 3 trials?
  **A:** To make sure results are reliable.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Change one feature based on your data and test again.

*How to teach:* Change one feature based on data; test again.

- **Q:** What did you change?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** Did it improve?
  **A:** Compare to earlier data.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Get feedback from a family member using 'one thing that works, one thing to try'.

*How to teach:* Get feedback: 'one thing that works, one thing to try.'

- **Q:** What feedback did you get?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** How will you use it?
  **A:** Answers vary.

**Assessment:** Prototype and test data.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = problem stated, 2 sketches, prototype built, 3 recorded trials, one improvement tested with results; 3 = missing one part; 2 = missing two; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Work at home and in the community

**Prepare:** A family chore chart template, thank-you card supplies, and interview questions for a community helper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking work only counts if it's paid: home work like cooking, cleaning, and caring for family is important unpaid work.
- Thinking community helpers are only police and firefighters: librarians, mail carriers, sanitation workers, nurses too.
- Thinking chores are only for adults: everyone can help.

### Day 1 (Monday): Home work
*Student:* List all the work family members do at home (cooking, cleaning, caring for children) and how it helps.

*How to teach:* List work family members do at home and how it helps.

- **Q:** Name home work.
  **A:** Cooking, cleaning, laundry, caring for children.
- **Q:** How does cooking help?
  **A:** Keeps the family fed and healthy.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Community helpers
*Student:* Interview a community helper (librarian, firefighter, mail carrier).

*How to teach:* Interview a community helper.

- **Q:** What question could you ask?
  **A:** What do you do every day? How do you help our community?
- **Q:** Why do we need mail carriers?
  **A:** To deliver letters and packages.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chore chart
*Student:* Make a family chore chart and take on a job for the week.

*How to teach:* Make a family chore chart; take on a job.

- **Q:** Name a chore you can do.
  **A:** Feed the pet, set the table, put away toys.
- **Q:** Why have a chart?
  **A:** So everyone knows their job.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Thank-you
*Student:* Write a thank-you card to a community or home worker.

*How to teach:* Write a thank-you card to a worker.

- **Q:** What goes in a thank-you card?
  **A:** Greeting, thanks for something specific, closing, your name.
- **Q:** Who will receive yours?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why work at home is important even though it is unpaid.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why unpaid home work is important.

- **Q:** Why is home work important?
  **A:** Families need meals, clean clothes, and care to be healthy and happy.
- **Q:** Who does unpaid work?
  **A:** Parents, kids, grandparents — everyone at home.

**Assessment:** Chore chart and interview notes.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = chore chart with jobs for each family member, interview notes with 3 answers, and explanation that home work matters because it keeps the family fed, clean, and cared for even though no one is paid; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = partial; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 1: Field day preparation

**Prepare:** Cones, pillowcases, spoons and plastic eggs, baton (paper towel tube), poster paper.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing the egg and spoon: steady, careful walking is faster than dropping.
- Running at the handoff: the receiver gets ready and grabs the baton smoothly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan events
*Student:* Choose 5 field-day events and draw a poster for each.

*How to teach:* Choose 5 field-day events and draw a poster for each.

- **Q:** What events did you choose?
  **A:** List of 5, e.g., sack race, egg and spoon, relay, beanbag toss, obstacle course.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sack race
*Student:* Practice pillowcase sack race.

*How to teach:* Practice a pillowcase sack race: two-footed jumps.

- **Q:** How do you move in a sack race?
  **A:** Jump with both feet together while holding the sack.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Egg and spoon
*Student:* Practice an egg-and-spoon race with a plastic egg, walking a 15-foot course.

*How to teach:* Walk a 15-foot egg-and-spoon course with a plastic egg.

- **Q:** What do you do if the egg falls?
  **A:** Stop, pick it up, and continue from that spot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Relay
*Student:* Practice a hop-gallop-slide relay with a baton handoff.

*How to teach:* Practice a hop-gallop-slide relay with a baton handoff.

- **Q:** How do you hand off a baton?
  **A:** Place it in your teammate's hand; they grab it and go.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a field day schedule with event times and invite family.

*How to teach:* Make a schedule with event times; invite family.

- **Q:** Why make a schedule?
  **A:** So everyone knows what happens and when.

**Assessment:** Field day plan.

**Answer key:** Field day plan: 5 events with a poster for each and a schedule with times and invited guests. Proficient = all 5 events chosen, posters made, schedule complete.

## Health 1: My health goals

**Prepare:** Trackers and charts from the year, a goal sheet (eating, activity, hygiene), and a water bottle.

**Watch for:**
- Setting a goal without steps: break goals into small steps.
- Forgetting to track: use a chart on the fridge.
- Setting goals that depend on others: choose things you control.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review habits
*Student:* Review trackers from the year.

*How to teach:* Review trackers from the year.

- **Q:** Which habit did you do best?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** Which needs work?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choose goals
*Student:* Choose a summer health goal in eating, activity and hygiene.

*How to teach:* Choose summer goals in eating, activity, hygiene.

- **Q:** Example eating goal.
  **A:** Drink water instead of juice.
- **Q:** Example hygiene goal.
  **A:** Brush 2 minutes twice a day.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan
*Student:* Write or draw 2 steps for each goal (e.g., keep a water bottle in the fridge to drink more water).

*How to teach:* Write or draw 2 steps for each goal.

- **Q:** Steps for drinking more water.
  **A:** Keep a water bottle in the fridge; drink a glass at each meal.
- **Q:** Steps for activity.
  **A:** Bike after lunch; walk after dinner.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Share your goals with family and ask one person to be your goal buddy.

*How to teach:* Share goals with family; choose a goal buddy.

- **Q:** What does a goal buddy do?
  **A:** Reminds and encourages you.
- **Q:** Who is yours?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one goal, its steps and how you will track it over the summer.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one goal, steps, and tracking.

- **Q:** Explain.
  **A:** Goal: play outside daily. Steps: after lunch, ride bike; after dinner, walk. Track: sticker chart.
- **Q:** How will you know you met it?
  **A:** Check the chart.

**Assessment:** Goal sheet.

**Answer key:** Goal sheet rubric: 4 = 3 goals (eating, activity, hygiene), each with 2 specific steps and a tracking method; 3 = 2 goals complete; 2 = goals without steps; 1 = incomplete.
