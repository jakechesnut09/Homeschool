# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 33

## ELA 1: Shared research: asking and answering questions

**Prepare:** 2 library books on a topic (e.g., sea turtles), sticky notes, an interview question sheet, and a note sheet with 2 question boxes.

**Watch for:**
- Writing questions that are answered yes/no: start with who, what, where, when, why, or how.
- Keeping notes that don't answer the question: cross them out.
- Forgetting which book a fact came from: write the book's title on each sticky note.

### Day 1 (Monday): Choose a question
*Student:* Pick a topic (e.g., sea turtles) and write 2 questions you want to answer.

*How to teach:* Pick a topic and write 2 questions (e.g., 'What do sea turtles eat?' 'Where do sea turtles lay eggs?').

- **Q:** Write a research question about sea turtles.
  **A:** Example: What do sea turtles eat?
- **Q:** What words do good research questions start with?
  **A:** Who, what, where, when, why, how.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read sources
*Student:* Read two library books on the topic; jot answers on sticky notes.

*How to teach:* Read two books and jot answers on sticky notes.

- **Q:** What do sea turtles eat?
  **A:** Depends on species: jellyfish, seagrass, algae, crabs, sponges.
- **Q:** Where do sea turtles lay eggs?
  **A:** In nests they dig on sandy beaches.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a family member or librarian with 3 questions; listen and ask a follow-up question.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member or librarian with 3 questions and ask a follow-up.

- **Q:** What is a follow-up question?
  **A:** A new question about something the person just said.
- **Q:** How do you show you are listening?
  **A:** Look at the speaker, nod, don't interrupt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Organize
*Student:* Sort notes by question; cross out notes that don't answer the question.

*How to teach:* Sort notes by question; cross out notes that don't fit.

- **Q:** A note says 'Sea turtles have shells.' Does it answer 'What do they eat?'
  **A:** No — cross it out or save for another question.
- **Q:** Why sort notes?
  **A:** So each question has its answers together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Tell answers to your questions using sources; name where each fact came from.

*How to teach:* Your child tells answers using sources and names where each fact came from.

- **Q:** Where did you learn that fact?
  **A:** Names the book title or person interviewed.
- **Q:** Tell an answer in a complete sentence.
  **A:** Example: Sea turtles lay eggs on sandy beaches.

**Assessment:** Note sheet answering 2 questions with sources named.

**Answer key:** Note sheet: proficient = 2 questions, each with at least 2 correct facts that answer it, each fact labeled with its source (book title or person). For sea turtles: eat jellyfish/seagrass/crabs (species vary); lay eggs in nests on sandy beaches; babies crawl to the ocean.

## Math 1: 3-D shapes and composing shapes

**Prepare:** Household boxes, cans, balls, a cone (party hat or ice-cream cone), paper and crayons for tracing, pattern blocks (or the free Pattern Shapes app), and a hexagon outline.

**Watch for:**
- Calling a cube a square or a sphere a circle: flat shapes are 2-D; solids you can hold are 3-D.
- Thinking a cylinder's face is a rectangle: trace the end of a can — it's a circle.
- Thinking there's only one way to fill a hexagon: try 2 trapezoids, 6 triangles, 3 rhombuses, or mixed.

### Day 1 (Monday): 3-D hunt
*Student:* Find cubes, cylinders, cones, spheres and rectangular prisms at home.

*How to teach:* Hunt for 3-D shapes at home: cube (dice), cylinder (can), cone (party hat), sphere (ball), rectangular prism (cereal box). Your child labels or draws each.

- **Q:** A soup can is what solid shape?
  **A:** A cylinder.
- **Q:** A cereal box?
  **A:** A rectangular prism.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Faces
*Student:* Trace faces of 3-D objects to find their 2-D shapes.

*How to teach:* Trace the flat faces of a box, can, and cube on paper. Name the 2-D shape each makes.

- **Q:** Trace the end of a can. What shape?
  **A:** A circle.
- **Q:** What shape are the faces of a cube?
  **A:** Squares (6 of them).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compose 2-D
*Student:* Make a hexagon from triangles and trapezoids with pattern blocks.

*How to teach:* Your child fills a hexagon with pattern blocks: 2 trapezoids, then 6 triangles, then 1 trapezoid + 3 triangles. Count blocks each time.

- **Q:** How many triangles make a hexagon?
  **A:** 6.
- **Q:** How many trapezoids make a hexagon?
  **A:** 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compose 3-D
*Student:* Build a castle from boxes and cans; name each shape.

*How to teach:* Build a castle from boxes and cans. Your child names each solid used (cylinder towers, cube blocks, cone roofs).

- **Q:** What solid makes a good tower that can roll on its side?
  **A:** A cylinder.
- **Q:** Which solid has no flat faces?
  **A:** A sphere.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Fill a hexagon outline three different ways with pattern blocks and record each way; name 5 solid shapes.

*How to teach:* Your child fills a hexagon outline three ways, records each by drawing or tracing, and names 5 solid shapes.

- **Q:** Name 3 ways to fill a hexagon.
  **A:** 2 trapezoids; 6 triangles; 3 rhombuses (or 1 trapezoid + 3 triangles; 2 rhombuses + 2 triangles).
- **Q:** Name 5 solid shapes.
  **A:** Cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, rectangular prism.

**Assessment:** Shape composition task.

**Answer key:** Composition task: any 3 different correct fillings, e.g., (a) 2 trapezoids, (b) 6 triangles, (c) 3 rhombuses, (d) 1 trapezoid + 3 triangles, (e) 2 rhombuses + 2 triangles, (f) 1 trapezoid + 1 rhombus + 1 triangle. Solids: cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, rectangular prism. Proficient: 3 correct fillings recorded and all 5 solids named.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Young plants are like their parents

**Prepare:** Bean or sunflower seedlings, a photo of an adult plant of the same kind, a centimeter ruler, 3 flowers of the same type (e.g., roses or dandelions).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all seedlings of one kind are identical: they vary a little in size and leaf length.
- Measuring from the wrong end of the ruler: start at 0.
- Thinking seedlings look nothing like adults: leaf shapes match even when size differs.

### Day 1 (Monday): Seedling vs. adult
*Student:* Compare bean seedlings with an adult bean plant photo; list matching leaf shapes and differences in size.

*How to teach:* Compare seedlings with an adult plant photo; list matching leaf shapes and size differences.

- **Q:** How are seedling and adult alike?
  **A:** Same leaf shape.
- **Q:** How different?
  **A:** The adult is much bigger with more leaves (and flowers/pods).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure leaves
*Student:* Measure the longest leaf on 5 seedlings in centimeters and record the results.

*How to teach:* Measure the longest leaf on 5 seedlings in cm; record.

- **Q:** Where does the ruler start?
  **A:** At 0, at the leaf's base.
- **Q:** Are all leaves the same length?
  **A:** No — they vary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Same kind of flower
*Student:* Compare 3 flowers from the same plant type (e.g., roses or dandelions); note color and petal differences.

*How to teach:* Compare 3 flowers of the same type; note differences.

- **Q:** What might be different?
  **A:** Color shade, petal size or number.
- **Q:** Why are they all the same type?
  **A:** They share the same kind of petals, leaves, and shape.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plant journal
*Student:* Draw and label two seedlings side by side showing how they are alike and different.

*How to teach:* Draw and label two seedlings side by side.

- **Q:** Label a likeness.
  **A:** Same leaf shape.
- **Q:** Label a difference.
  **A:** One is taller.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write an evidence statement about plants being like, but not exactly like, their parents.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an evidence statement.

- **Q:** Write it.
  **A:** Young plants are like their parents because they have the same leaf shape, but not exactly because their sizes and leaf lengths are different.
- **Q:** Evidence?
  **A:** My seedlings' leaves measured different lengths.

**Assessment:** Plant variation evidence statement.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = statement with measured evidence (e.g., leaf lengths 3, 4, 4, 5, 6 cm) and a likeness (leaf shape); 3 = evidence partial; 2 = no measurements; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: From farm to table: producing goods

**Prepare:** 'From Wheat to Bread' (library) or similar, simple bread or muffin ingredients, and a PBS clip on how crayons or apples are processed.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bread comes from the store only: many workers help before it reaches the store.
- Thinking one person does all the jobs: workers specialize.
- Thinking factories make everything by hand: factories use machines and workers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Farm work
*Student:* Learn how wheat grows on a California farm and is harvested.

*How to teach:* Learn how wheat grows on a farm and is harvested.

- **Q:** How is wheat harvested?
  **A:** With a combine machine that cuts and separates the grain.
- **Q:** What part of wheat becomes flour?
  **A:** The grain (seeds/kernels).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Make bread
*Student:* Bake simple bread or muffins with family; list jobs it takes.

*How to teach:* Bake bread or muffins; list jobs it takes.

- **Q:** Name jobs that help make bread.
  **A:** Farmer, miller, baker, truck driver, grocer.
- **Q:** What does a miller do?
  **A:** Grinds grain into flour.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Factory
*Student:* Watch a PBS clip on how crayons or apples are processed.

*How to teach:* Watch a PBS clip on processing crayons or apples.

- **Q:** What happens in a factory?
  **A:** Machines and workers make or package goods.
- **Q:** Name a step in making crayons.
  **A:** Melting wax, adding color, pouring into molds, wrapping labels.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Specialized jobs
*Student:* Make a flow chart of workers from farm to store.

*How to teach:* Make a flow chart of workers from farm to store.

- **Q:** Order: baker, farmer, grocer, miller, trucker.
  **A:** Farmer → miller → baker → trucker → grocer.
- **Q:** Why arrows?
  **A:** To show the order.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why people specialize in one job, using the bread workers as an example.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why people specialize, using bread workers.

- **Q:** Why specialize?
  **A:** People get really good at one job, so more goods get made faster.
- **Q:** Example.
  **A:** The farmer grows wheat, the miller grinds flour, the baker bakes.

**Assessment:** Farm-to-table flow chart.

**Answer key:** Flow chart: farmer (grows and harvests wheat) → miller (grinds flour) → baker (bakes bread) → truck driver (delivers) → grocer (sells). Proficient = correct order with labels and an explanation that specializing makes work faster and better.

## Art & Music 1: Preparing art for display; concert rehearsal

**Prepare:** Construction paper for mats, labels, pencil; instruments.

**Watch for:**
- Putting labels that only say the title: include title, medium and artist.
- Practicing only easy parts: fix tricky parts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mat art
*Student:* Mount art on colored paper mats.

*How to teach:* Mount art on colored paper mats with a border.

- **Q:** Why put art on a mat?
  **A:** To frame it and make it look finished.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Labels
*Student:* Write labels with title, medium and artist name.

*How to teach:* Write labels: title, medium, artist name.

- **Q:** What goes on a museum label?
  **A:** Title, medium, artist name.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse songs and fix tricky parts with feedback.

*How to teach:* Rehearse songs; fix tricky parts with feedback.

- **Q:** What part was tricky? How did you fix it?
  **A:** Specific answer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Add instruments
*Student:* Add a shaker on the beat and a drum on a ta/ti-ti rhythm to one concert song.

*How to teach:* Add a shaker on the beat and a drum on a ta/ti-ti rhythm.

- **Q:** Does the shaker play the beat or the rhythm?
  **A:** The beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Practice explaining one artwork to a visitor.

*How to teach:* Practice explaining one artwork to a visitor.

- **Q:** What would you tell a visitor?
  **A:** Title, how it was made, and an idea or feeling.

**Assessment:** Labels and rehearsal progress.

**Answer key:** Labels: each shows Title, Medium (e.g., tempera paint, collage), Artist name. Rehearsal: one tricky part improved; shaker on beat and drum on ta/ti-ti rhythm. Proficient = complete labels + steady instrument parts.

## PE 1: Fitness check-up

**Prepare:** Fitness journal with week 14-16 records, stopwatch, ruler.

**Watch for:**
- Being upset if one number didn't improve: bodies grow at different rates; look at effort and other gains.
- Comparing with others: compare only with your own past scores.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strength check
*Student:* Record push-ups, curl-ups, hang time.

*How to teach:* Record push-ups, curl-ups and hang time.

- **Q:** How many push-ups did you do?
  **A:** Record child's count.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Endurance check
*Student:* Walk-jog for 10 minutes; record laps.

*How to teach:* Walk-jog 10 minutes; record laps.

- **Q:** How many laps did you finish?
  **A:** Record child's count.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Flexibility check
*Student:* Measure sit-and-reach with a ruler after a warm-up and record it.

*How to teach:* Warm up, then measure sit-and-reach.

- **Q:** Why warm up before sit-and-reach?
  **A:** Warm muscles stretch farther and safely.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare this week's push-ups, hang, sit-and-reach and jog laps with your week 14 records.

*How to teach:* Compare scores with week 14 records.

- **Q:** Which number improved the most?
  **A:** Based on the journal entries.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Set a summer fitness goal (e.g., hang 20 seconds) and write 2 ways to practice.

*How to teach:* Set a summer fitness goal and write 2 ways to practice.

- **Q:** Give an example of a fitness goal.
  **A:** Hang 20 seconds; practice by hanging at the park twice a week and doing push-ups.

**Assessment:** Fitness comparison.

**Answer key:** Fitness comparison: child lists week 14 vs. now for push-ups, hang time, sit-and-reach and jog laps and says which improved. Proficient = accurate comparison + a goal with 2 practice ideas.

## Health 1: Health helpers

**Prepare:** A toy doctor kit, a community map, interview questions, and matching cards for 6 health helpers (doctor, nurse, dentist, pharmacist, eye doctor, school nurse/EMT).

**Watch for:**
- Being scared of doctors: they help keep us healthy.
- Thinking a dentist treats tummy aches: each helper has a special job.
- Thinking pharmacists are cashiers: they prepare medicines and explain how to take them.

### Day 1 (Monday): Who helps?
*Student:* Learn about doctors, nurses, dentists and pharmacists.

*How to teach:* Learn about doctors, nurses, dentists, pharmacists.

- **Q:** What does a pharmacist do?
  **A:** Prepares medicine the doctor orders and explains how to take it safely.
- **Q:** What does a nurse do?
  **A:** Cares for patients, checks temperature, gives shots, helps doctors.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Checkup
*Student:* Role-play a doctor checkup with a toy kit.

*How to teach:* Role-play a doctor checkup with a toy kit.

- **Q:** What does a stethoscope do?
  **A:** Listens to the heart and lungs.
- **Q:** Why have checkups?
  **A:** To make sure you're growing healthy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a health helper (family doctor, nurse) about their job.

*How to teach:* Interview a health helper about their job.

- **Q:** What could you ask?
  **A:** What do you do every day? How do you help people?
- **Q:** Who did you interview?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Map
*Student:* Find clinics, pharmacies and hospitals on a community map.

*How to teach:* Find clinics, pharmacies, hospitals on a community map.

- **Q:** Where would you go for medicine?
  **A:** A pharmacy.
- **Q:** For an emergency?
  **A:** A hospital emergency room.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match 6 health helpers to their jobs and tell which one you would visit for a toothache.

*How to teach:* Match helpers to jobs; tell who to visit for a toothache.

- **Q:** Toothache?
  **A:** Dentist.
- **Q:** Can't see the board clearly?
  **A:** Eye doctor (optometrist).

**Assessment:** Helper matching.

**Answer key:** Matching key: doctor — checks health and treats illness; nurse — cares for patients; dentist — teeth; pharmacist — medicine; eye doctor — eyes/vision; EMT/paramedic — emergency help in an ambulance. Toothache → dentist. Proficient 6/6.
