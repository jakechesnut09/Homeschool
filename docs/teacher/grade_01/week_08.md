# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 8

## ELA 1: Digraph review and friendship narrative

**Prepare:** Frog and Toad books, bingo cards with sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng words, paper stapled into a 4-page booklet, and a 20-word digraph check list.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing pictures that don't follow order: number the pages First, Next, Then, Last before drawing.
- Writing 'I felt good' with no reason: add 'because...'
- Reading digraph words by sounding each letter: remind 'two letters, one sound.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Digraph game
*Student:* Play digraph bingo with sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng words; read each word you cover.

*How to teach:* Play digraph bingo: call words (ship, chin, that, when, duck, ring), your child finds and reads each word before covering it.

- **Q:** Read: shut, chick, whip, sock, king, path.
  **A:** shut, chick, whip, sock, king, path.
- **Q:** Which digraph is in thank?
  **A:** th (and also -nk).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cookies
*Student:* Read 'Cookies' (Frog and Toad Together); discuss what willpower means and tell about a time you showed it.

*How to teach:* Read 'Cookies.' Ask what willpower means and when your child has shown it.

- **Q:** What is willpower, as Frog explains it?
  **A:** Trying hard not to do something that you really want to do.
- **Q:** What did Frog finally do with the cookies?
  **A:** He gave them to the birds.
- **Q:** What did Toad decide to do at the end?
  **A:** Go home and bake a cake.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a story
*Student:* Plan a story about doing something with a friend: draw first, next, last pictures in a booklet.

*How to teach:* Your child plans a story about doing something with a friend: draws First, Next, Then, Last pictures in the booklet.

- **Q:** What goes on the first page?
  **A:** The beginning — who and what you were doing.
- **Q:** What goes on the last page?
  **A:** How it ended and how you felt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write the story
*Student:* Write a sentence under each picture using first, next, then, last, and end with how you felt.

*How to teach:* Under each picture, your child writes one sentence beginning with First, Next, Then, Last, and ends with a feeling sentence.

- **Q:** Which word starts the last page?
  **A:** Last (or Finally).
- **Q:** Add a feeling: 'Last, we ate ice cream.'
  **A:** Example: I felt happy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Read the booklet aloud with expression; complete a 20-word digraph reading check.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the booklet aloud with expression, then reads the 20-word digraph list.

- **Q:** Read: fish, chop, this, what, back, song.
  **A:** fish, chop, this, what, back, song.
- **Q:** What makes reading with expression?
  **A:** Changing your voice to show feelings, pausing at periods.

**Assessment:** Friendship booklet scored on sequence words, capitals/periods; digraph reading check.

**Answer key:** Booklet rubric: 4 = 4 sentences in order using First/Next/Then/Last, feeling at the end, all capitals and periods; 3 = in order with 1–2 errors; 2 = missing sequence words or several errors; 1 = incomplete. Digraph check (20): ship, shop, fish, chin, chop, lunch, thin, this, bath, when, what, which, duck, back, rock, sing, long, rang, much, wish — proficient 18+.

## Math 1: Fluency within 10

**Prepare:** Fact flash cards within 10, 2 dice, the free Math Learning Center 'Number Rack' app (apps.mathlearningcenter.org), Khan Academy login, and a printed 30-fact sheet for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Counting on fingers for every fact: name a strategy for each card and practice a few 'just know it' facts each day.
- Subtracting the larger number from the smaller after a dice roll: always say 'big number minus small number'.
- Rushing on timed checks and guessing: tell your child accuracy comes first; speed grows with practice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Strategy sort
*Student:* Sort fact cards by strategy: count on, doubles, make 10, think addition.

*How to teach:* Lay out fact cards and four strategy labels: count on, doubles, make 10, think addition. Your child places each card under the strategy they'd use and explains. Accept any sensible choice.

- **Q:** Which strategy for 6 + 1?
  **A:** Count on (6... 7) — answer 7.
- **Q:** Which strategy for 8 − 3?
  **A:** Think addition: 3 + 5 = 8, so 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Number rack
*Student:* Use the virtual number rack app to show facts within 10 in one push.

*How to teach:* Open the Number Rack app. Say a fact (e.g., 6 + 3) and have your child slide beads to show it in one push (5 red + 1 white on the top row, then 3). Ask how they see the total without counting by ones.

- **Q:** Show 7 on the rack in one push. What did you move?
  **A:** 5 red and 2 white beads — 7.
- **Q:** Show 4 + 4. Total?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Roll and solve
*Student:* Roll two dice, add, then subtract the smaller from the larger; record 10 rounds.

*How to teach:* Roll two dice. Your child adds them, then subtracts the smaller from the larger, recording both: e.g., 5 and 3 → 5 + 3 = 8 and 5 − 3 = 2. Do 10 rounds.

- **Q:** Roll 6 and 2: sum and difference?
  **A:** 8 and 4.
- **Q:** Roll 4 and 4.
  **A:** 8 and 0.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fact practice
*Student:* Play Khan Academy 'add and subtract within 10' practice.

*How to teach:* Sit with your child for 15–20 minutes of Khan Academy 'add and subtract within 10.' Watch for slow facts and jot them down for flash-card review.

- **Q:** 9 − 5 = ?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** 3 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 10.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Timed fluency check within 10 (goal: 30 facts in 2 minutes) and written unit test.

*How to teach:* Give the 30-fact sheet with a 2-minute timer (record how many are correct), then a short written test. Practice missed facts next week in games.

- **Q:** 7 − 0 = ?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** 2 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 8.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 fluency check and test.

**Answer key:** Fluency goal: 30 facts correct in 2 minutes (proficient); 20–29 developing; under 20 keep practicing with games. Written test (suggested): 1) 5+4=9 2) 10−3=7 3) 6+2=8 4) 8−8=0 5) 3+3=6 6) 9−4=5 7) Fact family 2,6,8: 2+6=8, 6+2=8, 8−2=6, 8−6=2 8) 7+?=10 → 3 9) Story: 9 kites, 2 fly away → 7 10) Story: 4 red and 5 blue → 9.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Sound unit wrap-up

**Prepare:** A free Mystery Science sound lesson (mysteryscience.com), household materials for instruments (boxes, rubber bands, bottles, rice, paper towel tubes, pots), and the science notebook.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only the instrument's outside makes the sound: point to the part that actually vibrates (string, skin, air).
- Thinking bigger instruments are always louder: loudness depends on how hard you play.
- Thinking pitch and loudness can't both change together: you can play a high quiet note or a low loud note.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mystery Science
*Student:* Watch a free Mystery Science sound lesson and answer the mystery question.

*How to teach:* Watch a Mystery Science sound lesson and answer the mystery question.

- **Q:** What makes sound?
  **A:** Vibrations.
- **Q:** Answer the mystery question in one sentence.
  **A:** Answers vary; must mention vibration.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Instrument design
*Student:* Design and build a new instrument from household materials.

*How to teach:* Your child designs and builds an instrument from household materials.

- **Q:** What part of your instrument will vibrate?
  **A:** Example: the rubber bands, the rice hitting the bottle, the drum skin.
- **Q:** How will you play it?
  **A:** Pluck, tap, shake, or blow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare instruments
*Student:* Compare your instrument with another; which is louder? higher?

*How to teach:* Compare your instrument with another: louder? higher?

- **Q:** Which is louder?
  **A:** The one that makes bigger vibrations.
- **Q:** Which is higher?
  **A:** The one with faster vibrations (shorter, thinner, or tighter parts).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Concert
*Student:* Play your instrument and explain what vibrates.

*How to teach:* Your child plays the instrument and explains what vibrates.

- **Q:** How can you make it louder?
  **A:** Pluck or hit it harder.
- **Q:** How can you change the pitch?
  **A:** Change length, thickness, or tightness.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Complete a sound assessment: draw what vibrates in 3 instruments and answer short questions about loud/soft and high/low sounds.

*How to teach:* Give the sound assessment.

- **Q:** What vibrates on a guitar?
  **A:** The strings.
- **Q:** Is a tuba high or low?
  **A:** Low.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test (suggested): Draw what vibrates — drum: drum skin; guitar: strings; bell: the metal bell. Questions: 1) What makes sound? vibrations 2) How do you make a drum louder? hit it harder 3) Thin or thick band makes higher pitch? thin 4) Is a whisper loud or soft? soft 5) Which is higher: bird or cow? bird. Proficient 7–8 of 8.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: My neighborhood and community map

**Prepare:** Large paper, a printed street map of your area (or online map), colored pencils.

**Watch for:**
- Drawing landmarks in the wrong order along the route: walk or check the online map.
- Directions without landmarks: add 'turn left at the park.'
- Making the map too small to read: plan space before drawing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Walking tour
*Student:* Walk your neighborhood and list landmarks (library, park, store, fire station).

*How to teach:* Walk your neighborhood and list landmarks.

- **Q:** What is a landmark?
  **A:** An important or easy-to-see place that helps you find your way.
- **Q:** Name a landmark near home.
  **A:** Example: the library, park, fire station.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Street map
*Student:* Find your home on a printed street map or online map of your town.

*How to teach:* Find your home on a printed or online map.

- **Q:** How did you find your street?
  **A:** By its name on the map.
- **Q:** What is near your home?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draw the community
*Student:* Draw a community map with your home, 4 landmarks, a key and a compass rose.

*How to teach:* Draw a community map with home, 4 landmarks, key, compass rose.

- **Q:** What does your key show?
  **A:** The symbols for each landmark.
- **Q:** Where does the compass rose go?
  **A:** In a corner of the map.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Routes
*Student:* Draw a route from home to the library and describe it using north, south, east, west.

*How to teach:* Draw a route from home to the library; describe it with directions.

- **Q:** Describe your route.
  **A:** Example: Go north on Oak Street, turn east at the park, the library is on the left.
- **Q:** What is a route?
  **A:** The path you take from one place to another.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Give a family member directions to a landmark using your map.

*How to teach:* Your child gives a family member directions to a landmark using the map.

- **Q:** What makes directions clear?
  **A:** Using directions (N/S/E/W), street names, and landmarks.
- **Q:** Did the listener reach the landmark?
  **A:** Check by following on the map.

**Assessment:** Community map with key and route directions.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = map with home, 4 landmarks, key, compass rose, and accurate route described with cardinal directions; 3 = one element missing; 2 = route unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Tints, shades and color in art history; rhythm composition

**Prepare:** White, black, blue and red paint, paper strips, brushes; Chrome Music Lab 'Rhythm' (musiclab.chromeexperiments.com).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up tint and shade: tint adds white (lighter), shade adds black (darker).
- Adding too much black at once: add tiny amounts; black is strong.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tints
*Student:* Add white to blue in steps to make a tint ladder.

*How to teach:* Paint pure blue, then add a little white each step to make 4-5 lighter tints.

- **Q:** What is a tint?
  **A:** A color with white added; it gets lighter.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shades
*Student:* Add black to red in steps to make a shade ladder.

*How to teach:* Paint pure red, then add a tiny bit of black each step to make darker shades.

- **Q:** What is a shade?
  **A:** A color with black added; it gets darker.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chrome Music Lab
*Student:* Make rhythms on Chrome Music Lab Rhythm.

*How to teach:* Make rhythms on Chrome Music Lab Rhythm; describe what you hear.

- **Q:** What happens when you add more beats?
  **A:** More sounds play in the pattern.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compose
*Student:* Write an 8-beat rhythm with ta, ti-ti and rest; perform on body percussion.

*How to teach:* Write an 8-beat rhythm with ta, ti-ti and rest; perform with claps/pats.

- **Q:** How many beats is a rest worth?
  **A:** One beat (silent).

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Perform your composition for family and explain your color ladders.

*How to teach:* Perform the composition and explain the ladders.

- **Q:** How did you make light blue?
  **A:** Added white to blue (a tint).

**Assessment:** Rhythm composition and color ladders.

**Answer key:** Color ladders: blue tint ladder gets lighter step by step (white added); red shade ladder gets darker step by step (black added). Composition: 8 beats using ta, ti-ti and rest, performed on body percussion with a steady beat. Proficient = both ladders show gradual change + 8 correct beats.

## PE 1: Locomotor skills circuit

**Prepare:** 4 station signs (jog, hop, gallop, slide), cones, timer. Print or write a simple cue checklist for Day 5.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing the skill and losing form: slower with good form is better than fast and sloppy.
- Mixing up gallop and slide: gallop moves forward, slide moves sideways.

### Day 1 (Monday): Circuit
*Student:* Do a 4-station circuit: jog, hop, gallop, slide for 1 minute each.

*How to teach:* Set 4 stations. Do jog, hop, gallop, slide for 1 minute each, resting 20 seconds between stations.

- **Q:** What do you do at the slide station?
  **A:** Move sideways with step-together, feet not crossing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Animal moves
*Student:* Move like animals using locomotor skills (bunny hop, horse gallop, crab slide).

*How to teach:* Call animal moves: bunny hop (two-foot hops), horse gallop, crab slide (sideways on all fours). Let your child invent one.

- **Q:** Which skill does a horse use?
  **A:** Gallop.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Locomotor tag
*Student:* Play tag where the tagger calls the movement.

*How to teach:* Play tag where the tagger calls the movement everyone must use. Switch taggers.

- **Q:** If I call 'slide,' how do you move?
  **A:** Sideways with step-together feet.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner feedback
*Student:* Watch a family member gallop and give one cue as feedback.

*How to teach:* Have a family member gallop while your child watches and gives one cue as feedback.

- **Q:** What cue could you give someone galloping?
  **A:** 'Keep the same foot in front' or 'back foot catches up.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Skill check of hop, gallop, jog and slide using cue checklist.

*How to teach:* Use the cue checklist to check hop, gallop, jog and slide.

- **Q:** Name one cue for jogging.
  **A:** Bent arms swinging front to back / light steps / eyes forward.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 skill checklist.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 checklist (1 point per cue): Hop - same foot, soft landing, arms for balance. Gallop - lead foot stays front, step-together. Jog - arms bent swinging, light feet. Slide - sideways, feet don't cross, body faces forward. Proficient = at least 2 cues on each of the 4 skills.

## Health 1: Caring for my teeth

**Prepare:** A toothbrush and toothpaste, floss, a hard-boiled egg (white shell), a cup of cola, a 2-minute timer, play-dough, and food cards (apple, cheese, carrots, candy, raisins, soda, crackers, milk).

**Watch for:**
- Brushing for only a few seconds: brush 2 minutes, twice a day.
- Thinking only sugar from candy hurts teeth: sticky snacks and sugary drinks count too.
- Skipping the back teeth: brush outer, inner, and chewing surfaces of every tooth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Egg teeth
*Student:* Place a hard-boiled egg in cola overnight; brush the stain to see how brushing removes stains.

*How to teach:* Place an egg in cola overnight. Next day, brush the stain.

- **Q:** What happened to the egg?
  **A:** It turned brown from the cola.
- **Q:** What does brushing do?
  **A:** Removes stains and plaque from teeth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Brush right
*Student:* Practice brushing in circles for 2 minutes on all surfaces with a timer.

*How to teach:* Practice brushing in circles 2 minutes on all surfaces.

- **Q:** How long should you brush?
  **A:** 2 minutes.
- **Q:** How often?
  **A:** Twice a day — morning and bedtime.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Floss
*Student:* Practice flossing with help; use a play-dough 'teeth' model.

*How to teach:* Practice flossing with help; use a play-dough teeth model.

- **Q:** Why floss?
  **A:** To clean between teeth where the brush can't reach.
- **Q:** How often?
  **A:** Once a day.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tooth-healthy foods
*Student:* Sort foods into tooth-friendly and sticky/sugary.

*How to teach:* Sort foods into tooth-friendly and sticky/sugary.

- **Q:** Is cheese tooth-friendly?
  **A:** Yes.
- **Q:** Is candy?
  **A:** No — sticky/sugary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Fill in a 1-week brushing chart and explain why we visit the dentist.

*How to teach:* Fill in a 1-week brushing chart; explain dentist visits.

- **Q:** Why visit the dentist?
  **A:** To clean teeth and check for cavities.
- **Q:** How often?
  **A:** About every 6 months.

**Assessment:** Brushing chart.

**Answer key:** Brushing chart: proficient = 14 checks (morning and night for 7 days) or close; explanation: 'The dentist cleans teeth and checks for cavities about every 6 months.' Sort: tooth-friendly — apple, cheese, carrots, milk; sticky/sugary — candy, raisins, soda, crackers.
