# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 19

## ELA 1: Vowel teams oa, ow, and long-i (ie, igh); writing a poem

**Prepare:** Vowel-team cards (oa, ow, ie, igh), poetry notebook, an orange (or shell/leaf), chart paper, and a word list (boat, coat, road, snow, grow, slow, pie, tie, light, night, high, bright).

**Watch for:**
- Reading ow in snow like ow in cow: ow can say long o (snow) or /ou/ (cow); try both and pick the real word.
- Spelling light as 'lite': the igh team spells long i in many words.
- Writing a poem with no sensory words: ask 'What did you see, hear, smell, touch, taste?'

### Day 1 (Monday): oa and ow
*Student:* Read boat, coat, road, snow, grow, slow; build words with vowel-team cards.

*How to teach:* Read boat, coat, road, snow, grow, slow. Build words with vowel-team cards.

- **Q:** What sound does oa make in boat?
  **A:** Long o /ō/.
- **Q:** Read: road, slow.
  **A:** road, slow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): ie and igh
*Student:* Read pie, tie, light, night, high; read the phrase 'a bright light at night' three times.

*How to teach:* Read pie, tie, light, night, high. Read 'a bright light at night' three times, getting smoother.

- **Q:** What letters make long i in night?
  **A:** igh.
- **Q:** Read: tie, high.
  **A:** tie, high.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Five senses
*Student:* Observe an orange using all 5 senses; list sensory words on a chart.

*How to teach:* Explore an orange with all 5 senses and list sensory words.

- **Q:** Name a word for how it smells.
  **A:** Example: sweet, citrusy, fresh.
- **Q:** Name a word for how it feels.
  **A:** Example: bumpy, smooth inside, sticky.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write a poem
*Student:* Write a 5-line senses poem ('I see... I hear... I smell... I touch... I taste...') about the orange.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 5-line senses poem: I see... I hear... I smell... I touch... I taste...

- **Q:** Write a line for 'I hear.'
  **A:** Example: I hear the peel rip.
- **Q:** Why use sense words in a poem?
  **A:** They help readers picture and feel it.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Vowel-team word list read; perform your senses poem.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the vowel-team list and performs their senses poem.

- **Q:** Read: coat, grow, pie, bright.
  **A:** coat, grow, pie, bright.
- **Q:** Read: light, toad.
  **A:** light, toad.

**Assessment:** Senses poem and vowel-team (oa/ow/ie/igh) word read.

**Answer key:** Senses poem rubric: 4 = 5 lines, one per sense, with specific sensory words; 3 = 5 lines, some vague; 2 = fewer than 5 lines; 1 = incomplete. Word read (12): boat, coat, road, snow, grow, slow, pie, tie, light, night, high, bright — proficient 11–12.

## Math 1: Tens and ones

**Prepare:** About 100 craft sticks, rubber bands, base-ten blocks or paper strips (ten-strips and single squares), a place-value mat (two columns: Tens | Ones), and a list of 10 numbers for the quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Reading 34 as 3 and 4 (not 30 and 4): always say '3 tens is 30.'
- Thinking 1 ten and 15 ones is not 25: count it out — 10 + 15 = 25; trade 10 ones for a ten to show 2 tens 5 ones.
- Writing 504 for 'five tens and four ones': have the child build it and write the tens digit then the ones digit.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bundle ten
*Student:* Bundle craft sticks into tens and count bundles and loose sticks.

*How to teach:* Your child counts out sticks, bundling every 10 with a rubber band. Count bundles by tens and then loose sticks by ones: '10, 20, 30, 31, 32.'

- **Q:** 3 bundles and 2 loose sticks. How many?
  **A:** 32.
- **Q:** How many sticks in 5 bundles?
  **A:** 50.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Decade numbers
*Student:* Show 10, 20 ... 90 as 1 to 9 tens.

*How to teach:* Lay out 1 bundle and say 'one ten is 10.' Keep adding bundles: 2 tens = 20 ... 9 tens = 90. Write each on the mat.

- **Q:** How many tens is 60?
  **A:** 6 tens.
- **Q:** What number is 8 tens?
  **A:** 80.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build numbers
*Student:* Build 34, 58, 71 with base-ten blocks; write tens and ones.

*How to teach:* Build 34 with 3 rods and 4 units on the mat, writing '3 tens 4 ones = 34.' Your child builds 58 and 71 the same way.

- **Q:** How many tens and ones in 58?
  **A:** 5 tens 8 ones.
- **Q:** Build 71. How many rods and cubes?
  **A:** 7 rods and 1 cube.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Different ways
*Student:* Show 25 as 2 tens 5 ones and as 1 ten 15 ones.

*How to teach:* Build 25 as 2 rods and 5 units. Trade one rod for 10 units: now 1 ten 15 ones. Count to show it's still 25.

- **Q:** Is 1 ten 15 ones the same as 25?
  **A:** Yes: 10 + 15 = 25.
- **Q:** Show 43 another way.
  **A:** 3 tens 13 ones (or 2 tens 23 ones).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Place value mat quiz (10 numbers).

*How to teach:* Give 10 numbers; your child builds each on the mat and writes tens and ones.

- **Q:** 67 = __ tens __ ones
  **A:** 6 tens 7 ones.
- **Q:** 4 tens 0 ones = ?
  **A:** 40.

**Assessment:** Place value mat quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 23 = 2t 3o; 45 = 4t 5o; 60 = 6t 0o; 18 = 1t 8o; 92 = 9t 2o; 37 = 3t 7o; 81 = 8t 1o; 50 = 5t 0o; 74 = 7t 4o; 99 = 9t 9o. Proficient 9–10 built and recorded correctly.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Sky patterns wrap-up

**Prepare:** Science notebook, sun sketches, sundial, moon journal, stapled booklet, and a Mystery Science lesson in the 'Spaceship Earth' or sky unit.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up daily (sun) and monthly (moon) patterns: the sun pattern repeats every day; the moon's every month.
- Thinking stars move like the sun on purpose: Earth's spin makes sun, moon, and stars appear to move.
- Leaving out evidence in the book: each page needs a drawing from your data.

### Day 1 (Monday): Data review
*Student:* Spread out sun sketches, sundial and moon journal; list three patterns you found.

*How to teach:* Your child lists three patterns from their data.

- **Q:** Name a sun pattern.
  **A:** The sun rises in the east and sets in the west every day.
- **Q:** A moon pattern?
  **A:** The moon's shape changes in a pattern that repeats about every month.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mystery Science sky lesson
*Student:* Watch a free Mystery Science sky/spaceship-Earth lesson and answer its mystery question in a sentence.

*How to teach:* Watch a Mystery Science sky lesson; answer the question in a sentence.

- **Q:** Why does the sun seem to move across the sky?
  **A:** Because the Earth spins.
- **Q:** Answer in one sentence.
  **A:** Answers vary; must be accurate.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sky Patterns book, part 1
*Student:* Make pages about the sun's daily path and the moon's monthly shapes, with drawings from your data.

*How to teach:* Make pages about the sun's daily path and the moon's shapes.

- **Q:** What goes on the sun page?
  **A:** Sun rising in the east, high at noon, setting in the west.
- **Q:** What goes on the moon page?
  **A:** Drawings of phases in order.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sky Patterns book, part 2
*Student:* Add a page about stars at night and a 'What I predict for tonight' page; share the book with family.

*How to teach:* Add a page about stars and 'What I predict for tonight.' Share with family.

- **Q:** When can we see stars?
  **A:** At night when the sky is dark.
- **Q:** Predict tonight's moon.
  **A:** Based on the journal; answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Complete a sky-pattern assessment: order sun positions, sequence moon phases, and explain why stars aren't seen by day.

*How to teach:* Give the sky-pattern assessment.

- **Q:** Order the sun: noon, morning, evening.
  **A:** Morning (east), noon (high), evening (west).
- **Q:** Why can't we see stars by day?
  **A:** Sunlight is too bright.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 assessment and Sky Patterns book.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test (suggested): 1) Number sun positions: rising east = 1, high at noon = 2, setting west = 3 2) Sequence moon cards: new, crescent, half, gibbous, full 3) Stars aren't seen by day because the sun's light is too bright 4) Shortest shadow: noon 5) The moon's light comes from the sun. Book: proficient = sun, moon, star pages with data drawings.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Communities long ago

**Prepare:** Calisphere photos of a California main street around 1900 and a historic photo of your own town (Calisphere, local library, or historical society), plus boxes for a model.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking communities were completely different long ago: some things stayed (stores, schools, churches, post offices).
- Thinking all photos are 'long ago' if black and white: check dates.
- Thinking jobs like blacksmith still exist everywhere: some jobs became rare as technology changed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Old town photo
*Student:* Look at Calisphere photos of a California main street around 1900; list what is the same and different today.

*How to teach:* Look at a California main street around 1900; list same and different.

- **Q:** What is different?
  **A:** Horses and wagons instead of cars, dirt roads, different clothes.
- **Q:** What is the same?
  **A:** Stores, people shopping, buildings along a main street.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jobs then
*Student:* Learn about the general store, blacksmith and milkman.

*How to teach:* Learn about the general store, blacksmith, milkman.

- **Q:** What did a blacksmith do?
  **A:** Made and fixed metal tools and horseshoes.
- **Q:** What did a milkman do?
  **A:** Delivered milk to homes.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Our town's past
*Student:* Find a historic photo of your own town (Calisphere or local library) and compare it with today.

*How to teach:* Find a historic photo of your town; compare with today.

- **Q:** What changed in our town?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., more buildings, paved roads.
- **Q:** What stayed the same?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., same street, old building.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Model town
*Student:* Build a 'long ago' main street from boxes with 3 buildings.

*How to teach:* Build a 'long ago' main street from boxes with 3 buildings.

- **Q:** Which 3 buildings?
  **A:** Example: general store, blacksmith shop, schoolhouse.
- **Q:** What would be on the street?
  **A:** Horses and wagons.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 2 ways communities have changed and 1 way they stayed the same.

*How to teach:* Your child explains 2 changes and 1 thing that stayed the same.

- **Q:** Name 2 changes.
  **A:** Cars replaced horses; roads were paved.
- **Q:** Name 1 that stayed the same.
  **A:** People still shop at stores on main streets.

**Assessment:** Oral comparison with evidence from photos.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = 2 changes and 1 continuity, each supported by details in the photos; 3 = correct but little photo evidence; 2 = only changes; 1 = incorrect.

## Art & Music 1: Clay pinch pots; Peter and the Wolf

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay, toothpicks, plastic knife, recording of Prokofiev's 'Peter and the Wolf' (with narration).

**Watch for:**
- Pinching walls too thin: keep walls about as thick as a finger.
- Thinking each character is played by any instrument: each has a specific instrument.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clay safety
*Student:* Learn clay tools and safe handling; roll a ball.

*How to teach:* Show clay tools; roll a smooth ball between palms. Keep clay covered when not used.

- **Q:** How do you keep clay from drying?
  **A:** Cover it with plastic or a damp cloth.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pinch pot
*Student:* Make a pinch pot and add texture.

*How to teach:* Press a thumb into the ball, pinch and turn to make walls. Add texture with a toothpick.

- **Q:** How do you make a pinch pot?
  **A:** Push a thumb into a ball of clay, then pinch and turn to thin the walls.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Peter and the Wolf
*Student:* Listen to 'Peter and the Wolf' and match each instrument to its character (flute = bird).

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Peter and the Wolf'; match each instrument to its character.

- **Q:** Which instrument plays the bird?
  **A:** Flute.
- **Q:** Which instruments play the wolf?
  **A:** French horns.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draw characters
*Student:* Draw each character with its instrument.

*How to teach:* Draw each character with its instrument.

- **Q:** Which instrument is the cat?
  **A:** Clarinet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Show the pinch pot and retell Peter and the Wolf by instruments.

*How to teach:* Show the pot; retell the story by instruments.

- **Q:** Which instrument is the grandfather?
  **A:** Bassoon.
- **Q:** Which instrument is the duck?
  **A:** Oboe.

**Assessment:** Character-instrument matching.

**Answer key:** Matching key: Bird = flute; Duck = oboe; Cat = clarinet; Grandfather = bassoon; Wolf = French horns; Peter = string instruments; Hunters = woodwinds with timpani and bass drum for the shots. Proficient = 5+ correct.

## PE 1: Overhand throw

**Prepare:** Tennis balls or yarn balls; chalk target on a wall; plastic cups.

**Watch for:**
- Facing the target squarely: turn your side to the target.
- Stepping with the same foot as the throwing arm: step with the opposite foot.

### Day 1 (Monday): Throw cues
*Student:* Learn: side to target, arm back, step opposite, throw.

*How to teach:* Teach cues: side to target, arm back, step opposite, throw. Model slowly, then practice without a ball.

- **Q:** How do you stand for an overhand throw?
  **A:** With your side (non-throwing shoulder) toward the target.
- **Q:** Which foot steps forward when you throw with your right arm?
  **A:** Your left foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wall target
*Student:* Throw at a chalk target on a wall.

*How to teach:* Throw at a chalk target on a wall from 10 feet.

- **Q:** Where does your throwing arm finish?
  **A:** Across your body toward the opposite hip.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Knock down
*Student:* Throw to knock down plastic cups.

*How to teach:* Stack cups and knock them down with overhand throws.

- **Q:** Did stepping help your throw go farther?
  **A:** Yes, stepping adds power.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner practice
*Student:* Throw and catch with a partner, sharing the ball.

*How to teach:* Throw and catch with a partner, taking turns with one ball.

- **Q:** How do you share the ball fairly?
  **A:** Take turns and throw gently so your partner can catch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Throw overhand with cues 5 times.

*How to teach:* Have your child throw 5 times while you check cues.

- **Q:** Say the overhand throw cues.
  **A:** Side to target, arm back, step opposite, throw.

**Assessment:** Overhand cue checklist.

**Answer key:** Overhand cue checklist (5 throws): side to target, arm back (T or L shape), step with opposite foot, throw and follow through across body. Proficient = 3-4 cues on at least 4 of 5 throws.

## Health 1: Activity goal

**Prepare:** A goal chart with 7 boxes, crayons, and equipment for a new activity (scooter, hopscotch chalk, swimming access).

**Watch for:**
- Choosing a goal you can't control: choose something you can do daily.
- Forgetting to record: color the box right after the activity.
- Only doing favorite activities: trying new ones builds skills.

### Day 1 (Monday): Set goal
*Student:* Choose a goal like 'play outside 30 minutes every day.'

*How to teach:* Choose a goal like 'play outside 30 minutes every day.'

- **Q:** Example goal.
  **A:** Play outside 30 minutes every day.
- **Q:** Why write it down?
  **A:** To remember and track it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Track
*Student:* Color one box on your goal chart each day you meet the goal and note the activity you chose.

*How to teach:* Color a box each day you meet the goal; note the activity.

- **Q:** If you colored 5 of 7 boxes, how many days did you miss?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** What activity did you do today?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Try something new
*Student:* Try a new activity (scooter, swimming, hopscotch).

*How to teach:* Try a new activity.

- **Q:** What did you try?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** Was it hard or easy at first?
  **A:** Answers vary; new skills take practice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Encourage
*Student:* Invite a family member to join your goal.

*How to teach:* Invite a family member to join.

- **Q:** How does a buddy help?
  **A:** Makes it more fun and helps you remember.
- **Q:** Who joined?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Count your colored boxes, tell whether you met your activity goal, and name 3 benefits of moving every day.

*How to teach:* Count boxes; tell if you met the goal; name 3 benefits.

- **Q:** Did you meet your goal?
  **A:** Based on boxes.
- **Q:** Name 3 benefits.
  **A:** Strong heart, strong muscles, good mood.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 goal reflection.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 rubric: 4 = goal chart with boxes colored and activities named, honest reflection on meeting the goal, and 3 correct benefits; 3 = 2 benefits; 2 = incomplete chart; 1 = missing.
