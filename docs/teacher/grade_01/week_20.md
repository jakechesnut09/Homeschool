# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 20

## ELA 1: Poetry performance and vowel-team review

**Prepare:** Printed 'Jack and Jill' and 'Hickory Dickory Dock,' a vowel-team decodable passage (about 60 words), a timer, a phone for recording, graph paper, and a cold (new) passage for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Reading fast but skipping words: speed isn't the goal — accuracy and expression come first.
- Being discouraged by a 'low' WCPM: compare only to your own earlier score.
- Mumbling during performance: practice 'big voice' and looking up at the audience.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mother Goose fluency
*Student:* Practice 'Jack and Jill' and 'Hickory Dickory Dock' with a partner, reading with rhythm.

*How to teach:* Read 'Jack and Jill' and 'Hickory Dickory Dock' together with rhythm (clap the beat).

- **Q:** In 'Jack and Jill,' why did they go up the hill?
  **A:** To fetch a pail of water.
- **Q:** In 'Hickory Dickory Dock,' what happened when the clock struck one?
  **A:** The mouse ran down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fluency routine
*Student:* Do three timed repeated readings of a vowel-team decodable passage; graph words correct per minute.

*How to teach:* Do three 1-minute readings of the same passage. Count words correct each time and graph them.

- **Q:** How do you find words correct per minute?
  **A:** Count words read in 1 minute minus errors.
- **Q:** Why read it three times?
  **A:** Practice makes reading smoother and faster.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Choose 2 memorized poems; rehearse with gestures and volume.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses 2 memorized poems and rehearses with gestures and volume.

- **Q:** Name one way to show expression.
  **A:** Change voice for feeling, use gestures, pause at line ends.
- **Q:** Where should you look while performing?
  **A:** At the audience.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poetry café
*Student:* Perform poems for family and record a video; listeners give one compliment.

*How to teach:* Hold a 'poetry café' — your child performs, you record a video, and each listener gives one compliment.

- **Q:** What makes a good compliment?
  **A:** A specific praise, e.g., 'I liked your loud voice in the rain part.'
- **Q:** What does an audience do?
  **A:** Listens quietly, looks at the speaker, claps at the end.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Vowel-team assessment (25 words) and fluency check on a cold passage.

*How to teach:* Give the 25-word vowel-team assessment and a cold passage fluency check (1 minute).

- **Q:** Read: train, play, seed, team, boat.
  **A:** train, play, seed, team, boat.
- **Q:** Read: snow, pie, night.
  **A:** snow, pie, night.

**Assessment:** Vowel-team assessment and words-correct-per-minute fluency baseline.

**Answer key:** Vowel-team list (25): rain, tail, paint, wait, play, day, stay, tray, tree, feet, sleep, eat, sea, leaf, boat, coat, road, snow, grow, slow, pie, tie, light, night, high — proficient 22+. Fluency baseline: record words correct per minute; a common mid-grade-1 target is about 25–40 WCPM, but use it as a baseline for growth, not a pass/fail.

## Math 1: Teen numbers and two-digit numbers in expanded form

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks, index cards for a match game (e.g., 47 / 40 + 7 / 4 tens 7 ones), mystery-number riddle cards, and pictures of blocks for day 4.

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'eleven is one and one': build 11 as a ten and one.
- Writing expanded form as 4 + 7 instead of 40 + 7: point to the tens digit and ask 'What is it worth?'
- Writing 'sixty-three' as 603: build 6 tens 3 ones and write 63.

### Day 1 (Monday): Teens
*Student:* Build 11–19 and say 'one ten and ___ ones'.

*How to teach:* Build 11–19 one at a time with a ten rod and ones. Your child says 'Fourteen is one ten and four ones' for each.

- **Q:** 17 = 1 ten and __ ones
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** What is 1 ten and 2 ones?
  **A:** 12.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Expanded form
*Student:* Write 47 = 40 + 7; play a match game with cards.

*How to teach:* Show 47 with blocks and write 47 = 40 + 7. Play a matching game pairing standard form, expanded form, and tens/ones cards.

- **Q:** Write 63 in expanded form.
  **A:** 60 + 3.
- **Q:** 80 + 5 = ?
  **A:** 85.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mystery number
*Student:* Solve riddles: 'I have 6 tens and 3 ones. What am I?'

*How to teach:* Read riddles aloud: 'I have 6 tens and 3 ones. What am I?' Let your child build and answer, then make riddles for you.

- **Q:** 6 tens and 3 ones?
  **A:** 63.
- **Q:** I have 2 ones and 9 tens.
  **A:** 92.
- **Q:** I have 1 ten and 0 ones.
  **A:** 10.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write numbers
*Student:* Write numbers from words and pictures of blocks.

*How to teach:* Say numbers in words ('fifty-eight') and show pictures of blocks; your child writes the numeral.

- **Q:** Write 'seventy-one'.
  **A:** 71.
- **Q:** Picture: 4 rods, 6 cubes.
  **A:** 46.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Place value quiz with teen and two-digit numbers.

*How to teach:* Give a place-value quiz with teen and two-digit numbers.

- **Q:** Write 16 in expanded form.
  **A:** 10 + 6.
- **Q:** How many tens in 90?
  **A:** 9.

**Assessment:** Place value quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) 13 = 1 ten 3 ones 2) 19 = 10 + 9 3) 52 = 50 + 2 4) 7 tens 8 ones = 78 5) 30 + 4 = 34 6) 'eighty-six' = 86 7) 'forty' = 40 8) riddle 5 tens 1 one = 51 9) blocks 2 rods 9 cubes = 29 10) 1 ten 1 one = 11. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Measuring daylight in winter

**Prepare:** Printed sunrise/sunset times (timeanddate.com or a weather site), a paper clock, grid paper. For reference: in much of California, December daylight is about 9½–10 hours and June daylight about 14½–15 hours.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking day and night are always the same length: daylight changes with the seasons.
- Counting hours incorrectly from sunrise to sunset: count each hour jump on the clock.
- Thinking winter is colder because the sun is farther away: it's mainly because of Earth's tilt — fewer daylight hours and lower sun.

### Day 1 (Monday): Record times
*Student:* Record today's sunrise and sunset times from a weather website and check whether it is dark at dinnertime.

*How to teach:* Record today's sunrise and sunset; check if it's dark at dinnertime.

- **Q:** Is it dark at dinnertime in winter?
  **A:** Usually yes — the sun sets around 5 p.m.
- **Q:** Why record the date?
  **A:** Daylight changes over time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Count the hours
*Student:* Move the hand on a paper clock from sunrise to sunset hour by hour to count daylight hours.

*How to teach:* Count daylight hours on a paper clock, hour by hour.

- **Q:** If the sun rises at 7 a.m. and sets at 5 p.m., how many hours of daylight?
  **A:** 10 hours.
- **Q:** If sunrise is 7 and sunset is 4:45?
  **A:** About 9¾ hours (between 9 and 10).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Summer comparison
*Student:* Look up sunrise and sunset for June 21 in your city and count those daylight hours.

*How to teach:* Look up June 21 times; count daylight hours.

- **Q:** If June sunrise is 6 a.m. and sunset is 8 p.m., how many hours?
  **A:** 14 hours.
- **Q:** Which has more daylight, winter or summer?
  **A:** Summer.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bar graph
*Student:* Make a 2-bar graph comparing winter and summer daylight hours on grid paper.

*How to teach:* Make a 2-bar graph comparing winter and summer daylight.

- **Q:** Which bar is taller?
  **A:** Summer.
- **Q:** How much longer is summer?
  **A:** About 4–5 hours in California (e.g., 14 − 10 = 4).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain which season has more daylight and how your graph shows it.

*How to teach:* Your child explains which season has more daylight and how the graph shows it.

- **Q:** Which season has more daylight?
  **A:** Summer.
- **Q:** How does the graph show it?
  **A:** The summer bar is taller.

**Assessment:** Daylight bar graph with explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = graph with title, labels, accurate bars (e.g., winter about 10 hours, summer about 14–15 hours), and explanation 'Summer has more daylight; its bar is taller'; 3 = minor errors; 2 = inaccurate bars; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Transportation of earlier days

**Prepare:** LOC photos of a stagecoach, a steam train, a Model T, and an early airplane; a long paper strip for a timeline; recycled materials.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cars came before trains: steam trains came first (1800s), then cars (Model T, 1908), then airplanes for travelers.
- Thinking travel was always fast: a stagecoach trip took days.
- Thinking only fast travel matters: inventions also changed comfort and safety.

### Day 1 (Monday): Photo sort
*Student:* Sort Library of Congress photos of transportation (stagecoach, steam train, Model T, airplane) from oldest to newest.

*How to teach:* Sort photos oldest to newest.

- **Q:** Order: Model T, stagecoach, airplane, steam train.
  **A:** Stagecoach, steam train, Model T, airplane.
- **Q:** Which used horses?
  **A:** The stagecoach.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Timeline
*Student:* Create a transportation timeline with drawings.

*How to teach:* Create a transportation timeline with drawings.

- **Q:** What goes on the left of a timeline?
  **A:** The oldest things.
- **Q:** What's newest?
  **A:** Airplanes (or today's electric cars).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Then and now
*Student:* Compare a trip to San Francisco by stagecoach vs. car (time, comfort).

*How to teach:* Compare a stagecoach trip and a car trip to San Francisco.

- **Q:** Which was faster?
  **A:** The car — hours instead of days.
- **Q:** Which was more comfortable?
  **A:** The car — stagecoaches were bumpy and dusty.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build a vehicle
*Student:* Build a wagon or train from recycled materials.

*How to teach:* Build a wagon or train from recycled materials.

- **Q:** What did your vehicle use for power long ago?
  **A:** Horses (wagon) or steam (train).
- **Q:** Name its parts.
  **A:** Wheels, cars/carriage, engine.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how one invention changed how people travel.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how one invention changed travel.

- **Q:** Explain the train.
  **A:** Trains carried people and goods far, much faster than wagons.
- **Q:** Explain the car.
  **A:** Cars let families travel on their own, fast.

**Assessment:** Transportation timeline.

**Answer key:** Timeline order: walking/horse and wagon → stagecoach → steam train → early car (Model T) → airplane → today's cars/jets. Proficient = correct order with labels and a sentence about one change.

## Art & Music 1: Found-object sculpture; make an instrument

**Prepare:** Boxes, tubes, bottle caps, rubber bands, beans, containers, tape, glue.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all instruments sound the same: timbre is the special 'color' of each sound.
- Rubber bands too loose on a guitar: stretch them tighter for a clearer, higher sound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan a sculpture
*Student:* Sketch a robot or creature from recycled parts.

*How to teach:* Sketch a robot or creature from recycled parts.

- **Q:** What is recycled art?
  **A:** Art made from things that would be thrown away.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Build
*Student:* Assemble the sculpture with tape and glue.

*How to teach:* Assemble with tape and glue.

- **Q:** What is an assemblage?
  **A:** A sculpture made by putting found objects together.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Make an instrument
*Student:* Build a drum, shaker or rubber-band guitar.

*How to teach:* Build a drum, shaker or rubber-band guitar.

- **Q:** How does a rubber-band guitar make sound?
  **A:** The rubber bands vibrate when plucked.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Describe timbre
*Student:* Describe the sound: rattly, booming, twangy.

*How to teach:* Describe sounds: rattly, booming, twangy.

- **Q:** What is timbre?
  **A:** The special sound quality of an instrument or voice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Present your sculpture and play your instrument.

*How to teach:* Present the sculpture; play the instrument.

- **Q:** What word describes your instrument's sound?
  **A:** Example: 'rattly' for a bean shaker.

**Assessment:** Sculpture and instrument presentation.

**Answer key:** Presentation: sculpture built from recycled parts that stands on its own; instrument makes sound; child uses a timbre word (rattly, booming, twangy). Proficient = all three.

## PE 1: Throw and catch games

**Prepare:** Several soft balls, a large ball (beach ball), cones; family members to play.

**Watch for:**
- Throwing too hard in close games: match throw strength to distance and partner.
- Hogging the ball: in team games everyone gets a turn.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hot potato
*Student:* Pass quickly around a circle with family.

*How to teach:* Pass a ball quickly around a circle of family members like a hot potato.

- **Q:** Why pass quickly but gently?
  **A:** So everyone can catch it and the game moves fast.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Monster ball
*Student:* Throw balls to push a big ball across a line.

*How to teach:* Place a big ball between two lines. Throw small balls at it to push it over the other line.

- **Q:** Which throw makes the ball go hardest?
  **A:** Overhand throw with a step.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Catch count
*Student:* Partner catch with step back after each success.

*How to teach:* Partner catch: step back after each good catch; step forward after a miss.

- **Q:** What happens after a good catch?
  **A:** Both partners take a step back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ball relay
*Student:* Run a throw-and-run relay: throw overhand at a cone, run to get the ball, and hand it to the next player.

*How to teach:* Throw overhand at a cone, run to get the ball, and hand it to the next player.

- **Q:** What is teamwork in a relay?
  **A:** Everyone does their part and helps the team, handing off carefully.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Skill check of toss, throw and catch.

*How to teach:* Check toss, throw and catch with the cue checklist.

- **Q:** What is the difference between underhand and overhand?
  **A:** Underhand swings below the waist; overhand starts with the arm back above the shoulder.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 skill check.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 skill check: Underhand toss (swing back, step opposite, follow through); overhand throw (side to target, arm back, step opposite); catch (ready hands, eyes on ball, hands only). Proficient = 2+ cues on each skill.

## Health 1: My five senses

**Prepare:** A blindfold (or closed eyes), spices (cinnamon, garlic powder, mint, vanilla), a box with 6 textured objects (sponge, spoon, pinecone, cotton ball, rock, feather), and sweet/salty/sour foods (honey, pretzels, lemon). Check allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the tongue is the only part used for flavor: smell helps us taste too.
- Smelling strong spices too close: waft gently toward your nose.
- Thinking skin is not a sense organ: skin senses touch, heat, cold, pain.

### Day 1 (Monday): Senses intro
*Student:* Name the five senses and matching body parts.

*How to teach:* Name the five senses and body parts.

- **Q:** Name the senses and body parts.
  **A:** Sight–eyes, hearing–ears, smell–nose, taste–tongue, touch–skin.
- **Q:** Which organ is biggest?
  **A:** Skin.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Smell test
*Student:* Guess spices by smell with eyes closed.

*How to teach:* Guess spices by smell with eyes closed.

- **Q:** How do you smell safely?
  **A:** Wave the smell toward your nose; don't sniff deeply.
- **Q:** Which sense did you use?
  **A:** Smell.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Touch box
*Student:* Reach into a box and identify 6 hidden objects (sponge, spoon, pinecone) by touch, describing each texture.

*How to teach:* Identify 6 objects by touch; describe textures.

- **Q:** Describe the pinecone.
  **A:** Rough, bumpy, pointy.
- **Q:** Describe the cotton ball.
  **A:** Soft, fluffy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Taste test
*Student:* Taste sweet, salty, sour foods.

*How to teach:* Taste sweet, salty, sour foods.

- **Q:** Lemon tastes?
  **A:** Sour.
- **Q:** Pretzel tastes?
  **A:** Salty.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label a body diagram with sense organs.

*How to teach:* Label a body diagram with sense organs.

- **Q:** What organ for hearing?
  **A:** Ears.
- **Q:** For touch?
  **A:** Skin.

**Assessment:** Sense organ diagram.

**Answer key:** Diagram key: eyes — sight; ears — hearing; nose — smell; tongue — taste; skin — touch. Proficient 5/5 labels correct.
