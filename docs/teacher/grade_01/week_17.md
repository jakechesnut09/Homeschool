# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 17

## ELA 1: Vowel teams ai and ay; nursery rhymes and Stevenson

**Prepare:** 'A Child's Garden of Verses' (Gutenberg) — print 'Rain' and 'My Shadow,' a poetry notebook, crayons, and an ai/ay list (rain, tail, paint, wait, snail, play, day, stay, gray, tray).

**Watch for:**
- Spelling day as 'dai': ay usually comes at the end of a word; ai in the middle.
- Reciting a poem in a flat voice: practice pauses at line ends and stress on rhyming words.
- Thinking the shadow in 'My Shadow' is a real person: it is the child's shadow, described playfully.

### Day 1 (Monday): ai words
*Student:* Read rain, tail, paint, wait, snail; learn 'ai in the middle'.

*How to teach:* Read rain, tail, paint, wait, snail. Teach 'ai in the middle.'

- **Q:** Where do you see ai in a word?
  **A:** Usually in the middle.
- **Q:** Read: paint, snail.
  **A:** paint, snail.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): ay words
*Student:* Read play, day, stay, gray, tray; learn 'ay at the end' and sort ai/ay words.

*How to teach:* Read play, day, stay, gray, tray. Teach 'ay at the end.' Sort ai/ay word cards.

- **Q:** Spell the end of play.
  **A:** ay.
- **Q:** Sort: tail, stay.
  **A:** tail = ai; stay = ay.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rain
*Student:* Read Stevenson's 'Rain' and 'My Shadow'; circle words that help you see or hear something.

*How to teach:* Read 'Rain' and 'My Shadow.' Circle words that help you see or hear something.

- **Q:** In 'Rain,' where does the rain fall?
  **A:** On field and tree, on the umbrellas here, and on the ships at sea.
- **Q:** In 'My Shadow,' what does the child's shadow do when he jumps into bed?
  **A:** It jumps before him into the bed.
- **Q:** Name a word in 'My Shadow' that helps you picture the shadow.
  **A:** Examples: little, 'heels up to the head,' 'like an india-rubber ball.'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Memorize
*Student:* Begin memorizing 'Rain'; copy it into a poetry notebook and illustrate it.

*How to teach:* Begin memorizing 'Rain' (4 lines). Your child copies it into a poetry notebook and illustrates it.

- **Q:** Say the first line of 'Rain.'
  **A:** 'The rain is raining all around.'
- **Q:** Which words rhyme in 'Rain'?
  **A:** tree and sea.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Recite 'Rain' with expression; ai/ay word read and spelling.

*How to teach:* Your child recites 'Rain' with expression. Then give the ai/ay reading and spelling.

- **Q:** Spell rain.
  **A:** r-a-i-n.
- **Q:** Spell day.
  **A:** d-a-y.

**Assessment:** Recitation and ai/ay spelling.

**Answer key:** Recitation rubric: 4 = all 4 lines from memory with expression and clear voice ('The rain is raining all around, / It falls on field and tree, / It rains on the umbrellas here, / And on the ships at sea.'); 3 = 1 prompt needed; 2 = 2–3 prompts; 1 = reads instead. ai/ay spelling: rain, tail, wait, paint, play, day, stay, gray — proficient 7–8.

## Math 1: Survey project and bar graphs

**Prepare:** Clipboard, tally sheet with 3 categories, 1-inch grid paper, crayons, and a 3-category bar graph for the data quiz (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Starting bars above the baseline or skipping squares: each bar starts at the bottom line, one square per vote.
- Forgetting labels or a title: check 'Could someone else tell what this graph is about?'
- Thinking 'how many more' is found by adding: compare bar heights and count up from the shorter bar.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan a survey
*Student:* Choose a question with 3 answers (favorite pet: dog, cat, fish).

*How to teach:* Help your child pick a survey question with exactly 3 answer choices (e.g., favorite pet: dog, cat, fish). Write the question and choices on a tally sheet.

- **Q:** Why only 3 choices?
  **A:** So answers are easy to sort and graph; everyone must pick one of the three.
- **Q:** Is 'What's your name?' a good survey question?
  **A:** No — everyone has a different answer, so it can't be sorted into a few categories.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Collect
*Student:* Survey at least 10 friends or family members by phone or in person; record tallies.

*How to teach:* Your child asks at least 10 people (phone or in person) and makes a tally for each answer. Count each row at the end.

- **Q:** If you surveyed 10 people and dog got 5 and cat got 3, how many chose fish?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** How do you show a tally of 6?
  **A:** A bundle of 5 and 1 more line.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bar graph
*Student:* Turn tallies into a bar graph on grid paper with labels.

*How to teach:* On grid paper, draw a baseline, label the 3 categories underneath, and color one square up for each tally. Add a title and number labels on the side.

- **Q:** Dog had 5 tallies. How tall is its bar?
  **A:** 5 squares.
- **Q:** What goes at the top of the graph?
  **A:** A title, like 'Our Favorite Pets'.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interpret
*Student:* Write 3 statements and 2 how-many-more questions about your graph.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 3 statements ('More people like dogs than fish') and 2 how-many-more questions, with answers.

- **Q:** Dog 5, cat 3, fish 2: how many more chose dog than cat?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** Write a true statement about this data.
  **A:** Example: Dog is the favorite; fish got the fewest votes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Present your survey graph to family, then complete a data assessment reading a 3-category graph.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the graph to family. Then give the data quiz on a 3-category bar graph.

- **Q:** Bars: red 4, blue 8, green 6. Which is most?
  **A:** Blue.
- **Q:** How many more chose blue than red?
  **A:** 4.

**Assessment:** Survey graph project and data quiz.

**Answer key:** Project rubric: 4 = question with 3 choices, 10+ tallies, bar graph with title, labels, accurate bars, 3 statements and 2 correct how-many-more questions; 3 = one element missing; 2 = graph inaccurate or missing several parts; 1 = incomplete. Data quiz graph (suggested Favorite Colors: red 4, blue 8, green 6): 1) blue most 2) red fewest 3) total 18 4) blue − red = 4 more 5) green − red = 2 more.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Moon patterns over a month

**Prepare:** Moon journal, a lamp without a shade, a foam ball on a pencil, a dark room, and NASA Space Place (spaceplace.nasa.gov).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the pattern is random: it repeats about every 29½ days.
- Mixing up waxing and waning: waxing = growing lit part; waning = shrinking.
- Thinking clouds cause phases: clouds only hide the moon.

### Day 1 (Monday): Journal check
*Student:* Lay out two weeks of moon drawings and describe how the lit part is growing (waxing) or shrinking (waning).

*How to teach:* Lay out two weeks of drawings; describe whether the lit part is growing or shrinking.

- **Q:** What is waxing?
  **A:** The lit part getting bigger each night.
- **Q:** What is waning?
  **A:** The lit part getting smaller.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lamp-and-ball model
*Student:* In a dark room, hold a foam ball at arm's length and turn slowly around a lamp; watch the lit part change like moon phases.

*How to teach:* In a dark room, hold the foam ball at arm's length and turn slowly around the lamp. Watch the lit part change.

- **Q:** What does the lamp stand for?
  **A:** The sun.
- **Q:** What does your head stand for?
  **A:** The Earth.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): NASA Space Place
*Student:* Explore a NASA Space Place moon activity and add one new fact about the moon to your journal.

*How to teach:* Explore a NASA Space Place moon activity; add a fact.

- **Q:** Name a moon fact.
  **A:** Example: The moon goes around Earth about once a month; the moon has no air.
- **Q:** How long is the moon's cycle?
  **A:** About a month (29½ days).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Predict
*Student:* Using the pattern, draw what you think the moon will look like in 3 nights; check it when the day comes.

*How to teach:* Draw what the moon will look like in 3 nights; check.

- **Q:** If the moon is a crescent and growing, what will it look like in 3 nights?
  **A:** A bigger crescent, close to a half moon.
- **Q:** How can you check?
  **A:** Look at the sky that night and compare.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain in 2–3 sentences that the moon's pattern repeats about every month, pointing to your journal as evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child explains the moon pattern repeats about monthly.

- **Q:** How often does the pattern repeat?
  **A:** About every month.
- **Q:** What's your evidence?
  **A:** My journal shows the moon growing, full, shrinking, and starting again.

**Assessment:** Prediction accuracy and written pattern explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = prediction matches the observed moon and explanation says the pattern repeats about every month with journal evidence; 3 = mostly correct; 2 = prediction or explanation incorrect; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Presidents' Day: Washington and Lincoln

**Prepare:** Picture biographies of George Washington and Abraham Lincoln (library), a quarter, a penny, a $1 bill, a $5 bill (if available), LOC portraits, and photos of the Washington Monument and Lincoln Memorial.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up which president is on which coin: Washington on the quarter and $1; Lincoln on the penny and $5.
- Thinking Washington and Lincoln lived at the same time: Washington was first president (1789); Lincoln was 16th (1861).
- Thinking Presidents' Day honors only current presidents: it honors Washington, Lincoln, and all presidents.

### Day 1 (Monday): George Washington
*Student:* Read a picture biography and list 2 facts; find him on the quarter and dollar bill.

*How to teach:* Read a Washington biography; list 2 facts; find him on the quarter and $1.

- **Q:** Who was the first president?
  **A:** George Washington.
- **Q:** What did he do before he was president?
  **A:** Led the American army in the Revolutionary War.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Abraham Lincoln
*Student:* Read a picture biography and learn why he was called 'Honest Abe'; find him on the penny.

*How to teach:* Read a Lincoln biography; learn why he was 'Honest Abe'; find him on the penny.

- **Q:** Why was Lincoln called 'Honest Abe'?
  **A:** He was known for honesty — stories tell of him walking miles to return a customer's change.
- **Q:** What did Lincoln do as president?
  **A:** Led the country during the Civil War and worked to end slavery.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Compare Washington and Lincoln on a Venn diagram.

*How to teach:* Compare Washington and Lincoln on a Venn diagram.

- **Q:** How were they alike?
  **A:** Both were presidents and leaders, both are on money and have memorials in Washington, D.C.
- **Q:** How different?
  **A:** Washington was first president and a general; Lincoln was 16th and led during the Civil War.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Landmarks
*Student:* Look at photos of the Washington Monument and Lincoln Memorial and match them to each president.

*How to teach:* Match the Washington Monument and Lincoln Memorial to each president.

- **Q:** Which is a tall, thin tower?
  **A:** The Washington Monument.
- **Q:** Which has a big statue of a seated president?
  **A:** The Lincoln Memorial.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Assessment: symbols, documents, holidays and heroes.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 assessment.

- **Q:** What is on the penny?
  **A:** Abraham Lincoln.
- **Q:** What does the Liberty Bell stand for?
  **A:** Freedom/liberty.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test (suggested): 1) Flag stars = 50 states 2) Stripes = 13 colonies 3) National bird = bald eagle 4) Statue of Liberty = gift from France, freedom 5) Declaration = July 4, 1776, freedom from Britain 6) Constitution = 'We the People,' rulebook 7) Veterans Day honors people who served 8) MLK worked for equal rights 9) Washington = first president, quarter 10) Lincoln = Honest Abe, penny. Proficient 9–10.

## Art & Music 1: Pattern review; performance

**Prepare:** Prints from week 15, paper, glue; instruments.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking AB patterns only use two sounds: A and B can be groups of sounds (clap-clap / pat).
- Thinking art only happens in lessons: people make art everywhere.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pattern quilt
*Student:* Make a paper-quilt square with printed patterns.

*How to teach:* Glue printed patterns into a paper-quilt square.

- **Q:** What pattern is in your quilt square?
  **A:** Names a repeating pattern.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Body percussion pattern
*Student:* Create an AB body percussion pattern (clap-clap-pat).

*How to teach:* Create an AB pattern (A = clap-clap, B = pat) and repeat 4 times.

- **Q:** What comes after A in an AB pattern?
  **A:** B.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pattern combination
*Student:* Perform your body pattern while showing your quilt square.

*How to teach:* Perform the body pattern while showing the quilt square.

- **Q:** How are your quilt and pattern alike?
  **A:** Both repeat in order.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Art outside school
*Student:* Tell where you make art outside lessons (cards, chalk drawings).

*How to teach:* Tell where you make art outside lessons.

- **Q:** Where else do you make art?
  **A:** Birthday cards, chalk drawings, decorations.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 share
*Student:* Hold a mini-show of prints and patterns.

*How to teach:* Hold a mini-show of prints and patterns.

- **Q:** Which piece are you most proud of?
  **A:** Any with a reason.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 portfolio.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 portfolio: pattern border, prints, paper chain, quilt square showing repeating patterns. Body pattern: performs AB pattern (e.g., clap-clap-pat) 4 times steadily. Proficient = complete portfolio + steady pattern.

## PE 1: Underhand toss

**Prepare:** Beanbags (or rolled socks), laundry basket, hula hoop; tape to mark 6 and 8 feet.

**Watch for:**
- Stepping with the same foot as the throwing hand: step with the opposite foot.
- Stopping the arm at release: follow through toward the target.

### Day 1 (Monday): Toss cues
*Student:* Learn cues: swing back, step with the opposite foot, release, follow through.

*How to teach:* Teach cues: swing back, step with the opposite foot, release, follow through. Say them as you model.

- **Q:** Which foot do you step with if you toss with your right hand?
  **A:** Left foot (opposite foot).
- **Q:** Where does your hand point after the toss?
  **A:** At the target (follow through).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Target toss
*Student:* Toss beanbags into a basket from 6 feet.

*How to teach:* Toss beanbags into a basket from 6 feet.

- **Q:** What helps your aim?
  **A:** Look at the target and follow through toward it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distance challenge
*Student:* Move back after 3 successful tosses.

*How to teach:* After 3 successful tosses, step back one big step.

- **Q:** Do you need to swing harder from farther away?
  **A:** Yes, a bit more swing, but keep the same cues.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner toss
*Student:* Toss underhand to a partner's ready hands from 8 feet, taking a step back after every 3 good tosses.

*How to teach:* Toss underhand to a partner's ready hands from 8 feet; step back after every 3 good tosses.

- **Q:** What are 'ready hands'?
  **A:** Hands up in front, fingers spread, ready to catch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make 10 underhand tosses at the basket from 8 feet and record how many land in.

*How to teach:* Make 10 tosses at the basket from 8 feet and record how many land in.

- **Q:** Say the four toss cues.
  **A:** Swing back, step opposite, release, follow through.

**Assessment:** Toss accuracy count.

**Answer key:** Toss accuracy: record number of 10 underhand tosses landing in the basket from 8 feet. 6+ = proficient; 3-5 = developing; 0-2 = beginning. Also check form: arm swings back, opposite-foot step, follow-through to target.

## Health 1: Why move every day?

**Prepare:** A timer, a jump rope, paper for an activity log.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only sports count as activity: dancing, walking, playing tag count.
- Thinking a faster heartbeat is bad: it shows the heart is working and getting stronger.
- Thinking 60 minutes must be all at once: it can be added up through the day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Heart and lungs
*Student:* Feel heartbeat and breathing before and after 1 minute of jumping.

*How to teach:* Feel heartbeat and breathing before and after 1 minute of jumping.

- **Q:** What happened to your heartbeat?
  **A:** It got faster.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** Muscles need more oxygen, so the heart pumps faster.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Benefits
*Student:* List how activity helps heart, muscles, bones and mood.

*How to teach:* List how activity helps heart, muscles, bones, mood.

- **Q:** How does activity help the heart?
  **A:** Makes it stronger.
- **Q:** How does it help mood?
  **A:** Makes you feel happier and calmer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 60 minutes
*Student:* Learn kids need 60 minutes of activity daily; brainstorm fun ways.

*How to teach:* Learn kids need 60 minutes of activity daily; brainstorm.

- **Q:** How many minutes of activity daily?
  **A:** At least 60.
- **Q:** Name fun ways.
  **A:** Biking, dancing, tag, swimming, jumping rope.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Activity log
*Student:* Start a week-long activity log, drawing each active thing you do and how many minutes it lasted.

*How to teach:* Start an activity log with drawings and minutes.

- **Q:** You biked 20 minutes and played tag 15 minutes. Total?
  **A:** 35 minutes.
- **Q:** How many more to reach 60?
  **A:** 25.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 3 benefits of activity.

*How to teach:* Your child explains 3 benefits.

- **Q:** Name 3 benefits.
  **A:** Strong heart, strong muscles and bones, better mood/sleep.
- **Q:** Which activity makes your heart beat fastest?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., running.

**Assessment:** Benefits explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names 3 correct benefits (stronger heart/lungs, stronger muscles and bones, better mood, better sleep, healthy weight) with an example; 3 = 2; 2 = 1; 1 = none.
