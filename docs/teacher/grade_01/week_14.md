# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 14

## ELA 1: Long i and long o with silent e; text features

**Prepare:** A nonfiction animal book with a table of contents, headings, bold words, and a glossary; sticky notes; animal photos; and an i_e/o_e list (bike, kite, time, ride, five, home, rope, bone, nose, stone).

**Watch for:**
- Reading hope as 'hop': look at the end of the word for e.
- Using adjectives that don't describe (The frog is run): adjectives tell what kind, color, size, or how it feels.
- Thinking the glossary is the same as the table of contents: the table of contents (front) lists chapters; the glossary (back) explains words.

### Day 1 (Monday): i_e words
*Student:* Read bike, kite, time, ride, five; write them in sentences.

*How to teach:* Read bike, kite, time, ride, five. Your child writes each in a sentence.

- **Q:** Read: kite, ride.
  **A:** kite, ride.
- **Q:** Use five in a sentence.
  **A:** Example: I have five toys.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): o_e words
*Student:* Read home, rope, bone, nose, stone; play a 'change the vowel' game (hop→hope).

*How to teach:* Read home, rope, bone, nose, stone. Play 'change the vowel': hop → hope, not → note, rob → robe.

- **Q:** Add e to hop.
  **A:** hope.
- **Q:** Add e to not.
  **A:** note.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Text feature hunt
*Student:* Use sticky notes to find a table of contents, heading, bold word, and glossary in a nonfiction animal book.

*How to teach:* Your child uses sticky notes to find the table of contents, a heading, a bold word, and the glossary.

- **Q:** Where is the table of contents?
  **A:** At the front of the book.
- **Q:** What does a glossary tell you?
  **A:** The meanings of important words, in ABC order, at the back.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Adjectives
*Student:* Describe an animal photo with 5 adjectives (color, size, feel); write 'The ___ frog is ___.'

*How to teach:* Look at an animal photo. Your child lists 5 adjectives (color, size, feel) and writes 'The ___ frog is ___.'

- **Q:** Name an adjective for color.
  **A:** Example: green.
- **Q:** Complete: The ___ frog is ___.
  **A:** Example: The small frog is slimy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Use the table of contents to find two facts; read an i_e/o_e word list.

*How to teach:* Your child uses the table of contents to find two facts, then reads the i_e/o_e list.

- **Q:** How do you use the table of contents to find facts about what an animal eats?
  **A:** Look for a chapter title like 'Food' and turn to its page number.
- **Q:** Read: bone, stone.
  **A:** bone, stone.

**Assessment:** Text-feature scavenger hunt and long i/o word list.

**Answer key:** Scavenger hunt: 4 features flagged correctly (table of contents, heading, bold word, glossary) plus two facts found using the table of contents. Word list: bike, kite, time, ride, five, home, rope, bone, nose, stone — proficient 9–10.

## Math 1: Subtraction within 20

**Prepare:** Two ten-frames, counters, a 0–20 number line, and 5 subtraction story cards (see day 4).

**Watch for:**
- Taking 4 from 13 by removing ones from the full ten-frame first, then getting confused: take the 3 loose ones first to get to 10, then 1 more.
- For 'think addition' (15 − 7), answering 22 by adding: say '7 plus what makes 15?'
- Counting the starting number as a jump on the number line: always 'land then count' jumps.

### Day 1 (Monday): Take to ten
*Student:* Solve 13 − 4 as 13 − 3 − 1 on a ten-frame.

*How to teach:* Show 13 (full ten-frame + 3). For 13 − 4, take away the 3 extra counters first (to 10), then 1 more from the ten-frame: 9. Write 13 − 3 − 1 = 9.

- **Q:** 13 − 4 = ?
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** 14 − 6 = ?
  **A:** 8 (14 − 4 = 10, 10 − 2 = 8).
- **Q:** 12 − 5 = ?
  **A:** 7.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Think addition
*Student:* Solve 15 − 7 by thinking 7 + ? = 15.

*How to teach:* For 15 − 7, ask '7 plus what makes 15?' Count up from 7 to 10 (3) and 10 to 15 (5): 3 + 5 = 8. Practice with 3 more.

- **Q:** 15 − 7 = ?
  **A:** 8.
- **Q:** 13 − 8 = ?
  **A:** 5 (8 + 5 = 13).
- **Q:** 17 − 9 = ?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Number line jumps
*Student:* Show 16 − 9 with jumps on a number line.

*How to teach:* For 16 − 9, start at 16 on the number line and jump back 6 to 10, then 3 more to 7. Or jump forward from 9 to 16 (7 jumps). Compare both ways.

- **Q:** 16 − 9 = ?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** 14 − 8 = ?
  **A:** 6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Story problems
*Student:* Solve 5 subtraction stories within 20 with a model.

*How to teach:* Read 5 subtraction stories aloud. Your child chooses a model (ten-frame, number line, or drawing) and writes an equation.

- **Q:** Ben had 14 cars and gave away 5. How many are left?
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** There are 18 kids; 9 are boys. How many girls?
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** 11 grapes, ate 3. How many left?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Subtraction strategies quiz; explain a strategy for 14 − 6.

*How to teach:* Give a short strategy quiz. Then ask your child to explain 14 − 6 aloud using any strategy.

- **Q:** Explain 14 − 6.
  **A:** 8; e.g., 14 − 4 = 10, then 10 − 2 = 8; or 6 + 4 = 10, + 4 = 14, so 4 + 4 = 8.
- **Q:** 12 − 3 = ?
  **A:** 9.

**Assessment:** Subtraction strategy quiz with explanation.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) 13−4=9 2) 15−6=9 3) 12−7=5 4) 16−8=8 5) 11−2=9 6) 17−9=8 7) 14−5=9 8) 18−9=9. Explanation rubric for 14 − 6 = 8: 4 = names a strategy and shows each step (take to ten or think addition); 3 = correct answer, partial explanation; 2 = counts back by ones only; 1 = incorrect.

## Science 1 (NGSS): The sun's path across the sky

**Prepare:** A compass (phone compass works), sidewalk chalk, a fixed spot outside with a landmark (tree, roof, pole), and paper for sky sketches. Safety: teach never to look directly at the sun.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the sun moves around the Earth: the sun looks like it moves because the Earth spins.
- Looking directly at the sun: never — look at landmarks near it or at shadows.
- Thinking shadows always point the same way: shadows point away from the sun, so they change direction during the day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sun safety and landmarks
*Student:* Learn never to look directly at the sun; stand in the same spot and sketch where the sun is above a tree, roof or pole at 9 a.m.

*How to teach:* Teach sun safety. Stand in the same spot at 9 a.m. and sketch where the sun is relative to a landmark.

- **Q:** Why never look at the sun?
  **A:** It can hurt your eyes.
- **Q:** Where is the sun in the morning?
  **A:** Low in the eastern sky.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Three sky sketches
*Student:* Sketch the sun's position from the same spot at morning, noon and late afternoon, labeling east and west with a compass.

*How to teach:* Sketch the sun's position at morning, noon, and late afternoon from the same spot, labeling east and west.

- **Q:** Where is the sun at noon?
  **A:** Highest in the sky (toward the south in California).
- **Q:** Where is it in late afternoon?
  **A:** Lower in the western sky.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Chalk shadow tracing
*Student:* Stand on an X in the driveway and have someone trace your shadow at 9 a.m., noon and 3 p.m.; compare length and direction.

*How to teach:* Your child stands on a chalk X; trace the shadow at 9 a.m., noon, 3 p.m.

- **Q:** When is the shadow shortest?
  **A:** Around noon.
- **Q:** Which way does the morning shadow point?
  **A:** West (away from the sun in the east).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pattern talk
*Student:* Use your sketches and tracings to describe the pattern (rises in the east, highest at midday, sets in the west) in two sentences.

*How to teach:* Your child describes the pattern in two sentences.

- **Q:** Describe the sun's daily pattern.
  **A:** The sun rises in the east, is highest at midday, and sets in the west.
- **Q:** How does that change shadows?
  **A:** They're long in morning and evening and short at midday, pointing away from the sun.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Predict and draw where the sun and your shadow will be at noon tomorrow, then check your prediction outside.

*How to teach:* Your child predicts and draws where the sun and their shadow will be at noon tomorrow, then checks.

- **Q:** Predict the noon shadow.
  **A:** Short, pointing north.
- **Q:** Was your prediction right? How do you know?
  **A:** Compare with the real shadow tracing.

**Assessment:** Prediction drawing checked against a real observation.

**Answer key:** Prediction rubric: 4 = drawing shows sun high in the southern sky at noon and a short shadow pointing north, checked against real observation with comments; 3 = mostly correct; 2 = sun or shadow misplaced; 1 = no prediction.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: American symbols

**Prepare:** Smithsonian/LOC images of the flag, bald eagle, Great Seal (on the back of a $1 bill), Statue of Liberty, and Liberty Bell; construction paper; a quarter dated 1998 or earlier (eagle on back) if available.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the flag always had 50 stars: stars were added as states joined; there are 13 stripes for the 13 original colonies.
- Thinking the Statue of Liberty was built in America by Americans: it was a gift from the people of France (dedicated in 1886).
- Thinking the Liberty Bell is still rung: it cracked and is no longer rung.

### Day 1 (Monday): The flag
*Student:* Count the stars and stripes and learn what they stand for; make a paper flag.

*How to teach:* Count stars and stripes; learn their meaning; make a paper flag.

- **Q:** How many stars, and what do they stand for?
  **A:** 50, one for each state.
- **Q:** How many stripes, and why?
  **A:** 13, for the 13 original colonies (7 red, 6 white).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Bald eagle
*Student:* Learn why the bald eagle was chosen as the national bird; view it on the Great Seal and a quarter.

*How to teach:* Learn why the bald eagle is the national bird; find it on the Great Seal (back of the $1 bill) and older quarters.

- **Q:** Why was the bald eagle chosen?
  **A:** It is native to North America and stands for strength and freedom.
- **Q:** Where can you see the eagle?
  **A:** On the Great Seal (on the back of the $1 bill) and on many quarters from 1998 and earlier.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Statue of Liberty
*Student:* Look at historic Library of Congress photos of the Statue of Liberty being built; discuss what it welcomes.

*How to teach:* View LOC photos of the Statue of Liberty being built; discuss what it welcomes.

- **Q:** Who gave the Statue of Liberty to the U.S.?
  **A:** The people of France.
- **Q:** What does it welcome?
  **A:** Immigrants and visitors arriving by ship in New York Harbor; it stands for freedom.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Liberty Bell
*Student:* Find the crack in a Liberty Bell photo and learn its story.

*How to teach:* Find the crack in a Liberty Bell photo and learn its story.

- **Q:** Where is the Liberty Bell?
  **A:** Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.
- **Q:** Why isn't it rung?
  **A:** It cracked, and the crack grew too large (by the 1840s).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match 6 symbols to their names and meanings.

*How to teach:* Match 6 symbols to names and meanings.

- **Q:** Statue of Liberty means?
  **A:** Freedom and welcome.
- **Q:** Bald eagle means?
  **A:** Strength and freedom.

**Assessment:** Symbol matching quiz.

**Answer key:** Matching key: flag — our country (50 states, 13 colonies); bald eagle — national bird, strength/freedom; Statue of Liberty — freedom and welcome to immigrants; Liberty Bell — liberty/independence; Great Seal — official seal of the U.S.; White House — home of the president (if used). Proficient 6/6.

## Art & Music 1: Patterns all around; folk songs

**Prepare:** Patterned fabric or wrapping paper, markers, paper; lyrics for 'She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a pattern only repeats once: a pattern repeats again and again.
- Calling folk songs 'old pop songs': folk songs are passed down by people, often with unknown composers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pattern hunt
*Student:* Find patterns in clothing, floors and wrapping paper at home; draw three.

*How to teach:* Hunt patterns in clothes, floors and wrapping paper; draw three.

- **Q:** What is a pattern?
  **A:** Something that repeats in order.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Pattern borders
*Student:* Make an ABAB and ABC pattern border around a paper.

*How to teach:* Make an ABAB border and an ABC border.

- **Q:** What comes next: red, blue, red, blue, ___?
  **A:** Red.
- **Q:** What comes next in ABC: circle, square, triangle, circle, ___?
  **A:** Square.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Folk song
*Student:* Sing 'She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain' with motions.

*How to teach:* Sing 'She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain' with motions.

- **Q:** What repeats in this song?
  **A:** Each verse repeats its line several times, plus the motions.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Song story
*Student:* Learn where the song came from and why people sang while working.

*How to teach:* Explain that this American folk song was sung by workers (such as railroad workers) and passed down; songs help work go faster.

- **Q:** What is a folk song?
  **A:** A song passed down by people over time; often nobody knows who wrote it.
- **Q:** Why did people sing while working?
  **A:** To keep a rhythm together and make work more fun.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain your pattern border and lead a folk song.

*How to teach:* Explain the border; lead the folk song.

- **Q:** Tell me your pattern.
  **A:** Names the repeating units correctly.

**Assessment:** Pattern border.

**Answer key:** Pattern border: one ABAB border (e.g., star-heart-star-heart) and one ABC border (circle-square-triangle repeated) with no breaks. Proficient = both patterns correct all the way around.

## PE 1: Strong muscles and bones

**Prepare:** Monkey bars or a sturdy low bar, mat, and a notebook to start a fitness journal.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bones move by themselves: muscles pull on bones to move them.
- Sagging hips in push-ups: keep a straight line from head to knees.

### Day 1 (Monday): Muscles move bones
*Student:* Feel your arm muscle when you bend your elbow.

*How to teach:* Have your child bend the elbow and feel the biceps tighten. Explain muscles pull bones to make us move.

- **Q:** What makes your bones move?
  **A:** Muscles pull on them.
- **Q:** What does your arm muscle do when you bend your elbow?
  **A:** It gets tight and bunches up (contracts).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Animal strength
*Student:* Do bear crawls, crab walks and frog jumps.

*How to teach:* Do bear crawls, crab walks and frog jumps across the yard.

- **Q:** Which animal walk uses your arms most?
  **A:** Bear crawl and crab walk use arms a lot (accept either).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Modified push-ups
*Student:* Practice knee push-ups and curl-ups.

*How to teach:* Teach knee push-ups (straight line head to knees) and curl-ups (knees bent, lift shoulders).

- **Q:** What should your back look like in a push-up?
  **A:** Straight like a board, no sagging.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hang
*Student:* Hang from monkey bars for as long as possible.

*How to teach:* Hang from a bar with both hands; spot underneath and count seconds.

- **Q:** What muscles does hanging use?
  **A:** Hands, arms and shoulders (grip strength).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Record push-ups, curl-ups and hang time.

*How to teach:* Record push-ups, curl-ups and hang time in the journal.

- **Q:** Why write your scores down?
  **A:** So you can see how you improve later.

**Assessment:** Strength baseline record.

**Answer key:** Strength baseline: record number of knee push-ups with good form (straight back, chest near floor), curl-ups (knees bent, shoulders lift), and seconds of hang. No passing score: proficient = completes all three with correct form and records them in the journal for week 33.

## Health 1: Influences on food choices

**Prepare:** Cereal boxes, magazine ads, a recorded kids' food commercial, and paper and markers.

**Watch for:**
- Believing everything in ads: ads try to make you want to buy.
- Thinking cartoon characters mean a food is good for you: check the food, not the character.
- Thinking family food traditions are unhealthy: many traditional foods are healthy; balance is key.

### Day 1 (Monday): Cereal boxes
*Student:* Look at cereal boxes for cartoons and colors meant to attract kids.

*How to teach:* Look at cereal boxes for cartoons and colors.

- **Q:** Why do boxes have cartoons?
  **A:** To attract kids.
- **Q:** Where is the cereal placed in stores?
  **A:** Often at kids' eye level.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ad detective
*Student:* Watch a kid's food commercial and discuss its tricks.

*How to teach:* Watch a kid's food commercial; discuss tricks.

- **Q:** Name a trick.
  **A:** Fun music, toys, cartoons, kids having fun.
- **Q:** Does the ad say if the food is healthy?
  **A:** Not always.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Family food
*Student:* Interview a family member about favorite family foods and traditions.

*How to teach:* Interview a family member about family foods and traditions.

- **Q:** Ask: what food did you eat growing up?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** Why do families have food traditions?
  **A:** To celebrate, remember culture, share.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Design a healthy ad
*Student:* Make an ad for a fruit or vegetable.

*How to teach:* Make an ad for a fruit or vegetable.

- **Q:** What makes a good ad?
  **A:** Bright picture, catchy words, reasons to eat it.
- **Q:** Example slogan.
  **A:** Crunch into carrots — super snack for super eyes!

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one influence on your food choices.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one influence on food choices.

- **Q:** Name an influence.
  **A:** Ads, family, friends, culture, taste.
- **Q:** How does it affect you?
  **A:** Example: I wanted cereal because of the cartoon.

**Assessment:** Healthy food ad.

**Answer key:** Ad rubric: 4 = ad for a fruit/vegetable with picture, catchy slogan, and a true health reason (e.g., 'carrots have vitamins for healthy eyes'), plus explanation of one influence; 3 = missing reason; 2 = unclear; 1 = incomplete.
