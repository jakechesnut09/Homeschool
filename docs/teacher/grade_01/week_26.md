# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 26

## ELA 1: Two-syllable words; comparing two plant texts

**Prepare:** 'The Tiny Seed' by Eric Carle (library), a ReadWorks or Starfall plant passage, a same/different chart, the bean bag, a ruler, and 12 two-syllable words.

**Watch for:**
- Splitting words in the wrong place (ra-bbit): split between the two consonants: rab-bit.
- Comparing texts by listing everything: focus on what both say about seeds and what only one says.
- Measuring from the wrong end of the ruler: line up the 0 mark with the bottom of the sprout.

### Day 1 (Monday): Split words
*Student:* Split two-syllable words between consonants (rab-bit, nap-kin, kit-ten, muf-fin) and read them.

*How to teach:* Split two-syllable words between consonants: rab-bit, nap-kin, kit-ten, muf-fin. Read them.

- **Q:** Split napkin.
  **A:** nap-kin.
- **Q:** Read: muffin, kitten.
  **A:** muffin, kitten.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Second text
*Student:* Read 'The Tiny Seed' by Eric Carle and a ReadWorks plant passage.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Tiny Seed' and a plant passage.

- **Q:** What happens to many of the seeds in 'The Tiny Seed'?
  **A:** Some land on a mountain, in the ocean, or in the desert; one is eaten by a bird; others are eaten by a mouse or trampled.
- **Q:** What happens to the tiny seed in the end?
  **A:** It grows into a giant flower, and in the fall its seeds blow away to start again.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a chart of what both texts say about seeds and what only one says.

*How to teach:* Make a chart: what both texts say about seeds and what only one says.

- **Q:** Name something both texts say.
  **A:** Seeds need water/soil/sun to grow into plants.
- **Q:** What does only 'The Tiny Seed' show?
  **A:** A seed's journey on the wind through many dangers and the seasons.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shared research
*Student:* Measure your bean sprout and add a dated sentence to a class-style observation journal.

*How to teach:* Measure the bean sprout and write a dated sentence in the journal.

- **Q:** Write a journal sentence.
  **A:** Example: April 3, 2026: My bean sprout is 2 inches tall.
- **Q:** What should you write first?
  **A:** The date.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read 12 two-syllable words; complete the same/different chart.

*How to teach:* Your child reads 12 two-syllable words and finishes the chart.

- **Q:** Read: basket, picnic.
  **A:** basket, picnic.
- **Q:** Read: sunset, rabbit.
  **A:** sunset, rabbit.

**Assessment:** Two-syllable word read and text comparison chart.

**Answer key:** Two-syllable words: rabbit, napkin, kitten, muffin, basket, picnic, sunset, contest, insect, puppet, mitten, tennis — proficient 11–12. Chart: proficient = 2 'both' facts and 1 'only' fact for each text that match the books.

## Math 1: Mixed practice within 100

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks, open-number-line paper, 120 chart, a die, Khan Academy login, 5 worked problems with errors (see day 4), and the Unit 7 test.

**Watch for:**
- Adding tens and ones together as if both were ones (40 + 3 = 7): build both numbers before adding.
- When an error is found, erasing without explaining: ask your child to tell what went wrong.
- Racing to 100 by adding without tracking regrouping: keep the running total on blocks.

### Day 1 (Monday): Math stations
*Student:* Rotate through block-building, number line, and 120-chart stations.

*How to teach:* Set up three stations (blocks, number line, 120 chart). Give one problem per station (e.g., 46 + 20, 38 + 5, 70 − 40); your child solves each a different way.

- **Q:** 46 + 20 = ?
  **A:** 66.
- **Q:** 38 + 5 = ?
  **A:** 43.
- **Q:** 70 − 40 = ?
  **A:** 30.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Khan practice
*Student:* Complete 'add within 100' and 'subtract tens' practice.

*How to teach:* Sit with your child for Khan Academy 'add within 100' and 'subtract tens' practice. Ask them to explain one problem aloud.

- **Q:** 52 + 30 = ?
  **A:** 82.
- **Q:** 60 − 20 = ?
  **A:** 40.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Game
*Student:* Race to 100 – roll a die, add tens or ones, record your total.

*How to teach:* Race to 100: roll a die and choose to add that many tens or ones to your total. Record each move as an equation (e.g., 34 + 5 = 39). First to exactly 100 (or closest without going over) wins.

- **Q:** You're at 74 and roll 2. If you add tens, where are you? Ones?
  **A:** 94 with tens; 76 with ones.
- **Q:** You're at 96 and roll 4 ones.
  **A:** 100.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Error hunt
*Student:* Find and fix mistakes in 5 worked problems.

*How to teach:* Show 5 worked problems with mistakes, e.g., 45 + 30 = 48; 36 + 7 = 313; 80 − 20 = 78; 29 + 4 = 23; 54 + 20 = 56. Your child finds and fixes each and says what went wrong.

- **Q:** Fix 45 + 30 = 48.
  **A:** 75; 30 is 3 tens, not 3 ones.
- **Q:** Fix 36 + 7 = 313.
  **A:** 43; 13 ones = 1 ten 3 ones, so 4 tens 3 ones.
- **Q:** Fix 80 − 20 = 78.
  **A:** 60; take away 2 tens, not 2 ones.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Unit test on addition and subtraction within 100.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 test.

- **Q:** 64 + 9 = ?
  **A:** 73.
- **Q:** 50 − 30 = ?
  **A:** 20.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 assessment.

**Answer key:** Error-hunt fixes: 45+30=75, 36+7=43, 80−20=60, 29+4=33, 54+20=74. Unit 7 test (suggested): 1) 30+50=80 2) 42+30=72 3) 63+5=68 4) 27+8=35 5) 59+6=65 6) 90−40=50 7) 70−20=50 8) 34 + 60 = 94 9) Story: 48 stickers + 7 → 55 10) Story: 80¢ − 30¢ → 50¢. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Design a nature-inspired solution

**Prepare:** Cardboard, tape, foil, sponges, paper plates, an egg carton, and a 4-item checklist (solves the problem? copies a nature part? labeled? sturdy?).

**Watch for:**
- Building without connecting to the nature part: point to which part of the design copies which animal/plant part.
- Choosing a design without a reason: explain why the shape will work.
- Giving up when it doesn't look perfect: prototypes are first tries.

### Day 1 (Monday): Two sketches
*Student:* Sketch two designs for your problem inspired by an animal or plant part (e.g., a shell-shaped egg protector).

*How to teach:* Your child sketches two designs inspired by nature.

- **Q:** Describe a shell-shaped egg protector.
  **A:** A hard, curved cover over the egg like a turtle shell.
- **Q:** Why two sketches?
  **A:** To compare and choose.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choose and plan
*Student:* Choose one design and list materials you need; explain why its shape will work.

*How to teach:* Choose one design; list materials; explain why it'll work.

- **Q:** What materials could act like a cushion?
  **A:** Sponges, cotton, bubble wrap.
- **Q:** Why does a curved shape help?
  **A:** It spreads out force.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build the prototype from household materials.

*How to teach:* Build the prototype.

- **Q:** What is a prototype?
  **A:** A first model to test an idea.
- **Q:** What if a part falls off?
  **A:** Fix it with more tape or a stronger connection.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Label
*Student:* Label the prototype's parts and write which nature part each copies.

*How to teach:* Label parts and which nature part each copies.

- **Q:** Label example.
  **A:** Foil dome = turtle shell; sponge = soft fat/fur cushion.
- **Q:** Why label?
  **A:** So others understand the design.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present the prototype to family and use a 4-item design checklist to self-check.

*How to teach:* Present to family; self-check with the checklist.

- **Q:** What does your design do?
  **A:** Answers vary — must name the problem.
- **Q:** Which checklist item needs work?
  **A:** Answers vary.

**Assessment:** Design sketches and labeled prototype.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = two sketches, a labeled prototype naming the nature part copied, and explanation of why it works; 3 = labels incomplete; 2 = only one sketch; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Immigrants in California

**Prepare:** 'The Name Jar' by Yangsook Choi (library), Calisphere photos of Angel Island Immigration Station, a world map, and yarn.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking immigrants only came long ago: people still move to California today.
- Thinking everyone came through Angel Island: Angel Island (in San Francisco Bay, 1910–1940) processed many Pacific immigrants, especially from China; others came over land or by other ports.
- Thinking immigrants must give up their culture: many keep traditions while becoming Americans.

### Day 1 (Monday): Journeys
*Student:* Read a picture book about an immigrant child (e.g., 'The Name Jar' by Yangsook Choi, library).

*How to teach:* Read 'The Name Jar.'

- **Q:** Why did Unhei want a new name?
  **A:** She worried classmates couldn't say her Korean name and would tease her.
- **Q:** What did she decide in the end?
  **A:** To keep her own name, Unhei (which means 'grace'), and teach others to say it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Angel Island
*Student:* Look at Calisphere photos of Angel Island Immigration Station and discuss what newcomers felt.

*How to teach:* Look at Angel Island photos; discuss newcomers' feelings.

- **Q:** Where is Angel Island?
  **A:** In San Francisco Bay.
- **Q:** How did some people feel there?
  **A:** Worried, lonely, scared — some were held for weeks or months and carved poems on the walls.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Contributions
*Student:* List foods, words, holidays and music that immigrants brought to California.

*How to teach:* List foods, words, holidays, music immigrants brought.

- **Q:** Name a food from immigrants.
  **A:** Tacos, sushi, pho, pizza, dim sum.
- **Q:** Name a holiday.
  **A:** Lunar New Year, Cinco de Mayo, Diwali.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Map journeys
*Student:* Trace immigrant routes on a world map.

*How to teach:* Trace immigrant routes on a world map.

- **Q:** How did people come from China?
  **A:** By ship across the Pacific Ocean.
- **Q:** How did many come from Mexico?
  **A:** Over land, from the south.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Tell how one immigrant group helped shape California.

*How to teach:* Your child tells how one immigrant group helped shape California.

- **Q:** Example.
  **A:** Chinese immigrants helped build the railroad and brought foods and Lunar New Year celebrations.
- **Q:** Another example.
  **A:** Mexican Americans brought foods, music, Spanish place names, and celebrations.

**Assessment:** Contribution explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names an immigrant group and 2 accurate contributions (e.g., Chinese immigrants built the transcontinental railroad and started Chinatowns; Mexican Americans shaped food, language, music, and holidays); 3 = 1 contribution; 2 = vague; 1 = incorrect.

## Art & Music 1: Art and music of California Native peoples

**Prepare:** Photos of Pomo baskets (Calisphere), paper plate cut as loom, yarn; recording of a California Native song.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Native art is only in the past: California Native peoples still make baskets and sing today.
- Thinking baskets were only decoration: they were used for cooking, storing, carrying and ceremonies.

### Day 1 (Monday): Baskets
*Student:* Look at photos of Pomo baskets and discuss why they are valued.

*How to teach:* Look at Pomo basket photos; discuss why they are valued.

- **Q:** Who are the Pomo?
  **A:** A Native people of Northern California known for fine baskets.
- **Q:** Why are Pomo baskets valued?
  **A:** They are tightly and beautifully woven, sometimes with feathers and shells; they carry tradition.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Weave
*Student:* Weave yarn on a paper-plate loom.

*How to teach:* Weave yarn on a paper-plate loom.

- **Q:** What is weaving?
  **A:** Going over and under to make a fabric or basket.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening
*Student:* Listen to a recording of a California Native song (PBS or Smithsonian) and describe the beat.

*How to teach:* Listen to a California Native song; describe the beat.

- **Q:** Is there a steady beat?
  **A:** Usually yes, often a drum or rattle keeps it.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare art and music from 4 cultures studied.

*How to teach:* Compare art and music from Mexico, Japan, West Africa and California Native peoples.

- **Q:** How are these cultures' art alike?
  **A:** All create art to celebrate, tell stories and use in daily life.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 share
*Student:* Present a favorite artwork from another culture and explain why people made it.

*How to teach:* Present a favorite artwork from another culture and explain why people made it.

- **Q:** Why did people make it?
  **A:** Accurate reason, e.g., papel picado to celebrate.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 cultural comparison.

**Answer key:** Cultural comparison: child presents a favorite artwork from Mexico, Japan, West Africa or California Native peoples and explains why people made it (celebration, telling stories, everyday use, honoring tradition). Proficient = names culture, describes work and gives an accurate reason.

## PE 1: Self-turned jump rope

**Prepare:** Jump rope sized to child: standing on the middle, handles reach armpits.

**Watch for:**
- Turning with whole arms: turn with wrists close to hips.
- Jumping too high or double bouncing: small single jumps once per turn.

### Day 1 (Monday): Rope sizing
*Student:* Size the rope; practice turning and stepping over.

*How to teach:* Size the rope: stand on its middle, handles reach armpits. Turn it over your head and step over it.

- **Q:** How do you know the rope is the right size?
  **A:** Standing on the middle, handles reach your armpits.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Jump and turn
*Student:* Turn, stop, jump (one at a time).

*How to teach:* Turn the rope over, let it stop at the toes, jump over. Repeat one at a time.

- **Q:** Where should your hands be?
  **A:** Near your hips, turning with wrists.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Continuous
*Student:* Try continuous jumps with a single bounce per turn; count your best streak.

*How to teach:* Try continuous jumps, one small jump per turn; count your best streak.

- **Q:** How high should you jump?
  **A:** Just a little, enough for the rope to pass.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feelings
*Student:* Talk about feeling frustrated and proud while learning.

*How to teach:* Talk about frustration and pride while learning; practice a calm breath and 'I can try again.'

- **Q:** What can you do when you feel frustrated?
  **A:** Take a deep breath, rest, and try again; say 'I'm still learning.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Count consecutive self-turned jumps.

*How to teach:* Count consecutive self-turned jumps.

- **Q:** What was your best count?
  **A:** Record child's count.

**Assessment:** Jump rope count.

**Answer key:** Record consecutive self-turned jumps. 5+ = proficient for grade 1; 2-4 = developing; 0-1 = beginning. Note effort and good attitude.

## Health 1: Fire safety

**Prepare:** Your home's smoke alarm (adult tests it), a blanket held up for 'smoke,' paper for a floor plan, and a timer.

**Watch for:**
- Hiding under a bed or in a closet during a fire: get out and stay out.
- Going back in for a toy or pet: never go back inside; tell firefighters.
- Standing up in smoke: crawl low where the air is cleaner.

### Day 1 (Monday): Smoke alarms
*Student:* Listen to the smoke alarm test and learn what to do.

*How to teach:* Listen to the smoke alarm test; learn what to do.

- **Q:** What do you do when you hear the alarm?
  **A:** Get out fast and go to the meeting place.
- **Q:** How often should adults test alarms?
  **A:** Monthly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stop, drop, roll
*Student:* Practice stop, drop (cover face) and roll, and crawling low under 'smoke' (a held-up blanket).

*How to teach:* Practice stop, drop (cover face), roll, and crawling low.

- **Q:** If your clothes catch fire?
  **A:** Stop, drop, cover your face, and roll.
- **Q:** Why crawl low in smoke?
  **A:** Cleaner air is near the floor.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Escape plan
*Student:* Draw a home floor plan with two exits from each room and a meeting place.

*How to teach:* Draw a floor plan with two exits from each room and a meeting place.

- **Q:** How many ways out of each room?
  **A:** Two (usually a door and a window).
- **Q:** Where is our meeting place?
  **A:** A safe spot outside, like the mailbox or a neighbor's tree.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fire drill
*Student:* Hold a family fire drill at the sound of the alarm and time how fast everyone reaches the meeting place.

*How to teach:* Hold a family fire drill; time it.

- **Q:** How fast should everyone get out?
  **A:** As fast as possible — aim for under 2 minutes.
- **Q:** Before opening a door, what do you check?
  **A:** Feel it with the back of your hand; if hot, use the other exit.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain the escape plan: two ways out of your bedroom, where to meet, and never go back inside.

*How to teach:* Your child explains the escape plan.

- **Q:** Two ways out of your bedroom?
  **A:** Answers vary — door and window.
- **Q:** What do you never do?
  **A:** Go back inside.

**Assessment:** Escape plan and drill.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = floor plan with 2 exits from each room and a named meeting place; drill completed; explains 'two ways out, meet at ___, never go back inside'; 3 = minor gaps; 2 = only one exit; 1 = incomplete.
