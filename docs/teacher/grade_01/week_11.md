# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 11

## ELA 1: S-blends and end blends; Three Billy Goats Gruff

**Prepare:** A public-domain 'The Three Billy Goats Gruff,' craft sticks and paper for puppets, colored pencils, and a blends check list (stop, snap, spin, skip, swim, smell, nest, hand, jump, milk, tent, best).

**Watch for:**
- Missing the final blend (nes for nest): tap each sound and check the last two letters.
- Using 'trips' with plural subjects ('The goats trips'): one goat trips; two goats trip.
- Coloring the narrator's words as a character's: only words inside quotation marks are spoken.

### Day 1 (Monday): s-blends
*Student:* Read stop, snap, spin, skip, swim, smell; tap sounds and spell 5 words.

*How to teach:* Read stop, snap, spin, skip, swim, smell. Tap sounds and spell 5.

- **Q:** Spell spin.
  **A:** s-p-i-n.
- **Q:** Read: swim, smell.
  **A:** swim, smell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): End blends
*Student:* Read nest, hand, jump, milk, tent; underline the final blend in each.

*How to teach:* Read nest, hand, jump, milk, tent. Underline the final blend.

- **Q:** What is the final blend in jump?
  **A:** mp.
- **Q:** In milk?
  **A:** lk.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Who is talking?
*Student:* Read Three Billy Goats Gruff; use quotation marks as a clue to who speaks and color each speaker's words a different color.

*How to teach:* Read the story. Show that quotation marks show talking. Your child colors each speaker's words a different color.

- **Q:** What does the troll say when he hears someone on the bridge?
  **A:** 'Who's that tripping over my bridge?'
- **Q:** What do the first two goats tell the troll?
  **A:** Wait for my bigger brother, who is much bigger.
- **Q:** What happens to the troll?
  **A:** The biggest goat butts him off the bridge into the river.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Noun-verb match
*Student:* Practice 'The goat trips' vs. 'The goats trip'; complete 6 sentences choosing the right verb form.

*How to teach:* Practice 'The goat trips' (one) vs. 'The goats trip' (more than one). Your child completes 6 sentences.

- **Q:** The goats ___ (eat/eats) grass.
  **A:** eat.
- **Q:** The troll ___ (yell/yells).
  **A:** yells.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform the story with stick puppets, changing voices for narrator and each character; blend word check.

*How to teach:* Perform the story with stick puppets, using different voices for the narrator and each character. Then read the blends list.

- **Q:** How should the smallest goat's voice sound?
  **A:** Small and light.
- **Q:** Read: best, tent.
  **A:** best, tent.

**Assessment:** Puppet show voices for narrator/characters and singular/plural verb sentences.

**Answer key:** Puppet show rubric: 4 = clearly different voices for narrator, troll, and three goats, story in order; 3 = some voice changes; 2 = one voice throughout; 1 = could not perform. Verb sentences: 1) The goat trips. 2) The goats trip. 3) The troll yells. 4) The goats eat. 5) The bridge creaks. 6) The brothers run. Proficient 5–6.

## Math 1: Comparing and three-addend problems

**Prepare:** Connecting cubes in 2 colors, 3 dice, paper for drawing models, and a printed Unit 3 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- In 'Mia has 3 more than Leo (4)', answering 3: build Leo's train, then Mia's is the same plus 3 more: 7.
- In 'Leo has 2 fewer than Mia (9)', adding to get 11: 'fewer' means Leo's train is shorter — 9 − 2 = 7.
- Adding three numbers in a fixed left-to-right order only: encourage looking for a 10 or a double first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare bigger unknown
*Student:* Solve 'Leo has 4 shells. Mia has 3 more than Leo.' using cube trains.

*How to teach:* Build Leo's 4-cube train. Build Mia's train to match Leo's, then add 3 more cubes in another color. Count Mia's: 7. Write 4 + 3 = 7.

- **Q:** Leo has 4 shells. Mia has 3 more than Leo. How many does Mia have?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** Kai has 5 cars. Lily has 4 more than Kai. How many does Lily have?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare smaller unknown
*Student:* Solve 'Mia has 9 rocks; Leo has 2 fewer.'

*How to teach:* Build Mia's 9-cube train. Make Leo's train 2 shorter by lining up and removing 2. Count: 7. Write 9 − 2 = 7.

- **Q:** Mia has 9 rocks; Leo has 2 fewer. How many does Leo have?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** A red ribbon is 8 inches; a blue ribbon is 3 inches shorter. How long is the blue one?
  **A:** 5 inches.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Three addends warm-up
*Student:* Roll 3 dice and add, looking for a make-10 or double first.

*How to teach:* Roll 3 dice. Before adding, your child looks for two numbers that make 10 or a double and adds those first, then adds the third. Record each equation.

- **Q:** Roll 4, 6, 3. Sum?
  **A:** 13; 4 + 6 = 10, plus 3.
- **Q:** Roll 5, 2, 5.
  **A:** 12; 5 + 5 = 10, plus 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Problem solving
*Student:* Mixed story problems; draw a model for each before writing the equation.

*How to teach:* Read mixed problems one at a time. Before writing an equation, your child draws a model (cube train or picture). Ask: what do we know, what are we finding?

- **Q:** Tom has 6 apples. He has 2 more than Ann. How many does Ann have?
  **A:** 4 (6 − 2).
- **Q:** 3 red, 4 blue, and 2 yellow balls. How many?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Unit test on story problems and equal sign.

*How to teach:* Give the unit test, reading every item aloud; allow cubes.

- **Q:** Is 5 + 3 = 4 + 4 true?
  **A:** True (8 = 8).
- **Q:** Nina has 8 stickers, Joe has 5. How many more does Nina have?
  **A:** 3.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test (suggested): 1) 6 + ? = 9 → 3 2) ? − 4 = 5 → 9 3) Leo has 5, Mia has 2 more → 7 4) Mia has 8, Leo has 3 fewer → 5 5) 2 + 5 + 3 → 10 6) T/F 7 = 3 + 4 → T 7) T/F 6 + 1 = 5 + 3 → F 8) 4 + 5 = ? + 6 → 3 9) Jen has 10 beads, Al has 7; how many fewer does Al have → 3 10) 10 − ? = 2 → 8. Proficient 9–10; developing 6–8.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Shadows

**Prepare:** A flashlight, small toys, a ruler, sidewalk chalk, a bedsheet, a lamp, cardboard, and craft sticks.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a shadow is a reflection or 'copy' of you: a shadow is a dark area where your body blocks light.
- Thinking shadows don't change: they change size with distance from the light and direction/length as the sun moves.
- Thinking transparent objects make dark shadows: only opaque objects make dark shadows.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shadow puppets
*Student:* Make hand-shadow animals on a wall with a flashlight and explain that your hand blocks the light.

*How to teach:* Make hand-shadow animals with a flashlight. Your child explains that the hand blocks light.

- **Q:** Why does a shadow form?
  **A:** My hand blocks the light, so the area behind it is dark.
- **Q:** Would a clear plastic bag make a dark shadow?
  **A:** No — it lets light through.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Size change
*Student:* Move a toy closer and farther from the light; measure shadow size.

*How to teach:* Move a toy closer to and farther from the light; measure shadow size.

- **Q:** What happens when the toy moves closer to the light?
  **A:** The shadow gets bigger.
- **Q:** Farther away?
  **A:** The shadow gets smaller.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Outdoor shadows
*Student:* Trace your shadow with chalk at 9 a.m. and again at noon.

*How to teach:* Trace your child's shadow with chalk at 9 a.m. and noon, standing on the same spot.

- **Q:** When was your shadow longer?
  **A:** At 9 a.m. — the sun was lower in the sky.
- **Q:** Why is it shorter at noon?
  **A:** The sun is high in the sky.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shadow puppet theater
*Student:* Make cardboard shadow puppets on sticks and perform a short shadow show behind a bedsheet lit by a lamp.

*How to teach:* Make cardboard puppets on sticks and perform behind a bedsheet lit by a lamp.

- **Q:** Where does the lamp go?
  **A:** Behind the puppets, so their shadows fall on the sheet.
- **Q:** Why use cardboard?
  **A:** It's opaque and blocks light.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why a shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why a shadow forms.

- **Q:** Explain a shadow.
  **A:** An opaque object blocks light, leaving a dark shape behind it.
- **Q:** Where is a shadow compared to the light?
  **A:** On the opposite side of the object from the light.

**Assessment:** Shadow investigation record.

**Answer key:** Record: proficient includes toy shadow measurements (closer = bigger), 9 a.m. vs. noon tracings (morning longer), and the explanation 'An opaque object blocks light.' Rubric 4–1.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: How place shapes the way people live

**Prepare:** Photos of an igloo, a stilt house, an adobe home, and an apartment (Library of Congress/Smithsonian), clothing cards (snow, rain, hot, desert), and images of California beach, Sierra Nevada, and desert.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking people everywhere live the same way: environment shapes homes, food, clothing, and travel.
- Thinking igloos are where most Arctic people live today: they were traditional temporary shelters; most people live in modern houses now.
- Judging other ways of life as 'weird': they make sense for the place.

### Day 1 (Monday): Homes around the world
*Student:* Compare photos of an igloo, stilt house, adobe and apartment; tell how each fits its environment.

*How to teach:* Compare homes; tell how each fits its environment.

- **Q:** Why are stilt houses on stilts?
  **A:** To stay above floods, water, or wet ground.
- **Q:** Why are adobe homes good in hot places?
  **A:** Thick mud-brick walls keep the inside cool.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Clothing and weather
*Student:* Sort clothing cards for snowy, rainy, hot and desert places.

*How to teach:* Sort clothing cards by place.

- **Q:** What do people wear in snowy places?
  **A:** Warm coats, boots, mittens, hats.
- **Q:** In the desert?
  **A:** Light, loose clothing and hats to block the sun.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): California places
*Student:* Compare life at the beach, in the Sierra Nevada mountains and in the desert (food, clothing, fun).

*How to teach:* Compare life at the beach, in the Sierra Nevada, and in the desert.

- **Q:** A fun winter activity in the Sierra Nevada?
  **A:** Skiing or sledding in snow.
- **Q:** What do people need in the desert?
  **A:** Water, shade, sun protection.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Getting around
*Student:* Compare transportation in snowy places (snowmobile), islands (boats) and cities (trains).

*How to teach:* Compare transportation in snowy places, islands, and cities.

- **Q:** How do people travel between islands?
  **A:** By boat or plane.
- **Q:** Why use snowmobiles?
  **A:** They move easily over snow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Assessment: locate places on maps and explain how one environment affects people's lives.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 assessment.

- **Q:** Find California on a U.S. map.
  **A:** On the west coast.
- **Q:** How does living near snow affect clothing?
  **A:** People wear warm, waterproof clothes.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test (suggested): 1) Find California on U.S. map 2) Mark the Pacific Ocean 3) Name 4 directions on a compass rose 4) Use a map key to find a park symbol 5) Point to your continent (North America) 6) Explain: people in snowy places wear warm coats and build homes to keep heat in (or similar accurate example). Proficient 5–6 with a clear environment explanation.

## Art & Music 1: Eric Carle collage; melodies go up and down

**Prepare:** Tissue paper, paint, forks, sponges, old combs, paper; 'The Very Hungry Caterpillar' by Eric Carle.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking melody and beat are the same: melody is the tune of notes; beat is the steady pulse.
- Thinking Eric Carle drew with crayons: he painted papers and cut them into collage.

### Day 1 (Monday): Eric Carle
*Student:* Read 'The Very Hungry Caterpillar' and look at the textured paper in the illustrations.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Very Hungry Caterpillar'; look closely at textured painted paper.

- **Q:** How did Eric Carle make his pictures?
  **A:** He painted papers with texture and cut them into collage pieces.
- **Q:** What does the caterpillar become?
  **A:** A beautiful butterfly.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Textured paper
*Student:* Paint papers and add texture with forks, sponges and combs.

*How to teach:* Paint papers; drag forks, press sponges and comb designs for texture. Let dry.

- **Q:** What tools did you use for texture?
  **A:** Forks, sponges, combs.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Melody direction
*Student:* Move hands up, down or same while singing 'Jack and Jill'.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Jack and Jill' moving hands up, down or same with the notes.

- **Q:** What is a melody?
  **A:** A tune; a group of notes going up, down or staying the same.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Draw a melody
*Student:* Draw lines showing melody direction for 'Twinkle, Twinkle'.

*How to teach:* Draw lines for 'Twinkle, Twinkle': up on 'twinkle, twinkle,' then down by steps on 'how I wonder what you are.'

- **Q:** Does 'how I wonder what you are' go up or down?
  **A:** Down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Share your textured papers and draw-a-melody chart.

*How to teach:* Share textured papers and melody chart.

- **Q:** Where in 'Twinkle' does the melody jump up?
  **A:** At the second 'twinkle' ('twinkle, TWINKLE').

**Assessment:** Melody direction chart.

**Answer key:** Melody direction chart: lines show 'Twinkle, Twinkle' leaping up at 'twinkle, twinkle' and then stepping down on 'how I wonder what you are.' Proficient = chart shows correct up and down direction for both lines.

## PE 1: Rolling safely

**Prepare:** Mat, rug, or soft grass. Clear area with no hard objects nearby.

**Watch for:**
- Rolling with the head lifted on the rocking chair: tuck chin to chest to protect the neck.
- Log roll with bent body: keep body straight and long like a log, arms overhead.

### Day 1 (Monday): Log roll
*Student:* Roll sideways with arms overhead like a log.

*How to teach:* Lie long with arms overhead and roll sideways like a log. Roll across the mat and back.

- **Q:** How does a log roll look?
  **A:** Body straight and long, arms over your head, rolling sideways.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg roll
*Student:* Tuck knees and roll sideways like an egg.

*How to teach:* Tuck knees to chest, hug shins, and roll sideways like an egg.

- **Q:** What shape is your body in an egg roll?
  **A:** A small tucked ball.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rocking chair
*Student:* Rock on a rounded back with chin tucked.

*How to teach:* Sit, hug knees, tuck chin, rock back on a rounded back and forward again.

- **Q:** Why do you tuck your chin?
  **A:** To protect your head and neck.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice
*Student:* Practice each roll 5 times safely.

*How to teach:* Practice each roll 5 times. Watch for chin tucks and control.

- **Q:** Which roll keeps your body straight?
  **A:** Log roll.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate two rolls with control.

*How to teach:* Have your child demonstrate two rolls with control.

- **Q:** Name the three rolls.
  **A:** Log roll, egg roll, rocking chair.

**Assessment:** Roll demonstration.

**Answer key:** Roll demonstration: performs two of log roll (straight body, arms overhead, rolls sideways in a line), egg roll (knees tucked, holding shins, rolls sideways), rocking chair (rounded back, chin tucked, rocks back and forth). Proficient = two rolls with control and no neck strain.

## Health 1: MyPlate and food groups

**Prepare:** A MyPlate printout (myplate.gov), grocery ads, scissors, glue, a paper plate divided into 5 sections, and 15 food cards.

**Watch for:**
- Putting potatoes or corn in the grains group: they're vegetables (starchy).
- Thinking beans are only vegetables: beans count as vegetables or protein.
- Thinking cheese and yogurt are protein only: they're in the dairy group.

### Day 1 (Monday): MyPlate
*Student:* Look at MyPlate and name the 5 food groups and their colors.

*How to teach:* Look at MyPlate; name 5 food groups and their colors.

- **Q:** Name the 5 groups.
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy.
- **Q:** What color is vegetables on MyPlate?
  **A:** Green.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sort
*Student:* Cut food pictures from grocery ads and sort them onto a paper-plate MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Cut foods from ads and sort them on a paper-plate MyPlate.

- **Q:** Where does bread go?
  **A:** Grains.
- **Q:** Where does chicken go?
  **A:** Protein.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kitchen hunt
*Student:* Find one food from each group in the kitchen.

*How to teach:* Find one food from each group in the kitchen.

- **Q:** Name a dairy food.
  **A:** Milk, yogurt, cheese.
- **Q:** Name a grain.
  **A:** Rice, oatmeal, pasta.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lunch check
*Student:* Check today's lunch for food groups.

*How to teach:* Check today's lunch for food groups.

- **Q:** A turkey sandwich, apple, milk — which groups?
  **A:** Grains (bread), protein (turkey), fruit (apple), dairy (milk).
- **Q:** What's missing?
  **A:** Vegetables.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort 15 food cards into groups.

*How to teach:* Sort 15 food cards.

- **Q:** Banana?
  **A:** Fruit.
- **Q:** Broccoli?
  **A:** Vegetable.

**Assessment:** Food group sort.

**Answer key:** Sort key (suggested 15): Fruits — apple, banana, orange; Vegetables — carrot, broccoli, corn; Grains — bread, rice, oatmeal; Protein — chicken, eggs, beans (also accepted as vegetables); Dairy — milk, cheese, yogurt. Proficient 14–15.
