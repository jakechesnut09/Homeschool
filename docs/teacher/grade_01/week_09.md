# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 9

## ELA 1: L-blends; The Little Red Hen

**Prepare:** A public-domain 'The Little Red Hen' (e.g., from Gutenberg), stuffed animals, l-blend cards, and a retell checklist (characters, setting, problem, events, ending).

**Watch for:**
- Dropping the second consonant in blends (cap for clap): tap one finger for each sound /c/ /l/ /ă/ /p/.
- Retelling just one part: use the 5-finger retell — one finger for each part.
- Thinking the hen was mean for not sharing: discuss fairness — the others chose not to help.

### Day 1 (Monday): l-blends
*Student:* Blend and read clap, flag, glad, plum, slip, black; tap each sound.

*How to teach:* Blend and read clap, flag, glad, plum, slip, black, tapping one finger per sound.

- **Q:** How many sounds in flag?
  **A:** 4: /f/ /l/ /ă/ /g/.
- **Q:** Read: plum, slip.
  **A:** plum, slip.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read The Little Red Hen
*Student:* Read the folktale aloud; act it out with stuffed animals, repeating 'Not I!'

*How to teach:* Read 'The Little Red Hen' aloud. Act it out with stuffed animals, everyone shouting 'Not I!'

- **Q:** What did the hen find?
  **A:** Grains of wheat.
- **Q:** What did the other animals say each time she asked for help?
  **A:** 'Not I!'
- **Q:** Who ate the bread?
  **A:** The Little Red Hen (and her chicks, in some versions) — the others did not get any.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Retell
*Student:* Retell the story using a 5-finger retell (characters, setting, problem, events, ending).

*How to teach:* Your child retells using five fingers: characters, setting, problem, events, ending.

- **Q:** What was the problem?
  **A:** Nobody would help the hen plant, cut, grind, and bake the wheat.
- **Q:** What happened in the end?
  **A:** She made the bread herself, and when the others wanted to eat it, she ate it herself.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Lesson
*Student:* Discuss the lesson (people who help get to share); write one sentence telling the lesson.

*How to teach:* Discuss the lesson: those who help get to share. Your child writes one sentence telling the lesson.

- **Q:** What is the lesson of the story?
  **A:** If you don't help with the work, you shouldn't expect to share the reward.
- **Q:** Write the lesson in one sentence.
  **A:** Example: People who help get to share.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Spelling of 8 l-blend words; oral retell scored with retell checklist.

*How to teach:* Dictate 8 l-blend words. Then your child retells the story while you score it with the checklist.

- **Q:** Spell glad.
  **A:** g-l-a-d.
- **Q:** Spell flip.
  **A:** f-l-i-p.

**Assessment:** Retell checklist and l-blend spelling.

**Answer key:** Spelling: clap, flag, glad, plum, slip, black, sled, flat — proficient 7–8. Retell checklist (1 point each): characters (hen, plus the lazy animals, e.g., cat, dog, duck), setting (a farm), problem (no one helps), events in order (plant, cut, grind/mill, bake), ending (she eats the bread herself). 5 = proficient; 3–4 developing.

## Math 1: The equal sign and true/false equations

**Prepare:** A pan balance or coat-hanger balance (hang two cups from a hanger with string), 20 identical cubes or coins, and a 10-item true/false sheet (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking '=' means 'the answer comes next' (so 6 = 6 or 5 + 2 = 2 + 5 look 'wrong'): always read = as 'is the same as' and test with the balance.
- Solving 3 + 4 = ? + 5 as 7 (writing the sum in the box): model that both sides must balance — 7 on the left, so the right must also be 7: ? = 2.
- Calling 7 = 8 − 1 false because the answer is on the left: check by computing each side.

### Day 1 (Monday): Balance
*Student:* Put 4 + 2 cubes on one side and 6 on the other of a hanger balance; discuss 'the same as'.

*How to teach:* Put 4 cubes and 2 cubes in one cup and 6 cubes in the other. Watch the hanger balance. Say 'Four plus two is the same as six,' and write 4 + 2 = 6 and 6 = 4 + 2.

- **Q:** Is 6 = 4 + 2 true?
  **A:** Yes — both sides are 6.
- **Q:** Left cup has 3 + 3, right cup has 5. Does it balance?
  **A:** No: 6 is not the same as 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): True or false
*Student:* Decide if 6 = 6, 7 = 8 − 1, 5 + 2 = 2 + 5, 4 + 1 = 5 + 2 are true; prove with cubes.

*How to teach:* Write the four equations one at a time. For each, your child builds both sides with cubes and decides true or false: 6 = 6 (true), 7 = 8 − 1 (true), 5 + 2 = 2 + 5 (true), 4 + 1 = 5 + 2 (false: 5 vs 7).

- **Q:** Is 4 + 1 = 5 + 2 true?
  **A:** False; 4 + 1 = 5 but 5 + 2 = 7.
- **Q:** Is 7 = 8 − 1 true?
  **A:** True; 8 − 1 = 7.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Both sides
*Student:* Solve 3 + 4 = ? + 5 with cubes.

*How to teach:* Write 3 + 4 = ? + 5. Put 3 + 4 cubes on the left side (7). Put 5 on the right and ask how many more to balance (2).

- **Q:** 3 + 4 = ? + 5
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** 6 + 2 = 4 + ?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** 10 = 5 + ?
  **A:** 5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write your own
*Student:* Write 3 true and 2 false equations for a family member to sort.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 3 true and 2 false equations on cards and gives them to a family member to sort. They must check the family member's answers with cubes.

- **Q:** Is 9 = 9 true?
  **A:** True.
- **Q:** Make 2 + 3 = 1 + ? true.
  **A:** ? = 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* True/false equations quiz (10 items).

*How to teach:* Give the 10-item true/false quiz. Your child may use cubes and should circle T or F.

- **Q:** Is 8 = 4 + 4 true?
  **A:** True.
- **Q:** Is 3 + 2 = 6 true?
  **A:** False; 3 + 2 = 5.

**Assessment:** True/false equation quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested) and answers: 1) 5 = 5 T 2) 3 + 2 = 5 T 3) 6 = 3 + 2 F 4) 4 + 4 = 8 T 5) 7 = 9 − 2 T 6) 2 + 6 = 6 + 2 T 7) 5 + 1 = 4 + 3 F (6 vs 7) 8) 10 − 3 = 6 F 9) 1 + 8 = 3 + 6 T 10) 9 = 9 + 1 F. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): We see when there is light

**Prepare:** A shoebox with a small peephole cut in one end and a flap cut in the lid, a small toy, a flashlight, a closet you can darken, and pictures of light sources (sun, lamp, fire, candle) and non-sources (mirror, moon, spoon).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the moon is a light source: the moon reflects sunlight; it does not make its own light.
- Thinking eyes 'send out' light to see: we see when light bounces off objects into our eyes.
- Thinking we can see a little in total darkness if we wait long enough: with no light at all, we see nothing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Light sources
*Student:* Sort pictures into light sources (sun, lamp, fire) and non-sources (mirror, moon).

*How to teach:* Sort pictures into light sources (make their own light) and non-sources.

- **Q:** Is the moon a light source?
  **A:** No — it reflects light from the sun.
- **Q:** Is fire a light source?
  **A:** Yes — it makes its own light.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Peep box
*Student:* Look into a closed shoebox with a toy inside; then open a flap to let light in and compare.

*How to teach:* Look into the closed box through the peephole, then open the flap. Compare.

- **Q:** Could you see the toy when the box was closed?
  **A:** No (or barely), because no light got in.
- **Q:** What changed when you opened the flap?
  **A:** Light came in, bounced off the toy, and into my eye, so I could see it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dark room
*Student:* Sit in a dark closet and record what you can and cannot see; add light gradually.

*How to teach:* Sit in a dark closet; record what you see. Add light gradually (crack the door).

- **Q:** What could you see in total darkness?
  **A:** Nothing.
- **Q:** What happened as light was added?
  **A:** Shapes then colors became visible.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Evidence talk
*Student:* Explain why we need light to see, using your peep-box observations.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why we need light to see, using peep-box observations.

- **Q:** Why do we need light to see?
  **A:** Light must bounce off objects and into our eyes.
- **Q:** What is your evidence?
  **A:** In the closed box I couldn't see the toy; with light I could.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw what you saw in the box with and without light.

*How to teach:* Your child draws what they saw with and without light.

- **Q:** What does the 'no light' drawing look like?
  **A:** All dark/black, no toy visible.
- **Q:** What does the 'light' drawing show?
  **A:** The toy, with light coming in through the flap.

**Assessment:** Peep-box drawings with explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = two drawings (dark box: nothing visible; lit box: toy visible) plus explanation 'Light enters, bounces off the toy and goes into my eye'; 3 = drawings and partial explanation; 2 = drawings only; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: California and the United States on maps

**Prepare:** A globe, a U.S. map, a California map, blank outline maps, and paper for nesting circles.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking California is a country: it is a state in the United States.
- Thinking the capital is Los Angeles or San Francisco: the capital is Sacramento.
- Thinking globes and flat maps show different places: both show Earth; the globe is round like Earth.

### Day 1 (Monday): Nested places
*Student:* Make nesting circles: home → city → California → United States → North America → Earth.

*How to teach:* Make nesting circles: home → city → California → United States → North America → Earth.

- **Q:** Which is bigger: California or the United States?
  **A:** The United States.
- **Q:** What continent is the U.S. on?
  **A:** North America.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): California map
*Student:* Find your city, Sacramento (the capital) and the Pacific Ocean on a California map.

*How to teach:* Find your city, Sacramento, and the Pacific Ocean on a California map.

- **Q:** What is California's capital?
  **A:** Sacramento.
- **Q:** What ocean is along California?
  **A:** The Pacific Ocean.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): U.S. map
*Student:* Find California on a U.S. map; name the states that border it (Oregon, Nevada, Arizona).

*How to teach:* Find California on a U.S. map; name the bordering states.

- **Q:** Which states border California?
  **A:** Oregon (north), Nevada (east), Arizona (southeast).
- **Q:** What country borders California to the south?
  **A:** Mexico.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Globe find
*Student:* Find the United States and California on a globe; compare the globe to a flat map.

*How to teach:* Find the U.S. and California on a globe; compare to a flat map.

- **Q:** How is a globe different from a flat map?
  **A:** A globe is round like Earth; a flat map shows Earth flat.
- **Q:** Which shows shapes more accurately?
  **A:** The globe.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label your city, California and the U.S. on blank maps.

*How to teach:* Label your city, California, and the U.S. on blank maps.

- **Q:** Label California on a U.S. map.
  **A:** On the west coast, along the Pacific.
- **Q:** Mark Sacramento.
  **A:** In northern-central California.

**Assessment:** Labeled blank maps.

**Answer key:** Labeled maps: proficient = city in the right area of California, Sacramento marked, Pacific Ocean labeled, California correctly shaded on the U.S. map, and the U.S. labeled on a North America/world outline.

## Art & Music 1: Color project: Matisse-style cut paper; rhythm review

**Prepare:** Colored paper, scissors, glue, large background paper; image of Matisse's 'The Snail' (Tate); rhythm bingo cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking collage must look realistic: Matisse's 'The Snail' uses shapes and color to suggest the spiral.
- Reading ti-ti as two separate beats: it shares one beat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Matisse look
*Student:* Look at Matisse's 'The Snail' and find warm and cool colors.

*How to teach:* Look at Matisse's 'The Snail'; find warm and cool colors and the spiral arrangement.

- **Q:** How did Matisse make 'The Snail'?
  **A:** He cut shapes from painted paper and arranged them (a cut-paper collage).
- **Q:** What shape do the pieces make together?
  **A:** A spiral, like a snail shell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cut paper
*Student:* Make a Matisse-style cut-paper composition with warm and cool colors.

*How to teach:* Cut shapes from warm and cool paper and arrange a Matisse-style collage.

- **Q:** What is a collage?
  **A:** Art made by gluing pieces of paper or materials onto a background.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rhythm game
*Student:* Play rhythm bingo with ta, ti-ti and rest cards.

*How to teach:* Play rhythm bingo: clap a card; your child finds it.

- **Q:** Which card has a silent beat?
  **A:** The one with a quarter rest.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rhythm check
*Student:* Read and clap 4 rhythm cards accurately.

*How to teach:* Have your child read and clap 4 rhythm cards.

- **Q:** Clap: ti-ti ta rest ta.
  **A:** Clap-clap (quick), clap, silent, clap.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 share
*Student:* Show the Matisse collage and use color words to describe it.

*How to teach:* Your child describes the collage with color words.

- **Q:** Describe your collage using color words.
  **A:** Uses words like warm, cool, primary, secondary, bright.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 art critique and rhythm check.

**Answer key:** Critique: names warm and cool colors in their collage and Matisse's, says one feeling. Rhythm check: claps 4 cards (ta, ti-ti, rest) accurately. Proficient = 4/4 cards + uses at least 3 color words (warm, cool, primary, secondary).

## PE 1: Balancing on body parts

**Prepare:** A mat or carpeted rug; soft surface for balances.

**Watch for:**
- Counting the hand as one 'part' when the knee also touches: count every body part touching the floor.
- Wobbling because of looking around: fix the eyes on one spot and tighten the tummy.

### Day 1 (Monday): Body part balances
*Student:* Balance on 2 feet, 1 foot, 2 hands and 1 foot, then 5 body parts.

*How to teach:* Model balances on 2 feet, 1 foot, 2 hands + 1 foot, then 5 body parts (2 hands, 2 knees, 1 foot). Count touching parts out loud.

- **Q:** How many body parts are touching in a balance on hands and knees?
  **A:** Four (2 hands, 2 knees).
- **Q:** What helps you stay still?
  **A:** Look at one spot, tighten your tummy, arms out.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Statue game
*Student:* Freeze in a balance when music stops.

*How to teach:* Play music; when it stops, call a number and your child freezes balancing on that many body parts.

- **Q:** Show a balance on 3 body parts.
  **A:** Example: 2 feet and 1 hand.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner balance
*Student:* Create a balance with a partner touching hands.

*How to teach:* Press palms together with a partner and lean to make a shared balance. Try one-foot balances holding hands.

- **Q:** How does a partner help you balance?
  **A:** They give support so you can lean or stand on fewer parts.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hold it
*Student:* Hold three balances for 5 seconds each.

*How to teach:* Hold three balances for 5 seconds each while you count.

- **Q:** Is it easier to balance on more or fewer body parts?
  **A:** More body parts is easier; a wider base of support is more stable.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show balances on 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 body parts.

*How to teach:* Have your child show balances on 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 body parts while you check.

- **Q:** What is a base of support?
  **A:** The body parts touching the ground that hold you up.

**Assessment:** Balance demonstration.

**Answer key:** Balance demonstration: holds a still balance for about 3 seconds on 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 body parts (e.g., 1 foot; 2 feet; 2 feet + 1 hand; 2 hands + 2 feet; 2 hands + 2 knees + 1 foot). Proficient = all five held still.

## Health 1: Sleep and rest

**Prepare:** Paper for a routine chart and a sleep log (bedtime, wake time). A bedtime picture book (e.g., 'Goodnight Moon').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kids need the same sleep as adults: 6–12-year-olds need about 9–12 hours.
- Thinking screens before bed help you relax: screen light can make it harder to fall asleep.
- Thinking you can 'catch up' on sleep easily: regular bedtimes are best.

### Day 1 (Monday): Why sleep?
*Student:* Learn that 6-year-olds need about 9–12 hours of sleep and why.

*How to teach:* Learn that 6-year-olds need about 9–12 hours of sleep and why.

- **Q:** How much sleep do you need?
  **A:** About 9–12 hours each night.
- **Q:** Why do we sleep?
  **A:** Body rests, grows, and brain stores learning.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sleep log
*Student:* Start a sleep log of bedtime and wake time for a week.

*How to teach:* Start a sleep log.

- **Q:** Bedtime 8:00, wake 7:00. How many hours?
  **A:** 11 hours.
- **Q:** Bedtime 8:30, wake 6:30?
  **A:** 10 hours.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bedtime routine
*Student:* Make a picture chart for a calm bedtime routine.

*How to teach:* Make a picture chart for a calm bedtime routine.

- **Q:** Name routine steps.
  **A:** Bath, pajamas, brush teeth, story, lights out.
- **Q:** Why no screens before bed?
  **A:** Screen light can keep you awake.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read a bedtime-themed picture book and discuss rest.

*How to teach:* Read a bedtime picture book; discuss rest.

- **Q:** How does the book show getting ready for sleep?
  **A:** Answers vary by book (e.g., 'Goodnight Moon' — saying goodnight to things in the room as it gets darker).
- **Q:** What helps you fall asleep?
  **A:** Dark, quiet, calm routine.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Review sleep log and explain how you feel with good sleep.

*How to teach:* Review the log; explain how you feel with good sleep.

- **Q:** How do you feel after good sleep?
  **A:** Energetic, happy, able to focus.
- **Q:** After too little?
  **A:** Tired, grumpy.

**Assessment:** Sleep log and routine chart.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = complete 7-day log with hours calculated (goal 9–12 hours) and routine chart with 4+ calm steps; 3 = log mostly complete; 2 = incomplete; 1 = missing.
