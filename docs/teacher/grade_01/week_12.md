# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 12

## ELA 1: Comparing folktales and writing a fable retell

**Prepare:** Public-domain 'The Gingerbread Man' and Aesop's 'The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse,' a story map, a 30-word blends list, and lined paper.

**Watch for:**
- Retelling with present-tense verbs: remind that past events use -ed or irregular past verbs (ran, ate).
- Comparing by retelling one story then the other: use a chart with the same questions for both.
- Thinking the Gingerbread Man is a moral fable: it is a folktale; you can still discuss a lesson (don't trust tricksters).

### Day 1 (Monday): The Gingerbread Man
*Student:* Read the tale; chant the repeating line and list everyone who chased him.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Gingerbread Man.' Chant the repeating line and list everyone who chased him.

- **Q:** What is the repeating line?
  **A:** 'Run, run, as fast as you can! You can't catch me, I'm the Gingerbread Man!'
- **Q:** Who caught him at the end?
  **A:** The fox, who tricked him into riding on his nose to cross the river and then ate him.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Town Mouse and Country Mouse
*Student:* Read the fable; tell which home you would choose and why.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse.' Your child tells which home they'd choose and why.

- **Q:** Why did the Country Mouse go home?
  **A:** In town the feast kept getting interrupted by danger (people and dogs), and he would rather eat plain food in safety.
- **Q:** What is the moral?
  **A:** Better a simple life in peace than a rich life in fear.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Compare the Gingerbread Man and the Country Mouse: where they went, what problems they had, how each ended.

*How to teach:* Fill a chart: where each character went, the problem, how it ended.

- **Q:** Where did the Gingerbread Man go?
  **A:** He ran away from the kitchen down the road to the river.
- **Q:** How did the Country Mouse's story end compared to the Gingerbread Man's?
  **A:** The Country Mouse went safely home; the Gingerbread Man was eaten by the fox.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write a retell
*Student:* Choose a favorite fable and write a 4-sentence retelling with first, next, then, finally, using past-tense verbs.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a 4-sentence fable retelling with first, next, then, finally, in the past tense.

- **Q:** Change 'The hare runs' to past tense.
  **A:** The hare ran.
- **Q:** What word begins the last sentence?
  **A:** Finally.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Blends assessment (30 words); read your retelling aloud and state the moral.

*How to teach:* Give the 30-word blends assessment. Then your child reads their retelling and states the moral.

- **Q:** Read: frog, slip, nest, crab, plant.
  **A:** frog, slip, nest, crab, plant.
- **Q:** State the moral of your fable.
  **A:** Matches the fable, e.g., Tortoise and Hare: slow and steady wins the race.

**Assessment:** Blends assessment and scored written fable retelling.

**Answer key:** Blends (30): clap, flag, glad, plum, slip, black, crab, drum, frog, grin, trip, brush, stop, snap, spin, skip, swim, smell, nest, hand, jump, milk, tent, best, sled, frost, plant, crisp, stamp, grand — proficient 27+. Retell rubric: 4 = 4 past-tense sentences in order with sequence words and correct moral; 3 = minor errors; 2 = missing events or moral; 1 = incomplete.

## Math 1: Making ten to add within 20

**Prepare:** Two ten-frames side by side (double ten-frame), 20 counters in two colors, a deck of cards (Ace–9), and 10 make-ten problems (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 13 is '1 and 3' rather than 10 and 3: show the full ten-frame plus 3 every time you say a teen number.
- When making ten for 9 + 4, forgetting to take the 1 from the 4 (answering 14): physically move one counter and see 10 + 3.
- Making ten from the smaller number instead of the bigger: start with the number closest to 10.

### Day 1 (Monday): Teen numbers
*Student:* Show 13 as 10 + 3 on a double ten-frame; do this for 11–19.

*How to teach:* Show 13 as a full ten-frame and 3 in the second frame. Say 'Thirteen is ten and three: 10 + 3 = 13.' Your child builds 11–19 the same way and writes each as 10 + __.

- **Q:** Show 16 on the double ten-frame. Write it as 10 + ?
  **A:** 10 + 6.
- **Q:** What is 10 + 9?
  **A:** 19.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Make ten
*Student:* Solve 9 + 4 by moving 1 counter to make 10 + 3.

*How to teach:* Put 9 in one frame and 4 in the other. Move 1 counter from the 4 to fill the 9-frame. Now there is 10 + 3 = 13. Have your child explain 'I moved 1 to make 10.'

- **Q:** 9 + 4 = ?
  **A:** 13 (10 + 3).
- **Q:** 9 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 15 (10 + 5).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): 8 plus
*Student:* Solve 8 + 5, 8 + 6, 8 + 7 by making ten; record your thinking.

*How to teach:* For 8 + 5, put 8 in the first frame. It needs 2 to make 10, so split 5 into 2 and 3. 10 + 3 = 13. Record the thinking as a number bond under the 5.

- **Q:** 8 + 5 = ?
  **A:** 13.
- **Q:** 8 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 14 (10 + 4).
- **Q:** 8 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 15 (10 + 5).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Practice game
*Student:* Play 'Make Ten and Go' with cards; explain moves aloud.

*How to teach:* 'Make Ten and Go': flip two cards; add them by making ten, saying each step aloud ('9 needs 1, so 7 becomes 1 and 6; 10 + 6 = 16'). The correct sum earns the cards.

- **Q:** Flip 7 and 5. Explain.
  **A:** 7 needs 3; 5 = 3 + 2; 10 + 2 = 12.
- **Q:** Flip 9 and 8.
  **A:** 9 needs 1; 8 = 1 + 7; 10 + 7 = 17.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* 10 make-ten problems with drawings.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 10 make-ten problems, drawing the ten-frames for each.

- **Q:** 9 + 5 = ?
  **A:** 14.
- **Q:** 7 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 13.

**Assessment:** Make-ten problems with drawings.

**Answer key:** Answers: 1) 9+2=11 2) 9+3=12 3) 9+5=14 4) 9+7=16 5) 8+3=11 6) 8+4=12 7) 8+6=14 8) 7+4=11 9) 7+5=12 10) 6+5=11. Drawing should show the bigger number's frame filled to 10 and the leftover ones. Proficient: 9–10 correct with drawings that show the make-ten step.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Mirrors and reflecting light

**Prepare:** 2 small mirrors, a flashlight, foil (smooth and crumpled), white paper, a target (paper circle), a clean milk carton, tape, and scissors.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only mirrors reflect: all objects reflect some light; shiny smooth surfaces reflect it clearly.
- Thinking light bends around corners on its own: light travels in straight lines; mirrors change its direction.
- Looking directly into a flashlight or reflecting sunlight into eyes: never shine light into anyone's eyes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mirror bounce
*Student:* Bounce a flashlight beam off a mirror to hit a target.

*How to teach:* Bounce a flashlight beam off a mirror to hit a target. Safety: never aim at eyes.

- **Q:** What happens when light hits a mirror?
  **A:** It bounces off (reflects).
- **Q:** How do you change where the light goes?
  **A:** Tilt the mirror.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shiny vs. dull
*Student:* Compare reflection from foil, mirror and paper.

*How to teach:* Compare reflection from foil (smooth and crumpled), mirror, and paper.

- **Q:** Which reflects most clearly?
  **A:** The mirror.
- **Q:** What happens with crumpled foil?
  **A:** It scatters light in many directions.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Periscope
*Student:* Build a simple periscope from a milk carton and two mirrors.

*How to teach:* Build a periscope from a milk carton with two mirrors at angles (help with cutting).

- **Q:** How does a periscope work?
  **A:** Light bounces off the top mirror down to the bottom mirror and into your eye.
- **Q:** What angle are the mirrors?
  **A:** Slanted at about 45 degrees, facing each other.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mirror maze
*Student:* Use two mirrors to redirect light around a corner.

*How to teach:* Use two mirrors to redirect light around a corner.

- **Q:** How many times does the light bounce?
  **A:** Twice — once per mirror.
- **Q:** Does light travel in curves?
  **A:** No, in straight lines between bounces.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw arrows showing light's path in your mirror maze.

*How to teach:* Your child draws arrows showing light's path.

- **Q:** Draw the path.
  **A:** Straight arrows from flashlight to mirror 1, to mirror 2, to target.
- **Q:** Why straight?
  **A:** Light travels in straight lines.

**Assessment:** Light path drawing.

**Answer key:** Drawing: proficient = straight arrows from the flashlight to each mirror and to the target, with arrows pointing the direction light travels, and the mirrors labeled. Rubric 4–1.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: The Pledge of Allegiance and patriotic songs

**Prepare:** A U.S. flag, printed Pledge of Allegiance, lyrics to 'My Country 'Tis of Thee' (Samuel Francis Smith) and 'America the Beautiful' (Katharine Lee Bates), and photos of American landscapes (mountains, wheat fields, coasts).

**Watch for:**
- Saying the Pledge without knowing the words' meanings: discuss each key word.
- Thinking 'indivisible' means invisible: it means cannot be divided — one nation together.
- Thinking patriotic songs are only for holidays: they express love for the country any time.

### Day 1 (Monday): The Pledge
*Student:* Read the Pledge of Allegiance line by line and discuss what allegiance, liberty and justice mean.

*How to teach:* Read the Pledge line by line; discuss allegiance, liberty, justice.

- **Q:** What does allegiance mean?
  **A:** Loyalty — promising to be faithful.
- **Q:** What does liberty mean?
  **A:** Freedom.
- **Q:** What does justice mean?
  **A:** Fairness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Words to pictures
*Student:* Draw a picture for each key word (liberty, justice, indivisible).

*How to teach:* Draw a picture for liberty, justice, indivisible.

- **Q:** What does indivisible mean?
  **A:** Cannot be divided; we stay united as one nation.
- **Q:** How could you draw justice?
  **A:** Example: a balance scale or people being treated fairly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): My Country 'Tis of Thee
*Student:* Learn the first verse and talk about 'sweet land of liberty'.

*How to teach:* Learn the first verse of 'My Country 'Tis of Thee'; talk about 'sweet land of liberty.'

- **Q:** What does 'sweet land of liberty' mean?
  **A:** A country we love where people are free.
- **Q:** What is the last line of the first verse?
  **A:** 'Let freedom ring!'

### Day 4 (Thursday): America the Beautiful
*Student:* Learn the first verse and match lines ('purple mountain majesties') to photos of American landscapes.

*How to teach:* Learn the first verse of 'America the Beautiful'; match lines to landscape photos.

- **Q:** Match 'amber waves of grain.'
  **A:** Golden wheat fields.
- **Q:** Match 'purple mountain majesties.'
  **A:** Tall mountains that look purple from far away.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Recite the Pledge and sing one song; explain one idea it expresses.

*How to teach:* Your child recites the Pledge, sings one song, and explains one idea.

- **Q:** Say the Pledge.
  **A:** I pledge allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America, and to the Republic for which it stands, one Nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all.
- **Q:** Explain one idea.
  **A:** Example: 'Liberty and justice for all' means everyone should be free and treated fairly.

**Assessment:** Recitation and explanation of one key word.

**Answer key:** Recitation rubric: 4 = recites the Pledge accurately and explains a key word (liberty = freedom; justice = fairness; indivisible = can't be divided; allegiance = loyalty); 3 = 1–2 prompts; 2 = several prompts; 1 = cannot recite.

## Art & Music 1: Collage animals; sol and mi

**Prepare:** Textured papers from last week, glue, scissors; animal photos.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking sol is lower than mi: sol is higher.
- Drawing animal details first: start with big shapes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan the animal
*Student:* Observe a photo of a real animal and sketch its shapes.

*How to teach:* Observe an animal photo; sketch its big shapes (oval body, circle head).

- **Q:** What shape is the animal's body?
  **A:** Answers vary, e.g., an oval.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cut and glue
*Student:* Build an Eric Carle-style collage animal from textured papers.

*How to teach:* Cut and glue textured papers into an Eric Carle-style animal.

- **Q:** Why use textured paper?
  **A:** It adds interest and makes it look like fur, scales or feathers.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sol-mi
*Student:* Sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' using sol-mi hand signs.

*How to teach:* Teach sol (flat hand) and mi (hand facing chest lower). Sing 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' with signs.

- **Q:** Which is higher, sol or mi?
  **A:** Sol.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make it up
*Student:* Make up a sol-mi call ('Where are you?') and have family echo it.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Where are you?' (sol-sol-mi); your child creates a call for family to echo.

- **Q:** Sing 'Hel-lo' on sol-mi.
  **A:** High then low.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Present collage animal and sing your sol-mi call.

*How to teach:* Present collage animal and sing the sol-mi call.

- **Q:** Which textures did you use?
  **A:** Names textures in the collage.

**Assessment:** Collage animal and sol-mi singing.

**Answer key:** Collage animal: built from big shapes cut from textured papers with details added. Sol-mi singing: sings 'Rain, Rain, Go Away' and their own call with sol (higher) and mi (lower) matching hand signs. Proficient = recognizable animal + accurate high-low singing.

## PE 1: Balance and roll routine

**Prepare:** Mat; paper and crayons to draw the routine plan.

**Watch for:**
- Rushing transitions: moves should flow smoothly, not with long pauses.
- Forgetting the still start/end shape: a routine needs a clear beginning and ending.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose two rolls and one balance for your routine.

*How to teach:* Help your child choose two rolls and one balance and draw them in order on paper.

- **Q:** What three moves are in your routine?
  **A:** Two rolls and one balance that match their plan.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Practice
*Student:* Practice transitions between moves.

*How to teach:* Practice moving from one move to the next without stopping long.

- **Q:** What is a transition?
  **A:** The way you move from one part to the next.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add beginning and end
*Student:* Start and finish with a still shape.

*How to teach:* Add a still starting shape and a still ending shape (like arms up in a 'ta-da').

- **Q:** Why have a still shape at the beginning and end?
  **A:** To show clearly when the routine starts and ends.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Practice the routine 5 times, holding the balance for a 5-count each time.

*How to teach:* Practice 5 times, holding the balance for a 5-count each time.

- **Q:** How long do you hold your balance?
  **A:** 5 counts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Perform the routine for family.

*How to teach:* Have your child perform the routine for family. Score with the rubric.

- **Q:** What was the order of your routine?
  **A:** Start shape, moves in planned order, end shape.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 routine performance.

**Answer key:** Routine rubric: still beginning shape; two different rolls; one balance held for 5 counts; smooth transitions; still ending shape. 4 = all parts with control; 3 = all parts, one wobble; 2 = missing a part; 1 = needs prompting throughout.

## Health 1: Fruits and vegetables

**Prepare:** 4 fruits/vegetables of different colors (e.g., red strawberries, orange carrots, green cucumber, purple grapes — cut grapes for young children), a colander, a rainbow chart, and 'Eating the Alphabet' by Lois Ehlert (library). Check for food allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking fruits and vegetables are all the same nutritionally: different colors give different vitamins.
- Washing produce with soap: rinse under running water only.
- Thinking you dislike a food after one taste: it can take many tries.

### Day 1 (Monday): Taste test
*Student:* Taste 4 fruits/vegetables of different colors and describe them.

*How to teach:* Taste 4 colorful fruits/vegetables; describe them.

- **Q:** Describe a carrot.
  **A:** Crunchy, sweet, orange.
- **Q:** Which did you like best?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wash produce
*Student:* Wash produce properly before eating.

*How to teach:* Wash produce properly.

- **Q:** How do you wash produce?
  **A:** Rinse under cool running water and rub with hands; no soap.
- **Q:** Why wash it?
  **A:** To remove dirt and germs.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rainbow chart
*Student:* Track eating red, orange, yellow, green and purple foods this week.

*How to teach:* Track eating red, orange, yellow, green, purple foods this week.

- **Q:** Name a purple food.
  **A:** Grapes, eggplant, plums, purple cabbage.
- **Q:** Name a yellow food.
  **A:** Banana, corn, yellow pepper.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read 'Eating the Alphabet' by Lois Ehlert (library).

*How to teach:* Read 'Eating the Alphabet.'

- **Q:** What does the book show?
  **A:** Fruits and vegetables for every letter A to Z.
- **Q:** Name a fruit or vegetable for A.
  **A:** Apple, apricot, artichoke, avocado, asparagus.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why we eat many colors.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why we eat many colors.

- **Q:** Why eat a rainbow?
  **A:** Different colors have different vitamins and nutrients that help our bodies.
- **Q:** How many colors did you eat this week?
  **A:** Read from chart.

**Assessment:** Rainbow chart.

**Answer key:** Rainbow chart: proficient = at least one food in each of the 5 colors during the week, plus explanation 'Different colors give our bodies different vitamins/nutrients.'
