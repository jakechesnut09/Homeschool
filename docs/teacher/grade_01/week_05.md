# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 5

## ELA 1: Digraphs sh and ch; Frog and Toad

**Prepare:** 'Frog and Toad Are Friends' by Arnold Lobel (library), sh/ch digraph cards, picture cards (ship, shell, fish, sheep, chair, cheese, chick, lunch, bench, shoe), and lined paper.

**Watch for:**
- Reading sh as /s/ + /h/: two letters, one sound — underline the pair and say /sh/ like 'be quiet.'
- Mixing ch and sh: /ch/ is a quick sneeze 'choo'; /sh/ is a long quiet sound.
- Describing characters only by looks ('Frog is green'): ask what the character does and how he acts.

### Day 1 (Monday): sh words
*Student:* Learn that s+h says /sh/; read ship, shop, fish, dish, shut and underline the digraph.

*How to teach:* Teach that s and h together make one new sound /sh/. Read ship, shop, fish, dish, shut; your child underlines sh in each.

- **Q:** What sound do s and h make together?
  **A:** /sh/.
- **Q:** Read: shop, dish.
  **A:** shop, dish.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): ch words
*Student:* Read chip, chat, much, chop, lunch; sort picture cards into sh and ch piles.

*How to teach:* Read chip, chat, much, chop, lunch. Sort picture cards into sh and ch piles.

- **Q:** Does cheese go in the sh or ch pile?
  **A:** ch.
- **Q:** Does shell go in sh or ch?
  **A:** sh.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spring
*Student:* Read 'Spring' from Frog and Toad Are Friends; list two words that describe Frog and two that describe Toad.

*How to teach:* Read 'Spring.' Make two lists: words describing Frog (eager, cheerful, patient, clever) and Toad (sleepy, grumpy).

- **Q:** Why does Frog want Toad to wake up?
  **A:** It is spring and Frog wants to play with him.
- **Q:** What does Toad say?
  **A:** He wants to keep sleeping and tells Frog to come back about half past May.
- **Q:** How does Frog get Toad up?
  **A:** He tears pages off Toad's calendar until it shows May.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Picture clues
*Student:* Reread 'Spring' and use the illustrations to tell how Toad feels when he wakes up; write one sentence with a period.

*How to teach:* Reread 'Spring' and look at the pictures of Toad when Frog wakes him. Your child tells how Toad feels and writes one sentence ending with a period.

- **Q:** How does Toad feel when Frog wakes him? What in the picture shows it?
  **A:** Sleepy and grumpy — he is in bed, pulling covers or looking cross.
- **Q:** Write a sentence about Toad.
  **A:** Example: Toad is sleepy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Dictation of 6 sh/ch words and one sentence; oral description of Frog vs. Toad.

*How to teach:* Dictate 6 words and one sentence. Then your child tells you how Frog and Toad are different using two details.

- **Q:** Spell chop.
  **A:** c-h-o-p.
- **Q:** Describe Frog with two details from 'Spring.'
  **A:** Frog is cheerful and eager; he wakes up Toad and tears the calendar pages.

**Assessment:** Dictation accuracy for sh/ch and an oral character description using two details.

**Answer key:** Dictation (6 words): ship, fish, shut, chip, much, lunch — plus sentence 'The fish is in the dish.' Proficient 5–6 words and sentence with capital and period. Oral character description: proficient = 2 accurate details, e.g., 'Toad is sleepy — he wants to sleep until May; Frog is lively — he tears the calendar pages.'

## Math 1: Make 10 and partners of numbers

**Prepare:** Ten-frame, two-color counters, a deck of playing cards (remove face cards and 10s; Ace = 1), number-bond sheet (circle with two smaller circles), and a 1-minute make-10 list for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 5 + 5 is the only way to make 10: fill a ten-frame with two colors in every way and list them.
- Forgetting 0 + 10 and 10 + 0: show a ten-frame that is all one color.
- Mixing up the whole and the parts in a number bond: the whole always goes in the top (big) circle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Ten-frame partners
*Student:* Fill a ten-frame with two colors in every way and record 1+9, 2+8 ... 9+1.

*How to teach:* Have your child fill a ten-frame with red counters then swap one at a time to yellow, recording each: 9 + 1, 8 + 2, ... 1 + 9. Ask what pattern they notice (one number goes down by 1 while the other goes up by 1).

- **Q:** 7 red on the ten-frame. How many yellow to fill it?
  **A:** 3; 7 + 3 = 10.
- **Q:** What goes with 4 to make 10?
  **A:** 6.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Go Fish for 10
*Student:* Play Go Fish asking for the card that makes 10 with yours.

*How to teach:* Deal 5 cards each. On your turn, ask for the card that makes 10 with one in your hand ('I have 3; do you have a 7?'). Lay down pairs that make 10 and say the fact.

- **Q:** You hold a 2. What card do you ask for?
  **A:** 8, because 2 + 8 = 10.
- **Q:** You hold a 5. What do you ask for?
  **A:** Another 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partners of 7 and 8
*Student:* Break 7 and 8 into parts with counters; record number bonds.

*How to teach:* Put out 7 counters and have your child split them into two groups every way possible, drawing a number bond for each (7 on top; 2 and 5 below). Repeat with 8.

- **Q:** Name all the ways to split 7 into two parts.
  **A:** 0 and 7, 1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4 (and turned around).
- **Q:** 8 is 3 and what?
  **A:** 5.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Missing part
*Student:* Solve 6 + ? = 10 and ? + 3 = 8 using a ten-frame.

*How to teach:* Write 6 + ? = 10. Put 6 counters on the ten-frame and count the empty boxes (4). For ? + 3 = 8, put 3 counters out, count up to 8, and find the missing part.

- **Q:** 6 + ? = 10
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** ? + 3 = 8
  **A:** 5.
- **Q:** 2 + ? = 7
  **A:** 5.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make-10 fluency check (1 minute) and number bond worksheet.

*How to teach:* Give a 1-minute make-10 list (e.g., 7 + __ = 10) and then the number-bond sheet. Celebrate improvement, not speed alone.

- **Q:** 1 + __ = 10
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** Number bond: whole 9, one part 4. Other part?
  **A:** 5.

**Assessment:** Make-10 fluency check and number bonds.

**Answer key:** Make-10 list answers: 9+1, 8+2, 7+3, 6+4, 5+5, 4+6, 3+7, 2+8, 1+9, 0+10 (so blanks are: for 9→1, 8→2, 7→3, 6→4, 5→5, 4→6, 3→7, 2→8, 1→9, 0→10). Number bonds (suggested): whole 7 parts 3 & 4; whole 8 parts 2 & 6; whole 6 parts 1 & 5; whole 9 parts 4 & 5; whole 10 parts 7 & 3. Proficient = 8+ of 10 make-10 facts in 1 minute and all bonds correct.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Sound makes things vibrate

**Prepare:** Plastic wrap, a bowl, rubber band, uncooked rice, a pan and spoon, a metal spoon and a glass of water, and a PBS LearningMedia clip on how ears hear.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking sound can't move objects: the rice jumps because sound vibrations shake the plastic.
- Thinking our ears 'catch' sound like a net: sound makes the eardrum vibrate, and nerves send signals to the brain.
- Changing more than loudness when testing: keep distance and pan the same; only change how hard you bang.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dancing rice
*Student:* Stretch plastic wrap over a bowl, sprinkle rice, bang a pan nearby and watch the rice jump.

*How to teach:* Stretch plastic wrap tightly over a bowl, sprinkle rice, bang a pan nearby. Watch the rice.

- **Q:** Why does the rice jump?
  **A:** The sound vibrations make the plastic wrap vibrate, which bounces the rice.
- **Q:** What happens when the pan sound stops?
  **A:** The rice stops jumping.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Spoon and water
*Student:* Strike a metal spoon and touch it to water; observe ripples.

*How to teach:* Strike a metal spoon on a hard surface and touch it to water. Look for ripples.

- **Q:** What made the ripples?
  **A:** The spoon's vibrations moving the water.
- **Q:** What happens if you touch the spoon to your hand first?
  **A:** It stops vibrating, so no ripples or sound.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Your ears
*Student:* Watch a short PBS clip about how ears hear; model an eardrum with plastic wrap.

*How to teach:* Watch the PBS clip about ears. Model an eardrum with plastic wrap.

- **Q:** What part of the ear vibrates when sound enters?
  **A:** The eardrum.
- **Q:** Where does the signal go next?
  **A:** To the brain, which tells us what we heard.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan an investigation
*Student:* Plan how to test whether louder sounds make rice jump higher; carry it out.

*How to teach:* Plan how to test whether louder sounds make rice jump higher. Keep distance the same; bang soft then loud.

- **Q:** What do you change?
  **A:** Only how loud the sound is.
- **Q:** What stays the same?
  **A:** Distance, pan, bowl, and amount of rice.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain your evidence that sound makes materials vibrate.

*How to teach:* Your child explains their evidence.

- **Q:** What is your evidence that sound makes things vibrate?
  **A:** When I banged the pan, the rice jumped; louder sounds made it jump higher.
- **Q:** Predict: what happens to rice if the sound is very soft?
  **A:** It barely moves or doesn't move.

**Assessment:** Investigation plan and evidence statement.

**Answer key:** Plan: proficient lists what changes (loudness), what stays the same (distance, pan, bowl), and what to observe (how high rice jumps). Evidence statement: 'When the pan was louder, the rice jumped higher, so sound makes materials vibrate.' Rubric 4–1 on completeness.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Representative democracy: choosing leaders

**Prepare:** Your city's website or newspaper (to find mayor and city council), paper for campaign posters, two stuffed animals, and ballots.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking leaders can make any rule they want: they must follow laws and voters can choose new leaders.
- Mixing up direct and representative democracy: direct = everyone votes on the rule; representative = people elect leaders who make rules.
- Thinking only adults' voices matter: kids can share ideas with leaders by writing letters.

### Day 1 (Monday): Representatives
*Student:* Learn that people elect a small group (city council, mayor) to make rules for many people.

*How to teach:* Explain that in big places, people elect a small group (city council, mayor) to make rules for everyone.

- **Q:** What is a representative?
  **A:** A person elected to make decisions for a group of people.
- **Q:** Why can't everyone in a city vote on every rule?
  **A:** There are too many people and too many rules.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Our leaders
*Student:* Look up your city's mayor and city council members and find one rule (law) they made.

*How to teach:* Look up your mayor and city council; find one rule they made.

- **Q:** Who is our mayor?
  **A:** Name from city website.
- **Q:** Name a city rule.
  **A:** Examples: park hours, speed limits, leash laws, recycling rules.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mock election
*Student:* Make campaign posters for two stuffed-animal candidates and have family elect a 'family representative'.

*How to teach:* Make campaign posters for two stuffed-animal candidates; hold an election for 'family representative.'

- **Q:** What does a campaign poster tell?
  **A:** The candidate's name and what they promise to do.
- **Q:** Who chooses the representative?
  **A:** The voters (family).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Make a T-chart comparing direct democracy (everyone votes on rules) and representative democracy (leaders make rules).

*How to teach:* Make a T-chart comparing direct and representative democracy.

- **Q:** Direct democracy example?
  **A:** Family votes on a movie.
- **Q:** Representative example?
  **A:** City council makes a park rule after people elect them.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Assessment: sort examples into direct or representative democracy and explain the Golden Rule.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 assessment.

- **Q:** People elect a mayor. Direct or representative?
  **A:** Representative.
- **Q:** Explain the Golden Rule.
  **A:** Treat others the way you want to be treated.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test (suggested): Sort — 1) Family votes on dinner: direct 2) Voters elect a mayor who makes rules: representative 3) Class votes on a game: direct 4) City council passes a law: representative 5) Club members vote on a name: direct. 6) Golden Rule: treat others as you want to be treated. 7) Why rules? safety and fairness. Proficient 6–7.

## Art & Music 1: Primary colors; long and short sounds

**Prepare:** Red, yellow and blue tempera, paper plates, brushes, water cup; 'Mouse Paint' by Ellen Stoll Walsh (library).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking green is a primary color: primary colors are only red, yellow and blue.
- Confusing 'long' with 'loud': long means the sound lasts longer, not louder.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mouse Paint
*Student:* Read 'Mouse Paint' and name the primary colors.

*How to teach:* Read 'Mouse Paint' and name the three paint colors the mice find.

- **Q:** What colors do the mice find?
  **A:** Red, yellow and blue.
- **Q:** What are primary colors?
  **A:** Red, yellow and blue: colors you can't make by mixing others.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mix it
*Student:* Mix red+yellow, yellow+blue and blue+red on a plate and paint the new colors.

*How to teach:* Mix red+yellow, yellow+blue, blue+red on a plate and paint each new color.

- **Q:** Red + yellow makes?
  **A:** Orange.
- **Q:** Yellow + blue makes?
  **A:** Green.
- **Q:** Blue + red makes?
  **A:** Purple (violet).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Long or short
*Student:* Sing a song and decide if sounds are long (ta-a) or short (ti-ti); use arm movements.

*How to teach:* Sing a song; use long arm sweeps for long sounds (ta-a) and quick taps for short sounds (ti-ti).

- **Q:** Is 'mooo' a long or short sound?
  **A:** Long.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sound pictures
*Student:* Draw long lines for long sounds and dots for short sounds while listening to a song.

*How to teach:* Listen to a song; draw long lines for long sounds and dots for short sounds.

- **Q:** How do you show a short sound?
  **A:** With a dot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain your color mixing chart and perform a long-short sound pattern.

*How to teach:* Explain the color chart and perform a long-short pattern (long, short-short, long).

- **Q:** How did you make purple?
  **A:** Mixed blue and red.

**Assessment:** Color mixing chart.

**Answer key:** Color mixing chart: red + yellow = orange; yellow + blue = green; blue + red = purple (violet). Proficient = all three mixes correct and labeled; developing = 2; beginning = 0-1.

## PE 1: Jogging and the gallop

**Prepare:** 6 cones, music with a steady beat; a yard or park loop for jogging. Water.

**Watch for:**
- Swinging arms across the body when jogging: arms bent, swinging forward and back like pulling a rope.
- Galloping without keeping the same foot in front: the lead foot stays in front the whole time; the back foot chases it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Jog form
*Student:* Jog with bent arms swinging front to back; jog 3 laps around the yard.

*How to teach:* Model jogging with arms bent at 90 degrees swinging front to back. Jog 3 easy laps together; stop if your child is gasping and walk instead.

- **Q:** Where do your arms go when you jog?
  **A:** Bent, swinging forward and back by your sides.
- **Q:** How fast is a jog?
  **A:** A little faster than walking but slow enough to keep going and talk.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gallop
*Student:* Gallop like a horse with one foot leading, then switch leads.

*How to teach:* Gallop like a horse: step forward with the lead foot, bring the back foot up to it, repeat. Switch the lead foot after 10 gallops.

- **Q:** In a gallop, which foot stays in front?
  **A:** The lead foot always stays in front.
- **Q:** Can you gallop with the other foot leading?
  **A:** Shows the switch with the other foot forward.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Gallop relay
*Student:* Gallop to a cone and back; switch leads at the cone.

*How to teach:* Set a cone 15 feet away. Gallop to it with one lead, switch leads at the cone, gallop back. Repeat 4 times.

- **Q:** What changes at the cone?
  **A:** You switch your lead foot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Music moves
*Student:* Jog and gallop to music, changing on the beat.

*How to teach:* Play music; jog for 8 beats, then gallop for 8 beats, switching on your call. Keep it playful.

- **Q:** What is the difference between a jog and a gallop?
  **A:** A jog alternates feet one at a time; a gallop keeps one foot leading with a step-together rhythm.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show a gallop with both lead feet and say the cues.

*How to teach:* Have your child gallop with each lead foot and say the cues. Use the cue check.

- **Q:** Tell me the gallop cues.
  **A:** Lead foot in front, back foot catches up, keep the rhythm.

**Assessment:** Gallop and jog cue check.

**Answer key:** Cue check: Jog = bent arms swinging front to back, light steps on balls of feet, eyes forward. Gallop = same lead foot in front, back foot catches up (step-together), arms relaxed; can switch to other lead foot. Proficient = shows both gallop leads and says 2+ cues for each skill.

## Health 1: Solving conflicts and stopping bullying

**Prepare:** A conflict-resolution poster (Stop, Talk, Listen, Solve), stuffed animals, and teasing vs. bullying scenario cards. Note: bullying talk may bring up your child's own experiences — listen calmly.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking every mean moment is bullying: bullying is mean on purpose and repeated, often with a power difference.
- Thinking you should fight back: say 'Stop!' firmly, walk away, and tell a trusted adult.
- Thinking telling an adult is tattling: telling to get someone out of trouble or keep them safe is reporting.

### Day 1 (Monday): Conflict steps
*Student:* Learn stop, talk, listen, solve; act it out with stuffed animals.

*How to teach:* Learn Stop, Talk, Listen, Solve; act it out with stuffed animals.

- **Q:** What do you do first in a conflict?
  **A:** Stop and calm down.
- **Q:** What comes after talk?
  **A:** Listen, then solve together.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compromise
*Student:* Practice taking turns or sharing to solve a toy conflict.

*How to teach:* Practice taking turns or sharing to solve a toy conflict.

- **Q:** Two kids want the same truck. Name a solution.
  **A:** Take turns with a timer, or play together.
- **Q:** What is a compromise?
  **A:** Each person gives a little so both are okay.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Teasing vs. bullying
*Student:* Learn that bullying is mean on purpose and repeated; sort scenario cards.

*How to teach:* Learn that bullying is mean on purpose and repeated; sort cards.

- **Q:** A kid bumps you by accident once. Bullying?
  **A:** No — it was an accident.
- **Q:** A kid calls you names every day on purpose. Bullying?
  **A:** Yes — mean on purpose and repeated.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Be an upstander
*Student:* Role-play telling a bully 'Stop!' and getting a trusted adult.

*How to teach:* Role-play saying 'Stop!' and getting a trusted adult.

- **Q:** What is an upstander?
  **A:** Someone who helps when they see bullying.
- **Q:** What can an upstander do?
  **A:** Say 'Stop, that's not kind,' help the person get away, and tell an adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Unit assessment: name feelings, show a calm-down tool, solve a conflict scenario.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 assessment.

- **Q:** Name 3 feelings.
  **A:** Happy, sad, angry (any).
- **Q:** Show a calm-down tool.
  **A:** Belly breathing, calm jar.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 assessment: 1) Names 4 feelings from face cards 2) Demonstrates a calm-down tool correctly 3) Solves a scenario ('Your brother knocked over your blocks') with Stop–Talk–Listen–Solve and an I-message 4) Says what to do about bullying (say stop, walk away, tell a trusted adult). Proficient = all 4.
