# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 21

## ELA 1: ar and or words; what is an opinion?

**Prepare:** 'Green Eggs and Ham' by Dr. Seuss (library), ar/or picture cards (car, star, farm, shark, corn, horn, fork, storm), and opinion sentence frames ('I think... because...').

**Watch for:**
- Giving an opinion with no reason: always add 'because...'
- Confusing an opinion with a fact: an opinion tells what you think or feel; others may disagree.
- Reading car as 'cair' or 'ca-r': ar is one bossy-r sound /ar/.

### Day 1 (Monday): ar words
*Student:* Read car, star, farm, park, shark; write the bossy-r pattern.

*How to teach:* Read car, star, farm, park, shark. Write the pattern: a + r = /ar/.

- **Q:** Read: farm, shark.
  **A:** farm, shark.
- **Q:** What sound do a and r make together?
  **A:** /ar/ as in car.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): or words
*Student:* Read for, corn, horn, storm, fork; sort ar/or picture cards.

*How to teach:* Read for, corn, horn, storm, fork. Sort ar/or picture cards.

- **Q:** Which card goes in or: star or fork?
  **A:** fork.
- **Q:** Read: corn, storm.
  **A:** corn, storm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Opinion talk
*Student:* Read 'Green Eggs and Ham'; tell whether Sam's friend should have tried them and give a reason using 'because'.

*How to teach:* Read 'Green Eggs and Ham.' Ask whether Sam's friend should have tried them and why.

- **Q:** What does Sam-I-Am want his friend to do?
  **A:** Try green eggs and ham.
- **Q:** What happens when the friend finally tries them?
  **A:** He likes them and thanks Sam-I-Am.
- **Q:** Should he have tried them sooner? Give a reason.
  **A:** Opinion with a reason, e.g., Yes, because he liked them and wasted a lot of time saying no.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Opinion sentences
*Student:* Write 3 opinion sentences about favorite foods using 'I think... because...'.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 3 opinion sentences about foods using 'I think... because...'

- **Q:** Is 'Pizza is the best food' a fact or opinion?
  **A:** An opinion.
- **Q:** Finish: I think apples are great because...
  **A:** Example: ...they are crunchy and sweet.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* ar/or dictation; share an opinion orally in a complete sentence.

*How to teach:* Dictate ar/or words, then your child shares an opinion aloud in a complete sentence with 'because.'

- **Q:** Spell car.
  **A:** c-a-r.
- **Q:** Spell fork.
  **A:** f-o-r-k.

**Assessment:** ar/or dictation and opinion sentence with 'because'.

**Answer key:** Dictation: car, star, farm, park, for, corn, horn, fork — proficient 7–8. Opinion sentence: proficient = complete sentence with opinion + 'because' + a reason (e.g., 'I think dogs are the best pets because they love to play.').

## Math 1: Comparing two-digit numbers

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks, symbol cards (>, <, =), a deck of cards 0–9 (Ace = 1, paper 0s), and the 12-item comparison quiz.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing ones first (thinking 38 > 42 because 8 > 2): always compare tens first.
- Reversing < and >: 'the open mouth eats the bigger number' or 'the small point points to the smaller number.'
- Reading 52 < 61 backward: read left to right: '52 is less than 61.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Compare with blocks
*Student:* Build 42 and 38; compare tens first.

*How to teach:* Build 42 and 38 with blocks. Compare the rods first: 4 tens vs 3 tens, so 42 is greater even though 8 ones > 2 ones.

- **Q:** Which is greater: 42 or 38? Why?
  **A:** 42; 4 tens is more than 3 tens.
- **Q:** Which is less: 56 or 59?
  **A:** 56; same tens, 6 ones < 9 ones.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Symbols
*Student:* Learn >, <, =; write comparisons for 10 pairs.

*How to teach:* Teach the symbols: > means greater than, < means less than, = means equal. Your child writes comparisons for 10 pairs and reads each aloud.

- **Q:** 27 __ 72
  **A:** <
- **Q:** 85 __ 58
  **A:** >
- **Q:** 40 __ 40
  **A:** =

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Card game
*Student:* Two-digit 'War' with two cards each; record with symbols.

*How to teach:* Each player flips 2 cards and makes a 2-digit number. Compare and record with a symbol (e.g., 73 > 46). Bigger number wins the round.

- **Q:** You make 35, I make 53. Record it.
  **A:** 35 < 53.
- **Q:** With cards 6 and 2, what is the biggest number you can make?
  **A:** 62.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Order numbers
*Student:* Order 5 two-digit numbers from least to greatest.

*How to teach:* Your child orders 5 two-digit numbers from least to greatest by sorting tens first, then ones.

- **Q:** Order 45, 54, 39, 61, 40.
  **A:** 39, 40, 45, 54, 61.
- **Q:** Order 88, 18, 81.
  **A:** 18, 81, 88.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Comparison quiz (12 items).

*How to teach:* Give the 12-item comparison quiz.

- **Q:** 67 __ 76
  **A:** <
- **Q:** 90 __ 89
  **A:** >

**Assessment:** Comparison quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) 34 < 43 2) 56 > 52 3) 70 > 17 4) 28 = 28 5) 61 > 59 6) 45 < 54 7) 99 > 90 8) 12 < 21 9) 80 = 80 10) 38 < 83 11) 67 > 66 12) 50 > 49. Proficient 11–12; developing 8–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Seasons and daylight

**Prepare:** 'Sunshine Makes the Seasons' by Franklyn Branley (library), a globe or ball with a pencil axis, a lamp, monthly sunrise/sunset data, and 8 season cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Earth is closer to the sun in summer: Earth's tilt causes seasons — our half leans toward the sun in summer.
- Thinking the tilt changes direction during the year: Earth's axis stays tilted the same way as it travels around the sun.
- Thinking every place has seasons at the same time: when it's summer in the northern half, it's winter in the southern half.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read about seasons
*Student:* Read 'Sunshine Makes the Seasons' (Branley) or a similar library book and list one daylight fact per season.

*How to teach:* Read the book; list one daylight fact per season.

- **Q:** What causes seasons?
  **A:** Earth's tilt as it travels around the sun.
- **Q:** Which season has the longest days?
  **A:** Summer.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lamp-and-globe model
*Student:* Walk a tilted globe around a lamp and notice that your area gets more light in summer and less in winter.

*How to teach:* Walk a tilted globe around a lamp, keeping the tilt pointed the same way.

- **Q:** When our part leans toward the lamp, what season is it?
  **A:** Summer.
- **Q:** When it leans away?
  **A:** Winter.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Season chart
*Student:* Make a 4-box chart showing daylight, weather and clothing for each season using monthly sunrise/sunset data.

*How to teach:* Make a 4-box chart: daylight, weather, clothing per season.

- **Q:** Winter daylight?
  **A:** Shortest.
- **Q:** Summer clothing?
  **A:** Shorts, T-shirts, hats.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plan the day
*Student:* Plan when you could play outside after dinner in December vs. June and explain why the plans differ.

*How to teach:* Plan outside after-dinner play in December vs. June.

- **Q:** Can you play outside after a 6 p.m. dinner in December?
  **A:** Usually not — it's dark.
- **Q:** In June?
  **A:** Yes — it stays light until about 8 p.m.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match 8 cards (long days, short days, months, activities) to the correct season.

*How to teach:* Your child matches 8 cards to seasons.

- **Q:** Match 'long days' to a season.
  **A:** Summer.
- **Q:** Match 'December' to a season.
  **A:** Winter (in the northern half of Earth).

**Assessment:** Season card match and chart.

**Answer key:** Card match: summer — long days, June/July, swimming; winter — short days, December/January, coats; spring — March/April, days getting longer; fall — September/October, days getting shorter. Proficient: 8/8 and chart filled with accurate daylight info.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Family life and work long ago

**Prepare:** A washboard or photo, LOC photos of family life long ago, a jar with a lid and heavy cream (to shake into butter), and 'Ox-Cart Man' by Donald Hall (library).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking chores long ago were easy: they took much more time and effort by hand.
- Thinking everyone had appliances long ago: washing machines and refrigerators became common in the 1900s.
- Thinking the Ox-Cart Man's family bought everything: they made or grew almost everything themselves.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chores then
*Student:* Look at photos of washing clothes on a washboard and churning butter; try shaking cream into butter in a jar.

*How to teach:* Look at photos of washboards and butter churns; shake cream in a jar until it becomes butter (about 10–15 minutes).

- **Q:** How long did it take to make butter?
  **A:** Many minutes of shaking — hard work.
- **Q:** How was laundry washed long ago?
  **A:** By hand, scrubbing on a washboard.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Chores now
*Student:* List appliances that do those jobs today.

*How to teach:* List appliances that do those jobs today.

- **Q:** What washes clothes now?
  **A:** A washing machine.
- **Q:** Where do we get butter now?
  **A:** The grocery store (made in factories).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Jobs outside
*Student:* Compare jobs of grandparents' generation with jobs today.

*How to teach:* Compare jobs of grandparents' generation with jobs today.

- **Q:** Name a job that's newer.
  **A:** Computer programmer, app designer.
- **Q:** Name a job that's been around a long time.
  **A:** Farmer, teacher, doctor.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read 'Ox-Cart Man' by Donald Hall (library) and list the family's work through the year.

*How to teach:* Read 'Ox-Cart Man'; list the family's work through the year.

- **Q:** What did the father take to Portsmouth Market?
  **A:** Things the family made and grew — wool, a shawl, mittens, candles, shingles, birch brooms, potatoes, apples, honey, maple sugar, goose feathers.
- **Q:** What did he sell at the end?
  **A:** The cart and the ox too.
- **Q:** What did he buy?
  **A:** An iron kettle, an embroidery needle, a knife, and peppermint candies.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a then-and-now chart showing 4 household jobs done by hand long ago and by machine today.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a then-and-now chart of 4 household jobs.

- **Q:** Laundry then and now?
  **A:** Washboard by hand / washing machine.
- **Q:** Cooking then and now?
  **A:** Wood stove or fire / electric or gas stove, microwave.

**Assessment:** Then-and-now chart.

**Answer key:** Chart: proficient = 4 jobs, each with a correct 'then' (by hand) and 'now' (machine): laundry (washboard → washing machine), butter (churn → store-bought), cooking (wood stove → electric/gas stove), keeping food cold (icebox → refrigerator), cleaning floors (broom → vacuum).

## Art & Music 1: Sculptures around us; instrument ensemble

**Prepare:** Photos of the Statue of Liberty and local sculptures; sketchbook; homemade instruments.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking art only lives in museums: public art is in parks, streets and buildings.
- Everyone playing at once in solos: wait for your 4-beat turn.

### Day 1 (Monday): Public art
*Student:* Look at photos of public sculptures (Statue of Liberty, local park sculptures) and discuss.

*How to teach:* Look at the Statue of Liberty and other public sculptures.

- **Q:** What does the Statue of Liberty stand for?
  **A:** Freedom; it welcomed people arriving in New York.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Neighborhood walk
*Student:* Find public art in your community and sketch one piece.

*How to teach:* Walk in your community; sketch a piece of public art.

- **Q:** Where did you find art?
  **A:** Answers vary: murals, statues, fountains.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ensemble
*Student:* Play homemade instruments together with family on a steady beat.

*How to teach:* Play homemade instruments together on a steady beat.

- **Q:** What is an ensemble?
  **A:** A group performing music together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Take turns
*Student:* Each instrument plays a 4-beat solo.

*How to teach:* Each instrument plays a 4-beat solo in turn.

- **Q:** What is a solo?
  **A:** When one person plays alone.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain why sculptures are placed in public.

*How to teach:* Explain why sculptures are placed in public.

- **Q:** Why put sculptures in public places?
  **A:** To honor people or events, make places beautiful, and let everyone enjoy art.

**Assessment:** Ensemble performance.

**Answer key:** Ensemble: plays homemade instruments with family on a steady beat and takes a 4-beat solo at the right time. Proficient = steady beat and correct solo entry.

## PE 1: Kicking a stationary ball

**Prepare:** Soccer ball (or playground ball), cones for a goal; tape mark at 10 feet.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking with the toes: use the inside of the foot for accuracy.
- Plant foot too far from the ball: place it right beside the ball, toes pointing at target.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kick cues
*Student:* Learn plant foot beside ball, kick with inside of foot.

*How to teach:* Teach: plant foot beside the ball pointing at the target, swing the kicking leg, contact with the inside of the foot.

- **Q:** Where does your plant foot go?
  **A:** Right beside the ball, pointing at the target.
- **Q:** Which part of the foot do you kick with for aim?
  **A:** The inside of the foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Target kick
*Student:* Kick into a cone goal from 10 feet.

*How to teach:* Kick into a cone goal from 10 feet.

- **Q:** What do you look at when kicking?
  **A:** The ball as you kick it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Distance kick
*Student:* Kick a stationary ball for distance 3 times and mark the best kick with a cone.

*How to teach:* Kick a stationary ball for distance 3 times; mark the best with a cone.

- **Q:** What part of the foot kicks a ball far?
  **A:** The laces (top of the foot).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Partner pass
*Student:* Pass the ball back and forth with a partner 10 feet apart using the inside of the foot; count passes.

*How to teach:* Pass back and forth with a partner 10 feet apart using the inside of the foot; count passes.

- **Q:** How do you stop a pass coming to you?
  **A:** Put your foot on or against the ball (trap it).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* 10 kicks at target; count goals.

*How to teach:* 10 kicks at the target; count goals.

- **Q:** Say the kick cues.
  **A:** Plant foot beside ball, inside of foot, eyes on ball.

**Assessment:** Kick accuracy count.

**Answer key:** Kick accuracy: goals out of 10 from 10 feet. 6+ = proficient; 3-5 = developing; 0-2 = beginning. Form: plant foot beside ball, inside of foot, eyes on ball.

## Health 1: Inside my body: organs

**Prepare:** Butcher paper, crayons, a plastic bottle, two balloons, a rubber band, scissors (adult cuts the bottle), and tape.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the heart is on the left side of the chest like a valentine: it's near the center, tilted slightly left, and shaped like a fist.
- Thinking the stomach is where your belly button is: it's higher, under the left ribs.
- Thinking the brain only thinks: it controls everything — moving, breathing, heartbeat, feelings.

### Day 1 (Monday): Body outline
*Student:* Trace your body on butcher paper.

*How to teach:* Trace your child's body on butcher paper.

- **Q:** Why trace your body?
  **A:** To draw organs in the right places.
- **Q:** Where is your head on the outline?
  **A:** At the top.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add organs
*Student:* Draw and label heart, lungs, brain and stomach.

*How to teach:* Draw and label heart, lungs, brain, stomach.

- **Q:** Where is the heart?
  **A:** In the chest, between the lungs, slightly left.
- **Q:** Where are the lungs?
  **A:** In the chest, on both sides of the heart.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Lung model
*Student:* Make a lung model with a plastic bottle and balloons; pull the bottom balloon to see the lungs fill with air.

*How to teach:* Make a lung model with a bottle and balloons; pull the bottom balloon.

- **Q:** What happens when you pull the bottom balloon?
  **A:** The balloon inside fills with air, like lungs breathing in.
- **Q:** What does the bottom balloon act like?
  **A:** The diaphragm muscle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Brain boss
*Student:* Learn the brain sends messages; play a reaction game.

*How to teach:* Learn the brain sends messages; play a reaction game (drop and catch a ruler).

- **Q:** What does the brain do?
  **A:** Controls the body, thinks, sends messages through nerves.
- **Q:** How did the brain help you catch the ruler?
  **A:** Eyes saw it drop; brain told hand to catch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Point to the heart, lungs, brain and stomach on your outline and tell each organ's job.

*How to teach:* Your child points to each organ and tells its job.

- **Q:** Heart's job?
  **A:** Pumps blood around the body.
- **Q:** Stomach's job?
  **A:** Breaks down (digests) food.

**Assessment:** Body outline with organs.

**Answer key:** Outline rubric: 4 = brain (head; controls body), heart (chest; pumps blood), lungs (chest, both sides; breathe air in and out), stomach (upper belly, left side; digests food) drawn in correct places with correct jobs; 3 = one error; 2 = two; 1 = several.
