# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 27

## ELA 1: Inflectional endings -s, -ed, -ing; prefixes un- and re-

**Prepare:** Word-building cards (roots, -s, -es, -ed, -ing, un-, re-), the sprouting bean, and a short passage with root words (e.g., 'We planted seeds. The plants are growing. I am watering them. Dad replants the beans that fell.').

**Watch for:**
- Adding -s to words ending in x, s, sh, ch: add -es (boxes, wishes).
- Thinking -ed always says /ed/: it can say /t/ (jumped), /d/ (played), or /ed/ (planted).
- Thinking un- and re- mean the same: un- means not (or opposite), re- means again.

### Day 1 (Monday): -s and -es
*Student:* Add -s or -es to nouns and verbs (plants, boxes, grows); read in sentences.

*How to teach:* Add -s or -es to nouns and verbs: plant → plants, box → boxes, grow → grows. Read in sentences.

- **Q:** Add -s or -es to box.
  **A:** boxes.
- **Q:** Add -s to grow.
  **A:** grows.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): -ed sounds
*Student:* Sort -ed words by sound /t/, /d/, /ed/ (jumped, played, planted).

*How to teach:* Sort -ed words by sound: /t/ jumped, /d/ played, /ed/ planted.

- **Q:** What sound does -ed make in jumped?
  **A:** /t/.
- **Q:** In planted?
  **A:** /ed/.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): -ing
*Student:* Read planting, growing, watering; find the root word in each.

*How to teach:* Read planting, growing, watering. Find the root word.

- **Q:** Root word of growing?
  **A:** grow.
- **Q:** Root word of watering?
  **A:** water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): un- and re-
*Student:* Read unhappy, unlock, redo, replant; tell what each prefix means and draw one.

*How to teach:* Read unhappy, unlock, redo, replant. Tell each prefix's meaning and draw one.

- **Q:** What does unhappy mean?
  **A:** Not happy.
- **Q:** What does replant mean?
  **A:** Plant again.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Root-word hunt in a short passage; endings and prefix quiz.

*How to teach:* Hunt for root words in a passage, then a quiz.

- **Q:** Root of replants?
  **A:** plant.
- **Q:** Root of planted?
  **A:** plant.

**Assessment:** Inflectional-ending and prefix quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) fox + es = foxes 2) dog + s = dogs 3) jumped /t/ 4) played /d/ 5) wanted /ed/ 6) root of helping = help 7) unlock = not locked / open 8) redo = do again 9) unkind = not kind 10) reread = read again. Proficient 9–10.

## Math 1: Comparing and ordering lengths

**Prepare:** Pencils and crayons of different lengths, string or yarn, scissors, 3 ribbons of different lengths, household objects.

**Watch for:**
- Comparing without lining up ends (an object looks longer because it sticks out on one side): always line up the ends on a table edge.
- Thinking the string must be the same length as both objects: the string 'stands in' for the first object; cut or mark it, then compare to the second.
- Ordering by thickness or size instead of length: point along the long side only.

### Day 1 (Monday): Line up
*Student:* Order 3 pencils/crayons from shortest to longest.

*How to teach:* Give 3 pencils or crayons. Line up their bottom ends on the table edge and order shortest to longest. Ask your child to use the words shortest, longer, longest.

- **Q:** Which is longest, and how do you know?
  **A:** The one that sticks out farthest when the ends are lined up.
- **Q:** If A is longer than B and B is longer than C, which is shortest?
  **A:** C.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Direct compare
*Student:* Compare household objects by lining up ends.

*How to teach:* Compare pairs of household objects (spoon vs fork, book vs remote) by lining up ends. Your child says '___ is longer than ___.'

- **Q:** Why must you line up the ends?
  **A:** So you only compare how far each one reaches; otherwise the comparison isn't fair.
- **Q:** Which is longer, a spoon or a crayon (usually)?
  **A:** A spoon.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Indirect compare
*Student:* Use a string to compare the length of a door and a table that can't be moved.

*How to teach:* You can't move the door or table, so stretch string along the table's length and cut/mark it. Hold the string against the door width. Ask which is longer.

- **Q:** How does the string help?
  **A:** It copies the table's length so we can compare it with the door.
- **Q:** The string is longer than the door's width. What does that mean?
  **A:** The table is longer than the door is wide.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Length hunt
*Student:* Find 3 things longer and 3 shorter than your arm.

*How to teach:* Your child finds 3 things longer and 3 shorter than their arm by holding them alongside their arm, and draws them.

- **Q:** Name something longer than your arm.
  **A:** Example: a broom, a couch, a bed.
- **Q:** Something shorter?
  **A:** Example: a spoon, a crayon.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Order 3 ribbons by length and use a string to decide whether a couch or a bed is longer; explain your method.

*How to teach:* Your child orders 3 ribbons and uses a string to compare a couch and a bed, explaining their method.

- **Q:** Explain how you compared the couch and the bed.
  **A:** Stretch string along the couch, mark it, then hold it along the bed; whichever is longer than the string is longer.
- **Q:** Which ribbon goes first in shortest to longest?
  **A:** The shortest one.

**Assessment:** Comparing lengths performance task.

**Answer key:** Performance task rubric: 4 = lines up ends to order 3 ribbons correctly AND uses string to compare couch and bed with a clear explanation ('the string was the couch's length; the bed was longer than the string'); 3 = correct ordering and comparison with prompting; 2 = orders ribbons but cannot do indirect comparison; 1 = does not line up ends.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Test, compare and improve designs

**Prepare:** Prototypes, raw eggs or water balloons, a ruler/measuring tape, a timer, a tarp or towel for mess, and a data table. Do egg drops outside.

**Watch for:**
- Changing height and design together: test both designs from the same heights.
- Running only one trial: run 3 trials to be sure.
- Thinking a broken egg means the project failed: data tells what to improve.

### Day 1 (Monday): Test design A
*Student:* Run 3 trials with your first design (e.g., drop the protected egg from 30 cm, 60 cm, 90 cm) and record results.

*How to teach:* Run 3 trials with design A (30, 60, 90 cm).

- **Q:** Why the same heights for both designs?
  **A:** To keep the test fair.
- **Q:** What do you record?
  **A:** Whether the egg broke at each height.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test design B
*Student:* Build a quick version of your second sketch and run the same 3 trials.

*How to teach:* Build a quick version of design B; same trials.

- **Q:** What stays the same?
  **A:** Heights, egg size, landing surface.
- **Q:** What changes?
  **A:** Only the design.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Make a T-chart of strengths and weaknesses for each design using your data.

*How to teach:* Make a T-chart of strengths and weaknesses.

- **Q:** If A survived all 3 drops and B only 1, which is better?
  **A:** A.
- **Q:** Name a strength.
  **A:** Example: lots of padding.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Change one thing on the better design and test it again.

*How to teach:* Change one thing on the better design; retest.

- **Q:** Why one thing?
  **A:** So you know what made the difference.
- **Q:** What could you change?
  **A:** Add padding, a parachute, a wider base.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write or dictate what you changed, why, and whether the data improved.

*How to teach:* Your child explains what they changed, why, and if data improved.

- **Q:** Did it improve? How do you know?
  **A:** Compare the new results to the old.
- **Q:** What would you try next?
  **A:** Answers vary.

**Assessment:** Test data table and improvement reflection.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = complete data table for both designs (3 trials each), T-chart, one change tested, and reflection that cites data; 3 = data and reflection with gaps; 2 = incomplete data; 1 = no data.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Folklore from many cultures

**Prepare:** 'Lon Po Po' by Ed Young, a public-domain Anansi tale (e.g., 'Anansi and the Pot of Wisdom' / how wisdom spread), 'The Mitten' by Jan Brett (library), and a chart. Preview 'Lon Po Po': the wolf dies at the end, which may scare some children.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking folktales are true stories: folktales are passed down to teach lessons.
- Thinking cultures have nothing in common: many folktales share lessons (cleverness, kindness, sharing).
- Thinking Lon Po Po is just Little Red Riding Hood copied: it's the Chinese version with its own details.

### Day 1 (Monday): Anansi
*Student:* Read an Anansi the Spider tale (West Africa/Caribbean) and discuss the lesson.

*How to teach:* Read an Anansi tale and discuss its lesson.

- **Q:** Where do Anansi stories come from?
  **A:** West Africa (the Ashanti people), carried to the Caribbean.
- **Q:** In 'Anansi and the Pot of Wisdom,' what happens?
  **A:** Anansi tries to hide all the world's wisdom in a pot at the top of a tree, but it falls and breaks, spreading wisdom everywhere.
- **Q:** Lesson?
  **A:** No one has all the wisdom; listen to others (even children).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Lon Po Po
*Student:* Read 'Lon Po Po' (China) and compare it with Little Red Riding Hood.

*How to teach:* Read 'Lon Po Po'; compare with Little Red Riding Hood.

- **Q:** How are the stories alike?
  **A:** A wolf pretends to be the grandmother.
- **Q:** How are they different?
  **A:** In Lon Po Po, three sisters (led by Shang) trick the wolf themselves by getting him to climb into a basket to the top of a ginkgo tree and dropping him; no hunter saves them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Mitten
*Student:* Read 'The Mitten' (Ukraine) and identify cultural details in the clothing and setting.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Mitten'; identify cultural details.

- **Q:** What happens in the story?
  **A:** Nicki loses a white mitten; many animals squeeze inside until the bear sneezes and it flies away; Nicki finds it stretched.
- **Q:** What cultural details show Ukraine?
  **A:** Embroidered clothing, Baba (grandmother), village houses, decorated borders.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Make a chart comparing the lessons of the three tales.

*How to teach:* Make a chart comparing the lessons.

- **Q:** Lon Po Po lesson?
  **A:** Be clever and work together; be careful with strangers.
- **Q:** The Mitten lesson?
  **A:** Sharing space / there's always room for one more — until there isn't! (also: take care of your things).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain what folktales tell us about a culture's values.

*How to teach:* Your child explains what folktales tell about a culture's values.

- **Q:** What do folktales tell us?
  **A:** What a culture thinks is important, like cleverness, family, or sharing.
- **Q:** Give an example.
  **A:** Lon Po Po shows children respecting family and being brave and smart.

**Assessment:** Folktale comparison chart.

**Answer key:** Chart: proficient = each tale's culture (Anansi — West Africa/Caribbean; Lon Po Po — China; The Mitten — Ukraine), a correct lesson for each, and one shared value (e.g., cleverness, family, sharing).

## Art & Music 1: Landscapes; loud and soft

**Prepare:** Watercolors, brushes, paper; Haydn's 'Surprise Symphony' (Symphony No. 94, 2nd movement).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'piano' only means the instrument: in music it also means soft.
- Drawing the horizon at the very top: it is where sky meets land, often in the middle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Observe outside
*Student:* Sketch the view from a window: sky, horizon, ground.

*How to teach:* Sketch the window view: sky, horizon, ground.

- **Q:** What is a horizon?
  **A:** The line where the sky seems to meet the land or sea.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Landscape
*Student:* Paint the landscape with watercolors.

*How to teach:* Paint the landscape with watercolors (sky first, then ground).

- **Q:** What is a landscape?
  **A:** A picture of outdoor scenery.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Surprise Symphony
*Student:* Listen to Haydn's 'Surprise Symphony' and jump at the loud surprise.

*How to teach:* Listen to the 'Surprise Symphony'; jump at the loud chord.

- **Q:** What happened in the music?
  **A:** Quiet music suddenly had a very loud chord.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dynamics game
*Student:* Sing a song softly (piano) and loudly (forte) on cue.

*How to teach:* Sing softly (piano) and loudly (forte) on cue.

- **Q:** What does 'forte' mean?
  **A:** Loud.
- **Q:** What does 'piano' mean in music?
  **A:** Soft.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain your landscape and why Haydn used a surprise.

*How to teach:* Explain your landscape and why Haydn used a surprise.

- **Q:** Why did Haydn add a surprise?
  **A:** To wake up and surprise the audience and make the music exciting.

**Assessment:** Landscape painting and dynamics.

**Answer key:** Landscape: shows sky, horizon line and ground. Dynamics: sings softly (piano) and loudly (forte) on cue. Proficient = horizon visible + correct responses to cues.

## PE 1: Rhythm and dance

**Prepare:** Music with clear beats; GoNoodle video access.

**Watch for:**
- Moving off the beat: clap the beat first, then step to the claps.
- Doing a different number of counts per move: count out loud '1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Beat moves
*Student:* Clap and step side-to-side to the beat of a song, then march and clap together.

*How to teach:* Clap and step side to side to the beat, then march and clap together.

- **Q:** What is a beat?
  **A:** The steady pulse in music you can clap or tap to.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Folk dance
*Student:* Learn the Hokey Pokey and Chicken Dance.

*How to teach:* Learn the Hokey Pokey and the Chicken Dance.

- **Q:** In the Hokey Pokey, what do you do with your right foot?
  **A:** Put it in, take it out, put it in, shake it all about.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): GoNoodle
*Student:* Follow a GoNoodle dance video, then do it again with bigger, cleaner moves.

*How to teach:* Follow a GoNoodle dance; repeat with bigger, cleaner moves.

- **Q:** What makes a move 'clean'?
  **A:** Clear, strong and on the beat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Create
*Student:* Create an 8-count dance with 4 different moves (step, clap, turn, jump).

*How to teach:* Create an 8-count dance with 4 different moves (2 counts each).

- **Q:** How many counts in your dance?
  **A:** 8 counts.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform your 8-count dance 4 times in a row to music.

*How to teach:* Perform the 8-count dance 4 times to music.

- **Q:** What is a sequence?
  **A:** Moves done in a set order.

**Assessment:** Dance performance.

**Answer key:** Dance rubric: 8-count dance with 4 moves (step, clap, turn, jump), performed 4 times in a row on the beat. 4 = on beat all 4 times; 3 = mostly on beat; 2 = moves present but off beat; 1 = needs prompting.

## Health 1: Emergencies and 911

**Prepare:** A toy phone, an address card, and situation cards (fire, someone not breathing, a stranger trying to get in vs. a scraped knee, lost toy).

**Watch for:**
- Calling 911 for non-emergencies: 911 is for fires, serious injuries, and danger.
- Hanging up early: stay on the line until the operator says to hang up.
- Not knowing your address: practice it every day.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is an emergency?
*Student:* Sort situations into emergency and not.

*How to teach:* Sort situations into emergency and not.

- **Q:** A fire in the kitchen?
  **A:** Emergency — call 911 after getting out.
- **Q:** A lost toy?
  **A:** Not an emergency.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Learn address
*Student:* Memorize home address and a parent's phone number.

*How to teach:* Memorize home address and a parent's phone number.

- **Q:** Say your address.
  **A:** Answers vary — check accuracy.
- **Q:** Say a parent's phone number.
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Pretend call
*Student:* Practice a pretend 911 call with a toy phone.

*How to teach:* Practice a pretend 911 call.

- **Q:** What do you say?
  **A:** Your name, your address, what's wrong.
- **Q:** Who do you talk to?
  **A:** The 911 dispatcher/operator.

### Day 4 (Thursday): First aid
*Student:* Learn to tell an adult when someone is hurt.

*How to teach:* Learn to tell an adult when someone is hurt.

- **Q:** Your friend falls and is bleeding. What do you do?
  **A:** Get a trusted adult right away.
- **Q:** Who do you call if no adult is around and someone won't wake up?
  **A:** 911.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Recite address and role-play a 911 call.

*How to teach:* Your child recites address and role-plays a 911 call.

- **Q:** When can you hang up?
  **A:** When the operator says to.
- **Q:** Is calling 911 for a joke okay?
  **A:** No — it can keep help from people who need it.

**Assessment:** 911 role-play.

**Answer key:** Role-play checklist: knows when to call (real emergency); dials 911; says name; says full address; explains the problem; stays on the line. Proficient = all 6 plus correct address and phone number.
