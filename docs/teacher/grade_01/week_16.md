# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 16

## ELA 1: Writing an animal report

**Prepare:** Fact cards from week 15, a stapled report booklet (cover + 4 pages), crayons, and a 20-word silent-e reading list.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a topic sentence that is a fact detail: the topic sentence tells what the whole report is about.
- Fact sentences without capitals or end marks: check each sentence with a finger.
- Diagram labels without lines: draw a line from each label to the part.

### Day 1 (Monday): Topic sentence
*Student:* Write a topic sentence for your animal report ('Owls are birds that hunt at night.').

*How to teach:* Model a topic sentence: 'Owls are birds that hunt at night.' Your child writes one for their animal.

- **Q:** What does a topic sentence do?
  **A:** Tells the reader what the report is about.
- **Q:** Write a topic sentence about frogs.
  **A:** Example: Frogs are amphibians that live near water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Facts
*Student:* Turn 3 fact cards into 3 complete sentences in the report booklet, each with a capital and end mark.

*How to teach:* Your child turns 3 fact cards into 3 complete sentences, each with a capital and end mark.

- **Q:** Turn 'eat bugs' into a sentence about frogs.
  **A:** Frogs eat bugs.
- **Q:** What does every sentence need?
  **A:** A capital at the start and an end mark.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Closing & diagram
*Student:* Write a closing sentence and draw a labeled diagram of the animal with 4 labels.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a closing sentence and draws the animal with 4 labels.

- **Q:** Write a closing sentence for a frog report.
  **A:** Example: Frogs are amazing animals.
- **Q:** Name 4 labels for a frog diagram.
  **A:** Example: eyes, mouth, front legs, back legs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Read the report to a family member; add one describing adjective to each fact sentence.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the report to a family member, then adds one adjective to each fact sentence.

- **Q:** Add an adjective: 'Frogs have legs.'
  **A:** Example: Frogs have strong legs.
- **Q:** What is an adjective?
  **A:** A word that describes a noun.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Present the report aloud; long-vowel silent-e reading assessment (20 words).

*How to teach:* Your child presents the report aloud. Then give the 20-word silent-e reading assessment.

- **Q:** Read: cape, kite, rope, mule.
  **A:** cape, kite, rope, mule.
- **Q:** Read: these, plane.
  **A:** these, plane.

**Assessment:** Animal report scored with a simple informative rubric (topic, 3 facts, closing, conventions).

**Answer key:** Informative rubric: 4 = topic sentence, 3 accurate fact sentences, closing sentence, labeled diagram, capitals/end marks throughout; 3 = all parts with a few errors; 2 = missing a part; 1 = incomplete. Silent-e list (20): cape, tape, lake, game, made, bike, kite, time, ride, five, home, rope, bone, nose, stone, cube, tune, mule, these, Pete — proficient 18+.

## Math 1: Sorting and organizing data

**Prepare:** A bag of mixed buttons (or LEGO, toy cars), chart paper, crayons, and a simple pre-made picture graph for Friday (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Making tally marks as 5 straight lines instead of 4 with a slash: model 'four standing, the fifth lies across.'
- Drawing picture-graph pictures different sizes so rows look unequal: use one sticker or same-size square per vote, lined up.
- Answering 'how many more' by giving one category's total: compare the two rows and count the extras.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sort it
*Student:* Sort a bag of buttons by color, then by number of holes; name your categories.

*How to teach:* Dump a bag of buttons and ask 'How could we sort these?' Sort by color first and label each group. Then mix and sort again by number of holes (2 or 4). Discuss that the same things can be sorted different ways.

- **Q:** What did we sort by first? Second?
  **A:** Color; number of holes.
- **Q:** Name one category you made.
  **A:** Example: red buttons / buttons with 2 holes.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tally
*Student:* Record sorted buttons with tally marks in a three-row chart.

*How to teach:* Make a 3-row chart with category names. Your child records each sorted button with a tally mark, bundling fives. Then count each row.

- **Q:** Show 7 with tally marks.
  **A:** One bundle of 5 (4 lines with a slash) and 2 more lines.
- **Q:** A row has 2 bundles of 5 and 1 more. How many?
  **A:** 11.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Picture graph
*Student:* Make a picture graph of favorite fruits from a family survey (3 choices).

*How to teach:* Survey family about favorite fruit (apple, banana, orange). Your child draws one fruit picture per vote in rows, same size and lined up, and adds a title.

- **Q:** Why should each picture be the same size?
  **A:** So you can compare rows fairly; one picture = one vote.
- **Q:** What does a graph need besides pictures?
  **A:** A title and labels for each category.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ask questions
*Student:* Write questions about the graph: how many in all? how many more chose apples than bananas?

*How to teach:* Look at the fruit graph and have your child ask and answer questions: How many in all? Which is most? How many more chose apples than bananas?

- **Q:** Apples 6, bananas 3, oranges 4. How many people in all?
  **A:** 13.
- **Q:** How many more chose apples than bananas?
  **A:** 3.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read a given graph and answer 5 questions.

*How to teach:* Give a pre-made picture graph (e.g., Pets: dogs 7, cats 5, fish 2) and ask 5 questions.

- **Q:** Which pet is least popular?
  **A:** Fish (2).
- **Q:** How many more dogs than cats?
  **A:** 2.

**Assessment:** Graph reading questions.

**Answer key:** Graph (suggested): Favorite Pets — dog 7, cat 5, fish 2. Questions & answers: 1) How many chose dog? 7 2) Which is most popular? dog 3) How many in all? 14 4) How many more chose dog than fish? 5 5) How many fewer chose cat than dog? 2. Proficient 5/5; 4 = developing.

## Science 1 (NGSS): The moon's changing shape

**Prepare:** A moon journal (calendar page stapled to paper), sandwich cookies and a plastic knife, a paper plate, 'The Moon Book' by Gail Gibbons (library), and 6 moon-phase picture cards.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the moon changes shape: the moon is always round; we see different amounts of its sunlit side.
- Thinking the Earth's shadow causes phases: phases come from how much of the lit half faces us (Earth's shadow causes eclipses, which are rare).
- Thinking the moon only comes out at night: it is often visible during the day.

### Day 1 (Monday): Start a moon journal
*Student:* Go outside after dinner and draw the moon's shape and position in a calendar-style journal; continue nightly for four weeks.

*How to teach:* Go outside after dinner. Draw the moon's shape and position. Continue nightly for 4 weeks (skip cloudy nights and note them).

- **Q:** What shape was the moon tonight?
  **A:** Answers vary — check against a moon calendar.
- **Q:** Why write the date?
  **A:** To see the pattern over time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Read The Moon Book
*Student:* Read Gail Gibbons' 'The Moon Book' and list the names of 4 moon shapes (phases).

*How to teach:* Read 'The Moon Book' and list 4 moon phases.

- **Q:** Name 4 phases.
  **A:** New moon, crescent, first/last quarter (half), full moon (also gibbous).
- **Q:** Where does moonlight come from?
  **A:** Sunlight reflecting off the moon.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cookie phases
*Student:* Scrape frosting from sandwich cookies to model new, crescent, half and full moons; label them on a paper plate.

*How to teach:* Scrape frosting from cookies to model new, crescent, half, and full moons; label.

- **Q:** What does a new moon look like?
  **A:** Dark — we can't see the lit side.
- **Q:** A full moon?
  **A:** A completely lit circle.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Daytime moon
*Student:* Look for the moon in the morning or afternoon sky and record the time and shape; discuss why it can be seen in daytime.

*How to teach:* Look for the moon in daytime; record time and shape; discuss why.

- **Q:** Why can we see the moon in daytime?
  **A:** It reflects sunlight and is bright enough to see when it's above the horizon during the day.
- **Q:** Is it there every day?
  **A:** Not every day at the same time; its rise time changes daily.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Put 6 moon-phase picture cards in order and compare them with your journal so far.

*How to teach:* Order 6 phase cards; compare with the journal.

- **Q:** Order: full, new, crescent, half, gibbous.
  **A:** New, crescent, half (first quarter), gibbous, full.
- **Q:** After full, what happens?
  **A:** The lit part shrinks (waning).

**Assessment:** Moon-phase card order and journal entries.

**Answer key:** Card order (waxing): new moon → waxing crescent → first quarter (half) → waxing gibbous → full moon → waning (gibbous, last quarter, crescent). Proficient = cards in correct order and journal has entries on most clear nights.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Heroes behind our holidays: Martin Luther King Jr.

**Prepare:** 'Martin's Big Words' by Doreen Rappaport (library) and a Library of Congress photo of the 1963 March on Washington. Preview: the book mentions segregation and Dr. King's death in simple terms; be ready to answer questions gently.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Dr. King used fighting to make change: he taught peaceful protest and words.
- Thinking unfair laws were only long ago and everywhere: segregation laws were in many places (especially the South) and changed through the Civil Rights Movement.
- Thinking MLK Day is his birthday exactly: it's the third Monday in January, near his birthday (January 15).

### Day 1 (Monday): Read
*Student:* Read 'Martin's Big Words' and list 3 'big words' Dr. King used.

*How to teach:* Read 'Martin's Big Words' and list 3 'big words' Dr. King used.

- **Q:** Name some big words from the book.
  **A:** Examples: love, freedom, together, equal.
- **Q:** What did Dr. King want?
  **A:** Equal rights and fair treatment for all people, especially Black Americans.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Primary source
*Student:* Look at a Library of Congress photo of the 1963 March on Washington; describe what you see and wonder.

*How to teach:* Study the LOC photo of the March on Washington: see, think, wonder.

- **Q:** What do you see?
  **A:** Huge crowds of people, signs, the Lincoln Memorial or Washington Monument.
- **Q:** Why did so many people gather?
  **A:** To ask for jobs and freedom and equal rights; Dr. King gave his 'I Have a Dream' speech there.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Dream cloud
*Student:* Write and illustrate your own dream for a fairer world.

*How to teach:* Your child writes and illustrates their dream for a fairer world.

- **Q:** Example dream.
  **A:** I have a dream that everyone is treated kindly no matter how they look.
- **Q:** How does it connect to Dr. King?
  **A:** He dreamed of fairness and equality.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peaceful problem-solving
*Student:* Role-play solving a disagreement with words, like Dr. King taught.

*How to teach:* Role-play solving a disagreement with words.

- **Q:** How would Dr. King solve a disagreement?
  **A:** Peacefully, by talking and listening, not fighting.
- **Q:** What words help?
  **A:** 'I feel... when... Can we...?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Tell why we celebrate Martin Luther King Jr. Day in January and name one thing Dr. King worked for.

*How to teach:* Your child tells why we celebrate MLK Day and one thing Dr. King worked for.

- **Q:** When is MLK Day?
  **A:** The third Monday in January.
- **Q:** What did he work for?
  **A:** Equal rights, fairness, peace.

**Assessment:** Dream writing and oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = dream writing connects to fairness/equality and explanation says we honor Dr. King for working peacefully for equal rights (civil rights) on the third Monday in January; 3 = mostly correct; 2 = vague; 1 = incorrect.

## Art & Music 1: Songs and art of winter celebrations

**Prepare:** Paper strips, glue or tape; lyrics for 'Dreidel Song,' 'Feliz Navidad,' 'Jingle Bells.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking everyone celebrates the same holiday: families celebrate many traditions.
- Thinking audiences can talk during performances: listen quietly and clap at the end.

### Day 1 (Monday): Songs
*Student:* Learn 'Dreidel Song' and 'Feliz Navidad'; talk about where they come from.

*How to teach:* Learn 'Dreidel Song' (Hanukkah, Jewish tradition) and 'Feliz Navidad' (Christmas song by José Feliciano, Spanish and English).

- **Q:** What holiday is the Dreidel Song connected to?
  **A:** Hanukkah.
- **Q:** What does 'Feliz Navidad' mean?
  **A:** Merry Christmas (in Spanish).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paper chains
*Student:* Make a patterned paper chain decoration.

*How to teach:* Make a patterned paper chain (ABAB colors).

- **Q:** What is your chain's pattern?
  **A:** Names the repeating colors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Art at home
*Student:* Identify art made at home for celebrations (cards, decorations).

*How to teach:* Find art made at home for celebrations (cards, ornaments, decorations).

- **Q:** Name art people make for celebrations.
  **A:** Cards, decorations, paper chains, ornaments.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse songs for a family sing-along.

*How to teach:* Rehearse songs for a family sing-along.

- **Q:** Why rehearse?
  **A:** To learn the words and sing well together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Perform a seasonal sing-along with good audience manners.

*How to teach:* Perform; model audience manners.

- **Q:** What does a good audience do?
  **A:** Sits quietly, watches, and claps at the end.

**Assessment:** Sing-along performance.

**Answer key:** Sing-along: sings 2-3 songs with correct words, steady beat and expression; shows audience manners when others perform (quiet, eyes on performer, clap at end). Proficient = all.

## PE 1: Fuel, water and endurance

**Prepare:** Water bottle, timer, a park or safe loop. Fitness journal.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you should never walk: walk-jog is fine; endurance grows by keeping moving.
- Waiting until very thirsty to drink: sip water before, during and after activity.

### Day 1 (Monday): Walk-jog
*Student:* Walk-jog for 8 minutes around a park.

*How to teach:* Walk-jog for 8 minutes: jog until tired, walk to recover, jog again.

- **Q:** What is endurance?
  **A:** Being able to keep moving for a long time without stopping.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fuel
*Student:* Discuss foods that give energy and why water matters during activity.

*How to teach:* Talk about energy foods (fruits, whole grains, vegetables, milk) and why water matters.

- **Q:** Why drink water when exercising?
  **A:** Your body loses water when you sweat, and water helps you keep going.
- **Q:** Name a food that gives good energy.
  **A:** Examples: banana, oatmeal, apple, whole-grain bread.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Longer jog
*Student:* Walk-jog for 10 minutes, taking water breaks.

*How to teach:* Walk-jog for 10 minutes with water breaks.

- **Q:** Was 10 minutes harder than 8? Why?
  **A:** Usually yes, because it is longer; practice builds endurance.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rest
*Student:* Discuss why sleep helps muscles grow.

*How to teach:* Discuss why sleep helps muscles grow and repair (about 9-12 hours for kids).

- **Q:** Why is sleep important for active kids?
  **A:** Sleep lets muscles rest, repair and grow, and gives energy for tomorrow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Fitness journal: record jog time, push-ups and stretch.

*How to teach:* Record jog time, push-ups and stretch in the journal.

- **Q:** What three things help your body have energy?
  **A:** Healthy food, water and sleep.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 fitness journal.

**Answer key:** Fitness journal entries: jog minutes, push-up count and sit-and-reach distance recorded with date. Proficient = all three recorded and child can say one way to get more energy (healthy food, water, sleep).

## Health 1: Healthy eating goal

**Prepare:** A goal chart, stickers, a MyPlate printout for menu planning, and paper for a grocery list.

**Watch for:**
- Picking a goal that's too big: start small ('eat a vegetable at dinner').
- Giving up after a missed day: keep going the next day.
- Menus with no vegetables: check each meal for food groups.

### Day 1 (Monday): Set a goal
*Student:* Choose a goal like 'eat a vegetable at every dinner.'

*How to teach:* Choose a healthy eating goal.

- **Q:** Example goal.
  **A:** Eat a vegetable at every dinner.
- **Q:** Why a small goal?
  **A:** Easier to do and stick with.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Track
*Student:* Track the goal with stickers each day.

*How to teach:* Track the goal with stickers.

- **Q:** What does a sticker mean?
  **A:** You met the goal that day.
- **Q:** What if you miss a day?
  **A:** Try again tomorrow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Plan a menu
*Student:* Plan one day's meals using MyPlate.

*How to teach:* Plan one day's meals using MyPlate.

- **Q:** Example dinner.
  **A:** Chicken, rice, broccoli, milk, orange slices.
- **Q:** Which groups?
  **A:** Protein, grain, vegetable, dairy, fruit.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Grocery list
*Student:* Write a grocery list with foods from each group.

*How to teach:* Write a grocery list with foods from each group.

- **Q:** List one per group.
  **A:** Apples, carrots, bread, eggs, yogurt.
- **Q:** Why a list?
  **A:** To buy healthy foods and not forget.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Food-group assessment (sort and label a MyPlate) and tell whether you met your eating goal.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 3 assessment.

- **Q:** Label MyPlate.
  **A:** Fruits, vegetables, grains, protein, dairy.
- **Q:** Did you meet your goal?
  **A:** Count stickers.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test: sort 10 foods (apple-fruit, carrot-vegetable, rice-grain, fish-protein, yogurt-dairy, grapes-fruit, spinach-vegetable, bread-grain, beans-protein/vegetable, cheese-dairy) and label a blank MyPlate with 5 groups. Proficient 14–15 of 15 plus a goal reflection ('I met my goal 5 of 7 days').
