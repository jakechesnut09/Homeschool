# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 34

## ELA 1: Research poster and presentation

**Prepare:** Poster board, glue, markers, a computer or tablet and printer, notes from week 33, and a smiley-face self-assessment sheet (voice, eye contact, facts).

**Watch for:**
- Statements and questions with the same end mark: statements end with a period; questions with a question mark; exclamations with an exclamation point.
- Commands confused with statements: commands tell someone to do something ('Look at the turtle.').
- Reading the poster word-for-word with eyes down: practice talking from memory, glancing at the poster.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sentence types
*Student:* Write a statement, question, command and exclamation about your topic.

*How to teach:* Your child writes a statement, question, command, and exclamation about their topic.

- **Q:** Write a question about sea turtles.
  **A:** Example: Where do sea turtles live?
- **Q:** Write a command.
  **A:** Example: Save the sea turtles.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Type captions
*Student:* With help, type 4 fact captions on a computer and print them.

*How to teach:* With help, your child types 4 fact captions and prints them.

- **Q:** What is a caption?
  **A:** Words that tell about a picture.
- **Q:** Write a caption for a turtle on a beach.
  **A:** Example: Sea turtles lay eggs on the beach.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Poster
*Student:* Glue captions and drawings on a poster with a title.

*How to teach:* Glue captions and drawings on a poster with a title.

- **Q:** Where does the title go?
  **A:** At the top.
- **Q:** What should the title tell?
  **A:** The topic of the poster.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Present the poster using complete sentences; answer two audience questions.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the poster in complete sentences and answers two questions.

- **Q:** How should you speak during a presentation?
  **A:** Loud, clear, and not too fast.
- **Q:** What do you do when someone asks a question?
  **A:** Listen, think, and answer in a complete sentence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-assess the presentation (voice, eye contact, facts) on a smiley-face scale.

*How to teach:* Your child self-assesses with the smiley scale.

- **Q:** Which part went best?
  **A:** Answers vary — encourage a specific one.
- **Q:** What would you do differently?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., speak louder.

**Assessment:** Presentation checklist and 4 sentence types.

**Answer key:** Presentation checklist: clear voice, eye contact, 4 accurate facts, complete sentences, answered 2 questions. Sentence types: statement ends with period ('Sea turtles swim.'), question with ? ('What do sea turtles eat?'), command ('Look at the turtle.'), exclamation ('Wow, sea turtles are huge!'). Proficient = all 4 types correct with right punctuation.

## Math 1: Equal shares: halves and fourths

**Prepare:** Paper circles and rectangles to fold, scissors, crayons, a tortilla or sandwich, pictures of shapes cut into equal and unequal parts, and a Unit 10 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Calling any 2 pieces 'halves': halves must be the same size.
- Thinking more pieces means bigger pieces (fourths are bigger than halves): compare by laying a fourth on top of a half.
- Thinking a 'fourth' and a 'quarter' are different: they're two names for the same thing.

### Day 1 (Monday): Halves
*Student:* Fold paper shapes in half; check if parts are equal.

*How to teach:* Fold a paper rectangle and circle in half. Unfold and check if the two parts match by folding again. Color one half.

- **Q:** How many halves make a whole?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** What makes them halves?
  **A:** The two parts are equal in size.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fourths
*Student:* Fold into fourths; label 'fourth' and 'quarter'.

*How to teach:* Fold a rectangle and circle in half and in half again to get 4 equal parts. Label each part 'one fourth' or 'one quarter.'

- **Q:** How many fourths make a whole?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** What's another name for a fourth?
  **A:** A quarter.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Equal or not
*Student:* Sort pictures into equal and not-equal shares.

*How to teach:* Show pictures of shapes cut into parts. Your child sorts into 'equal shares' and 'not equal shares' and explains.

- **Q:** A circle is cut into 2 different-sized pieces. Are they halves?
  **A:** No — the pieces must be equal.
- **Q:** A rectangle is cut into 4 equal strips. What is each called?
  **A:** A fourth (quarter).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share food
*Student:* Cut a tortilla or sandwich into halves and fourths; compare piece sizes.

*How to teach:* Cut a tortilla in halves, then another in fourths. Compare: which piece is bigger, a half or a fourth? Then enjoy the snack.

- **Q:** Which is bigger: one half or one fourth of the same tortilla?
  **A:** One half.
- **Q:** If you share a sandwich equally with 3 friends (4 people), what does each person get?
  **A:** One fourth.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 10 check
*Student:* Geometry unit test: attributes, composing shapes, and partitioning circles and rectangles into halves and fourths.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 10 test.

- **Q:** How many sides does a trapezoid have?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** Draw a line to cut a circle into halves.
  **A:** A line straight through the center.

**Assessment:** Unit 10 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 10 test (suggested): 1) Triangle: 3 sides, 3 corners 2) Rectangle: 4 sides, 4 square corners 3) Circle the rectangles (including a square) 4) Pattern blocks to make a hexagon: e.g., 2 trapezoids 5) Name the solid: can = cylinder, ball = sphere 6) Shade one half of a rectangle 7) Shade one fourth of a circle 8) Which picture shows equal shares? 9) Which is bigger, a half or a fourth of the same pizza? half 10) How many quarters in a whole? 4. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Variation within a kind of animal or plant

**Prepare:** Photos of a Chihuahua and a Great Dane, 10 leaves from one tree, paper clips, grid paper, and an assessment picture (e.g., a mother horse and foal).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking different breeds are different kinds of animals: all dogs are one kind.
- Thinking leaves on one tree should be identical: variation is normal.
- Measuring leaves inconsistently: measure from the base of the leaf to the tip, not including the stem.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dog breeds
*Student:* Compare photos of a Chihuahua and a Great Dane; list what makes both dogs and what is different.

*How to teach:* Compare a Chihuahua and a Great Dane.

- **Q:** What makes both dogs?
  **A:** Same body parts — four legs, fur, tails, bark, dog-shaped bodies.
- **Q:** What's different?
  **A:** Size, ear shape, fur.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Leaf collection
*Student:* Collect 10 leaves from one tree and measure each length with paper clips.

*How to teach:* Collect 10 leaves from one tree; measure each with paper clips.

- **Q:** How do you measure fairly?
  **A:** Paper clips end to end, starting at the leaf's base.
- **Q:** Are all leaves the same length?
  **A:** No.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Leaf graph
*Student:* Graph leaf lengths into short, medium and long groups.

*How to teach:* Graph leaf lengths into short, medium, long.

- **Q:** Set categories.
  **A:** Example: short 1–2 clips, medium 3, long 4+.
- **Q:** Which group had most?
  **A:** Answers vary — read from graph.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Discuss
*Student:* Discuss why leaves on one tree and puppies in one litter are not exactly the same.

*How to teach:* Discuss why leaves on one tree and puppies in one litter aren't the same.

- **Q:** Why aren't puppies identical?
  **A:** They get different mixes of traits from parents.
- **Q:** Why aren't leaves identical?
  **A:** Each grows a little differently (sunlight, position, and variation).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Assessment: compare a parent and offspring picture and explain likenesses and differences.

*How to teach:* Give the assessment with a parent/offspring picture.

- **Q:** Horse and foal: alike?
  **A:** Same body shape, mane, hooves.
- **Q:** Different?
  **A:** Foal is smaller with longer legs compared to its body.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 assessment and leaf graph.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test: compare a parent and offspring picture (e.g., horse and foal): proficient names 2 likenesses (same body parts, coat color/pattern, shape) and 2 differences (size, proportions, color). Leaf graph: title, labels, accurate bars, one sentence about variation.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Transporting and marketing goods

**Prepare:** A grocery store visit (or virtual tour), pictures of trucks, trains, ships, planes, a cereal box, and paper and markers for an ad.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all goods are made nearby: many travel far by truck, train, ship, or plane.
- Believing everything ads say: ads are made to make you want to buy.
- Thinking cashiers are the only store workers: stockers, butchers, bakers, managers too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Grocery trip
*Student:* Visit a grocery store and list workers you see (stocker, cashier, butcher).

*How to teach:* Visit a grocery store; list workers.

- **Q:** Name 3 workers.
  **A:** Cashier, stocker, butcher, baker, manager.
- **Q:** What does a stocker do?
  **A:** Puts goods on shelves.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Transport
*Student:* Learn how goods travel by truck, train, ship and plane.

*How to teach:* Learn how goods travel.

- **Q:** Fastest way to move fresh flowers far?
  **A:** Airplane.
- **Q:** How do goods cross the ocean?
  **A:** On ships.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Advertising
*Student:* Look at cereal box ads and discuss how stores and companies market goods.

*How to teach:* Look at cereal box ads; discuss marketing.

- **Q:** Why do cereal boxes have cartoons?
  **A:** To get children's attention so they'll want it.
- **Q:** What is an ad?
  **A:** A message that tries to get people to buy something.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make an ad
*Student:* Design an ad poster for your homemade bread.

*How to teach:* Design an ad poster for homemade bread.

- **Q:** What goes in an ad?
  **A:** Product name, picture, price, reasons to buy.
- **Q:** Write a catchy line.
  **A:** Example: Warm, fresh bread — baked with love!

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one job that makes, one that moves, and one that sells goods.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one job that makes, moves, and sells goods.

- **Q:** Makes?
  **A:** Baker or farmer.
- **Q:** Moves?
  **A:** Truck driver.
- **Q:** Sells?
  **A:** Cashier/grocer.

**Assessment:** Job explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = correctly names and explains a maker (farmer/baker/factory worker), mover (truck driver/train engineer/pilot), and seller (cashier/grocer); 3 = two correct; 2 = one; 1 = none.

## Art & Music 1: Museums and audiences

**Prepare:** Virtual tour of the National Gallery of Art or the Met (online); paper for floor plan.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a curator makes the art: a curator chooses and cares for art; a docent gives tours.
- Clapping during a song: wait until it ends.

### Day 1 (Monday): Museum tour
*Student:* Take a virtual museum tour; discuss what a curator and docent do.

*How to teach:* Take a virtual museum tour; discuss curators and docents.

- **Q:** What does a curator do?
  **A:** Chooses, cares for and arranges art in a museum.
- **Q:** What does a docent do?
  **A:** Gives tours and explains the art.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Plan your show
*Student:* Draw a floor plan showing where each of your 6 artworks will hang at home and why.

*How to teach:* Draw a floor plan of where 6 artworks will hang and why.

- **Q:** Why put art at eye level?
  **A:** So visitors can see it easily.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Concert manners
*Student:* Practice audience and performer etiquette (bow, clap, listen).

*How to teach:* Practice audience and performer etiquette.

- **Q:** What does a performer do at the end?
  **A:** Bows.
- **Q:** What does the audience do?
  **A:** Listens quietly and claps at the end.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dress rehearsal
*Student:* Full rehearsal of spring concert.

*How to teach:* Full concert rehearsal.

- **Q:** What is a dress rehearsal?
  **A:** A practice of the whole show just like the real one.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Act as docent: give a family member a 2-minute tour of 3 artworks, naming materials and ideas.

*How to teach:* Act as docent for 3 artworks.

- **Q:** What is an audience?
  **A:** The people watching or listening.

**Assessment:** Docent practice.

**Answer key:** Docent practice: 2-minute tour of 3 artworks naming materials and an idea for each; speaks clearly and faces audience. Proficient = 3 artworks with materials + ideas.

## PE 1: Skill review: locomotor and manipulative

**Prepare:** Balls, cones, a 2x4 board, timer; cue checklist.

**Watch for:**
- Forgetting cues under time pressure: slow down and say cues aloud.
- Practicing only favorite skills: rotate through all.

### Day 1 (Monday): Locomotor review
*Student:* Circuit of hop, gallop, slide, skip.

*How to teach:* Circuit of hop, gallop, slide and skip.

- **Q:** What is a skip?
  **A:** Step-hop, alternating feet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Throw and catch
*Student:* Throw overhand and catch with a partner from 10 feet, saying the cues aloud.

*How to teach:* Throw overhand and catch from 10 feet, saying cues aloud.

- **Q:** Say the throw cues.
  **A:** Side to target, arm back, step opposite, throw.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kick and dribble
*Student:* Kick at target; dribble around cones.

*How to teach:* Kick at a target; dribble around cones.

- **Q:** Which part of the foot do you use for an accurate kick?
  **A:** Inside of the foot.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Station day
*Student:* Rotate through 5 stations (hop, balance beam, throw, kick, dribble) for 3 minutes each.

*How to teach:* Rotate 5 stations (hop, beam, throw, kick, dribble), 3 minutes each.

- **Q:** Which station was hardest?
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Do a skill checklist with a parent for hop, gallop, throw, catch, kick and dribble.

*How to teach:* Complete the skill checklist with a parent.

- **Q:** Name the catch cues.
  **A:** Ready hands, watch the ball, catch with hands.

**Assessment:** Skill checklist.

**Answer key:** Skill checklist (cues): Hop - same foot, soft landing. Gallop - lead foot front. Throw - side to target, step opposite. Catch - ready hands, eyes on ball. Kick - plant beside, inside of foot. Dribble - fingertips, waist high. Proficient = 2 cues on 5 of 6 skills.

## Health 1: Healthy community

**Prepare:** Trash bags, gloves (adult picks up sharp or unknown items), card supplies, and a healthy snack to share.

**Watch for:**
- Picking up sharp or unknown trash: only adults handle glass, needles, or unknown items.
- Thinking one person can't help a community: small acts add up.
- Thinking encouragement is only for winners: encourage effort.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clean-up
*Student:* Do a family neighborhood or park clean-up.

*How to teach:* Do a family clean-up.

- **Q:** What safety rules for clean-up?
  **A:** Wear gloves, don't touch sharp things, tell an adult.
- **Q:** How does it help health?
  **A:** Less trash means fewer germs and safer places to play.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Healthy messages
*Student:* Make a healthy-habits card for a neighbor.

*How to teach:* Make a healthy-habits card for a neighbor.

- **Q:** What message?
  **A:** Example: Wash your hands for 20 seconds!
- **Q:** Why share?
  **A:** To help others be healthy.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Share
*Student:* Share a healthy snack with friends.

*How to teach:* Share a healthy snack with friends.

- **Q:** What did you share?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., fruit.
- **Q:** Why share healthy?
  **A:** Helps friends eat well.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kind words
*Student:* Encourage a friend who is learning something new.

*How to teach:* Encourage a friend learning something new.

- **Q:** What can you say?
  **A:** You can do it! Keep trying!
- **Q:** How does encouragement help?
  **A:** Makes people feel confident.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how you helped your community.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how they helped the community.

- **Q:** How did you help?
  **A:** Cleaned up the park, shared healthy messages.
- **Q:** How does a healthy community help you?
  **A:** Clean, safe places to live and play.

**Assessment:** Community action reflection.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = describes 2+ community actions and explains how each helps health (clean park = fewer germs/safer; healthy card = reminds neighbors); 3 = 1 action explained; 2 = action named only; 1 = none.
