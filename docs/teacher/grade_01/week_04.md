# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 4

## ELA 1: Short e and all short vowels; narrative sentences

**Prepare:** Sound boxes and tiles, 20 word cards (4 per vowel: cat, map, bag, ham; pig, sit, lip, win; hot, top, dog, mop; bug, cup, sun, hut; bed, red, pet, ten), 'Henry and Mudge' (first book) by Cynthia Rylant, lined paper, and a 25-word CVC screener plus the 10 sight words from weeks 1–2 plus 'and, to, go, we.'

**Watch for:**
- Confusing short e and short i (bed → 'bid'): /ĕ/ is the 'Ed' sound with a slightly open mouth; practice pairs bed/bid, pet/pit.
- Starting every sentence with 'And then': teach first, then, and last as sequence words, and vary them.
- Thinking a story with one sentence is finished: ask 'What happened next? How did it end?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Short e
*Student:* Read bed, red, pet, ten, web; segment each word into sounds and spell it with tiles.

*How to teach:* Read bed, red, pet, ten, web. Your child segments each in sound boxes and builds it with tiles.

- **Q:** Segment ten.
  **A:** /t/ /ĕ/ /n/.
- **Q:** Spell web.
  **A:** w-e-b.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Vowel sort
*Student:* Sort 20 word cards into five short-vowel columns, read each column aloud.

*How to teach:* Lay out 5 vowel columns (a, e, i, o, u). Your child reads each of 20 cards and places it in its column, then reads each column aloud.

- **Q:** Where does hut go?
  **A:** The short-u column.
- **Q:** Where does pet go?
  **A:** The short-e column.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Read Henry and Mudge
*Student:* Read the first chapter of 'Henry and Mudge' together; answer questions about how Henry feels and why.

*How to teach:* Read the first chapter of 'Henry and Mudge' together. Ask how Henry feels at the beginning and after he gets Mudge.

- **Q:** How did Henry feel at the beginning, and why?
  **A:** Lonely, because he had no brothers or sisters and no children on his street.
- **Q:** What did Henry finally ask for that his parents said yes to?
  **A:** A dog.
- **Q:** What happened to Mudge as he grew?
  **A:** He grew very big — much bigger than Henry (about 180 pounds in the book).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write it
*Student:* Write a 2–3 sentence story about a pet or animal you know, using first and then; check each sentence for a capital and period.

*How to teach:* Your child writes 2–3 sentences about a pet or animal, using 'first' and 'then,' and checks each sentence for a capital and period.

- **Q:** Which sequence word goes first: then or first?
  **A:** First.
- **Q:** Check: 'my dog ran' — what is missing?
  **A:** A capital M and a period.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Phonics screener of 25 CVC words and 10 sight words; read the narrative aloud and add one detail.

*How to teach:* Give the phonics screener (25 CVC words and 10 sight words) one-on-one, marking errors. Then your child reads their narrative aloud and adds one detail.

- **Q:** Read: fed, pup, lid, jog, nap.
  **A:** fed, pup, lid, jog, nap.
- **Q:** Read: the, see, my, and.
  **A:** the, see, my, and.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 phonics screener (CVC + sight words) and a scored 2–3 sentence narrative.

**Answer key:** Screener: 25 CVC words, e.g., sat, man, bad, hit, pin, dig, hot, fox, log, cut, bus, fun, bed, wet, pen, map, lip, rug, top, ten, jam, fit, mud, hen, cob. Proficient = 22+/25 and 9+/10 sight words (the, a, is, I, see, my, and, to, go, we). Narrative rubric: 4 = 2–3 sentences in order using first/then, every sentence with capital and period, an added detail; 3 = in order with 1–2 punctuation errors; 2 = one sentence or out of order; 1 = not readable.

## Math 1: Comparing numbers and 'how many more'

**Prepare:** Connecting cubes (or LEGO bricks), a deck of cards with face cards removed (Ace = 1, add paper 0 cards), dot cards, a number path 0–20 with some blanks, and a printed Unit 1 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Answering 'how many more' with the bigger total (9 instead of 3): line up the towers side by side and point only to the extra cubes that stick up.
- Thinking 'fewer' means 'more': practice with physical towers — the shorter tower has fewer.
- Losing track when counting backward across the teens: practice saying 20 down to 10 with a number path in view.

### Day 1 (Monday): More or fewer
*Student:* Build two cube towers (9 and 6) and tell which has more and how many more.

*How to teach:* Build a tower of 9 and a tower of 6 and stand them side by side. Ask which has more and how you know, then point to the cubes that are 'extra' on the taller tower and count them: 3 more.

- **Q:** Towers of 9 and 6: how many more is 9?
  **A:** 3 more (9 − 6 = 3).
- **Q:** Towers of 5 and 8: which has fewer, and by how many?
  **A:** 5 has fewer, by 3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compare stories
*Student:* Solve 'Ana has 7 stickers, Ben has 4. How many more does Ana have?' with cube towers.

*How to teach:* Read 'Ana has 7 stickers, Ben has 4. How many more does Ana have?' Build a 7-tower and a 4-tower, match cube to cube, and count the unmatched cubes. Write 7 − 4 = 3 or 4 + 3 = 7.

- **Q:** Ana 7, Ben 4. How many more does Ana have?
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** Jo has 10 marbles and Kim has 6. How many fewer does Kim have?
  **A:** 4 fewer.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Number games
*Student:* Play 'War' with cards 0–10 and say who has more and by how much.

*How to teach:* Play 'War' with cards 0–10: each flips a card, the bigger card wins, and the winner must say how many more (e.g., '8 is 3 more than 5'). Use cubes to check when unsure.

- **Q:** You flip 8, I flip 5. Who has more and by how much?
  **A:** 8 has 3 more.
- **Q:** You flip 2, I flip 9. By how much does 9 win?
  **A:** By 7.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Count to 20 and back
*Student:* Count forward and backward from 20; fill in missing numbers on a number path.

*How to teach:* Count together from 1 to 20, then 20 back to 0 while pointing to the number path. Then have your child fill in blanks on a number path such as 11, __, 13, __, __, 16.

- **Q:** What number comes just before 15? Just after 19?
  **A:** 14; 20.
- **Q:** Fill in: 18, 17, __, 15.
  **A:** 16.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Unit test: counting, writing numerals, add/subtract and compare stories within 10.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 1 test one section at a time, reading every problem aloud. Allow counters or drawings.

- **Q:** Count back from 13 to 8.
  **A:** 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8.
- **Q:** Sam has 8 grapes, Tia has 5. How many more does Sam have?
  **A:** 3.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 test (suggested) with answers: 1) Count 16 objects → 16. 2) Write numerals dictated: 7, 11, 14, 18, 20. 3) Start at 9, count on 3 → 12. 4) 3 + 4 = 7. 5) 5 + 3 story → 8. 6) 9 − 2 = 7. 7) 8 cookies, 5 eaten → 3. 8) 6 kids, 2 are girls, boys? → 4. 9) Towers 7 and 3: how many more? → 4. 10) Fill 16, 15, __, 13 → 14. Score 9–10 proficient; 7–8 developing; under 7 reteach weak items.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Vibrations make sound

**Prepare:** Rubber bands, an empty tissue box, a 30 cm ruler, a pot and wooden spoon, and paper for labeled drawings.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking sound comes from 'nowhere' or only from mouths: all sounds come from something vibrating.
- Thinking vibration and sound are unrelated: when vibrating stops, sound stops — hold the ruler still to show this.
- Believing louder means faster vibration: louder means bigger vibration; faster vibration makes a higher pitch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Feel your voice
*Student:* Hum with fingers on your throat; hum loud and soft and describe the vibrations.

*How to teach:* Your child hums with fingers on their throat — loud, then soft — and describes the buzzing.

- **Q:** What did you feel when you hummed?
  **A:** A buzz — my throat (vocal cords) vibrating.
- **Q:** Did loud humming feel different from soft?
  **A:** Yes — loud humming had stronger vibrations.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Ruler twang
*Student:* Hang a ruler off a table and twang it at different lengths; record what you see and hear.

*How to teach:* Hold a ruler on a table edge with part hanging off. Twang it at long and short lengths. Record observations.

- **Q:** What do you see when the ruler makes sound?
  **A:** It moves back and forth fast — it vibrates.
- **Q:** What happens to the sound when the hanging part is shorter?
  **A:** The pitch gets higher because it vibrates faster.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rubber band guitar
*Student:* Stretch rubber bands over a tissue box; pluck and observe the vibrations.

*How to teach:* Stretch rubber bands over a tissue box. Pluck and watch.

- **Q:** What happens when you pluck?
  **A:** The band vibrates and makes a sound.
- **Q:** What happens if you touch the band to stop it?
  **A:** The vibration stops and the sound stops.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sound walk
*Student:* List 10 sounds around the house and what is vibrating to make each one.

*How to teach:* List 10 sounds around the house and what vibrates for each.

- **Q:** What vibrates when a dog barks?
  **A:** Its vocal cords.
- **Q:** What vibrates when you knock on a door?
  **A:** The door (wood).

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw and label how a sound is made, using the word vibrate.

*How to teach:* Your child draws and labels how a sound is made using the word vibrate.

- **Q:** Explain how a drum makes sound.
  **A:** Hitting it makes the drum skin vibrate, which makes sound.
- **Q:** Can there be sound without vibration?
  **A:** No — sound is made by vibrations.

**Assessment:** Labeled drawing explaining vibration and sound.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = drawing of an object (e.g., drum) with labels showing what vibrates and a sentence like 'The drum skin vibrates and makes sound'; 3 = drawing with vibrate label; 2 = drawing without the word vibrate; 1 = no connection to vibration.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Direct democracy: everyone votes

**Prepare:** 'Duck for President' by Doreen Cronin (library), paper ballots, a box for a ballot box, and a tally chart.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the majority means everyone agrees: some people still voted for the other choice.
- Thinking voting is only for presidents: we can vote in families and clubs.
- Thinking the losing side should sulk: we respect the result.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is voting?
*Student:* Read 'Duck for President' by Doreen Cronin (library) and talk about why the animals voted.

*How to teach:* Read 'Duck for President.' Talk about why the animals voted.

- **Q:** Why did Duck want an election?
  **A:** He was tired of doing chores for Farmer Brown and wanted to be in charge.
- **Q:** What did Duck learn about being a leader?
  **A:** Running things (the farm, the state, the country) was hard work, so he went back to the farm.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Family vote
*Student:* Hold a direct vote on Friday's family movie or snack with paper ballots; count the tallies.

*How to teach:* Hold a family vote on Friday's movie or snack with paper ballots. Count tallies.

- **Q:** How did we decide?
  **A:** Everyone voted, and we counted the votes.
- **Q:** Why are ballots secret?
  **A:** So people can vote how they want without pressure.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Majority rules
*Student:* Count votes and explain that the choice with the most votes wins; discuss how the others feel.

*How to teach:* Count votes; explain that the most votes wins; discuss feelings.

- **Q:** Popcorn 3 votes, fruit 2 votes. Which wins?
  **A:** Popcorn.
- **Q:** How should the fruit voters act?
  **A:** Accept the result kindly; there will be other votes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make a rule by vote
*Student:* Propose a new household rule and let everyone vote on it.

*How to teach:* Propose a new household rule and let everyone vote.

- **Q:** What happens if the rule gets more yes votes than no?
  **A:** It becomes a rule.
- **Q:** Is this direct democracy?
  **A:** Yes — everyone votes on the rule.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain direct democracy in one sentence and give the family-vote example.

*How to teach:* Your child explains direct democracy in one sentence and gives the example.

- **Q:** What is direct democracy?
  **A:** Everyone votes directly on a decision or rule.
- **Q:** Give an example.
  **A:** Our family voted on the Friday movie.

**Assessment:** Explanation of direct democracy with an example.

**Answer key:** Rubric: proficient = 'In direct democracy, everyone votes on the decision' plus an accurate example (e.g., the family movie vote where the choice with the most votes won).

## Art & Music 1: Line and shape design; beat review

**Prepare:** Paper, black marker or black tape, red/blue/yellow crayons; image of Mondrian's 'Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow'; empty bottle and rice (tape the lid shut).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Mondrian's art is 'easy': he planned lines and color balance carefully.
- Shaking the shaker continuously: shake once per beat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mondrian look
*Student:* Look at Mondrian's 'Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow' and find straight lines and rectangles.

*How to teach:* Look at Mondrian's 'Composition with Red, Blue and Yellow.' Find straight black lines and rectangles.

- **Q:** What kinds of lines did Mondrian use?
  **A:** Straight, black, vertical and horizontal lines.
- **Q:** What colors did he use?
  **A:** Red, blue and yellow plus white and black.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mondrian design
*Student:* Use black tape or marker lines and color rectangles red, blue and yellow.

*How to teach:* Draw straight black lines with marker or tape; color some rectangles red, blue and yellow.

- **Q:** What shapes are made by the lines?
  **A:** Rectangles and squares.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Make a shaker
*Student:* Make a rice-bottle shaker and play the beat to 'This Old Man'.

*How to teach:* Make a rice shaker; tape the lid. Play it on the beat to 'This Old Man.'

- **Q:** When do you shake?
  **A:** Once on each beat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Beat performance
*Student:* Perform a song with shaker on the beat while singing.

*How to teach:* Sing while playing the shaker on the beat.

- **Q:** Did the beat stay steady while singing?
  **A:** Yes, even shakes throughout.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 share
*Student:* Hold a mini-gallery of unit art and a beat performance; tell which line or shape you like best.

*How to teach:* Set out unit art as a mini gallery; perform the shaker song.

- **Q:** Which line or shape do you like best and why?
  **A:** Any answer naming a line or shape with a reason.

**Assessment:** Unit 1 portfolio check and beat performance.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 portfolio: line picture, circle art, shape animal, Mondrian design (straight black lines + red/blue/yellow rectangles). Beat performance: plays shaker on the beat while singing 'This Old Man.' Proficient = all 4 pieces + steady beat; child names a favorite line or shape.

## PE 1: Spatial words with objects

**Prepare:** Sturdy table or chairs, broomstick, hula hoop, blanket, beanbag or ball. Build a safe course with soft landing areas.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up left and right: put a sticker or bracelet on the right hand as a reminder.
- Thinking 'through' and 'around' are the same: through goes inside/across the middle; around goes along the outside.

### Day 1 (Monday): Obstacle words
*Student:* Build an obstacle course: crawl under a table, step over a broomstick, through a hoop, around a chair.

*How to teach:* Build a course: crawl under a table, step over a broomstick, go through a hoop, walk around a chair. Say each word as your child does it, then have them say it.

- **Q:** Did you go over or under the broomstick?
  **A:** Over.
- **Q:** Did you go through or around the hoop?
  **A:** Through.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Position game
*Student:* Place a beanbag or ball where called (behind, in front, next to you).

*How to teach:* Call positions for a beanbag: behind you, in front of you, next to you, on your right, on your left. Your child places it there.

- **Q:** Put the beanbag behind you.
  **A:** Places it at their back.
- **Q:** Put it on your left side.
  **A:** Places it beside the left hand side.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Partner mirror
*Student:* Mirror a partner's movement on the left and right.

*How to teach:* Face your child as a mirror; raise your right hand and have them mirror. Explain mirrors flip sides: your right is their left. Then practice 'right hand up, left foot out.'

- **Q:** Raise your right hand.
  **A:** Raises the right hand (check sticker/bracelet).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Course redesign
*Student:* Design and run your own obstacle course using 6 position words.

*How to teach:* Have your child design a new course using 6 position words and draw a simple map. Run it together.

- **Q:** Name the 6 position words in your course.
  **A:** Six of: over, under, through, around, next to, behind, in front of, up, down, left, right.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 1 check
*Student:* Run the course while calling out each position word.

*How to teach:* Have your child run their course while calling each position word aloud. Use the rubric.

- **Q:** What did you do at the chair?
  **A:** Matches their course, e.g., 'went around it.'

**Assessment:** Unit 1 performance check.

**Answer key:** Unit 1 check rubric: completes the course while correctly saying each position word (over, under, through, around, next to, behind/in front). 6/6 words correct = proficient; 4-5 = developing; 3 or fewer = beginning. Also note safe movement and stopping.

## Health 1: Kindness, respect and differences

**Prepare:** 'Last Stop on Market Street' by Matt de la Peña (library) and recipe-card paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kindness means only big gestures: small acts count.
- Thinking people who are different can't be friends: differences make friendships interesting.
- Waiting for others to include you: you can be the one to invite.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read
*Student:* Read 'Last Stop on Market Street' and talk about how CJ learns to see beauty in others.

*How to teach:* Read 'Last Stop on Market Street'; discuss how CJ learns to see beauty.

- **Q:** Where are CJ and Nana going?
  **A:** On the bus to the last stop on Market Street to help at a soup kitchen.
- **Q:** What does Nana help CJ notice?
  **A:** Beauty in everyday things — music from the man with the guitar, the people on the bus, the city.
- **Q:** How does CJ feel at the end?
  **A:** Glad they came.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Same and different
*Student:* List 3 ways you are the same as and 3 ways different from a friend.

*How to teach:* List 3 ways you are the same as and 3 ways different from a friend.

- **Q:** Name a way you're the same.
  **A:** Example: we both like soccer.
- **Q:** Name a difference.
  **A:** Example: we speak different languages at home.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kindness challenge
*Student:* Do one kind act each day and record it.

*How to teach:* Do one kind act each day; record it.

- **Q:** Name a kind act.
  **A:** Helping a sibling, saying something nice, sharing.
- **Q:** How do kind acts make others feel?
  **A:** Happy, cared for.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Include others
*Student:* Role-play inviting someone new to play.

*How to teach:* Role-play inviting someone new to play.

- **Q:** What can you say?
  **A:** Hi, I'm ___. Do you want to play with us?
- **Q:** Why include others?
  **A:** So no one feels left out.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a 'kindness recipe' card with 4 ingredients.

*How to teach:* Make a 'kindness recipe' card with 4 ingredients.

- **Q:** Example ingredients.
  **A:** 1 cup sharing, 2 spoons of smiles, a pinch of patience, a bowl of helping.
- **Q:** What's the 'dish'?
  **A:** A kind friend.

**Assessment:** Kindness recipe card.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = card names 4 specific kind actions (sharing, including, helping, kind words) with a creative recipe format; 3 = 3 ingredients; 2 = 1–2; 1 = incomplete.
