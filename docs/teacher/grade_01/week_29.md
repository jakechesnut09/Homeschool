# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 29

## ELA 1: Diphthongs ou/ow; The Tale of Peter Rabbit

**Prepare:** 'The Tale of Peter Rabbit' by Beatrix Potter (free on Gutenberg, with Potter's illustrations), a toy for acting out prepositions, a story map, and an ou/ow list (out, house, cloud, mouth, cow, town, owl, down).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking ou and ow make different sounds in these words: both say /ou/ as in ouch.
- Saying Peter's problem is 'he ate vegetables': the problem is that he disobeyed and was chased and nearly caught by Mr. McGregor.
- Using prepositions without the object (He went under.): say under what.

### Day 1 (Monday): ou and ow
*Student:* Read out, house, cloud, cow, town, owl; sort words by spelling.

*How to teach:* Read out, house, cloud, cow, town, owl. Sort by spelling ou/ow.

- **Q:** Sort: cloud, town.
  **A:** ou: cloud; ow: town.
- **Q:** Read: mouth, owl.
  **A:** mouth, owl.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Peter Rabbit, part 1
*Student:* Read the first half; describe Mr. McGregor's garden using the illustrations.

*How to teach:* Read the first half (until Peter is caught in the gooseberry net). Describe Mr. McGregor's garden using the illustrations.

- **Q:** What did Mrs. Rabbit tell her children not to do?
  **A:** Not to go into Mr. McGregor's garden.
- **Q:** What did Peter eat in the garden?
  **A:** Lettuces, French beans, and radishes (then he looked for parsley because he felt sick).
- **Q:** What did Flopsy, Mopsy, and Cotton-tail do?
  **A:** They were good and went to gather blackberries.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Prepositions
*Student:* Tell where Peter went using under, in, through, behind; act them out with a toy.

*How to teach:* Your child tells where Peter went using under, in, through, behind, acting it out with a toy.

- **Q:** Where did Peter squeeze to get into the garden?
  **A:** Under the gate.
- **Q:** Where did Peter hide from Mr. McGregor?
  **A:** In a watering can (in the tool-shed).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Peter Rabbit, part 2
*Student:* Finish the story; name Peter's problem and how it was solved.

*How to teach:* Finish the story. Name Peter's problem and how it was solved.

- **Q:** What was Peter's problem?
  **A:** He was chased by Mr. McGregor and couldn't find his way out of the garden.
- **Q:** How was it solved?
  **A:** He found the gate, slipped under it, and ran home.
- **Q:** What happened to Peter's jacket and shoes?
  **A:** Mr. McGregor used them for a scarecrow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* ou/ow dictation; story map of characters, setting, problem, solution.

*How to teach:* Dictate ou/ow words; complete the story map.

- **Q:** Spell house.
  **A:** h-o-u-s-e.
- **Q:** Spell cow.
  **A:** c-o-w.

**Assessment:** Story map and ou/ow dictation.

**Answer key:** Story map: Characters — Peter Rabbit, Mrs. Rabbit, Flopsy, Mopsy, Cotton-tail, Mr. McGregor (also sparrows, mouse, white cat). Setting — Mr. McGregor's garden (and the rabbit-hole home under a fir tree). Problem — Peter disobeys, goes into the garden, and is chased by Mr. McGregor. Solution — he finds the gate, squeezes under, and runs home; he is sick and gets camomile tea while his sisters have bread, milk and blackberries. Dictation: out, house, cloud, cow, town, owl — proficient 5–6.

## Math 1: Measurement and data project

**Prepare:** Connecting cubes, 10 toys of different lengths, grid paper, and a Unit 8 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Putting a toy in the wrong category because the boundaries are unclear: decide rules first, e.g., short = 1–4 cubes, medium = 5–8, long = 9 or more.
- Graph bars not starting at zero: draw a baseline and color from the bottom.
- Answering how-many-more by adding the bars: subtract or count up from the shorter bar.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Choose 3 categories of objects (short, medium, long) to measure.

*How to teach:* Your child decides 3 length categories and writes cube ranges for each (e.g., short 1–4, medium 5–8, long 9+).

- **Q:** Why do we need number rules for each category?
  **A:** So every toy fits in exactly one category.
- **Q:** A toy is 6 cubes. With our rules, which category?
  **A:** Medium (5–8).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure
*Student:* Measure 10 toys with cubes and record.

*How to teach:* Your child measures 10 toys with cubes and records each length and category.

- **Q:** A toy car is 4 cubes long. Category?
  **A:** Short (1–4).
- **Q:** A doll is 10 cubes. Category?
  **A:** Long (9+).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Graph
*Student:* Graph how many toys fall into each category.

*How to teach:* Make a bar graph with 3 bars (short, medium, long), one square per toy, with a title and labels.

- **Q:** If 4 toys are short, how tall is that bar?
  **A:** 4 squares.
- **Q:** What should the title say?
  **A:** Example: 'How Long Are My Toys?'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Questions
*Student:* Answer how-many-more questions about the graph.

*How to teach:* Ask how-many-more questions about the graph.

- **Q:** Short 4, medium 4, long 2. How many more short than long?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** How many toys in all?
  **A:** 10.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Measurement unit test: order lengths, compare indirectly, measure with units, and read a length graph.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 8 test.

- **Q:** A pencil is 8 cubes, a crayon is 5 cubes. Which is longer, and by how much?
  **A:** The pencil, by 3 cubes.
- **Q:** Rope A is longer than rope B; rope B is longer than rope C. Is A longer than C?
  **A:** Yes.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 8 test (suggested): 1) Order 3 pictured strips shortest to longest (check order). 2) A is longer than string; string is longer than B → A is longer than B. 3) Measure a pictured line with paper clips drawn end to end (count shown, e.g., 6). 4) Circle the picture with a gap → the gapped one. 5) Graph short 3, medium 5, long 2: most = medium; how many more medium than long = 3; total = 10. Proficient: all correct or one error.

## Science 1 (NGSS): How animal parents care for their young

**Prepare:** 'Are You My Mother?' by P.D. Eastman (library), a PBS LearningMedia emperor penguin clip, behavior picture cards (12), and chart paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all animal babies are cared for the same way: some parents feed and protect for years; some leave eggs alone.
- Thinking only mothers care for babies: emperor penguin fathers keep the egg warm.
- Thinking baby animals don't need care because they're born knowing everything: many learn from parents.

### Day 1 (Monday): Read Are You My Mother?
*Student:* Read the story and list what the baby bird needs from its mother.

*How to teach:* Read 'Are You My Mother?' List what the baby bird needs.

- **Q:** What did the baby bird do when he hatched?
  **A:** He went looking for his mother, who had gone to find food.
- **Q:** Who did he ask?
  **A:** A kitten, hen, dog, cow, a car, a boat, a plane, and a big digger (Snort).
- **Q:** What does the baby bird need from his mother?
  **A:** Food, safety, and a home/nest.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Penguin parents
*Student:* Watch a PBS clip of emperor penguins and list 3 ways parents keep chicks warm and fed.

*How to teach:* Watch the penguin clip; list 3 ways parents keep chicks warm and fed.

- **Q:** How does an emperor penguin dad keep the egg warm?
  **A:** He balances it on his feet under a warm flap of skin (brood pouch).
- **Q:** How do parents feed chicks?
  **A:** They bring up food from their stomachs into the chick's mouth.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Feeding young
*Student:* Compare how birds (bring worms), mammals (milk) and some fish (guard eggs) care for young on a chart.

*How to teach:* Compare how birds, mammals, and some fish care for young.

- **Q:** How do mammals feed babies?
  **A:** With milk.
- **Q:** How do birds feed babies?
  **A:** Bring food like worms or insects.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Protecting young
*Student:* Read about kangaroo pouches and crocodiles carrying babies in their mouths; draw one.

*How to teach:* Read about kangaroo pouches and crocodiles; draw one.

- **Q:** Where does a baby kangaroo (joey) live?
  **A:** In its mother's pouch.
- **Q:** Why does a crocodile carry babies in her mouth?
  **A:** To move them safely to the water.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort 12 behavior cards into feed, protect, keep warm, or teach.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts 12 behavior cards.

- **Q:** Bird brings worm?
  **A:** Feed.
- **Q:** Mother cat carries kitten away from danger?
  **A:** Protect.

**Assessment:** Parent-behavior card sort.

**Answer key:** Sort key (suggested 12 cards): Feed — bird brings worm, cow nurses calf, penguin feeds chick; Protect — crocodile carries babies in mouth, elephant herd circles calf, kangaroo pouch; Keep warm — penguin dad holds egg on feet, hen sits on chicks, puppies snuggle with mother; Teach — bear shows cub to fish, cat teaches kittens to hunt, duck leads ducklings to water. Proficient 11–12.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: One community, many cultures

**Prepare:** Paper squares, crayons, glue, and a long paper strip to connect quilt squares; a Unit 5 assessment sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking differences always cause problems: differences bring new ideas, foods, and friends.
- Thinking language barriers can't be solved: gestures, pictures, and kindness help.
- Thinking shared values are rare: most families value fairness, kindness, and family.

### Day 1 (Monday): Benefits
*Student:* List benefits of a diverse community (new foods, languages, ideas).

*How to teach:* List benefits of a diverse community.

- **Q:** Name a benefit.
  **A:** Trying new foods, learning languages, new ideas.
- **Q:** Another?
  **A:** Making friends with people from many backgrounds.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Challenges
*Student:* Discuss challenges (language barriers, misunderstandings) and how kindness helps.

*How to teach:* Discuss challenges and how kindness helps.

- **Q:** Name a challenge.
  **A:** Not understanding someone's language.
- **Q:** How can kindness help?
  **A:** Smile, use gestures, learn a few words, be patient.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paper quilt
*Student:* Make a paper quilt square showing your family culture and connect it with others' squares (family members).

*How to teach:* Make a paper quilt square showing your family culture; connect with others'.

- **Q:** What's on your square?
  **A:** A family tradition, food, flag, or language.
- **Q:** Why connect squares?
  **A:** To show many cultures make one community.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shared values
*Student:* Write a sentence about values everyone shares (fairness, family, freedom).

*How to teach:* Write a sentence about values everyone shares.

- **Q:** Example.
  **A:** Everyone wants to be treated fairly.
- **Q:** Another.
  **A:** Families love and take care of each other.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Assessment on American Indians, immigrants, folklore and diversity.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 5 assessment.

- **Q:** Name a California Indian contribution.
  **A:** Basket weaving; acorn foods; place names.
- **Q:** What did immigrants bring to California?
  **A:** Foods, languages, holidays, music.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test (suggested): 1) Name a local California Indian tribe (e.g., Ohlone, Tongva, Chumash, Miwok) 2) What were baskets used for? gathering, storing, cooking 3) Angel Island is in San Francisco Bay where many immigrants arrived 4) Name an immigrant contribution 5) Lon Po Po comes from China 6) Name one benefit of diversity 7) Name a shared value. Proficient 6–7.

## Art & Music 1: Carnival of the Animals

**Prepare:** Saint-Saëns 'Carnival of the Animals' recording ('Royal March of the Lion,' 'The Elephant,' 'The Swan,' 'Aquarium'), paints.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only words tell stories: music can describe animals with tempo, dynamics and instruments.
- Giving opinions without reasons: say 'I like it because…'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lion and elephant
*Student:* Listen to 'Royal March of the Lion' and 'The Elephant'; describe dynamics and tempo.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Royal March of the Lion' and 'The Elephant.'

- **Q:** How does the elephant music sound?
  **A:** Slow, low and heavy (played by the double bass).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Swan and aquarium
*Student:* Listen to 'The Swan' and 'Aquarium'; describe the mood.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'The Swan' and 'Aquarium'; describe the mood.

- **Q:** What instrument plays the swan?
  **A:** Cello.
- **Q:** How does 'The Swan' feel?
  **A:** Calm, smooth and graceful.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Paint to music
*Student:* Paint an animal while listening to its movement.

*How to teach:* Paint an animal while its music plays.

- **Q:** Which animal did you paint?
  **A:** Names the animal.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Favorite
*Student:* Choose your favorite movement and explain why.

*How to teach:* Choose a favorite movement and explain why.

- **Q:** Who composed 'Carnival of the Animals'?
  **A:** Camille Saint-Saëns.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Act out animals as the music plays.

*How to teach:* Act out animals as the music plays.

- **Q:** How would you move to the elephant music?
  **A:** Slowly and heavily.

**Assessment:** Listening responses with reasons.

**Answer key:** Listening responses with reasons: Lion - grand march with roaring runs (piano/strings); Elephant - slow, heavy, low double bass; Swan - slow, smooth, graceful cello; Aquarium - shimmering, flowing, mysterious. Proficient = describes 3 with tempo/dynamics/instrument words and gives reasons.

## PE 1: Taking turns and following rules

**Prepare:** Cones, a playground ball, chalk for a four-square court.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking rules only matter when winning: rules keep games fair and safe for everyone.
- Arguing calls: use rock-paper-scissors or a redo.

### Day 1 (Monday): Duck duck goose
*Student:* Play with family and practice turns.

*How to teach:* Play Duck Duck Goose; everyone gets a turn to be 'it.'

- **Q:** Why take turns?
  **A:** So everyone gets a fair chance to play.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Simon says
*Student:* Play Simon Says with locomotor and balance commands, then lead a round.

*How to teach:* Play Simon Says with locomotor and balance commands; let your child lead a round.

- **Q:** What do you do if Simon doesn't say it?
  **A:** Don't move.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Four square intro
*Student:* Learn four-square rules with chalk squares and play a slow-bounce version.

*How to teach:* Draw four squares; teach a slow-bounce version: let the ball bounce once in your square, then hit it into another square.

- **Q:** How many bounces in your square before you hit it?
  **A:** One.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make rules
*Student:* Invent a backyard game with 3 rules and one safety rule; write or draw them.

*How to teach:* Invent a backyard game with 3 rules and one safety rule; write or draw them.

- **Q:** What is your safety rule?
  **A:** Example: 'Tag with gentle touches only.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Teach and play your invented game with family, enforcing the rules fairly.

*How to teach:* Your child teaches and plays their game with family, enforcing rules fairly.

- **Q:** What makes a rule fair?
  **A:** It is the same for everyone.

**Assessment:** Rule-following observation.

**Answer key:** Rule-following observation: takes turns, follows game rules, uses the safety rule, accepts calls calmly. Proficient = all four during the invented game; developing = 2-3; beginning = 0-1.

## Health 1: Water and sun safety

**Prepare:** Construction paper (dark colors fade well), small objects (keys, leaves), sunscreen (SPF 30+), a hat, a life jacket (if available), and paper for a safety mini-book.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking you only need sunscreen at the beach: you need it whenever you're in the sun, even on cloudy days.
- Thinking floaties keep you safe: only life jackets (Coast Guard approved) and adult supervision do.
- Running near pools: walk — wet surfaces are slippery.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sun paper
*Student:* Place objects on construction paper in the sun to see fading and discuss sun protection.

*How to teach:* Place objects on construction paper in the sun; check fading after a few hours.

- **Q:** What happened to the paper?
  **A:** It faded except where objects blocked the sun.
- **Q:** What does this show about our skin?
  **A:** The sun's rays can damage skin, so we protect it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sunscreen
*Student:* Practice applying sunscreen and wearing a hat.

*How to teach:* Practice applying sunscreen and wearing a hat.

- **Q:** When should you reapply sunscreen?
  **A:** About every 2 hours and after swimming.
- **Q:** Name other sun protection.
  **A:** Hat, sunglasses, shade, long sleeves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Water rules
*Student:* Learn swim with an adult, life jackets and no running by pools.

*How to teach:* Learn water rules.

- **Q:** Can you swim alone?
  **A:** No — always with an adult watching.
- **Q:** What keeps you safe in a boat?
  **A:** A life jacket.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safety book
*Student:* Make a safety mini-book with one rule per page.

*How to teach:* Make a safety mini-book with one rule per page.

- **Q:** Example rule.
  **A:** Always swim with an adult.
- **Q:** Another.
  **A:** Wear sunscreen and a hat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Safety assessment: sort safe/unsafe pictures and explain road, fire, 911, touch and sun rules.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 assessment.

- **Q:** Look which way before crossing?
  **A:** Left, right, left.
- **Q:** What do you do if your clothes catch fire?
  **A:** Stop, drop, cover your face, and roll.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test: sort safe/unsafe pictures (helmet on/off, crossing at crosswalk/between cars, swimming with adult/alone, sunscreen/no sunscreen) and explain: road — stop, look left-right-left, hold hands; fire — get out, stay out, meeting place, stop-drop-roll; 911 — real emergencies, give name and address; touch — No, Go, Tell; sun — sunscreen, hat, shade. Proficient = 90%+.
