# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 2

## ELA 1: Short i and high-frequency words

**Prepare:** Index cards for sight words (the, a, is, I, see, my), Elkonin sound-box strips (3 boxes drawn in a row) with 3 counters, 'The Snowy Day' by Ezra Jack Keats, a Starfall short a/i reader (starfall.com), and 12 picture cards (cat, pig, bag, pin, hat, fish, map, lip, van, wig, ham, six).

**Watch for:**
- Spelling pig as 'pg' (dropping the vowel): push a counter for every sound in the sound boxes — every word needs a vowel.
- Sounding out sight words letter by letter (t-h-e): explain 'the' is a heart word we learn by heart; practice it in many quick reads.
- Confusing short a and short i: use mouth cues — /ă/ has a wide-open mouth like a yawning 'apple,' /ĭ/ is a small 'itchy' smile.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sound boxes
*Student:* Push a counter into an Elkonin box for each sound in pig, sit, lip, win; then write the letters in the boxes.

*How to teach:* Say pig. Push one counter into a box for each sound: /p/ /ĭ/ /g/. Your child does sit, lip, win, then writes a letter in each box.

- **Q:** How many sounds in sit?
  **A:** 3: /s/ /ĭ/ /t/.
- **Q:** Spell win in the boxes.
  **A:** w | i | n.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sight word cards
*Student:* Make flash cards for the, a, is, I, see, my; play 'Go Fish' with them and read sentences like 'I see a pig.'

*How to teach:* Write the, a, is, I, see, my on two sets of cards and play Go Fish, reading each card asked for. Then read sentences like 'I see a pig.' with your child pointing to each word.

- **Q:** Read: 'I see my cat.'
  **A:** I see my cat.
- **Q:** Which word in 'The pig is big' is a sight word?
  **A:** The and is.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Read-aloud questions
*Student:* Read 'The Snowy Day' and ask/answer who, what, where questions about Peter's day; draw a favorite part.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Snowy Day.' Afterward ask who, what, and where questions, then your child draws their favorite part.

- **Q:** Who is the main character and where does the story happen?
  **A:** Peter, in his city neighborhood covered with snow.
- **Q:** What happened to the snowball Peter put in his pocket?
  **A:** It melted, and the pocket was empty.
- **Q:** Name two things Peter did in the snow.
  **A:** Examples: made tracks with toes in and out, dragged a stick, knocked snow off a tree, made a snow angel, slid down a snow hill.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Handwriting i, t, l
*Student:* Practice lowercase straight-line letters i, t, l and write tip, lit, it, sit.

*How to teach:* Model i, t, l as 'straight-line letters' that start at the top. Your child writes each 5 times, then writes tip, lit, it, sit.

- **Q:** Which letter crosses: t or l?
  **A:** t has a short cross line.
- **Q:** What goes on top of the i?
  **A:** A dot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read a short decodable text from Starfall (short a and i), then sort 12 picture cards by middle sound a or i.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the Starfall short a/i text aloud, then sorts the 12 picture cards under 'a' or 'i' by middle sound. Finish by reading the 6 sight words.

- **Q:** Sort: hat, fish, map, wig.
  **A:** a: hat, map; i: fish, wig.
- **Q:** What is the middle sound in six?
  **A:** /ĭ/.

**Assessment:** Picture sort accuracy and fluent reading of 6 sight words.

**Answer key:** Picture sort key: short a — cat, bag, hat, map, van, ham; short i — pig, pin, fish, lip, wig, six. Proficient = 11–12 correct. Sight words: the, a, is, I, see, my — proficient = all 6 read within about 3 seconds each.

## Math 1: Adding as putting together and adding to

**Prepare:** Counters (two colors, or beans painted on one side), a cup, drawing paper, and 6 short story problems written on cards (see the key).

**Watch for:**
- Writing only the answer (7) instead of the whole equation: model saying the story as an equation ('4 red and 3 green is 7') then write 4 + 3 = 7.
- Counting all from 1 every time: once the child knows one group, cover it and say 'You know there are 6 here — count on from 6.'
- Thinking the + sign means 'and then count again from 1': connect + to 'joined with' using two piles pushed together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Put together
*Student:* Act out 'I have 4 red apples and 3 green apples' with counters and write 4 + 3 = 7.

*How to teach:* Tell the story 'I have 4 red apples and 3 green apples' while your child lays out 4 red and 3 green counters. Push the groups together and count, then show how to write 4 + 3 = 7 and read it aloud. Repeat with 2 more put-together stories.

- **Q:** 2 dogs and 5 cats are in the yard. How many animals?
  **A:** 7; 2 + 5 = 7.
- **Q:** 3 blue cars and 3 red cars. How many cars?
  **A:** 6; 3 + 3 = 6.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Add to
*Student:* Solve 'add to' stories (5 birds, 2 more come) with drawings; write the equation.

*How to teach:* Tell 'add to' stories where more join: '5 birds sit on a fence. 2 more fly in.' Have your child draw 5 birds, then 2, circle all, and write 5 + 2 = 7. Ask what number the story starts with and what number joins.

- **Q:** 5 birds, 2 more come. How many now?
  **A:** 7; 5 + 2 = 7.
- **Q:** 6 kids are playing. 3 more join. How many now?
  **A:** 9; 6 + 3 = 9.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count on to add
*Student:* Add by counting on from the bigger number (6 + 2: 'six... seven, eight').

*How to teach:* Show 6 + 2 with counters: cover the 6 with your hand and say 'six... seven, eight' while touching the 2. Teach your child to start with the bigger number even if it comes second (2 + 6: start at 6).

- **Q:** 6 + 2 = ?
  **A:** 8 (six... seven, eight).
- **Q:** 2 + 7 = ? Which number do you start from?
  **A:** 9; start from 7, the bigger number, and count on 2.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Shake and spill
*Student:* Shake 6 two-color counters, spill, and record all the addition combinations for 6.

*How to teach:* Shake 6 two-color counters in a cup and spill. Your child records the red + yellow combination (e.g., 4 + 2 = 6). Repeat about 10 times and then list all the ways for 6 found.

- **Q:** List all the ways to make 6 with two colors.
  **A:** 0+6, 1+5, 2+4, 3+3, 4+2, 5+1, 6+0.
- **Q:** If 1 counter landed red, how many landed yellow?
  **A:** 5, because 1 + 5 = 6.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Solve 6 picture story problems and write an equation for each.

*How to teach:* Read each of 6 story problems aloud (your child should not have to read). Let your child draw or use counters, then write an equation for each.

- **Q:** Mom bought 3 pears and 4 bananas. How many pieces of fruit?
  **A:** 7; 3 + 4 = 7.
- **Q:** 5 ants were on a log. 4 more came. How many now?
  **A:** 9; 5 + 4 = 9.

**Assessment:** Story-problem quiz (6 problems) with equations.

**Answer key:** Suggested 6 problems and answers: 1) 2 cats and 4 dogs: 2 + 4 = 6. 2) 5 frogs, 3 more hop in: 5 + 3 = 8. 3) 1 red ball and 6 blue balls: 1 + 6 = 7. 4) 4 cookies, Dad gives 4 more: 4 + 4 = 8. 5) 7 fish, 2 more swim in: 7 + 2 = 9. 6) 3 apples and 7 apples: 3 + 7 = 10. Score: 1 point for the correct answer and 1 for a matching equation (12 points). 10–12 proficient; 7–9 developing; below 7 reteach with counters.

## Science 1 (NGSS): What engineers do: defining problems and designing

**Prepare:** 'Rosie Revere, Engineer' by Andrea Beaty (library), paper, tape, craft sticks, two books of equal height, a ruler, and a small toy figure.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking engineers only build big things like buildings: engineers design any solution to a problem — even a better spoon.
- Building before planning: sketch first, label materials, then build.
- Seeing a design that fails as a failure: Rosie learns that failing is how engineers learn what to improve.

### Day 1 (Monday): Problems around us
*Student:* Read about engineers (e.g., 'Rosie Revere, Engineer' by Andrea Beaty, library) and list 3 problems at home that a tool could fix.

*How to teach:* Read 'Rosie Revere, Engineer.' Talk about what engineers do (design solutions to problems). Your child lists 3 home problems a tool could fix.

- **Q:** What happened when Rosie's flying machine crashed?
  **A:** It flew briefly then crashed; her great-great-aunt Rose cheered because Rosie's first try was a success and you only fail when you quit.
- **Q:** What is an engineer?
  **A:** A person who designs and builds things to solve problems.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Define the problem
*Student:* Problem: a toy needs to cross a 20 cm 'river' between two books; draw the problem.

*How to teach:* Set up two books 20 cm apart as river banks. Your child draws the problem: a toy needs to cross the 'river.'

- **Q:** What's the problem?
  **A:** The toy needs a way to cross a 20 cm gap between two books.
- **Q:** What makes a solution successful?
  **A:** The toy can get across without the bridge falling.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sketch
*Student:* Sketch two bridge designs and label the materials.

*How to teach:* Your child sketches two bridge designs with labeled materials (paper, sticks, tape).

- **Q:** Why sketch more than one design?
  **A:** To compare ideas and choose the best.
- **Q:** What should labels tell?
  **A:** What material each part is made of.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Build
*Student:* Build your favorite design from paper and craft sticks.

*How to teach:* Your child builds the favorite design.

- **Q:** How long must the bridge be?
  **A:** More than 20 cm, so it rests on both books.
- **Q:** How can you make paper stronger?
  **A:** Fold it into a beam or accordion shape, or roll it into tubes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Test whether the toy can cross; tell what worked and what you would change.

*How to teach:* Test: can the toy cross? Your child tells what worked and what to change.

- **Q:** If the bridge sagged, what could you change?
  **A:** Add folds, more craft sticks, or supports.
- **Q:** Why do engineers test?
  **A:** To see if the design solves the problem and find what to improve.

**Assessment:** Design sketch and oral explanation of the problem and solution.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = sketch with labeled materials, clearly states the problem (the toy needs to cross a 20 cm gap) and explains how the solution solves it and one improvement; 3 = sketch and problem stated, explanation partial; 2 = sketch without labels or unclear problem; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: The Golden Rule and respecting others

**Prepare:** 'The Golden Rule' by Ilene Cooper (library), role-play cards (someone cuts in line; a friend is left out; someone takes your toy; a sibling is sad), and a kindness log (paper with 3 boxes).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Golden Rule means 'do what others do to you': it means treat others the way you want to be treated.
- Thinking disagreeing is always rude: you can disagree politely and respectfully.
- Thinking kindness must be big: small acts (holding a door, sharing) count.

### Day 1 (Monday): Golden Rule
*Student:* Read 'The Golden Rule' by Ilene Cooper and say the rule in your own words.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Golden Rule.' Your child says the rule in their own words.

- **Q:** What is the Golden Rule?
  **A:** Treat others the way you want to be treated.
- **Q:** According to the book, is the Golden Rule found in only one religion or culture?
  **A:** No — versions of it are found in many religions and cultures around the world.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Role-play
*Student:* Act out 4 situations (someone cuts in line, a friend is left out) and show a Golden Rule choice.

*How to teach:* Act out 4 situations and show a Golden Rule choice for each.

- **Q:** A friend is left out of a game. What's a Golden Rule choice?
  **A:** Invite them to join.
- **Q:** Someone cuts in line. What can you do?
  **A:** Calmly say 'The line starts back there, please,' or tell an adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Different opinions
*Student:* Survey family on a favorite food; practice saying 'I see it differently because...' politely.

*How to teach:* Survey family on favorite food; practice saying 'I see it differently because...' politely.

- **Q:** Your brother says pizza is the best food; you like tacos. What can you say?
  **A:** 'I see it differently because I like tacos more.'
- **Q:** Is it okay to have different opinions?
  **A:** Yes, as long as we're respectful.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kindness log
*Student:* Do and record 3 kind acts this week, drawing each in a kindness log.

*How to teach:* Your child does 3 kind acts this week and draws each in the log.

- **Q:** Name a kind act.
  **A:** Example: helping set the table, sharing a toy.
- **Q:** How do kind acts follow the Golden Rule?
  **A:** We're kind because we'd like others to be kind to us.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw a comic strip showing someone following the Golden Rule and explain it.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a comic strip of someone following the Golden Rule and explains it.

- **Q:** Explain your comic.
  **A:** Example: A girl shares her snack with a boy who forgot his, because she'd want help if she forgot hers.
- **Q:** Why is that the Golden Rule?
  **A:** She treats him the way she'd want to be treated.

**Assessment:** Golden Rule comic strip with explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = comic clearly shows a Golden Rule choice and explanation names how the character treats someone the way they'd want to be treated; 3 = clear comic, partial explanation; 2 = comic unclear; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Shapes in art; beat with rhymes

**Prepare:** Construction paper, scissors, glue, paint or crayons; image of Kandinsky's 'Squares with Concentric Circles' (search a museum site); a drum or pot and spoon; a ball.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking all shapes are geometric: organic shapes are free-form like leaves and puddles.
- Speeding up while chanting: keep patting slowly and evenly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kandinsky look
*Student:* Look at Kandinsky's 'Squares with Concentric Circles' and count the circles and squares; describe colors.

*How to teach:* Look at Kandinsky's 'Squares with Concentric Circles.' Count circles and squares and describe the colors.

- **Q:** What shapes do you see?
  **A:** Squares, each with circles inside circles.
- **Q:** What does 'concentric' mean?
  **A:** Circles inside each other sharing the same center, like a target.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Kandinsky circles
*Student:* Paint or crayon your own concentric circle squares on a 4-square grid.

*How to teach:* Fold paper into 4 squares. In each, draw or paint circles inside circles with different colors.

- **Q:** Are circles and squares geometric or organic shapes?
  **A:** Geometric.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rhyme beat
*Student:* Chant 'Hickory Dickory Dock' while tapping the beat on a drum or pot.

*How to teach:* Chant 'Hickory Dickory Dock' while tapping a steady beat on a drum or pot.

- **Q:** What is a chant?
  **A:** Words spoken in rhythm, not sung.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Beat in songs
*Student:* Play a beat-passing game with a ball to 'Bingo'.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Bingo' and pass a ball around the circle on each beat.

- **Q:** When does the ball move?
  **A:** On each beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Compare your circles art with Kandinsky's and tell one way they are alike.

*How to teach:* Compare your circles art with Kandinsky's and tell one way they are alike.

- **Q:** How is your art like Kandinsky's?
  **A:** Example: both use circles inside squares and bright colors.

**Assessment:** Circle-square artwork and chant with steady beat.

**Answer key:** Artwork: 4-square grid, each square filled with circles inside circles (concentric) in varied colors. Chant: 'Hickory Dickory Dock' with a steady beat on a drum. Proficient = concentric circles in each square + steady beat throughout.

## PE 1: Pathways: straight, curved, zigzag

**Prepare:** Sidewalk chalk and a jump rope or long string; a paved area or driveway. Preview your child's name letters for Day 3.

**Watch for:**
- Calling a curved path 'zigzag': zigzag has sharp corners; curved is smooth and round like a snake.
- Only walking pathways: show that any movement (skip, jog, gallop) can follow any pathway.

### Day 1 (Monday): Chalk paths
*Student:* Draw straight, curved and zigzag chalk paths and walk, skip and jog along each.

*How to teach:* Draw a straight line, a curvy line, and a zigzag line in chalk. Name each, then have your child walk, skip and jog along each one.

- **Q:** What does a zigzag pathway look like?
  **A:** Sharp corners going back and forth, like a lightning bolt.
- **Q:** What does a curved pathway look like?
  **A:** Smooth bends, like a snake or a letter S.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Follow the leader
*Student:* Follow a family member's pathway around the yard, then lead.

*How to teach:* Lead your child around the yard on a mix of pathways while they copy you; then switch so they lead. Ask them to name the pathway as they go.

- **Q:** What pathway did I just use?
  **A:** Answer matches the path you led, e.g., 'straight' for a line with no turns.
- **Q:** Make a curved path for me to follow.
  **A:** Leads you in a smooth, rounded path without sharp turns.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Letter paths
*Student:* Walk the pathway of your name's letters.

*How to teach:* Have your child walk the letters of their name in big shapes on the grass or pavement. Discuss which letters use straight lines (L, T), curves (C, S) or zigzags (Z, W).

- **Q:** Which pathway is in the letter S?
  **A:** Curved.
- **Q:** Which pathway is in the letter Z?
  **A:** Zigzag (straight lines with sharp corners).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Obstacle path
*Student:* Run an obstacle course with all three pathways.

*How to teach:* Set up a course with a straight rope line, a curved chalk path and a zigzag of cones. Run it twice, then time a third try for fun.

- **Q:** Which part of the course was zigzag?
  **A:** The cones you weaved back and forth through with sharp turns.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Name and demonstrate each pathway on command.

*How to teach:* Call out a pathway and a movement (e.g., 'zigzag gallop'). Check each is shown correctly.

- **Q:** Name the three pathways.
  **A:** Straight, curved and zigzag.

**Assessment:** Pathway demonstration.

**Answer key:** Pathway demonstration rubric: names and moves on straight (no turns), curved (smooth bends) and zigzag (sharp corners) on command. 3 correct = proficient; 2 = developing; 0-1 = beginning.

## Health 1: Calm-down strategies

**Prepare:** A pinwheel, a clear jar with water, glitter glue and glitter (for a calm-down jar; seal the lid with glue), a pillow, and GoNoodle Flow videos (gonoodle.com).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking calming down means hiding feelings: calming down helps us think so we can solve the problem.
- Breathing too fast: slow breaths — in through the nose like smelling a flower, out through the mouth like blowing a candle.
- Using the calm corner as a punishment: it's a helpful place to choose, not a time-out.

### Day 1 (Monday): Belly breathing
*Student:* Practice 'smell the flower, blow out the candle' breathing with a pinwheel.

*How to teach:* Practice 'smell the flower, blow out the candle' breathing with a pinwheel.

- **Q:** How do you belly breathe?
  **A:** Breathe in slowly through your nose (belly gets big), out slowly through your mouth.
- **Q:** Why does slow breathing help?
  **A:** It slows your heart and helps your body calm down.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Calm-down jar
*Student:* Make a glitter calm-down jar and watch the glitter settle while breathing.

*How to teach:* Make a glitter calm-down jar; watch it settle while breathing.

- **Q:** How is the jar like your feelings?
  **A:** Shaken glitter is like upset feelings; as you breathe, it settles and you calm down.
- **Q:** How long should you watch?
  **A:** Until most glitter settles.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mindful moment
*Student:* Do a GoNoodle Flow mindfulness video and tell how your body feels after.

*How to teach:* Do a GoNoodle Flow video; tell how your body feels after.

- **Q:** How did your body feel?
  **A:** Calmer, slower, relaxed.
- **Q:** When could you use this?
  **A:** When feeling upset, worried, or wiggly.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Calm corner
*Student:* Set up a calm corner at home with 3 tools (jar, pillow, feelings chart).

*How to teach:* Set up a calm corner with 3 tools.

- **Q:** Name 3 calm-corner tools.
  **A:** Calm-down jar, pillow, feelings chart.
- **Q:** When do you use it?
  **A:** When you feel big feelings and need to calm down.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Role-play a frustrating moment and use a calm-down strategy.

*How to teach:* Role-play a frustrating moment (tower falls) and use a strategy.

- **Q:** Your tower fell. What can you do?
  **A:** Take 3 belly breaths, use the calm jar, then try again or ask for help.
- **Q:** Is it okay to feel frustrated?
  **A:** Yes — it's what we do next that matters.

**Assessment:** Calm-down role-play.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names the feeling, chooses a calm-down strategy (belly breathing, calm jar, calm corner), uses it correctly, then names a next step; 3 = uses strategy with prompting; 2 = names strategy but doesn't use it; 1 = does not use a strategy.
