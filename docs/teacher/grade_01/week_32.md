# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 32

## ELA 1: Writing an imaginative narrative

**Prepare:** A story planner (characters, setting, problem, events, solution), lined paper, and a list of prepositions (under, over, in, behind, through, near).

**Watch for:**
- Story without a problem: ask 'What goes wrong for your character?'
- Ending abruptly: tell how the problem was solved and how the character felt.
- Run-on sentences joined with 'and then and then': stop with periods.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan an animal adventure story (inspired by Peter Rabbit) with a character, setting, problem and solution.

*How to teach:* Plan an animal adventure story with a character, setting, problem, and solution.

- **Q:** What is a setting?
  **A:** Where and when the story happens.
- **Q:** What is a problem in a story?
  **A:** Something that goes wrong for the character.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Draft beginning
*Student:* Write the beginning: who, where, and the problem.

*How to teach:* Write the beginning: who, where, and the problem.

- **Q:** Write a beginning sentence.
  **A:** Example: Once there was a squirrel named Sam who lived in a big oak tree.
- **Q:** What should the beginning tell?
  **A:** Who, where, and the problem.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Draft middle/end
*Student:* Write 2 events using next and then, and a solution with how the character felt.

*How to teach:* Write 2 events using next and then, and a solution with how the character felt.

- **Q:** What words show order?
  **A:** First, next, then, finally.
- **Q:** Write a solution sentence.
  **A:** Example: Finally, Sam found his acorns under a log and felt happy.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise & edit
*Student:* Add prepositional phrases for where things happened; fix capitals and end marks.

*How to teach:* Add prepositional phrases for where things happened; fix capitals and end marks.

- **Q:** Add a prepositional phrase: 'Sam hid.'
  **A:** Sam hid under a log.
- **Q:** Fix: 'sam ran home'
  **A:** Sam ran home.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Diphthong/vowel-sound assessment and narrative rubric score; read the story aloud.

*How to teach:* Give the diphthong/vowel-sound assessment and score the narrative; your child reads the story aloud.

- **Q:** Read: cloud, coin, cow, boy.
  **A:** cloud, coin, cow, boy.
- **Q:** Read: paw, moon.
  **A:** paw, moon.

**Assessment:** Narrative writing rubric and diphthong assessment.

**Answer key:** Narrative rubric: 4 = character, setting, problem, 2+ events with sequence words, solution and feeling, prepositional phrases, correct capitals/end marks; 3 = all parts, minor errors; 2 = missing problem or ending; 1 = incomplete. Diphthong/vowel list (suggested 20): out, house, cloud, cow, town, owl, coin, soil, boil, boy, toy, joy, moon, food, book, look, saw, paw, haul, launch — proficient 18+.

## Math 1: Defining attributes of 2-D shapes

**Prepare:** Cut-out paper shapes (triangles, squares, rectangles, hexagons, circles, trapezoids in different sizes and colors), straws cut into pieces, playdough, and a geoboard or Math Learning Center Geoboard app.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a 'tipped' triangle or rotated square isn't the same shape: turn the paper shape and recount sides and corners.
- Using color or size to name a shape: say 'shape names come from sides and corners, not color or size.'
- Thinking a square isn't a rectangle: a square has 4 straight sides and 4 square corners, so it is a special rectangle.

### Day 1 (Monday): Shape sort
*Student:* Sort cut-out shapes by number of sides and corners.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts shapes into groups by number of sides and corners (3, 4, 6, 0) and names each group.

- **Q:** How many sides and corners does a triangle have?
  **A:** 3 sides and 3 corners.
- **Q:** How many does a hexagon have?
  **A:** 6 sides and 6 corners.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Attributes
*Student:* Explain why a big red triangle and small blue triangle are both triangles.

*How to teach:* Show a big red triangle and a small blue triangle. Ask why both are triangles. Guide to 'both have 3 straight sides and 3 corners and are closed.'

- **Q:** Are these both triangles: a big red one and a small blue one?
  **A:** Yes — both have 3 straight sides and 3 corners; color and size don't matter.
- **Q:** Is a shape with 3 sides but a gap a triangle?
  **A:** No — it must be closed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Build
*Student:* Build shapes with straws and playdough.

*How to teach:* Your child builds shapes from straws joined by playdough balls: a triangle, a square, a rectangle, a hexagon. Count straws (sides) and balls (corners).

- **Q:** How many straws for a square?
  **A:** 4, all the same length.
- **Q:** How many playdough balls for a hexagon?
  **A:** 6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Geoboard
*Student:* Make shapes on the geoboard app with given attributes.

*How to teach:* Give attribute challenges on the geoboard: 'Make a shape with 4 sides that's not a square,' 'Make a triangle that touches 5 pegs on its sides.'

- **Q:** Make a shape with 4 sides and 4 square corners but not all sides equal. What is it?
  **A:** A rectangle (not a square).
- **Q:** Make a closed shape with 3 sides.
  **A:** A triangle.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Shape attribute quiz: circle shapes that are triangles/rectangles and explain why color and size do not matter.

*How to teach:* Give the attribute quiz: circle triangles/rectangles in a mixed set and write why color/size do not matter.

- **Q:** Why do color and size not matter?
  **A:** Shapes are named by sides and corners; a triangle is a triangle in any color or size.
- **Q:** Is a circle a polygon with sides?
  **A:** No — a circle has no straight sides or corners.

**Assessment:** Attribute quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz rubric: Circle all triangles (3 sides, 3 corners, closed) and rectangles (4 sides, 4 square corners, including squares) in the set; do not circle open shapes, circles, or shapes with curved sides. Explanation: 'Shapes are named by sides and corners; color and size don't change that.' Proficient: all shapes sorted correctly and explanation names sides/corners.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Young animals are like their parents

**Prepare:** Baby/adult animal photo pairs (10), Venn diagram paper, puppy and dog photos, a litter of kittens photo, and family photos.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking babies look exactly like parents: they're similar but not identical.
- Thinking all babies look like parents at birth: some change a lot (tadpole → frog, caterpillar → butterfly).
- Discussing family looks in hurtful ways: focus on noticing, not judging.

### Day 1 (Monday): Match the families
*Student:* Match 10 baby animal photos to their parents and name one trait that gave it away.

*How to teach:* Match 10 baby animal photos to parents; name a trait.

- **Q:** How did you match the zebra foal?
  **A:** Black-and-white stripes.
- **Q:** How did you match a duckling?
  **A:** Bill and webbed feet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Puppy and dog Venn
*Student:* Compare a puppy and its parent in a Venn diagram: ears, fur color, size, tail.

*How to teach:* Compare a puppy and its parent in a Venn diagram.

- **Q:** How are they alike?
  **A:** Same kind of ears, fur color, tail shape.
- **Q:** How are they different?
  **A:** Size — the puppy is smaller.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Not exactly alike
*Student:* Look at a photo of a litter of kittens and list differences between the kittens and their mother.

*How to teach:* Look at a kitten litter; list differences.

- **Q:** Are all kittens the same color?
  **A:** No — they may have different colors or patterns.
- **Q:** Why not?
  **A:** Babies get traits from both parents in different mixes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Family photos
*Student:* Compare photos of yourself, siblings and parents; find 2 shared traits and 2 differences.

*How to teach:* Compare family photos; find 2 shared traits and 2 differences.

- **Q:** Name a shared trait.
  **A:** Example: same hair color or eye color.
- **Q:** Name a difference.
  **A:** Example: different height.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Write 'Young animals are like their parents because... but not exactly because...' with evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes an evidence sentence.

- **Q:** Complete the sentence.
  **A:** Young animals are like their parents because they have the same kind of body parts, but not exactly because their size or colors can differ.
- **Q:** Evidence?
  **A:** The puppy has its mother's floppy ears but different spots.

**Assessment:** Evidence sentence and Venn diagram.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = Venn diagram with 2+ likenesses and 2+ differences and a sentence using photo evidence; 3 = sentence with partial evidence; 2 = only likenesses or differences; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Saving, spending and making choices

**Prepare:** Three clear jars labeled Save, Spend, Share; 10 pennies (or chore earnings); 'Bunny Money' by Rosemary Wells (library); and a PBS clip about banks if you can't visit one.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking saving means never spending: saving means putting money aside for something important later.
- Thinking you can buy everything you want: choosing one thing means giving up another (opportunity cost).
- Thinking banks keep the same coins you gave them: banks keep track of how much you have and keep money safe.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bunny Money
*Student:* Read 'Bunny Money' by Rosemary Wells and track how Ruby's money is spent on choices for Grandma's gift.

*How to teach:* Read 'Bunny Money'; track how Ruby's money is spent.

- **Q:** What did Ruby want to buy?
  **A:** A birthday present for Grandma.
- **Q:** Why did Ruby have less money for the gift?
  **A:** Money was spent along the way on other things, including things Max wanted (like vampire teeth), so the gift had to change.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Choices
*Student:* Given 50 cents and 3 items priced 20, 30 and 45 cents, decide what to buy and explain what you gave up.

*How to teach:* Given 50¢ and items at 20¢, 30¢, 45¢, decide what to buy and what you gave up.

- **Q:** Can you buy the 20¢ and 30¢ items together?
  **A:** Yes — 20 + 30 = 50¢.
- **Q:** If you buy the 45¢ item, what do you give up?
  **A:** Both the 20¢ and 30¢ items (you'd have 5¢ left).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Save, spend, share jars
*Student:* Label three jars and divide 10 pennies (or chore earnings) among them; explain your plan.

*How to teach:* Label jars; divide 10 pennies; explain the plan.

- **Q:** Example plan.
  **A:** 5 save, 3 spend, 2 share.
- **Q:** Why share?
  **A:** To help others.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Bank visit
*Student:* Visit a bank (or watch a PBS clip) to learn how banks keep money safe for savers.

*How to teach:* Visit a bank or watch a clip on how banks keep money safe.

- **Q:** What does a bank do?
  **A:** Keeps money safe and keeps track of how much you have.
- **Q:** What is a savings account?
  **A:** A place at a bank to keep money you're saving.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw a savings goal and write how many weeks of saving it will take with a family member's help.

*How to teach:* Your child draws a savings goal and figures weeks of saving.

- **Q:** A toy costs $5 and you save $1 a week. How many weeks?
  **A:** 5 weeks.
- **Q:** If you save $2 a week?
  **A:** 3 weeks (you'd have $6 after 3 weeks).

**Assessment:** Savings-goal drawing and oral explanation of a spending choice.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = savings goal drawing with price, weekly savings amount, and correct number of weeks (e.g., $6 toy, $2/week = 3 weeks), plus oral explanation of a choice and what was given up; 3 = minor math error; 2 = no plan; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Choosing work for a portfolio

**Prepare:** All artwork from the year, a folder; list of songs learned.

**Watch for:**
- Choosing only the newest work: choose work you're proudest of or that shows learning.
- Saying 'I like it' without why: give a reason.

### Day 1 (Monday): Review art
*Student:* Lay out all art from the year in order and sort it by medium (drawing, painting, collage, clay, print).

*How to teach:* Lay out all art in order; sort by medium.

- **Q:** What is a medium?
  **A:** The material used: paint, crayon, clay, paper, print.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Select
*Student:* Choose 6 favorites and tell why for each.

*How to teach:* Choose 6 favorites; tell why for each.

- **Q:** Why did you choose this one?
  **A:** Specific reason using art words.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Song choices
*Student:* Choose 3 favorite songs to perform in a spring concert.

*How to teach:* Choose 3 favorite songs for a spring concert.

- **Q:** Which songs did you choose?
  **A:** Names 3 songs.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the songs with expression.

*How to teach:* Rehearse with expression (dynamics, tempo).

- **Q:** How can you sing with expression?
  **A:** Change loud/soft and fast/slow to fit the words.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain choices for art and songs.

*How to teach:* Explain choices for art and songs.

- **Q:** What is a portfolio?
  **A:** A collection of your best work.

**Assessment:** Portfolio selection with reasons.

**Answer key:** Portfolio selection: 6 artworks with a reason for each (e.g., 'I used warm colors,' 'I worked hard on texture'). Proficient = 6 pieces from at least 3 media with specific reasons.

## PE 1: Playground and traditional games

**Prepare:** Chalk, a playground ball (kickball), 3 bases (paper plates), access to a playground.

**Watch for:**
- Freezing incorrectly in freeze tag: stay frozen until a teammate unfreezes you.
- Unsafe playground use: go down the slide feet first and one at a time.

### Day 1 (Monday): Tag games
*Student:* Play freeze tag and shadow tag.

*How to teach:* Play freeze tag, then shadow tag (tag by stepping on someone's shadow).

- **Q:** How do you tag in shadow tag?
  **A:** Step on the other person's shadow.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hopscotch
*Student:* Draw a hopscotch board and play with family, hopping on one foot in single squares.

*How to teach:* Draw a hopscotch board and play, hopping on one foot in single squares.

- **Q:** How do you land in a double square?
  **A:** With one foot in each square.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kickball
*Student:* Play mini kickball with 3 bases, kicking a rolled ball and running the bases.

*How to teach:* Play mini kickball: kick a rolled ball and run 3 bases.

- **Q:** Which way do you run the bases?
  **A:** First base, then second, then third, then home (counterclockwise).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Playground
*Student:* Visit a playground and practice safe play.

*How to teach:* Visit a playground; practice safe play rules.

- **Q:** How do you go down a slide safely?
  **A:** Sitting, feet first, one at a time, after the slide is clear.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 8 check
*Student:* Lead a family game teaching rules.

*How to teach:* Your child leads a family game and teaches the rules.

- **Q:** What did you need to explain first?
  **A:** The goal and rules of the game.

**Assessment:** Unit 8 game leadership.

**Answer key:** Leadership rubric: explains the game's rules clearly, demonstrates, keeps it safe, and plays fairly. Proficient = family can play after the explanation.

## Health 1: Tobacco smoke and vaping

**Prepare:** The balloon lung model from week 21, refusal skill cards, and poster paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only the smoker is harmed: secondhand smoke hurts others nearby.
- Thinking vapes are just water: they usually contain nicotine and chemicals that harm the body.
- Thinking saying no is rude: it's a healthy choice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Healthy lungs
*Student:* Revisit the balloon lung model and discuss what smoke does.

*How to teach:* Revisit the balloon lung model; discuss what smoke does.

- **Q:** What do lungs do?
  **A:** Bring air into the body.
- **Q:** What does smoke do to lungs?
  **A:** Damages them so breathing is harder.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Secondhand smoke
*Student:* Learn that breathing others' smoke harms health.

*How to teach:* Learn that breathing others' smoke harms health.

- **Q:** What is secondhand smoke?
  **A:** Smoke from someone else's cigarette that you breathe in.
- **Q:** What can you do?
  **A:** Move away and ask for smoke-free spaces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Say no
*Student:* Practice saying 'No thanks' and walking away.

*How to teach:* Practice saying 'No thanks' and walking away.

- **Q:** Someone offers you a vape. What do you say?
  **A:** No thanks — and walk away and tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Why walk away?
  **A:** To stay safe.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Smoke-free poster
*Student:* Make a smoke-free home poster.

*How to teach:* Make a smoke-free home poster.

- **Q:** What does the poster say?
  **A:** Example: Smoke-free home — healthy lungs live here!
- **Q:** Why smoke-free?
  **A:** To protect everyone's lungs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Assessment: identify the poison symbol, explain who may give medicine, and model saying no to smoke.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 7 assessment.

- **Q:** What does skull-and-crossbones mean?
  **A:** Poison.
- **Q:** Who may give medicine?
  **A:** A trusted adult.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test: 1) Identify the poison symbol (skull-and-crossbones/Mr. Yuk) 2) Explain only trusted adults give medicine 3) Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222 (with adult) 4) Model saying 'No thanks' to smoke/vape and walking away 5) Secondhand smoke hurts people nearby. Proficient 5/5.
