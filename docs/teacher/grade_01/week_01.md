# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 1

## ELA 1: Print concepts, sentences, and short a

**Prepare:** Gather letter tiles or magnetic letters, primary-lined paper, a printed copy of 'Hey Diddle Diddle' (Mother Goose, Project Gutenberg), 'Chrysanthemum' by Kevin Henkes from the library, and a list of 10 short-a words (cat, map, bag, sat, hat, man, pan, dad, rag, ham). Preview 'Chrysanthemum' — it deals gently with teasing about a name.

**Watch for:**
- Writing a sentence without a capital or period: say 'Every sentence starts with a capital and stops with a period' and have your child point to both in every sentence they write.
- Reading CVC words by guessing from the first letter (cat → 'cup'): have your child touch and say each sound, then slide a finger under the word to blend.
- Confusing letters and words when pointing: frame each word with two fingers and count the words in a line together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sentence detectives
*Student:* Read aloud 'Hey Diddle Diddle' and point to each word; circle the capital letter and period in three printed sentences.

*How to teach:* Read 'Hey Diddle Diddle' aloud, pointing under each word as you read so your child sees that words are read left to right with spaces between. Then show three printed sentences; your child circles the capital letter at the start and the period at the end of each.

- **Q:** In 'Hey Diddle Diddle,' what jumped over the moon?
  **A:** The cow.
- **Q:** Who ran away with the spoon?
  **A:** The dish.
- **Q:** In 'The dog ran.', where is the capital and where is the period?
  **A:** The capital T at the start of The; the period after ran.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Short a sound
*Student:* Say /a/ words (cat, map, bag), isolate the middle sound, then build cat, mat, sat, hat with letter tiles and read each one.

*How to teach:* Say cat slowly, stretching the middle: /c/ /ă/ /t/, and ask which sound is in the middle. Your child builds cat with tiles, then swaps the first tile to make mat, sat, hat, reading each word by touching each tile and blending.

- **Q:** What is the middle sound in map?
  **A:** /ă/ (short a).
- **Q:** Change the c in cat to h. What word?
  **A:** hat.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Handwriting a, c, d
*Student:* Practice lowercase a, c, d (circle letters) on lined paper; write the CVC words bad, cad, dad.

*How to teach:* Model lowercase a, c, d as 'circle letters': start at the 2 o'clock position and curve back around. Your child writes each letter 5 times, then writes bad, cad, dad, leaving a finger space between words.

- **Q:** Where do you start a lowercase c?
  **A:** Just below the top line on the right side (2 o'clock), curving back around to the left.
- **Q:** Which letter is a circle plus a tall line: a or d?
  **A:** d — its line goes up to the top line.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Talking rules
*Student:* Read 'Chrysanthemum' (Kevin Henkes, library) and practice listening rules: take turns, look at the speaker, ask one question about Chrysanthemum's name.

*How to teach:* Review three listening rules: take turns, look at the speaker, ask a question. Read 'Chrysanthemum' and stop when the girls tease her name to ask how she feels. Your child asks you one question about her name.

- **Q:** How does Chrysanthemum feel about her name at the start of the story?
  **A:** She loves it; she thinks it is perfect.
- **Q:** Why does her name start to bother her at school?
  **A:** Other children (led by Victoria) laugh at it because it is long — it has 13 letters — and is the name of a flower.
- **Q:** What makes her love her name again?
  **A:** The music teacher, Mrs. Twinkle, says her own name is long too and she will name her baby Chrysanthemum.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read 10 short-a CVC words aloud and write a dictated sentence 'The cat sat.' with capital and period; tally correct sounds.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the 10 short-a words while you tally correct words. Then dictate 'The cat sat.' slowly, reminding them to start with a capital and end with a period.

- **Q:** Read: ham, pan, rag.
  **A:** ham, pan, rag (each with short a).
- **Q:** Spell sat.
  **A:** s-a-t.

**Assessment:** Oral CVC reading check (8/10 correct) and dictated sentence with correct capital and period.

**Answer key:** Oral CVC check: 10 short-a words (cat, map, bag, sat, hat, man, pan, dad, rag, ham) — goal 8/10 read correctly by blending. Dictated sentence: 'The cat sat.' — 1 point capital T, 1 point correct spelling of cat and sat, 1 point period, 1 point spaces between words. Proficient = 8/10 words and 4/4 on the sentence.

## Math 1: Counting and writing numbers to 20

**Prepare:** Gather 3–4 zip bags of 8–20 beans or buttons, two ten-frame mats (draw a 2×5 grid on paper), a 0–20 number line, sticky notes, and dot/ten-frame cards (draw 0–10 dots on index cards).

**Watch for:**
- Counting the same object twice or skipping one: have your child move each bean onto a ten-frame square as they say each number (one touch, one word).
- Writing numerals backward (e.g., Ɛ for 3): use the 'start at the top' rule and trace a model with a finger before writing.
- Counting on by starting with the first number ('7, 8, 9' for 7 + 3 = 9 gives only 9 if you count 7 as a jump): teach that you say the start number, then count 3 more jumps: 'seven... eight, nine, ten.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Count collections
*Student:* Count bags of 8–20 beans, organizing them on ten-frames, and write the numeral on a sticky note.

*How to teach:* Hand your child a bag of beans and say, 'Let's find out how many.' Model placing one bean in each ten-frame box, filling the top row left to right, then the bottom row. When a frame is full, say 'That's ten,' then count on from ten for the extras and write the numeral on a sticky note.

- **Q:** A full ten-frame and 4 more beans. How many beans?
  **A:** 14 — ten in the full frame, then 11, 12, 13, 14.
- **Q:** Count 17 beans onto ten-frames. How many frames are full?
  **A:** 1 full frame (10) with 7 beans in the second frame.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write numerals
*Student:* Practice writing 0–20 correctly; fix reversals of 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 with tracing and a 'start at the top' rule.

*How to teach:* Show each numeral 0–20 on a model strip. Say the rule: 'Every number starts at the top.' Have your child trace 2, 3, 5, 7, and 9 with a finger in the air, then on paper three times each, then write 0–20 in order while you watch for reversals.

- **Q:** Write 13 and 15. Which way do the 3 and 5 face?
  **A:** Both open to the left side (bumps/belly face right); 13 is a 1 then a 3, 15 is a 1 then a 5.
- **Q:** Where do you start writing a 5?
  **A:** At the top: draw the short line down, the belly around, then the flag across the top.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count on
*Student:* Start at a number on the 0–20 number line (e.g., 7) and count on 3; play 'Start at ___, count on ___'.

*How to teach:* Put a finger on 7 on the number line and say 'seven,' then hop 3 times saying 'eight, nine, ten.' Explain you land on the answer. Play 'Start at ___, count on ___' with your child choosing start numbers under 17 and counting on 1, 2, or 3.

- **Q:** Start at 7, count on 3. Where do you land?
  **A:** 10 (8, 9, 10).
- **Q:** Start at 12, count on 2.
  **A:** 14 (13, 14).
- **Q:** Start at 16, count on 4.
  **A:** 20 (17, 18, 19, 20).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Quick images
*Student:* Look at a dot or ten-frame card for 3 seconds and tell how many; explain how you saw it.

*How to teach:* Flash a dot or ten-frame card for 3 seconds, then hide it. Ask 'How many? How did you see it?' Praise seeing groups ('I saw 5 on top and 2 more') rather than counting by ones.

- **Q:** A ten-frame shows a full top row and 3 dots on the bottom. How many?
  **A:** 8 — 5 on top and 3 more.
- **Q:** A ten-frame has all boxes full except 1. How many dots?
  **A:** 9 — one less than 10.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Count 3 collections, write numerals 0–20 from dictation, and count on from 5 numbers.

*How to teach:* Run the check as a short, friendly interview. Have your child count 3 collections aloud while you watch for one-to-one matching, then dictate numerals (e.g., 6, 12, 19, 0, 15, 20) and give 5 count-on starts.

- **Q:** Start at 5, count on 3.
  **A:** 8.
- **Q:** Write the number nineteen.
  **A:** 19.

**Assessment:** Counting interview (collections up to 20) and numeral dictation.

**Answer key:** Counting interview: Proficient = counts each collection (e.g., 9, 14, 20) with one touch per object, says the last number as 'how many', and organizes on ten-frames. Count-on: 5+3→8, 9+2→11, 11+3→14, 14+4→18, 17+3→20. Dictation answers: whatever numbers you say written correctly with no reversals (e.g., 6, 12, 19, 0, 15, 20). 4 = all correct; 3 = one error or reversal; 2 = counts but loses track over 15 or several reversals; 1 = cannot count past 10 reliably — reteach with ten-frames.

## Science 1 (NGSS): What scientists do: observing and asking questions

**Prepare:** A magnifying glass, a science notebook (composition book), pencils/crayons, a leaf, rock and shell, a paper bag with 4 household objects (e.g., spoon, cotton ball, key, pinecone), and a jar for 'I wonder' questions.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up observations and guesses ('the rock is old'): an observation is something you notice with your senses ('the rock is gray and bumpy').
- Thinking scientists only work in labs: scientists observe anywhere — backyards, parks, kitchens.
- Tasting unknown things on a senses walk: we never taste things outdoors; we use sight, hearing, smell (wafting) and touch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Five senses walk
*Student:* Take a backyard or park walk and record one thing you see, hear, smell and touch in a science notebook.

*How to teach:* Walk outside for 15 minutes. Stop at 4 spots and ask 'What do you see? hear? smell? feel?' Your child draws or writes one thing per sense in the notebook. Remind them: no tasting outdoors.

- **Q:** Which senses did we use on the walk?
  **A:** Sight, hearing, smell, and touch (not taste — we don't taste unknown things outside).
- **Q:** Give an observation of something you heard.
  **A:** Example: I heard a bird chirping.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Magnifier lab
*Student:* Observe a leaf, rock and shell with a magnifying glass; draw and label details you couldn't see before.

*How to teach:* Have your child draw the leaf, rock, and shell first without the magnifier, then again with it. Compare: what new details appeared? Label at least two details.

- **Q:** What does a magnifying glass do?
  **A:** It makes things look bigger so we can see small details.
- **Q:** Name a detail you saw only with the magnifier.
  **A:** Example: tiny veins on the leaf, sparkly bits in the rock, lines on the shell.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Question jar
*Student:* Write or dictate 5 'I wonder' questions about nature and sort them into 'can test' and 'look up'.

*How to teach:* Your child dictates 5 'I wonder' questions. Sort them: questions we can test ourselves (Does a ball roll farther on carpet or floor?) and questions we look up (How old is the Earth?).

- **Q:** Is 'Do plants grow faster with more water?' a can-test or look-up question?
  **A:** Can-test — we could grow plants with different amounts of water.
- **Q:** Is 'How far away is the moon?' can-test or look-up?
  **A:** Look-up — we can't measure it ourselves.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mystery bag
*Student:* Identify 4 hidden objects by touch only and describe the clues you used.

*How to teach:* Put 4 objects in a bag. Your child reaches in, feels one, and describes clues (soft, hard, cold, pointy) before guessing.

- **Q:** What clues help you identify a cotton ball by touch?
  **A:** Soft, light, fluffy, squishes.
- **Q:** Which sense did you use?
  **A:** Touch.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain to a family member how scientists observe and ask questions, using your notebook drawings.

*How to teach:* Your child explains to a family member how scientists observe and ask questions, using notebook drawings.

- **Q:** What do scientists do first?
  **A:** Observe carefully and ask questions.
- **Q:** Why do scientists draw and label?
  **A:** To record exactly what they noticed so they and others can look back later.

**Assessment:** Science notebook observation entries with labeled drawings.

**Answer key:** Rubric for notebook entries: 4 = at least 4 dated entries with drawings and 2+ labels each, observations use the senses (color, shape, texture, sound); 3 = entries with 1 label each; 2 = drawings without labels or mostly guesses; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Why do we have rules?

**Prepare:** A simple board game, chart paper and markers, picture cards of community signs (stop sign, crosswalk, library 'quiet,' park rules), and 'What If Everybody Did That?' by Ellen Javernick (library).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking rules are only to keep kids from having fun: show how rules keep people safe and make things fair.
- Thinking rules only exist at home or school: point out community rules (traffic signs, park hours).
- Confusing rights with doing whatever you want: rights come with responsibilities.

### Day 1 (Monday): No-rules game
*Student:* Play a board game with no rules for two minutes, then talk about what went wrong and why rules matter.

*How to teach:* Play a board game for two minutes with no rules (anyone can move anywhere). Then stop and ask what went wrong and why rules matter.

- **Q:** What happened without rules?
  **A:** Answers vary: arguing, confusion, unfair play, no one knew who won.
- **Q:** Why do games have rules?
  **A:** To make play fair and help everyone know what to do.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rules around us
*Student:* Walk the neighborhood and photograph or draw 5 signs that show rules (stop sign, crosswalk, park rules); tell what each keeps safe.

*How to teach:* Walk the neighborhood. Your child draws or photographs 5 signs and says what each keeps safe.

- **Q:** What does a stop sign keep safe?
  **A:** Drivers, walkers, and bikers — cars stop so no one crashes.
- **Q:** Why is there a crosswalk?
  **A:** To show people a safe place to cross where drivers expect them.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Home rules chart
*Student:* Work with family to write 5 homeschool rules on chart paper; draw a picture for each.

*How to teach:* Write 5 home/homeschool rules together and illustrate each.

- **Q:** Give a good home rule and its reason.
  **A:** Example: Put toys away — so no one trips and things don't get lost.
- **Q:** Who should follow home rules?
  **A:** Everyone in the family.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rights and responsibilities
*Student:* Read 'What If Everybody Did That?' (Ellen Javernick, library) and match 3 rights with 3 responsibilities.

*How to teach:* Read 'What If Everybody Did That?' and match 3 rights with 3 responsibilities.

- **Q:** What does the book ask about small actions like dropping litter?
  **A:** What would happen if everybody did that — small actions add up to big problems.
- **Q:** Match: the right to use the park.
  **A:** Responsibility to keep it clean.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one home rule and one community rule and what could happen without each.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one home rule and one community rule and what could happen without each.

- **Q:** What would happen without traffic lights?
  **A:** Cars could crash and people could get hurt.
- **Q:** Why do we have a rule to wash hands before eating?
  **A:** To keep germs away so we don't get sick.

**Assessment:** Oral explanation of two rules and their purpose.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = states a home rule and a community rule, explains the purpose of each (safety, fairness, order), and what could happen without it; 3 = two rules with one clear purpose; 2 = names rules without purposes; 1 = cannot name a rule.

## Art & Music 1: Kinds of lines; finding the beat

**Prepare:** Crayons, large paper, masking tape for floor lines; 'Harold and the Purple Crayon' by Crockett Johnson (library); a recording of Sousa's 'Stars and Stripes Forever'; a ticking clock and a rain-sounds clip.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a line has to be straight: lines can curve, zigzag, wave, spiral or be dotted.
- Clapping the rhythm of the words instead of the steady beat: the beat is even like a heartbeat or clock.

### Day 1 (Monday): Line walk
*Student:* Walk and trace straight, curvy, zigzag, wavy, dotted and spiral lines in the air and on the floor with tape; draw each on paper.

*How to teach:* Make tape lines on the floor (straight, curvy, zigzag, wavy, dotted, spiral). Walk each one, trace it in the air, then draw each on paper while saying its name.

- **Q:** What does a zigzag line look like?
  **A:** It goes back and forth with sharp points, like a lightning bolt.
- **Q:** Draw a spiral line.
  **A:** A line that curls around and around a center, like a snail shell.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Line picture
*Student:* Read 'Harold and the Purple Crayon' (Crockett Johnson, library) and draw a one-line adventure using 4 kinds of lines.

*How to teach:* Read 'Harold and the Purple Crayon'; point out that Harold creates his whole adventure with one line. Your child draws a one-line adventure using at least 4 kinds of lines.

- **Q:** What does Harold use to make his adventure?
  **A:** His purple crayon; everything he draws becomes real.
- **Q:** Which kinds of lines did you use?
  **A:** Names at least 4 kinds that appear in the drawing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Find the beat
*Student:* Pat a steady beat on knees to 'Stars and Stripes Forever'; march around the room on the beat.

*How to teach:* Play 'Stars and Stripes Forever.' Pat knees on the steady beat, then march around the room, one step per beat.

- **Q:** What is a steady beat?
  **A:** An even pulse in music that keeps going, like a heartbeat or clock tick.
- **Q:** What kind of music is 'Stars and Stripes Forever'?
  **A:** A march (by John Philip Sousa).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Beat vs. no beat
*Student:* Listen to a ticking clock, a march and rain sounds; decide which have a steady beat.

*How to teach:* Listen to a ticking clock, the march, and rain sounds. After each, your child decides: steady beat or not?

- **Q:** Does a ticking clock have a steady beat?
  **A:** Yes.
- **Q:** Do rain sounds have a steady beat?
  **A:** No, the drops fall unevenly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Show your line picture and name its lines; lead family in a beat-keeping game.

*How to teach:* Your child shows the line picture and names the lines, then leads a beat game (patting or clapping a steady beat to a song while family copies).

- **Q:** Point to and name 4 lines in your picture.
  **A:** Names 4 line types correctly.

**Assessment:** Line picture labeled with 4 line types and steady-beat check.

**Answer key:** Line picture: labels 4 line types correctly (e.g., straight, curvy, zigzag, spiral, wavy, dotted). Beat check: pats/marches steadily with 'Stars and Stripes Forever' for 16 beats. Proficient = 4 labeled lines + steady beat; developing = 2-3 lines or beat drifts; beginning = 0-1 lines or no steady beat.

## PE 1: Personal space and general space

**Prepare:** Hula hoop and 6-8 cones or plastic cups; clear a yard or room space free of furniture and tripping hazards. Choose a clear 'Stop' signal (word or whistle).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking personal space means standing still: you can move arms and legs, you just stay in your own bubble.
- Running too fast to stop safely: practice slow-to-fast and stop on the signal with feet planted and hands to self.

### Day 1 (Monday): Space bubble
*Student:* Warm up with arm circles, then stand in a hula hoop 'bubble' and explore reaching high, low and wide without leaving it.

*How to teach:* Warm up with 10 arm circles each way. Lay the hoop down; your child stands in it and reaches high, low, and wide while you say 'Your bubble is your personal space.' Call out body parts to stretch toward the edge without stepping out.

- **Q:** What is personal space?
  **A:** The space all around your body that you can reach without moving from your spot (your bubble).
- **Q:** Can you move in your personal space?
  **A:** Yes, you can stretch, twist and reach as long as your feet stay in your spot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): General space
*Student:* Walk, then jog, around cones in the yard without touching cones or people; freeze on 'Stop'.

*How to teach:* Set cones around the yard. Have your child walk, then jog, among them without touching cones or people, freezing when you say 'Stop.' Point out that the whole shared area is general space.

- **Q:** What is general space?
  **A:** The open shared area everyone can move in.
- **Q:** What do you do when you hear 'Stop'?
  **A:** Freeze right away with feet still and balanced.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Stop and go
*Student:* Play red light/green light focusing on safe stops.

*How to teach:* Play red light/green light. Before playing, model a safe stop: slow down, bend knees, feet apart. Send back anyone who slides or falls past the line to start over.

- **Q:** How do you make a safe stop?
  **A:** Slow down, bend your knees, put feet apart and keep your balance.
- **Q:** Why is stopping fast important?
  **A:** So you don't bump into people or objects and get hurt.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Safety rules
*Student:* Make 3 PE safety rules (stay in your space, listen for stop, take care of equipment) and practice them in a tag game.

*How to teach:* Together write or draw 3 rules: stay in your space, listen for stop, take care of equipment. Play a gentle tag game (two-finger touches only) and stop the game to praise rule-following.

- **Q:** Name one PE safety rule.
  **A:** Stay in your space / listen for stop / take care of equipment.
- **Q:** How do you tag safely?
  **A:** Gentle touch with fingers on the back or arm, no pushing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Show personal vs. general space on command and explain why a warm-up comes first.

*How to teach:* Call 'personal space' and 'general space' and have your child show each. Ask why we warm up first; check that the answer mentions getting muscles and heart ready.

- **Q:** Show personal space, then general space.
  **A:** Personal: stays in one spot and reaches; general: moves around the open area safely.
- **Q:** Why do we warm up first?
  **A:** To get our muscles warm and heart pumping so we move better and don't get hurt.

**Assessment:** Teacher observation of safe stopping and spacing.

**Answer key:** Observation checklist: stops within 2 seconds of the signal with balanced feet; keeps a safe distance from people and cones; shows personal space (stays in one spot reaching high/low/wide) vs. general space (moves in open area). Proficient = all three most of the time; developing = needs reminders; beginning = bumps or does not stop.

## Health 1: Naming feelings

**Prepare:** 'The Way I Feel' by Janan Cain (library), feelings face cards (happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised, proud), a mirror, and a feelings chart for the fridge.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking some feelings are 'bad': all feelings are okay; what we do with them matters.
- Using only 'happy,' 'sad,' and 'mad': teach more words like frustrated, proud, nervous, disappointed.
- Thinking feelings are only in our heads: our bodies show clues (tight fists, fast heartbeat, butterflies).

### Day 1 (Monday): Feelings book
*Student:* Read 'The Way I Feel' by Janan Cain and name the feeling on each page.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Way I Feel' and name the feeling on each page. Point to faces and bodies in the pictures.

- **Q:** How can you tell a character is angry in the pictures?
  **A:** Red face, frown, tight body or fists.
- **Q:** Name a feeling from the book.
  **A:** Examples: silly, scared, disappointed, happy, sad, angry, bored, frustrated, shy, jealous, proud, excited, thankful.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mirror faces
*Student:* Make happy, sad, angry, scared, surprised and proud faces in a mirror; draw two of them.

*How to teach:* Make faces in a mirror for each feeling; draw two.

- **Q:** What does a surprised face look like?
  **A:** Wide eyes, raised eyebrows, open mouth.
- **Q:** What does a proud face look like?
  **A:** Big smile, head up, standing tall.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Body clues
*Student:* Talk about where you feel feelings in your body (tight fists, butterflies in tummy).

*How to teach:* Talk about where you feel feelings in your body.

- **Q:** Where might you feel scared?
  **A:** Fast heartbeat, butterflies in tummy, shaky legs.
- **Q:** Where might you feel angry?
  **A:** Hot face, tight fists, clenched teeth.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feelings check-in
*Student:* Start a daily feelings chart: point to how you feel each morning and tell why.

*How to teach:* Start a daily feelings chart; your child points and tells why.

- **Q:** Why check in on feelings?
  **A:** To notice and name how we feel so we can handle it.
- **Q:** Complete: I feel ___ because ___.
  **A:** Example: I feel excited because we're going to the park.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Match 6 feeling words to face pictures and tell about a time you felt one.

*How to teach:* Match 6 feeling words to faces; tell about a time.

- **Q:** Match 'sad.'
  **A:** Face with frown and tears.
- **Q:** Tell a time you felt proud.
  **A:** Example: when I learned to ride my bike.

**Assessment:** Feeling-word matching and personal example.

**Answer key:** Matching key: happy — smile; sad — frown/tears; angry — frown with furrowed brows; scared — wide eyes, open mouth, hands up; surprised — raised brows, round mouth; proud — big smile, head up. Proficient: 6/6 plus a personal example that matches the feeling.
