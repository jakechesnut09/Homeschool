# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 15

## ELA 1: Long u and e with silent e; pictures vs. words

**Prepare:** 'Frogs' by Gail Gibbons or another illustrated animal book, index cards, a ReadWorks grade 1 animal passage (free), a main-topic organizer, and a 10-word silent-e spelling list.

**Watch for:**
- Naming one detail as the main topic: the main topic is what the whole text is mostly about.
- Thinking pictures are just decoration: diagrams and captions give information too.
- Copying whole sentences from books as 'facts': write a few key words on each card.

### Day 1 (Monday): u_e and e_e
*Student:* Read cube, tune, mule, these, Pete; mixed silent-e word sort by vowel.

*How to teach:* Read cube, tune, mule, these, Pete. Sort mixed silent-e words by vowel.

- **Q:** Read: cube, these.
  **A:** cube, these.
- **Q:** Which vowel is in mule?
  **A:** Long u.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Main topic
*Student:* Read a ReadWorks grade 1 animal passage; name the main topic and two key details.

*How to teach:* Read a ReadWorks animal passage. Your child names the main topic and two key details.

- **Q:** What is a main topic?
  **A:** What the whole passage is mostly about.
- **Q:** What is a key detail?
  **A:** An important fact that tells more about the main topic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Picture vs. words
*Student:* Look at a diagram and caption in an animal book; tell one fact you learned from the picture and one from the words.

*How to teach:* Look at a diagram and caption. Your child tells one fact learned from the picture and one from the words.

- **Q:** What does a caption do?
  **A:** It explains what the picture shows.
- **Q:** What does a diagram with labels show?
  **A:** The parts of something, named.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fact cards
*Student:* Choose an animal for a mini report; write 3 facts on index cards from a library book.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses an animal and writes 3 facts on index cards from a library book.

- **Q:** Is 'Owls are cool' a fact?
  **A:** No — it's an opinion. A fact can be proved, e.g., 'Owls hunt at night.'
- **Q:** Where did you find your facts?
  **A:** Name of the library book.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Silent-e spelling test (10 words); main topic + 2 details organizer.

*How to teach:* Give the 10-word silent-e spelling test, then complete the main-topic + 2 details organizer.

- **Q:** Spell bike.
  **A:** b-i-k-e.
- **Q:** Spell home.
  **A:** h-o-m-e.

**Assessment:** Silent-e spelling test and main-topic organizer.

**Answer key:** Spelling: cake, game, bike, five, home, rope, cube, tune, these, nose — proficient 9–10. Organizer: main topic (e.g., 'Frogs') plus 2 accurate details from the passage — proficient = both details match the text.

## Math 1: Problem solving within 20

**Prepare:** Counters, number line 0–20, Khan Academy login, paper for illustrated problems, and a Unit 4 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Using key words only ('more' = add) without understanding: always ask the child to retell the story and show it with a model.
- Listing only a few ways to make 15: be systematic — 15 + 0, 14 + 1, 13 + 2 ... 8 + 7.
- Writing story problems that have no question: remind that a story problem ends with 'How many...?'

### Day 1 (Monday): Problem types
*Student:* Solve one of each: add to, take from, put together, compare within 20.

*How to teach:* Present one of each problem type (add to, take from, put together, compare) within 20. For each, your child models with counters before writing an equation.

- **Q:** 12 birds; 5 fly away. How many left?
  **A:** 7.
- **Q:** Ana has 13 beads, Joe has 9. How many more does Ana have?
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** 8 red and 7 blue blocks. How many blocks?
  **A:** 15.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Math talk
*Student:* Solve 'How many ways can you make 15?' and explain to a family member.

*How to teach:* Ask 'How many ways can you make 15 by adding two numbers?' Your child lists them in order and explains the pattern to a family member.

- **Q:** List the ways to make 15 with two numbers 0–15.
  **A:** 0+15, 1+14, 2+13, 3+12, 4+11, 5+10, 6+9, 7+8 (plus turn-arounds) — 16 ordered pairs in all.
- **Q:** If one part is 6, what is the other?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fact practice
*Student:* Khan Academy 'add and subtract within 20' practice set.

*How to teach:* Sit with your child for a Khan Academy 'add and subtract within 20' set. Note missed facts and practice them with cards afterward.

- **Q:** 9 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 16.
- **Q:** 18 − 9 = ?
  **A:** 9.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Create problems
*Student:* Write and illustrate two story problems for someone else to solve.

*How to teach:* Your child writes and illustrates two story problems (one adding, one subtracting) for a family member to solve, and writes the answer key on the back.

- **Q:** Check: 'I had 15 crayons. I lost 6. How many now?'
  **A:** 9.
- **Q:** What must every story problem have at the end?
  **A:** A question, like 'How many ... ?'

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 4 check
*Student:* Unit test: facts and story problems within 20.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 4 test, reading story problems aloud.

- **Q:** 8 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 14.
- **Q:** 17 − 8 = ?
  **A:** 9.

**Assessment:** Unit 4 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 4 test (suggested): Facts: 9+4=13, 8+7=15, 6+6=12, 7+5=12, 15−8=7, 13−6=7, 16−7=9, 11−4=7. Stories: 1) 9 cats, 8 dogs → 17 2) 14 apples, 6 eaten → 8 3) Al has 12, Bo has 7, how many more does Al have → 5 4) 4 + 5 + 6 pencils → 15. Proficient 11–12 of 12; developing 8–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Building a shadow-stick sundial

**Prepare:** A paper plate, a pencil, modeling clay, a marker, a clock, a compass, a coin, and a sunny spot that gets sun from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the shadow moves because the plate turned: keep the plate in the same place; the sun's position changes.
- Thinking a sundial works at night or on cloudy days: it needs sunlight to make a shadow.
- Thinking the sundial and clock should match exactly: sundial times can be off from clock time (daylight saving time, location).

### Day 1 (Monday): Build a sundial
*Student:* Push a pencil through the center of a paper plate, anchor it with clay in a sunny spot, and point the plate's '12' mark north.

*How to teach:* Push a pencil through the plate center, anchor with clay, and point the '12' mark north with a compass.

- **Q:** Why point the 12 north?
  **A:** In California, the noon shadow points north.
- **Q:** What will make the shadow?
  **A:** The pencil blocking sunlight.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mark the hours
*Student:* Every hour from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., mark the pencil's shadow on the plate and write the clock time beside it.

*How to teach:* Every hour from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., mark the shadow and write the time.

- **Q:** Which way does the shadow move during the day?
  **A:** From the west side, around through north, to the east side (clockwise).
- **Q:** When is the shadow shortest?
  **A:** Around midday.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Predict and test
*Student:* Before checking, predict where tomorrow's 10 a.m. shadow will fall; place a coin there and see if the shadow lands on it.

*How to teach:* Predict where tomorrow's 10 a.m. shadow will fall; place a coin; check.

- **Q:** Where should you put the coin?
  **A:** On today's 10 a.m. mark.
- **Q:** Why does it work?
  **A:** The sun follows nearly the same path every day.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sundial vs. clock
*Student:* Read the time from your sundial and from a clock at 3 different times; record both in a table.

*How to teach:* Read time on the sundial and clock at 3 times; record.

- **Q:** Why might they be different?
  **A:** Daylight saving time and our location can make sundial time differ from clock time.
- **Q:** What do you notice about the pattern?
  **A:** The difference stays about the same each time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain to a family member why the shadow moves (the sun's position changes) using your plate as evidence.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why the shadow moves.

- **Q:** Why does the shadow move?
  **A:** Because the sun's position in the sky changes as the Earth spins.
- **Q:** Would the sundial work at night?
  **A:** No — there's no sunlight.

**Assessment:** Sundial with hourly marks and a correct oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = sundial with hourly marks 9–3 labeled, and explanation 'The shadow moves because the sun's position in the sky changes'; 3 = most marks and partial explanation; 2 = few marks; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Essential documents: Declaration of Independence and Constitution

**Prepare:** National Archives (DocsTeach/archives.gov) images of the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution, a magnifying glass, and paper for a family constitution.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the Declaration and Constitution are the same: the Declaration (1776) said we're free from Britain; the Constitution (1787) set up how the government works.
- Thinking George Washington wrote the Declaration: Thomas Jefferson was the main writer.
- Thinking July 4th celebrates the Constitution: it celebrates the Declaration of Independence.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look closely
*Student:* Examine an image of the Declaration of Independence (National Archives) with a magnifier; find John Hancock's signature.

*How to teach:* Examine the Declaration with a magnifier; find John Hancock's signature.

- **Q:** Whose signature is biggest?
  **A:** John Hancock's.
- **Q:** When was the Declaration adopted?
  **A:** July 4, 1776.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): July 4th
*Student:* Learn that the Declaration said America would be free and that we celebrate on Independence Day.

*How to teach:* Learn that the Declaration said America would be free; we celebrate on Independence Day.

- **Q:** What did the Declaration say?
  **A:** The colonies would be free and independent from Great Britain's king.
- **Q:** How do we celebrate July 4th?
  **A:** Fireworks, parades, picnics, flags.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): We the People
*Student:* Look at the first words of the Constitution and discuss that it is the nation's rulebook.

*How to teach:* Look at 'We the People' on the Constitution; discuss it as the nation's rulebook.

- **Q:** What are the first three words of the Constitution?
  **A:** We the People.
- **Q:** What is the Constitution?
  **A:** The plan and rulebook for our government.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Class constitution
*Student:* Write a 'family constitution' with 3 rules everyone signs.

*How to teach:* Write a 'family constitution' with 3 rules everyone signs.

- **Q:** Why does everyone sign?
  **A:** To show they agree to follow it.
- **Q:** Give a family constitution rule.
  **A:** Example: We will speak kindly to each other.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain what each document is and name one person connected to it (Jefferson, Washington).

*How to teach:* Your child explains each document and names one person connected to it.

- **Q:** Who wrote most of the Declaration?
  **A:** Thomas Jefferson.
- **Q:** Who led the meeting where the Constitution was written?
  **A:** George Washington.

**Assessment:** Document explanation and family constitution.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = Declaration — said America would be free from Britain (1776), Jefferson; Constitution — the rulebook/plan for government that starts 'We the People' (1787), Washington; family constitution with 3 signed rules; 3 = mostly correct; 2 = confuses documents; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Stamp printmaking; patterns in music

**Prepare:** Corks, bottle caps, sponges, potatoes (adult cuts), paint, paper; a song with verse and chorus ('Jingle Bells').

**Watch for:**
- Thinking verse and chorus are the same: the chorus repeats the same words; verses have new words.
- Letting the child carve with a knife: adults carve; child designs and prints.

### Day 1 (Monday): Found-object prints
*Student:* Stamp patterns with corks, bottle caps and sponges.

*How to teach:* Stamp patterns with corks, bottle caps and sponges.

- **Q:** What is a print?
  **A:** A picture made by pressing a painted object onto paper.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Potato stamps
*Student:* With adult help, carve simple potato stamps and print a repeating pattern wrapping paper.

*How to teach:* Adult carves simple potato stamps; child prints a repeating pattern.

- **Q:** Why is a stamp good for patterns?
  **A:** It makes the same shape again and again.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Verse and chorus
*Student:* Listen to 'Jingle Bells' or a seasonal song and raise hands when the chorus repeats.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Jingle Bells'; raise a hand when the chorus repeats.

- **Q:** Which part repeats with the same words?
  **A:** The chorus ('Jingle bells, jingle bells, jingle all the way…').

### Day 4 (Thursday): Pattern songs
*Student:* Sing a cumulative song like 'The Twelve Days' or 'There Was an Old Lady' and notice the pattern.

*How to teach:* Sing a cumulative song like 'There Was an Old Lady' and notice how each verse adds on.

- **Q:** What pattern does the song have?
  **A:** Each verse adds one more thing to the list and repeats the earlier ones.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Display prints and identify verse/chorus.

*How to teach:* Display prints; identify verse and chorus.

- **Q:** What is a verse?
  **A:** A part of the song with new words each time.

**Assessment:** Print pattern and verse/chorus identification.

**Answer key:** Print: a repeating pattern (at least 2 stamps alternated) on wrapping paper. Verse/chorus: raises hand each time the 'Jingle bells, jingle bells' chorus returns. Proficient = clear repeating print + identifies chorus each time.

## PE 1: Stretching and flexibility

**Prepare:** Mat; a kids yoga video (e.g., Cosmic Kids or GoNoodle); a ruler or yardstick and tape.

**Watch for:**
- Bouncing in stretches: hold each stretch still and gently for about 10-15 seconds.
- Stretching cold muscles: warm up first, then stretch.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm-up
*Student:* Do a 3-minute warm-up of jogging in place and arm circles.

*How to teach:* Lead a 3-minute warm-up: jog in place and arm circles forward and back.

- **Q:** Why warm up before stretching?
  **A:** Warm muscles stretch better and are less likely to get hurt.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Stretch
*Student:* Learn 5 stretches (butterfly, toe touch, cat-cow, arm cross, side reach).

*How to teach:* Teach butterfly, toe touch, cat-cow, arm cross and side reach. Hold each 10-15 seconds with no bouncing.

- **Q:** Should you bounce when you stretch?
  **A:** No, hold it still and gently.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Kids yoga
*Student:* Do a GoNoodle or kids yoga video.

*How to teach:* Follow a kids yoga video together.

- **Q:** How did your body feel after yoga?
  **A:** Answers vary: calm, stretched, relaxed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sit and reach
*Student:* Measure sit-and-reach with a ruler.

*How to teach:* After a warm-up, sit with legs straight and reach toward a ruler at the toes; measure and record.

- **Q:** What does sit-and-reach measure?
  **A:** Flexibility of the back of your legs and lower back.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Lead a warm-up and stretch for family.

*How to teach:* Have your child lead the family in a warm-up and stretch.

- **Q:** What comes first: warm-up or stretch?
  **A:** Warm-up.

**Assessment:** Stretch routine leadership.

**Answer key:** Leadership rubric: leads a 3-minute warm-up (jog in place, arm circles) then at least 3 of the 5 stretches (butterfly, toe touch, cat-cow, arm cross, side reach), holding each still. Proficient = correct order (warm up before stretching), clear instructions, no bouncing.

## Health 1: Food safety in the kitchen

**Prepare:** Kitchen tools, a recipe card for a simple wrap or salad, a refrigerator thermometer (optional), and produce. Adult handles knives and stove.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving milk or meat out on the counter: cold foods go back in the fridge quickly (within 2 hours).
- Touching knives or hot pans: these are adult jobs.
- Skipping handwashing before cooking: always wash first.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clean hands, clean food
*Student:* Wash hands and produce before cooking.

*How to teach:* Wash hands and produce before cooking.

- **Q:** First step before cooking?
  **A:** Wash hands with soap.
- **Q:** How do you wash lettuce?
  **A:** Rinse under running water.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cold and hot
*Student:* Learn that some foods must stay cold in the fridge.

*How to teach:* Learn some foods must stay cold.

- **Q:** Name a food that stays cold.
  **A:** Milk, yogurt, meat, cheese.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** Germs grow fast in warm food.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cook together
*Student:* Make a simple MyPlate meal (wrap or salad) with an adult.

*How to teach:* Make a simple MyPlate wrap or salad with an adult.

- **Q:** What groups are in a turkey-cheese-lettuce wrap?
  **A:** Grain, protein, dairy, vegetable.
- **Q:** What can you add for fruit?
  **A:** Apple slices on the side.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Kitchen safety
*Student:* Learn knife, stove and hot-pan safety rules.

*How to teach:* Learn knife, stove, and hot-pan rules.

- **Q:** Who uses sharp knives?
  **A:** Adults (or kids only with close adult help and a kid-safe knife).
- **Q:** What do you do near a hot stove?
  **A:** Stay back; let adults handle it; turn pot handles inward.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 3 kitchen safety rules.

*How to teach:* Your child explains 3 kitchen safety rules.

- **Q:** Name 3 rules.
  **A:** Wash hands, adults handle knives/stove, keep cold foods cold.
- **Q:** What if you spill?
  **A:** Tell an adult and clean it up so no one slips.

**Assessment:** Kitchen safety explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = explains 3 correct rules (wash hands/produce; adults use knives, stove, hot pans; refrigerate cold foods; wipe spills) with reasons; 3 = 2 rules; 2 = 1; 1 = none.
