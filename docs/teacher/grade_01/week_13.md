# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 13

## ELA 1: Long a with silent e; what is nonfiction?

**Prepare:** Letter tiles plus an 'e' tile, a storybook and a nonfiction animal book, a T-chart, a National Geographic Kids 'Frogs' book (or similar), and 'short'/'long' cards. Also 6 books for sorting.

**Watch for:**
- Reading cape as 'cap' (ignoring the e): the silent e makes the vowel say its name.
- Thinking any book with animals is nonfiction: talking animals with a story = fiction; real facts and photos = nonfiction.
- Thinking the final e is pronounced: silent e doesn't make its own sound.

### Day 1 (Monday): Magic e with a
*Student:* Add e to cap, tap, can, mad to make cape, tape, cane, made; read each pair aloud.

*How to teach:* Build cap; add e to make cape. Do tap → tape, can → cane, mad → made. Your child reads each pair.

- **Q:** What happens when you add e to tap?
  **A:** It becomes tape; the a says its name /ā/.
- **Q:** Read: cane, made.
  **A:** cane, made.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Long or short?
*Student:* Listen to 12 spoken words and hold up a short or long card for the vowel sound.

*How to teach:* Say 12 words (cap, cape, man, mane, hat, hate, plan, plane, rat, rate, tap, tape). Your child holds up short or long.

- **Q:** Is the a in plane short or long?
  **A:** Long.
- **Q:** Is the a in hat short or long?
  **A:** Short.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Fiction or nonfiction
*Student:* Compare a storybook with a nonfiction animal book; list 3 differences on a T-chart.

*How to teach:* Compare a storybook and a nonfiction animal book. Your child names 3 differences on a T-chart.

- **Q:** Name a difference.
  **A:** Fiction tells a made-up story; nonfiction gives true facts. Nonfiction often has photos, headings, a table of contents.
- **Q:** Which book teaches you real facts?
  **A:** The nonfiction book.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ask about facts
*Student:* Read a National Geographic Kids 'Frogs' (or similar) book and ask and answer 3 questions about frog facts.

*How to teach:* Read a frog book. Your child asks and answers 3 questions about frog facts.

- **Q:** What do frogs hatch from?
  **A:** Eggs laid in water; they hatch into tadpoles.
- **Q:** How do tadpoles change?
  **A:** They grow legs, lose their tails, and become frogs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read 10 a_e words and sort 6 books into fiction and nonfiction with a reason.

*How to teach:* Your child reads 10 a_e words, then sorts 6 books into fiction and nonfiction, giving a reason for each.

- **Q:** Read: game, lake, cave.
  **A:** game, lake, cave.
- **Q:** A book about a pig who goes to school—fiction or nonfiction?
  **A:** Fiction — pigs don't really go to school.

**Assessment:** a_e word reading and fiction/nonfiction sort with reasons.

**Answer key:** a_e words: cape, tape, cane, made, game, lake, cave, gate, name, plane — proficient 9–10. Sort: each book correctly labeled with a reason (fiction = made-up story / characters; nonfiction = true facts, photos, labels). Proficient = 5–6 correct with reasons.

## Math 1: Doubles to 20 and three-addend sums

**Prepare:** Dominoes, 2–3 dice, counters, 2 egg cartons, two ten-frames, and a doubles quiz (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 7 + 8 = 7 + 7 = 14 (forgetting the 'plus one'): say 'double the smaller one, then add 1 more.'
- Believing changing the order of three addends changes the sum: rearrange cubes and recount to prove the total stays the same.
- Mixing up 6+6=12 and 7+7=14: link to real things — egg carton rows (6+6=12), two weeks (7+7=14).

### Day 1 (Monday): Big doubles
*Student:* Learn 6+6 through 10+10 with egg cartons and ten-frames.

*How to teach:* Fill 6 cups on each side of an egg carton: 6 + 6 = 12. Build 7+7 through 10+10 on double ten-frames and list them: 12, 14, 16, 18, 20. Chant the list.

- **Q:** 7 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 14.
- **Q:** 9 + 9 = ?
  **A:** 18.
- **Q:** 8 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 16.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Near doubles
*Student:* Solve 7 + 8 as 7 + 7 + 1; play a near-doubles card game.

*How to teach:* Solve 7 + 8 aloud: 'I know 7 + 7 = 14. 8 is one more, so 15.' Play a card game: flip two cards that are one apart and solve as near doubles.

- **Q:** 7 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 15.
- **Q:** 6 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 13 (6 + 6 + 1).
- **Q:** 9 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 17 (8 + 8 + 1).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Three addends
*Student:* Solve 'Sam saw 4 birds, 6 squirrels, and 3 rabbits'; choose which two to add first.

*How to teach:* Read 'Sam saw 4 birds, 6 squirrels, and 3 rabbits.' Ask which two to add first; 4 + 6 = 10, then 10 + 3 = 13. Write 4 + 6 + 3 = 13.

- **Q:** How many animals did Sam see?
  **A:** 13.
- **Q:** 2 + 7 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 17; 2 + 8 = 10, plus 7.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Change the order
*Student:* Show 5 + 2 + 5 = 5 + 5 + 2 and explain why it helps.

*How to teach:* Build 5 + 2 + 5 with cube trains, then rearrange to 5 + 5 + 2. Both are 12. Explain that adding the 5s first makes a 10, which is easier.

- **Q:** Why is 5 + 2 + 5 = 5 + 5 + 2 true?
  **A:** Same numbers in a different order; both equal 12.
- **Q:** 3 + 9 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 19; 3 + 7 = 10, plus 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Doubles to 20 quiz and three-addend word problems.

*How to teach:* Give the doubles quiz and 3 three-addend word problems; read each aloud.

- **Q:** 6 + 6 = ?
  **A:** 12.
- **Q:** Lia has 3 red, 3 blue, and 4 green beads. How many?
  **A:** 10.

**Assessment:** Doubles quiz and three-addend problems.

**Answer key:** Doubles quiz (suggested): 6+6=12, 7+7=14, 8+8=16, 9+9=18, 10+10=20, 6+7=13, 7+8=15, 8+9=17. Word problems: 1) 5 apples, 4 pears, 5 plums → 14 2) 3 + 8 + 7 stickers → 18 3) 2 + 6 + 4 kids → 12. Proficient: 10–11 of 11 correct and shows grouping (making 10 or a double) in at least one word problem.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Light signals and communicating

**Prepare:** A flashlight, colored cellophane (red, green), a mirror, a book about lighthouses (e.g., 'Hello Lighthouse' by Sophie Blackall), and a code sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking lighthouses are just decoration: they warn ships about rocks and show where the coast is.
- Sending codes too fast: slow, clear blinks are easier to decode.
- Thinking colors don't matter: colors can carry meanings (red = stop, green = go).

### Day 1 (Monday): Lighthouses
*Student:* Read about lighthouses and how ships use them.

*How to teach:* Read about lighthouses.

- **Q:** What does a lighthouse do?
  **A:** Shines a light to warn ships of rocks and the shore and to help them find their way at night.
- **Q:** Why do lighthouses have different flash patterns?
  **A:** So sailors can tell which lighthouse they're seeing.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Flashlight code
*Student:* Invent a flashlight blink code and send messages.

*How to teach:* Invent a flashlight code and send messages.

- **Q:** Write a code.
  **A:** Example: 1 blink = yes; 2 blinks = no; 3 = come here.
- **Q:** Why must both people know the code?
  **A:** So the message is understood.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Color filters
*Student:* Use colored cellophane for different signals.

*How to teach:* Use cellophane for colored signals.

- **Q:** What could red mean?
  **A:** Stop/danger.
- **Q:** Green?
  **A:** Go/safe.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Test outdoors
*Student:* Test your flashlight code at dusk from across the yard; record which messages a partner decoded correctly.

*How to teach:* Test the code at dusk across the yard; record decoded messages.

- **Q:** Why test at dusk?
  **A:** Light signals are easier to see in dim light.
- **Q:** How do you know your code works?
  **A:** Your partner decodes messages correctly.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 check
*Student:* Complete a light assessment (sort materials, draw a shadow, explain seeing in the dark) and explain how your signal device works.

*How to teach:* Give the light assessment and your child explains their signal device.

- **Q:** Is wax paper transparent, translucent, or opaque?
  **A:** Translucent.
- **Q:** Why can't you see in a dark room?
  **A:** No light bounces off objects into your eyes.

**Assessment:** Unit 3 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 3 test (suggested): 1) Sort: glass = transparent, wax paper = translucent, cardboard = opaque 2) Draw a lamp, a ball, and its shadow on the far side 3) Explain: you can't see in the dark because there is no light to reflect off things into your eyes 4) Moon a light source? no 5) Mirror reflects light. Signal device explanation: proficient = describes the code and how light carries the message.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Veterans Day and Thanksgiving

**Prepare:** Library of Congress photos of veterans, card-making supplies, a children's nonfiction book on the 1621 harvest feast, and 'Thank You, Sarah: The Woman Who Saved Thanksgiving' by Laurie Halse Anderson (library).

**Watch for:**
- Confusing Veterans Day and Memorial Day: Veterans Day (Nov. 11) honors everyone who served; Memorial Day honors those who died serving.
- Thinking the first Thanksgiving was only the Pilgrims: the Wampanoag people, led by Massasoit, were there too and had helped the Pilgrims.
- Thinking Thanksgiving was always a national holiday: Sarah Josepha Hale worked for years until President Lincoln made it national in 1863.

### Day 1 (Monday): Veterans Day
*Student:* Learn what a veteran is and why we honor them on November 11; look at Library of Congress photos of veterans.

*How to teach:* Learn what a veteran is and why we honor them on November 11. View LOC photos.

- **Q:** What is a veteran?
  **A:** Someone who served in the armed forces.
- **Q:** When is Veterans Day?
  **A:** November 11.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Thank-you card
*Student:* Write a thank-you card to a veteran (family member or local VA).

*How to teach:* Your child writes a thank-you card to a veteran.

- **Q:** What could you thank a veteran for?
  **A:** Serving and protecting our country.
- **Q:** Who could receive it?
  **A:** A family member, neighbor, or local VA.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Thanksgiving history
*Student:* Learn about the 1621 harvest feast of the Wampanoag and the Pilgrims from a children's nonfiction book.

*How to teach:* Learn about the 1621 harvest feast of the Wampanoag and the Pilgrims.

- **Q:** Who shared the 1621 harvest feast?
  **A:** The Pilgrims (English colonists at Plymouth) and the Wampanoag people.
- **Q:** How had the Wampanoag helped the Pilgrims?
  **A:** Taught them to grow corn and find food (Tisquantum/Squanto helped them plant corn and fish).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sarah Hale
*Student:* Read 'Thank You, Sarah' and learn how Sarah Josepha Hale helped make Thanksgiving a national holiday.

*How to teach:* Read 'Thank You, Sarah'; learn how Sarah Hale helped make Thanksgiving national.

- **Q:** How did Sarah Hale try to make Thanksgiving a national holiday?
  **A:** She wrote many letters to presidents and leaders for many years.
- **Q:** Which president agreed?
  **A:** Abraham Lincoln, in 1863.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a two-panel poster explaining Veterans Day and Thanksgiving.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a two-panel poster explaining Veterans Day and Thanksgiving.

- **Q:** What goes on the Veterans Day panel?
  **A:** Honoring people who served in the military, on November 11.
- **Q:** Thanksgiving panel?
  **A:** Giving thanks; harvest feast of 1621; Sarah Hale; fourth Thursday of November.

**Assessment:** Holiday poster with correct explanations.

**Answer key:** Poster rubric: 4 = Veterans Day honors people who served in the armed forces on Nov. 11; Thanksgiving is a day of giving thanks, recalling the 1621 harvest feast of the Wampanoag and Pilgrims and Sarah Hale's work (national holiday since Lincoln, 1863); 3 = both correct, few details; 2 = one holiday correct; 1 = incorrect.

## Art & Music 1: Texture review; melody game

**Prepare:** Unit artwork, paper strips in two colors, glockenspiel or piano app; recording of 'Flight of the Bumblebee' (Rimsky-Korsakov).

**Watch for:**
- Weaving every strip the same way: alternate over-under for each new strip.
- Playing Hot Cross Buns E-D-C backwards: it goes down from E.

### Day 1 (Monday): Look at art
*Student:* Look at a photograph of a woven basket and a quilt; describe textures.

*How to teach:* Look at a woven basket and a quilt photo; describe textures.

- **Q:** How would a basket feel?
  **A:** Bumpy, ridged, rough.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Texture weaving
*Student:* Weave paper strips into a simple mat.

*How to teach:* Cut slits in folded paper; weave strips over-under, alternating each row.

- **Q:** If row 1 starts 'over,' how does row 2 start?
  **A:** Under.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Play melody
*Student:* Play 'Hot Cross Buns' on a glockenspiel or piano app (E-D-C).

*How to teach:* Play 'Hot Cross Buns': E-D-C, E-D-C, C-C-C-C D-D-D-D, E-D-C.

- **Q:** What are the first three notes?
  **A:** E, D, C.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Listening
*Student:* Listen to 'Flight of the Bumblebee' and tell if the melody is high or low.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'Flight of the Bumblebee.'

- **Q:** Is it fast or slow? High or low?
  **A:** Very fast; mostly high, buzzing notes like a bee.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 3 share
*Student:* Show your weaving and play 'Hot Cross Buns'.

*How to teach:* Show weaving; play 'Hot Cross Buns.'

- **Q:** Does Hot Cross Buns go up or down at first?
  **A:** Down (E-D-C).

**Assessment:** Unit 3 performance and weaving.

**Answer key:** Weaving: alternating over-under pattern with at least 5 strips. 'Hot Cross Buns': E-D-C, E-D-C, C-C-C-C D-D-D-D, E-D-C. Proficient = correct notes with steady beat + tight over-under weaving.

## PE 1: My heart

**Prepare:** Stopwatch or phone timer, a toilet paper tube to use as a stethoscope, paper and crayons.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking the heart is on the far left side: it's in the middle of the chest, tilted a little to the left.
- Thinking the heart only works during exercise: it beats all the time, just faster when we move.

### Day 1 (Monday): Find your heart
*Student:* Place a hand on your chest to find your heart; listen with a paper-tube stethoscope.

*How to teach:* Have your child place a hand in the middle of the chest, a little to the left, and feel the heartbeat. Listen with the paper tube on each other's chests.

- **Q:** Where is your heart?
  **A:** In the middle of the chest, tilted a little to the left.
- **Q:** What does your heart do?
  **A:** Pumps blood all around your body.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Before and after
*Student:* Feel heartbeat at rest, then after 1 minute of jumping jacks; compare.

*How to teach:* Feel the heartbeat sitting still, then after 1 minute of jumping jacks. Compare.

- **Q:** Did your heart beat faster or slower after jumping jacks?
  **A:** Faster.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Heart-pumping game
*Student:* Play freeze tag for 10 minutes.

*How to teach:* Play freeze tag for 10 minutes; check heartbeats at the end.

- **Q:** What happened to your breathing during tag?
  **A:** It got faster/heavier.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Heart chart
*Student:* Draw a heart and list activities that make it pump.

*How to teach:* Draw a heart and list or draw activities that make it pump (running, swimming, biking, dancing).

- **Q:** Name an activity that makes your heart pump fast.
  **A:** Examples: running, jumping rope, dancing, swimming, biking.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why the heart beats faster when we move.

*How to teach:* Ask your child to explain why the heart beats faster when we move.

- **Q:** Why does your heart beat faster when you move?
  **A:** Your muscles need more blood and oxygen, so the heart pumps faster.

**Assessment:** Heart explanation.

**Answer key:** Expected explanation: 'When I move, my muscles need more oxygen (air) and energy, so my heart beats faster to pump more blood to them.' Proficient = mentions muscles needing more blood/oxygen; developing = 'because I'm moving' only.

## Health 1: Water, breakfast and healthy snacks

**Prepare:** Sugar cubes (each about 4 grams), a can of soda, a carton of milk, a water bottle, snack items (pairs: apple vs. candy, yogurt vs. cookies), celery, peanut/sun butter, raisins. Check allergies.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking juice and soda are healthy because they're drinks: they often have lots of sugar; water is best.
- Skipping breakfast: breakfast gives energy to learn.
- Thinking 'healthy' means tasteless: many healthy snacks are delicious.

### Day 1 (Monday): Sugar in drinks
*Student:* Count sugar cubes in soda vs. milk vs. water.

*How to teach:* Count sugar cubes in soda vs. milk vs. water.

- **Q:** About how many sugar cubes in a 12-ounce can of soda?
  **A:** About 10 (about 39 grams of sugar).
- **Q:** In water?
  **A:** Zero.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Breakfast power
*Student:* Discuss why breakfast fuels the brain; plan a healthy breakfast.

*How to teach:* Discuss why breakfast fuels the brain; plan a healthy breakfast.

- **Q:** Why eat breakfast?
  **A:** It gives energy to think, learn, and play.
- **Q:** Plan a healthy breakfast.
  **A:** Example: oatmeal, banana, milk.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Snack decisions
*Student:* Use 'stop, think, choose' to pick between snack pairs.

*How to teach:* Use 'stop, think, choose' between snack pairs.

- **Q:** Apple or candy?
  **A:** Apple — it has vitamins and fiber.
- **Q:** Water or soda?
  **A:** Water.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make a snack
*Student:* Make an ants-on-a-log or fruit-and-yogurt snack.

*How to teach:* Make ants-on-a-log or fruit-and-yogurt.

- **Q:** What food groups are in ants-on-a-log?
  **A:** Vegetable (celery), protein (nut/seed butter), fruit (raisins).
- **Q:** Is fruit-and-yogurt healthy?
  **A:** Yes — fruit and dairy.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Choose 3 healthy snacks and explain why.

*How to teach:* Your child chooses 3 healthy snacks and explains why.

- **Q:** Name 3 healthy snacks.
  **A:** Apple slices, carrot sticks, cheese, yogurt.
- **Q:** Why healthy?
  **A:** They come from food groups and don't have lots of added sugar.

**Assessment:** Snack decision explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names 3 healthy snacks from food groups and explains (vitamins, energy, low added sugar); 3 = 2 snacks; 2 = 1; 1 = sugary choices with no reasons.
