# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 28

## ELA 1: Shared research: an informative 'How a Plant Grows' book

**Prepare:** The bean journal, plant books, and paper folded into a 4-page book plus cover.

**Watch for:**
- Writing pages out of order: number pages 1–4 first.
- Copying from the book: write in your own words.
- Labels without matching drawings: point a label line to each part.

### Day 1 (Monday): Gather facts
*Student:* Reread plant books and the bean journal; list 5 facts about what plants need.

*How to teach:* Reread plant books and the bean journal. Your child lists 5 facts about what plants need.

- **Q:** Name what plants need.
  **A:** Water, sunlight, air, nutrients from soil, and space.
- **Q:** Why does a plant need sunlight?
  **A:** To make its own food.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Write pages
*Student:* Write page 1 (seed) and page 2 (sprout) of a How a Plant Grows book with labels.

*How to teach:* Your child writes page 1 (seed) and 2 (sprout) with labels.

- **Q:** What part grows first from the seed?
  **A:** The root grows down.
- **Q:** Label a sprout.
  **A:** Root, stem, first leaves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Finish pages
*Student:* Write pages 3 (plant) and 4 (flower/seed) and a closing sentence.

*How to teach:* Your child writes pages 3 (plant) and 4 (flower/seed) and a closing.

- **Q:** What do flowers make?
  **A:** Seeds.
- **Q:** Write a closing sentence.
  **A:** Example: Then the seeds can grow new plants!

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Read the book to a family member and answer their questions.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the book to a family member and answers questions.

- **Q:** What does a root do?
  **A:** Holds the plant in the ground and takes in water.
- **Q:** What does the stem do?
  **A:** Holds the plant up and carries water to the leaves.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Syllables/endings assessment and informative book rubric.

*How to teach:* Give the syllables/endings assessment and score the book.

- **Q:** Syllables in pumpkin?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** Root of jumping?
  **A:** jump.

**Assessment:** Informative book rubric and syllable/ending assessment.

**Answer key:** Book rubric: 4 = 4 pages in order (seed, sprout, plant, flower/seed) with accurate facts, labels, and a closing; 3 = all pages with minor errors; 2 = missing pages or inaccurate facts; 1 = incomplete. Syllables/endings (suggested): rabbit 2, sunset 2, banana 3, cat 1; boxes, played (/d/), jumped (/t/), planted (/ed/), root of replanting = plant, unhappy = not happy. Proficient 9–10.

## Math 1: Measuring with nonstandard units

**Prepare:** A box of same-size paper clips, connecting cubes, a book, spoon, shoe, pictures of measurements with gaps/overlaps (draw 3), and a recording sheet.

**Watch for:**
- Leaving gaps or overlapping units: line units end to end, touching, starting exactly at the edge.
- Mixing unit sizes (big and small paper clips): every unit must be the same size.
- Thinking a bigger number always means a longer object even with different units: more cubes than clips doesn't mean the object grew — smaller units give bigger counts.

### Day 1 (Monday): Paper clips
*Student:* Measure a book, a spoon and a shoe with paper clips.

*How to teach:* Show how to measure a book: start the first clip at the edge, line clips end to end with no gaps, and count. Record each measurement as 'The book is __ paper clips long.'

- **Q:** Where must the first paper clip start?
  **A:** Exactly at the end of the object.
- **Q:** If 7 clips fit along the spoon, how long is it?
  **A:** 7 paper clips long.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Gaps and overlaps
*Student:* Find mistakes in pictures where units overlap or leave gaps.

*How to teach:* Show drawings with gaps between clips, overlapping clips, and one correct. Your child circles the mistakes and explains.

- **Q:** Why is a gap a mistake?
  **A:** The gap is part of the length but not counted, so the number is too small.
- **Q:** What happens if clips overlap?
  **A:** You count more clips than fit, so the number is too big.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Cubes
*Student:* Measure the same objects with cubes and compare numbers.

*How to teach:* Measure the same book, spoon, and shoe with cubes. Compare: 'The book was 6 clips but 9 cubes.' Ask why.

- **Q:** Why did the book measure more cubes than clips?
  **A:** Cubes are shorter than paper clips, so it takes more of them.
- **Q:** Did the book's length change?
  **A:** No — only the unit changed.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Footsteps
*Student:* Measure the hallway with heel-to-toe steps; compare with a family member's.

*How to teach:* Measure the hallway with heel-to-toe steps. Then a family member measures it. Compare the numbers and discuss why they're different.

- **Q:** Why did Dad need fewer steps?
  **A:** His feet are longer, so each step covers more distance.
- **Q:** How could we get the same number?
  **A:** Use the same unit, like the same paper clips or a ruler.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Measure 5 household objects with paper clips, record each length, and circle any measurement where units had gaps or overlaps.

*How to teach:* Your child measures 5 objects with clips, records each, and circles any measurement where gaps/overlaps happened.

- **Q:** A pencil is 5 clips and a crayon 3 clips. How much longer is the pencil?
  **A:** 2 clips longer.
- **Q:** What makes a measurement fair?
  **A:** Same-size units, end to end, no gaps or overlaps.

**Assessment:** Measurement recording sheet.

**Answer key:** Recording sheet: answers vary by object; check that each entry has a number and the unit ('6 paper clips'). Proficient (4) = all 5 measured with units touching end to end, starting at the edge, counts within 1 of your own check, and any gap/overlap correctly circled; 3 = one error; 2 = several gaps or no units named; 1 = incomplete.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Nature as inventor wrap-up

**Prepare:** A free Mystery Science lesson (animal or plant parts), drawing paper or a camera, and the science notebook for a field guide.

**Watch for:**
- Labeling parts without their job: each label needs a job.
- Confusing 'Guess the animal' clues with color only: give part clues (webbed feet, flat bill).
- Thinking inventions can only copy animals: plants count too.

### Day 1 (Monday): Mystery Science
*Student:* Watch a free Mystery Science animal-parts or plant-parts lesson and answer the mystery question.

*How to teach:* Watch a Mystery Science lesson; answer the mystery question.

- **Q:** Answer the mystery question.
  **A:** Answers vary; must use the idea that parts help survival.
- **Q:** What helps a plant survive?
  **A:** Roots, stems, leaves, thorns, etc.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Backyard field guide
*Student:* Find 4 plants or animals outside (birds, insects, weeds) and draw each with one labeled survival part.

*How to teach:* Find 4 plants/animals outside; draw each with one labeled survival part.

- **Q:** Label a bird.
  **A:** Beak — eats seeds/bugs.
- **Q:** Label a weed.
  **A:** Roots — take in water.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Guess the animal
*Student:* Play 'Guess the animal from its parts' with a family member using clues like 'webbed feet and a flat bill'.

*How to teach:* Play 'Guess the animal from its parts.'

- **Q:** Webbed feet and a flat bill?
  **A:** A duck.
- **Q:** Long neck to reach treetops?
  **A:** A giraffe.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share the guide
*Student:* Present your field guide and explain one part that could inspire a new invention.

*How to teach:* Present the field guide; explain one part that could inspire an invention.

- **Q:** Example.
  **A:** A woodpecker's beak and skull could inspire a stronger helmet.
- **Q:** Why copy nature?
  **A:** Nature already solved similar problems.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Assessment: match parts to functions and explain one biomimicry design.

*How to teach:* Give the unit assessment.

- **Q:** Match: fins.
  **A:** Swim.
- **Q:** Explain one biomimicry design.
  **A:** Velcro copies burr hooks.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 assessment and field guide.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test (suggested): match parts to jobs — claws: grab/dig; beak: eat; shell: protect; fins: swim; roots: water/anchor; leaves: make food; thorns: protect. Biomimicry: Velcro ← burrs; helmet ← turtle shell; fins ← duck feet. Proficient: 9/10. Field guide: 4 entries with labeled parts.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Celebrations around the world

**Prepare:** Library books on Lunar New Year, Día de los Muertos, Diwali, Hanukkah, and Kwanzaa; red paper for a lantern; tissue paper for a marigold; paper for drawing a diya and menorah.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking Día de los Muertos is the same as Halloween: it is a time (Nov. 1–2) to remember and honor loved ones who died.
- Thinking all celebrations are on the same date every year: some follow lunar calendars and move.
- Thinking a celebration belongs only to one country: people celebrate many holidays around the world, including in California.

### Day 1 (Monday): Lunar New Year
*Student:* Learn customs (red envelopes, dragon dance) and make a paper lantern.

*How to teach:* Learn Lunar New Year customs; make a paper lantern.

- **Q:** What is in a red envelope?
  **A:** Money, given for good luck.
- **Q:** Why is red used?
  **A:** It stands for good luck and happiness.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Día de los Muertos
*Student:* Learn customs (ofrendas, marigolds) and make a paper marigold.

*How to teach:* Learn Día de los Muertos customs; make a paper marigold.

- **Q:** What is an ofrenda?
  **A:** An altar with photos, foods, and marigolds to remember loved ones.
- **Q:** When is Día de los Muertos?
  **A:** November 1 and 2.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Diwali and Hanukkah
*Student:* Compare these festivals of light; draw a diya and a menorah.

*How to teach:* Compare Diwali and Hanukkah; draw a diya and menorah.

- **Q:** How are they alike?
  **A:** Both are festivals of lights in autumn/winter.
- **Q:** How many nights is Hanukkah?
  **A:** Eight.
- **Q:** What is a diya?
  **A:** A small clay oil lamp lit for Diwali.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Make a chart of what the celebrations have in common (family, food, light, remembering).

*How to teach:* Chart what the celebrations have in common.

- **Q:** Name something in common.
  **A:** Family, special food, light, remembering.
- **Q:** Why do people celebrate?
  **A:** To remember, give thanks, and be together.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Present one celebration to family: when it happens, who celebrates, and two customs.

*How to teach:* Your child presents one celebration: when, who, two customs.

- **Q:** Example: Lunar New Year.
  **A:** Late January or February; celebrated by Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, and other families; red envelopes and dragon/lion dances.
- **Q:** Example: Hanukkah.
  **A:** Usually in December; Jewish families; lighting the menorah and playing dreidel.

**Assessment:** Celebration presentation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names the celebration, when it happens, who celebrates, and 2 accurate customs (e.g., Diwali — Oct./Nov.; Hindus, Sikhs, Jains; lighting diyas, rangoli designs, sweets); 3 = one detail missing; 2 = two missing; 1 = inaccurate.

## Art & Music 1: Van Gogh's Starry Night; fast and slow

**Prepare:** Image of Van Gogh's 'The Starry Night,' oil pastels, dark paper; recording of Grieg's 'In the Hall of the Mountain King.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking tempo means loudness: tempo is speed.
- Blending oil pastels too much: show visible swirl strokes like Van Gogh.

### Day 1 (Monday): Starry Night
*Student:* Look at Van Gogh's 'The Starry Night' and trace swirling lines.

*How to teach:* Look at 'The Starry Night'; trace swirling lines in the air.

- **Q:** What do you see?
  **A:** Swirling sky, bright stars, a crescent moon, a village, a tall dark cypress tree.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Night sky
*Student:* Draw a swirling night sky with oil pastels.

*How to teach:* Draw a swirling night sky with oil pastels.

- **Q:** What is a brushstroke?
  **A:** The mark a brush (or tool) leaves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mountain King
*Student:* Listen to Grieg's 'In the Hall of the Mountain King' and move as the tempo speeds up.

*How to teach:* Listen to 'In the Hall of the Mountain King'; move as it speeds up.

- **Q:** What happens to the tempo?
  **A:** It starts slow and quiet and gets faster and louder.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Tempo game
*Student:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' at slow and fast tempos.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Row, Row, Row Your Boat' slow, then fast.

- **Q:** What is tempo?
  **A:** How fast or slow music goes.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain how tempo changed the mood of the music.

*How to teach:* Explain how tempo changed the mood.

- **Q:** How did the fast part feel?
  **A:** Exciting, scary, rushed.

**Assessment:** Night sky drawing and tempo response.

**Answer key:** Night sky drawing shows swirling lines and bright stars. Tempo response: moves slowly at the start of 'Mountain King' and faster as it speeds up. Proficient = swirls + matching movement.

## PE 1: Dance creation and jump rope show

**Prepare:** Music, jump rope, space for a family show.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a show must be perfect: focus on trying your best and finishing.
- Losing count: count out loud or in your head during rehearsal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Dance sequence
*Student:* Build a 16-count dance from favorite moves.

*How to teach:* Build a 16-count dance by joining two 8-counts of favorite moves.

- **Q:** How many 8-counts make 16?
  **A:** Two.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rope tricks
*Student:* Practice jumping fast and slow.

*How to teach:* Practice jumping rope fast, then slow.

- **Q:** How do you jump faster?
  **A:** Turn the rope faster with wrists and use smaller jumps.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rehearse
*Student:* Rehearse the 16-count dance and a 20-second jump-rope routine together.

*How to teach:* Rehearse the dance and a 20-second rope routine together.

- **Q:** Why rehearse?
  **A:** Practice helps you remember and do it better.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Show
*Student:* Perform the dance and jump-rope show for family, ending with a bow.

*How to teach:* Perform for family; end with a bow.

- **Q:** What does a performer do at the end?
  **A:** Takes a bow.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Reflect on feelings and skill growth.

*How to teach:* Reflect on feelings and growth since starting rope and dance.

- **Q:** What skill got better?
  **A:** Answers vary, e.g., more rope jumps in a row.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 show.

**Answer key:** Show rubric: 16-count dance performed in order; 20-second jump-rope routine (fast and slow jumping); bow at the end. Proficient = completes both parts with few stops and good effort.

## Health 1: Safe and unsafe touch; trusted adults

**Prepare:** The trusted adults hand from Unit 1 and scenario cards. Parent preview: this week covers body safety and unsafe touch. Use calm, matter-of-fact language and correct names for body parts. If your child shares something concerning, stay calm, thank them, and contact your pediatrician or the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline (1-800-422-4453).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking strangers are the only danger: most important rule is 'No, Go, Tell' with anyone who makes you feel unsafe.
- Thinking secrets about touching should be kept: surprises (like a gift) are okay; secrets about touching must be told.
- Thinking a child is in trouble if they tell: they will never be in trouble for telling.

### Day 1 (Monday): Safe touch
*Student:* Discuss safe touches (hugs from family, high fives) and unsafe touches.

*How to teach:* Discuss safe touches (hugs from family, high fives) and unsafe touches.

- **Q:** Name a safe touch.
  **A:** A hug from family, a high five, a doctor's checkup with a parent there.
- **Q:** What is an unsafe touch?
  **A:** A touch that hurts or touches private parts (parts covered by a swimsuit), or makes you feel scared or uncomfortable.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): No, go, tell
*Student:* Practice the 'No, Go, Tell' rule.

*How to teach:* Practice 'No, Go, Tell.'

- **Q:** What are the three steps?
  **A:** Say No, Go away, Tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Who do you tell?
  **A:** A trusted adult from your hand.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Strangers
*Student:* Discuss what to do if a stranger offers a ride or treat.

*How to teach:* Discuss what to do if a stranger offers a ride or treat.

- **Q:** A stranger offers you candy to get in a car. What do you do?
  **A:** Say No, run away, tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Should you go with someone who says your mom sent them?
  **A:** No — only with people your family has told you about (or who knows your family password).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Body ownership
*Student:* Discuss that your body belongs to you and secrets about touching should be told.

*How to teach:* Discuss that your body belongs to you and secrets about touching should be told.

- **Q:** Who is the boss of your body?
  **A:** You are.
- **Q:** Surprise or secret: a birthday gift?
  **A:** A surprise — okay to keep for a short time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Role-play 'No, Go, Tell' in 3 scenarios and name 3 trusted adults you would tell.

*How to teach:* Role-play 'No, Go, Tell' in 3 scenarios; name 3 trusted adults.

- **Q:** Someone wants you to keep a touching secret. What do you do?
  **A:** Say No, Go, and Tell a trusted adult.
- **Q:** Name 3 trusted adults.
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., Mom, Dad, Grandma.

**Assessment:** No-Go-Tell role-play.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = uses No–Go–Tell correctly in all 3 scenarios (firm 'No,' leaves, names a trusted adult to tell) and names 3 trusted adults; 3 = 2 scenarios; 2 = 1; 1 = cannot demonstrate.
