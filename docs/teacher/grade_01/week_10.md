# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 10

## ELA 1: R-blends; Aesop's fables

**Prepare:** Aesop's Fables (Project Gutenberg, e.g., the 1919 Jacobs or 'The Aesop for Children'), r-blend picture cards, drawing paper, and an r-blend passage (e.g., 'The crab and the frog trip on a log. Brad grins and drums. Fran gets a brush.').

**Watch for:**
- Saying 'fog' for frog (dropping r): stretch the blend and have your child watch your mouth.
- Thinking a moral is a summary of what happened: a moral is a lesson for our own lives.
- Adding -ed and reading every ending as /ed/ (jump-ed): listen — jumped ends with /t/.

### Day 1 (Monday): r-blends
*Student:* Read crab, drum, frog, grin, trip, brush; sort picture cards by br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr.

*How to teach:* Read crab, drum, frog, grin, trip, brush. Sort picture cards by br, cr, dr, fr, gr, tr.

- **Q:** Which blend starts frog?
  **A:** fr.
- **Q:** Read: trip, brush.
  **A:** trip, brush.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): The Tortoise and the Hare
*Student:* Read the fable and discuss why slow and steady won; draw the race with a speech bubble for each animal.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Tortoise and the Hare.' Discuss why slow and steady won. Your child draws the race with speech bubbles.

- **Q:** Why did the Hare lose?
  **A:** He was so sure he would win that he stopped to nap, and the Tortoise passed him.
- **Q:** What is the moral?
  **A:** Slow and steady wins the race.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Lion and the Mouse
*Student:* Read the fable and state its moral (little friends can be great friends) in your own words.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Lion and the Mouse.' Your child states the moral in their own words.

- **Q:** How did the Mouse help the Lion?
  **A:** He gnawed through the ropes of the hunters' net.
- **Q:** What is the moral in your own words?
  **A:** Little friends can be great friends; even small people can help.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Past tense -ed
*Student:* Read jumped, helped, rested and turn 5 present verbs into past verbs to tell what the hare did.

*How to teach:* Read jumped, helped, rested. Turn 5 present-tense verbs into past tense to tell what the hare did: run → ran (irregular), nap → napped, laugh → laughed, rest → rested, stop → stopped.

- **Q:** Change 'The hare rests' to the past.
  **A:** The hare rested.
- **Q:** Change 'The hare jumps' to the past.
  **A:** The hare jumped.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read an r-blend passage; match 3 fables to their morals.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the r-blend passage aloud, then matches 3 fables to their morals.

- **Q:** Match: The Lion and the Mouse.
  **A:** Little friends may prove great friends / kindness is never wasted.
- **Q:** Match: The Tortoise and the Hare.
  **A:** Slow and steady wins the race.

**Assessment:** Fable–moral matching and r-blend passage accuracy.

**Answer key:** Matching key: Tortoise and the Hare → slow and steady wins the race; Lion and the Mouse → little friends can be great friends (kindness is repaid); The Boy Who Cried Wolf (or the Ant and the Grasshopper, if used) → liars are not believed even when they tell the truth (work now, prepare for later). Passage: proficient 95% words correct.

## Math 1: Unknowns in all positions

**Prepare:** Counters, story mats (draw a pond, fishbowl, and balloon bunch on paper), and 6 story cards with unknowns in different places (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Adding the two numbers in 'start unknown' stories (? + 3 = 8 answered as 11): act it out — hide the starting amount and add 3 to see the total must be 8.
- Thinking a story with 'more came' always means add the numbers given: ask 'What do we already know — the start, the change, or the total?'
- Writing equations that don't match the story: read the story slowly and write a box for the number you don't know.

### Day 1 (Monday): Unknown change
*Student:* Solve 'There were 5 ducks. Some more came. Now there are 9.' with counters and 5 + ? = 9.

*How to teach:* Read 'There were 5 ducks. Some more came. Now there are 9.' Put 5 counters on the pond mat and add counters one at a time until there are 9, counting the added ones (4). Write 5 + ? = 9, ? = 4.

- **Q:** 5 + ? = 9
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** There were 6 bees. Some more came. Now there are 10. How many came?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Unknown start
*Student:* Solve 'Some fish were in a bowl. 3 more were added. Now there are 8.' with ? + 3 = 8.

*How to teach:* Read 'Some fish were in a bowl. 3 more were added. Now there are 8.' Use a cup to 'hide' the unknown start. Work backward: take away the 3 added to find the start: 5.

- **Q:** ? + 3 = 8
  **A:** 5.
- **Q:** Some kids were at the park. 2 more came. Now there are 7. How many were there first?
  **A:** 5.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Take from unknowns
*Student:* Solve '10 balloons, some popped, 6 left' with drawings.

*How to teach:* Read '10 balloons, some popped, 6 left.' Your child draws 10 balloons and crosses out balloons until 6 are left, then counts the crossed-out ones (4). Write 10 − ? = 6.

- **Q:** 10 − ? = 6
  **A:** 4.
- **Q:** 9 cookies, some were eaten, 5 are left. How many were eaten?
  **A:** 4.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write a story
*Student:* Write a story problem for 7 − ? = 4 and solve it.

*How to teach:* Help your child invent a story for 7 − ? = 4 (e.g., '7 cars, some drove away, 4 are left'). They illustrate it and solve: 3.

- **Q:** 7 − ? = 4
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** Tell a story for ? − 2 = 5 and solve.
  **A:** Example: Some birds, 2 flew away, 5 left; there were 7.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Story problem set with unknowns in all positions (6 items).

*How to teach:* Read 6 problems aloud; your child uses counters or drawings and writes an equation with a box for each unknown.

- **Q:** 8 frogs; some hopped away; 3 left. How many hopped?
  **A:** 5.
- **Q:** Some cards; I got 4 more; now 9. How many first?
  **A:** 5.

**Assessment:** Unknown-position story problems.

**Answer key:** Six problems (suggested) and answers: 1) 4 + ? = 7 → 3 2) ? + 5 = 9 → 4 3) 8 − ? = 2 → 6 4) ? − 3 = 6 → 9 5) 6 + 4 = ? → 10 6) 9 − 5 = ? → 4. Proficient: 5–6 correct with a matching equation; developing: 3–4; beginning: 0–2 (reteach start-unknown with cups).

## Science 1 (NGSS): Transparent, translucent, opaque

**Prepare:** A flashlight, plastic wrap, wax paper, cardboard, fabric, foil, tissue paper, and a chart with Predict / Result columns.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking translucent means 'see-through': translucent lets some light through but you can't see clearly through it.
- Thinking all fabric is opaque: thin fabric may be translucent — test it.
- Thinking colored glass/plastic is opaque: if you can see clearly through it, it's transparent.

### Day 1 (Monday): Predict
*Student:* Predict which materials will let light through.

*How to teach:* Your child predicts which materials will let light through.

- **Q:** Predict: will light pass through cardboard?
  **A:** No — it's opaque.
- **Q:** Plastic wrap?
  **A:** Yes — it's transparent.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test
*Student:* Shine a flashlight through each material onto a wall and record results.

*How to teach:* Shine the flashlight through each material onto a wall and record.

- **Q:** What did wax paper do?
  **A:** Let some light through — a dim, fuzzy glow (translucent).
- **Q:** What did foil do?
  **A:** Blocked all light (opaque).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sort
*Student:* Sort materials into transparent, translucent, opaque.

*How to teach:* Sort materials into transparent, translucent, opaque.

- **Q:** Define transparent.
  **A:** Lets almost all light through; you can see clearly through it.
- **Q:** Define opaque.
  **A:** Blocks all light.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Home hunt
*Student:* Find 3 transparent, 3 translucent and 3 opaque objects at home and test each with the flashlight.

*How to teach:* Find 3 of each kind at home and test each.

- **Q:** Name a transparent object.
  **A:** Window glass, clear water bottle, clear plastic bag.
- **Q:** Name a translucent object.
  **A:** Wax paper, frosted glass, thin curtain, tissue paper.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain why window glass is transparent and curtains are opaque.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why window glass is transparent and curtains are opaque.

- **Q:** Why are windows transparent?
  **A:** So light comes in and we can see outside.
- **Q:** Why might bedroom curtains be opaque?
  **A:** To block light so we can sleep.

**Assessment:** Material test chart.

**Answer key:** Chart key: plastic wrap — transparent; wax paper — translucent; tissue paper — translucent; cardboard — opaque; foil — opaque; fabric — opaque or translucent depending on thickness (accept with test evidence). Proficient = all correct with test results recorded.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Continents and oceans

**Prepare:** A globe, a world map, paper continent cut-outs, a blue world map outline, animal pictures, and a blank world map.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking there is one big ocean: there are named oceans (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, and often the Southern Ocean).
- Confusing countries and continents: a continent is a huge land area with many countries.
- Mixing up Antarctica and the Arctic: Antarctica is the frozen continent at the bottom (south); the Arctic Ocean is at the top (north).

### Day 1 (Monday): Continents song
*Student:* Learn a continents song and point to each continent on the globe.

*How to teach:* Learn a continents song and point to each continent.

- **Q:** Name the 7 continents.
  **A:** North America, South America, Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia, Antarctica.
- **Q:** Which is the largest?
  **A:** Asia.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Oceans
*Student:* Find the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian and Arctic Oceans; trace them with a finger.

*How to teach:* Find the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic Oceans; trace them.

- **Q:** Which ocean is the largest?
  **A:** Pacific.
- **Q:** Which ocean is between North America and Europe?
  **A:** Atlantic.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Puzzle map
*Student:* Cut out continent shapes and glue them on a blue world map in the right places.

*How to teach:* Cut out continents and glue them in the right places.

- **Q:** Where does Australia go?
  **A:** Southeast of Asia, between the Indian and Pacific Oceans.
- **Q:** What continent is south of North America?
  **A:** South America.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Animal travel
*Student:* Place animal pictures on their continents (kangaroo → Australia, penguin → Antarctica).

*How to teach:* Place animals on their continents.

- **Q:** Kangaroo?
  **A:** Australia.
- **Q:** Penguin?
  **A:** Antarctica (and other southern lands).
- **Q:** Lion?
  **A:** Africa.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Label 7 continents and 4 oceans on a blank world map.

*How to teach:* Your child labels 7 continents and 4 oceans.

- **Q:** Which ocean is at the very top of the globe?
  **A:** Arctic.
- **Q:** Which continent do we live on?
  **A:** North America.

**Assessment:** Continents and oceans labeling quiz.

**Answer key:** Labeling key: North America, South America, Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia, Antarctica; Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic Oceans (Southern Ocean accepted if added). Proficient 10–11 of 11.

## Art & Music 1: Texture rubbings; high and low sounds

**Prepare:** Crayons with paper removed, thin paper, leaves, coins, sandpaper, mesh; xylophone, piano, or free piano app.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing 'high' with 'loud': high/low is pitch; loud/soft is dynamics.
- Pressing too hard in rubbings: use the side of the crayon lightly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Texture hunt
*Student:* Touch 10 surfaces at home and describe textures (bumpy, smooth, rough).

*How to teach:* Touch 10 surfaces at home; describe each texture.

- **Q:** What is texture?
  **A:** How something feels or looks like it would feel.
- **Q:** What texture is sandpaper?
  **A:** Rough.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rubbings
*Student:* Make crayon rubbings of leaves, coins, mesh and bark.

*How to teach:* Place objects under thin paper; rub with the side of a crayon.

- **Q:** Which crayon part do you use?
  **A:** The flat side, with the paper wrapper removed.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): High and low
*Student:* Listen to high and low sounds on a xylophone or piano app; reach high or touch low.

*How to teach:* Play high and low notes; your child reaches up for high, touches toes for low.

- **Q:** Is a bird's chirp high or low?
  **A:** High.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Animal sounds
*Student:* Sort animal sounds (bird, bear) into high and low.

*How to teach:* Sort animal sounds into high (bird, mouse) and low (bear, cow).

- **Q:** Is a lion's roar high or low?
  **A:** Low.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Display rubbings and guess each texture's source.

*How to teach:* Display rubbings; family guesses each source.

- **Q:** Which rubbing looks bumpy?
  **A:** Answers based on rubbings, e.g., bark or mesh.

**Assessment:** Texture rubbings collection.

**Answer key:** Rubbings collection: at least 4 clear rubbings (leaf, coin, mesh, bark or sandpaper) each labeled with a texture word (bumpy, smooth, rough, ridged). Proficient = 4 labeled rubbings + correctly guesses sources.

## PE 1: Beam walking

**Prepare:** Masking tape line, a 2x4 board laid flat on the ground (or a low curb), beanbag. Keep the beam at ground level.

**Watch for:**
- Looking at the feet: eyes on the end of the beam keeps you steadier.
- Crossing feet when walking sideways: step out, then bring feet together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Line walk
*Student:* Walk forward on a tape line with arms out, eyes forward.

*How to teach:* Walk forward on a tape line, arms out like airplane wings, eyes on the end. Repeat 4 times.

- **Q:** Where should your eyes look?
  **A:** At the end of the line, not your feet.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Board walk
*Student:* Walk forward on a 2x4 board laid on the ground.

*How to teach:* Walk forward on the 2x4 on the ground. Spot by walking beside, but don't hold unless needed.

- **Q:** What do your arms do?
  **A:** Stretch out to the sides to help balance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Sideways
*Student:* Walk sideways along the board with feet stepping together-apart, arms out, eyes on the far end.

*How to teach:* Walk sideways along the board: step out, bring feet together, arms out, eyes on the far end.

- **Q:** Do your feet cross when walking sideways on the beam?
  **A:** No, step apart then together.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Challenge
*Student:* Carry a beanbag on your head while walking the board.

*How to teach:* Walk the board with a beanbag on the head. Go slowly; restart if it falls.

- **Q:** Why go slowly with the beanbag?
  **A:** Slow, smooth steps keep your balance and the beanbag on.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Walk the board forward and sideways without stepping off.

*How to teach:* Have your child walk forward then sideways the full length. Count step-offs.

- **Q:** Name two tips for beam walking.
  **A:** Arms out, eyes forward, slow steps, tight tummy.

**Assessment:** Beam walk check.

**Answer key:** Beam walk check: walks the length forward and sideways without stepping off; arms out; eyes forward; sideways uses step-together without crossing. Proficient = both directions with no step-offs; developing = 1-2 step-offs; beginning = needs a hand.

## Health 1: Healthy habits champion

**Prepare:** Poster board, markers, a recycling bin, and a 5-day habit tracker (handwashing, brushing, sleep).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking healthy habits are only about yourself: clean spaces and recycling help everyone.
- Teaching handwashing steps out of order: follow the poster.
- Stopping the tracker after one day: habits form with daily practice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Healthy habits poster
*Student:* Make a poster encouraging handwashing for your bathroom.

*How to teach:* Make a handwashing poster for the bathroom.

- **Q:** What should the poster show?
  **A:** The handwashing steps and 20 seconds.
- **Q:** Where should it go?
  **A:** Near the sink.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Teach someone
*Student:* Teach a younger sibling or friend handwashing steps.

*How to teach:* Teach a younger sibling or friend handwashing.

- **Q:** How will you teach?
  **A:** Show each step and sing 'Happy Birthday' twice.
- **Q:** How do you check they did it right?
  **A:** Watch for soap, all surfaces, 20 seconds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Clean environment
*Student:* Sort household recycling and talk about how clean places keep us healthy.

*How to teach:* Sort recycling; discuss how clean places keep us healthy.

- **Q:** What can be recycled?
  **A:** Paper, cardboard, plastic bottles, cans (check local rules).
- **Q:** How do clean places help health?
  **A:** Less trash means fewer germs and pests.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Habit tracker
*Student:* Track handwashing, brushing and sleep for 5 days.

*How to teach:* Track handwashing, brushing, sleep for 5 days.

- **Q:** How many brushing checks in 5 days (twice a day)?
  **A:** 10.
- **Q:** Why track?
  **A:** To see if you're building the habit.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 2 check
*Student:* Unit assessment on germs, hygiene and sleep.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 2 assessment.

- **Q:** How long should you scrub hands?
  **A:** 20 seconds.
- **Q:** How much sleep do you need?
  **A:** About 9–12 hours.

**Assessment:** Unit 2 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 2 test (suggested): 1) Germs are too small to see 2) Stop germs: wash hands, sneeze in elbow, hands away from face 3) Scrub 20 seconds 4) Wash before eating and after bathroom 5) Brush teeth 2 minutes twice a day 6) Floss once a day 7) Visit dentist about every 6 months 8) Sleep 9–12 hours. Proficient 7–8.
