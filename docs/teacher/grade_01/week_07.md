# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 7

## ELA 1: ck, ng, and proper nouns; Frog and Toad Together

**Prepare:** 'Frog and Toad Together' by Arnold Lobel, lined paper, a decodable passage with digraphs (write: 'Chad has a red truck. He packs a lunch and a fish snack. The duck sings on the rock. Then Chad shuts the shed.'), and 5 sentences for noun sorting.

**Watch for:**
- Writing ck after a long vowel or consonant (bakck, milck): ck comes right after a short vowel only.
- Writing the ng sound as 'n' (sin for sing): stretch the word and feel the back of the tongue.
- Capitalizing every noun: only names of specific people, places, and things (proper nouns) get capitals.

### Day 1 (Monday): -ck words
*Student:* Read duck, back, sick, rock, truck; learn that ck comes right after a short vowel.

*How to teach:* Read duck, back, sick, rock, truck. Point out that ck comes right after a short vowel.

- **Q:** Which is spelled right: duck or duk?
  **A:** duck — ck comes after the short u.
- **Q:** Read: rock, sick.
  **A:** rock, sick.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): -ng words
*Student:* Read sing, ring, long, hung, bang; write a silly sentence using three -ng words.

*How to teach:* Read sing, ring, long, hung, bang. Your child writes a silly sentence using three -ng words.

- **Q:** Read: hung, bang.
  **A:** hung, bang.
- **Q:** Write a silly sentence with -ng words.
  **A:** Example: The king sang a long song.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): A List
*Student:* Read 'A List' from Frog and Toad Together; make your own to-do list for tomorrow with 4 items.

*How to teach:* Read 'A List.' Talk about why Toad needed his list. Your child writes a 4-item to-do list for tomorrow.

- **Q:** What happened to Toad's list?
  **A:** The wind blew it away.
- **Q:** Why didn't Toad run after it?
  **A:** Running after the list was not one of the things written on his list.
- **Q:** How does the story end?
  **A:** Toad sits until dark, then writes 'Go to sleep' on the ground with a stick, crosses it out, and they go to sleep.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Proper nouns
*Student:* Write the names of 5 family members, your street and your town, capitalizing each proper noun.

*How to teach:* Teach that proper nouns name a special person, place, or thing and start with a capital. Your child writes the names of 5 family members, their street, and town.

- **Q:** Is 'town' or 'Fresno' a proper noun?
  **A:** Fresno.
- **Q:** Fix: 'my sister ana lives on oak street.'
  **A:** My sister Ana lives on Oak Street.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read a decodable passage with digraphs; circle common nouns and box proper nouns in 5 sentences.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the digraph passage (count errors), then circles common nouns and boxes proper nouns in 5 sentences.

- **Q:** In 'Chad has a red truck,' which noun is proper?
  **A:** Chad.
- **Q:** Which nouns are common?
  **A:** truck.

**Assessment:** Noun sort worksheet and digraph passage read with 90% accuracy.

**Answer key:** Digraph passage (37 words): proficient = 34+ words correct (90%). Noun sort sentences (suggested): 1) Sam has a dog. (Sam = proper; dog = common) 2) We go to Texas. (Texas proper) 3) The cat sat on a mat. (cat, mat common) 4) Frog and Toad went to the pond. (Frog, Toad proper; pond common) 5) Mom drove to Lake Tahoe. (Lake Tahoe proper; Mom used as a name = proper). Proficient = all nouns correctly marked.

## Math 1: Relating addition and subtraction; fact families

**Prepare:** Triangle fact-family cards (draw triangles with the total at top and parts in bottom corners: 3-5-8, 2-7-9, 4-6-10, 1-5-6, 3-4-7), counters, a number line 0–10.

**Watch for:**
- Writing 5 − 8 = 3 in a fact family: the biggest number (the whole) always comes first in a subtraction fact.
- Thinking subtraction is unrelated to addition: use 'think addition' language ('4 and what make 9?').
- For ? − 2 = 6, subtracting 2 from 6: remind that the unknown is the start — act it out: 'Something, take away 2, leaves 6' so the start is 8.

### Day 1 (Monday): Think addition
*Student:* Solve 9 − 4 by asking '4 + ? = 9' with cubes.

*How to teach:* Write 9 − 4. Make a 9-cube train, break off 4, and ask '4 plus what makes 9?' Count the rest: 5. Say 'Subtraction is finding a missing part.'

- **Q:** 9 − 4 = ? (think 4 + ? = 9)
  **A:** 5.
- **Q:** 10 − 7 = ?
  **A:** 3 (7 + 3 = 10).

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Fact families
*Student:* Write the four facts for 3, 5, 8 using a triangle card.

*How to teach:* Show a triangle card 3-5-8. Write the four facts together: 3 + 5 = 8, 5 + 3 = 8, 8 − 3 = 5, 8 − 5 = 3. Have your child do the 2-7-9 card alone.

- **Q:** Write the fact family for 2, 7, 9.
  **A:** 2 + 7 = 9, 7 + 2 = 9, 9 − 2 = 7, 9 − 7 = 2.
- **Q:** Which number is the whole in 3, 5, 8?
  **A:** 8.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Missing number
*Student:* Solve 8 − ? = 5 and ? − 2 = 6 with a number line.

*How to teach:* For 8 − ? = 5, start at 8 on the number line and count jumps back to 5 (3 jumps). For ? − 2 = 6, start at 6 and jump forward 2 to find the start (8).

- **Q:** 8 − ? = 5
  **A:** 3.
- **Q:** ? − 2 = 6
  **A:** 8.
- **Q:** 10 − ? = 4
  **A:** 6.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Fact family game
*Student:* Cover one corner of a triangle card and name the hidden number.

*How to teach:* Cover one corner of a triangle card with your thumb and have your child name the hidden number and say a fact that proves it. Switch roles.

- **Q:** Card shows 10 on top, 4 at the bottom, one corner hidden. What is hidden?
  **A:** 6, because 4 + 6 = 10.
- **Q:** Card shows 3 and 4 at the bottom, top hidden.
  **A:** 7.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Fact family worksheet (5 families) and missing-number equations.

*How to teach:* Your child writes fact families for 5 triangle cards, then solves missing-number equations.

- **Q:** Fact family for 1, 5, 6.
  **A:** 1 + 5 = 6, 5 + 1 = 6, 6 − 1 = 5, 6 − 5 = 1.
- **Q:** 7 − ? = 3
  **A:** 4.

**Assessment:** Fact family and missing-number check.

**Answer key:** Families: 3-5-8 (3+5=8, 5+3=8, 8−3=5, 8−5=3); 2-7-9 (2+7=9, 7+2=9, 9−2=7, 9−7=2); 4-6-10 (4+6=10, 6+4=10, 10−4=6, 10−6=4); 1-5-6 (1+5=6, 5+1=6, 6−1=5, 6−5=1); 3-4-7 (3+4=7, 4+3=7, 7−3=4, 7−4=3). Missing numbers (suggested): 8 − ? = 5 → 3; ? − 2 = 6 → 8; 4 + ? = 9 → 5; ? + 3 = 10 → 7; 6 − ? = 6 → 0.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Communicating with sound over distance

**Prepare:** Paper cups, a sharpened pencil to poke holes, 3 m each of string, yarn, and thin wire, paper clips, and a drum or pot.

**Watch for:**
- Holding the string slack: the string must be pulled tight to carry vibrations.
- Thinking sound travels through the cup phone as 'air in a tube': vibrations travel along the string.
- Thinking drum codes are random noise: a code needs agreed meanings (3 fast beats = 'come here').

### Day 1 (Monday): History of sound signals
*Student:* Learn how drums, bells and whistles sent messages long ago.

*How to teach:* Talk about drums, bells, and whistles used long ago to send messages (church bells, ship bells, drum signals, whistles in sports).

- **Q:** Why did people use bells and drums?
  **A:** They are loud and carry far, so people far away could hear the message.
- **Q:** Name a sound signal used today.
  **A:** Fire alarm, school bell, car horn, referee whistle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Cup phone
*Student:* Build a cup-and-string telephone and test talking across a room.

*How to teach:* Build a cup phone: poke holes in cup bottoms, thread string, tie to paper clips. Pull tight and talk.

- **Q:** What must you do with the string?
  **A:** Keep it tight.
- **Q:** What happens if the string touches something?
  **A:** The vibrations stop or get weaker.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Test materials
*Student:* Compare string, yarn and wire for the cup phone; record which works best.

*How to teach:* Compare string, yarn, and wire.

- **Q:** Which works best?
  **A:** Usually the tight thin string or wire; fluffy yarn works worst because it soaks up vibrations.
- **Q:** How do you make the test fair?
  **A:** Same cups, same length, same words, same voice.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Signal code
*Student:* Create a 3-beat drum code for messages and test it outside.

*How to teach:* Create a 3-beat drum code and test it outside.

- **Q:** Write a code.
  **A:** Example: beat-beat-pause-beat = 'dinner time.'
- **Q:** What does the listener need?
  **A:** To know the code ahead of time.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain how the vibrations travel in your cup phone.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how vibrations travel in the cup phone.

- **Q:** How does the sound get to the other cup?
  **A:** My voice vibrates the cup bottom, the string vibrates, then the other cup vibrates and makes sound.
- **Q:** Why doesn't it work with a loose string?
  **A:** A loose string doesn't pass vibrations well.

**Assessment:** Cup-phone material comparison data.

**Answer key:** Data table: string/yarn/wire with ratings (clear/fuzzy/can't hear). Expected result: tight thin string or wire clearest; yarn weakest. Proficient: table complete with a sentence naming the best material and why.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Map symbols, keys and cardinal directions

**Prepare:** A compass (or phone compass), sticky notes labeled N, S, E, W, a park map with a key, and a small 'treasure.'

**Watch for:**
- Thinking north is always 'up' in real life: north is a direction; on most maps it's at the top.
- Mixing up east and west: 'Never Eat Soggy Waffles' (N, E, S, W clockwise).
- Ignoring the map key: the key explains what each symbol means.

### Day 1 (Monday): Directions in the house
*Student:* Label walls N, S, E, W using a compass; follow directions like 'take 3 steps north'.

*How to teach:* Use a compass to label walls N, S, E, W. Follow directions like 'take 3 steps north.'

- **Q:** What are the four cardinal directions?
  **A:** North, south, east, west.
- **Q:** What is opposite of east?
  **A:** West.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Map key
*Student:* Study a park map's key and find 5 symbols on the map.

*How to teach:* Study a park map's key; find 5 symbols.

- **Q:** What is a map key?
  **A:** A box that explains what the symbols mean.
- **Q:** What might a tree symbol mean?
  **A:** A wooded area or trees.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compass rose
*Student:* Draw a compass rose and add it to your room map.

*How to teach:* Draw a compass rose and add it to the room map.

- **Q:** What does a compass rose show?
  **A:** Directions (N, S, E, W).
- **Q:** Where is north usually on a map?
  **A:** At the top.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Treasure hunt
*Student:* Follow a family-made map with symbols and directions to find a hidden treasure.

*How to teach:* Follow a family-made map with symbols and directions to find hidden treasure.

- **Q:** How did you use the map?
  **A:** Followed symbols and directions.
- **Q:** If the map says go east from the couch, which way?
  **A:** Toward the side labeled E.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Make a map of your yard or street with a key of 4 symbols and a compass rose.

*How to teach:* Your child makes a yard/street map with a key of 4 symbols and a compass rose.

- **Q:** What 4 symbols did you use?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., house, tree, mailbox, driveway.
- **Q:** Where is your house compared to the street?
  **A:** Answers use directions.

**Assessment:** Yard or street map with key and compass rose.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = map with 4 symbols explained in a key, compass rose with N/S/E/W correct, and features placed accurately; 3 = one part missing; 2 = no key or compass rose; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Warm and cool colors; rhythm patterns

**Prepare:** Crayons or watercolors, paper; rhythm cards including the quarter rest.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a rest means stopping the music: a rest is a silent beat that still counts.
- Thinking colors don't change feelings: warm colors feel energetic/happy; cool feel calm/sad.

### Day 1 (Monday): Warm vs. cool
*Student:* Sort crayons into warm and cool colors; discuss what feelings they give.

*How to teach:* Sort crayons into warm (red, orange, yellow) and cool (blue, green, purple).

- **Q:** Name three warm colors.
  **A:** Red, orange, yellow.
- **Q:** How do cool colors feel?
  **A:** Calm, quiet, cold, peaceful.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Mood painting
*Student:* Paint a sunny day in warm colors and a rainy day in cool colors.

*How to teach:* Paint a sunny day with warm colors and a rainy day with cool colors.

- **Q:** Why use cool colors for a rainy day?
  **A:** They feel calm, cold and gloomy, like rain.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Rest
*Student:* Learn the quarter rest (silent beat); clap rhythms with rests.

*How to teach:* Teach the quarter rest: one silent beat. Clap rhythms like ta ta rest ta.

- **Q:** What do you do on a rest?
  **A:** Stay silent for one beat (hands apart).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Question and answer
*Student:* Clap a 4-beat rhythm 'question' and make up an 'answer' rhythm.

*How to teach:* Clap a 4-beat 'question'; your child claps a different 4-beat 'answer.'

- **Q:** Must your answer be the same as my question?
  **A:** No, it is different but the same length (4 beats).

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain how color changed the mood of your two paintings.

*How to teach:* Your child explains how color changed the mood.

- **Q:** How did warm colors make the sunny picture feel?
  **A:** Warm, happy and bright.

**Assessment:** Mood paintings and question-answer rhythm.

**Answer key:** Mood paintings: sunny day mostly warm colors (red, orange, yellow); rainy day mostly cool colors (blue, green, purple). Question-answer: claps a 4-beat question and a different 4-beat answer keeping the beat. Proficient = correct color families + steady 8 beats.

## PE 1: Sliding and skipping preview

**Prepare:** 2 cones about 15 feet apart, chalk line, music.

**Watch for:**
- Crossing feet during a slide: the feet come together but never cross.
- Turning to face the direction of travel: in a slide, your body faces forward and you move sideways.

### Day 1 (Monday): Slide
*Student:* Slide sideways leading with one side, then the other, between two cones.

*How to teach:* Model a slide: face forward, step sideways, bring the other foot to it, never cross. Slide cone to cone leading right, then return leading left.

- **Q:** Do your feet cross in a slide?
  **A:** No, they come together but don't cross.
- **Q:** Which way does your body face in a slide?
  **A:** Forward, while you move sideways.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Slide tag
*Student:* Play slide tag along a chalk line: everyone may only slide sideways, and the tagger switches each round.

*How to teach:* Play slide tag along a chalk line: everyone may only slide sideways. Switch the tagger each round.

- **Q:** What is the only way to move in slide tag?
  **A:** Sliding sideways.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Step-hop
*Student:* Practice step-hop slowly, then faster as a skip.

*How to teach:* Practice step-hop slowly: step on right, hop on right, step on left, hop on left. Speed it up until it becomes a skip.

- **Q:** What two moves make a skip?
  **A:** A step and a hop, switching feet each time.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Locomotor music
*Student:* Change locomotor skill each time the music stops.

*How to teach:* Play music. Each time it stops, call a new skill (walk, jog, gallop, slide, hop, step-hop).

- **Q:** Show me a slide, then a gallop.
  **A:** Slide goes sideways; gallop goes forward with a lead foot.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate slide and step-hop; encourage a family member.

*How to teach:* Have your child demonstrate the slide and step-hop, then cheer on a family member trying it.

- **Q:** What is something kind you can say to someone learning?
  **A:** Example: 'Nice try! Keep your feet together, you're getting it!'

**Assessment:** Slide demonstration.

**Answer key:** Slide demonstration: body faces forward, moves sideways; step-together without crossing feet; leads with both right and left side; brief moment with both feet off the ground. Proficient = all cues on both sides; developing = 2-3 cues; beginning = feet cross or turns body.

## Health 1: Handwashing

**Prepare:** Soap, a bowl of water with black pepper, a timer, poster paper, lotion with glitter.

**Watch for:**
- Washing too fast: scrub for 20 seconds (sing 'Happy Birthday' twice).
- Skipping backs of hands, between fingers, and under nails.
- Thinking water alone cleans hands: soap is needed to lift germs off.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pepper experiment
*Student:* Sprinkle pepper on water and touch with a soapy finger to see germs 'run away'.

*How to teach:* Sprinkle pepper on water; touch with a soapy finger.

- **Q:** What happened to the pepper?
  **A:** It moved away from the soapy finger.
- **Q:** What does that show?
  **A:** Soap helps push away and lift off germs.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Six steps
*Student:* Learn handwashing steps; sing 'Happy Birthday' twice while scrubbing.

*How to teach:* Learn six handwashing steps; sing 'Happy Birthday' twice.

- **Q:** List the steps.
  **A:** 1) Wet hands 2) Add soap 3) Scrub 20 seconds — palms, backs, between fingers, under nails 4) Rinse 5) Dry with a clean towel 6) Turn off faucet with the towel.
- **Q:** How long do you scrub?
  **A:** 20 seconds.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): When to wash
*Student:* Make a poster of times to wash (before eating, after bathroom, after coughing).

*How to teach:* Make a poster of times to wash.

- **Q:** When should you wash hands?
  **A:** Before eating, after using the bathroom, after coughing/sneezing, after playing outside, after touching pets.
- **Q:** Should you wash before cooking?
  **A:** Yes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Glow test
*Student:* Rub on lotion with glitter, wash, and check how much is left to test your scrubbing.

*How to teach:* Rub on lotion with glitter, wash, and check what's left.

- **Q:** Where was glitter left?
  **A:** Often under nails, between fingers, thumbs.
- **Q:** What does leftover glitter tell you?
  **A:** Those spots need more scrubbing.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate all handwashing steps.

*How to teach:* Your child demonstrates all steps.

- **Q:** Show the steps.
  **A:** Wet, soap, scrub 20 seconds, rinse, dry, turn off faucet with towel.
- **Q:** Why dry hands?
  **A:** Wet hands spread germs more easily.

**Assessment:** Handwashing demonstration.

**Answer key:** Demonstration checklist (1 point each): wets hands; uses soap; scrubs palms; backs; between fingers; under nails/thumbs; scrubs at least 20 seconds; rinses; dries with clean towel; turns off faucet with towel. Proficient 9–10.
