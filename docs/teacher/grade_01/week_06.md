# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 6

## ELA 1: Digraphs th and wh; comparing characters

**Prepare:** 'Frog and Toad Are Friends,' Venn diagram paper (two overlapping circles), index cards, and a th/wh word list (thin, this, that, path, bath, with, when, what, which, whip, whiz, then).

**Watch for:**
- Reading wh words with a /w/ + /h/ split: wh makes one sound /w/ (in most American speech).
- Thinking the voiced th in this and unvoiced th in thin are different letters: same letters, two sounds — feel the throat buzz.
- Calling verbs or describing words nouns: ask 'Can you see it, go there, or meet it?'

### Day 1 (Monday): th words
*Student:* Read thin, this, that, path, bath; feel your tongue for voiced and unvoiced th.

*How to teach:* Read thin, this, that, path, bath. Your child puts a hand on their throat to feel buzzing (this, that) or none (thin, path, bath).

- **Q:** Does th in this buzz?
  **A:** Yes — it is voiced.
- **Q:** Does th in bath buzz?
  **A:** No — it is unvoiced.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): wh words
*Student:* Read when, what, which, whip, whiz; write three question words on cards and ask a family member a question with each.

*How to teach:* Read when, what, which, whip, whiz. Your child writes 3 question words on cards and asks a family member a question with each.

- **Q:** Read: when, which.
  **A:** when, which.
- **Q:** Ask a question that starts with What.
  **A:** Example: What is your favorite food?

### Day 3 (Wednesday): The Story
*Student:* Read 'The Story' (Frog and Toad); retell what Toad tries in order to think of a story.

*How to teach:* Read 'The Story.' Your child retells, in order, the things Toad does to try to think of a story.

- **Q:** Why does Frog want a story?
  **A:** Frog is not feeling well and asks Toad to tell him a story.
- **Q:** What did Toad do to think of a story?
  **A:** Walked up and down the porch, stood on his head, poured water over his head, and banged his head against the wall.
- **Q:** How does it end?
  **A:** Toad feels sick, so Frog tells Toad a story about what just happened, and Toad falls asleep.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Nouns
*Student:* Learn that nouns name people, places, and things; find 6 nouns in 'The Lost Button' and sort into person/place/thing.

*How to teach:* Teach that nouns name people, places, and things. Read 'A Lost Button' and find 6 nouns; sort them on a chart.

- **Q:** Name a person/animal noun from the story.
  **A:** Toad, Frog, sparrow, raccoon.
- **Q:** Name a thing noun.
  **A:** button, jacket.
- **Q:** How does the story end?
  **A:** Toad finds his own button on the floor at home, sews all the found buttons onto his jacket, and gives it to Frog.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Fill a Venn diagram comparing Frog and Toad; read th/wh word list for accuracy.

*How to teach:* Your child fills a Venn diagram: Frog only, Toad only, both. Then reads the th/wh list.

- **Q:** Name one way Frog and Toad are alike.
  **A:** They are both friends who care about each other / both are amphibians who live near each other.
- **Q:** Name one way they are different.
  **A:** Frog is usually calm and cheerful; Toad worries or gets grumpy.

**Assessment:** Venn diagram with at least 2 likenesses and 2 differences; th/wh word list read.

**Answer key:** Venn diagram: Proficient = at least 2 likenesses (both are friends, both help each other, both are amphibians) and 2 differences (Frog is cheerful/calm and helps Toad; Toad is sleepy, grumpy, or worried and loses things). th/wh list: proficient 11–12 of 12 read correctly.

## Math 1: Doubles and doubles plus one

**Prepare:** A small mirror, counters, dominoes, 2 dice, an egg carton, and pictures/toys showing doubles (spider = 8 legs, 2 hands = 10 fingers).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a double means 'twice the first number plus something' or confusing 4 + 4 with 4 + 5: use the mirror to show two equal groups.
- For near doubles, adding 1 to the wrong number or forgetting the +1: say 'double the smaller, then one more.'
- Believing 2 + 7 and 7 + 2 have different answers: build both cube trains and turn one around to see they match.

### Day 1 (Monday): Doubles
*Student:* Use a mirror with counters to see doubles (3 + 3, 4 + 4, 5 + 5); write facts.

*How to teach:* Place 3 counters next to a mirror so your child sees 6. Say '3 + 3 = 6.' Repeat with 1–5, writing each doubles fact in a list.

- **Q:** 4 + 4 = ?
  **A:** 8.
- **Q:** 5 + 5 = ?
  **A:** 10.
- **Q:** What double makes 6?
  **A:** 3 + 3.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Doubles in life
*Student:* Find doubles in real life (egg carton 6 + 6, legs of a spider 4 + 4) and draw them.

*How to teach:* Hunt for doubles at home: an egg carton (6 + 6 = 12), spider legs (4 + 4 = 8), hands (5 + 5 = 10). Your child draws two and writes the fact under each drawing.

- **Q:** A spider has 4 legs on each side. How many legs?
  **A:** 8; 4 + 4 = 8.
- **Q:** An egg carton has 6 in each row. How many eggs?
  **A:** 12; 6 + 6 = 12.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Doubles plus one
*Student:* Solve 4 + 5 by thinking 4 + 4 + 1; practice with dominoes.

*How to teach:* Show a domino with 4 and 5. Say 'I know 4 + 4 = 8, and 5 is one more than 4, so 4 + 5 = 9.' Have your child sort dominoes into doubles and doubles-plus-one and solve them.

- **Q:** 4 + 5 = ? Explain.
  **A:** 9; 4 + 4 = 8 and 1 more is 9.
- **Q:** 3 + 4 = ?
  **A:** 7; 3 + 3 + 1.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Turn-around facts
*Student:* Show 2 + 7 and 7 + 2 with cubes and explain why the sum is the same.

*How to teach:* Build a train of 2 red and 7 blue cubes. Turn it around: now it's 7 + 2. Ask whether the number of cubes changed (no). Practice saying turn-around pairs.

- **Q:** If 2 + 7 = 9, what is 7 + 2?
  **A:** 9 — same parts, switched order.
- **Q:** Write the turn-around fact for 6 + 3 = 9.
  **A:** 3 + 6 = 9.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Doubles and near-doubles quiz; roll-two-dice game recording sums.

*How to teach:* Give a short doubles/near-doubles quiz, then play: roll two dice, say the sum, and record. Ask 'Was that a double or near double?'

- **Q:** 2 + 3 = ?
  **A:** 5 (2 + 2 + 1).
- **Q:** Roll 5 and 5. Sum?
  **A:** 10.

**Assessment:** Doubles/near-doubles quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested) and answers: 1) 1+1=2 2) 2+2=4 3) 3+3=6 4) 4+4=8 5) 5+5=10 6) 2+3=5 7) 3+4=7 8) 4+5=9 9) 1+2=3 10) 5+4=9. Proficient: 9–10 correct and can explain one near double using a double.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Loud and soft, high and low

**Prepare:** 4–5 glass jars with different water levels, a metal spoon, rubber bands of different thickness, a tissue box, and picture cards (lion roar, whisper, bird tweet, tuba, siren, mouse squeak).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing pitch and volume: pitch is high/low (like a bird vs. a tuba); volume is loud/soft.
- Thinking more water always means higher sound: when you TAP the glass, more water makes a lower pitch.
- Thinking a thick band is louder: thicker bands make lower pitch; plucking harder makes louder.

### Day 1 (Monday): Water jars
*Student:* Tap jars with different water levels; order them from high to low pitch.

*How to teach:* Tap jars with different water levels and order from high to low pitch.

- **Q:** Which jar makes the highest pitch when tapped?
  **A:** The one with the least water.
- **Q:** Which makes the lowest?
  **A:** The fullest one.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Band thickness
*Student:* Pluck thick and thin rubber bands; record pitch.

*How to teach:* Pluck thick and thin bands. Record pitch.

- **Q:** Which band makes a lower sound?
  **A:** The thick band.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** It vibrates more slowly.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Loud vs. soft
*Student:* Pluck gently and strongly; compare vibration size.

*How to teach:* Pluck gently and strongly. Compare vibration size.

- **Q:** What changes when you pluck hard?
  **A:** The band vibrates in bigger movements and the sound is louder.
- **Q:** Does the pitch change?
  **A:** No — mostly only loudness changes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Make a song
*Student:* Play a short tune on your water-jar xylophone.

*How to teach:* Play a short tune (e.g., 'Hot Cross Buns') on the jar xylophone.

- **Q:** Which jars play the low notes?
  **A:** The fuller ones.
- **Q:** How can you make a note higher?
  **A:** Pour out some water.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Pitch and volume sort quiz with picture cards.

*How to teach:* Sort picture cards by pitch and volume.

- **Q:** Is a mouse squeak high or low?
  **A:** High.
- **Q:** Is a whisper loud or soft?
  **A:** Soft.

**Assessment:** Pitch/volume sort.

**Answer key:** Sort key: high pitch — bird tweet, mouse squeak, siren (also loud); low pitch — lion roar, tuba. Loud — lion roar, siren, tuba; soft — whisper, mouse squeak, bird tweet. Proficient = all cards sorted correctly on both pitch and volume.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: What is a map? Models vs. pictures

**Prepare:** Blocks or small boxes, a toy, a camera/phone, and paper.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking maps must show everything: maps show the important things from above using symbols.
- Drawing a side view instead of bird's-eye view: imagine you're a bird looking straight down.
- Thinking a photo and a map are the same: a photo shows what something looks like; a map shows where things are.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bird's-eye view
*Student:* Look down at a toy from above and from the side; draw both views.

*How to teach:* Look at a toy from above and from the side; draw both.

- **Q:** What is a bird's-eye view?
  **A:** Looking straight down from above.
- **Q:** What does a cup look like from above?
  **A:** A circle.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Room model
*Student:* Build a 3-D model of your bedroom with blocks and small boxes.

*How to teach:* Build a 3-D model of your bedroom with blocks and boxes.

- **Q:** What could stand for your bed?
  **A:** A rectangular block or box.
- **Q:** What is a model?
  **A:** A small copy of something real.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Photo vs. model
*Student:* Photograph your room and compare: what does the model show that the photo doesn't (and vice versa)?

*How to teach:* Photograph the room and compare with the model.

- **Q:** What does the photo show that the model doesn't?
  **A:** Real colors, details, objects.
- **Q:** What does the model show better?
  **A:** Where things are compared to each other, from any side.

### Day 4 (Thursday): First map
*Student:* Draw a bird's-eye map of your room with simple symbols.

*How to teach:* Draw a bird's-eye map of the room with simple symbols.

- **Q:** How would you draw a bed on the map?
  **A:** A rectangle.
- **Q:** How would you draw a round rug?
  **A:** A circle.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one thing a model shows better and one thing a picture shows better.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one thing a model shows better and one thing a picture shows better.

- **Q:** Model shows better?
  **A:** Where things are in the room and their size from all sides.
- **Q:** Picture shows better?
  **A:** Real colors and details.

**Assessment:** Room map and model-vs-picture comparison.

**Answer key:** Map rubric: 4 = bird's-eye view, main furniture placed correctly with simple symbols, plus clear model-vs-picture comparison; 3 = mostly correct; 2 = side views or major misplacements; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Secondary colors and the color wheel; quarter and eighth notes

**Prepare:** Paint, paper plate divided into 6, images of Van Gogh's 'Sunflowers' and another sunflower painting; rhythm cards with ta (quarter note) and ti-ti (two eighth notes).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking ti-ti is two beats: ti-ti is two quick sounds in ONE beat.
- Placing colors randomly on the wheel: each secondary sits between the two primaries that make it.

### Day 1 (Monday): Color wheel
*Student:* Paint a 6-part color wheel on a paper plate.

*How to teach:* Paint red, yellow, blue in every other section; mix to paint orange between red and yellow, green between yellow and blue, purple between blue and red.

- **Q:** What goes between red and yellow?
  **A:** Orange.
- **Q:** What are secondary colors?
  **A:** Orange, green and purple; made by mixing two primary colors.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Same subject
*Student:* Compare two paintings of sunflowers (Van Gogh and another artist); talk about color choices.

*How to teach:* Compare Van Gogh's 'Sunflowers' with another sunflower painting; talk about color choices.

- **Q:** What colors did Van Gogh mostly use?
  **A:** Yellows, oranges and browns (warm colors).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Ta and ti-ti
*Student:* Learn quarter notes (ta) and pairs of eighth notes (ti-ti); clap 4-beat rhythm cards.

*How to teach:* Clap ta (one sound per beat) and ti-ti (two quick sounds per beat); read 4-beat cards.

- **Q:** How many sounds in ti-ti?
  **A:** Two sounds on one beat.
- **Q:** Clap: ta ta ti-ti ta.
  **A:** Clap, clap, clap-clap (quick), clap.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Write rhythms
*Student:* Write your own 4-beat rhythm using ta and ti-ti.

*How to teach:* Write a 4-beat rhythm using ta and ti-ti.

- **Q:** How many beats in your rhythm?
  **A:** Four.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Play your rhythm on a drum and show your color wheel.

*How to teach:* Play your rhythm on a drum and show the color wheel.

- **Q:** Which colors are across from each other: red and ___?
  **A:** Green.

**Assessment:** Color wheel and rhythm reading.

**Answer key:** Color wheel: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple in order, with each secondary between its parents. Rhythm reading: claps 4-beat cards correctly (ta = 1 sound on a beat; ti-ti = 2 even sounds on a beat). Proficient = wheel in correct order + 3 of 4 cards correct.

## PE 1: Hopping

**Prepare:** Chalk for a hopscotch board and lily pads, beanbag (or small stone). Flat surface.

**Watch for:**
- Using both feet (a jump) instead of one (a hop): a hop takes off and lands on the same one foot.
- Landing stiff-legged: bend the knee and land on the ball of the foot softly.

### Day 1 (Monday): Hop cues
*Student:* Hop on right foot 5 times, then left foot, landing softly.

*How to teach:* Show a hop: take off and land on the same foot, knee bent, arms out. Hop 5 on the right, then 5 on the left.

- **Q:** What is a hop?
  **A:** Take off and land on the same one foot.
- **Q:** How do you land softly?
  **A:** Bend your knee and land on the ball of your foot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hopscotch
*Student:* Play hopscotch, tossing a beanbag into squares.

*How to teach:* Draw a hopscotch board. Toss the beanbag into square 1, hop over it on one foot in single squares, land with two feet in double squares, then hop back and pick it up.

- **Q:** In hopscotch, how do you land in a single square?
  **A:** On one foot.
- **Q:** Do you hop in the square with your beanbag?
  **A:** No, you skip over it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Hop patterns
*Student:* Hop in a pattern: right-right-left-left.

*How to teach:* Model right-right-left-left hops. Have your child say the pattern aloud while hopping.

- **Q:** What comes after right-right?
  **A:** Left-left.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Hop and go
*Student:* Hop across lily-pad spots drawn in chalk.

*How to teach:* Draw chalk circles as lily pads a short hop apart. Your child hops from pad to pad on one foot, switching feet halfway.

- **Q:** Why do your arms go out when you hop?
  **A:** To help you balance.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Hop 10 times on each foot in a row.

*How to teach:* Count hops in a row on each foot without the other foot touching. Record on the checklist.

- **Q:** Which foot is easier to hop on?
  **A:** Answers vary; encourage practice on the weaker foot.

**Assessment:** Hop count each foot.

**Answer key:** Hop count: record consecutive hops on right and left foot (goal 10 each without the other foot touching). 10/10 both feet = proficient; 5-9 = developing; under 5 = beginning. Note soft landings and arms used for balance.

## Health 1: What are germs?

**Prepare:** Lotion, glitter, a spray bottle with water, paper towels, disinfectant wipes (adult use), and a KidsHealth article on germs (kidshealth.org/kids).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking germs are only on dirty-looking things: germs are too small to see.
- Sneezing into hands: hands then spread germs; sneeze into your elbow or a tissue.
- Thinking all germs are harmful: some germs make us sick; many tiny living things are harmless or helpful.

### Day 1 (Monday): Glitter germs
*Student:* Put lotion and glitter on hands, shake hands with family and see how 'germs' spread.

*How to teach:* Put lotion and glitter on hands; shake hands with family and see 'germs' spread.

- **Q:** What does the glitter show?
  **A:** How germs spread from hand to hand.
- **Q:** Why can't we see real germs?
  **A:** They're too tiny — you need a microscope.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Sneeze spray
*Student:* Use a spray bottle to show how far a sneeze travels; practice the elbow sneeze.

*How to teach:* Use a spray bottle to show how far a sneeze travels; practice the elbow sneeze.

- **Q:** How far can a sneeze spray?
  **A:** Several feet (across a room).
- **Q:** Where should you sneeze?
  **A:** Into your elbow or a tissue.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Germ hideouts
*Student:* Find places germs collect (doorknobs, phones) and wipe them with an adult.

*How to teach:* Find germ hideouts and wipe them with an adult.

- **Q:** Name a germ hideout.
  **A:** Doorknobs, phones, remotes, faucet handles.
- **Q:** Who uses disinfectant wipes?
  **A:** An adult (or with an adult helping).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read a KidsHealth article on germs together.

*How to teach:* Read a KidsHealth article on germs.

- **Q:** How do germs get into our bodies?
  **A:** Through mouth, nose, eyes, and cuts.
- **Q:** What can germs cause?
  **A:** Colds, flu, stomach bugs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 3 ways to stop germs (wash hands, sneeze into elbow, keep hands away from face) and show the elbow sneeze.

*How to teach:* Your child explains 3 ways to stop germs and shows the elbow sneeze.

- **Q:** Name 3 ways.
  **A:** Wash hands, sneeze/cough into elbow, keep hands away from face.
- **Q:** Show the elbow sneeze.
  **A:** Cover nose and mouth with inside of elbow.

**Assessment:** Oral explanation of germ prevention.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names 3 correct ways (wash hands, sneeze/cough into elbow or tissue, keep hands away from face/eyes/mouth) and demonstrates elbow sneeze; 3 = 2 ways; 2 = 1 way; 1 = none.
