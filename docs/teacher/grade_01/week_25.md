# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 25

## ELA 1: Syllables and compound words; plant life cycle text

**Prepare:** 'From Seed to Plant' by Gail Gibbons (library), 2–3 dry beans, a zip bag with a wet paper towel, tape (to stick the bag in a window), life-cycle cards (seed, sprout, plant, flower), and 12 printed words for syllable counting.

**Watch for:**
- Clapping every letter or sound as a syllable: syllables are beats — put a hand under your chin; each time the chin drops is a syllable.
- Thinking compound words are any long words: a compound is two real words joined (sun + set).
- Thinking a seed turns into a flower right away: plants go seed → sprout → plant → flower → seed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clap syllables
*Student:* Clap and count syllables in names and objects; find the vowel in each syllable.

*How to teach:* Clap names and objects (Ma-ri-a, ta-ble, ba-na-na). Find the vowel in each syllable.

- **Q:** How many syllables in banana?
  **A:** 3: ba-na-na.
- **Q:** How many in table?
  **A:** 2: ta-ble.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Compound words
*Student:* Read sunset, rainbow, backpack, cupcake; split them and draw both parts.

*How to teach:* Read sunset, rainbow, backpack, cupcake. Split them and draw both parts.

- **Q:** What two words make rainbow?
  **A:** rain + bow.
- **Q:** What two words make backpack?
  **A:** back + pack.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Seed to plant
*Student:* Read 'From Seed to Plant'; start a bean in a wet paper towel bag and draw it.

*How to teach:* Read 'From Seed to Plant.' Start the bean bag and draw it.

- **Q:** What does a seed need to sprout?
  **A:** Water, warmth, and air (soil helps the plant grow; sunlight once leaves grow).
- **Q:** What carries pollen from flower to flower in the book?
  **A:** Insects such as bees (and the wind) carry pollen.

### Day 4 (Thursday): How things connect
*Student:* Explain how a seed connects to a flower using the book's pictures; order 4 life-cycle cards.

*How to teach:* Explain how a seed becomes a flower using the book's pictures. Your child orders 4 cards.

- **Q:** Order: plant, seed, flower, sprout.
  **A:** seed, sprout, plant, flower.
- **Q:** What does the flower make so new plants can grow?
  **A:** Seeds.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Syllable count of 12 printed words; retell the plant life cycle in order.

*How to teach:* Your child counts syllables in 12 words and retells the life cycle in order.

- **Q:** Syllables in garden?
  **A:** 2.
- **Q:** Retell the plant life cycle.
  **A:** Seed, sprout (seedling), grown plant, flower, new seeds.

**Assessment:** Syllable sort and life-cycle sequencing.

**Answer key:** Syllable sort (suggested 12): cat 1, frog 1, seed 1, sun 1, garden 2, rabbit 2, sunset 2, flower 2, kitten 2, banana 3, dinosaur 3, butterfly 3. Proficient 11–12. Life-cycle sequence: seed → sprout → plant → flower (→ seeds).

## Math 1: Subtracting multiples of 10

**Prepare:** Ten rods, a 120 chart, 10 real dimes (or paper), and a quiz of 10 subtracting-tens problems.

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting 3 from 70 when asked 70 − 30 (getting 67): say 'seven tens take away three tens.'
- Moving down the chart instead of up to subtract tens: up = 10 less.
- Thinking dimes are worth 1 each: a dime is 10¢, so count dimes by tens.

### Day 1 (Monday): Take away tens
*Student:* Solve 70 − 30 with ten rods.

*How to teach:* Lay out 7 rods (70). Take away 3 rods. Count: 4 rods = 40. Write 70 − 30 = 40.

- **Q:** 70 − 30 = ?
  **A:** 40.
- **Q:** 50 − 20 = ?
  **A:** 30.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Think addition
*Student:* Solve 90 − 60 by thinking 60 + ? = 90.

*How to teach:* For 90 − 60, ask '60 plus what makes 90?' Count up by tens: 70, 80, 90 = 3 tens = 30.

- **Q:** 90 − 60 = ?
  **A:** 30.
- **Q:** 80 − 40 = ?
  **A:** 40.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): On the chart
*Student:* Jump up columns on the 120 chart to subtract tens.

*How to teach:* On the chart, start at 80 and jump up 3 rows to subtract 30: 70, 60, 50.

- **Q:** 80 − 30 = ?
  **A:** 50.
- **Q:** 60 − 50 = ?
  **A:** 10.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Money tens
*Student:* Use dimes as tens: 80¢ − 50¢.

*How to teach:* Count 8 dimes: 10, 20 ... 80¢. Take away 5 dimes: 30¢ left. Write 80¢ − 50¢ = 30¢.

- **Q:** 80¢ − 50¢ = ?
  **A:** 30¢.
- **Q:** You have 6 dimes and spend 4. How much is left?
  **A:** 20¢.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* 10 subtracting-tens problems.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 10 subtracting-tens problems with rods or the chart.

- **Q:** 90 − 10 = ?
  **A:** 80.
- **Q:** 40 − 40 = ?
  **A:** 0.

**Assessment:** Subtracting tens quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) 50−20=30 2) 70−40=30 3) 90−30=60 4) 60−10=50 5) 80−80=0 6) 40−20=20 7) 90−50=40 8) 30−10=20 9) 70−60=10 10) 80−30=50. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Biomimicry: people copy nature

**Prepare:** A Velcro strip, burrs or seed heads (if available), magnifier, and invention picture cards (bike helmet, swim fins, sun hat, tweezers).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking inventions copied nature exactly: people copy an idea or shape, not the animal itself.
- Thinking biomimicry is only about animals: plants inspire inventions too (burrs → Velcro).
- Matching by looks instead of function: match by what the part does.

### Day 1 (Monday): Velcro and burrs
*Student:* Compare Velcro and a burr under a magnifier; draw the tiny hooks on both.

*How to teach:* Compare Velcro and a burr under a magnifier; draw tiny hooks.

- **Q:** What do burrs have that Velcro copies?
  **A:** Tiny hooks that stick to loops (like fur or fabric).
- **Q:** Who invented Velcro?
  **A:** George de Mestral, after burrs stuck to his dog's fur.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Invention match
*Student:* Match inventions (bike helmet, swim fins, sun hat, tweezers) to the animal or plant part they copy (turtle shell, duck feet, leaves, bird beak).

*How to teach:* Match inventions to nature parts.

- **Q:** What do swim fins copy?
  **A:** Duck's webbed feet.
- **Q:** What does a bike helmet copy?
  **A:** A turtle's hard shell.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Define a problem
*Student:* Choose a human problem: keeping your head safe, staying cool in the sun, or picking up tiny things; draw the problem.

*How to teach:* Choose a human problem and draw it.

- **Q:** Choose: keep head safe, stay cool, or pick up tiny things.
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** What is your problem in one sentence?
  **A:** Example: I need to stay cool in the sun.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Brainstorm
*Student:* List animal or plant parts that solve a similar problem in nature for your chosen problem.

*How to teach:* List animal or plant parts that solve a similar problem.

- **Q:** What in nature stays cool in the sun?
  **A:** Big leaves that shade; elephant's big ears; a desert animal's burrow.
- **Q:** What in nature picks up tiny things?
  **A:** A bird's thin beak.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one biomimicry example in two sentences: the nature part and the invention.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one biomimicry example in two sentences.

- **Q:** Explain Velcro.
  **A:** Burrs have tiny hooks that stick to fur. Velcro copies these hooks to stick to loops.
- **Q:** Explain swim fins.
  **A:** Ducks have webbed feet to push water. Swim fins copy that shape to help people swim.

**Assessment:** Biomimicry explanation and problem statement.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names a nature part and the invention, and explains how the function is copied, plus a clear problem statement; 3 = correct pair with partial explanation; 2 = pair only; 1 = incorrect.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: California's first peoples

**Prepare:** Calisphere photos of Pomo and Miwok baskets, a map of California tribal homelands (e.g., the California Indian Heritage Center or a library resource), information about the tribe(s) native to your area (e.g., Ohlone, Tongva, Chumash, Miwok), sunflower seeds and two smooth stones.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking American Indians only lived long ago: California Indian nations live, work, and keep traditions today.
- Thinking all American Indians were the same: California had many tribes with different languages and ways of life.
- Using stereotypes (feather headdresses, teepees) for California tribes: most California tribes lived in homes made of tule reeds, bark, or brush and had their own customs.

### Day 1 (Monday): First Californians
*Student:* Learn the name of the tribe(s) native to your area and locate their homeland on a map.

*How to teach:* Learn the tribe(s) native to your area; locate the homeland on a map.

- **Q:** Which tribe's homeland are we on?
  **A:** Answers depend on location — e.g., Ohlone (San Francisco Bay Area), Tongva (Los Angeles area), Chumash (Santa Barbara coast), Miwok (central California).
- **Q:** Were there many tribes in California?
  **A:** Yes — more than 100 tribes speaking many languages.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Baskets
*Student:* Examine Calisphere photos of Pomo and Miwok baskets; describe patterns and uses.

*How to teach:* Examine Pomo and Miwok basket photos; describe patterns and uses.

- **Q:** What were baskets used for?
  **A:** Gathering, storing, cooking (with hot stones), carrying babies, and ceremonies.
- **Q:** Why are Pomo baskets famous?
  **A:** They are very finely woven, some with feathers and shells.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Acorns to food
*Student:* Learn how acorns were gathered and ground into flour; grind sunflower seeds with stones to model it.

*How to teach:* Learn how acorns became flour; grind sunflower seeds with stones.

- **Q:** How were acorns made into food?
  **A:** Gathered, cracked, ground into flour with stone tools, washed (leached) with water to remove bitterness, then cooked as mush or bread.
- **Q:** Why wash the acorn flour?
  **A:** To remove bitter tannins.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Living today
*Student:* Read about California Indian communities today and their traditions.

*How to teach:* Read about California Indian communities today.

- **Q:** Do California Indians still live in California?
  **A:** Yes — in many communities, keeping traditions like basket weaving, language, and dance.
- **Q:** Name a tradition kept today.
  **A:** Basket weaving, language classes, ceremonies, acorn gatherings.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain two contributions of American Indians to California culture.

*How to teach:* Your child explains two contributions of American Indians to California.

- **Q:** Name a contribution.
  **A:** Basket weaving art; knowledge of plants and acorn food; place names (e.g., Napa, Yosemite, Tahoe).
- **Q:** Another?
  **A:** Caring for the land, such as controlled burning to keep forests healthy.

**Assessment:** Oral explanation with evidence.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = two accurate contributions with evidence (e.g., Pomo basket photos show skilled weaving; acorn grinding shows how they made food from local plants; place names) and names the local tribe; 3 = two contributions, little evidence; 2 = one; 1 = inaccurate or stereotyped.

## Art & Music 1: Art and music of West Africa

**Prepare:** Paper strips in gold (yellow), green, red and black; images of kente cloth; bucket or drum; 'Funga Alafia' recording.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking kente colors are random: each color has meaning.
- Answering before the call ends: wait for the leader, then respond.

### Day 1 (Monday): Kente
*Student:* Look at kente cloth and learn its colors' meanings.

*How to teach:* Look at kente cloth from Ghana; learn color meanings.

- **Q:** Where does kente cloth come from?
  **A:** Ghana in West Africa.
- **Q:** What does gold mean in kente?
  **A:** Wealth, royalty, high status.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Paper kente
*Student:* Weave strips of gold, green, red and black paper into a kente-style pattern and glue the ends.

*How to teach:* Weave gold, green, red and black strips over-under; glue ends.

- **Q:** What does green stand for?
  **A:** Growth, harvest, new life.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Drumming
*Student:* Play djembe-style call-and-response rhythms on a bucket.

*How to teach:* Play call-and-response rhythms on a bucket like a djembe.

- **Q:** What is call and response?
  **A:** A leader plays or sings a part and the group answers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Song
*Student:* Sing 'Funga Alafia' with motions.

*How to teach:* Sing 'Funga Alafia' with welcoming motions.

- **Q:** What kind of song is 'Funga Alafia'?
  **A:** A West African welcome song.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Present kente weaving and lead a call-and-response.

*How to teach:* Present weaving; lead a call-and-response.

- **Q:** Why do people wear kente?
  **A:** For special occasions; it shows pride and meaning.

**Assessment:** Kente weaving and call-and-response.

**Answer key:** Kente weaving: alternating strips in gold, green, red, black glued at ends. Child names one meaning (gold = wealth/royalty; green = growth/harvest; red = sacrifice/strength; black = maturity/ancestors). Call-and-response: echoes leader and leads one call. Proficient = all.

## PE 1: Jumping and landing; long rope

**Prepare:** Long rope (8-12 feet), chalk; two turners (family members).

**Watch for:**
- Landing on flat, stiff feet: land softly on balls of feet with bent knees.
- Running in when the rope is at the top: enter when the rope just passed the bottom and is going away.

### Day 1 (Monday): Jump and land
*Student:* Jump forward and land softly on two feet.

*How to teach:* Jump forward with two feet and land softly with knees bent.

- **Q:** How do you land softly?
  **A:** On the balls of your feet with bent knees.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Snake rope
*Student:* Jump over a wiggling 'snake' rope on the ground.

*How to teach:* Wiggle a rope on the ground like a snake; your child jumps over it.

- **Q:** Do you jump with one foot or two?
  **A:** Two feet together.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Long rope
*Student:* Jump a long rope turned by two family members.

*How to teach:* Two turners swing a long rope; your child stands in the middle and jumps each time it reaches the feet.

- **Q:** When do you jump?
  **A:** When the rope comes near your feet.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Rhyme
*Student:* Jump to 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear' rhyme.

*How to teach:* Jump to 'Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear, turn around…' doing the actions.

- **Q:** What helps you keep the rhythm?
  **A:** Saying the rhyme and jumping on the beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Count your best number of long-rope jumps in a row out of 3 tries.

*How to teach:* Count best long-rope jumps in a row from 3 tries.

- **Q:** What was your best count?
  **A:** Record child's count.

**Assessment:** Long rope count.

**Answer key:** Record the best number of consecutive long-rope jumps out of 3 tries. 10+ = proficient; 5-9 = developing; under 5 = beginning.

## Health 1: Bike and playground safety

**Prepare:** A bike helmet, a bike or scooter, 2 raw eggs and foam (or a sponge) for an egg 'helmet,' and a park.

**Watch for:**
- Wearing the helmet tipped back: it should sit low on the forehead, two fingers above the eyebrows.
- Loose straps: they should form a V under each ear, with one finger of space under the chin strap.
- Thinking you only need a helmet for long rides: always wear one.

### Day 1 (Monday): Helmet fit
*Student:* Practice the 2-V-1 helmet fit test.

*How to teach:* Practice the 2-V-1 helmet fit test.

- **Q:** What does 2 mean?
  **A:** 2 fingers between eyebrows and helmet.
- **Q:** V?
  **A:** Straps make a V under each ear.
- **Q:** 1?
  **A:** 1 finger fits under the chin strap.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Egg helmet
*Student:* Drop an egg with and without a foam 'helmet' to see protection.

*How to teach:* Drop an egg with and without a foam helmet (outside, low height).

- **Q:** What happened to the egg without a helmet?
  **A:** It cracked.
- **Q:** What does a helmet do?
  **A:** Cushions and protects your head.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Bike ride
*Student:* Ride with helmet in a safe area.

*How to teach:* Ride with a helmet in a safe area.

- **Q:** Where is a safe place to ride?
  **A:** Driveway, park path, empty lot with an adult watching.
- **Q:** What else should you wear?
  **A:** Closed-toe shoes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Playground rules
*Student:* Make a list of playground safety rules and practice at a park.

*How to teach:* List playground rules and practice at a park.

- **Q:** Name 2 playground rules.
  **A:** Go down the slide feet first; walk around swings; take turns.
- **Q:** Why feet first?
  **A:** So you don't hit your head.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Put on your helmet alone and pass the 2-V-1 fit check; name 2 playground rules.

*How to teach:* Your child puts on the helmet alone and passes 2-V-1; names 2 rules.

- **Q:** Show 2-V-1.
  **A:** Two fingers, V under ears, one finger under chin strap.
- **Q:** Name 2 rules.
  **A:** Take turns; slide feet first.

**Assessment:** Helmet fit check.

**Answer key:** Helmet check: 2 fingers above brows ✓, V under each ear ✓, 1 finger under chin strap ✓, buckled snug ✓. Proficient = all 4 done independently plus 2 correct playground rules.
