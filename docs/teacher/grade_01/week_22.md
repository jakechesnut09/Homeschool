# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 22

## ELA 1: er, ir, ur words; authors' reasons

**Prepare:** A ReadWorks passage about recycling (grade 1), conjunction cards (and, but, or, so, because), irregular word flash cards (said, were, there, could, would), and an er/ir/ur sort.

**Watch for:**
- Spelling bird as 'burd': er, ir, ur all sound /er/ — memorize spelling by word families.
- Sounding out irregular words: said doesn't follow patterns — practice it as a heart word.
- Using 'and' to join everything: but shows contrast, so shows a result, or shows a choice.

### Day 1 (Monday): er/ir/ur
*Student:* Read her, bird, girl, fur, turn, nurse; learn all three spell the same sound.

*How to teach:* Read her, bird, girl, fur, turn, nurse. Teach that er, ir, ur spell the same sound.

- **Q:** Read: bird, nurse.
  **A:** bird, nurse.
- **Q:** Which spelling is in fur?
  **A:** ur.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Tricky words
*Student:* Practice irregular words (said, were, there, could, would) with a flash-card race.

*How to teach:* Flash-card race with said, were, there, could, would.

- **Q:** Read: said, were.
  **A:** said, were.
- **Q:** Use 'could' in a sentence.
  **A:** Example: I could jump high.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Author's reasons
*Student:* Read a short passage on why we should recycle; underline two reasons the author gives.

*How to teach:* Read a short recycling passage. Your child underlines two reasons the author gives.

- **Q:** What is the author's main point?
  **A:** We should recycle.
- **Q:** Name a reason an author might give for recycling.
  **A:** It saves resources/trees, keeps trash out of landfills, and can be made into new things.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Conjunctions
*Student:* Join sentence pairs with and, but, or, so; write 4 compound sentences.

*How to teach:* Join sentence pairs with and, but, or, so. Your child writes 4 compound sentences.

- **Q:** Join: 'It rained. We stayed in.'
  **A:** It rained, so we stayed in.
- **Q:** Join: 'I like cats. My sister likes dogs.'
  **A:** I like cats, but my sister likes dogs.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* er/ir/ur sort; name the author's point and two reasons.

*How to teach:* Your child sorts er/ir/ur words and tells the author's point and two reasons.

- **Q:** Sort: her, girl, turn.
  **A:** er: her; ir: girl; ur: turn.
- **Q:** What is an author's reason?
  **A:** A fact or idea that supports the author's point.

**Assessment:** Author's point/reasons organizer and conjunction sentences.

**Answer key:** Organizer: author's point = we should recycle; reasons from the passage (commonly: recycling saves trees/resources, reduces trash in landfills, things can be made new). Conjunction sentences: 4 correct compound sentences using and, but, or, so — proficient 4/4 with a comma before the conjunction (comma not required for proficiency in grade 1).

## Math 1: 10 more and 10 less

**Prepare:** 120 chart, base-ten blocks, a list of 20 numbers for mental 10 more/10 less, plus-shaped chart pieces, and a Unit 6 test (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 10 more than 47 is 48 (adding 1): show on the chart that 10 more is straight down, not to the right.
- Changing the ones digit when finding 10 less: only the tens digit changes; the ones stay the same.
- Forgetting that 10 more than 95 is 105 (not 96 or 15): count it on the 120 chart.

### Day 1 (Monday): On the chart
*Student:* Move up and down columns on the 120 chart to find 10 more/less.

*How to teach:* Put a finger on 34. Move down one row: 44 (10 more). Move up: 24 (10 less). Practice with 6 numbers.

- **Q:** 10 more than 34?
  **A:** 44.
- **Q:** 10 less than 71?
  **A:** 61.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): With blocks
*Student:* Add or remove a ten rod; tell the new number.

*How to teach:* Build 56 with blocks. Add a ten rod: 66. Remove a rod: 46. Ask which digit changed.

- **Q:** 10 more than 56?
  **A:** 66 — the tens digit changes from 5 to 6.
- **Q:** 10 less than 20?
  **A:** 10.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mental math
*Student:* Answer 10 more/10 less for 20 numbers without a chart.

*How to teach:* Say 20 numbers quickly and your child says 10 more or 10 less without the chart. If stuck, picture the blocks.

- **Q:** 10 more than 89?
  **A:** 99.
- **Q:** 10 less than 15?
  **A:** 5.
- **Q:** 10 more than 105?
  **A:** 115.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Number puzzles
*Student:* Fill 'plus-shaped' 120 chart pieces.

*How to teach:* Give plus-shaped pieces with only the center filled. Your child fills up (10 less), down (10 more), left (1 less), right (1 more).

- **Q:** Center 67: fill up, down, left, right.
  **A:** Up 57, down 77, left 66, right 68.
- **Q:** Center 100?
  **A:** Up 90, down 110, left 99, right 101.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Place value unit test: count to 120, tens and ones, comparing, and 10 more/10 less.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 6 test, reading aloud as needed.

- **Q:** What is 7 tens 2 ones?
  **A:** 72.
- **Q:** 10 less than 43?
  **A:** 33.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 test (suggested): 1) Count from 96 to 104: 96, 97, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104 2) 58 = 5 tens 8 ones 3) 30 + 6 = 36 4) 47 < 74 5) 82 > 28 6) 65 = 65 7) 10 more than 39 = 49 8) 10 less than 90 = 80 9) 10 more than 110 = 120 10) Order 33, 13, 31 → 13, 31, 33. Proficient 9–10.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Daylight and seasons wrap-up

**Prepare:** Daylight graph and data, paper plates, brad fastener, crayons, and NASA Space Place (spaceplace.nasa.gov) seasons activity.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking daylight changes suddenly: it changes a little every day.
- Thinking the longest day is in July: it's around June 21 (summer solstice).
- Thinking spring and fall have the same weather everywhere: their daylight is similar (about 12 hours) but weather varies.

### Day 1 (Monday): Update data
*Student:* Record this week's sunrise/sunset and add it to your daylight graph; notice daylight growing since winter.

*How to teach:* Record this week's sunrise/sunset; add to graph; notice growth since winter.

- **Q:** Is daylight longer now than in December?
  **A:** Yes — daylight grows from late December to late June.
- **Q:** By about how much?
  **A:** Answers vary with date — compare with recorded numbers.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): NASA Space Place seasons
*Student:* Complete a NASA Space Place seasons activity and write one sentence about what you learned.

*How to teach:* Complete a NASA Space Place seasons activity and write a sentence.

- **Q:** Write a sentence.
  **A:** Example: Earth's tilt causes seasons.
- **Q:** When is the shortest day?
  **A:** Around December 21.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Seasons wheel
*Student:* Make a paper-plate seasons wheel with a spinner showing daylight length and a picture for each season.

*How to teach:* Make a seasons wheel showing daylight length and pictures.

- **Q:** What do you show for summer?
  **A:** Long days, sun high.
- **Q:** For winter?
  **A:** Short days.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Present
*Student:* Use the wheel to teach a family member how daylight changes through the year.

*How to teach:* Your child teaches a family member how daylight changes.

- **Q:** How does daylight change from winter to summer?
  **A:** Days get longer.
- **Q:** From summer to winter?
  **A:** Days get shorter.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 check
*Student:* Daylight assessment: read a daylight graph, name the longest/shortest season, and predict next month's daylight.

*How to teach:* Give the daylight assessment.

- **Q:** Which season has the shortest days?
  **A:** Winter.
- **Q:** Predict: next month (spring), longer or shorter days?
  **A:** Longer.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 5 test (suggested): read a graph showing Dec ≈ 10 h, Mar ≈ 12 h, Jun ≈ 14½ h, Sep ≈ 12 h: 1) longest = June/summer 2) shortest = December/winter 3) March and September about equal 4) predict April: longer than March (about 13 h) 5) Why? Earth's tilt. Proficient 5/5.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Games, toys, clothes and manners long ago

**Prepare:** Marbles, jacks, chalk for hopscotch, a button and string (whirligig), a cup-and-ball (cup, string, ball), and photos of children's clothing from 1900.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking old toys were boring: kids made up many games with simple toys.
- Thinking manners are old-fashioned: many manners (please, thank you, shaking hands) are still used.
- Thinking children wore the same clothes then: girls often wore long dresses and boys wore knickers.

### Day 1 (Monday): Old games
*Student:* Learn and play marbles, jacks and hopscotch.

*How to teach:* Learn and play marbles, jacks, and hopscotch.

- **Q:** How do you play hopscotch?
  **A:** Toss a marker, hop through squares on one foot (two in side-by-side squares), skip the marked square.
- **Q:** What do you need for jacks?
  **A:** Small jacks and a little ball.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Toys
*Student:* Make a simple toy like a whirligig (button on a string) or a cup-and-ball.

*How to teach:* Make a whirligig or cup-and-ball.

- **Q:** How does a whirligig work?
  **A:** Wind up the string, then pull to make the button spin.
- **Q:** What were toys long ago made from?
  **A:** Wood, string, cloth, tin.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Clothes
*Student:* Compare photos of children's clothing from 1900 and today.

*How to teach:* Compare children's clothing from 1900 and today.

- **Q:** What did girls wear in 1900?
  **A:** Long dresses, aprons/pinafores, high boots.
- **Q:** What do children wear today?
  **A:** T-shirts, jeans, sneakers.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Manners
*Student:* Discuss manners of long ago (bowing, curtsying, 'Yes, ma'am') and which we still use.

*How to teach:* Discuss manners long ago and which we still use.

- **Q:** Name an old manner.
  **A:** Bowing or curtsying, saying 'Yes, ma'am.'
- **Q:** Name a manner still used.
  **A:** Please and thank you, shaking hands.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Teach an old game to a family member and explain how it was played long ago.

*How to teach:* Your child teaches an old game and explains it.

- **Q:** How was hopscotch played long ago?
  **A:** On chalk or scratched lines in dirt, same as today.
- **Q:** Why did kids play simple games?
  **A:** Fewer toys; games used things they had.

**Assessment:** Old-game demonstration.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = teaches an old game with clear rules and explains it was played long ago with simple materials; 3 = rules mostly clear; 2 = demo without explanation; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Form and timbre review

**Prepare:** Air-dry clay, paint for later; recordings of 6 instruments (violin, flute, trumpet, drum, piano, guitar).

**Watch for:**
- Attaching clay pieces without scoring: scratch and add a little water ('score and slip') so pieces stick.
- Guessing by look not sound: listen with eyes closed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Clay animal
*Student:* Sculpt a clay animal with pinch and coil techniques.

*How to teach:* Sculpt an animal using pinch and coil techniques; score and slip to join.

- **Q:** How do you make a coil?
  **A:** Roll clay back and forth into a long snake.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Finish
*Student:* Add details and let it dry; paint when dry.

*How to teach:* Add details; let dry; paint when dry.

- **Q:** Why let clay dry before painting?
  **A:** Paint sticks better and the clay won't smudge.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Listening quiz
*Student:* Identify 6 instruments by sound alone.

*How to teach:* Play 6 instrument sounds; your child names each.

- **Q:** A low, rumbling string sound is probably?
  **A:** Cello or double bass.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Sound story
*Student:* Create a short sound story using different instruments for characters.

*How to teach:* Create a short sound story using different instruments for characters.

- **Q:** Which instrument is your main character?
  **A:** Names instrument chosen.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 5 share
*Student:* Show clay animal and perform sound story.

*How to teach:* Show clay animal; perform sound story.

- **Q:** Why give each character a different instrument?
  **A:** So listeners can tell who is in the story by the sound.

**Assessment:** Unit 5 listening quiz and sound story.

**Answer key:** Listening quiz (6 instruments by sound): answers depend on clips played; record child's identifications against your key. Sound story: different instrument for each character, clear beginning-middle-end. Proficient = 5/6 + complete story.

## PE 1: Kicking a rolling ball and dribbling with feet

**Prepare:** Soccer ball, cones.

**Watch for:**
- Kicking a moving ball without stopping it first: trap, then kick, until timing improves.
- Kicking the ball far ahead while dribbling: use small, soft touches so it stays close.

### Day 1 (Monday): Trap and kick
*Student:* Stop a rolling ball with your foot, then kick.

*How to teach:* Roll the ball slowly; your child stops it with the sole or inside of the foot, then kicks.

- **Q:** How do you trap a rolling ball?
  **A:** Put the sole or inside of your foot on it to stop it.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Rolling kick
*Student:* Kick a ball rolled by a partner.

*How to teach:* Roll the ball slowly to your child, who kicks it without stopping it.

- **Q:** Where do you put your plant foot for a rolling ball?
  **A:** Beside the ball as it arrives.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Foot dribble
*Student:* Dribble with soft touches around cones.

*How to teach:* Dribble around cones with small, soft taps using both feet.

- **Q:** What are soft touches?
  **A:** Small, gentle taps so the ball stays close.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Mini game
*Student:* Play 5 minutes of 1-on-1 soccer with a family member using small cone goals and soft kicks.

*How to teach:* Play 5 minutes of gentle 1-on-1 soccer with cone goals.

- **Q:** How do you play fairly?
  **A:** No pushing, no hands, take turns starting.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Kick a ball rolled slowly toward you 5 times, aiming at a cone goal.

*How to teach:* Roll the ball slowly 5 times; your child kicks toward the cone goal.

- **Q:** What is dribbling?
  **A:** Moving the ball with small kicks while you walk or run.

**Assessment:** Rolling kick check.

**Answer key:** Rolling kick check (5 tries): moves to the ball, plants beside it, kicks with inside of foot toward cone goal. Proficient = 3+ solid contacts toward the goal; developing = 1-2; beginning = misses.

## Health 1: Growing and changing

**Prepare:** Your child's baby photos and current photos, a measuring tape, and paper for a timeline.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking growing only means getting taller: we also grow skills, teeth, and abilities.
- Comparing growth with others in a hurtful way: everyone grows at their own pace.
- Thinking growth stops at 6: you'll keep growing until you're grown up.

### Day 1 (Monday): Baby photos
*Student:* Compare baby photos and current photos.

*How to teach:* Compare baby photos and current photos.

- **Q:** How have you changed?
  **A:** Taller, more hair, more teeth, can walk and talk.
- **Q:** What stayed the same?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., eye color.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Measure
*Student:* Measure height and hand size and compare with family.

*How to teach:* Measure height and hand size; compare with family.

- **Q:** Who is tallest?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** Will your hand get bigger?
  **A:** Yes — as you grow.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Timeline
*Student:* Make a life timeline: baby, toddler, now, grown-up.

*How to teach:* Make a life timeline: baby, toddler, now, grown-up.

- **Q:** What could a baby do?
  **A:** Cry, drink milk, sleep.
- **Q:** What can a grown-up do?
  **A:** Drive, work, take care of others.

### Day 4 (Thursday): What I can do now
*Student:* List things you can do now that you couldn't as a baby.

*How to teach:* List things you can do now you couldn't as a baby.

- **Q:** Name 3.
  **A:** Walk, talk, read, ride a bike.
- **Q:** Name something you're still learning.
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain 3 ways you have grown and changed since you were a baby, using your timeline.

*How to teach:* Your child explains 3 ways they've grown and changed.

- **Q:** Name 3.
  **A:** Taller, can read, lost baby teeth.
- **Q:** What's next on your timeline?
  **A:** Answers vary.

**Assessment:** Life timeline.

**Answer key:** Timeline rubric: 4 = 4 stages in order (baby, toddler, now, grown-up) with drawings and 3 accurate growth changes explained; 3 = 2 changes; 2 = out of order; 1 = incomplete.
