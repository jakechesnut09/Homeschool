# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 24

## ELA 1: Writing, revising and sharing an opinion piece

**Prepare:** The completed OREO organizer, editing checklist (capitals, spaces, punctuation, spelling), paper for publishing, and a 24-word r-controlled list.

**Watch for:**
- Revising by recopying only: revising means adding or changing ideas; editing means fixing mistakes.
- Reasons without 'because': connect each reason to the opinion.
- Spelling r-controlled words like 'shurt': check spelling patterns on the word wall.

### Day 1 (Monday): Draft
*Student:* Write the opinion piece from the organizer: opinion sentence, two reasons with 'because', closing.

*How to teach:* Your child drafts from the organizer: opinion sentence, two reasons with 'because,' closing.

- **Q:** What's the first sentence of an opinion piece?
  **A:** The opinion, e.g., 'I think Mercy Watson is the best book.'
- **Q:** What word links a reason to an opinion?
  **A:** Because.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Revise
*Student:* Listen to a family member's question and add one more detail or reason.

*How to teach:* A family member asks a question about the piece; your child adds one detail or reason.

- **Q:** What is revising?
  **A:** Changing or adding ideas to make writing better.
- **Q:** Add a detail: 'The book is funny.'
  **A:** Example: The book is funny because the pig gets stuck in a hole.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Edit
*Student:* Use a checklist to fix capitals, end marks, commas and spelling of r-controlled words.

*How to teach:* Use the checklist to fix capitals, end marks, commas, and r-controlled spelling.

- **Q:** Fix: 'i think the girl is smart'
  **A:** I think the girl is smart.
- **Q:** Spell turn.
  **A:** t-u-r-n.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Publish
*Student:* Copy neatly and illustrate; read it aloud as a 'book review'.

*How to teach:* Copy neatly, illustrate, and read it aloud as a 'book review.'

- **Q:** What should a book review tell?
  **A:** The book's title, your opinion, and reasons.
- **Q:** What should you do when reading aloud?
  **A:** Speak clearly, look up at the audience.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* R-controlled vowel assessment (24 words) and opinion piece rubric score.

*How to teach:* Give the 24-word r-controlled assessment and score the opinion piece with the rubric.

- **Q:** Read: car, horn, bird, fur.
  **A:** car, horn, bird, fur.
- **Q:** Read: her, storm.
  **A:** her, storm.

**Assessment:** Opinion writing rubric and r-controlled vowel assessment.

**Answer key:** Opinion rubric: 4 = clear opinion, 2 reasons using 'because,' closing, correct capitals/end marks; 3 = opinion and 1–2 reasons with minor errors; 2 = opinion with no reasons; 1 = unclear. R-controlled (24): car, star, farm, park, shark, yard, for, corn, horn, storm, fork, born, her, fern, bird, girl, first, shirt, fur, turn, nurse, burn, hurt, perch — proficient 21+.

## Math 1: Strategies for adding within 100

**Prepare:** Base-ten blocks, paper for open number lines, and a strategy quiz (see key).

**Watch for:**
- Jumping by ones on an open number line for +30: show one big jump of 10 three times (47 → 57 → 67 → 77).
- Drawing ten-sticks and dots but miscounting the new ten: circle 10 dots and turn them into a stick.
- Writing an explanation that only restates the answer ('it is 63'): prompt with 'First I…, then I…'.

### Day 1 (Monday): Open number line
*Student:* Solve 47 + 30 and 56 + 8 with jumps on an open number line.

*How to teach:* Draw an open number line. For 47 + 30, mark 47 and draw three jumps of 10: 57, 67, 77. For 56 + 8, jump 4 to 60 then 4 more to 64.

- **Q:** 47 + 30 = ?
  **A:** 77.
- **Q:** 56 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 64.
- **Q:** 65 + 20 = ?
  **A:** 85.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Quick draws
*Student:* Use sticks and dots to solve 25 + 7 and 63 + 20.

*How to teach:* Use quick drawings: a line for a ten, a dot for a one. 25 + 7: 2 sticks 5 dots + 7 dots = 12 dots, circle 10 to make a stick: 3 sticks 2 dots = 32.

- **Q:** 25 + 7 = ?
  **A:** 32.
- **Q:** 63 + 20 = ?
  **A:** 83.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Story problems
*Student:* Solve 'A farm has 46 chickens and 8 ducks' problems.

*How to teach:* Read problems like 'A farm has 46 chickens and 8 ducks. How many birds?' Your child chooses a strategy and writes an equation.

- **Q:** 46 chickens and 8 ducks. How many birds?
  **A:** 54.
- **Q:** A farm has 35 cows; 40 more come. How many cows?
  **A:** 75.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Explain
*Student:* Write one sentence explaining how you added 59 + 4.

*How to teach:* Your child solves 59 + 4 and writes one sentence telling how: e.g., 'I added 1 to make 60 and then 3 more to get 63.'

- **Q:** 59 + 4 = ?
  **A:** 63.
- **Q:** Write a sentence for 28 + 5.
  **A:** 33; e.g., 'I added 2 to get 30, then 3 more is 33.'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Strategy quiz with written explanations.

*How to teach:* Give the strategy quiz; each problem needs an answer and a drawing or sentence.

- **Q:** 74 + 8 = ?
  **A:** 82.
- **Q:** 30 + 52 = ?
  **A:** 82.

**Assessment:** Strategy quiz with explanations.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested): 1) 36+40=76 2) 58+7=65 3) 44+5=49 4) 27+60=87 5) 69+3=72 6) 15+9=24. Explanation rubric: 4 = correct answer plus clear steps (make a ten, jumps, or tens/ones); 3 = correct with partial explanation; 2 = some errors but sensible strategy; 1 = no strategy shown.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Plant external parts and their jobs

**Prepare:** A celery stalk with leaves, blue food coloring, cups, a weed or flower to dissect, grass clump, rose stem or cactus photo, and tape.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking plants eat food through roots: roots take in water and nutrients; leaves use sunlight to make food.
- Thinking the stem is just a stick: it carries water up and holds the plant up.
- Touching thorns or spines carelessly: look closely, but touch gently or not at all.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plant dissection
*Student:* Take apart a weed or flower and tape roots, stem, leaves and flower onto paper with labels.

*How to teach:* Take apart a weed or flower; tape roots, stem, leaves, flower with labels.

- **Q:** What does the root do?
  **A:** Holds the plant in the soil and takes in water.
- **Q:** What do leaves do?
  **A:** Use sunlight to make food for the plant.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Celery lab
*Student:* Put a celery stalk in blue-colored water; check after 4 hours and next morning to see how the stem carries water.

*How to teach:* Put celery in blue water; check after 4 hours and next morning.

- **Q:** What happened to the celery?
  **A:** Blue color moved up the stem into the leaves.
- **Q:** What does this show?
  **A:** The stem carries water up to the leaves.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Root pull
*Student:* Pull up a weed and a grass clump; compare roots and explain how roots hold plants and take in water.

*How to teach:* Pull up a weed and grass; compare roots.

- **Q:** Why are weeds hard to pull up?
  **A:** Their roots grip the soil.
- **Q:** Which had more spread-out roots?
  **A:** Grass usually has many thin, spread roots.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Plant protection
*Student:* Look closely (safely) at rose thorns or cactus spines and discuss how they protect the plant from animals.

*How to teach:* Observe thorns/spines safely; discuss protection.

- **Q:** Why do roses have thorns?
  **A:** To protect them from animals eating them.
- **Q:** Cactus spines?
  **A:** Protect from animals and help shade the plant.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Plant-parts quiz: label a diagram and match each part to its job.

*How to teach:* Plant-parts quiz.

- **Q:** Which part carries water?
  **A:** The stem.
- **Q:** Which makes seeds?
  **A:** The flower.

**Assessment:** Plant-parts diagram quiz.

**Answer key:** Diagram labels: roots (take in water, hold plant), stem (carries water, holds up), leaves (make food from sunlight), flower (makes seeds). Matching: root–water/anchor; stem–transport/support; leaf–make food; flower–seeds; thorns–protection. Proficient: all correct.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: My family's story and our diverse community

**Prepare:** Family photos or drawings, a world map, yarn and tape, and paper for drawing a tradition.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking everyone's family looks the same: families come in many shapes and sizes.
- Thinking a 'diverse community' means only different languages: it includes foods, traditions, religions, abilities, and ideas.
- Sharing family information that's private: share only what your family is comfortable with.

### Day 1 (Monday): Family tree
*Student:* Make a simple family tree with photos or drawings.

*How to teach:* Make a simple family tree with photos or drawings.

- **Q:** What goes at the bottom (or center) of your family tree?
  **A:** You.
- **Q:** Who goes above you?
  **A:** Parents, then grandparents.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Where are we from?
*Student:* Find places your family came from on a world map and connect them with yarn to your home.

*How to teach:* Find where your family came from on a world map; connect with yarn.

- **Q:** Where did your family come from?
  **A:** Answers vary.
- **Q:** What does the yarn show?
  **A:** The journey from that place to where we live now.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Traditions
*Student:* Share a family tradition (food, holiday, song) and draw it.

*How to teach:* Share and draw a family tradition.

- **Q:** Name a family tradition.
  **A:** Examples: a holiday meal, a special song, a birthday custom.
- **Q:** Why are traditions important?
  **A:** They connect us to family and culture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Community diversity
*Student:* Visit the library or a farmers' market and note languages, foods and cultures you see.

*How to teach:* Visit the library or a farmers' market; note languages, foods, cultures.

- **Q:** What did you notice?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., people speaking Spanish, Vietnamese; tamales, dumplings.
- **Q:** What does diverse mean?
  **A:** Having many different kinds of people and cultures.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one way your community is diverse and one value people share.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one way the community is diverse and one shared value.

- **Q:** Example diverse way.
  **A:** People speak many languages.
- **Q:** Example shared value.
  **A:** Everyone cares about family and being treated fairly.

**Assessment:** Family tree and oral explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = family tree with at least 3 generations (or the family's chosen structure), clear example of community diversity, and a shared value; 3 = mostly complete; 2 = missing explanation; 1 = incomplete.

## Art & Music 1: Art of Japan; music of Japan

**Prepare:** Image of Hokusai's 'The Great Wave off Kanagawa,' blue paint, white and black; koto music recording; 'Sakura' recording.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'The Great Wave' is a painting: it is a woodblock print.
- Thinking the koto is a guitar: it is a long Japanese zither played lying flat.

### Day 1 (Monday): Great Wave
*Student:* Look at Hokusai's 'The Great Wave' and describe lines and colors.

*How to teach:* Look at Hokusai's 'The Great Wave'; describe curling lines, white foam, blue colors, boats, Mount Fuji.

- **Q:** What mountain is in the background?
  **A:** Mount Fuji.
- **Q:** How was it made?
  **A:** As a woodblock print in Japan.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Wave painting
*Student:* Paint your own wave with blue tints and shades.

*How to teach:* Paint a wave using blue tints and shades.

- **Q:** How do you make light blue?
  **A:** Add white (a tint).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Koto
*Student:* Listen to koto music and describe its sound.

*How to teach:* Listen to koto music; describe its sound.

- **Q:** How is the koto played?
  **A:** By plucking its strings while it lies flat.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Song
*Student:* Learn 'Sakura' and describe the melody.

*How to teach:* Learn 'Sakura.'

- **Q:** What is 'Sakura' about?
  **A:** Cherry blossoms.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain why museums keep 'The Great Wave' safe.

*How to teach:* Explain why museums keep 'The Great Wave' safe.

- **Q:** Why keep it safe?
  **A:** It's old paper; light and handling can fade and damage it, and it's important to people.

**Assessment:** Wave painting and listening response.

**Answer key:** Wave painting uses blue tints and shades. Listening response: koto described as plucked strings, gentle/twangy; 'Sakura' melody slow, calm, about cherry blossoms. Proficient = 3+ blues and accurate descriptions.

## PE 1: Hand dribbling

**Prepare:** Playground ball or small basketball; chalk lines.

**Watch for:**
- Slapping the ball with a flat palm: push with fingertips.
- Bouncing too high: keep the ball about waist high.

### Day 1 (Monday): Bounce and catch
*Student:* Bounce and catch a ball with two hands.

*How to teach:* Bounce and catch with two hands 10 times.

- **Q:** How high should the ball bounce?
  **A:** About waist high.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): One-hand dribble
*Student:* Dribble with fingertip pushes, stationary.

*How to teach:* Dribble in place with one hand using fingertip pushes.

- **Q:** What part of the hand do you dribble with?
  **A:** Fingertips (finger pads), not a slap of the palm.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Walk and dribble
*Student:* Dribble while walking along a straight chalk line, then a curved one.

*How to teach:* Dribble while walking along a straight chalk line, then a curved line.

- **Q:** Where should the ball be as you walk?
  **A:** Out in front and to the side of your dribbling hand.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Dribble tag
*Student:* Play dribble tag: everyone dribbles while trying to tag others without losing their ball.

*How to teach:* Play dribble tag: everyone dribbles while trying to tag others.

- **Q:** Why try to look up while dribbling?
  **A:** To see where you're going and avoid others.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 6 check
*Student:* Skill check of kick, strike and dribble.

*How to teach:* Check kick, strike and dribble skills.

- **Q:** Name one dribbling cue.
  **A:** Push with fingertips / waist high / eyes up.

**Assessment:** Unit 6 skill check.

**Answer key:** Unit 6 skill check: Kick - plant foot beside, inside of foot; Strike - flat paddle, controlled taps; Dribble - fingertip push, waist-high, eyes up when possible. Proficient = 2 cues on each of the three skills.

## Health 1: Pedestrian and car safety

**Prepare:** Chalk to draw a crosswalk, a booster seat, and a walk route with crosswalks and a signal.

**Watch for:**
- Looking only one way before crossing: look left, right, then left again.
- Crossing between parked cars: drivers can't see you; cross at corners and crosswalks.
- Thinking a seat belt alone is safe for a 6-year-old: use a booster seat in the back until the belt fits (usually about 4'9").

### Day 1 (Monday): Crossing streets
*Student:* Practice stop, look left-right-left, listen, and hold an adult's hand.

*How to teach:* Practice stop, look left-right-left, listen, and hold an adult's hand.

- **Q:** What do you do at the curb?
  **A:** Stop.
- **Q:** Which way do you look?
  **A:** Left, right, left.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Traffic signs
*Student:* Learn walk/don't walk signals and stop signs.

*How to teach:* Learn walk/don't walk signals and stop signs.

- **Q:** What does the walking person mean?
  **A:** You may cross (still look).
- **Q:** What does the flashing hand mean?
  **A:** Don't start crossing.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Car safety
*Student:* Practice buckling into a booster seat correctly.

*How to teach:* Practice buckling into a booster seat.

- **Q:** Where should the lap belt go?
  **A:** Low across the hips/upper thighs, not the tummy.
- **Q:** Where should the shoulder belt go?
  **A:** Across the chest and shoulder, not the neck.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Neighborhood walk
*Student:* Walk with an adult and use crosswalks.

*How to teach:* Walk with an adult and use crosswalks.

- **Q:** Where should you cross?
  **A:** At crosswalks or corners.
- **Q:** Who holds hands?
  **A:** You and the adult.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Demonstrate safe crossing steps.

*How to teach:* Your child demonstrates safe crossing.

- **Q:** Steps?
  **A:** Stop at curb, look left-right-left, listen, cross with an adult, keep looking.
- **Q:** Why listen?
  **A:** To hear cars you can't see.

**Assessment:** Crossing demonstration.

**Answer key:** Demonstration checklist: stops at curb; looks left, right, left; listens; holds adult's hand; crosses at crosswalk; walks (doesn't run) and keeps looking. Proficient 6/6.
