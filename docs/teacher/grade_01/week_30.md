# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 30

## ELA 1: Diphthongs oi/oy; Little Bear and context clues

**Prepare:** 'Little Bear' by Else Holmelund Minarik (library), a short passage with tricky words for self-correcting, and an oi/oy list (coin, soil, boil, boy, toy, joy, point, enjoy).

**Watch for:**
- Spelling boy as 'boi': oy usually ends words; oi is in the middle.
- Reading on after a mistake that doesn't make sense: stop and ask 'Does that make sense?'
- Guessing unknown words without using the sentence: reread and look for clues around the word.

### Day 1 (Monday): oi and oy
*Student:* Read coin, soil, boil, boy, toy, joy; write a sentence with two of them.

*How to teach:* Read coin, soil, boil, boy, toy, joy. Your child writes a sentence with two.

- **Q:** Where does oy usually go?
  **A:** At the end of a word.
- **Q:** Write a sentence with two oi/oy words.
  **A:** Example: The boy found a coin.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Little Bear
*Student:* Read 'What Will Little Bear Wear?'; describe how Little Bear solves his problem.

*How to teach:* Read 'What Will Little Bear Wear?' Describe how Little Bear solves his problem.

- **Q:** What is Little Bear's problem?
  **A:** He is cold in the snow.
- **Q:** What does Mother Bear make for him?
  **A:** A hat, a coat, and snow pants.
- **Q:** How is it solved?
  **A:** Mother Bear reminds him he has his own fur coat; he takes off the other clothes and isn't cold.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Self-correct
*Student:* Practice 'Does that make sense?' rereading with a passage that has tricky words.

*How to teach:* Practice 'Does that make sense?' with a passage. When your child misreads, pause and ask; they reread.

- **Q:** What do you do if a word doesn't make sense?
  **A:** Stop, go back, and reread; check the letters and the meaning.
- **Q:** 'The horse ran in the house'—does that make sense?
  **A:** No, likely 'horse ran in the field.' Reread.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Context clues
*Student:* Use the sentence to figure out 5 unknown words in a short passage and explain the clue.

*How to teach:* Use the sentence to figure out 5 unknown words and explain each clue.

- **Q:** 'The tiny kitten was so minute it fit in a cup.' What does minute mean?
  **A:** Very small; the clues are tiny and fit in a cup.
- **Q:** Where do you look for clues?
  **A:** In the words and sentences around the unknown word, and pictures.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* oi/oy word read; running-record style reading with self-corrections noted.

*How to teach:* Read the oi/oy list and do a running record on a passage, noting self-corrections.

- **Q:** Read: point, enjoy.
  **A:** point, enjoy.
- **Q:** Read: soil, toy.
  **A:** soil, toy.

**Assessment:** Running record with accuracy and self-corrections.

**Answer key:** Running record: Accuracy = (words − errors) ÷ words. 95–100% independent level; 90–94% instructional; below 90% too hard. Self-correction ratio: count self-corrections (SC); a ratio of about 1:3 to 1:5 shows good monitoring. Proficient: 90%+ with some self-corrections.

## Math 1: Telling time to the hour and half hour

**Prepare:** Paper plate, brass fastener, construction paper hands (short hour hand, long minute hand), markers, a real analog clock, and 10 clock pictures for Friday.

**Watch for:**
- Mixing up the hour and minute hands: the short hand tells the hour; the long hand tells minutes.
- At half past, reading the hour as the next number (2:30 read as 3:30) because the hour hand is near 3: the hour hand is between 2 and 3, so it's still 2-something.
- Writing 3:00 as 3:0 or 30:0: show digital clocks — hours, colon, two digits for minutes.

### Day 1 (Monday): Make a clock
*Student:* Make a paper-plate clock with hour and minute hands.

*How to teach:* Write 1–12 around a paper plate (12, 3, 6, 9 first so spacing is even). Attach a short and a long hand with a fastener. Your child shows 'clock face parts' and names each hand.

- **Q:** Which hand is shorter?
  **A:** The hour hand.
- **Q:** What number is at the top of a clock?
  **A:** 12.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): O'clock
*Student:* Show and write times on the hour (3:00).

*How to teach:* Show times on the hour: long hand on 12, short hand on the hour. Your child shows and writes 3:00, 7:00, 12:00.

- **Q:** The short hand is on 8 and the long hand on 12. What time?
  **A:** 8:00.
- **Q:** Show 5:00. Where are the hands?
  **A:** Short hand on 5, long hand on 12.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Half past
*Student:* Show half-past times and explain where the hour hand is.

*How to teach:* Move the long hand to 6 and show that the hour hand slid halfway between two numbers. Read it as 'half past' or ':30'.

- **Q:** Long hand on 6, short hand halfway between 4 and 5. Time?
  **A:** 4:30, half past four.
- **Q:** Where is the hour hand at 9:30?
  **A:** Halfway between 9 and 10.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Daily schedule
*Student:* Make a picture schedule of your day with clock times.

*How to teach:* Your child draws 4–5 parts of their day (breakfast, lessons, lunch, play, bed) with a clock time for each, on the hour or half hour.

- **Q:** If lunch is at 12:30, draw the hands.
  **A:** Long hand on 6, short hand halfway between 12 and 1.
- **Q:** Which comes first: 8:00 or 7:30?
  **A:** 7:30.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Read 10 clocks and write the time.

*How to teach:* Your child reads 10 clock pictures and writes each time.

- **Q:** Hands: short on 2, long on 12.
  **A:** 2:00.
- **Q:** Short between 11 and 12, long on 6.
  **A:** 11:30.

**Assessment:** Clock reading quiz.

**Answer key:** Quiz (suggested 10 clocks): 1:00, 3:00, 6:00, 9:00, 12:00, 2:30, 5:30, 7:30, 10:30, 12:30 — your child should write each as shown. Proficient 9–10; watch especially for half-past times read one hour too late.

## Science 1 (NGSS): How young animals help themselves survive

**Prepare:** Recordings of baby birds chirping and kittens mewing (PBS or library), 'Make Way for Ducklings' by Robert McCloskey, animal images (bear cubs, chimpanzees).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking babies cry or call for no reason: calls tell parents what they need.
- Thinking babies survive alone: many need parents' help and signals.
- Thinking learning by copying is only for people: many animals learn by watching parents.

### Day 1 (Monday): Calling for help
*Student:* Listen to recordings (PBS or library) of baby birds chirping and kittens mewing; tell what each baby is asking for.

*How to teach:* Listen to baby bird chirps and kitten mews; tell what each asks for.

- **Q:** What do baby birds chirping usually ask for?
  **A:** Food.
- **Q:** What might a kitten's mew mean?
  **A:** It's hungry, cold, or lost and wants its mother.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Follow the leader
*Student:* Read 'Make Way for Ducklings' and explain why the ducklings stay in a line behind their mother.

*How to teach:* Read 'Make Way for Ducklings'; explain why the ducklings stay in line.

- **Q:** Why do ducklings follow in a line?
  **A:** To stay close to their mother and be safe.
- **Q:** Who helped them cross the street?
  **A:** Michael the policeman and other police.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Learning by copying
*Student:* Read about bear cubs and young chimpanzees learning to find food by watching parents.

*How to teach:* Read about bear cubs and young chimps learning by watching parents.

- **Q:** How do bear cubs learn to find food?
  **A:** By watching their mother catch fish or dig for food.
- **Q:** How do chimpanzees learn to use tools?
  **A:** By watching adults use sticks to get termites.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Interview
*Student:* Interview a parent about how you signaled your needs as a baby and how they responded; record 3 answers.

*How to teach:* Interview a parent about how your child signaled needs as a baby; record 3 answers.

- **Q:** How did you signal you were hungry as a baby?
  **A:** Cried (answers vary).
- **Q:** How is that like a baby bird?
  **A:** Both call out so a parent will feed them.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain one offspring behavior and how it helps the baby survive, in two sentences.

*How to teach:* Your child explains one offspring behavior and how it helps survival.

- **Q:** Example.
  **A:** Baby birds chirp loudly. This tells parents they're hungry, so they get fed.
- **Q:** Another example.
  **A:** Ducklings follow their mother. This keeps them safe from danger.

**Assessment:** Written offspring-behavior explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = names a behavior (calling, following, copying) and explains how it helps the baby survive in 2 clear sentences; 3 = behavior plus partial reason; 2 = behavior only; 1 = incorrect.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Needs, wants, goods and services

**Prepare:** Magazine or printed pictures (food, house, coat, toys, candy, haircut, doctor, bread, shoes, car repair), sorting mats, and 'A Chair for My Mother' by Vera B. Williams (library).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking wants are bad: wants are fine; needs come first.
- Mixing up goods and services: goods are things you can touch; services are work done for you.
- Thinking a need is anything you really want: needs are things required to live (food, water, shelter, clothing).

### Day 1 (Monday): Needs and wants
*Student:* Sort pictures into needs (food, shelter) and wants (toys).

*How to teach:* Sort pictures into needs and wants.

- **Q:** Is a coat a need or want?
  **A:** A need (clothing for warmth).
- **Q:** Is a video game?
  **A:** A want.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Goods and services
*Student:* Sort pictures into goods (bread, shoes) and services (haircut, doctor visit).

*How to teach:* Sort into goods and services.

- **Q:** Is a haircut a good or service?
  **A:** A service.
- **Q:** Is bread?
  **A:** A good.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): At home
*Student:* List goods and services your family used this week.

*How to teach:* List goods and services your family used this week.

- **Q:** Name a good.
  **A:** Milk, shoes, books.
- **Q:** Name a service.
  **A:** Mail delivery, doctor visit, trash pickup.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Read
*Student:* Read 'A Chair for My Mother' by Vera B. Williams and discuss saving for a want.

*How to teach:* Read 'A Chair for My Mother'; discuss saving.

- **Q:** Why did the family need a new chair?
  **A:** A fire burned their home and furniture.
- **Q:** How did they save?
  **A:** They put coins in a big jar until it was full.
- **Q:** Was the chair a need or want?
  **A:** Mostly a want — but it gave the tired mother a comfortable place to rest.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Goods/services and needs/wants sort quiz.

*How to teach:* Give the sort quiz.

- **Q:** Water: need or want?
  **A:** Need.
- **Q:** A doctor visit: good or service?
  **A:** Service.

**Assessment:** Sort quiz.

**Answer key:** Sort key (suggested): Needs — food, water, house, coat; Wants — toy, candy, video game, bike. Goods — bread, shoes, toy, apple; Services — haircut, doctor visit, car repair, teaching. Proficient 15–16 of 16.

## Art & Music 1: Georgia O'Keeffe flowers; Vivaldi's Spring

**Prepare:** A fresh flower, magnifier; image of a Georgia O'Keeffe flower painting; Vivaldi's 'Spring' from The Four Seasons.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking big art must be of big things: O'Keeffe painted small flowers huge so people would notice.
- Drawing the flower small in the middle: fill the page.

### Day 1 (Monday): O'Keeffe
*Student:* Look at an O'Keeffe flower painting and notice its size.

*How to teach:* Look at an O'Keeffe flower painting; notice how large it is.

- **Q:** Why did O'Keeffe paint flowers so big?
  **A:** So people would stop and really look at them.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Close-up
*Student:* Observe a flower with a magnifier and draw it large to fill the page.

*How to teach:* Observe a flower with a magnifier; draw it large to fill the page.

- **Q:** What details did you notice?
  **A:** Veins, center parts, colors.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Spring
*Student:* Listen to Vivaldi's 'Spring' and identify bird sounds and storm parts.

*How to teach:* Listen to Vivaldi's 'Spring'; identify bird sounds and the storm.

- **Q:** Which instrument makes the bird sounds?
  **A:** Violins.
- **Q:** How does the storm part sound?
  **A:** Fast, loud, rumbling notes.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Compare
*Student:* Compare how O'Keeffe and Vivaldi show nature.

*How to teach:* Compare how O'Keeffe and Vivaldi show nature.

- **Q:** How are they alike?
  **A:** Both show nature: O'Keeffe with paint, Vivaldi with sound.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Explain your flower drawing choices.

*How to teach:* Explain your flower drawing choices.

- **Q:** What is a close-up?
  **A:** A view very near the subject so it looks big.

**Assessment:** Close-up flower drawing.

**Answer key:** Close-up flower drawing: fills the page, shows petal details seen through magnifier. Proficient = drawing fills page + child explains choices (size, color, details).

## PE 1: Partner cooperation

**Prepare:** Bath towel, ball, balloons.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking cooperation means doing all the work yourself: partners share the job.
- Pulling unevenly on the towel: count '1-2-3, toss!' together.

### Day 1 (Monday): Towel toss
*Student:* Toss a ball with a towel held by two people.

*How to teach:* Two people hold a towel and toss a ball up and catch it together.

- **Q:** How did you and your partner toss together?
  **A:** Counted together and lifted at the same time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Balloon partners
*Student:* Keep a balloon up with a partner, alternating taps; count your team record.

*How to teach:* Keep a balloon up with a partner, alternating taps; count your record.

- **Q:** Why take turns tapping?
  **A:** So both partners help and it's fair.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Wheelbarrow
*Student:* Wheelbarrow walk 10 feet with a partner holding your ankles, then switch.

*How to teach:* Wheelbarrow walk 10 feet: partner holds ankles (or knees for easier), then switch.

- **Q:** What does the person holding the legs need to do?
  **A:** Go slowly and listen to the walker.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Encourage
*Student:* Practice encouraging words during games.

*How to teach:* Practice encouraging words during a simple game.

- **Q:** Give an encouraging phrase.
  **A:** 'Nice try!' 'You can do it!' 'Great job!'

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Partner challenge with cooperation.

*How to teach:* Do a partner challenge (e.g., towel toss 10 in a row).

- **Q:** What made your team successful?
  **A:** Talking, taking turns, encouraging each other.

**Assessment:** Partner cooperation check.

**Answer key:** Cooperation check: communicates with partner, takes turns, uses encouraging words, and keeps trying after mistakes. Proficient = all four.

## Health 1: Medicines: help or harm

**Prepare:** Empty medicine bottles (rinsed, caps on), pictures of look-alike candy and pills (e.g., candy-coated chocolates vs. pills), and a medicine label. Keep real medicine locked away.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking medicine is candy: some medicines look and taste like candy, but too much can make you very sick.
- Thinking you can take medicine on your own if you feel sick: only a trusted adult gives medicine.
- Thinking more medicine works faster: the right dose matters.

### Day 1 (Monday): What medicine does
*Student:* Learn medicines can help when sick but only trusted adults give them.

*How to teach:* Learn medicines can help when sick but only trusted adults give them.

- **Q:** Who can give you medicine?
  **A:** A parent or trusted adult (or a nurse/doctor).
- **Q:** Can medicine be harmful?
  **A:** Yes, if you take the wrong kind or too much.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Look-alikes
*Student:* Compare pictures of candy and pills that look alike.

*How to teach:* Compare pictures of look-alike candy and pills.

- **Q:** Can you always tell candy and pills apart?
  **A:** No — they can look alike.
- **Q:** What should you do if you find a pill?
  **A:** Don't touch it; tell an adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Labels
*Student:* Look at a medicine label with an adult.

*How to teach:* Look at a medicine label with an adult.

- **Q:** What does the label tell?
  **A:** How much to take, how often, and warnings.
- **Q:** Who reads the label?
  **A:** An adult.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Role-play
*Student:* Practice saying 'I need to ask my mom/dad first.'

*How to teach:* Practice saying 'I need to ask my mom/dad first.'

- **Q:** A friend offers you a vitamin gummy. What do you say?
  **A:** No thanks, I need to ask my mom/dad first.
- **Q:** Why?
  **A:** Only trusted adults give medicine or vitamins.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain who can give medicine.

*How to teach:* Your child explains who can give medicine.

- **Q:** Who?
  **A:** A parent, trusted adult, nurse, or doctor.
- **Q:** Why not take it yourself?
  **A:** You could take the wrong one or too much.

**Assessment:** Medicine safety explanation.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = explains that only a trusted adult gives medicine, that medicine can look like candy, and that you never take it on your own; 3 = 2 points; 2 = 1; 1 = incorrect.
