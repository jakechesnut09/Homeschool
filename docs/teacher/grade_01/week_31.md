# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 31

## ELA 1: Other vowel sounds (oo, au, aw); comparing classic tales

**Prepare:** 'Make Way for Ducklings' by Robert McCloskey and a picture-book version of Andersen's 'The Ugly Duckling' (library), a comparison chart, and a word list (moon, food, spoon, book, look, foot, saw, paw, draw, haul, launch).

**Watch for:**
- Reading oo in book like moon: oo has two sounds — try both and pick the real word.
- Saying the central message is 'he became a swan': the message is about not judging by looks or being true to yourself.
- Using 'a' before a vowel sound (a owl): use 'an' before vowel sounds (an owl).

### Day 1 (Monday): oo two ways
*Student:* Sort moon, food, spoon vs. book, look, foot; read both lists.

*How to teach:* Sort moon, food, spoon vs. book, look, foot. Read both lists.

- **Q:** Which sound does oo make in foot?
  **A:** The short /oo/ as in book.
- **Q:** In spoon?
  **A:** The long /oo/ as in moon.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): au and aw
*Student:* Read saw, paw, draw, haul, launch; illustrate three words.

*How to teach:* Read saw, paw, draw, haul, launch. Illustrate three.

- **Q:** What sound do au and aw make?
  **A:** /aw/ as in saw.
- **Q:** Read: haul, launch.
  **A:** haul, launch.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Two duck stories
*Student:* Read 'Make Way for Ducklings' and 'The Ugly Duckling'; compare the ducks' journeys on a chart.

*How to teach:* Read both stories. Compare the ducks' journeys on a chart.

- **Q:** Where did Mr. and Mrs. Mallard decide to live?
  **A:** On an island in the Charles River in Boston, and later the island in the Public Garden.
- **Q:** Who stopped traffic for the ducklings?
  **A:** Michael the policeman (with other police officers).
- **Q:** What happened to the ugly duckling?
  **A:** He was teased, left home, suffered a hard winter, and in spring found he had grown into a beautiful swan.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Determiners
*Student:* Choose a/an/the and this/that/these/those to complete 8 sentences about the stories.

*How to teach:* Your child fills 8 sentences with a/an/the and this/that/these/those.

- **Q:** ___ ugly duckling (a/an).
  **A:** an.
- **Q:** ___ ducklings over here (these/those).
  **A:** these.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Vowel-sound word list read; state the central message of 'The Ugly Duckling'.

*How to teach:* Read the vowel-sound list and state the central message of 'The Ugly Duckling.'

- **Q:** What is the central message?
  **A:** Don't judge others by how they look; everyone can grow into who they truly are.
- **Q:** Read: draw, book.
  **A:** draw, book.

**Assessment:** Comparison chart and central-message sentence.

**Answer key:** Chart: Make Way for Ducklings — Mallards search for a safe home in Boston, ducklings hatch, family walks across busy streets with police help, ends safely in the Public Garden. Ugly Duckling — hatched different, teased, lonely journey through winter, ends as a swan accepted by swans. Central message proficient: 'It's what's inside that matters / don't judge by appearance.'

## Math 1: Coins and their values

**Prepare:** Real pennies, nickels, dimes, and quarters; a magnifying glass; a coin chart sheet; play-store toys with price tags under 50¢; a Unit 9 test.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking bigger coins are worth more (nickel more than dime): the dime is smallest but worth 10¢.
- Counting all coins by ones: count dimes by tens first, then pennies by ones.
- Mixing up penny and nickel names: penny is copper-colored, 1¢; nickel is silver, bigger, 5¢.

### Day 1 (Monday): Coin features
*Student:* Study real coins with a magnifier; sort and name them.

*How to teach:* Let your child examine each coin with a magnifier: color, size, edge (dimes and quarters have ridges), and the pictures. Sort into four piles and name them.

- **Q:** Which coin is copper colored?
  **A:** The penny.
- **Q:** Which coin is the smallest?
  **A:** The dime.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Values
*Student:* Learn 1¢, 5¢, 10¢, 25¢; make a coin chart.

*How to teach:* Make a coin chart: penny 1¢, nickel 5¢, dime 10¢, quarter 25¢. Tape or draw each coin next to its value.

- **Q:** How much is a nickel worth?
  **A:** 5¢.
- **Q:** Which is worth more, a dime or a nickel?
  **A:** A dime (10¢ vs 5¢).

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count dimes and pennies
*Student:* Count dimes then pennies (connects to tens and ones).

*How to teach:* Lay out 3 dimes and 4 pennies. Count dimes by tens (10, 20, 30) then pennies by ones (31, 32, 33, 34): 34¢. Connect to 3 tens and 4 ones.

- **Q:** 4 dimes and 2 pennies?
  **A:** 42¢.
- **Q:** 6 dimes and 0 pennies?
  **A:** 60¢.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Play store
*Student:* Price toys under 50¢ and pay with coins.

*How to teach:* Price toys under 50¢ with tags. Your child 'buys' them by counting out dimes and pennies (and nickels if ready).

- **Q:** A toy costs 23¢. Which dimes and pennies pay exactly?
  **A:** 2 dimes and 3 pennies.
- **Q:** You buy a 15¢ toy. Pay with a dime and what?
  **A:** A nickel (or 5 pennies).

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 9 check
*Student:* Time and money assessment: read and write 6 hour/half-hour times and name and value 8 coins.

*How to teach:* Give the Unit 9 test: read and write 6 clock times, and name and value 8 coins.

- **Q:** Name and value of the silver coin with ridges, biggest of the 4.
  **A:** Quarter, 25¢.
- **Q:** Short hand between 6 and 7, long hand on 6.
  **A:** 6:30.

**Assessment:** Unit 9 assessment.

**Answer key:** Unit 9 test (suggested): Times: 4:00, 8:00, 11:00, 1:30, 3:30, 9:30. Coins (show real coins): penny 1¢, nickel 5¢, dime 10¢, quarter 25¢, penny 1¢, dime 10¢, nickel 5¢, quarter 25¢. Proficient: 13–14 of 14 correct.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Patterns in parent and offspring behavior

**Prepare:** Chart paper, library animal books (e.g., robins, bears, penguins, elephants, ducks, kangaroos), and a stapled 4-page mini-book.

**Watch for:**
- Seeing patterns as one animal's behavior: a pattern repeats across many animals.
- Writing mini-book pages with opinions: each page needs a fact.
- Thinking all babies stay with parents the same time: it varies by animal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Pattern chart
*Student:* Make a chart of 6 animals with columns for 'what parents do' and 'what babies do'.

*How to teach:* Make a chart: 6 animals, 'what parents do' and 'what babies do.'

- **Q:** What do robin parents do?
  **A:** Build a nest, sit on eggs, bring worms.
- **Q:** What do robin babies do?
  **A:** Open mouths and chirp to be fed.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Find patterns
*Student:* Circle behaviors that appear for many animals (feeding, protecting, calling) and state one pattern.

*How to teach:* Circle behaviors that repeat; state a pattern.

- **Q:** State a pattern.
  **A:** Many parents feed and protect their babies; many babies call when they need help.
- **Q:** Name a behavior shared by many animals.
  **A:** Feeding.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Mini-book draft
*Student:* Write a 4-page mini-book about one animal family (e.g., robins) with a fact on each page.

*How to teach:* Write a 4-page mini-book about one animal family.

- **Q:** Example robin fact.
  **A:** Robins lay blue eggs in a nest.
- **Q:** What goes on each page?
  **A:** One fact and a picture.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Share
*Student:* Read your mini-book aloud and answer two questions from a listener.

*How to teach:* Read the mini-book aloud; answer questions.

- **Q:** Why answer questions?
  **A:** To share knowledge and check understanding.
- **Q:** How do you answer?
  **A:** In a complete sentence.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 check
*Student:* Assessment: read a short passage about a new animal and identify parent and offspring behaviors.

*How to teach:* Give the unit assessment with a new animal passage.

- **Q:** Example passage: Mother elephants protect their calves. Calves hold mothers' tails. Parent behavior?
  **A:** Protecting.
- **Q:** Offspring behavior?
  **A:** Holding the mother's tail to stay close.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 assessment and mini-book.

**Answer key:** Unit 7 test: read a short passage (e.g., 'Sea lion mothers call to find their pups on the beach. Pups bark back so mom can find them. Mothers feed pups milk.') Parent behaviors: calling, feeding milk. Offspring behaviors: barking back to be found. Proficient: names both correctly with text evidence. Mini-book: 4 accurate facts with pictures.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Exchange and money

**Prepare:** Coins (pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters), play store items, price tags under $1, a store flyer, and toys/snacks to trade.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking trading is always easy: you must find someone who wants what you have and has what you want.
- Thinking bigger coins are worth more: a dime (smaller) is worth more than a nickel.
- Thinking money has always been paper and coins: long ago people used shells, beads, and metal.

### Day 1 (Monday): Barter
*Student:* Trade toys or snacks with a family member; talk about problems with trading.

*How to teach:* Trade toys or snacks; talk about problems with trading.

- **Q:** What's hard about trading?
  **A:** The other person must want what you have.
- **Q:** What is bartering?
  **A:** Trading goods or services without money.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Money history
*Student:* Learn why money makes trading easier; look at historical coins (Smithsonian).

*How to teach:* Learn why money makes trading easier; look at historical coins.

- **Q:** Why is money easier than trading?
  **A:** Everyone accepts it, so you can buy what you need without finding a perfect trade.
- **Q:** What did people use before coins?
  **A:** Shells, beads, salt, metal pieces.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Play store
*Student:* Run a pretend store with price tags under $1 and pay with coins.

*How to teach:* Run a pretend store with price tags under $1; pay with coins.

- **Q:** A toy costs 35¢. Which coins could pay?
  **A:** A quarter and a dime (or 3 dimes and a nickel).
- **Q:** Pay 60¢.
  **A:** 2 quarters and a dime.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Prices
*Student:* Compare prices of 3 snacks at a store or online flyer and decide which you could buy with 50 cents.

*How to teach:* Compare prices of 3 snacks; decide what to buy with 50¢.

- **Q:** Snacks cost 25¢, 40¢, 60¢. Which can you buy with 50¢?
  **A:** 25¢ or 40¢ (not 60¢); you could buy only one of them.
- **Q:** Can you buy two 25¢ snacks?
  **A:** Yes — 25 + 25 = 50¢.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Explain in two sentences why people use money instead of trading, using your barter game as an example.

*How to teach:* Your child explains why people use money instead of trading.

- **Q:** Explain.
  **A:** Money is easier because everyone takes it. In our trading game, I couldn't trade because my sister didn't want my toy.
- **Q:** What is money for?
  **A:** Buying goods and services.

**Assessment:** Oral explanation and store role-play.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = two-sentence explanation that money is accepted by everyone so you don't need a matching trade, with barter-game example; correct coin payments in store role-play; 3 = explanation correct, some coin errors; 2 = partial; 1 = incorrect.

## Art & Music 1: Nature story in art and music

**Prepare:** Paper (4 panels), crayons, instruments.

**Watch for:**
- Ignoring feedback: one suggestion can make work better.
- Using the same sound for every panel: change tempo and dynamics to match the mood.

### Day 1 (Monday): Plan
*Student:* Plan a 4-panel nature story (storm, sun, birds, night).

*How to teach:* Plan a 4-panel nature story: storm, sun, birds, night.

- **Q:** What happens in each panel?
  **A:** Describes the 4 panels in order.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Illustrate
*Student:* Draw the 4 panels with crayons, using warm or cool colors to match each part's mood.

*How to teach:* Draw the panels using warm or cool colors to match mood.

- **Q:** What colors suit the night panel?
  **A:** Cool colors like blue and purple.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Add sound
*Student:* Add instruments and dynamics/tempo for each panel.

*How to teach:* Add instruments, dynamics and tempo for each panel.

- **Q:** How should the storm sound?
  **A:** Loud and fast.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Revise
*Student:* Perform for family and revise based on one suggestion.

*How to teach:* Perform; revise based on one suggestion.

- **Q:** What change did you make?
  **A:** Names a specific change.

### Day 5 (Friday): Unit 7 share
*Student:* Perform the revised sound story.

*How to teach:* Perform the revised story.

- **Q:** What does 'revise' mean?
  **A:** To change and improve your work.

**Assessment:** Unit 7 sound-story performance.

**Answer key:** Sound story rubric: 4 panels (storm, sun, birds, night) with matching warm/cool colors; instruments, dynamics and tempo match each panel; revised using one suggestion. 4 = all; 3 = minor gap; 2 = missing panel or sound; 1 = incomplete.

## PE 1: Group games and sportsmanship

**Prepare:** Bedsheet as a parachute, small soft balls, a sock 'flag' for each team.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking a good sport only matters when you lose: win or lose, be kind.
- Pouting or bragging: practice 'good game' and a high five.

### Day 1 (Monday): Parachute games
*Student:* Play parachute (bedsheet) popcorn.

*How to teach:* Hold the sheet's edges; toss balls on it and shake it to make 'popcorn.'

- **Q:** Why must everyone work together?
  **A:** Everyone holds and moves the sheet at the same time.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Capture the flag
*Student:* Play simplified capture the flag with two teams and a sock 'flag' in each yard half.

*How to teach:* Play simplified capture the flag with a sock flag in each half of the yard.

- **Q:** What is the goal of capture the flag?
  **A:** Take the other team's flag back to your side without being tagged.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Good sport
*Student:* Practice ending every game with a high five and 'good game,' and name one thing the other player did well.

*How to teach:* End every game with a high five and 'good game' and name one thing the other player did well.

- **Q:** What do you say at the end of a game?
  **A:** 'Good game!'

### Day 4 (Thursday): Feelings
*Student:* Talk about feelings when you win and lose.

*How to teach:* Talk about feelings when winning and losing.

- **Q:** What can you do if you lose?
  **A:** Take a breath, congratulate the winner, and try again.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Self-rate sportsmanship (rules, turns, kind words) on a 3-face scale and set one goal.

*How to teach:* Self-rate sportsmanship on a 3-face scale; set one goal.

- **Q:** What is sportsmanship?
  **A:** Playing fair and being kind whether you win or lose.

**Assessment:** Sportsmanship self-rating.

**Answer key:** Self-rating: child circles a face (great / OK / needs work) for following rules, taking turns and kind words, and writes or tells one goal. Proficient = honest rating plus a specific goal (e.g., 'say good game even when I lose').

## Health 1: Harmful household products

**Prepare:** Empty, rinsed cleaning bottles, poison symbol stickers (skull-and-crossbones, Mr. Yuk), and product cards. Keep real products locked up during the hunt.

**Watch for:**
- Thinking only things labeled 'poison' are dangerous: cleaners, medicines, batteries, and some plants can be too.
- Thinking you can smell or taste a product to check it: never smell, taste, or touch unknown products.
- Thinking bright-colored products are drinks: some cleaners look like juice.

### Day 1 (Monday): Poison symbols
*Student:* Learn skull-and-crossbones and Mr. Yuk symbols.

*How to teach:* Learn skull-and-crossbones and Mr. Yuk.

- **Q:** What does skull-and-crossbones mean?
  **A:** Poison — dangerous; don't touch.
- **Q:** What does Mr. Yuk's face mean?
  **A:** No! Poison — stay away.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Safety hunt
*Student:* With an adult, find products under the sink with warning labels.

*How to teach:* With an adult, find products under the sink with warning labels.

- **Q:** Name a product with a warning.
  **A:** Bleach, dish detergent, drain cleaner.
- **Q:** What do you do if you see one?
  **A:** Don't touch; tell an adult.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Safe storage
*Student:* Help put dangerous products up high or locked.

*How to teach:* Help put dangerous products up high or locked.

- **Q:** Why up high or locked?
  **A:** So young children can't reach them.
- **Q:** Who should move bottles?
  **A:** Adults (child can help with empty ones).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Poison Control
*Student:* Learn the Poison Control number (1-800-222-1222).

*How to teach:* Learn the Poison Control number.

- **Q:** What is the Poison Control number?
  **A:** 1-800-222-1222.
- **Q:** When would an adult call it?
  **A:** If someone swallowed or touched something poisonous.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Sort product cards into safe to touch and ask an adult.

*How to teach:* Sort product cards: safe to touch vs. ask an adult.

- **Q:** Bleach?
  **A:** Ask an adult.
- **Q:** Apple?
  **A:** Safe to touch.

**Assessment:** Product sort.

**Answer key:** Sort key (suggested): Safe to touch — apple, crayons, book, water cup, toothbrush; Ask an adult — bleach, dish soap pods, medicine, batteries, bug spray, window cleaner. Proficient 10–11 of 11.
