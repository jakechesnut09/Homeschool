# Teacher's Guide — 1st Grade, Week 3

## ELA 1: Short o and short u; personal narrative start

**Prepare:** Letter tiles, drawing paper, 'Owl Moon' by Jane Yolen (library), 8 spelling words (hot, mop, top, dot, bug, sun, cup, run), and a mixed short-vowel list (cat, pig, hot, bug, map, sit, top, rub).

**Watch for:**
- Mixing short o and short u (hot → 'hut'): o is a round 'oh-oh' mouth /ŏ/; u is a relaxed grunt /ŭ/ — say pairs like hot/hut, cot/cut.
- Retelling a small moment as a list of everything that happened all summer: focus on one short event (like jumping in a pool) with a beginning and end.
- Changing more than one letter in word chains: say the new word, ask which sound changed, then swap just that tile.

### Day 1 (Monday): Short o
*Student:* Build and read hot, pot, dot, mop, top with tiles; change one letter to make a new word (hot→hop→hip).

*How to teach:* Build hot, pot, dot, mop, top with tiles and read them. Then play the chain: hot → hop (change last letter) → hip (change vowel). Your child says which sound changed each time.

- **Q:** Change hot to hop. Which letter changed?
  **A:** The last letter: t became p.
- **Q:** Read: pot, mop, top.
  **A:** pot, mop, top — each with short o.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Short u
*Student:* Read bug, cup, sun, run, hut; play 'I spy' with short u sounds around the house.

*How to teach:* Read bug, cup, sun, run, hut together. Play 'I spy' with short u things around the house (rug, cup, bus, tub, mug).

- **Q:** Which word has short u: cup or cap?
  **A:** cup.
- **Q:** Name a short-u thing you spied.
  **A:** Examples: rug, cup, mug, tub, bus.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Characters & setting
*Student:* Read 'Owl Moon' and name the characters and setting; draw the snowy woods and label two details.

*How to teach:* Read 'Owl Moon.' Then ask who is in the story and where it happens. Your child draws the snowy woods at night and labels two details.

- **Q:** Who are the characters?
  **A:** A girl (the narrator), her Pa, and an owl.
- **Q:** What is the setting?
  **A:** Snowy woods on a cold winter night with the moon shining.
- **Q:** Why do they have to be quiet?
  **A:** To have a chance of hearing and seeing an owl; when you go owling you must be quiet.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Small moment story
*Student:* Draw a picture of something you did this summer, then tell the story aloud and write one sentence about it.

*How to teach:* Your child draws one summer moment, tells it aloud (what happened first and how it ended), then writes one sentence about it while you help stretch out words.

- **Q:** What is a small moment?
  **A:** One short event, like the time you caught a fish, told with a beginning and an end.
- **Q:** What should your sentence start with?
  **A:** A capital letter, and it should end with a period.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Mixed short-vowel word read (a, i, o, u) and spelling test of 8 CVC words; reread the small-moment sentence to a family member.

*How to teach:* Your child reads the mixed short-vowel list, then you dictate the 8 spelling words. Finish by having them read their small-moment sentence to a family member.

- **Q:** Spell sun.
  **A:** s-u-n.
- **Q:** Read: map, sit, top, rub.
  **A:** map, sit, top, rub.

**Assessment:** Spelling of 8 CVC words and oral retell of a small-moment story with a beginning and end.

**Answer key:** Spelling (8): hot, mop, top, dot, bug, sun, cup, run — proficient 7–8 correct. Oral retell rubric: 4 = tells a clear beginning, middle detail, and ending of one moment; 3 = beginning and end; 2 = only a list of events or no ending; 1 = cannot retell.

## Math 1: Subtracting as taking from and taking apart

**Prepare:** Counters, 3–4 paper cups, a 0–20 number line, drawing paper, and a few cookies or crackers for acting out 'take from' stories.

**Watch for:**
- Subtracting the smaller number from the bigger no matter the story, or writing 3 − 8: remind that we start with the whole amount (8) and take away part.
- Counting back incorrectly by saying the starting number as the first jump (for 8 − 2, saying '8, 7' and answering 7): model hopping off the start: 'eight... seven, six.'
- Thinking 'take apart' (9 kids, 4 girls) is addition because there are no 'taken away' words: draw 9, circle the 4 girls, and show the rest are the boys.

### Day 1 (Monday): Take from
*Student:* Act out '8 cookies, eat 3' with counters and write 8 − 3 = 5.

*How to teach:* Put 8 cookies (or counters) out and act out eating 3. Ask how many are left and count. Write 8 − 3 = 5 and read it 'eight take away three equals five.' Do 3 more take-from stories.

- **Q:** 8 cookies, eat 3. How many left?
  **A:** 5; 8 − 3 = 5.
- **Q:** 7 balloons, 2 fly away. How many left?
  **A:** 5; 7 − 2 = 5.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Hide and find
*Student:* Hide some of 7 counters under a cup; tell how many are hidden and write the subtraction.

*How to teach:* Show 7 counters, then hide some under a cup while your child closes their eyes. They count the counters still showing and figure out how many are hidden. Write the subtraction (7 − 4 = 3 when 4 are showing).

- **Q:** 7 counters total, 4 showing. How many are hidden?
  **A:** 3; 7 − 4 = 3.
- **Q:** 7 total, 6 showing. How many hidden?
  **A:** 1; 7 − 6 = 1.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Count back
*Student:* Subtract 1, 2, or 3 by counting back on a number line.

*How to teach:* On the number line, start at the first number and hop backward, saying the numbers you land on. Practice − 1, − 2, and − 3 from numbers up to 15.

- **Q:** 9 − 2 = ? (count back)
  **A:** 7 (eight, seven).
- **Q:** 12 − 3 = ?
  **A:** 9 (eleven, ten, nine).

### Day 4 (Thursday): Take apart
*Student:* Solve '9 kids, 4 are girls, how many boys?' with drawings.

*How to teach:* Read 'There are 9 kids. 4 are girls. How many are boys?' Have your child draw 9 stick people, circle the 4 girls, and count the rest. Write 9 − 4 = 5 and also 4 + 5 = 9.

- **Q:** 9 kids, 4 are girls. How many boys?
  **A:** 5; 9 − 4 = 5.
- **Q:** 10 pets: 6 are dogs, the rest are cats. How many cats?
  **A:** 4; 10 − 6 = 4.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Mixed add/subtract story problems; explain one strategy aloud.

*How to teach:* Give a mix of add and subtract story problems. Before solving each, ask 'Is the amount getting bigger or smaller?' Have your child explain one strategy out loud (drawing, counting on, counting back).

- **Q:** 6 frogs, 3 hop away. How many left?
  **A:** 3; 6 − 3 = 3.
- **Q:** 4 crayons, 4 more. How many?
  **A:** 8; 4 + 4 = 8.

**Assessment:** Mixed operation story problems and verbal explanation.

**Answer key:** Suggested problems: 1) 9 apples, 3 eaten: 9 − 3 = 6. 2) 5 birds + 4 birds: 5 + 4 = 9. 3) 10 bubbles, 7 pop: 10 − 7 = 3. 4) 8 kids, 5 are boys, how many girls: 8 − 5 = 3. 5) 2 + 6 = 8 marbles. 6) 7 cars, 1 drives away: 6. Verbal explanation rubric: 4 = names strategy and shows it ('I started at 9 and counted back 3: 8, 7, 6'); 3 = correct strategy with some prompting; 2 = correct answer but cannot explain; 1 = chooses wrong operation.

## Science 1 (NGSS): Test, compare, improve

**Prepare:** Aluminum foil (two 15 cm squares per test), about 100 pennies, a tub of water, tape, and a data table (boat, pennies before sinking).

**Watch for:**
- Changing several things at once when improving: change only one feature so you know what made the difference.
- Thinking heavier boats always sink: boats float when their shape spreads out and pushes aside lots of water.
- Dumping pennies in one spot: place them carefully and evenly, the same way for every test, to keep it fair.

### Day 1 (Monday): Foil boats
*Student:* Shape two 15 cm squares of foil into two different boat designs (flat raft and tall-sided tub) and sketch each.

*How to teach:* Your child shapes one foil square into a flat raft and another into a tall-sided tub, and sketches each.

- **Q:** How are the two boats different?
  **A:** One is flat with low/no sides; the other has tall sides.
- **Q:** Why use the same size foil for both?
  **A:** So the test is fair — only the shape is different.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Test
*Student:* Add pennies one at a time to each boat until it sinks; record counts in a table.

*How to teach:* Float each boat and add pennies one at a time until it sinks. Record counts.

- **Q:** Why add one penny at a time?
  **A:** To count exactly how many it holds before sinking.
- **Q:** What should you record?
  **A:** The number of pennies each boat held.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Compare
*Student:* Compare data: which shape held more pennies and why?

*How to teach:* Compare data: which held more and why?

- **Q:** Which shape usually holds more?
  **A:** A boat with a wide, flat bottom and sides tall enough to keep water out.
- **Q:** Why might the flat raft sink sooner?
  **A:** Water spills over the low edges.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Improve
*Student:* Redesign the weaker boat by changing one feature (wider base or taller sides) and retest it with pennies.

*How to teach:* Your child changes one feature on the weaker boat and retests.

- **Q:** Why change only one thing?
  **A:** So you know which change made the difference.
- **Q:** Did it improve? How do you know?
  **A:** Compare the new penny count to the old count.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Draw the engineering cycle (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve) with your boat as the example.

*How to teach:* Your child draws the engineering cycle (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve) using the boat as the example.

- **Q:** Name the steps of the engineering cycle.
  **A:** Ask, imagine, plan, create, improve.
- **Q:** Which step was the penny test?
  **A:** Testing as part of create/improve.

**Assessment:** Data table and engineering cycle drawing.

**Answer key:** Data table: two boats with penny counts recorded; proficient includes the redesign count. Cycle drawing: 4 = all 5 steps in order with a boat example for each; 3 = all steps, few examples; 2 = steps missing; 1 = incomplete.

## A Child's Place in Time and Space: Fair play and good sportsmanship

**Prepare:** A simple outdoor game (tag, kickball) or board game, scenario cards (fair/unfair), and a T-chart (Good Sport / Poor Sport).

**Watch for:**
- Thinking 'fair' always means 'exactly the same': fair means everyone gets what they need (e.g., glasses for someone who can't see).
- Thinking good sports only win: good sportsmanship is how you act whether you win or lose.
- Bragging when winning: say 'good game' and thank the other player.

### Day 1 (Monday): What is fair?
*Student:* Discuss whether 'fair' always means 'the same'; sort scenario cards into fair and unfair.

*How to teach:* Discuss whether fair always means the same. Sort scenario cards into fair and unfair.

- **Q:** Is it fair if only one child gets to choose the game every day?
  **A:** No — taking turns is fair.
- **Q:** Is it fair that a child who needs glasses gets them and others don't?
  **A:** Yes — fair means getting what you need.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Game day
*Student:* Play a backyard game and practice taking turns, following rules and shaking hands at the end.

*How to teach:* Play a backyard game; practice taking turns, following rules, and shaking hands at the end.

- **Q:** What do you say at the end of a game?
  **A:** 'Good game!'
- **Q:** Why take turns?
  **A:** So everyone gets a chance.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Winning and losing
*Student:* Role-play good and poor ways to win and lose; make a T-chart of sportsmanlike words.

*How to teach:* Role-play good and poor ways to win and lose. Fill a T-chart of sportsmanlike words.

- **Q:** Good-sport words when you lose?
  **A:** 'Good game, you played well.'
- **Q:** Poor-sport action?
  **A:** Yelling, quitting, bragging, or blaming others.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Team pledge
*Student:* Write a 3-line good-sport pledge and sign it.

*How to teach:* Your child writes and signs a 3-line good-sport pledge.

- **Q:** Example pledge line?
  **A:** I will follow the rules.
- **Q:** Another line?
  **A:** I will say 'good game' whether I win or lose.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Tell how a good sport acts in a game you played this week.

*How to teach:* Your child tells how a good sport acted in a game this week.

- **Q:** How did you act like a good sport?
  **A:** Answers vary — e.g., I took turns and congratulated my sister.
- **Q:** What does a good sport do if a game feels unfair?
  **A:** Calmly talk about it or ask an adult.

**Assessment:** Sportsmanship T-chart and pledge.

**Answer key:** T-chart: proficient lists at least 3 good-sport words/actions (take turns, follow rules, say 'good game,' encourage others) and 3 poor-sport ones (cheat, brag, quit, yell). Pledge: 3 positive lines, signed.

## Art & Music 1: Safe tools and shape animals; beat and movement

**Prepare:** Safety scissors, glue stick, pre-drawn shapes or paper to cut; a march recording or GoNoodle.

**Watch for:**
- Carrying scissors open or pointing out: close the blades and hold them pointing down.
- Using too much glue: 'dot, dot, not a lot.'

### Day 1 (Monday): Tool safety
*Student:* Practice safe scissors handling (walk with closed blades, cut away from body) and dot-dot-not-a-lot glue.

*How to teach:* Model walking with scissors closed, blades pointing down; cutting with thumb up; gluing with 'dot, dot, not a lot.'

- **Q:** How do you carry scissors?
  **A:** Blades closed, pointing down, walking.
- **Q:** How much glue do you use?
  **A:** Dot, dot, not a lot.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Shape animals
*Student:* Cut circles, squares, triangles and rectangles and glue them into an animal.

*How to teach:* Cut circles, squares, triangles and rectangles and glue them into an animal.

- **Q:** What shape did you use for the body?
  **A:** Names the shape used, e.g., 'a big circle.'

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Beat moves
*Student:* Do 4 different beat movements (pat, clap, tap, stomp) to a march, changing every 8 beats.

*How to teach:* Play a march; do pats, claps, taps and stomps, changing every 8 beats.

- **Q:** How many beats before we change?
  **A:** 8 beats.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Echo beats
*Student:* Echo 4-beat clapping patterns a family member claps.

*How to teach:* Clap 4-beat patterns; your child echoes them exactly.

- **Q:** Echo: clap-clap-clap-rest.
  **A:** Claps three times, then a silent beat.

### Day 5 (Friday): Share
*Student:* Present the shape animal and tell what shapes you used.

*How to teach:* Your child presents the animal and names the shapes.

- **Q:** Name a geometric shape in your animal.
  **A:** Circle, square, triangle or rectangle.

**Assessment:** Shape animal and safe tool use observation.

**Answer key:** Shape animal: uses circles, squares, triangles and rectangles glued neatly to make an animal; child names the shapes. Tool observation: carries scissors closed and pointing down, cuts away from body, small glue dots. Proficient = all 4 shapes and safe tool use.

## PE 1: Levels and directions

**Prepare:** Music player and 2-3 scarves (or plastic grocery bags). Clear space for moving freely.

**Watch for:**
- Confusing level with direction: level is how high or low your body is; direction is which way you move.
- Walking backward without looking: teach a quick look over the shoulder and slow speed.

### Day 1 (Monday): Levels freeze
*Student:* Move to music and freeze at the level called (high, medium, low).

*How to teach:* Play music; when it pauses, call 'high' (on tiptoes, arms up), 'medium' (standing, bent), or 'low' (close to the ground). Have your child freeze in that level.

- **Q:** Show a low level.
  **A:** Body close to the ground: crouching, crawling, or lying.
- **Q:** What level is standing on tiptoes with arms up?
  **A:** High level.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Scarf toss
*Student:* Toss a scarf high and catch it at a low level.

*How to teach:* Toss a scarf high, then catch it as low as possible (crouching). Repeat, catching at medium and high levels.

- **Q:** Where did you catch the scarf when it was at a low level?
  **A:** Near the ground, crouched down.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Directions
*Student:* Walk forward, backward and sideways safely in a game of 'Simon Says'.

*How to teach:* Play Simon Says with 'walk forward,' 'walk backward,' 'step sideways.' Remind your child to look over their shoulder when going backward.

- **Q:** How do you move backward safely?
  **A:** Go slowly and look over your shoulder.
- **Q:** Which way is sideways?
  **A:** Toward your side, left or right, with your body facing the front.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Combination
*Student:* Gallop forward at a medium level, crab-walk backward at a low level.

*How to teach:* Model a gallop forward at medium level, then a crab walk backward at low level. Let your child copy and then invent a new combo.

- **Q:** A crab walk is at which level?
  **A:** Low level.
- **Q:** What direction does a crab walk go in our game?
  **A:** Backward.

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Perform a 4-move sequence with two levels and two directions.

*How to teach:* Have your child build and perform a 4-move sequence with two levels and two directions, saying each as they do it.

- **Q:** Tell me the levels and directions in your sequence.
  **A:** Names two levels and two directions that match what they performed.

**Assessment:** Levels/directions sequence.

**Answer key:** Sequence rubric: performs 4 moves using two different levels (high/medium/low) and two different directions (forward/backward/sideways) and can name them. Proficient = 4 correct moves named; developing = mixes up levels or directions once; beginning = needs help to create moves.

## Health 1: Using I-messages and asking for help

**Prepare:** A sentence-frame card ('I feel ___ when ___. I need ___.'), paper for a hand outline, and phone numbers of trusted adults.

**Watch for:**
- Blaming in I-messages ('I feel mad when YOU are mean'): describe the action, not the person.
- Thinking asking for help is babyish: everyone asks for help.
- Thinking any adult is a trusted adult: trusted adults are people your family knows and trusts.

### Day 1 (Monday): I-messages
*Student:* Learn 'I feel ___ when ___. I need ___.' and practice with 3 example problems.

*How to teach:* Learn the I-message frame; practice with 3 problems.

- **Q:** Your sister takes your crayon. Make an I-message.
  **A:** I feel upset when you take my crayon. I need you to ask first.
- **Q:** Why use I-messages?
  **A:** They tell your feelings calmly without blaming.

### Day 2 (Tuesday): Listening
*Student:* Practice good listening (eyes, ears, quiet body) while a family member shares.

*How to teach:* Practice good listening while a family member shares.

- **Q:** What are the three parts of good listening?
  **A:** Eyes on the speaker, ears listening, quiet body.
- **Q:** How do you show you listened?
  **A:** Repeat back or ask a question about it.

### Day 3 (Wednesday): Trusted adults hand
*Student:* Trace your hand and write a trusted adult on each finger.

*How to teach:* Trace your hand; write a trusted adult on each finger.

- **Q:** Who is a trusted adult?
  **A:** A grown-up your family trusts who keeps you safe — parent, grandparent, teacher, doctor.
- **Q:** Name someone on your hand.
  **A:** Answers vary.

### Day 4 (Thursday): Ask for help
*Student:* Role-play asking a trusted adult for help when lost, hurt or sad.

*How to teach:* Role-play asking a trusted adult for help when lost, hurt, or sad.

- **Q:** You're lost in a store. Who can you ask?
  **A:** A store worker at the counter or a parent with kids.
- **Q:** What do you say?
  **A:** I'm lost. Can you help me find my mom/dad?

### Day 5 (Friday): Review & check
*Student:* Use an I-message to solve a pretend problem.

*How to teach:* Your child uses an I-message to solve a pretend problem.

- **Q:** Problem: friend won't share the swing.
  **A:** I feel sad when I don't get a turn. I need us to take turns.
- **Q:** What's the last part of an I-message?
  **A:** I need ___.

**Assessment:** I-message demonstration and trusted adults hand.

**Answer key:** Rubric: 4 = complete I-message with feeling, specific action, and need, said calmly; trusted adults hand has 5 appropriate names; 3 = I-message missing one part; 2 = blames instead; 1 = cannot form an I-message.
