# California, A Changing State (Grade 4)

Grade 4 History-Social Science follows the California History-Social Science Content Standards and the 2016 HSS Framework. Students study California's geography and regions, the diverse nations of California Indians, Spanish exploration and the mission system from multiple perspectives, Mexican California and the ranchos, the Bear Flag Revolt and Mexican-American War, the Gold Rush and statehood, and California's growth into an agricultural, industrial and cultural power, ending with how local, state and federal governments work. Primary sources from Calisphere's curated sets, the Online Archive of California and the Library of Congress anchor every unit.

*Framework:* California History-Social Science Content Standards (Grade 4: California, A Changing State); 2016 California History-Social Science Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: The Geography of California (weeks 1–4)
California's location, regions and physical features shape where and how people live.

- Week 1: Latitude, longitude and the globe
- Week 2: California's regions
- Week 3: Rivers, valleys and mountain passes
- Week 4: Comparing California communities

### Unit 2: California's First Peoples (weeks 5–8)
Diverse California Indian nations adapted to and shaped many environments and continue as living communities today.

- Week 5: California Indian nations and regions
- Week 6: Trade, technology and the environment
- Week 7: Beliefs, legends and oral tradition
- Week 8: California Indians today

### Unit 3: Exploration by Land and Sea (weeks 9–10)
Competing empires explored the North Pacific coast for trade, wealth and territory.

- Week 9: Sea explorers of the North Pacific
- Week 10: Land routes and the Russians

### Unit 4: Missions, Presidios and Pueblos (weeks 11–15)
Spanish colonization brought soldiers, missionaries and settlers whose institutions transformed Native lives, land and economy.

- Week 11: Spanish colonization begins: the Sacred Expedition
- Week 12: Mapping the missions
- Week 13: Life in the missions
- Week 14: Presidios and pueblos
- Week 15: Missions from many perspectives

### Unit 5: Mexican California and the Ranchos (weeks 16–18)
Mexican independence secularized the missions and created a rancho society built on land grants and Native labor.

- Week 16: Mexican independence and Alta California
- Week 17: Secularization and land grants
- Week 18: Life on the ranchos

### Unit 6: Trails West and the Bear Flag Revolt (weeks 19–21)
Overland migrants and the Mexican-American War brought California under U.S. control.

- Week 19: Overland trails to California
- Week 20: The Bear Flag Revolt
- Week 21: The Mexican-American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo

### Unit 7: The Gold Rush (weeks 22–24)
The discovery of gold rapidly transformed California's population, economy, environment and people's lives.

- Week 22: Gold discovered!
- Week 23: Gold Rush lives and communities
- Week 24: The Gold Rush transforms California

### Unit 8: Statehood (weeks 25–26)
California became the 31st state with a constitution and government unlike the Spanish and Mexican systems.

- Week 25: The road to statehood
- Week 26: New government compared

### Unit 9: Connecting California: Transportation and Immigration (weeks 27–30)
Railroads and telegraphs tied California to the nation while immigrants built the state amid conflict and cooperation.

- Week 27: Pony Express, telegraph and mail
- Week 28: The transcontinental railroad
- Week 29: Immigration and the Chinese Exclusion Act
- Week 30: Growth of cities: Los Angeles and beyond

### Unit 10: Modern California: Water, Work and Culture (weeks 31–33)
Water projects, new industries, schools and artists made California a national leader through hard times and war.

- Week 31: Water: dams, aqueducts and reservoirs
- Week 32: Hard times: the Great Depression, Dust Bowl and World War II
- Week 33: Industry, education and culture

### Unit 11: Governing California (weeks 34–36)
Federal, state and local governments share power through written constitutions.

- Week 34: Constitutions: U.S. and California
- Week 35: State government
- Week 36: Local government and year review

## Standards

- **4.1.1** Explain and use the coordinate grid system of latitude and longitude to determine the absolute locations of places in California and on Earth.
- **4.1.2** Distinguish between the North and South Poles; the equator and prime meridian; the tropics; and the hemispheres, using coordinates to plot locations.
- **4.1.3** Identify the state capital and describe the regions of California, including how their characteristics and physical environments affect human activity.
- **4.1.4** Identify the locations of the Pacific Ocean, rivers, valleys and mountain passes and explain their effects on the growth of towns.
- **4.1.5** Use maps, charts and pictures to describe how communities in California vary in land use, vegetation, wildlife, climate, population density, architecture, services and transportation.
- **4.2.1** Discuss the major nations of California Indians, their geographic distribution, economic activities, legends and religious beliefs, and how they depended on, adapted to and modified the environment.
- **4.2.2** Identify the early land and sea routes to, and European settlements in, California, with a focus on exploration of the North Pacific.
- **4.2.3** Describe the Spanish exploration and colonization of California, including the relationships among soldiers, missionaries and Indians.
- **4.2.4** Describe the mapping of, geographic basis of, and economic factors in the placement and function of the Spanish missions; understand how the mission system expanded the influence of Spain and Catholicism.
- **4.2.5** Describe the daily lives of the people, native and non-native, who occupied the presidios, missions, ranchos and pueblos.
- **4.2.6** Discuss the role of the Franciscans in changing the economy of California from a hunter-gatherer economy to an agricultural economy.
- **4.2.7** Describe the effects of the Mexican War for Independence on Alta California, including its effects on territorial boundaries.
- **4.2.8** Discuss the period of Mexican rule in California, including land grants, secularization of the missions and the rise of the rancheros.
- **4.3.1** Identify the locations of Mexican settlements in California and other settlements, including Fort Ross and Sutter's Fort.
- **4.3.2** Compare how and why people traveled to California and the routes they traveled (e.g., James Beckwourth, John Bidwell, John C. Frémont, Pío Pico).
- **4.3.3** Analyze the effects of the Gold Rush on settlements, daily life, politics and the physical environment.
- **4.3.4** Study the lives of women who helped build early California (e.g., Biddy Mason).
- **4.3.5** Discuss how California became a state and how its new government differed from those during the Spanish and Mexican periods.
- **4.4.1** Understand the story and lasting influence of the Pony Express, Overland Mail Service, Western Union and the transcontinental railroad, including the contributions of Chinese workers.
- **4.4.2** Explain how the Gold Rush transformed California's economy, including products, changes in towns, and economic conflicts between diverse groups.
- **4.4.3** Discuss immigration and migration to California between 1850 and 1900, including countries of origin and conflicts and accords among groups (e.g., the 1882 Chinese Exclusion Act).
- **4.4.4** Describe rapid American immigration, internal migration, settlement and the growth of towns and cities (e.g., Los Angeles).
- **4.4.5** Discuss the effects of the Great Depression, the Dust Bowl and World War II on California.
- **4.4.6** Describe the development and locations of new industries since the nineteenth century (aerospace, electronics, agriculture and irrigation, oil, automobiles, Pacific trade).
- **4.4.7** Trace the evolution of California's water system into a network of dams, aqueducts and reservoirs.
- **4.4.8** Describe the history and development of California's public education system, including universities and community colleges.
- **4.4.9** Analyze the impact of twentieth-century Californians on the nation's artistic and cultural development, including the entertainment industry.
- **4.5.1** Discuss what the U.S. Constitution is and why it is important.
- **4.5.2** Understand the purpose of the California Constitution, its key principles and its relationship to the U.S. Constitution.
- **4.5.3** Describe the similarities and differences among federal, state and local governments.
- **4.5.4** Explain the structures and functions of state governments, including the roles and responsibilities of elected officials.
- **4.5.5** Describe the components of California's governance structure (cities and towns, Indian rancherias and reservations, counties, school districts).
- **CST.1** Analysis skill: place key events and people in chronological sequence and in geographic context; construct timelines.
- **HI.1** Analysis skill: identify the human and physical characteristics of places and explain how those features form the unique character of those places.
- **HI.2** Analysis skill: identify and interpret the multiple causes and effects of historical events.
- **REP.1** Analysis skill: differentiate between primary and secondary sources.
- **REP.4** Analysis skill: pose relevant questions about events encountered in historical documents, eyewitness accounts, oral histories, letters, diaries, artifacts, photographs, maps and artworks.
