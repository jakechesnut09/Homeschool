# ELA 4 (Grade 4)

Fourth-grade English Language Arts aligned to the California Common Core State Standards and the 2014 ELA/ELD Framework. Students read whole novels (Island of the Blue Dolphins, The Wonderful Wizard of Oz), myths, fables, poetry, drama and informational texts tied to California, and write personal narratives, explanatory reports, opinion essays and a short research report. Every week weaves reading, writing, grammar and conventions, morphology-based word study, and speaking and listening.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for ELA/Literacy; 2014 California ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 90 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Launching Readers & Writers: Story Elements and Personal Narrative (weeks 1–4)
Readers notice how characters, settings and events work together; writers shape real experiences into stories.

- Week 1: Story elements in Just So Stories
- Week 2: Character and personal narrative beginnings
- Week 3: Drafting narratives with dialogue and detail
- Week 4: Revising, publishing and theme

### Unit 2: Myths, Fables & Traditional Tales (weeks 5–8)
Stories from many cultures share themes and patterns, and mythology lives on in our language.

- Week 5: Aesop's fables and morals
- Week 6: Greek myths and allusions
- Week 7: Coyote and trickster tales across cultures
- Week 8: Writing an original myth

### Unit 3: Reading to Learn: Informational Text and Explanatory Writing (weeks 9–12)
Authors organize information in structures; writers explain a topic clearly with facts and details.

- Week 9: Main idea and key details
- Week 10: Text structures
- Week 11: Visual information and note-taking
- Week 12: Writing an explanatory report

### Unit 4: Novel Study: Island of the Blue Dolphins (weeks 13–17)
Survival tests and reveals character; fiction and nonfiction together tell a fuller story.

- Week 13: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 1-6
- Week 14: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 7-14
- Week 15: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 15-22
- Week 16: Island of the Blue Dolphins, chapters 23-29
- Week 17: Fiction meets fact: the Lone Woman of San Nicolas

### Unit 5: Poetry & Drama (weeks 18–20)
Poets and playwrights use structure, sound and figurative language to create meaning.

- Week 18: Poetry elements
- Week 19: Narrative poetry and performance
- Week 20: Drama: reading and performing a play

### Unit 6: Opinion and Argument (weeks 21–24)
Strong opinions rest on reasons supported by evidence.

- Week 21: Reasons and evidence in opinion texts
- Week 22: Planning an opinion essay
- Week 23: Drafting and revising the opinion essay
- Week 24: Publishing and speaking: formal vs informal

### Unit 7: Novel Study: The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (weeks 25–29)
Journeys change characters, and a story can be retold through film, stage and art.

- Week 25: Oz chapters 1-6
- Week 26: Oz chapters 7-12
- Week 27: Oz chapters 13-18
- Week 28: Oz chapters 19-24 and the 1939 film
- Week 29: Retelling Oz: narrative and comparison

### Unit 8: Research and Report: California Natural Wonders (weeks 30–34)
Researchers gather, compare and integrate sources, including firsthand accounts, to build knowledge.

- Week 30: Research questions and sources
- Week 31: Firsthand and secondhand accounts
- Week 32: Drafting the research report
- Week 33: Revising and publishing the report
- Week 34: Presenting research

### Unit 9: Reflection, Fluency & Portfolio (weeks 35–36)
Readers and writers reflect on growth and set goals.

- Week 35: Fluency and reading reflection
- Week 36: Writing portfolio and celebration

## Standards

- **L.4.1** Demonstrate command of grammar and usage: relative pronouns and adverbs, progressive verb tenses, modal auxiliaries, adjective order, prepositional phrases, complete sentences, frequently confused words.
- **L.4.2** Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation (commas, quotation marks) and spelling.
- **L.4.3** Use knowledge of language and its conventions: choose precise words and punctuation for effect.
- **L.4.4** Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, Greek and Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials.
- **L.4.5** Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships and nuances (similes, metaphors, idioms, adages, proverbs, antonyms, synonyms).
- **L.4.6** Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RF.4.3** Use combined knowledge of letter-sound correspondences, syllabication patterns and morphology (roots and affixes) to read unfamiliar multisyllabic words.
- **RF.4.4** Read with sufficient accuracy and fluency to support comprehension.
- **RI.4.1** Refer to details and examples in an informational text when explaining what it says and drawing inferences.
- **RI.4.10** Read and comprehend grade 4-5 informational texts proficiently.
- **RI.4.2** Determine the main idea of a text and explain how it is supported by key details; summarize.
- **RI.4.3** Explain events, procedures, ideas or concepts in a historical, scientific or technical text, including what happened and why.
- **RI.4.4** Determine the meaning of general academic and domain-specific words in a grade 4 text.
- **RI.4.5** Describe the overall structure (chronology, comparison, cause/effect, problem/solution) of events, ideas or information in a text.
- **RI.4.6** Compare and contrast a firsthand and secondhand account of the same event or topic.
- **RI.4.7** Interpret information presented visually, orally or quantitatively (charts, graphs, diagrams, timelines) and explain how it contributes to understanding.
- **RI.4.8** Explain how an author uses reasons and evidence to support particular points in a text.
- **RI.4.9** Integrate information from two texts on the same topic to write or speak about the subject knowledgeably.
- **RL.4.1** Refer to details and examples in a text when explaining what it says explicitly and when drawing inferences.
- **RL.4.10** Read and comprehend grade 4-5 literature proficiently.
- **RL.4.2** Determine a theme of a story, drama or poem from details in the text; summarize the text.
- **RL.4.3** Describe in depth a character, setting or event, drawing on specific details (thoughts, words, actions).
- **RL.4.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including those that allude to significant characters in mythology.
- **RL.4.5** Explain major differences between poems, drama and prose and refer to their structural elements (verse, rhythm, meter, cast, settings, stage directions).
- **RL.4.6** Compare and contrast the point of view from which different stories are narrated, including first- and third-person.
- **RL.4.7** Make connections between the text of a story or drama and a visual or oral presentation of it.
- **RL.4.9** Compare and contrast the treatment of similar themes and patterns of events in stories, myths and traditional literature from different cultures.
- **SL.4.1** Engage effectively in collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing one's own clearly.
- **SL.4.2** Paraphrase portions of a text read aloud or information presented in diverse media and formats.
- **SL.4.3** Identify the reasons and evidence a speaker provides to support particular points.
- **SL.4.4** Report on a topic or text, tell a story or recount an experience in an organized manner with appropriate facts and details; speak clearly at an understandable pace.
- **SL.4.5** Add audio recordings and visual displays to presentations to enhance main ideas or themes.
- **SL.4.6** Differentiate between contexts that call for formal English and informal discourse.
- **W.4.1** Write opinion pieces supporting a point of view with reasons and information.
- **W.4.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks and purposes.
- **W.4.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas clearly.
- **W.4.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using technique, descriptive details and clear event sequences.
- **W.4.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose and audience.
- **W.4.5** With guidance, develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising and editing.
- **W.4.6** With guidance, use technology to produce and publish writing; type a minimum of one page in a single sitting.
- **W.4.7** Conduct short research projects that build knowledge through investigation of different aspects of a topic.
- **W.4.8** Recall relevant information from experiences or gather information from print and digital sources; take notes, categorize and list sources.
- **W.4.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection and research.
