# Science 4 (Grade 4)

Fourth-grade science built on the California Next Generation Science Standards and the 2016 CA Science Framework. Students investigate energy (speed, collisions, circuits, sound, light and heat), waves and information transfer, how plant and animal structures support survival and sensing, how weathering, erosion and rock layers record Earth's history, how maps reveal patterns of earthquakes and volcanoes, and how humans use natural resources and reduce hazard impacts. Each week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials and a science notebook entry.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 California Science Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Energy and Motion (weeks 1–4)
Faster objects have more energy, and collisions transfer energy between objects.

- Week 1: What is energy?
- Week 2: Speed and energy
- Week 3: Collisions
- Week 4: Energy in motion: Newton's cradle and roller coasters

### Unit 2: Energy Transfer: Sound, Heat and Electricity (weeks 5–8)
Energy moves from place to place through sound, light, heat and electric currents.

- Week 5: Sound energy
- Week 6: Heat energy
- Week 7: Electric circuits
- Week 8: Energy conversion

### Unit 3: Waves and Information (weeks 9–12)
Waves have patterns of amplitude and wavelength, and patterns can carry information.

- Week 9: Wave patterns
- Week 10: Waves cause motion
- Week 11: Patterns that carry information
- Week 12: Designing an information system

### Unit 4: Light and Sight (weeks 13–15)
We see objects when light reflects off them and enters our eyes.

- Week 13: How light travels
- Week 14: Seeing: light and the eye
- Week 15: Light energy and color

### Unit 5: Structures and Senses of Living Things (weeks 16–19)
Internal and external structures help organisms survive, and senses send information to the brain.

- Week 16: Plant structures
- Week 17: Animal external structures
- Week 18: Animal internal structures
- Week 19: Senses and the brain

### Unit 6: Earth's Changing Surface (weeks 20–23)
Weathering and erosion slowly reshape land, and rock layers and fossils record the changes.

- Week 20: Weathering
- Week 21: Erosion by water, wind and ice
- Week 22: Rock layers and fossils
- Week 23: California landscapes over time

### Unit 7: Mapping Earth's Features and Natural Hazards (weeks 24–27)
Maps reveal patterns of mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes, and engineers design ways to reduce hazards.

- Week 24: Maps of Earth's features
- Week 25: Earthquakes and volcanoes patterns
- Week 26: Reducing hazard impacts
- Week 27: Other hazards: tsunamis, landslides, floods

### Unit 8: Energy Resources and the Environment (weeks 28–31)
Energy and fuels come from natural resources, renewable and nonrenewable, and their use affects the environment.

- Week 28: Natural resources and energy
- Week 29: Renewable energy investigations
- Week 30: Environmental effects of energy use
- Week 31: Energy research and presentation

### Unit 9: Engineering Design Capstone (weeks 32–36)
Engineers define problems, test prototypes fairly and improve designs that convert energy.

- Week 32: Defining a design problem: the wind-powered lifter
- Week 33: Generating and comparing solutions
- Week 34: Building and testing the prototype
- Week 35: Improving and presenting the design
- Week 36: Science year in review

## Standards

- **3-5-ETS1-1** Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or want, including criteria for success and constraints on materials, time or cost.
- **3-5-ETS1-2** Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each meets the criteria and constraints.
- **3-5-ETS1-3** Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved.
- **4-ESS1-1** Identify evidence from patterns in rock formations and fossils in rock layers to support an explanation for changes in a landscape over time.
- **4-ESS2-1** Make observations and/or measurements to provide evidence of the effects of weathering or the rate of erosion by water, ice, wind or vegetation.
- **4-ESS2-2** Analyze and interpret data from maps to describe patterns of Earth's features.
- **4-ESS3-1** Obtain and combine information to describe that energy and fuels are derived from natural resources and their uses affect the environment.
- **4-ESS3-2** Generate and compare multiple solutions to reduce the impacts of natural Earth processes on humans.
- **4-LS1-1** Construct an argument that plants and animals have internal and external structures that support survival, growth, behavior and reproduction.
- **4-LS1-2** Use a model to describe that animals receive different types of information through their senses, process it in the brain and respond.
- **4-PS3-1** Use evidence to construct an explanation relating the speed of an object to the energy of that object.
- **4-PS3-2** Make observations to provide evidence that energy can be transferred from place to place by sound, light, heat and electric currents.
- **4-PS3-3** Ask questions and predict outcomes about the changes in energy that occur when objects collide.
- **4-PS3-4** Apply scientific ideas to design, test and refine a device that converts energy from one form to another.
- **4-PS4-1** Develop a model of waves to describe patterns in terms of amplitude and wavelength and that waves can cause objects to move.
- **4-PS4-2** Develop a model to describe that light reflecting from objects and entering the eye allows objects to be seen.
- **4-PS4-3** Generate and compare multiple solutions that use patterns to transfer information.
