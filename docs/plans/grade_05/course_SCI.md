# Science 5 (Grade 5)

Fifth-grade NGSS science organized around the CA Science Framework's grade 5 storyline: matter is made of tiny particles that are conserved through change, plants build their bodies from air and water using the sun's energy, matter and energy cycle through ecosystems, Earth's four spheres interact, water is unevenly distributed, communities protect resources, and patterns of shadows and stars come from Earth's motion in space. Nearly every week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials, a science notebook entry, and a claim-evidence-reasoning (CER) write-up. Engineering design challenges are woven in and featured in two dedicated units.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 California Science Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Matter Is Made of Particles (weeks 1–5)
All matter is made of particles too small to see, and materials can be identified by their properties.

- Week 1: The mystery of disappearing sugar: evidence for particles
- Week 2: Gases are matter too
- Week 3: Properties of materials
- Week 4: Identifying mystery powders
- Week 5: Particle model and states of matter

### Unit 2: Changes in Matter (weeks 6–9)
When substances change or mix, weight is conserved, and sometimes new substances form.

- Week 6: Conservation of weight when melting and freezing
- Week 7: Mixtures and solutions
- Week 8: Chemical reactions: making new substances
- Week 9: Conservation of weight in reactions

### Unit 3: Engineering Design: Water Filter Challenge (weeks 10–11)
Engineers define problems, compare solutions and use fair tests to improve designs.

- Week 10: Designing a water filter: define and generate
- Week 11: Testing and improving the filter

### Unit 4: Plants, Food and Energy from the Sun (weeks 12–15)
Plants build their bodies mostly from air and water, capturing the sun's energy that animals later use.

- Week 12: Where does a plant's mass come from?
- Week 13: Plants need air and water
- Week 14: Energy in food comes from the sun
- Week 15: Plant investigation results and argument

### Unit 5: Ecosystems: Cycling Matter (weeks 16–19)
Matter moves among producers, consumers, decomposers and the environment in a continuous cycle.

- Week 16: Food webs: producers, consumers and decomposers
- Week 17: Decomposers and the matter cycle
- Week 18: Modeling matter cycling in an ecosystem
- Week 19: Ecosystem balance and invasive species

### Unit 6: Earth's Interacting Systems (weeks 20–23)
The geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere constantly interact to shape Earth.

- Week 20: Introducing Earth's four spheres
- Week 21: Weathering, erosion and deposition
- Week 22: Atmosphere and weather interactions
- Week 23: Sphere interactions in a California event

### Unit 7: Water on Earth (weeks 24–25)
Most of Earth's water is salty or frozen, so fresh water is precious.

- Week 24: Where is Earth's water?
- Week 25: California's water: sources and the water cycle

### Unit 8: Protecting Earth's Resources (weeks 26–28)
Communities use science to reduce human impact on land, water, air and living things.

- Week 26: Human impact on land, water and air
- Week 27: Communities protecting the environment
- Week 28: Engineering a solution: reduce, reuse or protect

### Unit 9: Gravity and the Stars (weeks 29–31)
Earth's gravity pulls everything toward its center, and stars look bright or dim depending on distance.

- Week 29: Gravity pulls down toward Earth's center
- Week 30: The sun is a star
- Week 31: Scale of the solar system and stars

### Unit 10: Patterns in the Sky (weeks 32–34)
Earth's rotation and orbit cause daily shadow patterns, day and night, and seasonal star patterns.

- Week 32: Shadows during the day
- Week 33: Day length and seasons
- Week 34: Seasonal star patterns

### Unit 11: Capstone Investigation (weeks 35–36)
Scientists ask questions, plan investigations, analyze data and communicate findings.

- Week 35: Capstone investigation: planning and testing
- Week 36: Capstone: analyzing and presenting

## Standards

- **3-5-ETS1-1** Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or want that includes specified criteria for success and constraints on materials, time, or cost.
- **3-5-ETS1-2** Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints.
- **3-5-ETS1-3** Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved.
- **5-ESS1-1** Support an argument that differences in the apparent brightness of the sun compared to other stars are due to their relative distances from Earth.
- **5-ESS1-2** Represent data in graphical displays to reveal patterns of daily changes in shadows, day and night, and the seasonal appearance of some stars.
- **5-ESS2-1** Develop a model using an example to describe ways the geosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere, and/or atmosphere interact.
- **5-ESS2-2** Describe and graph the amounts and percentages of water and fresh water in various reservoirs to provide evidence about the distribution of water on Earth.
- **5-ESS3-1** Obtain and combine information about ways individual communities use science ideas to protect the Earth's resources and environment.
- **5-LS1-1** Support an argument that plants get the materials they need for growth chiefly from air and water.
- **5-LS2-1** Develop a model to describe the movement of matter among plants, animals, decomposers, and the environment.
- **5-PS1-1** Develop a model to describe that matter is made of particles too small to be seen.
- **5-PS1-2** Measure and graph quantities to provide evidence that the total weight of matter is conserved when heating, cooling, or mixing substances.
- **5-PS1-3** Make observations and measurements to identify materials based on their properties.
- **5-PS1-4** Conduct an investigation to determine whether the mixing of two or more substances results in new substances.
- **5-PS2-1** Support an argument that the gravitational force exerted by Earth on objects is directed down.
- **5-PS3-1** Use models to describe that energy in animals' food (used for repair, growth, motion, and warmth) was once energy from the sun.
