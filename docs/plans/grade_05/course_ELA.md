# ELA 5 (Grade 5)

Fifth-grade English Language Arts built around whole novels (Hatchet, Esperanza Rising, The Secret Garden), classic short stories, narrative and lyric poetry, Greek myths, and informational texts tied to early American history. Every week integrates close reading with text evidence, Greek and Latin word study, grammar and conventions, a writing product (narrative, informative/research or opinion), and a speaking and listening task. Students build a writing portfolio and finish the year with a multimedia research presentation.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts & Literacy; 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 90 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Launching: Short Stories and Personal Narrative (weeks 1–4)
Writers turn ordinary moments into stories by choosing vivid details and showing how a character changes.

- Week 1: Reading 'Eleven' by Sandra Cisneros; writer's notebook launch
- Week 2: 'The Ransom of Red Chief' by O. Henry; character and plot
- Week 3: 'Thank You, Ma'am' by Langston Hughes; drafting narratives
- Week 4: 'All Summer in a Day' by Ray Bradbury; revising and publishing narratives

### Unit 2: Novel Study: Hatchet and the Survival Story (weeks 5–9)
Characters reveal who they are by how they respond to challenges.

- Week 5: Hatchet chapters 1–4: the crash
- Week 6: Hatchet chapters 5–9: first days of survival
- Week 7: Hatchet chapters 10–13: the new Brian
- Week 8: Hatchet chapters 14–17: the moose and the tornado
- Week 9: Hatchet chapters 18–19 and epilogue; literary essay

### Unit 3: Exploring Early America: Informational Reading and Research (weeks 10–14)
Readers compare multiple accounts and sources to build a fuller, fairer picture of the past.

- Week 10: Informational text structures: navigating the oceans
- Week 11: Multiple accounts: Columbus and the Taíno
- Week 12: Biography: 'And Then What Happened, Paul Revere?' by Jean Fritz; research launch
- Week 13: Research: note-taking and drafting an informative report
- Week 14: Publishing and presenting the research report

### Unit 4: Poetry: Sound, Image and Meaning (weeks 15–17)
Poets choose every word, sound and line break to create images and feeling.

- Week 15: Narrative poetry: 'Paul Revere's Ride' by Henry Wadsworth Longfellow
- Week 16: Lyric poetry: Frost and Dickinson
- Week 17: Poets play with language: Hughes and Carroll; poetry anthology

### Unit 5: Historical Fiction: Esperanza Rising (weeks 18–22)
Historical fiction helps us understand how real events shape ordinary people's lives.

- Week 18: Esperanza Rising: 'Aguascalientes, Mexico 1924' – Las Uvas through Las Guayabas
- Week 19: Esperanza Rising: arrival in California – Los Melones through Las Almendras
- Week 20: Esperanza Rising: Las Ciruelas, Las Papas, Los Aguacates
- Week 21: Esperanza Rising: Los Espárragos, Los Duraznos, Las Uvas
- Week 22: Comparing stories: Esperanza Rising and Hatchet; compare-contrast essay

### Unit 6: Making a Case: Opinion Reading and Writing (weeks 23–26)
A strong opinion is supported by logically ordered reasons and evidence.

- Week 23: Analyzing arguments: reasons and evidence
- Week 24: Planning an opinion essay
- Week 25: Drafting and revising the opinion essay
- Week 26: Publishing and debating

### Unit 7: Classic Literature and Drama: The Secret Garden (weeks 27–30)
Settings and relationships can change characters, and drama brings story structure to life.

- Week 27: The Secret Garden chapters 1–7: Mary arrives at Misselthwaite
- Week 28: The Secret Garden chapters 8–15: the garden and Dickon
- Week 29: The Secret Garden chapters 16–22: Colin's change
- Week 30: The Secret Garden chapters 23–27 and readers' theater

### Unit 8: Myths and Comparing Genres (weeks 31–33)
Myths from many cultures explain the world and teach lessons about human nature.

- Week 31: Greek myths: gods, heroes and lessons
- Week 32: Myths across cultures: comparing themes
- Week 33: Writing an original myth

### Unit 9: Inquiry and Multimedia Presentation (weeks 34–36)
Researchers synthesize sources and use media to teach an audience.

- Week 34: Inquiry: researching a California national park or natural wonder
- Week 35: Building a multimedia presentation
- Week 36: Presentation and year-end portfolio

## Standards

- **L.5.1c** Use verb tense to convey various times, sequences, states, and conditions.
- **L.5.1d** Recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb tense.
- **L.5.1e** Use correlative conjunctions (either/or, neither/nor).
- **L.5.2a** Use punctuation to separate items in a series.
- **L.5.2b** Use a comma to separate an introductory element from the rest of the sentence.
- **L.5.2c** Use a comma to set off yes and no, a tag question, and to indicate direct address.
- **L.5.2d** Use underlining, quotation marks, or italics to indicate titles of works.
- **L.5.2e** Spell grade-appropriate words correctly, consulting references as needed.
- **L.5.3a** Expand, combine, and reduce sentences for meaning, reader/listener interest, and style.

- **L.5.4b** Use common grade-appropriate Greek and Latin affixes and roots as clues to the meaning of a word.
