# Art & Music 5 (Grade 5)

A year of visual art and music that alternates studio and music units and connects to the fifth-grade study of early America. Students master the elements of art and principles of design through drawing, painting, printmaking and sculpture, study artists from Native American traditions to the Hudson River School, and build a portfolio. In music they read rhythmic and melodic notation, play recorder (or a free virtual instrument), sing American folk and patriotic songs, explore the orchestra, and compose with Chrome Music Lab, ending with an exhibition and concert.

*Framework:* 2019 California Arts Standards for Public Schools (Visual Arts and Music); 2020 California Arts Education Framework  
*Time:* 30 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Drawing: Line, Shape, Value and Space (weeks 1–4)
Artists use line, value and perspective to create the illusion of form and depth on a flat surface.

- Week 1: Line and contour drawing
- Week 2: Value and shading
- Week 3: Perspective and space
- Week 4: Drawing project: a detailed observational drawing

### Unit 2: Rhythm, Meter and Beginning Recorder (weeks 5–8)
Music is organized in time through beat, meter and rhythm, which we can read and write.

- Week 5: Beat, meter and note values
- Week 6: Syncopation, dotted rhythms and body percussion
- Week 7: Beginning recorder: B, A, G
- Week 8: Recorder: C, D and E; ensemble playing

### Unit 3: Color and Painting: Landscapes of America (weeks 9–12)
Color choices create mood, and landscape painters shaped how Americans saw their land.

- Week 9: Color theory
- Week 10: The Hudson River School and American landscapes
- Week 11: Painting a landscape
- Week 12: Finishing and critiquing the landscape

### Unit 4: Songs of America (weeks 13–16)
Folk, spiritual and patriotic songs carry the history and ideals of the people who sang them.

- Week 13: Native American and early colonial music
- Week 14: Songs of the Revolution and the nation
- Week 15: Spirituals and work songs
- Week 16: Folk songs of a growing nation

### Unit 5: Art of Early America: Craft, Pattern and Print (weeks 17–20)
Art can preserve culture, tell stories and persuade people.

- Week 17: Native American art: pattern and symbolism
- Week 18: Colonial portraits and folk art
- Week 19: Printmaking and persuasive images
- Week 20: Quilts and collaborative design

### Unit 6: Melody, Harmony and Form (weeks 21–24)
Melodies move by steps and leaps, and harmony and form give music its structure.

- Week 21: Melody: steps, skips and leaps
- Week 22: Major and minor; scales
- Week 23: Harmony: rounds, partner songs and chords
- Week 24: Musical form: AB, ABA, rondo and theme and variations

### Unit 7: Sculpture and Three-Dimensional Form (weeks 25–28)
Sculptors shape space and form, and good craftsmanship makes ideas last.

- Week 25: Form and space: paper sculpture
- Week 26: Clay or salt dough sculpture: planning and building
- Week 27: Finishing and presenting sculpture
- Week 28: Found-object and environmental sculpture

### Unit 8: Instruments of the Orchestra and Composing (weeks 29–32)
Composers choose instruments, timbre and form to express ideas, and anyone can compose.

- Week 29: Instruments of the orchestra
- Week 30: Program music: telling stories with sound
- Week 31: Composing a melody with Chrome Music Lab
- Week 32: Revising and presenting the composition

### Unit 9: Portfolio, Exhibition and Concert (weeks 33–36)
Artists and musicians reflect on growth and share their best work with an audience.

- Week 33: Curating the art portfolio
- Week 34: Preparing the exhibition
- Week 35: Concert preparation
- Week 36: Exhibition and concert night

## Free resources

- [Chrome Music Lab](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/) — Free browser tools (Song Maker, Rhythm, Kandinsky, Chords) for composing and exploring sound
- [Classics for Kids](https://www.classicsforkids.com/) — Free composer biographies, listening episodes and orchestra games
- [Art for Kids Hub](https://www.artforkidshub.com/) — Free step-by-step drawing videos for skill-building warm-ups
- [musictheory.net](https://www.musictheory.net/) — Free lessons and exercises on note reading, rhythm and key signatures
- [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/) — Zoomable images of artworks from the Met, National Gallery, Smithsonian and more

## Standards

- **MU:Cn10.0.5** Demonstrate how interests, knowledge, and skills relate to personal choices and intent when creating, performing, and responding to music.
- **MU:Cn11.0.5** Demonstrate understanding of relationships between music and the other arts, other disciplines, varied contexts, and daily life.
- **MU:Cr1.1.5** Improvise rhythmic, melodic, and harmonic ideas, and explain connection to specific purpose and context.
- **MU:Cr2.1.5** Demonstrate selected and developed musical ideas for improvisations, arrangements, or compositions to express intent, and explain connection to purpose and context.
- **MU:Cr2.1.5b** Use standard and/or iconic notation and/or recording technology to document personal rhythmic, melodic, and two-chord harmonic musical ideas.
- **MU:Cr3.1.5** Evaluate, refine, and document revisions to personal music, applying teacher-provided and collaboratively developed criteria and feedback.
- **MU:Cr3.2.5** Present the final version of personal created music to others that demonstrates craftsmanship, and explain connection to expressive intent.
- **MU:Pr4.1.5** Demonstrate and explain how the selection of music to perform is influenced by personal interest, knowledge, context, and technical skill.
- **MU:Pr4.2.5** Demonstrate understanding of the structure and the elements of music (rhythm, pitch, form, harmony) in music selected for performance.
- **MU:Pr4.2.5b** When analyzing selected music, read and perform using standard notation.
- **MU:Pr4.3.5** Demonstrate and explain how intent is conveyed through interpretive decisions and expressive qualities (dynamics, tempo, timbre, articulation/style).
- **MU:Pr5.1.5** Apply teacher-provided and collaboratively developed criteria and feedback to evaluate accuracy and expressiveness of ensemble and personal performances.
- **MU:Pr6.1.5** Perform music, alone or with others, with expression, technical accuracy, and appropriate interpretation, demonstrating performance decorum and audience etiquette.
- **MU:Re7.1.5** Demonstrate and explain, citing evidence, how selected music connects to and is influenced by specific interests, experiences, purposes, or contexts.
- **MU:Re7.2.5** Demonstrate and explain, citing evidence, how responses to music are informed by the structure, the use of the elements of music, and context.
- **MU:Re8.1.5** Demonstrate and explain how the expressive qualities (dynamics, tempo, timbre, articulation) are used in performers' and personal interpretations to reflect expressive intent.
- **MU:Re9.1.5** Evaluate musical works and performances, applying established criteria, and explain appropriateness to the context, citing evidence from the elements of music.
- **VA:Cn10.1.5** Apply formal and conceptual vocabularies of art and design to view surroundings in new ways through artmaking.
- **VA:Cn11.1.5** Identify how art is used to inform or change beliefs, values, or behaviors of an individual or society.
- **VA:Cr1.1.5** Combine ideas to generate an innovative idea for art-making.
- **VA:Cr1.2.5** Identify and demonstrate diverse methods of artistic investigation to choose an approach for beginning a work of art.
- **VA:Cr2.1.5** Experiment and develop skills in multiple art-making techniques and approaches through practice.
- **VA:Cr2.2.5** Demonstrate quality craftsmanship through care for and use of materials, tools, and equipment.
- **VA:Cr2.3.5** Identify, describe, and visually document places and/or objects of personal significance.
- **VA:Cr3.1.5** Use art vocabulary to describe personal choices in artmaking and to explain revisions to refine work.
- **VA:Pr4.1.5** Define the roles and responsibilities of a curator, explaining the skills and knowledge needed in preserving and presenting artifacts or artwork.
- **VA:Pr5.1.5** Develop a logical argument for safe and effective use of materials and techniques for preparing and presenting artwork.
- **VA:Pr6.1.5** Cite evidence about how an exhibition in a museum or other venue presents ideas and provides information about a specific concept or topic.
- **VA:Re7.1.5** Compare one's own interpretation of a work of art with the interpretation of others.
- **VA:Re7.2.5** Identify messages communicated by images.
- **VA:Re8.1.5** Interpret art by analyzing characteristics of form and structure, contextual information, subject matter, visual elements, and use of media to identify ideas and mood conveyed.
- **VA:Re9.1.5** Recognize differences in criteria used to evaluate works of art depending on styles, genres, and media as well as historical and cultural contexts.
