# United States History and Geography: Making a New Nation (Grade 5)

Fifth-grade U.S. history from the first peoples of North America through European exploration, the thirteen colonies, the American Revolution, the Constitution, and westward expansion to 1850. Following the 2016 CA HSS Framework, each unit is driven by an inquiry question and built on primary sources (journals, letters, maps, laws, engravings and speeches) read alongside multiple perspectives, including American Indian, African American and women's voices. A weekly map strand builds toward locating all 50 states and capitals.

*Framework:* California History–Social Science Content Standards; 2016 California History–Social Science Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Geography and the First Peoples of North America (weeks 1–4)
How did geography and climate shape the ways American Indian nations lived, governed and told their stories?

- Week 1: Geography of North America and map skills
- Week 2: Peoples of the desert Southwest and Pacific Northwest
- Week 3: Peoples of the Great Plains and the Eastern Woodlands
- Week 4: Comparing First Peoples; unit project

### Unit 2: The Age of Exploration (weeks 5–8)
Why did Europeans explore, and what were the consequences of contact for the peoples of the Americas, Africa and Europe?

- Week 5: Why explore? Motives and technology
- Week 6: Columbus and the Spanish in the Americas
- Week 7: French, English and Dutch explorers
- Week 8: The Atlantic world and unit review

### Unit 3: Founding the Thirteen Colonies (weeks 9–13)
Why did people come to the English colonies, and how did place shape colonial life?

- Week 9: Jamestown and the Virginia Colony
- Week 10: Plymouth and the Massachusetts Bay Colony
- Week 11: New England colonies and religious dissent
- Week 12: Middle and Southern colonies
- Week 13: The First Great Awakening and colonial daily life

### Unit 4: Colonial Economy, Slavery and Self-Government (weeks 14–16)
How did colonial society plant seeds of both self-government and slavery?

- Week 14: Colonial economy and the British system
- Week 15: Slavery in the colonies
- Week 16: Seeds of self-government

### Unit 5: Cooperation and Conflict in Colonial North America (weeks 17–19)
How did competition for land and trade lead to cooperation and conflict among colonists and Native nations?

- Week 17: Cooperation: trade, alliances and exchange
- Week 18: Conflict: Powhatan Wars, Pequot War and King Philip's War
- Week 19: The French and Indian War

### Unit 6: The Road to Revolution (weeks 20–23)
Why would colonists break from the most powerful empire in the world?

- Week 20: Taxation without representation
- Week 21: Boston Massacre, Boston Tea Party and the Coercive Acts
- Week 22: The Continental Congresses and the first shots
- Week 23: Common Sense and the Declaration of Independence

### Unit 7: The American Revolution (weeks 24–27)
How did the Patriots win independence, and did the Revolution's ideals apply to everyone?

- Week 24: Early battles and turning points
- Week 25: Allies, heroes and Native nations in the war
- Week 26: Women, families and the home front
- Week 27: Victory at Yorktown and new governments

### Unit 8: Creating the Constitution (weeks 28–31)
How did the Constitution create a government that is both strong and limited?

- Week 28: The Articles of Confederation
- Week 29: The Constitutional Convention
- Week 30: Three branches, checks and balances, and federalism
- Week 31: Ratification, the Bill of Rights and the American creed

### Unit 9: A Growing Nation: Moving West (weeks 32–35)
How did westward expansion change the nation and the lives of American Indians and settlers?

- Week 32: Crossing the Appalachians and new immigrants
- Week 33: The Louisiana Purchase and western explorers
- Week 34: Native nations and removal
- Week 35: The overland trails

### Unit 10: States, Capitals and Year Review (weeks 36–36)
The nation that began as 13 colonies grew into 50 states.

- Week 36: 50 states and capitals; year review

## Standards

- **5.1** Describe the major pre-Columbian settlements, including the cliff dwellers and pueblo people of the desert Southwest, the American Indians of the Pacific Northwest, the nomadic nations of the Great Plains, and the woodland peoples east of the Mississippi.
- **5.1.1** Describe how geography and climate influenced the way various nations lived and adjusted to the natural environment, including locations of villages, structures, and use of resources.
- **5.1.2** Describe their varied customs and folklore traditions.
- **5.1.3** Explain their varied economies and systems of government.
- **5.2** Trace the routes of early explorers and describe the early explorations of the Americas.
- **5.2.1** Describe the entrepreneurial characteristics of early explorers and the technological developments (compass, astrolabe, seaworthy ships) that made sea exploration possible.
- **5.2.2** Explain the aims, obstacles, and accomplishments of explorers, sponsors, and leaders of key European expeditions and the reasons Europeans chose to explore and colonize.
- **5.2.3** Trace the routes of major land explorers of the United States and the Atlantic trade routes linking Africa, the West Indies, the British colonies, and Europe.
- **5.2.4** Locate on maps of North and South America land claimed by Spain, France, England, Portugal, the Netherlands, Sweden, and Russia.
- **5.3** Describe the cooperation and conflict that existed among the American Indians and between the Indian nations and the new settlers.
- **5.3.1** Describe the competition among the English, French, Spanish, Dutch, and Indian nations for control of North America.
- **5.3.2** Describe the cooperation between colonists and Indians during the 1600s and 1700s (agriculture, fur trade, military alliances, treaties, cultural interchanges).
- **5.3.3** Examine the conflicts before the Revolutionary War (Pequot and King Philip's Wars, Powhatan Wars, French and Indian War).
- **5.3.4** Discuss the role of broken treaties and massacres and the factors that led to Indians' defeat, including resistance to encroachment and assimilation.
- **5.3.5** Describe the internecine Indian conflicts, including competing claims for control of lands.
- **5.3.6** Explain the influence and achievements of significant leaders of the time (John Marshall, Andrew Jackson, Tecumseh, Chief Logan, John Ross, Sequoyah).
- **5.4** Understand the political, religious, social, and economic institutions that evolved in the colonial era.
- **5.4.1** Understand the influence of location and physical setting on the founding of the original 13 colonies and identify on a map the colonies and the American Indian nations already there.
- **5.4.2** Identify the major individuals and groups responsible for founding the colonies and their reasons (John Smith, Roger Williams, William Penn, Lord Baltimore, William Bradford, John Winthrop).
- **5.4.3** Describe the religious aspects of the earliest colonies (Puritanism, Anglicanism, Catholicism, Quakerism).
- **5.4.4** Identify the significance and leaders of the First Great Awakening.
- **5.4.5** Understand how the British colonial period created the basis for self-government and a free-market economy, and compare British, Spanish, and French colonial systems.
- **5.4.6** Describe the introduction of slavery into America, the responses of enslaved families, the struggle between proponents and opponents of slavery, and its institutionalization in the South.
- **5.4.7** Explain the early democratic ideas and practices of the colonial period, including representative assemblies and town meetings.
- **5.5** Explain the causes of the American Revolution.
- **5.5.1** Understand how political, religious, and economic ideas and interests brought about the Revolution (Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, taxes on tea, Coercive Acts).
- **5.5.2** Know the significance of the First and Second Continental Congresses and the Committees of Correspondence.
- **5.5.3** Understand the people and events associated with drafting and signing the Declaration of Independence and its key political concepts and significance.
- **5.5.4** Describe the views, lives, and impact of key individuals (King George III, Patrick Henry, Thomas Jefferson, George Washington, Benjamin Franklin, John Adams).
- **5.6** Understand the course and consequences of the American Revolution.
- **5.6.1** Identify and map the major battles, campaigns, and turning points of the Revolutionary War, the roles of American and British leaders, and Indian alliances on both sides.
- **5.6.2** Describe the contributions of France and other nations and individuals (Franklin, Lafayette, Kosciuszko, von Steuben) to the outcome of the Revolution.
- **5.6.3** Identify the different roles women played during the Revolution (Abigail Adams, Martha Washington, Molly Pitcher, Phillis Wheatley, Mercy Otis Warren).
- **5.6.4** Understand the personal impact and economic hardship on families caused by the war, including shortages and inflation.
- **5.6.5** Explain how state constitutions established after 1776 embodied the ideals of the Revolution and served as models for the U.S. Constitution.
- **5.6.6** Demonstrate knowledge of the land policies of the Continental Congress (Land Ordinance of 1785, Northwest Ordinance of 1787) and their impact on American Indians' land.
- **5.6.7** Understand how the ideals of the Declaration of Independence changed the way people viewed slavery.
- **5.7** Describe the people and events associated with the development of the U.S. Constitution and analyze its significance as the foundation of the American republic.
- **5.7.1** List the shortcomings of the Articles of Confederation as set forth by their critics.
- **5.7.2** Explain the significance of the Constitution of 1787, the struggles over its ratification, and the reasons for adding the Bill of Rights.
- **5.7.3** Understand the fundamental principles of American constitutional democracy, including government power from the people and the primacy of individual liberty.
- **5.7.4** Understand how the Constitution both empowers and limits the central government, and compare the powers of citizens, Congress, the president, the Supreme Court, and the states.
- **5.7.5** Discuss the meaning of the American creed: safeguarding individual liberty within a unified nation, respecting the rule of law, and preserving the Constitution.
- **5.7.6** Know the songs that express American ideals (e.g., 'America the Beautiful,' 'The Star-Spangled Banner').
- **5.8** Trace the colonization, immigration, and settlement patterns of the American people from 1789 to the mid-1800s.
- **5.8.1** Discuss the waves of immigrants from Europe between 1789 and 1850 and their modes of transportation (wagons, canals, flatboats, steamboats, Cumberland Gap).
- **5.8.2** Name the states and territories that existed in 1850, their locations, and major geographical features.
- **5.8.3** Demonstrate knowledge of the explorations of the trans-Mississippi West following the Louisiana Purchase (Lewis and Clark, Zebulon Pike, John Fremont).
- **5.8.4** Discuss the experiences of settlers on the overland trails to the West, including the influence of terrain, rivers, vegetation, and climate.
- **5.9** Know the location of the current 50 states and the names of their capitals.
