# English 9 (Grade 9)

English 9 builds the reading, writing, speaking, and language skills of the CA CCSS grades 9–10 band through short stories, poetry, rhetoric, Homer's Odyssey, Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet, and To Kill a Mockingbird. Students write a personal narrative, literary analyses, an argument essay, and a sourced research paper, and practice discussion, debate, recitation, and presentation. Weekly grammar and vocabulary lessons are applied directly to student writing.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts & Literacy (grades 9–10); 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* b · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: The Short Story and Personal Narrative (weeks 1–4)
Writers shape conflict, suspense, and point of view to pull readers through a story — and we can use the same tools to tell our own.

- Week 1: Plot, conflict, and suspense in "The Most Dangerous Game"
- Week 2: Point of view and mood: Poe's "The Cask of Amontillado"
- Week 3: Irony and craft in "The Gift of the Magi"; planning a personal narrative
- Week 4: Drafting, revising, and publishing a personal narrative

### Unit 2: Literary Analysis: Character, Theme, and Structure (weeks 5–8)
Theme emerges from what characters want, choose, and learn; a strong analysis proves a claim about that with evidence.

- Week 5: Character and theme in "The Necklace"
- Week 6: Structure and time: "The Monkey's Paw" and "An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge"
- Week 7: Satire and voice: "Harrison Bergeron" and "The Story of an Hour"
- Week 8: Writing the literary analysis essay

### Unit 3: Rhetoric and Argument (weeks 9–12)
Speakers and writers persuade through claims, evidence, and appeals; strong arguments anticipate and answer the other side.

- Week 9: Rhetorical appeals in famous speeches
- Week 10: Argument structure: "Letter from Birmingham Jail"
- Week 11: Seminal U.S. documents and evaluating arguments
- Week 12: Writing and debating an argument essay

### Unit 4: Poetry: Sound, Image, and Form (weeks 13–15)
Poets compress meaning into imagery, figurative language, sound, and form.

- Week 13: Figurative language and imagery
- Week 14: Sound and form: sonnets and the Harlem Renaissance
- Week 15: Poetry analysis, original poem, and recitation

### Unit 5: The Epic Hero: Homer's Odyssey (weeks 16–21)
The Odyssey asks what makes a hero and how cultures pass down values through story.

- Week 16: Introduction to the epic: The Odyssey, Books 1 and 5
- Week 17: The Odyssey: the Cyclops and Circe (Books 9–10)
- Week 18: The Odyssey: the Underworld and the Sirens (Books 11–12); semester review
- Week 19: The Odyssey: homecoming (Books 13–16)
- Week 20: The Odyssey: the suitors and the bow (Books 17–23)
- Week 21: Transforming source material: Tennyson's "Ulysses"; epic hero essay

### Unit 6: Drama: Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet (weeks 22–27)
Shakespeare's tragedy shows how choices, haste, and conflict between groups drive characters to disaster.

- Week 22: Shakespeare's world; Romeo and Juliet, Prologue and Act 1
- Week 23: Romeo and Juliet, Act 2: the balcony and the secret marriage
- Week 24: Romeo and Juliet, Act 3: the turning point
- Week 25: Romeo and Juliet, Act 4: plans and dramatic irony
- Week 26: Romeo and Juliet, Act 5 and film adaptation
- Week 27: Romeo and Juliet argument essay and scene performance

### Unit 7: The Novel: To Kill a Mockingbird (weeks 28–32)
Courage, empathy, and justice are tested when a community confronts its own prejudice.

- Week 28: Historical context and Maycomb: To Kill a Mockingbird, Chapters 1–6
- Week 29: Courage and conscience: Chapters 7–11
- Week 30: The trial: Chapters 12–19
- Week 31: Verdict and aftermath: Chapters 20–26
- Week 32: Resolution and theme essay: Chapters 27–31

### Unit 8: Research and Presentation (weeks 33–36)
Good research starts with a real question, weighs sources for credibility, and shares findings clearly with an audience.

- Week 33: Research questions and credible sources
- Week 34: Note-taking, outlining, and drafting the research paper
- Week 35: Revision and multimedia presentation
- Week 36: Presentations, portfolio reflection, and final exam

## Standards

- **L.9-10.1** Demonstrate command of standard English grammar and usage, including parallel structure and varied phrases and clauses.
- **L.9-10.2** Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation (semicolons, colons), and spelling.
- **L.9-10.3** Apply knowledge of language to make effective choices for meaning or style; write and edit work to conform to a style manual (e.g., MLA).
- **L.9-10.4** Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, word parts, and reference materials.
- **L.9-10.5** Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and nuances in word meanings.
- **L.9-10.6** Acquire and use accurately general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RI.9-10.1** Cite strong, thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what an informational text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RI.9-10.10** Read and comprehend literary nonfiction in the grades 9–10 complexity band proficiently.
- **RI.9-10.2** Determine a central idea of an informational text, analyze its development, and provide an objective summary.
- **RI.9-10.3** Analyze how an author unfolds an analysis or series of ideas or events and the connections drawn between them.
- **RI.9-10.4** Determine the figurative, connotative, and technical meanings of words in informational text and their impact on meaning and tone.
- **RI.9-10.5** Analyze in detail how an author's ideas or claims are developed and refined by particular sentences, paragraphs, or sections.
- **RI.9-10.6** Determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze how rhetoric advances it.
- **RI.9-10.7** Analyze various accounts of a subject told in different mediums and determine which details are emphasized in each.
- **RI.9-10.8** Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing reasoning and evidence and identifying false statements and fallacies.
- **RI.9-10.9** Analyze seminal U.S. documents of historical and literary significance, including how they address related themes and concepts.
- **RL.9-10.1** Cite strong, thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what a literary text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RL.9-10.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas, and poems, in the grades 9–10 complexity band proficiently.
- **RL.9-10.2** Determine a theme of a text, analyze its development, and provide an objective summary.
- **RL.9-10.3** Analyze how complex characters develop, interact with others, and advance the plot or theme.
- **RL.9-10.4** Determine figurative and connotative meanings of words and analyze the cumulative impact of word choice on meaning and tone.
- **RL.9-10.5** Analyze how an author's choices about structure, order of events, and manipulation of time create effects such as mystery, tension, or surprise.
- **RL.9-10.6** Analyze a particular point of view or cultural experience reflected in a work of literature from outside the United States.
- **RL.9-10.7** Analyze the representation of a subject or key scene in two different artistic mediums.
- **RL.9-10.9** Analyze how an author draws on and transforms source material in a specific work.
- **SL.9-10.1** Initiate and participate effectively in a range of collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing one's own clearly and persuasively.
- **SL.9-10.2** Integrate multiple sources of information presented in diverse media or formats, evaluating credibility and accuracy.
- **SL.9-10.3** Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric.
- **SL.9-10.4** Present information, findings, and supporting evidence clearly, concisely, and logically.
- **SL.9-10.5** Make strategic use of digital media in presentations to enhance understanding and add interest.
- **SL.9-10.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when appropriate.
- **W.9-10.1** Write arguments to support claims using valid reasoning and relevant, sufficient evidence, including counterclaims.
- **W.9-10.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences.
- **W.9-10.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine and convey complex ideas clearly and accurately.
- **W.9-10.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.9-10.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience.
- **W.9-10.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach.
- **W.9-10.6** Use technology, including the Internet, to produce, publish, and update writing products.
- **W.9-10.7** Conduct short and sustained research projects to answer a question or solve a problem, synthesizing multiple sources.
- **W.9-10.8** Gather relevant information from multiple sources, assess usefulness, integrate information, avoid plagiarism, and follow a standard citation format.
- **W.9-10.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection, and research.
