# Algebra I (Grade 9)

Algebra I follows the CA CCSS traditional pathway: expressions, equations and inequalities, functions, linear and exponential models, systems, statistics, polynomials, and quadratics. Students model real data from home investigations (candle burning, coin decay, projectile tosses) and use Desmos for graphing and regression. Each unit ends with a test, with semester and final exams.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (Algebra I, traditional pathway); 2023 CA Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* c · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Expressions, Quantities, and the Real Numbers (weeks 1–3)
Expressions and units describe real quantities; the structure of an expression tells a story.

- Week 1: Real numbers and properties
- Week 2: Interpreting algebraic expressions
- Week 3: Units, quantities, and measurement accuracy

### Unit 2: Linear Equations and Inequalities in One Variable (weeks 4–7)
Solving is a chain of reasoned, reversible steps that keep both sides equivalent.

- Week 4: Solving multi-step linear equations
- Week 5: Writing equations to solve problems; literal equations
- Week 6: Linear inequalities in one variable
- Week 7: Absolute value equations and inequalities; unit review

### Unit 3: Functions and Their Representations (weeks 8–10)
A function pairs each input with exactly one output and can be shown with words, tables, graphs, and equations.

- Week 8: What is a function?
- Week 9: Interpreting graphs and key features
- Week 10: Sequences as functions

### Unit 4: Linear Functions and Modeling (weeks 11–14)
Linear functions model constant rate of change; slope and intercept have meaning in context.

- Week 11: Slope and rate of change
- Week 12: Writing and graphing linear equations
- Week 13: Linear models, transformations, and inverses
- Week 14: Linear modeling project

### Unit 5: Systems of Equations and Inequalities (weeks 15–18)
Systems find values that satisfy several conditions at once; inequalities describe regions of possibilities.

- Week 15: Solving systems by graphing and substitution
- Week 16: Solving systems by elimination
- Week 17: Linear inequalities in two variables and systems of inequalities
- Week 18: Semester review and exam

### Unit 6: Statistics: One- and Two-Variable Data (weeks 19–21)
Data analysis uses shape, center, spread, and association to make claims — but correlation is not causation.

- Week 19: Representing and summarizing one-variable data
- Week 20: Comparing distributions and categorical data
- Week 21: Scatter plots, regression, and correlation

### Unit 7: Exponents and Exponential Functions (weeks 22–24)
Exponential functions grow or decay by a constant factor and eventually outpace linear growth.

- Week 22: Properties of exponents and radicals
- Week 23: Exponential growth and decay
- Week 24: Exponential models and comparing growth

### Unit 8: Polynomials and Factoring (weeks 25–27)
Polynomials behave like integers: we can add, multiply, and factor them by using structure.

- Week 25: Adding, subtracting, and multiplying polynomials
- Week 26: Factoring polynomials
- Week 27: Factoring practice and Unit 8 test

### Unit 9: Quadratic Functions and Their Graphs (weeks 28–30)
Quadratics model area and projectile motion; their forms reveal vertex, zeros, and symmetry.

- Week 28: Graphing quadratic functions
- Week 29: Forms of quadratic functions
- Week 30: Quadratic modeling: projectiles and area

### Unit 10: Solving Quadratic Equations (weeks 31–34)
Every quadratic can be solved by completing the square, which leads to the quadratic formula.

- Week 31: Solving quadratics by factoring and square roots
- Week 32: Completing the square and the quadratic formula
- Week 33: Choosing methods and quadratic applications
- Week 34: Linear-quadratic systems and Unit 10 test

### Unit 11: Other Functions and Course Review (weeks 35–36)
Absolute value, piecewise, and square root functions extend our toolkit of models.

- Week 35: Absolute value, piecewise, and square root functions
- Week 36: Final review and exam

## Standards

- **A-APR.1** Understand that polynomials are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication; add, subtract, and multiply polynomials.
- **A-APR.3** Identify zeros of polynomials when suitable factorizations are available and use them to sketch a graph.
- **A-CED.1** Create equations and inequalities in one variable and use them to solve problems.
- **A-CED.2** Create equations in two or more variables to represent relationships; graph them on coordinate axes.
- **A-CED.3** Represent constraints by equations or inequalities and systems; interpret solutions as viable or nonviable.
- **A-CED.4** Rearrange formulas to highlight a quantity of interest.
- **A-REI.1** Explain each step in solving a simple equation and construct a viable argument to justify a solution method.
- **A-REI.10** Understand that the graph of an equation in two variables is the set of all its solutions plotted in the coordinate plane.
- **A-REI.11** Explain why the x-coordinates of intersection points of y=f(x) and y=g(x) are solutions of f(x)=g(x); find solutions approximately.
- **A-REI.12** Graph the solutions to a linear inequality in two variables as a half-plane and graph solution sets of systems of linear inequalities.
- **A-REI.3** Solve linear equations and inequalities in one variable, including those with literal coefficients.
- **A-REI.3.1** (CA) Solve equations and inequalities involving absolute value.
- **A-REI.4** Solve quadratic equations in one variable by inspection, square roots, completing the square, the quadratic formula, and factoring.
- **A-REI.5** Prove that replacing one equation in a system by the sum of it and a multiple of the other produces a system with the same solutions.
- **A-REI.6** Solve systems of linear equations exactly and approximately, focusing on pairs of linear equations in two variables.
- **A-REI.7** Solve a simple system consisting of a linear equation and a quadratic equation in two variables algebraically and graphically.
- **A-SSE.1** Interpret expressions that represent a quantity in terms of its context (terms, factors, coefficients).
- **A-SSE.2** Use the structure of an expression to identify ways to rewrite it.
- **A-SSE.3** Choose and produce an equivalent form of an expression (factor, complete the square, transform exponentials) to reveal properties of a quantity.
- **F-BF.1** Write a function that describes a relationship between two quantities, including explicit and recursive expressions.
- **F-BF.2** Write arithmetic and geometric sequences recursively and explicitly and use them to model situations.
- **F-BF.3** Identify the effect on the graph of replacing f(x) by f(x)+k, kf(x), f(kx), and f(x+k).
- **F-BF.4** Find inverse functions by solving f(x)=c for a simple function.
- **F-IF.1** Understand that a function assigns to each element of the domain exactly one element of the range; use function notation.
- **F-IF.2** Use function notation, evaluate functions for inputs in their domains, and interpret statements in context.
- **F-IF.3** Recognize that sequences are functions whose domain is a subset of the integers.
- **F-IF.4** Interpret key features of graphs and tables (intercepts, intervals of increase/decrease, maxima/minima, symmetry) in context.
- **F-IF.5** Relate the domain of a function to its graph and to the quantitative relationship it describes.
- **F-IF.6** Calculate and interpret the average rate of change of a function over a specified interval.
- **F-IF.7** Graph functions expressed symbolically and show key features (linear, quadratic, square root, piecewise, absolute value, exponential).
- **F-IF.8** Write a function in different but equivalent forms to reveal and explain properties (factoring/completing the square; exponential growth/decay).
- **F-IF.9** Compare properties of two functions each represented in a different way.
- **F-LE.1** Distinguish between situations that can be modeled with linear functions and with exponential functions.
- **F-LE.2** Construct linear and exponential functions given a graph, a description, or input-output pairs.
- **F-LE.3** Observe that a quantity increasing exponentially eventually exceeds a quantity increasing linearly or quadratically.
- **F-LE.5** Interpret the parameters in a linear or exponential function in terms of a context.
- **F-LE.6** (CA) Apply quadratic functions to physical problems such as the motion of an object under gravity.
- **N-Q.1** Use units to understand problems and guide multi-step solutions; choose and interpret units and scales in graphs and formulas.
- **N-Q.2** Define appropriate quantities for the purpose of descriptive modeling.
- **N-Q.3** Choose a level of accuracy appropriate to limitations on measurement when reporting quantities.
- **N-RN.1** Explain how the meaning of rational exponents follows from extending the properties of integer exponents.
- **N-RN.2** Rewrite expressions involving radicals and rational exponents using the properties of exponents.
- **N-RN.3** Explain why sums and products of rational and irrational numbers are rational or irrational.
- **S-ID.1** Represent data with plots on the real number line (dot plots, histograms, box plots).
- **S-ID.2** Use statistics appropriate to the shape of the data to compare center and spread of two or more data sets.
- **S-ID.3** Interpret differences in shape, center, and spread in context, accounting for outliers.
- **S-ID.5** Summarize categorical data for two categories in two-way frequency tables; interpret relative frequencies.
- **S-ID.6** Represent data on two quantitative variables on a scatter plot and describe how they are related; fit a function to the data.
- **S-ID.7** Interpret the slope and intercept of a linear model in the context of the data.
- **S-ID.8** Compute (using technology) and interpret the correlation coefficient of a linear fit.
- **S-ID.9** Distinguish between correlation and causation.
