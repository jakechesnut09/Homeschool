# World Geography (S1) & Ethnic Studies (S2) (Grade 9)

Semester 1 (World Geography) builds spatial thinking through maps, GIS, physical geography, population and migration, culture and political borders, economic development, and California case studies. Semester 2 (Ethnic Studies) follows the 2021 CA Ethnic Studies Model Curriculum, examining identity and the histories, struggles, and contributions of Native American, African American, Chicana/o and Latina/o, and Asian American and Pacific Islander communities in California through primary sources, ending in a community action project.

*Framework:* CA History–Social Science Content Standards (Historical and Social Sciences Analysis Skills, grades 9–12); 2016 CA HSS Framework (grade 9 electives); National Geography Standards; 2021 CA Ethnic Studies Model Curriculum  
*Time:* 50 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* g · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: The Geographer's Toolkit (weeks 1–3)
Geographers ask where things are and why there, using maps, data, and spatial thinking.

- Week 1: Thinking like a geographer: the five themes
- Week 2: Maps, projections, and geospatial technology
- Week 3: Regions

### Unit 2: Physical Geography: Landforms, Climate, and Biomes (weeks 4–6)
Physical processes shape Earth's surface and climates, which in turn shape where and how people live.

- Week 4: Plate tectonics and landforms
- Week 5: Weather, climate, and climate regions
- Week 6: Biomes and human-environment interaction

### Unit 3: Population and Migration (weeks 7–9)
Population patterns and migration flows result from push and pull factors and change places and people.

- Week 7: Population distribution and growth
- Week 8: Migration
- Week 9: Settlement patterns

### Unit 4: Cultural and Political Geography (weeks 10–12)
Language, religion, and borders shape identity and are sources of both cooperation and conflict.

- Week 10: Culture, language, and religion
- Week 11: Political geography: states, borders, and conflict
- Week 12: Supranational organizations and cooperation

### Unit 5: Economic Geography, Development, and Resources (weeks 13–15)
Resources, trade, and development link places unequally across the globe.

- Week 13: Economic geography and development
- Week 14: Resources, trade, and supply chains
- Week 15: Human impact on environments: California water case studies

### Unit 6: Cities, Globalization, and California's Regions (weeks 16–18)
Urbanization and globalization reshape places — California is a case study of a global region.

- Week 16: Urbanization and urban challenges
- Week 17: Globalization and California as a global region
- Week 18: Country geography profile and semester exam

### Unit 7: Foundations of Ethnic Studies: Identity (weeks 19–21)
Identity is shaped by family, community, history, and how society names and treats groups.

- Week 19: What is Ethnic Studies? Identity and community
- Week 20: Stereotypes, bias, and the levels of oppression
- Week 21: Family history and oral history methods

### Unit 8: Native American Studies (weeks 22–24)
California's Native peoples have survived colonization through resistance, cultural continuity, and sovereignty movements.

- Week 22: California Native peoples before and during the Mission era
- Week 23: Gold Rush, statehood, and Native survival
- Week 24: Native activism and sovereignty

### Unit 9: African American Studies (weeks 25–27)
African Americans have resisted slavery, segregation, and discrimination and shaped American democracy.

- Week 25: African resistance to slavery and the meaning of freedom
- Week 26: Segregation, the Great Migration, and civil rights
- Week 27: African American culture and contemporary issues

### Unit 10: Chicana/o and Latina/o Studies (weeks 28–30)
Borders, labor, and education rights shape the history of Mexican Americans and Latinos in California.

- Week 28: Borders and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo
- Week 29: Labor and the farmworker movement
- Week 30: Education rights and Chicano movement

### Unit 11: Asian American and Pacific Islander Studies (weeks 31–33)
Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders have challenged exclusion laws and built communities across California.

- Week 31: Chinese immigration and exclusion
- Week 32: Japanese American incarceration
- Week 33: Filipino, Southeast Asian, and Pacific Islander communities

### Unit 12: Social Movements and Community Action (weeks 34–36)
Communities have built coalitions for justice; students can research and act on issues in their own communities.

- Week 34: Coalitions and multiracial social movements
- Week 35: Community action project: research and planning
- Week 36: Community action presentation and final reflection

## Standards

- **ESMC-1** (CA Ethnic Studies Model Curriculum guiding value 1) Cultivate empathy, community, self-worth, self-determination, and the holistic well-being of all participants.
- **ESMC-2** (ESMC 2) Honor Native peoples of the land and communities of color by sharing their stories of struggle and resistance and their intellectual and cultural wealth.
- **ESMC-3** (ESMC 3) Value pre-colonial and ancestral knowledge, narratives, and communal experiences of Native peoples and people of color.
- **ESMC-4** (ESMC 4) Critically examine systems of power, including colonialism and racism, and their historical and present effects.
- **ESMC-5** (ESMC 5) Challenge discriminatory beliefs and practices on ideological, institutional, interpersonal, and internalized levels.
- **ESMC-6** (ESMC 6) Connect to past and contemporary social movements that struggle for social justice and an equitable, democratic society.
- **ESMC-7** (ESMC 7) Imagine and build new possibilities that promote critical hope, collective narratives, and civic engagement.
- **ESMC-T1** (ESMC theme) Identity: how individual and group identities are formed, named, and expressed.
- **ESMC-T2** (ESMC theme) History and Movement: the histories, migrations, and contributions of ethnic groups in California and the U.S.
- **ESMC-T3** (ESMC theme) Systems of Power: how laws, policies, and institutions have shaped opportunity and exclusion.
- **ESMC-T4** (ESMC theme) Social Movements and Equity: how communities organize for civil rights and justice.
- **HSS-CST-1** (CA HSS Analysis Skills 9–12, Chronological & Spatial Thinking 1) Compare the present with the past, evaluating consequences of past events and decisions.
- **HSS-CST-2** (CST 2) Analyze how change happens at different rates at different times; some aspects can change while others stay the same.
- **HSS-CST-3** (CST 3) Use a variety of maps and documents to interpret human movement, including migration, trade, and the diffusion of ideas, technology, and goods.
- **HSS-CST-4** (CST 4) Relate current events to the physical and human characteristics of places and regions.
- **HSS-HI-1** (Historical Interpretation 1) Show the connections, causal and otherwise, between particular historical events and larger social, economic, and political trends.
- **HSS-HI-2** (HI 2) Recognize the complexity of historical causes and effects, including the limitations on determining cause and effect.
- **HSS-HI-3** (HI 3) Interpret past events and issues within the context in which they unfolded rather than solely in terms of present-day norms.
- **HSS-HI-6** (HI 6) Analyze human modifications of landscapes and examine the resulting environmental policy issues.
- **HSS-HR-1** (Historical Research, Evidence & Point of View 1) Distinguish valid from fallacious arguments in historical interpretations.
- **HSS-HR-2** (HR 2) Identify bias and prejudice in historical interpretations.
- **HSS-HR-3** (HR 3) Evaluate major debates among historians concerning alternative interpretations of the past.
- **HSS-HR-4** (HR 4) Construct and test hypotheses; collect, evaluate, and employ information from multiple primary and secondary sources; apply it in oral and written presentations.
- **NGS-1** (National Geography Standards) Use maps and other geographic representations, geospatial technologies, and spatial thinking to understand and communicate information.
- **NGS-10** Describe the characteristics, distribution, and complexity of Earth's cultural mosaics.
- **NGS-11** Explain the patterns and networks of economic interdependence on Earth's surface.
- **NGS-12** Explain the processes, patterns, and functions of human settlement.
- **NGS-13** Explain how forces of cooperation and conflict among people influence the division and control of Earth's surface.
- **NGS-14** Explain how human actions modify the physical environment.
- **NGS-15** Explain how physical systems affect human systems.
- **NGS-16** Explain changes in the meaning, use, distribution, and importance of resources.
- **NGS-18** Apply geography to interpret the present and plan for the future.
- **NGS-3** Analyze the spatial organization of people, places, and environments on Earth's surface.
- **NGS-4** Describe the physical and human characteristics of places.
- **NGS-5** Explain how people create regions to interpret Earth's complexity.
- **NGS-7** Explain the physical processes that shape the patterns of Earth's surface.
- **NGS-8** Describe the characteristics and spatial distribution of ecosystems and biomes.
- **NGS-9** Analyze the characteristics, distribution, and migration of human populations.
