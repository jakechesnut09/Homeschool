# Biology – The Living Earth (Grade 9)

The Living Earth is the CA three-course-model high school biology course integrating life science with Earth science. Students study ecosystems, photosynthesis and respiration in the context of Earth's atmosphere and carbon cycle, evolution, inheritance, cell structure and homeostasis, and human impacts on biodiversity. Weekly hands-on investigations use household materials (yeast, spinach leaf disks, strawberries, eggs), and the year ends with an engineering design project.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (HS Life Science with integrated Earth & Space Science); 2016 CA Science Framework (The Living Earth course)  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* d · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Ecosystems: Interactions, Energy, and Populations (weeks 1–5)
Populations are limited by resources and interactions; energy flows and matter cycles through food webs.

- Week 1: Science practices and ecosystem components
- Week 2: Population growth and carrying capacity
- Week 3: Biodiversity and species interactions
- Week 4: Energy flow and matter cycling in food webs
- Week 5: Group behavior and ecosystem stability

### Unit 2: Photosynthesis, Respiration, and Earth's Changing Atmosphere (weeks 6–10)
Life transformed Earth's atmosphere; photosynthesis and respiration move energy and carbon through Earth's spheres.

- Week 6: Early Earth and the oxygen revolution
- Week 7: Photosynthesis
- Week 8: Cellular respiration and fermentation
- Week 9: Building biomolecules from sugar
- Week 10: The carbon cycle and Earth's spheres

### Unit 3: Evolution: Evidence of Common Ancestry and Diversity (weeks 11–18)
Natural selection acting on heritable variation explains both the unity and the diversity of life.

- Week 11: Evidence for evolution: fossils and the geologic record
- Week 12: Evidence for evolution: anatomy, embryology, and molecules
- Week 13: Natural selection
- Week 14: Natural selection and probability
- Week 15: Mechanisms of evolution beyond selection
- Week 16: Speciation
- Week 17: Extinction and changing environments
- Week 18: Semester review and exam

### Unit 4: Inheritance and Variation of Traits (weeks 19–24)
DNA codes for proteins that produce traits; meiosis and mutation create the variation that inheritance passes on.

- Week 19: DNA structure and replication
- Week 20: From DNA to protein
- Week 21: Mitosis, meiosis, and variation
- Week 22: Mendelian genetics and probability
- Week 23: Non-Mendelian inheritance and human traits
- Week 24: Mutations and genetic technology

### Unit 5: Structure, Function, and Growth: From Cells to Organisms (weeks 25–30)
Organisms are hierarchies of interacting systems that keep internal conditions stable through feedback.

- Week 25: Cell structure and function
- Week 26: Cell membranes and transport
- Week 27: Levels of organization and body systems
- Week 28: Homeostasis and feedback
- Week 29: Growth, differentiation, and stem cells
- Week 30: Nutrition, enzymes, and Unit 5 test

### Unit 6: Ecosystem Stability and Human Impact (weeks 31–36)
Human activities alter ecosystems and Earth systems, and engineering solutions can reduce harm to biodiversity.

- Week 31: Human impacts on ecosystems
- Week 32: Climate change and Earth systems
- Week 33: Engineering solutions for biodiversity: defining the problem
- Week 34: Designing and simulating solutions
- Week 35: Evaluating solutions and presenting
- Week 36: Final review and exam

## Standards

- **HS-ESS2-6** Develop a quantitative model to describe the cycling of carbon among the hydrosphere, atmosphere, geosphere, and biosphere.
- **HS-ESS2-7** Construct an argument based on evidence about the simultaneous coevolution of Earth's systems and life on Earth.
- **HS-ESS3-6** Use a computational representation to illustrate the relationships among Earth systems and how those relationships are being modified by human activity.
- **HS-ETS1-1** Analyze a major global challenge to specify qualitative and quantitative criteria and constraints for solutions.
- **HS-ETS1-2** Design a solution to a complex real-world problem by breaking it down into smaller, more manageable problems.
- **HS-ETS1-3** Evaluate a solution to a complex real-world problem based on prioritized criteria and trade-offs.
- **HS-LS1-1** Construct an explanation for how the structure of DNA determines the structure of proteins, which carry out essential functions through specialized cells.
- **HS-LS1-2** Develop and use a model to illustrate the hierarchical organization of interacting systems that provide specific functions in multicellular organisms.
- **HS-LS1-3** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence that feedback mechanisms maintain homeostasis.
- **HS-LS1-4** Use a model to illustrate the role of cellular division (mitosis) and differentiation in producing and maintaining complex organisms.
- **HS-LS1-5** Use a model to illustrate how photosynthesis transforms light energy into stored chemical energy.
- **HS-LS1-6** Construct an explanation for how carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen from sugar molecules combine with other elements to form amino acids and other large carbon-based molecules.
- **HS-LS1-7** Use a model to illustrate that cellular respiration is a chemical process in which bonds are broken and new compounds formed, resulting in a net transfer of energy.
- **HS-LS2-1** Use mathematical representations to support explanations of factors that affect carrying capacity of ecosystems at different scales.
- **HS-LS2-2** Use mathematical representations to support and revise explanations about factors affecting biodiversity and populations in ecosystems.
- **HS-LS2-3** Construct and revise an explanation for the cycling of matter and flow of energy in aerobic and anaerobic conditions.
- **HS-LS2-4** Use mathematical representations to support claims for the cycling of matter and flow of energy among organisms in an ecosystem.
- **HS-LS2-5** Develop a model to illustrate the role of photosynthesis and cellular respiration in the cycling of carbon among the biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, and geosphere.
- **HS-LS2-6** Evaluate claims, evidence, and reasoning that complex ecosystem interactions maintain relatively consistent numbers of organisms in stable conditions, but changing conditions may result in a new ecosystem.
- **HS-LS2-7** Design, evaluate, and refine a solution for reducing the impacts of human activities on the environment and biodiversity.
- **HS-LS2-8** Evaluate evidence for the role of group behavior on individual and species' chances to survive and reproduce.
- **HS-LS3-1** Ask questions to clarify relationships about the role of DNA and chromosomes in coding the instructions for characteristic traits passed from parents to offspring.
- **HS-LS3-2** Make and defend a claim that inheritable genetic variations may result from new combinations through meiosis, viable errors in replication, and/or mutations caused by environmental factors.
- **HS-LS3-3** Apply concepts of statistics and probability to explain the variation and distribution of expressed traits in a population.
- **HS-LS4-1** Communicate scientific information that common ancestry and biological evolution are supported by multiple lines of empirical evidence.
- **HS-LS4-2** Construct an explanation that evolution results primarily from population potential to increase, heritable genetic variation, competition for limited resources, and proliferation of better-adapted organisms.
- **HS-LS4-3** Apply concepts of statistics and probability to support explanations that organisms with an advantageous heritable trait tend to increase in proportion.
- **HS-LS4-4** Construct an explanation based on evidence for how natural selection leads to adaptation of populations.
- **HS-LS4-5** Evaluate evidence supporting claims that changes in environmental conditions may result in increases in species, emergence of new species, and extinction.
- **HS-LS4-6** Create or revise a simulation to test a solution to mitigate adverse impacts of human activity on biodiversity.
