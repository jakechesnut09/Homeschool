# Science 1 (NGSS) (Grade 1)

First-grade science follows the California NGSS: students investigate how vibrations make sound and how light lets us see, cast shadows, and pass through materials, then design devices that communicate with light and sound. They track patterns of the sun, moon, stars and daylight, explore how plant and animal parts help them survive (and inspire human inventions), and compare parents and offspring. Every unit includes hands-on investigations with household materials and simple engineering design challenges.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards; 2016 CA Science Framework  
*Time:* 30 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Scientists and Engineers at Work (weeks 1–3)
Scientists ask questions about the world; engineers solve problems by designing, testing and improving.

- Week 1: What scientists do: observing and asking questions
- Week 2: What engineers do: defining problems and designing
- Week 3: Test, compare, improve

### Unit 2: Sound and Vibration (weeks 4–8)
Sound is made by vibrating objects, and sound can make things vibrate.

- Week 4: Vibrations make sound
- Week 5: Sound makes things vibrate
- Week 6: Loud and soft, high and low
- Week 7: Communicating with sound over distance
- Week 8: Sound unit wrap-up

### Unit 3: Light, Darkness and Shadows (weeks 9–13)
We see objects only when light shines on them; materials let all, some, or no light through.

- Week 9: We see when there is light
- Week 10: Transparent, translucent, opaque
- Week 11: Shadows
- Week 12: Mirrors and reflecting light
- Week 13: Light signals and communicating

### Unit 4: Patterns in the Sky: Sun, Moon and Stars (weeks 14–19)
The sun, moon and stars move across the sky in patterns we can predict.

- Week 14: The sun's path across the sky
- Week 15: Building a shadow-stick sundial
- Week 16: The moon's changing shape
- Week 17: Moon patterns over a month
- Week 18: Stars at night
- Week 19: Sky patterns wrap-up

### Unit 5: Daylight and the Seasons (weeks 20–22)
The amount of daylight changes in a pattern through the year.

- Week 20: Measuring daylight in winter
- Week 21: Seasons and daylight
- Week 22: Daylight and seasons wrap-up

### Unit 6: Plant and Animal Parts: Nature as Inventor (weeks 23–28)
Plants and animals have external parts that help them survive, and people copy those ideas to solve problems.

- Week 23: Animal external parts and their jobs
- Week 24: Plant external parts and their jobs
- Week 25: Biomimicry: people copy nature
- Week 26: Design a nature-inspired solution
- Week 27: Test, compare and improve designs
- Week 28: Nature as inventor wrap-up

### Unit 7: Animal Parents and Their Young (weeks 29–31)
Parents and offspring behave in ways that help the young survive.

- Week 29: How animal parents care for their young
- Week 30: How young animals help themselves survive
- Week 31: Patterns in parent and offspring behavior

### Unit 8: Like Parent, Like Offspring (weeks 32–34)
Young plants and animals are like, but not exactly like, their parents.

- Week 32: Young animals are like their parents
- Week 33: Young plants are like their parents
- Week 34: Variation within a kind of animal or plant

### Unit 9: Engineering Showcase and Year Review (weeks 35–36)
Science ideas help us design solutions to real problems.

- Week 35: Engineering showcase project
- Week 36: Science fair and year review

## Standards

- **1-ESS1-1** Use observations of the sun, moon, and stars to describe patterns that can be predicted.
- **1-ESS1-2** Make observations at different times of year to relate the amount of daylight to the time of year.
- **1-LS1-1** Use materials to design a solution to a human problem by mimicking how plants and/or animals use their external parts to help them survive, grow, and meet their needs.
- **1-LS1-2** Read texts and use media to determine patterns in behavior of parents and offspring that help offspring survive.
- **1-LS3-1** Make observations to construct an evidence-based account that young plants and animals are like, but not exactly like, their parents.
- **1-PS4-1** Plan and conduct investigations to provide evidence that vibrating materials can make sound and that sound can make materials vibrate.
- **1-PS4-2** Make observations to construct an evidence-based account that objects in darkness can be seen only when illuminated.
- **1-PS4-3** Plan and conduct investigations to determine the effect of placing objects made with different materials in the path of a beam of light.
- **1-PS4-4** Use tools and materials to design and build a device that uses light or sound to solve the problem of communicating over a distance.
- **K-2-ETS1-1** Ask questions, make observations, and gather information about a situation people want to change to define a simple problem that can be solved through a new or improved object or tool.
- **K-2-ETS1-2** Develop a simple sketch, drawing, or physical model to illustrate how the shape of an object helps it function as needed to solve a given problem.
- **K-2-ETS1-3** Analyze data from tests of two objects designed to solve the same problem to compare the strengths and weaknesses of how each performs.
