# ELA 1 (Grade 1)

First-grade English Language Arts builds strong readers through systematic phonics (short vowels, digraphs, blends, silent e, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, diphthongs, syllables), fluency practice, and rich read-alouds of classic and public-domain literature such as Frog and Toad, Aesop's Fables, Beatrix Potter and Robert Louis Stevenson. Students write narratives, informative reports and opinion pieces, and grow grammar, vocabulary, speaking and listening skills every week.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for ELA/Literacy; 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 90 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Readers and Writers Begin: Print Concepts & Short Vowels (weeks 1–4)
Letters stand for sounds, and sentences carry ideas from writer to reader.

- Week 1: Print concepts, sentences, and short a
- Week 2: Short i and high-frequency words
- Week 3: Short o and short u; personal narrative start
- Week 4: Short e and all short vowels; narrative sentences

### Unit 2: Digraphs and Friendship Stories (weeks 5–8)
Two letters can work together to make one sound; good readers notice how characters act and feel.

- Week 5: Digraphs sh and ch; Frog and Toad
- Week 6: Digraphs th and wh; comparing characters
- Week 7: ck, ng, and proper nouns; Frog and Toad Together
- Week 8: Digraph review and friendship narrative

### Unit 3: Consonant Blends and Fables & Folktales (weeks 9–12)
Stories from long ago teach lessons that still matter today.

- Week 9: L-blends; The Little Red Hen
- Week 10: R-blends; Aesop's fables
- Week 11: S-blends and end blends; Three Billy Goats Gruff
- Week 12: Comparing folktales and writing a fable retell

### Unit 4: Silent e and Animal Information Books (weeks 13–16)
Information books teach facts, and text features help us find them.

- Week 13: Long a with silent e; what is nonfiction?
- Week 14: Long i and long o with silent e; text features
- Week 15: Long u and e with silent e; pictures vs. words
- Week 16: Writing an animal report

### Unit 5: Vowel Teams and Poetry (weeks 17–20)
Poets choose words for sound and feeling; vowel teams help us read long-vowel words.

- Week 17: Vowel teams ai and ay; nursery rhymes and Stevenson
- Week 18: Vowel teams ee, ea, and pronouns; poems about feelings
- Week 19: Vowel teams oa, ow, and long-i (ie, igh); writing a poem
- Week 20: Poetry performance and vowel-team review

### Unit 6: R-Controlled Vowels and Opinion Writing (weeks 21–24)
Writers share opinions and give reasons to convince readers.

- Week 21: ar and or words; what is an opinion?
- Week 22: er, ir, ur words; authors' reasons
- Week 23: Planning an opinion piece; commas in a series
- Week 24: Writing, revising and sharing an opinion piece

### Unit 7: Syllables, Endings and Plant Science Reading (weeks 25–28)
Big words are made of smaller parts; informational texts on the same topic can be compared.

- Week 25: Syllables and compound words; plant life cycle text
- Week 26: Two-syllable words; comparing two plant texts
- Week 27: Inflectional endings -s, -ed, -ing; prefixes un- and re-
- Week 28: Shared research: an informative 'How a Plant Grows' book

### Unit 8: Diphthongs and Classic Storybooks (weeks 29–32)
Stories have characters, settings, problems and solutions that readers can compare.

- Week 29: Diphthongs ou/ow; The Tale of Peter Rabbit
- Week 30: Diphthongs oi/oy; Little Bear and context clues
- Week 31: Other vowel sounds (oo, au, aw); comparing classic tales
- Week 32: Writing an imaginative narrative

### Unit 9: Shared Research, Author Study and Celebration (weeks 33–36)
Readers and writers ask questions, find answers, and share what they learn.

- Week 33: Shared research: asking and answering questions
- Week 34: Research poster and presentation
- Week 35: Author study: Arnold Lobel and Cynthia Rylant
- Week 36: Year-end review and celebration of learning

## Standards

- **L.1.1a** Print all upper- and lowercase letters.
- **L.1.1b** Use common, proper, and possessive nouns.
- **L.1.1c** Use singular and plural nouns with matching verbs in basic sentences.
- **L.1.1d** Use personal, possessive, and indefinite pronouns.
- **L.1.1e** Use verbs to convey a sense of past, present, and future.
- **L.1.1f** Use frequently occurring adjectives.
- **L.1.1g** Use frequently occurring conjunctions (and, but, or, so, because).
- **L.1.1h** Use determiners (articles, demonstratives).
- **L.1.1i** Use frequently occurring prepositions.
- **L.1.1j** Produce and expand complete simple and compound declarative, interrogative, imperative, and exclamatory sentences.
- **L.1.2a** Capitalize dates and names of people.
- **L.1.2b** Use end punctuation for sentences.
- **L.1.2c** Use commas in dates and to separate single words in a series.
- **L.1.2d** Use conventional spelling for words with common spelling patterns and frequently occurring irregular words.
- **L.1.2e** Spell untaught words phonetically, drawing on phonemic awareness and spelling conventions.
- **L.1.4a** Use sentence-level context as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase.
- **L.1.4b** Use frequently occurring affixes as a clue to the meaning of a word.
- **L.1.4c** Identify frequently occurring root words and their inflectional forms.
- **L.1.5a** Sort words into categories to gain a sense of the concepts the categories represent.
- **L.1.5b** Define words by category and by one or more key attributes.
- **L.1.5c** Identify real-life connections between words and their use.
- **L.1.5d** Distinguish shades of meaning among verbs and adjectives differing in manner or intensity.
- **L.1.6** Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading and being read to, including conjunctions for simple relationships.
- **RF.1.1a** Recognize the distinguishing features of a sentence (first word, capitalization, ending punctuation).
- **RF.1.2a** Distinguish long from short vowel sounds in spoken single-syllable words.
- **RF.1.2b** Orally produce single-syllable words by blending sounds, including consonant blends.
- **RF.1.2c** Isolate and pronounce initial, medial vowel, and final sounds in spoken single-syllable words.
- **RF.1.2d** Segment spoken single-syllable words into their complete sequence of individual sounds.
- **RF.1.3a** Know the spelling-sound correspondences for common consonant digraphs.
- **RF.1.3b** Decode regularly spelled one-syllable words.
- **RF.1.3c** Know final -e and common vowel team conventions for representing long vowel sounds.
- **RF.1.3d** Use knowledge that every syllable must have a vowel sound to determine the number of syllables in a printed word.
- **RF.1.3e** Decode two-syllable words following basic patterns by breaking the words into syllables.
- **RF.1.3f** Read words with inflectional endings.
- **RF.1.3g** Recognize and read grade-appropriate irregularly spelled words.
- **RF.1.4a** Read grade-level text with purpose and understanding.
- **RF.1.4b** Read grade-level text orally with accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings.
- **RF.1.4c** Use context to confirm or self-correct word recognition and understanding, rereading as necessary.
- **RI.1.1** Ask and answer questions about key details in an informational text.
- **RI.1.10** With prompting and support, read informational texts appropriately complex for grade 1.
- **RI.1.2** Identify the main topic and retell key details of a text.
- **RI.1.3** Describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text.
- **RI.1.4** Ask and answer questions to determine or clarify the meaning of words and phrases in a text.
- **RI.1.5** Know and use text features (headings, tables of contents, glossaries, icons) to locate facts.
- **RI.1.6** Distinguish between information provided by pictures or illustrations and information provided by the words.
- **RI.1.7** Use the illustrations and details in a text to describe its key ideas.
- **RI.1.8** Identify the reasons an author gives to support points in a text.
- **RI.1.9** Identify basic similarities and differences between two texts on the same topic.
- **RL.1.1** Ask and answer questions about key details in a story.
- **RL.1.10** With prompting and support, read prose and poetry of appropriate complexity for grade 1.
- **RL.1.2** Retell stories, including key details, and demonstrate understanding of their central message or lesson.
- **RL.1.3** Describe characters, settings, and major events in a story, using key details.
- **RL.1.4** Identify words and phrases in stories or poems that suggest feelings or appeal to the senses.
- **RL.1.5** Explain major differences between books that tell stories and books that give information.
- **RL.1.6** Identify who is telling the story at various points in a text.
- **RL.1.7** Use illustrations and details in a story to describe its characters, setting, or events.
- **RL.1.9** Compare and contrast the adventures and experiences of characters in stories.
- **SL.1.1** Participate in collaborative conversations, following agreed-upon rules, building on others' talk, and asking questions.
- **SL.1.2** Ask and answer questions about key details in a text read aloud or information presented orally or through media.
- **SL.1.3** Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says to gather information or clarify something not understood.
- **SL.1.4** Describe people, places, things, and events with relevant details, expressing ideas and feelings clearly.
- **SL.1.4a** Memorize and recite poems, rhymes, and songs with expression (CA addition).
- **SL.1.5** Add drawings or other visual displays to descriptions when appropriate to clarify ideas.
- **SL.1.6** Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation.
- **W.1.1** Write opinion pieces that introduce the topic, state an opinion, supply a reason, and provide closure.
- **W.1.2** Write informative/explanatory texts that name a topic, supply facts, and provide closure.
- **W.1.3** Write narratives recounting two or more sequenced events with details, temporal words, and closure.
- **W.1.5** With guidance, focus on a topic, respond to questions and suggestions, and add details to strengthen writing.
- **W.1.6** With guidance, use digital tools to produce and publish writing, including in collaboration with peers.
- **W.1.7** Participate in shared research and writing projects.
- **W.1.8** With guidance, recall information from experiences or gather information from sources to answer a question.
