# Art & Music 1 (Grade 1)

Art & Music 1 combines visual art and general music. Students explore line, shape, color, texture, pattern and form through drawing, painting, collage, printmaking and clay, looking closely at works by Kandinsky, Matisse, Eric Carle, Hokusai, Van Gogh and O'Keeffe and at art from many cultures. In music they keep a steady beat, read simple rhythms (ta, ti-ti, rest), sing sol-mi melodies and folk songs, identify instrument families, and respond to dynamics and tempo in pieces such as 'Peter and the Wolf' and 'Carnival of the Animals', ending the year with an art show and concert.

*Framework:* 2019 California Arts Standards (Visual Arts and Music); 2020 CA Arts Framework  
*Time:* 30 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Lines and Shapes / Steady Beat (weeks 1–4)
Artists use lines and shapes to make pictures; music has a steady heartbeat we can feel and move to.

- Week 1: Kinds of lines; finding the beat
- Week 2: Shapes in art; beat with rhymes
- Week 3: Safe tools and shape animals; beat and movement
- Week 4: Line and shape design; beat review

### Unit 2: Color and Mood / Rhythm: Long and Short Sounds (weeks 5–9)
Colors can show feelings; rhythms are patterns of long and short sounds we can read and write.

- Week 5: Primary colors; long and short sounds
- Week 6: Secondary colors and the color wheel; quarter and eighth notes
- Week 7: Warm and cool colors; rhythm patterns
- Week 8: Tints, shades and color in art history; rhythm composition
- Week 9: Color project: Matisse-style cut paper; rhythm review

### Unit 3: Texture and Collage / High and Low: Pitch and Melody (weeks 10–13)
Texture is how things feel or look like they feel; melodies move up, down and stay the same.

- Week 10: Texture rubbings; high and low sounds
- Week 11: Eric Carle collage; melodies go up and down
- Week 12: Collage animals; sol and mi
- Week 13: Texture review; melody game

### Unit 4: Pattern and Printmaking / Songs of the Season and Folk Songs (weeks 14–17)
Repeating patterns make art and music; songs carry traditions from many places.

- Week 14: Patterns all around; folk songs
- Week 15: Stamp printmaking; patterns in music
- Week 16: Songs and art of winter celebrations
- Week 17: Pattern review; performance

### Unit 5: Form and Sculpture / Instruments and Timbre (weeks 18–22)
Form is 3-D art we can walk around; every instrument has its own tone color.

- Week 18: Form vs. shape; instrument families
- Week 19: Clay pinch pots; Peter and the Wolf
- Week 20: Found-object sculpture; make an instrument
- Week 21: Sculptures around us; instrument ensemble
- Week 22: Form and timbre review

### Unit 6: Art and Music from Many Cultures (weeks 23–26)
People in different places and times make art and music for many reasons.

- Week 23: Art of Mexico; music of Mexico
- Week 24: Art of Japan; music of Japan
- Week 25: Art and music of West Africa
- Week 26: Art and music of California Native peoples

### Unit 7: Nature, Animals and Stories / Dynamics and Tempo (weeks 27–31)
Artists and composers tell stories about nature and animals using color, loud/soft and fast/slow.

- Week 27: Landscapes; loud and soft
- Week 28: Van Gogh's Starry Night; fast and slow
- Week 29: Carnival of the Animals
- Week 30: Georgia O'Keeffe flowers; Vivaldi's Spring
- Week 31: Nature story in art and music

### Unit 8: Portfolio, Art Show and Spring Concert (weeks 32–36)
Artists choose, prepare and share their best work with an audience.

- Week 32: Choosing work for a portfolio
- Week 33: Preparing art for display; concert rehearsal
- Week 34: Museums and audiences
- Week 35: Art show and spring concert
- Week 36: Year reflection and summer art plan

## Standards

- **MU:Cn10.1.1** Demonstrate how interests, knowledge, and skills relate to personal choices and intent when creating, performing, and responding to music.
- **MU:Cn11.1.1** Demonstrate understanding of relationships between music and other arts, disciplines, contexts, and daily life.
- **MU:Cr1.1.1** With guidance, generate musical ideas (such as answering a musical question) for a specific purpose.
- **MU:Cr2.1.1** With guidance, demonstrate and choose favorite musical ideas; use iconic or standard notation to document them.
- **MU:Cr3.1.1** With guidance, discuss and apply personal, peer, and teacher feedback to revise music.
- **MU:Cr3.2.1** With guidance, convey creative intent when sharing personal musical ideas.
- **MU:Pr4.1.1** With guidance, demonstrate and explain personal interest in, knowledge about, and purpose of varied musical selections.
- **MU:Pr4.2.1** With guidance, demonstrate knowledge of musical concepts (beat, melodic contour) and read iconic/standard notation.
- **MU:Pr4.3.1** Demonstrate and describe music's expressive qualities (dynamics, tempo).
- **MU:Pr5.1.1** With guidance, apply feedback to refine performances and use suggested strategies in rehearsal.
- **MU:Pr6.1.1** With guidance, perform music for a specific purpose with expression and appropriate audience etiquette.
- **MU:Re7.1.1** With guidance, demonstrate and identify personal interests, experiences, and purposes for choosing music.
- **MU:Re7.2.1** With guidance, demonstrate and identify how specific music concepts (beat, pitch) are used in various styles of music.
- **MU:Re8.1.1** With guidance, demonstrate and identify expressive qualities (dynamics, tempo) that reflect creators' intent.
- **MU:Re9.1.1** With guidance, apply personal and expressive preferences in the evaluation of music.
- **VA:Cn10.1.1** Identify times, places, and reasons by which students make art outside of school.
- **VA:Cn11.1.1** Understand that people from different places and times have made art for a variety of reasons.
- **VA:Cr1.1.1** Engage collaboratively in exploration and imaginative play with materials.
- **VA:Cr1.2.1** Use observation and investigation in preparation for making a work of art.
- **VA:Cr2.1.1** Explore uses of materials and tools to create works of art or design.
- **VA:Cr2.2.1** Demonstrate safe and proper procedures for using materials, tools, and equipment while making art.
- **VA:Cr2.3.1** Identify and classify uses of everyday objects through drawings, diagrams, sculptures, or other visual means.
- **VA:Cr3.1.1** Use art vocabulary to describe choices while creating art.
- **VA:Pr4.1.1** Explain why some objects, artifacts, and artwork are valued over others.
- **VA:Pr5.1.1** Ask and answer questions about where, how, and why to prepare artwork for presentation or preservation.
- **VA:Pr6.1.1** Identify the roles and responsibilities of people who work in and visit museums and other art venues.
- **VA:Re7.1.1** Select and describe works of art that illustrate daily life experiences of one's self and others.
- **VA:Re7.2.1** Compare images that represent the same subject.
- **VA:Re8.1.1** Interpret art by categorizing subject matter and identifying the characteristics of form.
- **VA:Re9.1.1** Classify artwork based on different reasons for preferences.
