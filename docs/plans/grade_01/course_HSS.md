# A Child's Place in Time and Space (Grade 1)

Grade 1 History–Social Science helps children understand their place in the world: rules and fair play, direct and representative democracy, maps and globes, American symbols, documents and holidays, how life has changed over time, the contributions of American Indians and immigrants, and basic economics of work, goods, services and money. Students analyze historic photographs and documents from the Library of Congress, National Archives and Calisphere and conduct an oral-history interview.

*Framework:* California History–Social Science Content Standards; 2016 CA History–Social Science Framework  
*Time:* 30 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Rules, Fairness and Good Citizenship (weeks 1–5)
Rules help us live and learn together, and good citizens treat others fairly.

- Week 1: Why do we have rules?
- Week 2: The Golden Rule and respecting others
- Week 3: Fair play and good sportsmanship
- Week 4: Direct democracy: everyone votes
- Week 5: Representative democracy: choosing leaders

### Unit 2: Maps, Globes and Where We Live (weeks 6–11)
Maps and globes show where places are, and where we live shapes how we live.

- Week 6: What is a map? Models vs. pictures
- Week 7: Map symbols, keys and cardinal directions
- Week 8: My neighborhood and community map
- Week 9: California and the United States on maps
- Week 10: Continents and oceans
- Week 11: How place shapes the way people live

### Unit 3: American Symbols, Holidays and Heroes (weeks 12–17)
Symbols, songs, documents and holidays remind Americans of shared ideals and the people who worked for them.

- Week 12: The Pledge of Allegiance and patriotic songs
- Week 13: Veterans Day and Thanksgiving
- Week 14: American symbols
- Week 15: Essential documents: Declaration of Independence and Constitution
- Week 16: Heroes behind our holidays: Martin Luther King Jr.
- Week 17: Presidents' Day: Washington and Lincoln

### Unit 4: Then and Now: Schools, Communities, Transportation and Families (weeks 18–23)
Life has changed in many ways over time, but some things stay the same.

- Week 18: Schools long ago and today
- Week 19: Communities long ago
- Week 20: Transportation of earlier days
- Week 21: Family life and work long ago
- Week 22: Games, toys, clothes and manners long ago
- Week 23: Oral history interview

### Unit 5: Many Cultures, One Community (weeks 24–29)
People of many backgrounds, including American Indians and immigrants, have shaped California and America.

- Week 24: My family's story and our diverse community
- Week 25: California's first peoples
- Week 26: Immigrants in California
- Week 27: Folklore from many cultures
- Week 28: Celebrations around the world
- Week 29: One community, many cultures

### Unit 6: Work, Goods, Services and Money (weeks 30–36)
People work at different jobs to make, move, and sell goods and services, and we use money to exchange.

- Week 30: Needs, wants, goods and services
- Week 31: Exchange and money
- Week 32: Saving, spending and making choices
- Week 33: From farm to table: producing goods
- Week 34: Transporting and marketing goods
- Week 35: Work at home and in the community
- Week 36: Market day and year review

## Standards

- **1.1** Students describe the rights and individual responsibilities of citizenship.
- **1.1.1** Understand the rule-making process in a direct democracy (everyone votes on the rules) and in a representative democracy (an elected group makes the rules), with examples from classroom, school and community.
- **1.1.2** Understand fair play, good sportsmanship, respect for the rights and opinions of others, and respect for rules, including the meaning of the Golden Rule.
- **1.2.1** Locate on maps and globes their local community, California, the United States, the seven continents, and the four oceans.
- **1.2.2** Compare the information from a three-dimensional model to the information from a picture of the same location.
- **1.2.3** Construct a simple map, using cardinal directions and map symbols.
- **1.2.4** Describe how location, weather, and physical environment affect the way people live (food, clothing, shelter, transportation, recreation).
- **1.3.1** Recite the Pledge of Allegiance and sing songs that express American ideals (e.g., 'My Country 'Tis of Thee').
- **1.3.2** Understand the significance of national holidays and the heroism and achievements of the people associated with them.
- **1.3.3** Identify American symbols, landmarks, and essential documents (flag, bald eagle, Statue of Liberty, U.S. Constitution, Declaration of Independence) and the people and events associated with them.
- **1.4.1** Examine the structure of schools and communities in the past.
- **1.4.2** Study transportation methods of earlier days.
- **1.4.3** Recognize similarities and differences of earlier generations in work, dress, manners, stories, games, and festivals, drawing from biographies, oral histories, and folklore.
- **1.5.1** Recognize that they are all part of the same community, sharing principles, goals and traditions despite varied ancestry; the forms, benefits and challenges of diversity.
- **1.5.2** Understand the ways in which American Indians and immigrants have helped define Californian and American culture.
- **1.5.3** Compare the beliefs, customs, ceremonies, traditions, and social practices of varied cultures, drawing from folklore.
- **1.6.1** Understand the concept of exchange and the use of money to purchase goods and services.
- **1.6.2** Identify the specialized work people do to manufacture, transport, and market goods and services, and the contributions of those who work in the home.
