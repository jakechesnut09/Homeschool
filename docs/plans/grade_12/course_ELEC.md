# Personal Finance (S1) & College/Career Readiness (S2) (Grade 12)

Semester 1 is a full personal finance course aligned to the Jump$tart National Standards and California's personal finance graduation requirement (AB 2927): earning income and paychecks, budgeting, banking, credit and debt, federal and California taxes, insurance, investing and retirement, consumer protection and fraud, and renting. Semester 2 is college and career readiness for a California homeschool senior: career exploration, UC/CSU applications and Personal Insight Questions, the FAFSA or California Dream Act Application, Cal Grant and Middle Class Scholarship, community college transfer paths, resumes, job applications, work permits and interviews, and a finished homeschool transcript and private-school diploma. Because UC/CSU applications and the FAFSA/CADAA open in the fall, week 1 sets a deadline calendar so real submissions happen on time; Semester 2 then completes, verifies, and builds on them.

*Framework:* Jump$tart National Standards for Personal Financial Education (2021) and California AB 2927 personal finance topics; California College and Career Readiness (CSAC, UC, CSU, CCC guidance)  
*Time:* 50 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* g · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Earning Income, Paychecks & Budgeting (weeks 1–4)
Income is limited, so a written spending plan turns your paycheck into your priorities.

- Week 1: Income, careers & senior-year money calendar
- Week 2: Paychecks, pay stubs & the W-4
- Week 3: Building a spending plan
- Week 4: Smart spending & big purchases

### Unit 2: Banking & Payments (weeks 5–6)
Choosing the right accounts and payment tools keeps money safe, cheap, and accessible.

- Week 5: Banks, credit unions & checking accounts
- Week 6: Savings, interest & digital payments

### Unit 3: Credit & Debt (weeks 7–9)
Credit is a tool that costs money; your borrowing history follows you and shapes future costs.

- Week 7: Credit reports & credit scores
- Week 8: Credit cards, loans & the cost of borrowing
- Week 9: Managing debt & student loans

### Unit 4: Taxes (weeks 10–11)
Taxes fund public goods, and understanding forms and credits lets you keep what you are owed.

- Week 10: How taxes work
- Week 11: Filing a return: W-2, 1040 & CA 540

### Unit 5: Insurance & Managing Risk (weeks 12–13)
Insurance transfers large financial risks you cannot afford to carry alone.

- Week 12: Insurance basics & health insurance
- Week 13: Auto, renters, disability & life insurance

### Unit 6: Investing, Compound Interest & Retirement (weeks 14–16)
Time plus compound growth, diversification, and low fees build long-term wealth.

- Week 14: Compound growth & investment basics
- Week 15: Diversification, fees & investor behavior
- Week 16: Retirement accounts

### Unit 7: Consumer Protection, Fraud & Renting (weeks 17–18)
Knowing your rights and the warning signs of scams protects your money and your home.

- Week 17: Consumer protection, scams & identity theft
- Week 18: Renting your first apartment & semester capstone

### Unit 8: Postsecondary Pathways & College Applications (weeks 19–23)
Many California pathways lead to a good career; strong applications tell a clear, honest story.

- Week 19: Self-assessment & career exploration
- Week 20: California postsecondary pathways
- Week 21: Homeschool applicants: a–g, testing & records
- Week 22: UC & CSU applications
- Week 23: Personal Insight Questions

### Unit 9: Paying for College: FAFSA, Dream Act & State Aid (weeks 24–28)
Free money comes first; the FAFSA or CADAA unlocks federal, Cal Grant, and college aid.

- Week 24: Cost of attendance & the FAFSA
- Week 25: California Dream Act Application & CSAC WebGrants
- Week 26: Cal Grant & Middle Class Scholarship
- Week 27: Scholarships
- Week 28: Comparing financial aid offers

### Unit 10: Community College & Transfer (weeks 29–30)
California's community colleges are an affordable, guaranteed route to a degree, credential, or UC/CSU transfer.

- Week 29: California Community Colleges
- Week 30: Transfer paths: ADT, TAG & ASSIST

### Unit 11: Career Readiness: Resumes, Applications & Interviews (weeks 31–34)
Employers hire evidence: a targeted resume, accurate paperwork, and a prepared interview.

- Week 31: Resumes & cover letters
- Week 32: Job applications & work permits
- Week 33: Interviews
- Week 34: Workplace skills & workers' rights

### Unit 12: Homeschool Records, Diploma & Launch Plan (weeks 35–36)
A complete transcript, diploma, and first-year plan make the transition to adulthood official.

- Week 35: Homeschool transcript & diploma
- Week 36: Launch plan & course capstone

## Standards

- **CCR-1** Assess interests, skills, and values and research careers using labor-market data to set postsecondary and career goals.
- **CCR-10** Assemble homeschool academic records: transcript, course descriptions, credit and GPA calculation, and a diploma issued by the student's private school (PSA).
- **CCR-11** Build a transition plan: compare offers, accept admission or employment, budget the first year, and complete enrollment steps.
- **CCR-2** Compare postsecondary pathways in California: UC, CSU, California Community Colleges, private/independent colleges, apprenticeships, military, and direct employment.
- **CCR-3** Complete UC and CSU applications, including a–g course reporting, activities, and the UC Personal Insight Questions.
- **CCR-4** Complete the FAFSA or California Dream Act Application (CADAA), including the FSA ID/StudentAid.gov account, contributors, and the Student Aid Index.
- **CCR-5** Apply for California state aid and scholarships (Cal Grant, Middle Class Scholarship, California College Promise Grant, WebGrants) and compare financial aid offers.
- **CCR-6** Plan community college and transfer paths (Associate Degree for Transfer, UC Transfer Admission Guarantee, ASSIST.org articulation, CTE certificates).
- **CCR-7** Write a professional resume, cover letter, and references list tailored to a specific job or program.
- **CCR-8** Prepare for and perform in interviews and professional communication (STAR answers, email etiquette, follow-up, workplace conduct).
- **CCR-9** Complete job applications and employment paperwork, including the California work permit for minors, Form I-9, W-4, and workers' rights under California labor law.
- **PF-CREDIT-1** Explain credit reports and credit scores, how they are built and used, and how to obtain free reports and dispute errors.
- **PF-CREDIT-2** Compare the costs of credit cards, auto loans, personal loans, and predatory products (APR, fees, minimum payments, payday loans, buy-now-pay-later).
- **PF-CREDIT-3** Manage and repay debt, including student loans (federal vs. private, subsidized vs. unsubsidized, repayment plans) and debt-reduction strategies.
- **PF-EARN-1** Explain how education, skills, and career choices affect lifetime income, and compare sources of income (wages, salary, tips, self-employment, benefits).
- **PF-EARN-2** Read a paycheck and pay stub: gross vs. net pay, payroll deductions (federal/CA withholding, FICA, CA SDI), and complete Form W-4 and the CA DE 4.
- **PF-EARN-3** Explain federal and California income taxes and file a basic return (Form W-2, Form 1040, CA Form 540, CA Franchise Tax Board, CalEITC/Young Child Tax Credit, free filing options).
- **PF-INVEST-1** Explain the relationship between risk and return and describe stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and index funds.
- **PF-INVEST-2** Compare retirement accounts (401(k)/403(b), traditional and Roth IRA, CalSavers, employer match) and their tax treatment.
- **PF-INVEST-3** Apply diversification, dollar-cost averaging, and fee comparison, and recognize behavioral biases and investment fraud.
- **PF-RISK-1** Explain how insurance manages risk (premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximum) and when to self-insure.
- **PF-RISK-2** Compare auto, health (Covered California, Medi-Cal, employer plans), renters, disability, and life insurance and choose appropriate coverage.
- **PF-SAVE-1** Compare financial institutions and accounts (banks, credit unions, checking, savings, debit, digital payments, FDIC/NCUA insurance) and manage an account.
- **PF-SAVE-2** Set savings goals, build an emergency fund, and calculate simple and compound interest and the time value of money.
- **PF-SPEND-1** Create, follow, and revise a spending plan (budget) that balances needs, wants, savings, and goals.
- **PF-SPEND-2** Make informed consumer decisions: comparison shopping, opportunity cost, transportation costs, and housing choices including renting and leases.
- **PF-SPEND-3** Recognize consumer rights and protections, and prevent and respond to fraud, scams, and identity theft (CFPB, FTC, CA DFPI, CA tenant law).
