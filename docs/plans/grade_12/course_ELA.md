# English 12 – World & British Literature + Senior Research (Grade 12)

A senior English course that traces British and world literature from Beowulf and Chaucer through Shakespeare, the Romantics, the Victorians, colonial and postcolonial voices, and Modernism. Students write the UC Personal Insight Questions and college essays in the fall, produce literary analyses and a timed-writing portfolio, and complete a six-week Senior Research Paper (MLA) with an oral defense. Grammar, vocabulary, and speaking & listening are woven into every week.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for ELA/Literacy (grades 11–12); 2014 California ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* b · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Voice & the College Essay (weeks 1–3)
Strong personal writing turns specific experiences into insight about who you are.

- Week 1: Reading personal essays as models
- Week 2: UC Personal Insight Questions – drafting
- Week 3: College essay revision and publishing

### Unit 2: Heroes and Pilgrims: Anglo-Saxon and Medieval Literature (weeks 4–7)
Early English literature reveals what a culture honors, fears, and satirizes.

- Week 4: Beowulf: the heroic code
- Week 5: Beowulf: fate, age, and legacy
- Week 6: The Canterbury Tales: General Prologue
- Week 7: The Pardoner's Tale and the Wife of Bath

### Unit 3: Ambition and Power: Shakespeare's Macbeth (weeks 8–11)
Tragedy shows how unchecked ambition corrupts both a person and a kingdom.

- Week 8: Macbeth Act I – prophecy and temptation
- Week 9: Macbeth Acts II–III – murder and guilt
- Week 10: Macbeth Acts IV–V – downfall
- Week 11: Macbeth argument essay and the sonnets

### Unit 4: Reason and Revolt: Satire and Romantic Poetry (weeks 12–13)
Writers answered the Age of Reason with satire on one side and passionate feeling for nature and the individual on the other.

- Week 12: Satire: Swift and Pope
- Week 13: Romantic poetry

### Unit 5: The Gothic and the Creature: Frankenstein (weeks 14–18)
Frankenstein asks who is responsible for what science creates and who counts as human.

- Week 14: Frankenstein: frame and creation
- Week 15: Frankenstein: the creature's story
- Week 16: Frankenstein: pursuit and ending
- Week 17: Frankenstein literary argument
- Week 18: Semester 1 synthesis and final

### Unit 6: Society on Stage: Victorian and Realist Literature (weeks 19–21)
Victorian and realist writers expose the gap between respectable appearances and private truth.

- Week 19: Victorian poetry
- Week 20: A Doll's House
- Week 21: The Importance of Being Earnest

### Unit 7: Empire and Its Answers: Things Fall Apart (weeks 22–24)
Postcolonial literature talks back to the empire's version of history.

- Week 22: Empire: context and Things Fall Apart Part One
- Week 23: Things Fall Apart Parts Two and Three
- Week 24: Achebe's argument and postcolonial essay

### Unit 8: Senior Research Paper (weeks 25–30)
Real research begins with a genuine question and ends with a defensible, documented claim.

- Week 25: Research question and preliminary sources
- Week 26: Reading, note-taking, and annotated bibliography
- Week 27: Thesis, outline, and primary research
- Week 28: Drafting the research paper
- Week 29: Revising and editing the research paper
- Week 30: Final paper and presentation prep

### Unit 9: Fractured Worlds: Modernism (weeks 31–33)
War and rapid change broke old forms, and Modernist writers built new ones to show alienation.

- Week 31: War poets and Modernist poetry
- Week 32: The Metamorphosis
- Week 33: Orwell: language and power

### Unit 10: Senior Defense & Portfolio (weeks 34–36)
A graduate can present, defend, and reflect on their own thinking and growth.

- Week 34: Research presentation rehearsal
- Week 35: Senior research defense
- Week 36: Senior portfolio and final reflection

- **RI.11-12.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases in a text, including how an author refines the meaning of a key term over the course of a text.

- **RI.11-12.7** Integrate and evaluate multiple sources of information presented in different media or formats to address a question or solve a problem.
- **RI.11-12.8** Delineate and evaluate the reasoning in seminal texts, including the premises, purposes, and arguments.

- **RL.11-12.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas, and poems, at the high end of the grades 11–CCR text complexity band independently.
- **RL.11-12.2** Determine two or more themes and analyze their development and interaction; provide an objective summary.
- **RL.11-12.3** Analyze the impact of an author's choices about developing and relating elements of a story or drama.

- **RL.11-12.6** Analyze a case where grasping point of view requires distinguishing what is directly stated from what is meant (satire, sarcasm, irony, understatement).
- **RL.11-12.7** Analyze multiple interpretations of a story, drama, or poem (recorded or live production), evaluating how each interprets the source text.
- **RL.11-12.9** Demonstrate knowledge of foundational works of literature, including how two or more texts from the same period treat similar themes or topics.

- **SL.11-12.4** Present information, findings, and supporting evidence conveying a clear and distinct perspective appropriate to purpose, audience, and task.
- **SL.11-12.5** Make strategic use of digital media in presentations to enhance understanding of findings, reasoning, and evidence.
- **SL.11-12.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when appropriate.
- **W.11-12.1** Write arguments to support claims using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence.
- **W.11-12.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences.
- **W.11-12.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine and convey complex ideas clearly and accurately.
- **W.11-12.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.11-12.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience.
- **W.11-12.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach.

- **W.11-12.7** Conduct short and sustained research projects to answer a question or solve a problem; synthesize multiple sources.
- **W.11-12.8** Gather relevant information from multiple authoritative print and digital sources; assess strengths and limitations of each; integrate information while avoiding plagiarism and following a standard citation format.
- **W.11-12.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection, and research.
