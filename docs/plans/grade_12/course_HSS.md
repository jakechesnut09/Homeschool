# American Government & Civics (S1) / Economics (S2) (Grade 12)

Semester 1 (Principles of American Democracy) examines the philosophical foundations of the Constitution, rights and responsibilities, the three branches, landmark Supreme Court cases, elections, federalism with a focus on California state and local government, the media, comparative systems, and a civic action project. Semester 2 (Economics) covers scarcity and economic systems, supply and demand, market structures, the role of government, labor markets, macroeconomic indicators, fiscal and monetary policy, and international trade. Primary sources (Federalist Papers, court opinions, ballot measures, Fed statements, BLS data) anchor every week.

*Framework:* California History–Social Science Content Standards, Grade 12 (Principles of American Democracy and Economics); 2016 California History–Social Science Framework. Government codes are prefixed GOV-12.x and economics codes ECON-12.x.  
*Time:* 55 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* a · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Foundations of American Democracy (weeks 1–2)
The Constitution blends ancient, English, and Enlightenment ideas to limit power and protect liberty.

- Week 1: Roots of American government
- Week 2: The Constitution and the Federalist Papers

### Unit 2: Rights, Responsibilities, and Civil Society (weeks 3–4)
Rights depend on citizens who respect others' rights and participate in civil society.

- Week 3: The Bill of Rights
- Week 4: Citizenship and civil society

### Unit 3: The Three Branches (weeks 5–8)
Separated and shared powers force the branches to cooperate and check one another.

- Week 5: Congress: structure and powers
- Week 6: How a bill becomes law; amending the Constitution
- Week 7: The executive branch
- Week 8: The judicial branch

### Unit 4: The Supreme Court and Landmark Cases (weeks 9–10)
The Court's interpretations of the Constitution change over time and reshape American life.

- Week 9: Judicial review and federal power
- Week 10: Civil rights and civil liberties cases

### Unit 5: Elections, Parties, and Participation (weeks 11–12)
Citizens influence government through parties, campaigns, voting, and direct democracy.

- Week 11: Parties, campaigns, and money
- Week 12: Voting, direct democracy, and the Electoral College

### Unit 6: Federalism: California State, Local, and Tribal Government (weeks 13–14)
Power is shared among national, state, tribal, and local governments, and most daily services are local.

- Week 13: Federalism and the California Constitution
- Week 14: Local and tribal government, budgets, and courts

### Unit 7: Media, Comparative Government, and Civic Action (weeks 15–18)
A free press, comparison with other systems, and informed action keep democracy healthy.

- Week 15: Media and democracy
- Week 16: Comparative political systems
- Week 17: Enduring tensions and civic action project
- Week 18: Government semester review and final

### Unit 8: Economic Fundamentals (weeks 19–20)
Scarcity forces choices, and every choice has an opportunity cost.

- Week 19: Scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost
- Week 20: Incentives and economic systems

### Unit 9: Markets: Supply, Demand, and Competition (weeks 21–24)
Prices coordinate millions of decisions by signaling relative scarcity.

- Week 21: Demand
- Week 22: Supply and market equilibrium
- Week 23: Price controls and market structures
- Week 24: Entrepreneurship, financial markets, and location

### Unit 10: Government and the Economy (weeks 25–26)
Government corrects some market failures, but its actions also have costs.

- Week 25: Market failures and the role of government
- Week 26: Taxes, spending, and the budget

### Unit 11: Labor Markets (weeks 27–28)
Wages reflect supply, demand, and productivity in a changing global labor market.

- Week 27: Labor markets and wages
- Week 28: Unions, minimum wage, and global labor

### Unit 12: Macroeconomics, Money, and the Fed (weeks 29–31)
GDP, unemployment, and inflation measure the economy's health, and fiscal and monetary policy try to stabilize it.

- Week 29: GDP and economic growth
- Week 30: Unemployment and inflation
- Week 31: Money, the Fed, and fiscal vs. monetary policy

### Unit 13: International Economics (weeks 32–34)
Trade raises total output through specialization but creates winners and losers.

- Week 32: Comparative advantage and trade
- Week 33: Trade policy: tariffs and agreements
- Week 34: Exchange rates and the global economy

### Unit 14: Economics Capstone (weeks 35–36)
Economic reasoning helps citizens evaluate real policy choices.

- Week 35: Economics capstone: policy analysis
- Week 36: Economics review and final
