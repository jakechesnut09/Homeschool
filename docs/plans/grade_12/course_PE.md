# Lifetime Fitness II (Grade 12)

An advanced elective physical education course that prepares seniors to stay active for life after high school. Students design and run their own strength, cardiorespiratory, and flexibility programs, learn individual and dual lifetime activities (pickleball/tennis, golf, hiking and orienteering, cycling, swimming, dance), practice stress management and recovery, and finish with a personal adult fitness plan they can use in college or the workplace.

*Framework:* California Physical Education Model Content Standards – High School Course 3 (advanced elective); 2009 California Physical Education Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Fitness Assessment and Goal Setting (weeks 1–3)
You can't plan what you haven't measured.

- Week 1: Baseline fitness testing
- Week 2: Heart rate and intensity
- Week 3: SMART goals and motivation

### Unit 2: Resistance Training Program Design (weeks 4–8)
Safe technique plus progressive overload builds strength for life.

- Week 4: Resistance training safety and technique
- Week 5: Program variables: sets, reps, load
- Week 6: Designing a personal strength program
- Week 7: Program in progress
- Week 8: Strength retest and program evaluation

### Unit 3: Cardiorespiratory Endurance: Run, Bike, Swim (weeks 9–13)
Training the heart and lungs with the right intensity improves health and performance.

- Week 9: Aerobic training principles
- Week 10: 5K training: building base
- Week 11: Swimming or cycling skills
- Week 12: 5K peak and taper
- Week 13: 5K time trial

### Unit 4: Flexibility, Yoga, and Recovery (weeks 14–16)
Mobility, rest, and nutrition make training sustainable.

- Week 14: Flexibility and mobility
- Week 15: Yoga and stress management
- Week 16: Nutrition, hydration, sleep, recovery

### Unit 5: Semester Reassessment (weeks 17–18)
Data shows what worked and what to change.

- Week 17: Semester fitness reassessment
- Week 18: Semester reflection and new plan

### Unit 6: Racket Sports: Pickleball and Tennis (weeks 19–22)
Dual sports combine skill, strategy, and social connection for a lifetime.

- Week 19: Pickleball fundamentals
- Week 20: Pickleball strategy and play
- Week 21: Tennis fundamentals
- Week 22: Tennis play

### Unit 7: Golf and Precision Activities (weeks 23–24)
Precision sports reward consistent mechanics and focus.

- Week 23: Golf fundamentals
- Week 24: Golf course play and focus

### Unit 8: Outdoor Adventure: Hiking, Orienteering, Cycling (weeks 25–28)
California's outdoors is a lifelong gym when you know how to navigate it safely.

- Week 25: Hiking safety and preparation
- Week 26: Map, compass, and orienteering
- Week 27: Cycling for fitness and transport
- Week 28: Outdoor adventure challenge

### Unit 9: Dance and Rhythmic Activities (weeks 29–30)
Dance builds fitness, coordination, and cultural connection.

- Week 29: Social and cultural dance
- Week 30: Dance fitness choreography

### Unit 10: Adult Fitness Plan and Final Assessment (weeks 31–36)
An adult plan fits activity into real schedules, budgets, and communities.

- Week 31: Evaluating fitness resources and claims
- Week 32: Fitting activity into adult life
- Week 33: Writing the adult fitness plan
- Week 34: Final fitness assessment
- Week 35: Presenting the plan
- Week 36: Course wrap-up

## Standards

- **PE-HS3-1.1** Combine and apply movement patterns, from simple to complex, in individual and dual lifetime activities (racket sports, golf).
- **PE-HS3-1.2** Demonstrate proficient movement skills in outdoor and adventure activities (hiking, orienteering, cycling).
- **PE-HS3-1.3** Demonstrate proficient movement skills in aquatic and rhythmic/dance activities.
- **PE-HS3-1.4** Analyze and apply biomechanical principles (force, leverage, stability) to improve performance of lifetime activities.
- **PE-HS3-1.5** Apply offensive and defensive strategies and rules in individual and dual activities.
- **PE-HS3-2.1** Assess personal fitness levels on the five components of health-related fitness and set goals.
- **PE-HS3-2.2** Design, implement, and evaluate a personal fitness plan using the FITT principle, progression, overload, and specificity.
- **PE-HS3-2.3** Meet health-related fitness standards for cardiorespiratory endurance, muscular strength and endurance, flexibility, and body composition.
- **PE-HS3-2.4** Explain and apply safe resistance-training technique and program design, including periodization.
- **PE-HS3-2.5** Evaluate nutrition, hydration, sleep, and recovery practices that support activity and long-term health.
- **PE-HS3-2.6** Evaluate community and commercial fitness resources, consumer claims, and costs for lifelong activity.
- **PE-HS3-3.1** Explain the psychological benefits of physical activity, including stress reduction and improved mood, and use activity to manage stress.
- **PE-HS3-3.2** Identify and use strategies for intrinsic motivation, self-monitoring, and overcoming barriers to adult physical activity.
- **PE-HS3-3.3** Demonstrate leadership, cooperation, safety, and respect for others in group and partner activities.
- **PE-HS3-3.4** Analyze the social and cultural roles of physical activity and how to build activity into adult life (work, family, community).
