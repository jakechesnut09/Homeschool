# Advanced Art & Portfolio (Grade 12)

An advanced studio course for seniors that builds technical mastery in drawing, painting, printmaking, mixed media, and digital/photo work, then centers on a year-long Sustained Investigation modeled on AP Art and Design. Students keep a process sketchbook, write artist statements, photograph and curate a college-ready portfolio, and mount a public senior exhibition.

*Framework:* 2019 California Arts Standards for Public Schools – Visual Arts (High School Advanced proficiency level); 2020 California Arts Education Framework  
*Time:* 50 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* f · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Studio Practice and the Visual Journal (weeks 1–3)
Serious artists build habits: daily sketching, research, and reflection.

- Week 1: Setting up a studio practice
- Week 2: Idea generation strategies
- Week 3: Looking at contemporary art

### Unit 2: Advanced Observational Drawing (weeks 4–7)
Accurate seeing is the foundation for intentional distortion.

- Week 4: Value and form
- Week 5: Perspective and space
- Week 6: The figure
- Week 7: Portraiture and expressive drawing

### Unit 3: Painting: Color and Surface (weeks 8–11)
Color relationships and surface handling carry mood and meaning.

- Week 8: Color theory in paint
- Week 9: Painting from life
- Week 10: Plein air and landscape
- Week 11: Abstraction and expressive painting

### Unit 4: Launching the Sustained Investigation (weeks 12–15)
An inquiry question drives a body of work forward through practice, experimentation, and revision.

- Week 12: Defining an inquiry
- Week 13: Artist research and influences
- Week 14: Sustained Investigation pieces 1–2
- Week 15: Sustained Investigation piece 3 and review

### Unit 5: Digital, Photo, and Mixed Media (weeks 16–18)
New media and ethical image use expand what an artist can say.

- Week 16: Digital art and ethics
- Week 17: Photography and visual culture
- Week 18: Mixed media and semester review

### Unit 6: Sustained Investigation: Production (weeks 19–25)
Depth comes from making many related works and letting each one inform the next.

- Week 19: Investigation: new directions
- Week 20: Investigation: piece 7
- Week 21: Investigation: piece 8
- Week 22: Mid-investigation critique
- Week 23: Investigation: pieces 9–10
- Week 24: Investigation: pieces 11–12
- Week 25: Investigation: completion

### Unit 7: Printmaking and Sculpture Extensions (weeks 26–28)
Translating an idea into multiples or three dimensions reveals new possibilities.

- Week 26: Relief printmaking
- Week 27: Sculpture and assemblage
- Week 28: Public art proposal

### Unit 8: Portfolio Curation and Documentation (weeks 29–31)
How work is selected, photographed, and described shapes how it is understood.

- Week 29: Photographing artwork
- Week 30: Selecting and sequencing
- Week 31: Artist statement and online portfolio

### Unit 9: Senior Exhibition (weeks 32–34)
Exhibiting art is a public conversation with a community.

- Week 32: Planning the exhibition
- Week 33: Preparing and protecting work
- Week 34: Senior exhibition

### Unit 10: Reflection and Next Steps (weeks 35–36)
An artist's practice continues beyond school through intention and community.

- Week 35: Reflection on growth
- Week 36: Art beyond high school

## Standards

- **VA:Cn10.1.HSIII** Synthesize knowledge of social, cultural, historical, and personal life with art-making approaches to create meaningful works of art or design.
- **VA:Cn11.1.HSIII** Appraise the impact of an artist or a group of artists on the beliefs, values, and behaviors of a society.
- **VA:Cr1.1.HSIII** Visualize and hypothesize to generate plans for ideas and directions for creating art and design that can affect social change.
- **VA:Cr1.2.HSIII** Choose from a range of materials and methods of traditional and contemporary artistic practices, following or breaking established conventions, to plan the making of multiple works of art and design based on a theme, idea, or concept.
- **VA:Cr2.1.HSIII** Experiment, plan, and make multiple works of art and design that explore a personally meaningful theme, idea, or concept.
- **VA:Cr2.2.HSIII** Demonstrate understanding of the importance of balancing freedom and responsibility in the use of images, materials, tools, and equipment in the creation and circulation of creative work.
- **VA:Cr2.3.HSIII** Demonstrate in works of art or design how visual and material culture defines, shapes, enhances, inhibits, and/or empowers people's lives.
- **VA:Cr3.1.HSIII** Reflect on, reengage, revise, and refine works of art or design considering relevant traditional and contemporary criteria as well as personal artistic vision.
- **VA:Pr4.1.HSIII** Critique, justify, and present choices in the process of analyzing, selecting, curating, and presenting artwork for a specific exhibit or event.
- **VA:Pr5.1.HSIII** Investigate, compare, and contrast methods for preserving and protecting art; prepare and present artwork, including digital documentation.
- **VA:Pr6.1.HSIII** Curate a collection of objects, artifacts, or artwork to impact the viewer's understanding of social, cultural, and/or political experiences.
- **VA:Re7.1.HSIII** Analyze how responses to art develop over time based on knowledge of and experience with art and life.
- **VA:Re7.2.HSIII** Determine the commonalities within a group of artists or visual images attributed to a particular type of art, timeframe, or culture.
- **VA:Re8.1.HSIII** Analyze differing interpretations of an artwork or collection of works in order to select and defend a plausible critical analysis.
- **VA:Re9.1.HSIII** Construct evaluations of a work of art or collection of works based on differing sets of criteria.
