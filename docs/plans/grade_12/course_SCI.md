# Earth & Space / Environmental Science (Grade 12)

A capstone lab science built on the NGSS high-school Earth and Space Science performance expectations plus the environmental (human-impact and engineering) expectations. Students move from the life of stars and the origin of the elements to plate tectonics, Earth's climate system, natural hazards and resources in California, ecosystems and biodiversity, pollution, climate change, and engineering for sustainability, ending with an independent local environmental investigation. Every week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials or real NASA/USGS/NOAA data.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (HS Earth and Space Sciences, HS-LS2/LS4 human impacts, HS Engineering Design); 2016 California Science Framework; California Environmental Principles and Concepts  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* d · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Stars and the Origin of the Universe (weeks 1–4)
Light from distant objects reveals how the universe began, how stars shine, and where the elements came from.

- Week 1: The Sun: fusion and energy
- Week 2: Light, spectra, and the Big Bang
- Week 3: Stellar life cycles and nucleosynthesis
- Week 4: Orbits and Kepler's laws

### Unit 2: Earth's Formation and History (weeks 5–7)
Rocks, meteorites, and other planets record Earth's 4.6-billion-year story.

- Week 5: Early Earth and the solar system's formation
- Week 6: Seafloor spreading and the ages of rocks
- Week 7: Geologic time

### Unit 3: Earth's Dynamic Systems and Plate Tectonics (weeks 8–11)
Heat from Earth's interior and water at its surface continuously reshape the planet, and changes in one system cascade through others.

- Week 8: Earth's interior and mantle convection
- Week 9: Plate boundaries and California landforms
- Week 10: Water as a geologic agent
- Week 11: Feedbacks among Earth systems

### Unit 4: Weather, Climate, and the Coevolution of Earth and Life (weeks 12–14)
Earth's climate depends on energy balance and the carbon cycle, and life has changed Earth as Earth has changed life.

- Week 12: Earth's energy budget and climate drivers
- Week 13: The carbon cycle
- Week 14: Coevolution of Earth and life

### Unit 5: Natural Hazards and Resources in California (weeks 15–18)
Where people live and how they use energy and minerals are shaped by hazards and resources, and every resource choice has costs and benefits.

- Week 15: Natural hazards and human activity
- Week 16: Natural resources and civilization
- Week 17: Energy and mineral resource design solutions
- Week 18: Semester 1 review and final

### Unit 6: Ecosystems and Biodiversity (weeks 19–22)
Biodiversity supports ecosystem stability, and human resource use can protect or erode it.

- Week 19: Ecosystem stability and change
- Week 20: Human impacts on biodiversity
- Week 21: Simulating resources, population, and biodiversity
- Week 22: Designing solutions for biodiversity

### Unit 7: Water, Air, and Pollution (weeks 23–25)
Pollutants move through connected Earth systems, and technology can reduce their impact.

- Week 23: Water quality
- Week 24: Air quality
- Week 25: Waste, plastics, and pollution solutions

### Unit 8: Global Climate Change (weeks 26–29)
Data and models show that human activity is changing climate at an unprecedented rate, with regional impacts in California.

- Week 26: Evidence of climate change
- Week 27: Climate models and forecasts
- Week 28: Climate impacts on California
- Week 29: Mitigation and your carbon footprint

### Unit 9: Engineering for Sustainability (weeks 30–32)
Engineering design breaks global challenges into solvable problems with criteria, constraints, and trade-offs.

- Week 30: Defining a global challenge
- Week 31: Designing and building a prototype
- Week 32: Evaluating trade-offs and simulating impacts

### Unit 10: Capstone: Local Environmental Investigation (weeks 33–36)
Scientists answer local questions with data and communicate findings to their community.

- Week 33: Capstone: research question and plan
- Week 34: Capstone: data collection
- Week 35: Capstone: analysis and solution
- Week 36: Capstone presentation and final

## Standards

- **HS-ESS1-1** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life span of the Sun and the role of nuclear fusion in releasing energy that reaches Earth as radiation.
- **HS-ESS1-2** Construct an explanation of the Big Bang theory based on astronomical evidence of light spectra, motion of distant galaxies, and composition of matter in the universe.
- **HS-ESS1-3** Communicate scientific ideas about the way stars, over their life cycle, produce elements.
- **HS-ESS1-4** Use mathematical or computational representations to predict the motion of orbiting objects in the solar system.
- **HS-ESS1-5** Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
- **HS-ESS1-6** Apply scientific reasoning and evidence from ancient Earth materials, meteorites, and other planetary surfaces to construct an account of Earth's formation and early history.
- **HS-ESS2-1** Develop a model to illustrate how Earth's internal and surface processes operate at different spatial and temporal scales to form continental and ocean-floor features.
- **HS-ESS2-2** Analyze geoscience data to make the claim that one change to Earth's surface can create feedbacks that cause changes to other Earth systems.
- **HS-ESS2-3** Develop a model based on evidence of Earth's interior to describe the cycling of matter by thermal convection.
- **HS-ESS2-4** Use a model to describe how variations in the flow of energy into and out of Earth's systems result in changes in climate.
- **HS-ESS2-5** Plan and conduct an investigation of the properties of water and its effects on Earth materials and surface processes.
- **HS-ESS2-6** Develop a quantitative model to describe the cycling of carbon among the hydrosphere, atmosphere, geosphere, and biosphere.
- **HS-ESS2-7** Construct an argument based on evidence about the simultaneous coevolution of Earth's systems and life on Earth.
- **HS-ESS3-1** Construct an explanation based on evidence for how the availability of natural resources, occurrence of natural hazards, and changes in climate have influenced human activity.
- **HS-ESS3-2** Evaluate competing design solutions for developing, managing, and utilizing energy and mineral resources based on cost-benefit ratios.
- **HS-ESS3-3** Create a computational simulation to illustrate the relationships among management of natural resources, the sustainability of human populations, and biodiversity.
- **HS-ESS3-4** Evaluate or refine a technological solution that reduces impacts of human activities on natural systems.
- **HS-ESS3-5** Analyze geoscience data and the results from global climate models to make an evidence-based forecast of the current rate of global or regional climate change and associated future impacts to Earth systems.
- **HS-ESS3-6** Use a computational representation to illustrate the relationships among Earth systems and how those relationships are being modified due to human activity.
- **HS-ETS1-1** Analyze a major global challenge to specify qualitative and quantitative criteria and constraints for solutions that account for societal needs and wants.
- **HS-ETS1-2** Design a solution to a complex real-world problem by breaking it down into smaller, more manageable problems that can be solved through engineering.
- **HS-ETS1-3** Evaluate a solution to a complex real-world problem based on prioritized criteria and trade-offs, including cost, safety, reliability, aesthetics, and social, cultural, and environmental impacts.
- **HS-ETS1-4** Use a computer simulation to model the impact of proposed solutions to a complex real-world problem with numerous criteria and constraints.
- **HS-LS2-6** Evaluate claims, evidence, and reasoning that complex ecosystem interactions maintain relatively consistent numbers and types of organisms in stable conditions, but changing conditions may result in a new ecosystem.
- **HS-LS2-7** Design, evaluate, and refine a solution for reducing the impacts of human activities on the environment and biodiversity.
- **HS-LS4-6** Create or revise a simulation to test a solution to mitigate adverse impacts of human activity on biodiversity.
