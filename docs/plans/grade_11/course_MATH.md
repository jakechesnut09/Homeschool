# Algebra II (Grade 11)

Algebra II extends the function families of Algebra I to polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions, and introduces complex numbers, sequences and series, and statistical inference. Each unit pairs procedural fluency with modeling tasks drawn from real data, consistent with the 2023 California Mathematics Framework's emphasis on big ideas and data science. Graphing technology (Desmos) is used throughout.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (Algebra II, traditional pathway); 2023 California Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* c · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Functions, Transformations, and Systems (weeks 1–3)
Every function family can be moved and stretched the same ways, and systems model competing constraints.

- Week 1: Parent functions and transformations
- Week 2: Piecewise and absolute value functions
- Week 3: Systems of equations and inequalities

### Unit 2: Quadratic Functions and Complex Numbers (weeks 4–7)
Quadratics can be written in forms that reveal different features, and complex numbers complete their solutions.

- Week 4: Quadratic functions in three forms
- Week 5: Solving quadratic equations
- Week 6: Complex numbers
- Week 7: Quadratic modeling and linear-quadratic systems

### Unit 3: Polynomial Functions (weeks 8–12)
Polynomials behave like integers: they can be factored, divided, and their zeros determine their graphs.

- Week 8: Polynomial operations
- Week 9: Dividing polynomials and the Remainder Theorem
- Week 10: Factoring and zeros of polynomials
- Week 11: Graphing polynomial functions
- Week 12: Polynomial modeling and the Binomial Theorem

### Unit 4: Rational Expressions and Functions (weeks 13–15)
Rational functions are quotients of polynomials whose restrictions create asymptotes and holes.

- Week 13: Rational expressions
- Week 14: Rational functions and graphs
- Week 15: Rational equations and applications

### Unit 5: Radical Functions, Rational Exponents, and Inverses (weeks 16–18)
Radicals and rational exponents are two notations for the same idea, and inverses undo functions.

- Week 16: Rational exponents and radicals
- Week 17: Radical functions, equations, and inverses
- Week 18: Semester 1 review and exam

### Unit 6: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions (weeks 19–23)
Exponential functions model constant-percent change, and logarithms are the tool to solve for the exponent.

- Week 19: Exponential growth and decay
- Week 20: Compound interest and e
- Week 21: Logarithms and their graphs
- Week 22: Properties of logarithms
- Week 23: Exponential and logarithmic equations and modeling

### Unit 7: Trigonometric Functions (weeks 24–28)
The unit circle extends trigonometry to all real numbers and lets us model periodic phenomena.

- Week 24: Radian measure and the unit circle
- Week 25: The Pythagorean identity and other trig values
- Week 26: Graphing sine and cosine
- Week 27: Other trig graphs
- Week 28: Modeling periodic phenomena

### Unit 8: Sequences and Series (weeks 29–30)
Patterns of repeated addition or multiplication can be written recursively, explicitly, and summed.

- Week 29: Arithmetic and geometric sequences
- Week 30: Series and sigma notation

### Unit 9: Statistics and Inference (weeks 31–34)
Randomization and variability let us draw conclusions about populations and treatments from samples.

- Week 31: Data collection: surveys, experiments, and observational studies
- Week 32: Normal distributions
- Week 33: Simulation and margin of error
- Week 34: Comparing treatments and statistics project

### Unit 10: Modeling Capstone and Final Exam (weeks 35–36)
Choosing the right function family is the heart of mathematical modeling.

- Week 35: Modeling capstone
- Week 36: Final exam

- **A-APR.6** Rewrite simple rational expressions in different forms using inspection, long division, or a CAS.
- **A-APR.7** (+) Understand that rational expressions form a system analogous to the rational numbers; add, subtract, multiply, and divide them.

- **A-REI.7** Solve a simple system consisting of a linear equation and a quadratic equation algebraically and graphically.

- **A-SSE.2** Use the structure of an expression to identify ways to rewrite it.
- **A-SSE.3c** Use the properties of exponents to transform expressions for exponential functions.
- **A-SSE.4** Derive the formula for the sum of a finite geometric series and use it to solve problems.
- **F-BF.1b** Combine standard function types using arithmetic operations.
- **F-BF.2** Write arithmetic and geometric sequences both recursively and with an explicit formula, and use them to model situations.

- **F-LE.4** For exponential models, express as a logarithm the solution to ab^(ct) = d and evaluate the logarithm using technology.

- **F-LE.4.3** (CA) Understand and use the properties of logarithms to simplify logarithmic numeric expressions and to identify their approximate values.
- **F-LE.5** Interpret the parameters in a linear or exponential function in terms of a context.
