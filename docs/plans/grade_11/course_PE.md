# Lifetime Fitness (Grade 11)

Lifetime Fitness (CA high school Course 3 level) prepares students to stay active for life. Students assess their own fitness, design and carry out progressive cardio, strength, and flexibility programs, and build skill in lifetime activities such as pickleball, badminton, hiking and orienteering, dance, and yoga. Classroom components cover training science, nutrition, stress management, and evaluating fitness products, ending with a personal 5-year fitness plan.

*Framework:* California Physical Education Model Content Standards (High School Course 3); 2009 California Physical Education Framework  
*Time:* 45 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Fitness Assessment and Goal Setting (weeks 1–3)
Knowing your baseline is the first step to improving and maintaining fitness for life.

- Week 1: Baseline fitness testing
- Week 2: SMART goals and the FITT principle
- Week 3: Warm-up, cool-down, and safety

### Unit 2: Cardiorespiratory Endurance (weeks 4–7)
A strong heart and lungs come from progressive aerobic training at the right intensity.

- Week 4: Heart rate and intensity
- Week 5: Building an aerobic base
- Week 6: Interval and HIIT training
- Week 7: Cardio assessment: 5K challenge

### Unit 3: Resistance Training (weeks 8–12)
Safe, progressive resistance training builds strength, bone density, and confidence at any age.

- Week 8: Resistance training fundamentals
- Week 9: Lower body strength
- Week 10: Upper body and core strength
- Week 11: Designing a strength program
- Week 12: Strength reassessment

### Unit 4: Flexibility, Yoga, and Mobility (weeks 13–15)
Mobility and mindful movement prevent injury and reduce stress.

- Week 13: Flexibility and mobility
- Week 14: Yoga foundations
- Week 15: Mind-body fitness: Pilates and balance

### Unit 5: Lifetime Racquet Sports (weeks 16–19)
Racquet sports combine skill, strategy, and fitness and can be played for a lifetime.

- Week 16: Pickleball fundamentals
- Week 17: Badminton and racquet strategy
- Week 18: Semester fitness reassessment
- Week 19: Tennis and racquet tournament

### Unit 6: Outdoor Pursuits (weeks 20–23)
California's parks and trails offer lifelong opportunities for active recreation.

- Week 20: Hiking and trail safety
- Week 21: Orienteering and navigation
- Week 22: Cycling and active transportation
- Week 23: Aquatics or outdoor challenge

### Unit 7: Dance and Group Fitness (weeks 24–26)
Rhythm and group exercise make fitness social and enjoyable.

- Week 24: Dance fitness
- Week 25: Group fitness: circuits and boot camp
- Week 26: Self-defense basics

### Unit 8: Team Strategy and Officiating (weeks 27–29)
Understanding strategy and rules lets you play, lead, and officiate in community sports.

- Week 27: Volleyball: skills and strategy
- Week 28: Ultimate and invasion-game strategy
- Week 29: Officiating and sport leadership

### Unit 9: Nutrition, Recovery, and Smart Consumerism (weeks 30–32)
Fuel, rest, and informed choices support all physical activity.

- Week 30: Nutrition for performance
- Week 31: Sleep, recovery, and stress
- Week 32: Fitness consumerism

### Unit 10: Personal Fitness Plan and Post-Assessment (weeks 33–36)
A lifelong plan turns knowledge into habit.

- Week 33: Designing a personal fitness plan
- Week 34: Implementing the plan
- Week 35: Final fitness assessment
- Week 36: Five-year fitness plan and final reflection

## Standards

- **PE-HS3-1.1** Demonstrate proficient movement skills in at least two lifetime individual or dual activities (e.g., racquet sports, golf, hiking).
- **PE-HS3-1.2** Demonstrate proficient movement skills in outdoor or aquatic activities and in rhythmic/dance activities.
- **PE-HS3-1.3** Analyze and apply biomechanical principles (force, leverage, stability) to improve performance of complex skills.
- **PE-HS3-1.4** Apply offensive, defensive, and cooperative strategies in team and dual activities.
- **PE-HS3-2.1** Participate in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity at least four days per week and meet health-related fitness standards.
- **PE-HS3-2.2** Assess personal levels of health-related fitness (aerobic capacity, muscular strength and endurance, flexibility, body composition) and interpret results.
- **PE-HS3-2.3** Apply the FITT principle, overload, specificity, and progression to design and implement a personal fitness program.
- **PE-HS3-2.4** Explain and apply safe resistance-training techniques and the physiological effects of training.
- **PE-HS3-2.5** Monitor intensity using heart rate and perceived exertion and adjust training accordingly.
- **PE-HS3-2.6** Explain the relationships among nutrition, rest, physical activity, and long-term health, and evaluate fitness products and claims.
- **PE-HS3-3.1** Identify and apply strategies for maintaining lifelong physical activity, including overcoming barriers and using community resources.
- **PE-HS3-3.2** Analyze how physical activity reduces stress and improves mental and emotional health.
- **PE-HS3-3.3** Demonstrate responsible leadership, sportsmanship, and safe practices, including officiating and risk management.
- **PE-HS3-3.4** Set, monitor, and evaluate personal goals for physical activity and fitness.
