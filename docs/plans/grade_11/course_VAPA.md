# Theatre & Performing Arts (Grade 11)

A year-long introduction to theatre as both art form and craft. Students build ensemble and improvisation skills, train voice and body, learn Stanislavski-based acting technique through monologues and scenes, survey theatre history from ancient Greece to contemporary American and Latinx theatre, explore technical design, write and devise original short plays, and culminate in a directed one-act production for a live audience. Students keep a performance journal and write formal play critiques.

*Framework:* 2019 California Arts Standards for Public Schools – Theatre (High School Proficient); 2020 California Arts Education Framework  
*Time:* 50 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* f · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Ensemble and Improvisation (weeks 1–4)
Theatre begins with trust, listening, and saying 'yes, and' to a scene partner.

- Week 1: Building the ensemble
- Week 2: Improvisation fundamentals
- Week 3: Character through improvisation
- Week 4: Long-form improvisation and storytelling

### Unit 2: Voice and Movement (weeks 5–7)
An actor's body and voice are instruments that must be trained to communicate clearly and expressively.

- Week 5: Voice: breath, resonance, and articulation
- Week 6: Movement and physical theatre
- Week 7: Voice and body together: dialects and choral speaking

### Unit 3: Acting Technique and the Monologue (weeks 8–11)
Truthful acting comes from playing objectives against obstacles within given circumstances.

- Week 8: Stanislavski and given circumstances
- Week 9: Choosing and analyzing a monologue
- Week 10: Monologue rehearsal
- Week 11: Monologue performance and critique

### Unit 4: Theatre History: Greece to Shakespeare (weeks 12–15)
Theatre's conventions grew from the beliefs and playing spaces of each culture and era.

- Week 12: Greek theatre
- Week 13: Medieval, commedia dell'arte, and world traditions
- Week 14: Shakespeare: language and verse
- Week 15: Shakespeare scene work

### Unit 5: Scene Study and Winter Showcase (weeks 16–18)
Scenes come alive when partners listen and react truthfully to each other.

- Week 16: Scene study: partner work
- Week 17: Showcase rehearsal
- Week 18: Winter showcase and semester reflection

### Unit 6: American Realism and Diverse Voices (weeks 19–22)
American playwrights used realism and new forms to examine family, society, and identity.

- Week 19: Realism and Trifles
- Week 20: Our Town and theatrical convention
- Week 21: Diverse American voices: Hansberry, Valdez, and Wilson
- Week 22: Writing a play critique

### Unit 7: Technical Theatre and Design (weeks 23–26)
Sets, lights, costumes, and sound are storytelling tools that must serve the production concept.

- Week 23: Theatre spaces and set design
- Week 24: Lighting and sound design
- Week 25: Costume and makeup design
- Week 26: Production design concept

### Unit 8: Playwriting and Devised Theatre (weeks 27–30)
Original theatre grows from personal and community stories shaped by dramatic structure.

- Week 27: Elements of playwriting
- Week 28: Writing a ten-minute play
- Week 29: Devised theatre
- Week 30: New work festival

### Unit 9: Directing and Final Production (weeks 31–36)
A production unites actors, designers, and director in a shared interpretation for a live audience.

- Week 31: The director's role and play selection
- Week 32: Blocking and staging
- Week 33: Rehearsal process
- Week 34: Technical rehearsal
- Week 35: Final production
- Week 36: Final critique and portfolio

## Standards

- **TH:Cn10.1.HSI** Investigate how cultural perspectives, community ideas, and personal beliefs impact a drama/theatre work.
- **TH:Cn11.1.HSI** Explore how cultural, global, and historic belief systems affect creative choices in a drama/theatre work.
- **TH:Cn11.2.HSI** Research how other theatre artists apply creative processes to tell stories in a devised or scripted drama/theatre work, using theatre research methods.
- **TH:Cr1.1.HSI** Apply basic research to construct ideas about the visual composition of a drama/theatre work; explore physical, vocal, and physiological choices to develop a performance.
- **TH:Cr2.1.HSI** Explore the function of history and culture in the development of a dramatic concept through a critical analysis of original ideas; investigate collaborative roles.
- **TH:Cr3.1.HSI** Practice and revise a devised or scripted drama/theatre work using theatrical staging conventions; refine technical design choices.
- **TH:Pr4.1.HSI** Examine how character relationships assist in telling the story; shape character choices using given circumstances.
- **TH:Pr5.1.HSI** Practice various acting techniques to expand skills in a rehearsal or performance; use researched technical elements to increase the impact of design.
- **TH:Pr6.1.HSI** Perform a scripted drama/theatre work for a specific audience.
- **TH:Re7.1.HSI** Respond to what is seen, felt, and heard in a drama/theatre work to develop criteria for artistic choices.
- **TH:Re8.1.HSI** Analyze and compare artistic choices developed from personal experiences and cultural contexts in multiple drama/theatre works.
- **TH:Re9.1.HSI** Examine a drama/theatre work using supporting evidence and criteria while considering art forms, history, culture, and other disciplines.
