# Physics of the Universe (Grade 11)

The third course of California's high school three-course NGSS model, Physics of the Universe connects motion, forces, energy, electromagnetism, and waves to Earth's interior, the Sun, stars, and the origin of the universe. Every unit is anchored in a phenomenon (a car crash, the San Andreas Fault, a solar panel, the night sky) and includes hands-on labs with household materials, PhET simulations, and engineering design challenges. Students keep a lab notebook and write claim-evidence-reasoning explanations.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (HS Physical and Earth & Space Sciences, Engineering Design); 2016 California Science Framework – Physics of the Universe (three-course model)  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* d · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Forces and Motion: Collisions (weeks 1–5)
Newton's laws and momentum conservation explain and predict what happens in collisions, and engineers use them to save lives.

- Week 1: Describing motion
- Week 2: Newton's first and second laws
- Week 3: Newton's third law and friction
- Week 4: Momentum and impulse
- Week 5: Engineering challenge: egg crash protection

### Unit 2: Gravity and Orbits (weeks 6–8)
The same gravitational force that pulls an apple to Earth keeps planets and satellites in orbit.

- Week 6: Newton's law of universal gravitation
- Week 7: Orbits and Kepler's laws
- Week 8: Coulomb's law and the two forces at a distance

### Unit 3: Energy Conservation and Transfer (weeks 9–13)
Energy is conserved; it transforms between forms and spreads out through thermal transfer.

- Week 9: Work, energy, and power
- Week 10: Conservation of energy
- Week 11: Thermal energy and heat transfer
- Week 12: Engineering: energy conversion devices
- Week 13: Energy unit synthesis

### Unit 4: Electricity and Magnetism (weeks 14–18)
Electric and magnetic fields store energy and exert forces, and moving charges and changing fields generate each other.

- Week 14: Electric circuits
- Week 15: Magnetism and electromagnetism
- Week 16: Electromagnetic induction and generators
- Week 17: Materials science
- Week 18: Semester 1 review and exam

### Unit 5: Waves and Electromagnetic Radiation (weeks 19–23)
Waves carry energy and information; light behaves as both wave and particle.

- Week 19: Wave properties
- Week 20: Sound and wave interactions
- Week 21: The electromagnetic spectrum
- Week 22: Wave-particle duality
- Week 23: Digital information and wave technology

### Unit 6: Earth's Dynamic Interior: Plate Tectonics (weeks 24–26)
Convection in Earth's mantle drives plate motion that shapes California's landscape and hazards.

- Week 24: Earth's interior and convection
- Week 25: Plate tectonics and crustal ages
- Week 26: Natural hazards and human activity

### Unit 7: Stars and the Sun (weeks 27–30)
Nuclear fusion powers stars, and stellar life cycles forge the elements we are made of.

- Week 27: The Sun
- Week 28: Nuclear processes
- Week 29: Stars and the elements
- Week 30: Observing the night sky

### Unit 8: The Big Bang and Earth's Origin (weeks 31–33)
Light from distant galaxies and ancient rocks reveal the history of the universe and our planet.

- Week 31: Evidence for the Big Bang
- Week 32: Composition of matter in the universe
- Week 33: Earth's formation and early history

### Unit 9: Energy for Society: Engineering Capstone (weeks 34–36)
Engineers convert energy to meet human needs while weighing costs, benefits, and environmental impacts.

- Week 34: Energy resources and trade-offs
- Week 35: Engineering capstone: build a generator or wind turbine
- Week 36: Final review and exam

## Standards

- **HS-ESS1-1** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life span of the sun and the role of nuclear fusion in releasing energy that reaches Earth.
- **HS-ESS1-2** Construct an explanation of the Big Bang theory based on astronomical evidence of light spectra, motion of distant galaxies, and composition of matter.
- **HS-ESS1-3** Communicate scientific ideas about the way stars, over their life cycle, produce elements.
- **HS-ESS1-4** Use mathematical or computational representations to predict the motion of orbiting objects in the solar system.
- **HS-ESS1-5** Evaluate evidence of the past and current movements of continental and oceanic crust and the theory of plate tectonics to explain the ages of crustal rocks.
- **HS-ESS1-6** Apply scientific reasoning and evidence from ancient Earth materials, meteorites, and other planetary surfaces to construct an account of Earth's formation and early history.
- **HS-ESS2-1** Develop a model to illustrate how Earth's internal and surface processes operate at different spatial and temporal scales to form continental and ocean-floor features.
- **HS-ESS2-3** Develop a model based on evidence of Earth's interior to describe the cycling of matter by thermal convection.
- **HS-ESS3-1** Construct an explanation based on evidence for how the availability of natural resources, occurrence of natural hazards, and changes in climate have influenced human activity.
- **HS-ESS3-2** Evaluate competing design solutions for developing, managing, and utilizing energy and mineral resources based on cost-benefit ratios.
- **HS-ETS1-1** Analyze a major global challenge to specify qualitative and quantitative criteria and constraints for solutions.
- **HS-ETS1-2** Design a solution to a complex real-world problem by breaking it down into smaller, more manageable problems.
- **HS-ETS1-3** Evaluate a solution to a complex real-world problem based on prioritized criteria and trade-offs.
- **HS-ETS1-4** Use a computer simulation to model the impact of proposed solutions to a complex real-world problem.
- **HS-PS1-8** Develop models to illustrate changes in the composition of the nucleus and energy released during fission, fusion, and radioactive decay.
- **HS-PS2-1** Analyze data to support the claim that Newton's second law describes the relationship among net force, mass, and acceleration.
- **HS-PS2-2** Use mathematical representations to support the claim that total momentum of a system is conserved when there is no net external force.
- **HS-PS2-3** Apply science and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on a macroscopic object during a collision.
- **HS-PS2-4** Use mathematical representations of Newton's Law of Gravitation and Coulomb's Law to describe and predict gravitational and electrostatic forces.
- **HS-PS2-5** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence that an electric current can produce a magnetic field and a changing magnetic field can produce an electric current.
- **HS-PS2-6** Communicate scientific and technical information about why molecular-level structure is important in the functioning of designed materials.
- **HS-PS3-1** Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component of a system when the change in energy of other components and flows are known.
- **HS-PS3-2** Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motions of particles and energy stored in fields.
- **HS-PS3-3** Design, build, and refine a device that works within given constraints to convert one form of energy into another.
- **HS-PS3-4** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence that thermal energy transfer between components of a closed system results in more uniform energy distribution.
- **HS-PS3-5** Develop and use a model of two objects interacting through electric or magnetic fields to illustrate the forces and changes in energy.
- **HS-PS4-1** Use mathematical representations to support a claim regarding relationships among frequency, wavelength, and speed of waves traveling in various media.
- **HS-PS4-2** Evaluate questions about the advantages of using digital transmission and storage of information.
- **HS-PS4-3** Evaluate the claims, evidence, and reasoning behind the idea that electromagnetic radiation can be described by a wave model or a particle model.
- **HS-PS4-4** Evaluate the validity and reliability of claims about the effects of different frequencies of electromagnetic radiation when absorbed by matter.
- **HS-PS4-5** Communicate technical information about how some technological devices use the principles of wave behavior and wave interactions with matter.
