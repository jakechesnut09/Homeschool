# Mathematics 7 (Grade 7)

Grade 7 mathematics centered on proportional reasoning, operations with rational numbers, expressions and equations, geometry of scale, circles, angles, area, surface area and volume, and probability and sampling. The sequence mirrors the Illustrative Mathematics Grade 7 scope used by many California districts, with rich tasks, number talks, and real-world modeling aligned to the 2023 CA Mathematics Framework's big ideas.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics; 2023 CA Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Scale Drawings (weeks 1–4)
Scaled copies keep angles and multiply every length by the same scale factor; areas scale by the square of the factor.

- Week 1: What are scaled copies?
- Week 2: Scale factor, distance and area
- Week 3: Scale drawings and maps
- Week 4: Scale drawing project & unit test

### Unit 2: Introducing Proportional Relationships (weeks 5–9)
A proportional relationship has a constant of proportionality that appears in tables, equations y = kx and graphs through the origin.

- Week 5: Proportional relationships in tables
- Week 6: Unit rates with fractions
- Week 7: Equations for proportional relationships
- Week 8: Graphs of proportional relationships
- Week 9: Proportional reasoning project & unit test

### Unit 3: Measuring Circles (weeks 10–12)
Circumference is proportional to diameter with constant pi; area of a circle is pi r squared.

- Week 10: Circumference
- Week 11: Area of a circle
- Week 12: Composite circular figures & unit test

### Unit 4: Proportional Relationships and Percentages (weeks 13–16)
Percent change, tax, tips, discounts, interest and error are all proportional reasoning.

- Week 13: Percent increase and decrease
- Week 14: Tax, tips, commission and markup
- Week 15: Interest and percent error
- Week 16: Percent project & unit test

### Unit 5: Rational Number Arithmetic (weeks 17–22)
Positive and negative numbers obey consistent rules for all four operations, modeled with number lines and real contexts.

- Week 17: Positive and negative numbers; adding rational numbers
- Week 18: Subtracting rational numbers
- Week 19: Multiplying rational numbers
- Week 20: Dividing rational numbers and decimals
- Week 21: Four operations with rational numbers in context
- Week 22: Rational numbers unit test

### Unit 6: Expressions, Equations, and Inequalities (weeks 23–28)
Equivalent expressions and balanced equations let us solve for unknowns; inequalities describe ranges of solutions.

- Week 23: Tape diagrams and equations
- Week 24: Solving two-step equations
- Week 25: Inequalities
- Week 26: Equivalent expressions
- Week 27: Expressions and equations in context
- Week 28: Unit 6 review & test

### Unit 7: Angles, Triangles, and Prisms (weeks 29–32)
Angle relationships produce equations; triangles are determined by certain measures; prisms have measurable surface area and volume.

- Week 29: Angle relationships
- Week 30: Drawing triangles
- Week 31: Cross sections and volume of prisms
- Week 32: Surface area & unit test

### Unit 8: Probability and Sampling (weeks 33–36)
Probability measures likelihood; random samples let us make inferences and compare populations.

- Week 33: Chance experiments and probability
- Week 34: Compound events and simulation
- Week 35: Sampling and comparing populations
- Week 36: Statistics project & final exam

## Standards

- **7.EE.A.1** Apply properties of operations to add, subtract, factor and expand linear expressions with rational coefficients.
- **7.EE.A.2** Understand that rewriting an expression in different forms can shed light on how quantities are related.
- **7.EE.B.3** Solve multistep real-life problems with positive and negative rational numbers in any form; assess reasonableness with estimation.
- **7.EE.B.4** Use variables to represent quantities and construct simple equations and inequalities to solve problems.
- **7.EE.B.4a** Solve word problems leading to equations px + q = r and p(x + q) = r fluently; compare algebraic and arithmetic solutions.
- **7.EE.B.4b** Solve word problems leading to inequalities px + q > r or px + q < r; graph and interpret the solution set.
- **7.G.A.1** Solve problems involving scale drawings of geometric figures, including computing actual lengths and areas and reproducing a drawing at a different scale.
- **7.G.A.2** Draw geometric shapes with given conditions; construct triangles from three measures of angles or sides and note when a unique triangle results.
- **7.G.A.3** Describe the two-dimensional figures that result from slicing three-dimensional figures.
- **7.G.B.4** Know and use the formulas for the area and circumference of a circle; give an informal derivation of the relationship.
- **7.G.B.5** Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical and adjacent angles to write and solve equations for an unknown angle.
- **7.G.B.6** Solve real-world problems involving area, volume and surface area of 2-D and 3-D objects composed of triangles, quadrilaterals, polygons, cubes and right prisms.
- **7.NS.A.1** Apply and extend understandings of addition and subtraction to add and subtract rational numbers; represent on a number line.
- **7.NS.A.2** Apply and extend understandings of multiplication and division to multiply and divide rational numbers.
- **7.NS.A.2d** Convert a rational number to a decimal using long division; know it terminates or repeats.
- **7.NS.A.3** Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving the four operations with rational numbers.
- **7.RP.A.1** Compute unit rates associated with ratios of fractions, including ratios of lengths, areas and other quantities.
- **7.RP.A.2** Recognize and represent proportional relationships between quantities.
- **7.RP.A.2a** Decide whether two quantities are in a proportional relationship using tables or graphs through the origin.
- **7.RP.A.2b** Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs, equations, diagrams and verbal descriptions.
- **7.RP.A.2c** Represent proportional relationships by equations such as y = kx.
- **7.RP.A.2d** Explain what a point (x, y) on the graph of a proportional relationship means, including (0,0) and (1, r).
- **7.RP.A.3** Use proportional relationships to solve multistep ratio and percent problems (tax, tips, markups, discounts, interest, percent error).
- **7.SP.A.1** Understand that statistics can be used to gain information about a population by examining a representative sample; random sampling.
- **7.SP.A.2** Use data from a random sample to draw inferences about a population; gauge variation in estimates from multiple samples.
- **7.SP.B.3** Informally assess the degree of visual overlap of two numerical data distributions with similar variabilities.
- **7.SP.B.4** Use measures of center and variability from random samples to draw informal comparative inferences about two populations.
- **7.SP.C.5** Understand that the probability of a chance event is a number between 0 and 1 expressing likelihood.
- **7.SP.C.6** Approximate the probability of a chance event by collecting data and observing long-run relative frequency.
- **7.SP.C.7** Develop a probability model and use it to find probabilities of events; compare to observed frequencies.
- **7.SP.C.8** Find probabilities of compound events using organized lists, tables, tree diagrams and simulation.
