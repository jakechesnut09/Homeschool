# Science 7 (Integrated) (Grade 7)

Grade 7 integrated science following the California preferred integrated model: matter is made of atoms that rearrange in chemical reactions, matter cycles and energy flows through organisms and ecosystems, Earth's materials cycle and its surface records a long history, and humans must sustain biodiversity and resources. Every week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials, science notebooks with claim-evidence-reasoning writing, and engineering design challenges.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 CA Science Framework, Grade 7 Preferred Integrated Model  
*Time:* 50 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Atoms, Molecules and Materials (weeks 1–5)
All matter, living and nonliving, is made of atoms that combine into molecules and extended structures; people turn natural resources into synthetic materials.

- Week 1: What is everything made of? Particles and atoms
- Week 2: Elements and the periodic table
- Week 3: Modeling molecules and extended structures
- Week 4: Properties of substances
- Week 5: Natural resources to synthetic materials

### Unit 2: Chemical Reactions and Conservation of Matter (weeks 6–10)
In a chemical reaction atoms rearrange into new substances with new properties, mass is conserved, and energy can be released or absorbed.

- Week 6: Evidence of chemical reactions
- Week 7: Atoms rearrange: chemical equations
- Week 8: Conservation of mass
- Week 9: Energy in reactions: hot and cold
- Week 10: Engineering challenge: design a hand warmer or cold pack

### Unit 3: Matter and Energy in Organisms (weeks 11–14)
Plants capture light energy to build sugar from carbon dioxide and water; organisms rearrange food molecules to grow and release energy.

- Week 11: Photosynthesis: where does a plant's mass come from?
- Week 12: Cellular respiration
- Week 13: Food, energy and the body
- Week 14: Matter and energy in organisms: synthesis

### Unit 4: Ecosystems: Interactions, Matter and Energy (weeks 15–19)
Resource availability and interactions shape populations; matter cycles and energy flows through ecosystems.

- Week 15: Resources and populations
- Week 16: Interactions among organisms
- Week 17: Food webs and energy flow
- Week 18: Decomposers and cycling matter
- Week 19: Ecosystem model project

### Unit 5: Earth's Materials and Resources (weeks 20–24)
Earth's materials cycle through the rock cycle driven by internal heat and the sun, and geologic processes leave resources unevenly distributed.

- Week 20: Minerals and rocks
- Week 21: The rock cycle and energy that drives it
- Week 22: Weathering, erosion and deposition shape the surface
- Week 23: Earth's resources: minerals, energy, groundwater
- Week 24: Resource use and unit assessment

### Unit 6: Earth's History and Plate Tectonics (weeks 25–30)
Rock layers, fossils and plate motions record Earth's 4.6-billion-year history at many time and spatial scales.

- Week 25: Rock layers and relative dating
- Week 26: The geologic time scale
- Week 27: Fossils and the history of life
- Week 28: Continental drift and seafloor evidence
- Week 29: Plate tectonics and California
- Week 30: Changes at many scales and unit assessment

### Unit 7: Sustaining Biodiversity and Earth's Systems (weeks 31–36)
Changes to ecosystems affect populations; human population and consumption impact Earth, and engineers design solutions to monitor and reduce those impacts.

- Week 31: Disrupting ecosystems
- Week 32: Biodiversity and ecosystem services
- Week 33: Human population and resource consumption
- Week 34: Engineering to monitor and reduce human impact
- Week 35: Evaluating competing solutions for biodiversity
- Week 36: Science fair and final assessment

## Standards

- **MS-ESS1-4** Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence from rock strata for how the geologic time scale is used to organize Earth's 4.6-billion-year history.
- **MS-ESS2-1** Develop a model to describe the cycling of Earth's materials and the flow of energy that drives this process.
- **MS-ESS2-2** Construct an explanation based on evidence for how geoscience processes have changed Earth's surface at varying time and spatial scales.
- **MS-ESS2-3** Analyze and interpret data on the distribution of fossils and rocks, continental shapes and seafloor structures to provide evidence of past plate motions.
- **MS-ESS3-1** Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence for how the uneven distributions of Earth's mineral, energy and groundwater resources are the result of past and current geoscience processes.
- **MS-ESS3-3** Apply scientific principles to design a method for monitoring and minimizing a human impact on the environment.
- **MS-ESS3-4** Construct an argument supported by evidence for how increases in human population and per-capita consumption of natural resources impact Earth's systems.
- **MS-ETS1-1** Define the criteria and constraints of a design problem with sufficient precision to ensure a successful solution.
- **MS-ETS1-2** Evaluate competing design solutions using a systematic process to determine how well they meet the criteria and constraints.
- **MS-ETS1-3** Analyze data from tests to determine similarities and differences among several design solutions to identify the best characteristics of each.
- **MS-ETS1-4** Develop a model to generate data for iterative testing and modification of a proposed object, tool or process.
- **MS-LS1-6** Construct a scientific explanation based on evidence for the role of photosynthesis in the cycling of matter and flow of energy into and out of organisms.
- **MS-LS1-7** Develop a model to describe how food is rearranged through chemical reactions forming new molecules that support growth and/or release energy.
- **MS-LS2-1** Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence for the effects of resource availability on organisms and populations in an ecosystem.
- **MS-LS2-2** Construct an explanation that predicts patterns of interactions among organisms across multiple ecosystems.
- **MS-LS2-3** Develop a model to describe the cycling of matter and flow of energy among living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem.
- **MS-LS2-4** Construct an argument supported by empirical evidence that changes to physical or biological components of an ecosystem affect populations.
- **MS-LS2-5** Evaluate competing design solutions for maintaining biodiversity and ecosystem services.
- **MS-LS4-1** Analyze and interpret data for patterns in the fossil record that document the existence, diversity, extinction and change of life forms.
- **MS-PS1-1** Develop models to describe the atomic composition of simple molecules and extended structures.
- **MS-PS1-2** Analyze and interpret data on the properties of substances before and after interaction to determine if a chemical reaction occurred.
- **MS-PS1-3** Gather and make sense of information to describe that synthetic materials come from natural resources and impact society.
- **MS-PS1-5** Develop and use a model to describe how the total number of atoms does not change in a chemical reaction and thus mass is conserved.
- **MS-PS1-6** Undertake a design project to construct, test and modify a device that either releases or absorbs thermal energy by chemical processes.
