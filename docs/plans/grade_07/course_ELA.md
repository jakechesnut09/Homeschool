# English Language Arts 7 (Grade 7)

A literature-rich seventh-grade course built around short stories, a classic novel, poetry, Shakespearean drama, historical fiction and high-interest nonfiction. Students write narratives, literary analyses, argumentative essays and a research paper, while weekly grammar (phrases, clauses, sentence variety, modifiers) and Greek/Latin word study are woven into the reading. Speaking and listening grow through discussions, a dramatic reading, a debate and a multimedia presentation.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts & Literacy; 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 75 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Short Stories: Conflict and Character (weeks 1–5)
Authors build suspense and reveal character through conflict, setting and point of view.

- Week 1: Launching the year; 'Thank You, Ma'am' by Langston Hughes
- Week 2: Suspense: 'The Most Dangerous Game' by Richard Connell
- Week 3: Point of view: 'The Tell-Tale Heart' by Edgar Allan Poe
- Week 4: Personal narrative drafting
- Week 5: Narrative revision, 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi' and unit assessment

### Unit 2: Novel Study: The Call of the Wild (weeks 6–10)
Setting and survival shape who a character becomes; themes develop across a whole novel.

- Week 6: The Call of the Wild, chapters 1–2
- Week 7: The Call of the Wild, chapters 3–4
- Week 8: The Call of the Wild, chapters 5–6
- Week 9: The Call of the Wild, chapter 7 and theme
- Week 10: Literary analysis essay

### Unit 3: Informational Reading and Argument (weeks 11–15)
Strong arguments rest on clear claims, relevant evidence and fair treatment of counterclaims.

- Week 11: Reading informational text: central ideas and structure
- Week 12: Evaluating arguments
- Week 13: Paired texts: same topic, different authors
- Week 14: Drafting the argumentative essay
- Week 15: Argument revision and unit assessment

### Unit 4: Poetry: Sound, Form and Meaning (weeks 16–18)
Poets use sound, figurative language and form to create meaning and emotion.

- Week 16: Sound and figurative language in poetry
- Week 17: Form and meaning: sonnets, free verse and Frost
- Week 18: Writing and performing poetry

### Unit 5: Drama: A Midsummer Night's Dream (weeks 19–22)
Drama's structure and staging choices shape how audiences understand characters and themes.

- Week 19: Shakespeare's world and Act 1 of A Midsummer Night's Dream
- Week 20: Acts 2–3: the forest and the mix-ups
- Week 21: Acts 4–5 and play-within-a-play
- Week 22: Page vs. stage and performance

### Unit 6: Research Writing (weeks 23–26)
Researchers ask focused questions, evaluate sources and synthesize evidence ethically.

- Week 23: Research questions and sources
- Week 24: Note-taking, paraphrasing and avoiding plagiarism
- Week 25: Drafting the research report
- Week 26: Research revision and multimedia presentation

### Unit 7: Historical Fiction: Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry (weeks 27–31)
Historical fiction illuminates real history; comparing it to historical accounts sharpens understanding of both.

- Week 27: Roll of Thunder, Hear My Cry: background and chapters 1–3
- Week 28: Roll of Thunder, chapters 4–6
- Week 29: Roll of Thunder, chapters 7–9
- Week 30: Roll of Thunder, chapters 10–12
- Week 31: Fiction vs. history: comparative essay

### Unit 8: Voices for Change: Speeches, Debate and Portfolio (weeks 32–36)
Speakers and writers persuade through reasoning, evidence and rhetoric; growth is shown through reflection.

- Week 32: Famous speeches: rhetoric and purpose
- Week 33: Comparing authors on one issue
- Week 34: Debate and persuasive speech
- Week 35: Timed writing and year review
- Week 36: Portfolio and reflection

## Standards

- **L.7.1a** Explain the function of phrases and clauses in general and their function in specific sentences.
- **L.7.1b** Choose among simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences to signal differing relationships among ideas.
- **L.7.1c** Place phrases and clauses within a sentence, recognizing and correcting misplaced and dangling modifiers.
- **L.7.2a** Use a comma to separate coordinate adjectives.
- **L.7.2b** Spell correctly.
- **L.7.3a** Choose language that expresses ideas precisely and concisely, eliminating wordiness and redundancy.
- **L.7.4a** Use context as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase.
- **L.7.4b** Use common, grade-appropriate Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues to the meaning of a word.
- **L.7.4c** Consult reference materials to find pronunciation and clarify precise meaning or part of speech.
- **L.7.5a** Interpret figures of speech (e.g., literary, biblical and mythological allusions) in context.
- **L.7.5b** Use the relationship between particular words (synonym/antonym, analogy) to better understand each.
- **L.7.5c** Distinguish among the connotations of words with similar denotations.
- **L.7.6** Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RI.7.1** Cite several pieces of textual evidence to support analysis of what an informational text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RI.7.10** Read and comprehend literary nonfiction in the grades 6–8 complexity band proficiently.
- **RI.7.2** Determine two or more central ideas in a text and analyze their development; provide an objective summary.
- **RI.7.3** Analyze the interactions between individuals, events and ideas in a text.
- **RI.7.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases in informational text, including figurative, connotative and technical meanings.
- **RI.7.5** Analyze the structure an author uses to organize a text and how major sections contribute to the whole.
- **RI.7.6** Determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze how the author distinguishes his or her position from others'.
- **RI.7.7** Compare and contrast a text to an audio, video or multimedia version, analyzing each medium's portrayal of the subject.
- **RI.7.8** Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether reasoning is sound and evidence relevant and sufficient.
- **RI.7.9** Analyze how two or more authors writing about the same topic shape their presentations by emphasizing different evidence or interpretations.
- **RL.7.1** Cite several pieces of textual evidence to support analysis of what a literary text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RL.7.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas and poems, in the grades 6–8 complexity band proficiently.
- **RL.7.2** Determine a theme or central idea of a text and analyze its development; provide an objective summary.
- **RL.7.3** Analyze how particular elements of a story or drama interact (e.g., how setting shapes characters or plot).
- **RL.7.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including figurative and connotative meanings; analyze the impact of rhymes and repetitions of sounds.
- **RL.7.5** Analyze how a drama's or poem's form or structure (e.g., soliloquy, sonnet) contributes to its meaning.
- **RL.7.6** Analyze how an author develops and contrasts the points of view of different characters or narrators.
- **RL.7.7** Compare and contrast a written story, drama or poem to its audio, filmed, staged or multimedia version.
- **RL.7.9** Compare and contrast a fictional portrayal of a time, place or character and a historical account of the same period.
- **SL.7.1** Engage effectively in a range of collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing one's own clearly.
- **SL.7.2** Analyze the main ideas and supporting details presented in diverse media and formats and explain how they clarify a topic.
- **SL.7.3** Delineate a speaker's argument and specific claims, evaluating the soundness of reasoning and relevance of evidence.
- **SL.7.4** Present claims and findings, emphasizing salient points in a focused, coherent manner with pertinent evidence, eye contact, adequate volume and clear pronunciation.
- **SL.7.5** Include multimedia components and visual displays in presentations to clarify claims and findings.
- **SL.7.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when appropriate.
- **W.7.1** Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence, acknowledging alternate or opposing claims.
- **W.7.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks and purposes.
- **W.7.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas through selection, organization and analysis of relevant content.
- **W.7.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using effective technique, relevant details and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.7.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose and audience.
- **W.7.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting or trying a new approach.
- **W.7.6** Use technology, including the Internet, to produce and publish writing and link to and cite sources.
- **W.7.7** Conduct short research projects to answer a question, drawing on several sources and generating additional focused questions.
- **W.7.8** Gather relevant information from multiple print and digital sources; assess credibility; quote or paraphrase while avoiding plagiarism; follow a standard citation format.
- **W.7.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection and research.
