# English 10 (Grade 10)

A tenth-grade English course organized around world literature and the rhetoric of power, conflict, and freedom, paired with Modern World History. Students read short fiction, Greek and Shakespearean tragedy, Things Fall Apart, Night, Fahrenheit 451, A Doll's House, war poetry and landmark speeches. Every unit weaves close reading, grammar and vocabulary, discussion, and a major written product (narrative, literary analysis, argument, and a multi-source research paper).

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for ELA/Literacy (grades 9–10); 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 55 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* b · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: The Short Story: Craft and Suspense (weeks 1–4)
Writers manipulate structure, point of view and detail to create tension and meaning.

- Week 1: Suspense and structure: 'The Most Dangerous Game'
- Week 2: Unreliable narrators: Poe's 'The Cask of Amontillado'
- Week 3: Time and twist: 'An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge' and 'The Necklace'
- Week 4: Narrative writing workshop

### Unit 2: Greek Tragedy: Antigone (weeks 5–8)
Conflicts between individual conscience and state law are as old as drama.

- Week 5: Greek theater and Antigone, Prologue–Ode 1
- Week 6: Antigone: Creon vs. Antigone
- Week 7: Antigone: Downfall and the tragic hero
- Week 8: Argument essay: Antigone and civil disobedience

### Unit 3: Julius Caesar and the Power of Rhetoric (weeks 9–13)
Language can win crowds, topple leaders and justify violence.

- Week 9: Shakespeare's Rome: Julius Caesar Act 1
- Week 10: Julius Caesar Acts 2–3.1: Conspiracy and assassination
- Week 11: The funeral orations: rhetoric in action
- Week 12: Julius Caesar Acts 4–5: Consequences
- Week 13: Speech writing: persuasive oration

### Unit 4: Things Fall Apart: Colonialism and Culture (weeks 14–18)
A culture's story looks different when told from inside it.

- Week 14: Things Fall Apart Part 1: Igbo society
- Week 15: Things Fall Apart Part 1 (cont.): Tradition and change
- Week 16: Things Fall Apart Parts 2–3: Colonial arrival
- Week 17: Literary analysis essay: Things Fall Apart
- Week 18: Semester 1 final

### Unit 5: Literature of War and Witness (weeks 19–23)
Testimony and poetry make the cost of war personal and unforgettable.

- Week 19: Poetry of the Great War
- Week 20: Night by Elie Wiesel, sections 1–3
- Week 21: Night sections 4–9
- Week 22: Informative essay on witness literature
- Week 23: Comparing mediums: war in film and text

### Unit 6: Rhetoric of Freedom: Research and Argument (weeks 24–27)
Seminal speeches and documents shaped how the modern world defines human rights.

- Week 24: Seminal speeches of the 20th century
- Week 25: Letter from Birmingham Jail and the UDHR
- Week 26: Research paper: question, sources and notes
- Week 27: Research paper: drafting and presenting

### Unit 7: Dystopia: Fahrenheit 451 (weeks 28–31)
Societies that suppress ideas reveal what makes free thought valuable.

- Week 28: Fahrenheit 451 Part 1: The Hearth and the Salamander
- Week 29: Fahrenheit 451 Parts 2–3
- Week 30: Orwell and language
- Week 31: Dystopian writing and Unit 7 assessment

### Unit 8: A Doll's House and Poetry of Identity (weeks 32–34)
Individuals push against the roles their societies assign them.

- Week 32: A Doll's House Acts 1–2
- Week 33: A Doll's House Act 3 and poetry of identity
- Week 34: Writing poetry and dramatic monologue

### Unit 9: Portfolio, Reflection and Final Assessment (weeks 35–36)
Growth as a reader and writer is visible when you revisit and revise your work.

- Week 35: Portfolio revision
- Week 36: Final exam and presentation

## Standards

- **L.9-10.1** Demonstrate command of grammar and usage, including parallel structure and various types of phrases and clauses.
- **L.9-10.2** Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation, and spelling, including semicolons, colons, and conjunctive adverbs.
- **L.9-10.3** Apply knowledge of language to make effective choices for meaning or style; write and edit work to conform to a style manual (MLA).
- **L.9-10.4** Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, word parts (roots and affixes), and reference materials.
- **L.9-10.5** Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships, and nuances in word meanings.
- **L.9-10.6** Acquire and use accurately general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RI.9-10.1** Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what an informational text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RI.9-10.10** Read and comprehend literary nonfiction in the grades 9–10 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
- **RI.9-10.2** Determine a central idea of an informational text, analyze its development, and provide an objective summary.
- **RI.9-10.3** Analyze how an author unfolds an analysis or series of ideas or events and the connections drawn between them.
- **RI.9-10.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases in informational text and analyze the cumulative impact of word choice on meaning and tone.
- **RI.9-10.5** Analyze in detail how an author's ideas or claims are developed and refined by particular sentences, paragraphs, or sections.
- **RI.9-10.6** Determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze how rhetoric advances it.
- **RI.9-10.7** Analyze various accounts of a subject told in different mediums, determining which details are emphasized in each.
- **RI.9-10.8** Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing reasoning and evidence and identifying false statements and fallacies.
- **RI.9-10.9** Analyze seminal U.S. and world documents of historical and literary significance, including how they address related themes and concepts.
- **RL.9-10.1** Cite strong and thorough textual evidence to support analysis of what a literary text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RL.9-10.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas, and poems, in the grades 9–10 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
- **RL.9-10.2** Determine a theme of a literary text, analyze its development, and provide an objective summary.
- **RL.9-10.3** Analyze how complex characters develop, interact, and advance the plot or develop the theme.
- **RL.9-10.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including figurative language, and analyze the cumulative impact of word choice on meaning and tone.
- **RL.9-10.5** Analyze how an author's choices about structure, order of events, and manipulation of time create mystery, tension, or surprise.
- **RL.9-10.6** Analyze a particular point of view or cultural experience reflected in a work of literature from outside the United States.
- **RL.9-10.7** Analyze the representation of a subject or key scene in two different artistic mediums.
- **RL.9-10.9** Analyze how an author draws on and transforms source material in a specific work.
- **SL.9-10.1** Initiate and participate effectively in collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing their own clearly and persuasively.
- **SL.9-10.2** Integrate multiple sources of information presented in diverse media or formats, evaluating credibility and accuracy.
- **SL.9-10.3** Evaluate a speaker's point of view, reasoning, and use of evidence and rhetoric.
- **SL.9-10.4** Present information, findings, and evidence clearly, concisely, and logically so listeners can follow the line of reasoning.
- **SL.9-10.5** Make strategic use of digital media in presentations to enhance understanding.
- **SL.9-10.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English.
- **W.9-10.1** Write arguments to support claims using valid reasoning and relevant and sufficient evidence.
- **W.9-10.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks, purposes, and audiences.
- **W.9-10.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine and convey complex ideas clearly and accurately.
- **W.9-10.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using effective technique, well-chosen details, and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.9-10.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose, and audience.
- **W.9-10.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach.
- **W.9-10.6** Use technology, including the Internet, to produce, publish, and update individual or shared writing products.
- **W.9-10.7** Conduct short and sustained research projects to answer a question or solve a problem, synthesizing multiple sources.
- **W.9-10.8** Gather relevant information from multiple authoritative sources, assess usefulness, and integrate information while avoiding plagiarism and following a standard citation format.
- **W.9-10.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection, and research.
