# Geometry (Grade 10)

A full-year high school Geometry course following the CA Common Core traditional pathway. Students build geometry from transformations, write two-column, paragraph and coordinate proofs, and apply congruence, similarity, right-triangle trigonometry, circles, area and volume to real modeling problems. The course closes with the CA Geometry conditional probability standards. Compass, straightedge, patty paper and free dynamic geometry software (GeoGebra) are used throughout.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (Geometry); 2023 CA Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* c · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Foundations and Constructions (weeks 1–3)
Geometry is built from precise definitions and tools.

- Week 1: Points, lines, planes and precise definitions
- Week 2: Compass-and-straightedge constructions
- Week 3: Midpoint, distance, and inscribed polygons

### Unit 2: Transformations and Rigid Motion (weeks 4–7)
Congruence means one figure maps onto another by rigid motions.

- Week 4: Translations and reflections
- Week 5: Rotations and symmetry
- Week 6: Compositions of transformations
- Week 7: Congruence through rigid motion

### Unit 3: Congruence and Proof (weeks 8–11)
Logical arguments justify why geometric relationships always hold.

- Week 8: Logic and reasoning
- Week 9: Proving theorems about lines and angles
- Week 10: Proving theorems about triangles
- Week 11: Triangle congruence proofs

### Unit 4: Quadrilaterals and Coordinate Proof (weeks 12–14)
Algebra on the coordinate plane can prove geometric theorems.

- Week 12: Parallelograms
- Week 13: Rectangles, rhombi, squares, trapezoids and kites
- Week 14: Coordinate proofs

### Unit 5: Similarity (weeks 15–18)
Dilations create proportional figures that preserve angles.

- Week 15: Dilations
- Week 16: Similar triangles
- Week 17: Proportionality theorems
- Week 18: Semester 1 review and final

### Unit 6: Right Triangles and Trigonometry (weeks 19–22)
Similar right triangles give fixed side ratios for each angle.

- Week 19: Pythagorean theorem and special right triangles
- Week 20: Trigonometric ratios
- Week 21: Solving right triangles and applications
- Week 22: Laws of Sines and Cosines

### Unit 7: Circles (weeks 23–26)
All circles are similar; angles, arcs and chords relate in predictable ways.

- Week 23: Circle basics, arcs and similarity
- Week 24: Inscribed angles and tangents
- Week 25: Arc length, sectors and radians
- Week 26: Equations of circles and parabolas

### Unit 8: Area, Volume and Modeling (weeks 27–30)
Formulas for measurement can be derived and applied to design.

- Week 27: Area of polygons and composite figures
- Week 28: Three-dimensional figures and cross sections
- Week 29: Volume
- Week 30: Modeling with density and design

### Unit 9: Conditional Probability (weeks 31–34)
Probability quantifies uncertainty and depends on given information.

- Week 31: Sample spaces and set operations
- Week 32: Independence and conditional probability
- Week 33: Two-way tables
- Week 34: Multiplication rule, counting and fair decisions

### Unit 10: Course Review and Final (weeks 35–36)
Geometry ideas connect across transformations, proof and measurement.

- Week 35: Cumulative review
- Week 36: Final exam and reflection

## Standards

- **G-C.1** Prove that all circles are similar.
- **G-C.2** Identify and describe relationships among inscribed angles, radii, and chords.
- **G-C.3** Construct inscribed and circumscribed circles of a triangle; prove properties of angles of an inscribed quadrilateral.
- **G-C.5** Derive arc length using similarity; define radian measure; derive the area of a sector.
- **G-CO.1** Know precise definitions of angle, circle, perpendicular line, parallel line, and line segment.
- **G-CO.10** Prove theorems about triangles.
- **G-CO.11** Prove theorems about parallelograms.
- **G-CO.12** Make formal geometric constructions with compass and straightedge and other tools.
- **G-CO.13** Construct an equilateral triangle, a square, and a regular hexagon inscribed in a circle.
- **G-CO.2** Represent transformations in the plane; describe transformations as functions of points.
- **G-CO.3** Describe the rotations and reflections that carry a polygon onto itself.
- **G-CO.4** Develop definitions of rotations, reflections, and translations.
- **G-CO.5** Draw a transformed figure and specify a sequence of transformations that maps one figure onto another.
- **G-CO.6** Use rigid motions to predict effects on figures and decide if two figures are congruent.
- **G-CO.7** Use rigid motions to show two triangles are congruent iff corresponding parts are congruent.
- **G-CO.8** Explain ASA, SAS, and SSS congruence criteria from rigid motions.
- **G-CO.9** Prove theorems about lines and angles.
- **G-GMD.1** Give informal arguments for circumference, area of circle, volume of cylinder, pyramid, cone formulas.
- **G-GMD.3** Use volume formulas for cylinders, pyramids, cones, and spheres to solve problems.
- **G-GMD.4** Identify cross-sections of 3-D objects and 3-D objects generated by rotations.
- **G-GMD.5** (CA) Know that the effect of a scale factor k > 0 on length, area, and volume is to multiply each by k, k², and k³.
- **G-GPE.1** Derive the equation of a circle by completing the square.
- **G-GPE.2** Derive the equation of a parabola given a focus and directrix.
- **G-GPE.4** Use coordinates to prove simple geometric theorems algebraically.
- **G-GPE.5** Prove the slope criteria for parallel and perpendicular lines and use them.
- **G-GPE.6** Find the point on a segment that partitions it in a given ratio.
- **G-GPE.7** Use coordinates to compute perimeters and areas of polygons.
- **G-MG.1** Use geometric shapes, their measures, and properties to describe objects.
- **G-MG.2** Apply concepts of density based on area and volume in modeling situations.
- **G-MG.3** Apply geometric methods to solve design problems.
- **G-SRT.1** Verify properties of dilations given by a center and scale factor.
- **G-SRT.10** (+) Prove the Laws of Sines and Cosines and use them to solve problems.
- **G-SRT.11** (+) Apply the Laws of Sines and Cosines to find unknown measures in triangles.
- **G-SRT.2** Use similarity transformations to decide if two figures are similar.
- **G-SRT.3** Use similarity transformations to establish the AA criterion.
- **G-SRT.4** Prove theorems about triangles using similarity (triangle proportionality, Pythagorean theorem).
- **G-SRT.5** Use congruence and similarity criteria to solve problems and prove relationships.
- **G-SRT.6** Understand that trigonometric ratios are properties of angles in right triangles via similarity.
- **G-SRT.7** Explain and use the relationship between sine and cosine of complementary angles.
- **G-SRT.8** Use trigonometric ratios and the Pythagorean theorem to solve right triangles in applied problems.
- **G-SRT.9** (+) Derive the formula A = ½ab sin(C) for the area of a triangle.
- **S-CP.1** Describe events as subsets of a sample space using unions, intersections, complements.
- **S-CP.2** Understand independence: P(A and B) = P(A)P(B).
- **S-CP.3** Understand conditional probability P(A|B).
- **S-CP.4** Construct and interpret two-way frequency tables.
- **S-CP.5** Recognize and explain conditional probability and independence in everyday situations.
- **S-CP.6** Find conditional probability as a fraction of B's outcomes that also belong to A.
- **S-CP.7** Apply the Addition Rule P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) – P(A and B).
- **S-CP.8** (+) Apply the general Multiplication Rule.
- **S-CP.9** (+) Use permutations and combinations to compute probabilities.
- **S-MD.6** (+) Use probabilities to make fair decisions.
- **S-MD.7** (+) Analyze decisions and strategies using probability concepts.
