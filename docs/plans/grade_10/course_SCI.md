# Chemistry in the Earth System (Grade 10)

The second course in California's three-course NGSS high school model, connecting chemistry to Earth's systems. Students investigate combustion, atomic structure and the origin of elements in stars, bonding and material properties, energy flow in the Earth system, reaction stoichiometry, the chemistry of climate change, reaction rates and equilibrium, and ocean acidification. Every week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials or a PhET simulation, plus data analysis and claim-evidence-reasoning writing.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (HS); 2016 CA Science Framework – Chemistry in the Earth System  
*Time:* 60 min/day
*UC/CSU a–g:* d · *Credits:* 10.0

## Units

### Unit 1: Combustion: Matter and Energy (weeks 1–4)
Burning fuel rearranges atoms and releases stored energy that powers society and changes the atmosphere.

- Week 1: Lab safety, measurement and the phenomenon of fire
- Week 2: What happens when things burn?
- Week 3: Energy from fuels
- Week 4: Combustion and the atmosphere

### Unit 2: Atoms, Elements and the Stars (weeks 5–9)
Every element on Earth was forged in stars; atomic structure explains periodic patterns.

- Week 5: Atomic structure and history of the atom
- Week 6: Electrons and light
- Week 7: Periodic table trends
- Week 8: Nuclear chemistry
- Week 9: Stars forge elements

### Unit 3: Bonding, Structure and Materials (weeks 10–14)
Electrical forces between particles determine a substance's properties and uses.

- Week 10: Ionic bonding
- Week 11: Covalent bonding and molecular shape
- Week 12: Intermolecular forces and properties
- Week 13: Metallic bonding and designed materials
- Week 14: Naming, moles and molar mass

### Unit 4: Heat and Energy in the Earth System (weeks 15–18)
Thermal energy flows from hot to cold, driving Earth's interior convection and surface systems.

- Week 15: Thermal energy and particle motion
- Week 16: Specific heat and the second law
- Week 17: Earth's interior and convection
- Week 18: Semester 1 review and final

### Unit 5: Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry (weeks 19–23)
Atoms are conserved in reactions, so quantities can be predicted mathematically.

- Week 19: Chemical equations and conservation of mass
- Week 20: Types of chemical reactions
- Week 21: Stoichiometry
- Week 22: Limiting reactants
- Week 23: Bond energy and energy in reactions

### Unit 6: Chemistry of Climate Change (weeks 24–27)
Greenhouse gases alter Earth's energy balance; carbon cycles among spheres.

- Week 24: Earth's energy budget
- Week 25: The carbon cycle
- Week 26: Climate data and feedbacks
- Week 27: Climate solutions

### Unit 7: Reaction Rates and Equilibrium (weeks 28–31)
Collisions control reaction speed, and systems shift to counter changes.

- Week 28: Collision theory and reaction rates
- Week 29: Dynamic equilibrium
- Week 30: Le Chatelier's principle
- Week 31: Engineering an equilibrium system

### Unit 8: Water, Acids and Ocean Acidification (weeks 32–34)
Water's unique properties shape Earth, and dissolved CO₂ is changing ocean chemistry.

- Week 32: Water's properties and Earth's surface
- Week 33: Solutions, acids and bases
- Week 34: Ocean acidification

### Unit 9: Capstone and Final Assessment (weeks 35–36)
Chemistry explains and helps solve real environmental challenges.

- Week 35: Capstone: Chemistry solution for an Earth system challenge
- Week 36: Final exam and capstone presentation

## Standards

- **HS-ESS1-1** Develop a model based on evidence to illustrate the life span of the Sun and the role of nuclear fusion in releasing energy that reaches Earth.
- **HS-ESS1-3** Communicate scientific ideas about the way stars, over their life cycle, produce elements.
- **HS-ESS2-2** Analyze geoscience data to make the claim that one change to Earth's surface can create feedbacks that cause changes to other Earth systems.
- **HS-ESS2-3** Develop a model based on evidence of Earth's interior to describe the cycling of matter by thermal convection.
- **HS-ESS2-4** Use a model to describe how variations in the flow of energy into and out of Earth's systems result in changes in climate.
- **HS-ESS2-5** Plan and conduct an investigation of the properties of water and its effects on Earth materials and surface processes.
- **HS-ESS2-6** Develop a quantitative model to describe the cycling of carbon among the hydrosphere, atmosphere, geosphere, and biosphere.
- **HS-ESS3-4** Evaluate or refine a technological solution that reduces impacts of human activities on natural systems.
- **HS-ESS3-5** Analyze geoscience data and results from global climate models to make an evidence-based forecast of the current rate of global or regional climate change.
- **HS-ESS3-6** Use a computational representation to illustrate relationships among Earth systems and how those relationships are being modified by human activity.
- **HS-ETS1-1** Analyze a major global challenge to specify qualitative and quantitative criteria and constraints for solutions.
- **HS-ETS1-3** Evaluate a solution to a complex real-world problem based on prioritized criteria and trade-offs.
- **HS-PS1-1** Use the periodic table as a model to predict relative properties of elements based on outer-energy-level electrons.
- **HS-PS1-2** Construct and revise an explanation for the outcome of a simple chemical reaction based on atomic structure and periodic trends.
- **HS-PS1-3** Plan and conduct an investigation to gather evidence to compare bulk-scale structure of substances to infer strength of electrical forces between particles.
- **HS-PS1-4** Develop a model to illustrate that release or absorption of energy from a reaction depends on changes in total bond energy.
- **HS-PS1-5** Apply scientific principles and evidence to explain effects of temperature and concentration on reaction rate.
- **HS-PS1-6** Refine the design of a chemical system by specifying a change in conditions that would produce increased amounts of products at equilibrium.
- **HS-PS1-7** Use mathematical representations to support the claim that atoms, and therefore mass, are conserved during a chemical reaction.
- **HS-PS1-8** Develop models to illustrate changes in the composition of the nucleus and energy released during fission, fusion, and radioactive decay.
- **HS-PS2-6** Communicate why molecular-level structure is important in the functioning of designed materials.
- **HS-PS3-1** Create a computational model to calculate the change in energy of one component in a system when the change in energy of others is known.
- **HS-PS3-2** Develop and use models to illustrate that energy at the macroscopic scale can be accounted for as motion of particles and energy stored in fields.
- **HS-PS3-3** Design, build, and refine a device that converts one form of energy into another within constraints.
- **HS-PS3-4** Plan and conduct an investigation to show that transfer of thermal energy in a closed system results in more uniform energy distribution (second law).
