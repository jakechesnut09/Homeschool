# Science 8 (Integrated) (Grade 8)

Integrated Grade 8 science following the California preferred integrated model. Students investigate forces, motion and energy; gravity and the solar system; waves and information technology; Earth's history and the fossil record; heredity; and natural selection. Each week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials, data analysis, and written scientific explanations, ending with an engineering design capstone.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 California Science Framework (Preferred Integrated Model, Grade 8)  
*Time:* 50 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Forces and Motion (weeks 1–5)
An object's motion changes only when the forces on it are unbalanced, and every force comes in an equal and opposite pair.

- Week 1: Describing motion
- Week 2: Forces, net force and friction
- Week 3: Newton's second law: force, mass and acceleration
- Week 4: Newton's third law and collisions
- Week 5: Engineering challenge: protect the egg

### Unit 2: Energy in Motion and at a Distance (weeks 6–9)
Kinetic energy depends on mass and speed; energy stored in fields changes with position and transfers whenever motion changes.

- Week 6: Kinetic energy
- Week 7: Potential energy and fields
- Week 8: Energy transfer and transformation
- Week 9: Collisions and energy review

### Unit 3: Gravity and the Solar System (weeks 10–14)
Gravity, which depends on mass, shapes orbits, tides, and the cyclic patterns we see in the sky.

- Week 10: Gravity is attractive and depends on mass
- Week 11: Moon phases and eclipses
- Week 12: Seasons
- Week 13: Gravity, orbits and galaxies
- Week 14: Scale of the solar system

### Unit 4: Waves, Light, and Information (weeks 15–19)
Waves carry energy and information; materials reflect, absorb or transmit them, and digital signals carry information reliably.

- Week 15: Wave properties
- Week 16: Sound waves
- Week 17: Light: reflection, refraction, absorption, transmission
- Week 18: Light and color; semester review
- Week 19: Analog vs digital signals

### Unit 5: Earth's History and the Fossil Record (weeks 20–24)
Rock layers and fossils record 4.6 billion years of Earth and life history and reveal shared ancestry.

- Week 20: Rock layers and relative dating
- Week 21: The geologic time scale
- Week 22: Patterns in the fossil record
- Week 23: Anatomical similarities
- Week 24: Embryological evidence

### Unit 6: Heredity: Genes and Reproduction (weeks 25–29)
Genes code for proteins that shape traits; reproduction and mutations create variation, and people now influence inheritance with technology.

- Week 25: Asexual and sexual reproduction
- Week 26: Genes, chromosomes and Punnett squares
- Week 27: DNA, genes and proteins
- Week 28: Mutations
- Week 29: Technology and inheritance

### Unit 7: Natural Selection and Adaptation (weeks 30–33)
Variation plus environmental pressures change trait frequencies in populations over generations.

- Week 30: Variation and survival
- Week 31: Natural selection in numbers
- Week 32: Case studies: finches, moths and bacteria
- Week 33: Adaptation and unit review

### Unit 8: Engineering Design Capstone (weeks 34–36)
Engineers define problems, test competing solutions, and iterate using data.

- Week 34: Capstone: define and design
- Week 35: Capstone: build, test, iterate
- Week 36: Capstone presentation and final exam

## Standards

- **MS-ESS1-1** Develop and use a model of the Earth-sun-moon system to describe cyclic patterns of lunar phases, eclipses, and seasons.
- **MS-ESS1-2** Develop and use a model describing the role of gravity in the motions within galaxies and the solar system.
- **MS-ESS1-3** Analyze and interpret data to determine scale properties of objects in the solar system.
- **MS-ESS1-4** Construct an explanation from rock-strata evidence for how the geologic time scale organizes Earth's 4.6-billion-year history.
- **MS-ETS1-1** Define the criteria and constraints of a design problem.
- **MS-ETS1-2** Evaluate competing design solutions using a systematic process.
- **MS-ETS1-3** Analyze data from tests to determine similarities and differences among design solutions and combine their best characteristics.
- **MS-ETS1-4** Develop a model to generate data for iterative testing and modification of a proposed design.
- **MS-LS3-1** Develop and use a model of why mutations in genes may have harmful, beneficial, or neutral effects on the structure and function of the organism.
- **MS-LS3-2** Model why asexual reproduction produces genetically identical offspring and sexual reproduction produces genetic variation.
- **MS-LS4-1** Analyze and interpret data for patterns in the fossil record documenting the existence, diversity, extinction, and change of life forms.
- **MS-LS4-2** Explain anatomical similarities and differences among modern and fossil organisms to infer evolutionary relationships.
- **MS-LS4-3** Analyze pictorial data to compare patterns of similarity in embryological development across species.
- **MS-LS4-4** Explain how genetic variations of traits in a population increase some individuals' probability of surviving and reproducing.
- **MS-LS4-5** Gather and synthesize information about technologies that have changed the way humans influence the inheritance of desired traits.
- **MS-LS4-6** Use mathematical representations to explain how natural selection may lead to increases and decreases of specific traits over time.
- **MS-PS2-1** Apply Newton's Third Law to design a solution to a problem involving the motion of two colliding objects.
- **MS-PS2-2** Plan an investigation showing that the change in an object's motion depends on the sum of the forces on it and its mass.
- **MS-PS2-4** Argue from evidence that gravitational interactions are attractive and depend on the masses of interacting objects.
- **MS-PS3-1** Construct and interpret graphical displays of data describing the relationships of kinetic energy to mass and to speed.
- **MS-PS3-2** Develop a model showing that changing the arrangement of objects interacting at a distance changes the potential energy stored.
- **MS-PS3-5** Argue that when the kinetic energy of an object changes, energy is transferred to or from the object.
- **MS-PS4-1** Use mathematical representations to describe a simple model for waves including how amplitude relates to energy.
- **MS-PS4-2** Develop and use a model describing how waves are reflected, absorbed, or transmitted through various materials.
- **MS-PS4-3** Integrate information to support the claim that digitized signals are a more reliable way to encode and transmit information than analog signals.
