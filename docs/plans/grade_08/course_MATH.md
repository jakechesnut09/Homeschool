# Mathematics 8 (Grade 8)

Grade 8 mathematics following the California Common Core sequence used by Illustrative Mathematics Grade 8. Students study rigid transformations, similarity and slope, linear relationships and equations, systems of equations, functions, volume, bivariate data, exponents and scientific notation, and the Pythagorean theorem with irrational numbers. Every week mixes concept-building tasks, practice, a hands-on or graphing activity, and a Friday check.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics; 2023 CA Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Rigid Transformations and Congruence (weeks 1–4)
Translations, rotations, and reflections move figures without changing size or shape, so congruent figures are linked by rigid motions.

- Week 1: Moving figures: translations, rotations, reflections
- Week 2: Properties of rigid motions and coordinates
- Week 3: Congruence through sequences of rigid motions
- Week 4: Angles: parallel lines, transversals and triangles

### Unit 2: Dilations, Similarity, and Slope (weeks 5–7)
Dilations scale figures; similar triangles explain why a line has a constant slope.

- Week 5: Dilations
- Week 6: Similarity
- Week 7: Slope from similar triangles

### Unit 3: Linear Relationships (weeks 8–11)
A linear relationship has a constant rate of change (slope) and an initial value, and can be shown in tables, graphs, equations and words.

- Week 8: Proportional relationships and graphs
- Week 9: Representing linear relationships
- Week 10: Slope signs, special lines, writing equations
- Week 11: Solutions to linear equations in two variables

### Unit 4: Linear Equations and Linear Systems (weeks 12–16)
Equations are solved by keeping them balanced; the solution to a system is the point that makes both equations true.

- Week 12: Balanced moves and solving equations
- Week 13: Equations with variables on both sides and rational coefficients
- Week 14: How many solutions?
- Week 15: Systems of linear equations by graphing
- Week 16: Solving systems algebraically

### Unit 5: Functions and Volume (weeks 17–21)
A function gives exactly one output per input; volume formulas for cylinders, cones and spheres are functions of their dimensions.

- Week 17: Introduction to functions
- Week 18: Representing and comparing functions; semester review
- Week 19: Linear functions, rates of change, and graphs of situations
- Week 20: Volume of cylinders and cones
- Week 21: Spheres and dimensions as functions

### Unit 6: Associations in Data (weeks 22–24)
Scatter plots and two-way tables reveal patterns of association that can be modeled and interpreted.

- Week 22: Scatter plots and patterns
- Week 23: Lines of fit and linear models
- Week 24: Two-way tables and categorical association

### Unit 7: Exponents and Scientific Notation (weeks 25–28)
Exponent rules follow from repeated multiplication and let us write and compute with very large and very small numbers.

- Week 25: Exponent rules for products, quotients and powers
- Week 26: Zero, negative exponents and different bases
- Week 27: Powers of 10 and scientific notation
- Week 28: Operations with scientific notation

### Unit 8: Pythagorean Theorem and Irrational Numbers (weeks 29–33)
In a right triangle a^2 + b^2 = c^2, and many side lengths are irrational numbers we can locate and approximate.

- Week 29: Square roots and side lengths of squares
- Week 30: The Pythagorean theorem and its converse
- Week 31: Applying the Pythagorean theorem
- Week 32: Cube roots and rational vs irrational numbers
- Week 33: Approximating irrational numbers

### Unit 9: Putting It All Together (weeks 34–36)
Grade 8 tools (linear models, systems, volume, data) combine to solve open-ended real-world problems.

- Week 34: Modeling: Fermi problems and estimation
- Week 35: Modeling project: planning a small business
- Week 36: Final review and cumulative exam

## Standards

- **8.EE.A.1** Know and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions.
- **8.EE.A.2** Use square root and cube root symbols to represent solutions to x^2 = p and x^3 = p; know small perfect squares and cubes; know sqrt 2 is irrational.
- **8.EE.A.3** Use numbers expressed as a single digit times an integer power of 10 to estimate very large or very small quantities and compare them.
- **8.EE.A.4** Perform operations with numbers in scientific notation and choose units of appropriate size; interpret technology output.
- **8.EE.B.5** Graph proportional relationships, interpreting the unit rate as the slope; compare proportional relationships represented in different ways.
- **8.EE.B.6** Use similar triangles to explain why slope is the same between any two points on a line; derive y = mx and y = mx + b.
- **8.EE.C.7a** Give examples of linear equations in one variable with one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solutions.
- **8.EE.C.7b** Solve linear equations with rational coefficients, including equations needing the distributive property and collecting like terms.
- **8.EE.C.8a** Understand that solutions to a system of two linear equations correspond to points of intersection of their graphs.
- **8.EE.C.8b** Solve systems of two linear equations algebraically and estimate solutions by graphing; solve simple cases by inspection.
- **8.EE.C.8c** Solve real-world and mathematical problems leading to two linear equations in two variables.
- **8.F.A.1** Understand that a function assigns to each input exactly one output; the graph is the set of ordered pairs.
- **8.F.A.2** Compare properties of two functions each represented in a different way (algebraically, graphically, numerically, verbally).
- **8.F.A.3** Interpret y = mx + b as defining a linear function whose graph is a straight line; give examples of nonlinear functions.
- **8.F.B.4** Construct a function to model a linear relationship; determine and interpret rate of change and initial value.
- **8.F.B.5** Describe qualitatively the functional relationship between two quantities by analyzing a graph; sketch graphs from verbal descriptions.
- **8.G.A.1** Verify experimentally the properties of rotations, reflections, and translations (lines to lines, angles and lengths preserved, parallel lines preserved).
- **8.G.A.2** Understand that two figures are congruent if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of rigid motions; describe the sequence.
- **8.G.A.3** Describe the effect of dilations, translations, rotations, and reflections on two-dimensional figures using coordinates.
- **8.G.A.4** Understand that two figures are similar if one can be obtained from the other by rigid motions and dilations; describe the sequence.
- **8.G.A.5** Use informal arguments about the angle sum and exterior angle of triangles, angles formed by parallel lines and a transversal, and the AA criterion for similarity.
- **8.G.B.6** Explain a proof of the Pythagorean theorem and its converse.
- **8.G.B.7** Apply the Pythagorean theorem to find unknown side lengths in right triangles in two and three dimensions.
- **8.G.B.8** Apply the Pythagorean theorem to find the distance between two points in a coordinate system.
- **8.G.C.9** Know the formulas for the volumes of cones, cylinders, and spheres and use them to solve problems.
- **8.NS.A.1** Know that numbers that are not rational are irrational; every number has a decimal expansion; convert repeating decimals to fractions.
- **8.NS.A.2** Use rational approximations of irrational numbers to compare them, locate them on a number line, and estimate expressions.
- **8.SP.A.1** Construct and interpret scatter plots for bivariate data; describe clustering, outliers, positive or negative association, linear and nonlinear association.
- **8.SP.A.2** Know that straight lines model linear association; informally fit a line and judge closeness of fit.
- **8.SP.A.3** Use the equation of a linear model to solve problems, interpreting slope and intercept.
- **8.SP.A.4** Understand patterns of association in categorical data using two-way tables and relative frequencies.
