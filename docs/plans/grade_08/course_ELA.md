# English Language Arts 8 (Grade 8)

Grade 8 English Language Arts built around classic short stories, S. E. Hinton's The Outsiders, founding-era and abolition speeches, the Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, American poetry, the play The Diary of Anne Frank, Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream with its Ovidian source, and a culminating research project. Each week integrates close reading, text-based discussion, writing (narrative, argument, literary analysis, informative and research), grammar (verbals, voice and mood, punctuation) and Greek/Latin vocabulary study, plus 20–30 minutes of daily independent reading.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts & Literacy; 2014 California ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 75 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Short Stories: Suspense, Irony and Point of View (weeks 1–5)
Authors create suspense and irony by controlling what characters and readers know.

- Week 1: 'The Tell-Tale Heart': unreliable narrators
- Week 2: 'The Monkey's Paw': foreshadowing and dialogue
- Week 3: 'The Gift of the Magi': irony and theme
- Week 4: 'The Most Dangerous Game' and narrative techniques
- Week 5: Narrative writing workshop and unit assessment

### Unit 2: Novel Study: The Outsiders (weeks 6–10)
Loyalty, class and identity shape who we become, and a novel's theme grows through characters' choices.

- Week 6: The Outsiders, chapters 1–3: characters and class
- Week 7: The Outsiders, chapters 4–6: turning point and allusion
- Week 8: The Outsiders, chapters 7–9: conflict and consequences
- Week 9: The Outsiders, chapters 10–12: resolution and theme
- Week 10: Literary analysis essay and film comparison

### Unit 3: Voices of Liberty: Rhetoric and Argument (weeks 11–15)
Effective arguments combine clear claims, relevant evidence and persuasive appeals while answering opposing views.

- Week 11: Rhetoric: Patrick Henry's 'Give Me Liberty'
- Week 12: The Declaration of Independence and the Gettysburg Address
- Week 13: Women's rights voices: Sojourner Truth and Elizabeth Cady Stanton
- Week 14: Contemporary argument: conflicting sources
- Week 15: Argument essay workshop

### Unit 4: Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (weeks 16–19)
Literacy is a path to freedom; autobiography uses personal experience to argue for justice.

- Week 16: Douglass's Narrative: preface and chapters 1–4
- Week 17: Douglass's Narrative: chapters 5–8, literacy and freedom
- Week 18: Douglass's Narrative: chapters 9–11; semester review
- Week 19: Informative essay on Douglass

### Unit 5: American Poetry (weeks 20–22)
Poets use form, sound and figurative language to express individual and national identity.

- Week 20: Poetry of American identity: Whitman and Hughes
- Week 21: Form and sound: Dunbar, Lazarus and Frost
- Week 22: Narrative poetry, recitation and original poems

### Unit 6: Drama and History: The Diary of Anne Frank (weeks 23–27)
Drama and nonfiction preserve memory; dramatic irony lets audiences feel the weight of history.

- Week 23: Historical context and The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 1, Scenes 1–2
- Week 24: The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 1, Scenes 3–5
- Week 25: The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 2, Scenes 1–3: dramatic irony
- Week 26: The Diary of Anne Frank, Act 2, Scenes 4–5 and film comparison
- Week 27: Remembering: Holocaust nonfiction and research

### Unit 7: Myth Made New: Ovid and A Midsummer Night's Dream (weeks 28–31)
Writers transform ancient stories, reshaping familiar patterns for new audiences and purposes.

- Week 28: Ovid's 'Pyramus and Thisbe' and patterns from myth
- Week 29: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 1–2
- Week 30: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 3–4
- Week 31: Act 5: the play within a play and comparison essay

### Unit 8: Research and Presentation (weeks 32–36)
Strong research begins with good questions, credible sources and honest citation, and ends with clear communication.

- Week 32: Research: questions and sources
- Week 33: Research: notes, paraphrase and citation
- Week 34: Drafting the research paper
- Week 35: Multimedia presentation
- Week 36: Portfolio and final assessment

## Standards

- **L.8.1** Demonstrate command of grammar and usage: explain the function of verbals (gerunds, participles, infinitives); form and use active and passive voice; form and use verbs in the indicative, imperative, interrogative, conditional and subjunctive mood; recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in verb voice and mood.
- **L.8.2** Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation and spelling: use commas, ellipses and dashes to indicate a pause or break; use an ellipsis to indicate an omission; spell correctly.
- **L.8.3** Use knowledge of language and its conventions, using verbs in the active and passive voice and in the conditional and subjunctive mood to achieve particular effects.
- **L.8.4** Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, Greek or Latin affixes and roots, and reference materials.
- **L.8.5** Demonstrate understanding of figurative language (verbal irony, puns), word relationships and nuances in word meanings (connotation).
- **L.8.6** Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RI.8.1** Cite the textual evidence that most strongly supports an analysis of what an informational text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RI.8.10** Read and comprehend literary nonfiction at the high end of the grades 6–8 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
- **RI.8.2** Determine a central idea of a text and analyze its development, including its relationship to supporting ideas; provide an objective summary.
- **RI.8.3** Analyze how a text makes connections among and distinctions between individuals, ideas, or events (through comparisons, analogies or categories).
- **RI.8.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases, including figurative, connotative and technical meanings; analyze the impact of word choice on meaning and tone.
- **RI.8.5** Analyze in detail the structure of a specific paragraph in a text, including the role of particular sentences in developing and refining a key concept.
- **RI.8.6** Determine an author's point of view or purpose and analyze how the author acknowledges and responds to conflicting evidence or viewpoints.
- **RI.8.7** Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of using different mediums (print, digital text, video, multimedia) to present a particular topic or idea.
- **RI.8.8** Delineate and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, assessing whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence relevant and sufficient; recognize irrelevant evidence.
- **RI.8.9** Analyze a case in which two or more texts provide conflicting information on the same topic and identify where the texts disagree on matters of fact or interpretation.
- **RL.8.1** Cite the textual evidence that most strongly supports an analysis of what a literary text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RL.8.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories, dramas and poems, at the high end of the grades 6–8 text complexity band independently and proficiently.
- **RL.8.2** Determine a theme or central idea of a text, analyze its development including its relationship to characters, setting and plot; summarize objectively.
- **RL.8.3** Analyze how particular lines of dialogue or incidents in a story or drama propel the action, reveal aspects of a character, or provoke a decision.
- **RL.8.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases including figurative and connotative meanings; analyze the impact of word choice on meaning and tone, including analogies and allusions.
- **RL.8.5** Compare and contrast the structure of two or more texts and analyze how the differing structure of each contributes to meaning and style.
- **RL.8.6** Analyze how differences in the points of view of characters and the audience or reader (dramatic irony) create suspense or humor.
- **RL.8.7** Analyze the extent to which a filmed or live production of a story or drama stays faithful to or departs from the text or script.
- **RL.8.9** Analyze how a modern work of fiction draws on themes, patterns of events, or character types from myths, traditional stories, or religious works, describing how the material is rendered new.
- **SL.8.1** Engage effectively in collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas, posing and responding to questions, and qualifying or justifying views.
- **SL.8.2** Analyze the purpose of information presented in diverse media and formats and evaluate the motives (social, commercial, political) behind its presentation.
- **SL.8.3** Delineate a speaker's argument and specific claims, evaluating the soundness of reasoning and relevance and sufficiency of evidence.
- **SL.8.4** Present claims and findings emphasizing salient points in a focused, coherent manner with relevant evidence, sound reasoning, eye contact, adequate volume and clear pronunciation.
- **SL.8.5** Integrate multimedia and visual displays into presentations to clarify information, strengthen claims and evidence, and add interest.
- **SL.8.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when appropriate.
- **W.8.1** Write arguments to support claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence, acknowledging and distinguishing alternate or opposing claims.
- **W.8.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks, purposes and audiences.
- **W.8.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas, concepts and information through relevant content.
- **W.8.3** Write narratives to develop real or imagined experiences using effective technique, relevant details and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.8.4** Produce clear and coherent writing in which development, organization and style are appropriate to task, purpose and audience.
- **W.8.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting or trying a new approach.
- **W.8.6** Use technology, including the Internet, to produce and publish writing and present relationships between information and ideas efficiently.
- **W.8.7** Conduct short research projects to answer a question, drawing on several sources and generating additional related questions.
- **W.8.8** Gather relevant information from multiple print and digital sources, assess credibility, quote or paraphrase while avoiding plagiarism, and follow a standard citation format.
- **W.8.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection and research.
