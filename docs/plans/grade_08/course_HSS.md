# U.S. History and Geography: Growth and Conflict (Grade 8)

Grade 8 United States history from the ideas behind the American Revolution through the Constitution, the early Republic, regional divergence, westward expansion, the Civil War, Reconstruction and the rise of industrial America. Students read primary sources every week (founding documents, speeches, letters, photographs and maps), practice historical thinking skills such as sourcing and corroboration, and write evidence-based arguments.

*Framework:* California History-Social Science Content Standards (Grade 8); 2016 California History-Social Science Framework  
*Time:* 50 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Roots of Revolution and the Declaration (weeks 1–3)
Enlightenment and English traditions inspired colonists to claim natural rights and declare independence.

- Week 1: Foundations: English rights and Enlightenment ideas
- Week 2: The Great Awakening and the road to revolution
- Week 3: The Declaration of Independence and the Revolution

### Unit 2: Creating the Constitution (weeks 4–7)
The framers replaced a weak confederation with a Constitution balancing power, rights and compromise, including compromises over slavery.

- Week 4: The Articles of Confederation
- Week 5: The Constitutional Convention
- Week 6: Ratification and the Bill of Rights
- Week 7: How the Constitution works

### Unit 3: Launching the New Nation (weeks 8–10)
Early leaders turned the Constitution into a working government, sparking party conflict and debates about national identity.

- Week 8: Washington's presidency and the first parties
- Week 9: Adams, Jefferson and the Louisiana Purchase
- Week 10: Life, economy and culture in the early Republic

### Unit 4: Foreign Policy and the Industrializing Northeast (weeks 11–13)
The young nation asserted itself abroad while the Northeast industrialized and reformers pushed for change.

- Week 11: The War of 1812 and the Monroe Doctrine
- Week 12: Industrial Revolution in the Northeast
- Week 13: Reform movements

### Unit 5: The South and Slavery (weeks 14–15)
Cotton made slavery central to the Southern economy, while enslaved people resisted in many ways.

- Week 14: Cotton and the Southern economy
- Week 15: Life under slavery and resistance

### Unit 6: Jacksonian Democracy and Westward Expansion (weeks 16–19)
Expanding democracy and Manifest Destiny drove growth that came at great cost to American Indians and Mexico.

- Week 16: Jacksonian democracy and Indian Removal
- Week 17: Manifest Destiny and the trails west
- Week 18: Mexican California and Texas independence; semester review
- Week 19: The Mexican-American War and the Gold Rush

### Unit 7: Abolition and the Road to Civil War (weeks 20–23)
Reform movements and failed compromises over slavery's expansion split the nation.

- Week 20: The abolition movement
- Week 21: Compromises over slavery's expansion
- Week 22: Bleeding Kansas, Dred Scott and the rise of Lincoln
- Week 23: Nullification and secession

### Unit 8: The Civil War (weeks 24–27)
The war to preserve the Union became a war to end slavery, transforming the nation.

- Week 24: The war begins: North vs South
- Week 25: Emancipation and Black soldiers
- Week 26: Turning points and home fronts
- Week 27: The end of the war

### Unit 9: Reconstruction (weeks 28–30)
Reconstruction amendments expanded freedom, but violence and Jim Crow laws rolled back many gains.

- Week 28: Plans for Reconstruction
- Week 29: Freedom and new rights
- Week 30: The end of Reconstruction and Jim Crow

### Unit 10: Industrial America (weeks 31–35)
Industry, railroads and immigration transformed the economy and society, prompting labor and farmer movements.

- Week 31: Railroads and the West
- Week 32: Big business and inventors
- Week 33: Immigration, cities and nativism
- Week 34: Labor and farmer movements
- Week 35: Industrial America museum project

### Unit 11: Year in Review (weeks 36–36)
The struggle to realize the ideals of the Declaration connects every era studied this year.

- Week 36: Year in review: the expanding promise of the Declaration

## Standards

- **8.1** Understand the major events preceding the founding of the nation and their significance to American constitutional democracy.
- **8.1.1** Describe how the moral and political ideas of the Great Awakening related to revolutionary fervor.
- **8.1.2** Analyze the philosophy of government in the Declaration of Independence, emphasizing government as a means of securing individual rights.
- **8.1.3** Analyze how the American Revolution affected other nations, especially France.
- **8.1.4** Describe the nation's blend of civic republicanism, classical liberal principles, and English parliamentary traditions.
- **8.10** Analyze the multiple causes, key events, and complex consequences of the Civil War.
- **8.10.1** Compare interpretations of state and federal authority in the writings of Daniel Webster and John C. Calhoun.
- **8.10.2** Trace boundaries and geographic and economic differences between North and South.
- **8.10.3** Identify constitutional issues posed by nullification and secession.
- **8.10.4** Discuss Lincoln's presidency and writings (House Divided, Gettysburg Address, Emancipation Proclamation, inaugural addresses).
- **8.10.5** Study leaders and soldiers on both sides, including Black soldiers and regiments.
- **8.10.6** Describe major battles, geographic advantages, technology, and Lee's surrender at Appomattox.
- **8.10.7** Explain how the war affected combatants, civilians, the environment and future warfare.
- **8.11** Analyze the character and lasting consequences of Reconstruction.
- **8.11.1** List the original aims of Reconstruction and its effects on political and social structures.
- **8.11.2** Identify push-pull factors in the movement of freedpeople to Northern cities and the West (Buffalo Soldiers).
- **8.11.3** Understand the Freedmen's Bureau and restrictions on freedpeople, including segregation and Jim Crow laws.
- **8.11.4** Trace the rise of the Ku Klux Klan and its effects.
- **8.11.5** Understand the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments and their connection to Reconstruction.
- **8.12** Analyze the transformation of the American economy and changing social and political conditions in response to the Industrial Revolution.
- **8.12.1** Trace patterns of agricultural and industrial development related to climate, resources, markets and trade.
- **8.12.2** Identify reasons for federal Indian policy and the wars with American Indians and their link to development.
- **8.12.3** Explain how governments encouraged business expansion through tariffs, banking, land grants and subsidies.
- **8.12.4** Discuss entrepreneurs, industrialists and bankers (Carnegie, Rockefeller, Leland Stanford).
- **8.12.5** Examine the effects of urbanization, renewed immigration and industrialization, including the conservation movement.
- **8.12.6** Discuss child labor, working conditions, laissez-faire policy and the labor movement (Samuel Gompers).
- **8.12.7** Identify new sources of immigration, immigrant contributions, assimilation, and nativism.
- **8.12.8** Identify the characteristics and impact of Grangerism and Populism.
- **8.12.9** Name significant inventors and inventions (Edison, Bell, the Wright brothers) and how they improved life.
- **8.2** Analyze the political principles underlying the U.S. Constitution and compare enumerated and implied powers.
- **8.2.1** Discuss the significance of the Magna Carta, the English Bill of Rights, and the Mayflower Compact.
- **8.2.2** Analyze the Articles of Confederation and the Constitution and how well each implemented the ideals of the Declaration.
- **8.2.3** Evaluate major debates at the Constitutional Convention (shared power, state-federal power, slavery, individual rights, American Indian nations) and their resolutions.
- **8.2.4** Describe the philosophy of the Federalist Papers and the roles of Madison, Washington, Sherman, Morris and Wilson in writing and ratifying the Constitution.
- **8.2.5** Understand Jefferson's Statute for Religious Freedom as a forerunner of the First Amendment and views on separation of church and state.
- **8.2.6** Enumerate the powers of government in the Constitution and the fundamental liberties ensured by the Bill of Rights.
- **8.2.7** Describe federalism, separation of powers, checks and balances, majority rule, and how constitutionalism preserves individual rights.
- **8.3** Understand the foundation of the American political system and the ways citizens participate in it.
- **8.3.1** Analyze principles codified in state constitutions of 1777-1781.
- **8.3.2** Explain how the Land Ordinance of 1785 and Northwest Ordinance of 1787 transferred federal lands to private holdings, townships and states.
- **8.3.3** Enumerate the advantages of a common market among the states protected by the Constitution.
- **8.3.4** Understand how conflicts between Jefferson and Hamilton led to two political parties.
- **8.3.5** Know the significance of domestic resistance movements (Shays' Rebellion, Whiskey Rebellion) and the government's response.
- **8.3.6** Describe the law-making process and how citizens participate in and influence government.
- **8.3.7** Understand the functions and responsibilities of a free press.
- **8.4** Analyze the aspirations and ideals of the people of the new nation.
- **8.4.1** Describe the country's landscapes, political divisions and territorial expansion under the first four presidents.
- **8.4.2** Explain the policy significance of famous speeches (Washington's Farewell Address, Jefferson's 1801 Inaugural, J. Q. Adams's 1821 address).
- **8.4.3** Analyze the rise of capitalism and related conflicts (the National Bank, early Supreme Court decisions on contracts).
- **8.4.4** Discuss daily life, art, music and literature of early national America (Washington Irving, James Fenimore Cooper).
- **8.5** Analyze U.S. foreign policy in the early Republic.
- **8.5.1** Understand the causes, consequences, major battles and leaders of the War of 1812.
- **8.5.2** Know changing U.S. boundaries and relations with Mexico, Canada and Europe, including the Monroe Doctrine.
- **8.5.3** Outline major treaties with American Indian nations under the first four presidents and their outcomes.
- **8.6** Analyze the divergent paths of the American people from 1800 to the mid-1800s, with emphasis on the Northeast.
- **8.6.1** Discuss the influence of industrialization and technology on the Northeast and human modification of the landscape.
- **8.6.2** Outline obstacles and factors in building roads, canals and railroads (Henry Clay's American System).
- **8.6.3** List reasons for immigration from Northern Europe and describe the growth of cities (Irish Famine).
- **8.6.4** Study the lives of free Black Americans in the North who founded schools and churches.
- **8.6.5** Trace the development of American education, including Horace Mann's campaign for free public schools.
- **8.6.6** Examine the women's rights movement (Stanton, Fuller, Mott, Anthony).
- **8.6.7** Identify themes in American art, transcendentalism and individualism (Emerson, Thoreau, Melville, Alcott, Hawthorne, Longfellow).
- **8.7** Analyze the divergent paths of the American people in the South from 1800 to the mid-1800s.
- **8.7.1** Describe the South's agrarian economy, cotton-producing states, and the significance of cotton and the cotton gin.
- **8.7.2** Trace the origins and development of slavery, its effects, and strategies to overturn or preserve it (Nat Turner, Denmark Vesey).
- **8.7.3** Examine white Southern society and how the physical environment influenced conditions before the Civil War.
- **8.7.4** Compare the lives and opportunities of free Black people in the North and in the South.
- **8.8** Analyze the divergent paths of the American people in the West from 1800 to the mid-1800s.
- **8.8.1** Discuss Andrew Jackson's election, Jacksonian democracy, and his actions (spoils system, Bank veto, Indian removal).
- **8.8.2** Describe westward expansion and Manifest Destiny (Lewis and Clark, Trail of Tears, Great Plains settlement, territorial acquisitions).
- **8.8.3** Describe the role of pioneer women and the new status western women achieved.
- **8.8.4** Examine the importance of the great rivers and struggles over water rights.
- **8.8.5** Discuss Mexican settlements, cultural traditions, attitudes toward slavery, land-grant system and economies.
- **8.8.6** Describe the Texas War for Independence and the Mexican-American War and their effects, including on Mexican Americans today.
- **8.9** Analyze the early and steady attempts to abolish slavery and realize the ideals of the Declaration of Independence.
- **8.9.1** Describe leaders of the abolition movement (Douglass, Garrison, Tubman, John Brown, Weld, J. Q. Adams).
- **8.9.2** Discuss the abolition of slavery in early state constitutions.
- **8.9.3** Describe the significance of the Northwest Ordinance for education and banning slavery north of the Ohio River.
- **8.9.4** Discuss the slavery issue raised by Texas annexation and California's admission as a free state in the Compromise of 1850.
- **8.9.5** Analyze states' rights doctrine, the Missouri Compromise, Wilmot Proviso, Compromise of 1850, Kansas-Nebraska Act, Dred Scott decision and Lincoln-Douglas debates.
- **8.9.6** Describe the lives of free Black people and the laws that limited their freedom and opportunities.
