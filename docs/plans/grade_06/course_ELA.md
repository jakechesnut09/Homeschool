# English Language Arts 6 (Grade 6)

Grade 6 ELA builds close reading of literature and nonfiction, evidence-based writing in all three types (narrative, explanatory, argument), and middle-school grammar and vocabulary. Students read classic short stories, the survival novel Hatchet, Greek myths and the Odyssey, poetry, Helen Keller's autobiography, a Shakespeare comedy and The Secret Garden, then finish with a research project. Grammar (pronouns, punctuation of nonrestrictive elements, sentence variety), Greek and Latin roots, and speaking and listening are woven into every week.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts/Literacy; 2014 CA ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 90 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Short Stories and Personal Narrative (weeks 1–4)
Authors build plot, character and suspense through deliberate choices we can imitate in our own narratives.

- Week 1: Plot and suspense: 'Rikki-Tikki-Tavi'
- Week 2: Irony and point of view: O. Henry and Saki
- Week 3: Personal narrative: drafting
- Week 4: Personal narrative: revising and publishing

### Unit 2: Survival: Hatchet Novel Study (weeks 5–9)
Facing adversity changes people; fiction and nonfiction together show how humans survive.

- Week 5: Hatchet chapters 1-4: the crash
- Week 6: Hatchet chapters 5-9: first lessons
- Week 7: Hatchet chapters 10-13: mistakes and growth
- Week 8: Hatchet chapters 14-19 and epilogue
- Week 9: Explanatory essay: how Brian survives

### Unit 3: Myths, Heroes and the Odyssey (weeks 10–13)
Ancient myths and epics carry themes about courage, pride and home that still shape our language and stories.

- Week 10: Greek myths and Greek/Latin roots
- Week 11: The Odyssey, part 1
- Week 12: The Odyssey, part 2: homecoming
- Week 13: Writing: modern myth or hero's journey story

### Unit 4: Argument: Claims and Evidence (weeks 14–17)
Strong arguments make clear claims supported by relevant, credible evidence and fair reasoning.

- Week 14: Analyzing arguments
- Week 15: Planning an argument essay
- Week 16: Drafting and revising the argument
- Week 17: Speaking: debate

### Unit 5: Poetry: Sound, Image and Meaning (weeks 18–21)
Poets compress meaning into images, sounds and structure; figurative language shapes tone.

- Week 18: Figurative language in poetry
- Week 19: Sound and structure
- Week 20: Speaker, tone and theme
- Week 21: Poetry anthology

### Unit 6: Memoir and Biography: Helen Keller (weeks 22–25)
Writers of memoir shape their own story; comparing memoir and biography reveals point of view.

- Week 22: The Story of My Life, chapters 1-4
- Week 23: The Story of My Life, selected chapters 5-23
- Week 24: Memoir vs. biography vs. drama
- Week 25: Writing a memoir chapter

### Unit 7: Drama: A Midsummer Night's Dream (weeks 26–28)
Plays are written to be performed; staging, voice and movement bring the text's comedy and conflict to life.

- Week 26: Shakespeare's world and A Midsummer Night's Dream, Act 1-2
- Week 27: A Midsummer Night's Dream, Acts 3-5
- Week 28: Performing a scene

### Unit 8: Novel Study: The Secret Garden (weeks 29–32)
Characters and settings can transform together; a theme emerges from how characters change.

- Week 29: The Secret Garden, chapters 1-7
- Week 30: The Secret Garden, chapters 8-15
- Week 31: The Secret Garden, chapters 16-27
- Week 32: Literary argument essay

### Unit 9: Research and Presentation (weeks 33–36)
Researchers ask focused questions, evaluate sources, synthesize information and share it clearly.

- Week 33: Research questions and sources
- Week 34: Drafting the research report
- Week 35: Revising and presenting research
- Week 36: Year-end reflection and assessment

## Standards

- **L.6.1** Demonstrate command of standard English grammar and usage when writing or speaking.
- **L.6.1a** Ensure that pronouns are in the proper case (subjective, objective, possessive).
- **L.6.1b** Use intensive pronouns (e.g., myself, ourselves).
- **L.6.1c** Recognize and correct inappropriate shifts in pronoun number and person.
- **L.6.1d** Recognize and correct vague pronouns (unclear or ambiguous antecedents).
- **L.6.1e** Recognize variations from standard English in writing and speaking and use strategies to improve expression.
- **L.6.2** Demonstrate command of capitalization, punctuation and spelling when writing.
- **L.6.2a** Use commas, parentheses or dashes to set off nonrestrictive or parenthetical elements.
- **L.6.3** Use knowledge of language and its conventions; vary sentence patterns for meaning, interest and style; maintain consistency in style and tone.
- **L.6.4** Determine or clarify the meaning of unknown words using context, affixes and roots, and reference materials.
- **L.6.4b** Use common grade-appropriate Greek or Latin affixes and roots as clues to meaning (e.g., audience, auditory, audible).
- **L.6.5** Demonstrate understanding of figurative language, word relationships and nuances in word meanings (personification, connotation).
- **L.6.6** Acquire and use accurately grade-appropriate general academic and domain-specific words and phrases.
- **RI.6.1** Cite textual evidence to support analysis of what an informational text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RI.6.10** Read and comprehend grade 6-8 literary nonfiction proficiently.
- **RI.6.2** Determine a central idea and how it is conveyed through details; provide an objective summary.
- **RI.6.3** Analyze how a key individual, event or idea is introduced, illustrated and elaborated in a text.
- **RI.6.4** Determine meanings of words and phrases in informational text, including figurative, connotative and technical meanings.
- **RI.6.5** Analyze how a sentence, paragraph, chapter or section fits into the overall structure of a text.
- **RI.6.6** Determine an author's point of view or purpose and explain how it is conveyed.
- **RI.6.7** Integrate information presented in different media or formats as well as in words.
- **RI.6.8** Trace and evaluate the argument and specific claims in a text, distinguishing supported from unsupported claims.
- **RI.6.9** Compare and contrast one author's presentation of events with that of another (e.g., memoir vs. biography).
- **RL.6.1** Cite textual evidence to support analysis of what a literary text says explicitly and by inference.
- **RL.6.10** Read and comprehend grade 6-8 literature, including stories, dramas and poems, proficiently.
- **RL.6.2** Determine a theme or central idea and how it is conveyed through details; summarize without personal opinions.
- **RL.6.3** Describe how a plot unfolds in episodes and how characters respond or change as the plot moves toward resolution.
- **RL.6.4** Determine meanings of words and phrases, including figurative and connotative meanings; analyze impact of word choice on meaning and tone.
- **RL.6.5** Analyze how a sentence, chapter, scene or stanza fits into the overall structure and contributes to theme, setting or plot.
- **RL.6.6** Explain how an author develops the point of view of the narrator or speaker.
- **RL.6.7** Compare and contrast reading a story, drama or poem with listening to or viewing an audio, video or live version.
- **RL.6.9** Compare and contrast texts in different forms or genres in terms of their approaches to similar themes and topics.
- **SL.6.1** Engage effectively in collaborative discussions, building on others' ideas and expressing one's own clearly.
- **SL.6.2** Interpret information presented in diverse media and formats and explain how it contributes to a topic.
- **SL.6.3** Delineate a speaker's argument and claims, distinguishing supported from unsupported claims.
- **SL.6.4** Present claims and findings, sequencing ideas logically with pertinent details; use appropriate eye contact, volume and pronunciation.
- **SL.6.5** Include multimedia components and visual displays in presentations to clarify information.
- **SL.6.6** Adapt speech to a variety of contexts and tasks, demonstrating command of formal English when appropriate.
- **W.6.1** Write arguments supporting claims with clear reasons and relevant evidence.
- **W.6.10** Write routinely over extended and shorter time frames for a range of tasks and purposes.
- **W.6.2** Write informative/explanatory texts to examine a topic and convey ideas through relevant content.
- **W.6.3** Write narratives developing real or imagined experiences using effective technique, descriptive details and well-structured event sequences.
- **W.6.4** Produce clear and coherent writing appropriate to task, purpose and audience.
- **W.6.5** Develop and strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting or trying a new approach.
- **W.6.6** Use technology to produce and publish writing and to interact with others; type a minimum of three pages in one sitting.
- **W.6.7** Conduct short research projects to answer a question, drawing on several sources and refocusing as appropriate.
- **W.6.8** Gather relevant information from print and digital sources, assess credibility, quote or paraphrase, and provide bibliographic information.
- **W.6.9** Draw evidence from literary or informational texts to support analysis, reflection and research.
