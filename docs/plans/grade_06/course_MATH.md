# Mathematics 6 (Grade 6)

Grade 6 math moves from whole-number arithmetic toward algebraic thinking. Students reason with ratios, rates and percents, complete their understanding of division with fractions, extend the number system to negative rational numbers, write and solve one-variable equations, find area, surface area and volume, and begin statistics by describing distributions. The sequence follows the Illustrative Mathematics / Open Up Resources Grade 6 scope used by many California districts.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics; 2023 CA Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Area and Surface Area (weeks 1–4)
Area can be found by cutting, rearranging and decomposing shapes; surface area is the total area of a solid's faces.

- Week 1: Area of rectangles and parallelograms
- Week 2: Area of triangles
- Week 3: Polygons and real-world area
- Week 4: Nets, surface area, squares and cubes

### Unit 2: Introducing Ratios (weeks 5–8)
A ratio compares two quantities, and equivalent ratios describe the same relationship at different scales.

- Week 5: What are ratios?
- Week 6: Equivalent ratios and double number lines
- Week 7: Tables of equivalent ratios
- Week 8: Part-part-whole ratios and tape diagrams

### Unit 3: Unit Rates and Percentages (weeks 9–12)
Unit rates and percents (rates per 100) let us compare and convert quantities efficiently.

- Week 9: Units of measure and unit rates
- Week 10: Speed and unit price
- Week 11: Percentages
- Week 12: Finding the whole and percent problems

### Unit 4: Dividing Fractions (weeks 13–16)
Division asks 'how many groups?' or 'how much in each group?', and those questions work for fractions too.

- Week 13: Meanings of division
- Week 14: Dividing fractions by fractions
- Week 15: Fractions in real-world problems
- Week 16: Volume with fractional edge lengths

### Unit 5: Arithmetic in Base Ten (weeks 17–19)
Place value makes the standard algorithms for decimals and long division work.

- Week 17: Decimals: adding, subtracting and multiplying
- Week 18: Long division and dividing decimals
- Week 19: Factors, multiples and the distributive property

### Unit 6: Expressions and Equations (weeks 20–24)
Variables let us write general relationships, find unknowns and show how one quantity depends on another.

- Week 20: Variables and writing expressions
- Week 21: Solving one-step equations
- Week 22: Equivalent expressions
- Week 23: Exponents and order of operations
- Week 24: Relationships between quantities

### Unit 7: Rational Numbers (weeks 25–29)
Negative numbers extend the number line and the coordinate plane to describe opposite directions and values below zero.

- Week 25: Positive and negative numbers
- Week 26: Comparing and absolute value
- Week 27: Inequalities
- Week 28: The coordinate plane
- Week 29: Polygons in the coordinate plane

### Unit 8: Data Sets and Distributions (weeks 30–34)
Statistical questions expect variability; we describe a distribution by its center, spread and shape.

- Week 30: Statistical questions and dot plots
- Week 31: Histograms
- Week 32: Mean and mean absolute deviation
- Week 33: Median, IQR and box plots
- Week 34: Statistics project

### Unit 9: Putting It All Together (weeks 35–36)
The year's tools combine to model real situations.

- Week 35: Modeling project: plan a trip
- Week 36: Cumulative review and final assessment

## Standards

- **6.EE.A.1** Write and evaluate numerical expressions involving whole-number exponents.
- **6.EE.A.2** Write, read and evaluate expressions in which letters stand for numbers.
- **6.EE.A.2c** Evaluate expressions and formulas (e.g., V = s^3, A = 6s^2) at specific values using order of operations.
- **6.EE.A.3** Apply the properties of operations to generate equivalent expressions.
- **6.EE.A.4** Identify when two expressions are equivalent.
- **6.EE.B.5** Understand solving an equation or inequality as finding which values make it true; use substitution to check.
- **6.EE.B.6** Use variables to represent numbers and write expressions when solving real-world problems.
- **6.EE.B.7** Solve real-world problems by writing and solving equations of the form x + p = q and px = q for nonnegative rational numbers.
- **6.EE.B.8** Write inequalities of the form x > c or x < c for real-world constraints and represent solutions on number lines.
- **6.EE.C.9** Use variables to represent two quantities that change together; relate dependent and independent variables with tables, graphs and equations.
- **6.G.A.1** Find the area of right triangles, other triangles, special quadrilaterals and polygons by composing and decomposing shapes.
- **6.G.A.2** Find the volume of right rectangular prisms with fractional edge lengths; apply V = lwh and V = bh.
- **6.G.A.3** Draw polygons in the coordinate plane given vertices; use coordinates to find side lengths.
- **6.G.A.4** Represent three-dimensional figures using nets of rectangles and triangles and use nets to find surface area.
- **6.NS.A.1** Interpret and compute quotients of fractions and solve word problems involving division of fractions by fractions.
- **6.NS.B.2** Fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm.
- **6.NS.B.3** Fluently add, subtract, multiply and divide multi-digit decimals using standard algorithms.
- **6.NS.B.4** Find the greatest common factor (to 100) and least common multiple (to 12); use the distributive property to factor sums.
- **6.NS.C.5** Use positive and negative numbers to represent quantities in real-world contexts and explain the meaning of 0.
- **6.NS.C.6** Understand a rational number as a point on the number line; extend number lines and coordinate axes to negative numbers.
- **6.NS.C.6c** Find and position integers and other rational numbers on a number line and coordinate plane.
- **6.NS.C.7** Understand ordering and absolute value of rational numbers.
- **6.NS.C.8** Solve real-world problems by graphing points in all four quadrants, including distances between points sharing a coordinate.
- **6.RP.A.1** Understand the concept of a ratio and use ratio language to describe a relationship between two quantities.
- **6.RP.A.2** Understand the concept of a unit rate a/b associated with a ratio a:b (b not 0) and use rate language.
- **6.RP.A.3** Use ratio and rate reasoning (tables, tape diagrams, double number lines, equations) to solve real-world problems.
- **6.RP.A.3a** Make tables of equivalent ratios, find missing values, plot pairs on the coordinate plane, and compare ratios.
- **6.RP.A.3b** Solve unit rate problems, including unit pricing and constant speed.
- **6.RP.A.3c** Find a percent of a quantity as a rate per 100; solve problems finding the whole given a part and the percent.
- **6.RP.A.3d** Use ratio reasoning to convert measurement units; transform units when multiplying or dividing quantities.
- **6.SP.A.1** Recognize a statistical question as one that anticipates variability in the data.
- **6.SP.A.2** Understand that a data distribution can be described by its center, spread and overall shape.
- **6.SP.A.3** Recognize that a measure of center summarizes all values with one number, while a measure of variation describes how values vary.
- **6.SP.B.4** Display numerical data in plots on a number line, including dot plots, histograms and box plots.
- **6.SP.B.5** Summarize numerical data sets in relation to their context (count, attribute measured, center and variability, shape, outliers).
- **MP.1** Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them.
- **MP.3** Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others.
- **MP.4** Model with mathematics.
