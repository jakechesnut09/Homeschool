# People Who Make a Difference (Grade 2)

Grade 2 History–Social Science following the California 'People Who Make a Difference' course. Students trace their own family history with photographs, artifacts, and interviews; build map skills from a letter-number grid of their neighborhood to a map of North America; learn how laws are made, carried out, and judged and how nations cooperate; follow food from farm to table as producers and consumers; and study the lives of people who made a difference, from Abraham Lincoln and George Washington Carver to Marie Curie, Jackie Robinson, Cesar Chavez, and Sally Ride. Every unit uses primary sources such as photographs, letters, maps, and documents from the Library of Congress, National Archives, and Calisphere.

*Framework:* California History–Social Science Content Standards (Grade 2: People Who Make a Difference); 2016 California History–Social Science Framework  
*Time:* 30 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: My Family's Story: Long Ago and Today (weeks 1–5)
Families have histories we can uncover with photos, artifacts, documents, and interviews, and daily life has changed over time.

- Week 1: What is history? Long ago and yesterday
- Week 2: My timeline
- Week 3: Family artifacts and documents
- Week 4: Interviewing family: then and now
- Week 5: Family history book and unit check

### Unit 2: Maps and Where We Live (weeks 6–11)
Maps help us describe where people and places are, from our neighborhood to North America.

- Week 6: Map elements
- Week 7: Letter-number grids in my neighborhood
- Week 8: North America: countries and oceans
- Week 9: North America: rivers, lakes, and mountains
- Week 10: Where my family came from
- Week 11: Urban, suburban, and rural California

### Unit 3: Rules, Laws, and Government (weeks 12–15)
Governments make laws, carry them out, and decide when they have been broken, to keep people safe and treat them fairly.

- Week 12: Why we have rules and laws
- Week 13: Who makes laws?
- Week 14: Carrying out laws and courts
- Week 15: Government in other countries and unit check

### Unit 4: Getting Along: How Groups and Nations Solve Problems (weeks 16–18)
Groups and nations solve problems through trade, sharing cultures, agreements, and talking things out.

- Week 16: Trade between nations
- Week 17: Sharing cultures and making agreements
- Week 18: Diplomacy and solving conflicts

### Unit 5: Producers, Consumers, and Our Food (weeks 19–25)
Many people and resources work together to bring food from farms to our tables, and limited resources mean making choices.

- Week 19: Producers and consumers
- Week 20: From farm to table: today
- Week 21: Food long ago
- Week 22: Weather, land, and water resources
- Week 23: Limited resources and choices
- Week 24: Money, trade, and markets
- Week 25: Economics project: my food's journey

### Unit 6: People Who Make a Difference (weeks 26–35)
Ordinary people with courage, curiosity, and hard work have changed the lives of others.

- Week 26: What makes a hero? Abraham Lincoln
- Week 27: Sitting Bull and George Washington Carver
- Week 28: Science heroes: Louis Pasteur and Marie Curie
- Week 29: Albert Einstein and Golda Meir
- Week 30: Jackie Robinson
- Week 31: Rosa Parks, Martin Luther King Jr., and Ruby Bridges
- Week 32: Cesar Chavez and Dolores Huerta
- Week 33: Sally Ride and Ellen Ochoa: reaching for the stars
- Week 34: Heroes in our community
- Week 35: Hero project: living museum

### Unit 7: Celebrating Our Stories (weeks 36–36)
Our families, communities, and heroes are all part of history.

- Week 36: Celebrating our stories: year review

## Standards

- **2.1** Differentiate between things that happened long ago and things that happened yesterday.
- **2.1.1** Trace the history of a family through primary and secondary sources, including artifacts, photographs, interviews, and documents.
- **2.1.2** Compare and contrast daily lives with those of parents, grandparents, and/or guardians.
- **2.1.3** Place important events in their lives in the order in which they occurred (e.g., on a time line or storyboard).
- **2.2** Demonstrate map skills by describing the absolute and relative locations of people, places, and environments.
- **2.2.1** Locate on a simple letter-number grid system the specific locations and geographic features in their neighborhood or community.
- **2.2.2** Label from memory a simple map of North America (countries, oceans, Great Lakes, major rivers, mountain ranges) and identify map elements: title, legend, directional indicator, scale, and date.
- **2.2.3** Locate on a map where their ancestors live(d), telling when the family moved to the local community and how and why they made the trip.
- **2.2.4** Compare and contrast basic land use in urban, suburban, and rural environments in California.
- **2.3** Explain governmental institutions and practices in the United States and other countries.
- **2.3.1** Explain how the United States and other countries make laws, carry out laws, determine whether laws have been violated, and punish wrongdoers.
- **2.3.2** Describe the ways in which groups and nations interact to try to resolve problems in such areas as trade, cultural contacts, treaties, diplomacy, and military force.
- **2.4** Understand basic economic concepts and their individual roles in the economy, and demonstrate basic economic reasoning skills.
- **2.4.1** Describe food production and consumption long ago and today, including the roles of farmers, processors, distributors, weather, and land and water resources.
- **2.4.2** Understand the role and interdependence of buyers (consumers) and sellers (producers) of goods and services.
- **2.4.3** Understand how limits on resources affect production and consumption (what to produce and what to consume).
- **2.5** Understand the importance of individual action and character and explain how heroes from long ago and the recent past have made a difference in others' lives (e.g., Abraham Lincoln, Louis Pasteur, Sitting Bull, George Washington Carver, Marie Curie, Albert Einstein, Golda Meir, Jackie Robinson, Sally Ride).
