# ELA 2 (Grade 2)

A year of second-grade English Language Arts built around real books: Frog and Toad, Aesop's fables, Beatrix Potter, Robert Louis Stevenson's poems, Cinderella tales from many cultures, biographies, science read-alouds, and Winnie-the-Pooh. Every week blends daily phonics and word study (vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, prefixes and suffixes), fluency practice, grammar, a writing project (narrative, informative, opinion, research), and speaking and listening. Day 5 reviews the week with a spelling check, fluency read, and short comprehension or writing assessment.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts & Literacy; 2014 California ELA/ELD Framework  
*Time:* 90 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Friends and Feelings: Story Elements (weeks 1–4)
Good readers notice characters, settings, and events, and good writers tell true stories with details.

- Week 1: Reading routines with Frog and Toad
- Week 2: How characters respond to problems
- Week 3: Digraphs and drafting a personal narrative
- Week 4: Henry and Mudge; revising and sharing stories

### Unit 2: Fables and Folktales (weeks 5–8)
Stories from many cultures teach lessons about how to live.

- Week 5: Aesop's fables: the lesson of the story
- Week 6: Fables continued: vowel teams ai/ay and ee/ea
- Week 7: Folktales from many cultures and character voices
- Week 8: Anansi tales and writing an original fable

### Unit 3: Amazing Animals: Reading for Information (weeks 9–12)
Informational texts use features like headings, glossaries, and diagrams to teach facts.

- Week 9: Nonfiction text features
- Week 10: Life cycles: connecting steps in a process
- Week 11: Main topic, paragraphs, and diagrams
- Week 12: Author's purpose and publishing an animal report

### Unit 4: Characters Who Change (weeks 13–16)
Characters respond to big events, and stories have beginnings that introduce and endings that conclude.

- Week 13: Peter Rabbit: story structure and irregular past tense
- Week 14: The Velveteen Rabbit and planning a fiction story
- Week 15: Feelings, shades of meaning, adjectives and adverbs
- Week 16: Ira Sleeps Over; revising and publishing fiction

### Unit 5: Poetry and Word Play (weeks 17–20)
Poets use rhythm, rhyme, repetition, and carefully chosen words to create meaning and feeling.

- Week 17: Poems with rhythm and rhyme
- Week 18: Alliteration, repetition, and two-syllable words
- Week 19: Sensory poems and compound sentences
- Week 20: Poetry recording and anthology

### Unit 6: Cinderella Around the World and Point of View (weeks 21–24)
The same story changes when told by different cultures or from a different character's point of view.

- Week 21: Cinderella from France and China
- Week 22: Cinderella stories from Africa and North America
- Week 23: Point of view: whose story is it?
- Week 24: Publishing opinions and comparatives

### Unit 7: People Who Make a Difference: Biographies (weeks 25–28)
Biographies connect the events of a real person's life and show how one person can change the world.

- Week 25: Biography basics: Martin Luther King Jr.
- Week 26: Biographies of courage: Rosa Parks and Jackie Robinson
- Week 27: Researching a person who made a difference
- Week 28: Writing and presenting a biography report

### Unit 8: Our Changing Earth: Comparing Texts (weeks 29–32)
Authors give reasons to support their points, and two texts on one topic can teach different things.

- Week 29: Volcanoes and earthquakes: reasons support points
- Week 30: Comparing two texts on one topic
- Week 31: How-to writing: steps in a procedure
- Week 32: Friendly letters, formal and informal language

### Unit 9: Chapter Book Club and Year-End Celebration (weeks 33–36)
Readers follow characters across chapters and share opinions about books they love.

- Week 33: Winnie-the-Pooh: following characters across chapters
- Week 34: Winnie-the-Pooh: structure and points of view
- Week 35: Book review: opinion writing about a favorite book
- Week 36: Year-end showcase and assessment

## Standards

- **L.2.1a** Use collective nouns (e.g., group, herd, flock).
- **L.2.1b** Form and use frequently occurring irregular plural nouns (e.g., feet, children, teeth, mice, fish).
- **L.2.1c** Use reflexive pronouns (e.g., myself, ourselves).
- **L.2.1d** Form and use the past tense of frequently occurring irregular verbs (e.g., sat, hid, told).
- **L.2.1e** Use adjectives and adverbs, and choose between them depending on what is to be modified.
- **L.2.1f** Produce, expand, and rearrange complete simple and compound sentences.
- **L.2.2** Use capitalization (holidays, product and geographic names), commas in letters, apostrophes for contractions and possessives, learned spelling patterns, and reference materials when writing.
- **L.2.3** Use knowledge of language and its conventions; compare formal and informal uses of English.
- **L.2.4** Determine or clarify unknown words using sentence context, prefixes, root words, compound-word parts, and glossaries or dictionaries.
- **L.2.5** Demonstrate understanding of word relationships and nuances (real-life connections; shades of meaning among related verbs and adjectives).
- **L.2.6** Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading, and being read to, including adjectives and adverbs to describe.
- **RF.2.3a** Distinguish long and short vowels when reading regularly spelled one-syllable words.
- **RF.2.3b** Know spelling-sound correspondences for additional common vowel teams.
- **RF.2.3c** Decode regularly spelled two-syllable words with long vowels.
- **RF.2.3d** Decode words with common prefixes and suffixes.
- **RF.2.3e** Identify words with inconsistent but common spelling-sound correspondences.
- **RF.2.3f** Recognize and read grade-appropriate irregularly spelled words.
- **RF.2.4** Read on-level text with sufficient accuracy and fluency (purpose, rate, expression), using context to self-correct.
- **RI.2.1** Ask and answer who, what, where, when, why, and how questions about key details in an informational text.
- **RI.2.10** Read and comprehend informational texts, including history/social studies, science, and technical texts, in the grades 2–3 band.
- **RI.2.2** Identify the main topic of a multiparagraph text and the focus of specific paragraphs within it.
- **RI.2.3** Describe the connection between a series of historical events, scientific ideas, or steps in technical procedures.
- **RI.2.4** Determine the meaning of words and phrases in a grade 2 topic or subject-area text.
- **RI.2.5** Know and use text features (captions, bold print, subheadings, glossaries, indexes, menus, icons) to locate key facts efficiently.
- **RI.2.6** Identify the main purpose of a text, including what the author wants to answer, explain, or describe.
- **RI.2.7** Explain how specific images (e.g., a diagram showing how a machine works) contribute to and clarify a text.
- **RI.2.8** Describe how reasons support specific points the author makes in a text.
- **RI.2.9** Compare and contrast the most important points presented by two texts on the same topic.
- **RL.2.1** Ask and answer who, what, where, when, why, and how questions to show understanding of key details in a story.
- **RL.2.10** Read and comprehend literature, including stories and poetry, in the grades 2–3 text complexity band.
- **RL.2.2** Recount stories, including fables and folktales from diverse cultures, and determine their central message, lesson, or moral.
- **RL.2.3** Describe how characters in a story respond to major events and challenges.
- **RL.2.4** Describe how words and phrases (regular beats, alliteration, rhymes, repeated lines) supply rhythm and meaning in a story, poem, or song.
- **RL.2.5** Describe the overall structure of a story, including how the beginning introduces the story and the ending concludes the action.
- **RL.2.6** Acknowledge differences in the points of view of characters, including speaking in a different voice for each character when reading aloud.
- **RL.2.7** Use information from illustrations and words in a print or digital text to show understanding of characters, setting, or plot.
- **RL.2.9** Compare and contrast two or more versions of the same story (e.g., Cinderella stories) by different authors or from different cultures.
- **SL.2.1** Participate in collaborative conversations about grade 2 topics and texts, following agreed-upon rules and building on others' talk.
- **SL.2.2** Recount or describe key ideas or details from a text read aloud or information presented orally or through other media.
- **SL.2.3** Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says to clarify comprehension or gather more information.
- **SL.2.4** Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences.
- **SL.2.5** Create audio recordings of stories or poems; add drawings or other visual displays to stories or recounts to clarify ideas.
- **SL.2.6** Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation to provide requested detail or clarification.
- **W.2.1** Write opinion pieces that introduce the topic, state an opinion, supply reasons using linking words, and provide a concluding statement.
- **W.2.2** Write informative/explanatory texts that introduce a topic, use facts and definitions to develop points, and provide a concluding statement.
- **W.2.3** Write narratives that recount a well-elaborated event or short sequence of events with details, temporal words, and a sense of closure.
- **W.2.5** With guidance and support from adults and peers, focus on a topic and strengthen writing by revising and editing.
- **W.2.6** With guidance and support, use a variety of digital tools to produce and publish writing, including in collaboration with peers.
- **W.2.7** Participate in shared research and writing projects (e.g., read books on a single topic to produce a report).
- **W.2.8** Recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question.
