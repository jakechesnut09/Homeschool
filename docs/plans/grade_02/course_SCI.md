# Science 2 (Grade 2)

Second-grade science aligned to the California NGSS. Students investigate the properties of materials and how heating and cooling change them, build and rebuild objects from small pieces, test whether plants need light and water, model how animals pollinate flowers and spread seeds, compare the diversity of life in different habitats, and study how Earth's surface changes quickly and slowly through volcanoes, earthquakes, and erosion. Each week includes a hands-on investigation with household materials recorded in a science notebook, and engineering design challenges run throughout the year.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 California Science Framework  
*Time:* 40 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Thinking Like Scientists and Engineers (weeks 1–2)
Scientists ask questions and investigate; engineers define problems and design solutions.

- Week 1: Scientists observe and ask questions
- Week 2: Engineers solve problems

### Unit 2: Properties of Materials (weeks 3–7)
Materials have properties we can observe and test, and we choose materials whose properties fit the job.

- Week 3: Describing materials by properties
- Week 4: Testing materials: absorbency and waterproofing
- Week 5: Testing materials: strength and flexibility
- Week 6: Hot and cold: insulators
- Week 7: Materials unit project: build a better umbrella

### Unit 3: Building Blocks and Changing Matter (weeks 8–11)
Objects can be taken apart and rebuilt into new things, and heating or cooling changes some materials for good.

- Week 8: Pieces make a whole
- Week 9: Reversible changes with heating and cooling
- Week 10: Irreversible changes
- Week 11: Matter unit project: recycled materials

### Unit 4: What Plants Need (weeks 12–15)
Plants need water and light to grow, and fair tests show what they need.

- Week 12: Parts of plants and plant needs
- Week 13: Fair test: Do plants need light?
- Week 14: Fair test: Do plants need water?
- Week 15: Plant needs conclusions

### Unit 5: Seeds, Pollinators, and Plant Partners (weeks 16–19)
Plants depend on animals to move pollen and spread seeds.

- Week 16: Seeds on the move
- Week 17: Modeling seed dispersal
- Week 18: Pollination
- Week 19: Build a pollinator model

### Unit 6: Habitats and Biodiversity (weeks 20–23)
Different habitats hold different kinds and numbers of living things.

- Week 20: What is a habitat? Surveying living things nearby
- Week 21: Comparing land habitats: forest, desert, grassland
- Week 22: Comparing water habitats: kelp forest, pond, tide pool
- Week 23: Habitat diorama project

### Unit 7: Earth's Changes, Fast and Slow (weeks 24–27)
Some Earth events happen in minutes and others take thousands of years.

- Week 24: Fast Earth changes: volcanoes, earthquakes, landslides
- Week 25: Slow Earth changes: weathering and erosion
- Week 26: Comparing fast and slow changes on a timeline
- Week 27: Earth changes model and unit check

### Unit 8: Wind, Water, and Protecting the Land (weeks 28–31)
Wind and water reshape land, and people design solutions to slow erosion.

- Week 28: How wind and water change land
- Week 29: Testing solutions that slow erosion
- Week 30: Design challenge: protect the house on the hill
- Week 31: Improve, communicate, and unit check

### Unit 9: Maps of Land and Water (weeks 32–34)
Models and maps show the shapes of land and water, and Earth's water can be solid or liquid.

- Week 32: Landforms and bodies of water
- Week 33: Mapping land and water
- Week 34: Where water is found: solid and liquid

### Unit 10: Engineering Showcase (weeks 35–36)
Engineers use what they know about materials and Earth to design, test, and improve solutions.

- Week 35: Engineering challenge: design a bridge
- Week 36: Science fair and year review

## Standards

- **2-ESS1-1** Use information from several sources to provide evidence that Earth events can occur quickly or slowly.
- **2-ESS2-1** Compare multiple solutions designed to slow or prevent wind or water from changing the shape of the land.
- **2-ESS2-2** Develop a model to represent the shapes and kinds of land and bodies of water in an area.
- **2-ESS2-3** Obtain information to identify where water is found on Earth and that it can be solid or liquid.
- **2-LS2-1** Plan and conduct an investigation to determine if plants need sunlight and water to grow.
- **2-LS2-2** Develop a simple model that mimics the function of an animal in dispersing seeds or pollinating plants.
- **2-LS4-1** Make observations of plants and animals to compare the diversity of life in different habitats.
- **2-PS1-1** Plan and conduct an investigation to describe and classify different kinds of materials by their observable properties.
- **2-PS1-2** Analyze data obtained from testing different materials to determine which materials have the properties best suited for an intended purpose.
- **2-PS1-3** Make observations to construct an evidence-based account of how an object made of a small set of pieces can be disassembled and made into a new object.
- **2-PS1-4** Construct an argument with evidence that some changes caused by heating or cooling can be reversed and some cannot.
- **K-2-ETS1-1** Ask questions, make observations, and gather information about a situation people want to change to define a simple problem that can be solved through a new or improved object or tool.
- **K-2-ETS1-2** Develop a simple sketch, drawing, or physical model to illustrate how the shape of an object helps it function as needed to solve a given problem.
- **K-2-ETS1-3** Analyze data from tests of two objects designed to solve the same problem to compare the strengths and weaknesses of how each performs.
