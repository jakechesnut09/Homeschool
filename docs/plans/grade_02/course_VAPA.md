# Art & Music 2 (Grade 2)

A second-grade arts course that alternates visual art and music units, with a short dance and drama unit, aligned to the 2019 California Arts Standards. Students explore line, shape, color, texture, and form through drawing, painting, collage, clay, printmaking, and recycled sculpture while studying artists such as Kandinsky, Matisse, Van Gogh, Monet, and Louise Nevelson and art traditions from around the world. In music they keep a steady beat, read and write rhythms (ta, ti-ti, rest), sing sol-mi-la melodies, identify instrument families in Peter and the Wolf and Carnival of the Animals, and compose short pieces. The year ends with a home art exhibit and music performance.

*Framework:* 2019 California Arts Standards for Public Schools (Visual Arts, Music, Dance, Theatre); 2020 California Arts Education Framework  
*Time:* 40 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Art: Line and Shape (weeks 1–4)
Artists use many kinds of lines and shapes to show movement, feeling, and ideas.

- Week 1: Kinds of lines
- Week 2: Geometric and organic shapes: Kandinsky
- Week 3: Matisse cut-outs: drawing with scissors
- Week 4: Line and shape project: Mondrian and my neighborhood

### Unit 2: Music: Steady Beat and Rhythm (weeks 5–8)
Music has a steady beat, and rhythms are patterns of long and short sounds we can read and write.

- Week 5: Steady beat
- Week 6: Reading rhythm: ta, ti-ti, and rest
- Week 7: Body percussion and rhythm ostinatos
- Week 8: Meter and rhythm composition

### Unit 3: Art: Color and Mood (weeks 9–12)
Colors can be mixed, and artists choose colors to create a mood.

- Week 9: The color wheel
- Week 10: Warm and cool colors and mood
- Week 11: Tints, shades, and Monet's gardens
- Week 12: Color project: a painting with a mood

### Unit 4: Music: Melody and Pitch (weeks 13–16)
Melodies move up, down, and stay the same, and we can sing, play, and write them.

- Week 13: High and low: pitch
- Week 14: Sol, mi, and la
- Week 15: Folk songs and singing games
- Week 16: Compose a melody

### Unit 5: Art: Texture, Form, and Sculpture (weeks 17–20)
Artists create texture and three-dimensional forms, and everyday objects can be repurposed into art.

- Week 17: Texture: rubbings and implied texture
- Week 18: Clay forms: pinch pots
- Week 19: Repurposed sculpture: Louise Nevelson
- Week 20: Sculpture project and unit reflection

### Unit 6: Music: Instruments, Timbre, Dynamics, and Tempo (weeks 21–24)
Each instrument has its own sound, and composers use loud/soft and fast/slow to tell stories.

- Week 21: Instrument families
- Week 22: Peter and the Wolf
- Week 23: Dynamics and tempo: Carnival of the Animals
- Week 24: Music in daily life and unit reflection

### Unit 7: Art Around the World (weeks 25–28)
People in different times and places make art for celebrations, everyday use, and storytelling.

- Week 25: Papel picado (Mexico)
- Week 26: Gyotaku fish printing (Japan)
- Week 27: Patterns in textiles and baskets: kente cloth and Native California baskets
- Week 28: Murals and public art in communities

### Unit 8: Dance and Drama (weeks 29–31)
Bodies and voices can tell stories through movement and pretend play.

- Week 29: Creative movement: levels, shapes, and pathways
- Week 30: Story drama: The Three Billy Goats Gruff
- Week 31: Moving stories: dance, drama, and music together

### Unit 9: Create and Compose: My Community (weeks 32–35)
Artists and musicians create works about their own lives and communities and prepare them to share.

- Week 32: Art about my life: drawing home and community
- Week 33: Compose a song about my community
- Week 34: Preparing art for presentation
- Week 35: Rehearsing the showcase

### Unit 10: Showcase (weeks 36–36)
Sharing art and music with an audience celebrates our growth.

- Week 36: Art and music showcase

## Standards

- **DA:Cr1.1.2** Explore movement inspired by a variety of stimuli (music, sound, text, objects, images) and suggest additional sources for movement ideas.
- **DA:Pr5.1.2** Demonstrate locomotor and nonlocomotor movements that change body shapes, levels, and facings, moving in straight, curved, and zigzag pathways.
- **MU:Cn11.0.2** Demonstrate understanding of relationships between music and the other arts, other disciplines, varied contexts, and daily life.
- **MU:Cr1.1.2** Improvise rhythmic and melodic patterns and musical ideas for a specific purpose, within a given tonality and meter.
- **MU:Cr2.1.2** Demonstrate and explain personal reasons for selecting musical patterns and ideas, and use iconic or standard notation and/or recording to document them.
- **MU:Cr3.1.2** Interpret and apply personal, peer, and teacher feedback to revise music.
- **MU:Cr3.2.2** Convey creative expression and present a final version of personal musical ideas to peers or an audience.
- **MU:Pr4.1.2** Demonstrate and explain personal interest in, knowledge about, and purpose of varied musical selections.
- **MU:Pr4.2.2** Demonstrate knowledge of music concepts (such as tonality and meter) and read and perform rhythmic and melodic patterns using iconic or standard notation.
- **MU:Pr4.3.2** Demonstrate understanding of expressive qualities (such as dynamics and tempo) and how creators use them to convey expressive intent.
- **MU:Pr5.1.2** Apply feedback to refine performances and use suggested strategies in rehearsal to address challenges.
- **MU:Pr6.1.2** Perform music for a specific purpose with expression and technical accuracy, showing audience etiquette appropriate to the context.
- **MU:Re7.1.2** Explain and demonstrate how personal interests and experiences influence musical selection for specific purposes.
- **MU:Re7.2.2** Describe how specific music concepts are used to support a specific purpose in music.
- **MU:Re8.1.2** Demonstrate knowledge of music concepts and how they support creators' and performers' expressive intent.
- **MU:Re9.1.2** Apply personal and expressive preferences in the evaluation of music for specific purposes.
- **TH:Cr1.1.2** Propose potential choices characters could make in a guided drama experience and collaborate to determine story elements.
- **TH:Pr6.1.2** Contribute to group guided drama experiences (e.g., process drama, story drama, creative drama) and informally share with peers.
- **VA:Cn10.1.2** Create works of art about events in home, school, or community life.
- **VA:Cn11.1.2** Compare and contrast cultural uses of artwork from different times and places.
- **VA:Cr1.1.2** Brainstorm collaboratively multiple approaches to an art or design problem.
- **VA:Cr1.2.2** Make art or design with various materials and tools to explore personal interests, questions, and curiosity.
- **VA:Cr2.1.2** Experiment with various materials and tools to explore personal interests in a work of art or design.
- **VA:Cr2.2.2** Demonstrate safe procedures for using and cleaning art tools, equipment, and studio spaces.
- **VA:Cr2.3.2** Repurpose objects to make something new.
- **VA:Cr3.1.2** Discuss and reflect with peers about choices made in creating artwork.
- **VA:Pr4.1.2** Categorize artwork based on a theme or concept for an exhibit.
- **VA:Pr5.1.2** Distinguish between different materials or artistic techniques for preparing artwork for presentation.
- **VA:Pr6.1.2** Analyze how art exhibited inside and outside of schools (museums, galleries, murals) contributes to communities.
- **VA:Re7.1.2** Perceive and describe aesthetic characteristics of one's natural world and constructed environments.
- **VA:Re7.2.2** Categorize images based on expressive properties.
- **VA:Re8.1.2** Interpret art by identifying the mood suggested by a work of art and describing relevant subject matter and characteristics of form.
- **VA:Re9.1.2** Use learned art vocabulary to express preferences about artwork.
