# Math 2 (Grade 2)

Second-grade mathematics aligned to the California Common Core: fluency with addition and subtraction facts within 20, place value to 1,000, adding and subtracting within 100 and 1,000 with place-value strategies, measuring length in standard units, money and time, picture graphs, bar graphs and line plots, arrays and odd/even numbers as a foundation for multiplication, and shapes and equal shares. Lessons follow a concrete–pictorial–abstract path with base-ten blocks, number lines, and real objects, and every week includes word problems, a fact-fluency routine, and a Friday check.

*Framework:* California Common Core State Standards for Mathematics; 2023 California Mathematics Framework  
*Time:* 60 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Addition and Subtraction Fluency Within 20 (weeks 1–4)
Facts within 20 can be found quickly with strategies like making ten, doubles, and thinking addition for subtraction.

- Week 1: Facts within 10 and counting on
- Week 2: Make-a-ten and near doubles
- Week 3: Subtraction strategies within 20
- Week 4: Unknowns in all positions and fluency check

### Unit 2: Place Value to 1,000 (weeks 5–8)
Three-digit numbers are made of hundreds, tens, and ones, and place value lets us read, write, and compare them.

- Week 5: Hundreds, tens, and ones
- Week 6: Counting and skip-counting to 1,000
- Week 7: Reading and writing three-digit numbers
- Week 8: Comparing three-digit numbers

### Unit 3: Measuring Length (weeks 9–11)
We measure length by repeating equal units with no gaps, and smaller units give larger numbers.

- Week 9: Measuring in inches and feet
- Week 10: Measuring in centimeters and meters
- Week 11: Comparing lengths

### Unit 4: Adding and Subtracting Within 100 (weeks 12–16)
Place value lets us add and subtract two-digit numbers by combining or breaking apart tens and ones.

- Week 12: Adding within 100 using place value
- Week 13: Subtracting within 100 using place value
- Week 14: Adding several two-digit numbers
- Week 15: One- and two-step word problems within 100
- Week 16: Fluency within 100 and unit review

### Unit 5: Data: Graphs and Line Plots (weeks 17–18)
Graphs organize data so we can compare categories and answer questions.

- Week 17: Picture graphs and bar graphs
- Week 18: Measurement data and line plots

### Unit 6: Adding and Subtracting Within 1,000 (weeks 19–22)
The same place-value strategies work for hundreds: compose and decompose tens and hundreds as needed.

- Week 19: Adding within 1,000
- Week 20: Subtracting within 1,000
- Week 21: Mental math with 10 and 100
- Week 22: Problem solving within 1,000

### Unit 7: Money and Time (weeks 23–25)
Coins and bills have values we can count and combine; clocks show time in five-minute intervals.

- Week 23: Coins and counting money
- Week 24: Dollars and money word problems
- Week 25: Telling time to five minutes

### Unit 8: Length Word Problems and Number Lines (weeks 26–27)
A number line is a ruler for numbers, and it helps solve length problems.

- Week 26: Number line diagrams
- Week 27: Length word problems

### Unit 9: Equal Groups, Arrays, Odd and Even (weeks 28–30)
Equal groups and arrays prepare us for multiplication; even numbers can be split into two equal groups.

- Week 28: Odd and even numbers
- Week 29: Equal groups and arrays
- Week 30: Arrays and repeated addition problem solving

### Unit 10: Geometry and Equal Shares (weeks 31–33)
Shapes are defined by their attributes, and wholes can be cut into equal shares called halves, thirds, and fourths.

- Week 31: Two-dimensional shapes
- Week 32: Three-dimensional shapes and partitioning rectangles
- Week 33: Equal shares: halves, thirds, fourths

### Unit 11: Putting It All Together (weeks 34–36)
Mathematicians choose strategies and tools to solve multi-step problems and explain their thinking.

- Week 34: Review: number and operations
- Week 35: Review: measurement, data, money, time
- Week 36: Year-end assessment and math fair

## Standards

- **2.G.A.1** Recognize and draw shapes having specified attributes (number of angles or equal faces); identify triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and cubes.
- **2.G.A.2** Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and count to find the total number of them.
- **2.G.A.3** Partition circles and rectangles into two, three, or four equal shares, describe the shares as halves, thirds, fourths, and recognize equal shares of identical wholes need not have the same shape.
- **2.MD.A.1** Measure the length of an object by selecting and using appropriate tools (rulers, yardsticks, meter sticks, measuring tapes).
- **2.MD.A.2** Measure the length of an object twice using different length units and describe how the measurements relate to the size of the unit.
- **2.MD.A.3** Estimate lengths using units of inches, feet, centimeters, and meters.
- **2.MD.A.4** Measure to determine how much longer one object is than another, expressing the difference in a standard length unit.
- **2.MD.B.5** Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve word problems involving lengths given in the same units.
- **2.MD.B.6** Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0 on a number line diagram and represent sums and differences within 100 on a number line.
- **2.MD.C.7** Tell and write time from analog and digital clocks to the nearest five minutes, using a.m. and p.m.
- **2.MD.C.8** Solve word problems involving dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels, and pennies, using $ and ¢ symbols appropriately.
- **2.MD.D.10** Draw a picture graph and a bar graph (single-unit scale) for a data set with up to four categories; solve put-together, take-apart, and compare problems using the graph.
- **2.MD.D.9** Generate measurement data by measuring lengths to the nearest whole unit and show the data on a line plot.
- **2.NBT.A.1** Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent hundreds, tens, and ones; 100 is a bundle of ten tens.
- **2.NBT.A.2** Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s.
- **2.NBT.A.3** Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.
- **2.NBT.A.4** Compare two three-digit numbers based on hundreds, tens, and ones digits using >, =, and <.
- **2.NBT.B.5** Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **2.NBT.B.6** Add up to four two-digit numbers using strategies based on place value and properties of operations.
- **2.NBT.B.7** Add and subtract within 1000 using concrete models, drawings, and place-value strategies; understand composing and decomposing tens and hundreds.
- **2.NBT.B.8** Mentally add 10 or 100 to, and subtract 10 or 100 from, a number 100–900.
- **2.NBT.B.9** Explain why addition and subtraction strategies work, using place value and properties of operations.
- **2.OA.A.1** Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve one- and two-step word problems (adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, comparing) with unknowns in all positions.
- **2.OA.B.2** Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies; by end of grade 2, know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers.
- **2.OA.C.3** Determine whether a group of objects (up to 20) has an odd or even number of members; write an even number as a sum of two equal addends.
- **2.OA.C.4** Use addition to find the total number of objects in rectangular arrays with up to 5 rows and 5 columns; write an equation of equal addends.
