# Science 3 (Grade 3)

A hands-on, NGSS-aligned third-grade science course covering forces and motion, magnets and static electricity, life cycles, animal groups, inheritance and variation, fossils, habitats and environmental change, weather and climate, and weather-related hazards. Students conduct investigations with household materials, keep a science notebook, construct evidence-based arguments, and complete engineering design challenges.

*Framework:* California Next Generation Science Standards (CA NGSS); 2016 CA Science Framework  
*Time:* 40 min/day

## Units

### Unit 1: Forces and Motion (weeks 1–4)
Pushes and pulls change motion when forces are unbalanced, and patterns of motion can be predicted.

- Week 1: Pushes, pulls and balanced forces
- Week 2: Investigating force and motion
- Week 3: Patterns of motion
- Week 4: Forces and motion design challenge

### Unit 2: Magnets and Static Electricity (weeks 5–7)
Some forces act at a distance; we can use magnets to solve problems.

- Week 5: Magnetic forces
- Week 6: Static electricity
- Week 7: Magnet design problem

### Unit 3: Life Cycles (weeks 8–11)
All living things are born, grow, reproduce and die, but their life cycles differ.

- Week 8: Plant life cycles
- Week 9: Animal life cycles: insects
- Week 10: Animal life cycles: frogs, birds, mammals
- Week 11: Life cycle models

### Unit 4: Animal Groups and Survival (weeks 12–13)
Living in groups helps some animals survive.

- Week 12: Why animals live in groups
- Week 13: Argument: groups help survival

### Unit 5: Inheritance, Variation and Environment (weeks 14–17)
Offspring inherit traits from parents, traits vary, and environment can influence traits.

- Week 14: Inherited traits
- Week 15: Variation within a group
- Week 16: Environment and traits
- Week 17: Variation and survival advantage

### Unit 6: Fossils and Past Environments (weeks 18–20)
Fossils are evidence of organisms and environments from long ago.

- Week 18: What fossils tell us
- Week 19: Fossils as evidence of past environments
- Week 20: Extinct organisms and modern relatives

### Unit 7: Habitats and Environmental Change (weeks 21–24)
Organisms survive best in habitats that meet their needs; when environments change, solutions can help.

- Week 21: Habitats meet needs
- Week 22: California habitats
- Week 23: Environmental change
- Week 24: Solutions to environmental problems

### Unit 8: Weather and Climate (weeks 25–30)
Weather follows seasonal patterns that can be measured, and climates differ around the world.

- Week 25: Weather tools
- Week 26: Collecting weather data
- Week 27: Seasonal weather patterns
- Week 28: Climates of the world
- Week 29: California climates
- Week 30: Weather and climate review

### Unit 9: Natural Hazards and Engineering Design (weeks 31–36)
Engineers design solutions that reduce the impacts of weather-related hazards.

- Week 31: Weather-related hazards
- Week 32: Design solutions for hazards
- Week 33: Engineering: define the problem
- Week 34: Engineering: build and test
- Week 35: Engineering: improve and present
- Week 36: Science year review

## Standards

- **3-5-ETS1-1** Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or want that includes specified criteria for success and constraints on materials, time or cost.
- **3-5-ETS1-2** Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints.
- **3-5-ETS1-3** Plan and carry out fair tests in which variables are controlled and failure points are considered to identify aspects of a model or prototype that can be improved.
- **3-ESS2-1** Represent data in tables and graphical displays to describe typical weather conditions expected during a particular season.
- **3-ESS2-2** Obtain and combine information to describe climates in different regions of the world.
- **3-ESS3-1** Make a claim about the merit of a design solution that reduces the impacts of a weather-related hazard.
- **3-LS1-1** Develop models to describe that organisms have unique and diverse life cycles but all have in common birth, growth, reproduction and death.
- **3-LS2-1** Construct an argument that some animals form groups that help members survive.
- **3-LS3-1** Analyze and interpret data to provide evidence that plants and animals have traits inherited from parents and that variation of these traits exists in a group of similar organisms.
- **3-LS3-2** Use evidence to support the explanation that traits can be influenced by the environment.
- **3-LS4-1** Analyze and interpret data from fossils to provide evidence of the organisms and the environments in which they lived long ago.
- **3-LS4-2** Use evidence to construct an explanation for how variations in characteristics among individuals of the same species may provide advantages in surviving, finding mates and reproducing.
- **3-LS4-3** Construct an argument with evidence that in a particular habitat some organisms can survive well, some survive less well, and some cannot survive at all.
- **3-LS4-4** Make a claim about the merit of a solution to a problem caused when the environment changes and the types of plants and animals that live there may change.
- **3-PS2-1** Plan and conduct an investigation to provide evidence of the effects of balanced and unbalanced forces on the motion of an object.
- **3-PS2-2** Make observations and/or measurements of an object's motion to provide evidence that a pattern can be used to predict future motion.
- **3-PS2-3** Ask questions to determine cause and effect relationships of electric or magnetic interactions between two objects not in contact with each other.
- **3-PS2-4** Define a simple design problem that can be solved by applying scientific ideas about magnets.
